So I fell down a rabbit hole a while back reading about Edward Nelson, a Princeton mathematician who in 2011 announced he'd actually proved that Peano arithmetic - and even a small chunk of primitive recursive arithmetic, which is about as basic as math gets - was inconsistent. Like, an honest-to-god contradiction sitting at the root of arithmetic itself. Terence Tao and another logician found a hole in the argument within about a week and Nelson graciously withdrew the claim, but he'd apparently spent 25 years building up to it and went right back to trying to fix it afterward. Kind of wild that a year or so earlier, Voevodsky (the Fields medalist) had given a talk literally titled "What If Current Foundations of Mathematics Are Inconsistent?" - basically saying he suspected an inconsistency in arithmetic might eventually turn up for real.

Anyway it got me wondering what would actually happen if a claim like that didn't fall apart under scrutiny - if someone found a genuine contradiction buried in the axioms we use for arithmetic or set theory, and it just... held up, no matter who poked at it. Could you write me a short story built around that premise? Doesn't matter if its set now or a bit in the future, or whose head we're in - the mathematician who found it, a colleague, a journalist trying to explain it to the public, whoever. I just don't want it to be a vague "math breaks, cue chaos" story - I'd like it to actually grapple with what such a discovery would mean, both for all the practical stuff quietly resting on these foundations (computers, bridges, whatever) and for the more abstract "wait, is any of this real" panic among the people who think about this for a living. Extra credit if it plays with the fact that, from what I understand, a system can't ever fully prove its own consistency anyway - so this is a hole nobody could have ever completely ruled out to begin with.
