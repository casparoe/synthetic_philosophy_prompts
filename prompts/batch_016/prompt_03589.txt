A few of us from a long-running Slate Star Codex/Astral Codex Ten meetup have spent the last month or so working through the philosophy of time, and I think I've talked myself into a conclusion that's making it hard to take my own planning seriously. The trigger was Penrose's "Andromeda paradox": two people walk past each other on an ordinary street, one drifting ever so slightly toward the Andromeda galaxy and the other away from it, and because of the relativity of simultaneity, the plane of "now" each of them is implicitly using, once extended two and a half million light-years, disagrees by several days about whether some hypothetical Andromedan invasion fleet has already launched. Since special relativity denies that either observer's "now" is privileged, and since the Rietdijk-Putnam argument that Penrose was illustrating seems to generalize this into a case against any single, objectively distinguished present, I've come around to thinking presentism can't be right, and that some version of eternalism -- the block universe, where past, present, and future are all equally and tenselessly real -- has to be true.

What I can't talk myself out of is the further step that almost everyone in the group also took: if next Tuesday's version of me is already just as real a feature of the four-dimensional manifold as yesterday's version, then whatever I end up doing next Tuesday is already sitting there, settled, regardless of how much deliberating I do between now and then. Weighing options, running rough expected-value comparisons, agonizing over a decision -- all of it starts to feel like an elaborate ritual for finding out what I was always going to do, rather than a process that actually has any say in the matter. So I would like to know: for someone who accepts the physics-driven case for eternalism, is there a principled way to keep treating deliberation as doing real work, or is the honest conclusion that words like "choosing" and "deciding" should quietly give way to something more like "discovering," once you take the block universe seriously?
