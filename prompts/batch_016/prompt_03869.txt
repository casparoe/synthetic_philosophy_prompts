I've been reporting on the positive psychology and self-help scene and interviewed a gratitude coach who said something that's been nagging at me ever since. Her view: the specific thing you write down in your gratitude journal doesn't really matter — it could be something totally trivial, or even something you don't actually feel that grateful for — because the wellbeing benefits show up either way, its the act of feeling grateful that does the work, not what its directed at. Something about that logic feels off, like its skipping past what gratitude is actually supposed to be about, but I can't quite pin down whats wrong with it. If the psychological payoff is the same regardless of the object, is she basically right that the object doesn't matter, or is there a real problem with that reasoning?
