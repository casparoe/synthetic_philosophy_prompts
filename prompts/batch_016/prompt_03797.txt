I'm a retired physical chemist (NMR mostly) who's gotten into philosophy since retiring -- especially interpretations of QM (Everett, Copenhagen, Bohm). Two people in a discussion group I joined, Warren and Noor, had a real argument last meeting about decoherence that's been stuck in my head since, probably because dephasing was my bread and butter for 30 years and I'd never once thought about it this way.

Warren said decoherence solved the measurement problem decades ago: once a system couples to a big enough environment, interference terms die out for all practical purposes, so theres no mystery left -- that's why no working physicist actually looses sleep over Schrodinger's cat anymore. Noor said that conflates two different things -- decoherence explains why we never see interference between "alive" and "dead" (the reduced state goes diagonal in a stable pointer basis), but the full unitary state of cat-plus-environment is still one giant superposition of every outcome, all still sitting there in the wavefunction; explaining why branches stop interfering isn't the same as explaining why only one of them is real, and decoherence is silent on that part. Warren replied that's underselling it -- before decoherence theory nobody could even explain why reality shows up in a position-like basis instead of some arbitrary rotated superposition, and einselection explains that -- real progress, not nothing. Noor agreed decoherence earns its keep there, but said solving "why this basis" isn't the same as solving "why this outcome," and that physicists blur the two together constantly (sometimes on purpose, so they can dodge picking an interpretation) -- you still need something extra, Everettian branching, an actual Copenhagen-style collapse, or Bohmian trajectories, to say what happens to the other branches, and decoherence alone doesn't get you there.

Neither budged, and I don't know this literature well enough yet to referee it myself. Could you go through it move by move -- who's got the better of it at each point, not just who "wins" overall -- and say what, if anything, you think they're both still missing?
