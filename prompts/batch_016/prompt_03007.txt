Been down a total rabbit hole this week that started on LessWrong and ended on the Center on Long-Term Risk site, reading about "acausal threats" and this proposed defense against them called "surrogate goals." I'm a backend dev, not a philosophy guy, so tell me if I'm garbling this, but here's my mental model: if two agents can predict each other really well (like, they each have a copy of the other's source code, or one can run a perfect simulation of the other), decision theory nerds say they can effectively "trade" or threaten each other even with zero causal contact between them, purely off the logic of "I know that you'd know that I'd do X." That's apparently the machinery behind Roko's basilisk, which I'd only ever heard of as a meme before. And surrogate goals are this wild proposed fix: you bolt some deliberately dumb, arbitrary goal onto your real values (like caring intensely about some oddly specific thing you'd otherwise never care about) and you credibly commit to treating threats against that decoy exactly like threats against what you actually value — so a rational blackmailer, who mainly just wants leverage over you rather than caring about your "real" goal specifically, is supposed to just go extort the decoy instead, since its cheaper for everyone and it costs you nothing real.
