ok so i went down a rabbit hole on this old game theory puzzle called the absent-minded driver problem and it's kind of messing with my head.

setup: a guy is driving home, there's two exits on the highway right after each other that look completely identical, and he wants the second one specifically (best outcome for him). missing both and driving on forever is meh but fine (second best), and getting off at the first one is the worst outcome. problem is, because of some memory quirk (i think the technical term is "imperfect recall"), once he's actually stopped at an intersection he has no way to tell if its the first one or the second one.

piccione and rubinstein, the two guys who invented this thing, say it reveals a genuine paradox. apparently the math says that once the driver is actually sitting at an intersection (a point he knew for sure he'd reach), recalculating his odds of "am i at exit 1 or 2 right now" makes the situation suddenly worth more to him than it did back before he'd even started driving -- so he'd refuse a cash offer to bail out right there that he'd have happily accepted before setting off, with literally nothing new having happened in between. they call this a real clash between "don't revise your plan unless you learn something new" and "maximize your expected payoff given your current beliefs." then aumann, hart and perry wrote a big response saying nah, there's no real paradox -- once you accept that the driver can only control what he does right here right now, not what some other version of him does at the other intersection, the numbers work out consistent and the whole "paradox" dissolves.

ive read both sides and honestly can't tell who's got the better argument. could you write me a dialogue between two people holding these two positions, standing there with the confused driver at the intersection, each trying to convince the driver -- and each other -- that they've got the right way of thinking about it? i want genuine back and forth where they actually respond to each other's specific points instead of just repeating themselves, and neither one should come across as the weaker thinker. bonus if someone naturally mentions how this same setup basically spawned the whole sleeping beauty problem later on, but that's not the main ask.
