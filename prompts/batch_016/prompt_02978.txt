I'm a backend engineer, and a few of us at work run an informal lunchtime reading group working through a game theory textbook, partly because two of my colleagues are prepping for quant trading interviews. We recently spent a couple of sessions on the centipede game: two players alternate turns over a shared pot that starts at, say, $1 and doubles every round for up to six rounds; at each turn, the player to move can either take (ending the game immediately and claiming the larger share of the current pot) or pass (growing the pot further but handing the next decision to the other player). Backward induction implies that the very first player should take immediately, since reasoning backward from the last round always favors taking at every node, yet in practice, and even in our own toy simulations, everyone wanted to pass for a while, since the joint payoff grows so much faster than the individual risk of being cut out.

What's actually been nagging me is a subtler issue I ran into afterward while reading around online, and it's less about the empirical mismatch between theory and behavior (nobody expects real people to be fully rational) and more about whether the backward-induction argument is even internally consistent on its own terms. The argument seems to rest on common knowledge that both players are rational at every node, including nodes that, by the argument's own logic, should never be reached if that common knowledge genuinely held. So once a player has passed even a single time, doesn't that very fact undercut the assumption that was supposed to license the reasoning at every node from then on? I gather this is a real, unresolved-feeling dispute among game theorists rather than a confusion unique to me -- Aumann's name kept coming up, alongside pushback from people like Binmore and Reny. Could you explain, in fairly intuitive terms and without leaning on formal epistemic-logic notation, why this is treated as a genuine paradox about the coherence of backward induction itself, rather than just a technical footnote, and what the main proposed ways of resolving or dissolving it look like?
