So I got into a whole debate with my housemate last night — she's doing a philosophy MA and has this running thing about how its still an open question whether neural nets "really" have representations, or whether that's just a metaphor us engineers project onto them because it's convenient. I brought up the Golden Gate Claude demo Anthropic did last year, where they took the SAE feature that fires on Golden Gate Bridge stuff, cranked the coefficient way up, and the model started shoehorning the bridge into every single reply, even ones that had nothing to do with San Francisco. That feels like about as close to a smoking gun as your gonna get: you find the exact spot in activation space where the concept lives, turn the dial, and the behavior shifts exactly the way the theory predicts. Isn't that just a representation, in more or less the same sense that a variable in memory represents a value? She said the steering result doesn't actually settle the metaphysical question and got into stuff about superposition and how the "feature" might not even be a unique or privileged way to carve up the activation space, but honestly "it's more subtle than that" doesn't feel like a real argument when the intervention demonstrably works every time you try it.

I'm not totally coming at this cold either — I trained a small SAE on an open-weights model for a side project a few months back, so I get the mechanics fine, I just don't see what's left philosophically unresolved once you can point at the vector and steer the behavior on demand. Is she onto something real here, or is this one of those cases where the philosophy side just keeps moving the goalposts on what would even count as counterevidence, so the "debate" is basically unfalsifiable dressed up as profound? Kind of want the steelman of her position instead of just telling her she's overthinking it next time it comes up.
