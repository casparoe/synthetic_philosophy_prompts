A while back I read Palle Yourgrau's book on Gödel and Einstein, A World Without Time, and one step in the argument it reconstructs has stuck with me, even though I have no formal training in philosophy or physics and have mostly puzzled through it alone. The argument is meant to support the eternalist, block-universe picture of time over any view, presentism included, on which time objectively flows.

Special relativity already rules out a single, universe-wide "now." Gödel went further, finding an exact solution to Einstein's field equations describing a rotating universe with closed timelike curves, so that a traveler could loop back into their own past — meaning that no definition of "world time" in such a universe could track an objective passage of time. The step I can't get past is this: because this rotating universe obeys exactly the same field equations as ours, differing only in how matter is distributed and moving, Gödel holds it would be unacceptable for something as fundamental as time's objective lapse to hinge on that kind of contingent arrangement. So even though our own universe isn't, as far as we can tell, rotating in his sense, he concludes it too must lack an objective lapse of time.

This seems to prove too much: general relativity is equally compatible with universes that are flat or curved, or that expand forever or recollapse, yet nobody thinks the mere possibility of a curved universe shows that our own, if flat, isn't really flat. Those look like exactly the sort of features allowed to depend on the contingent arrangement of matter — so why should time's objective lapse be any different?

I would like you to argue for Gödel's side of this as rigorously as you can: defend the claim that time's objective passage could not be the kind of thing that varies contingently between universes the way curvature or overall rotation does, so that the mere possibility of his rotating, timeless universe settles the matter for every universe under the same laws, ours included. Please engage directly with the flatness/curvature comparison rather than setting it aside.
