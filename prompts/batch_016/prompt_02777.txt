Okay so this is a total tangent from the novel I'm actually supposed to be writing, but I fell down a rabbit hole last night reading about decision theory (peak procrastination, I know) and got stuck on something economists call the absent-minded driver problem. The setup: a guy is at the pub planning his drive home, and to get there he needs to take the *second* of two exits on a highway - but the exits look identical, and he's absent-minded enough that once he's actually on the road he can't tell if he's at the first intersection or the second, or remember how many he's already passed. So instead of just deciding "exit" or "don't," he has to commit ahead of time to some fixed probability of exiting, which he'll use no matter which intersection he ends up at. The kicker is that if you sit down and do the math at the pub, there's one probability that's provably your best strategy (I've seen 1/3 thrown around, though apparently the exact number depends on how you set up the payoffs) - but then once he's actually stopped at an intersection, with literally zero new information, recalculating in the moment seems to justify a totally different, worse probability. People apparently call these the "planning-optimal" and "action-optimal" answers, and whole papers have been written arguing about whether the gap between them is a genuine paradox about rationality or just a mistake in how you're allowed to update your beliefs when you cant remember where you are.

I've got a side character in my current book, a insufferably smug game-theory postdoc, who needs a good line for a dinner party scene, and I want her joke to actually be about this problem, not just some generic "I always miss my exit" pun. I want the humor to turn on the real weirdness here - the gap between what's optimal to plan in advance and what's optimal to do once you're actually there and can't tell where "there" is - so that another decision theorist at the table gets a genuine laugh (or groan) out of it while everyone else just thinks she's bad with directions. Can you write me that joke, maybe with a one-line bit of setup for how she delivers it?
