I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on a dissertation chapter about Augustine's theory of lying, and I've been rereading De Mendacio (the early treatise, not Contra Mendacium) for what feels like the tenth time this month. Everyone who's taken one ethics class "knows" that Augustine thinks lying is absolutely forbidden, full stop, but the thing that actually keeps tripping me up is the wierd eightfold ranking of lies he lays out near the end of the treatise, going roughly from most to least sinful:

1. lies about religious doctrine
2. lies that harm someone and benefit nobody
3. lies that benefit one person at another's expense
4. lies told purely for the pleasure of lying itself
5. lies told to flatter or please someone in conversation
6. lies that harm no one and protect someone's property
7. lies that harm no one and protect a fugitive from execution (even a guilty one!)
8. lies that harm no one and protect someone from sexual/bodily defilement (the least bad, on his view)

What's been bugging my reading group is that on Augustines own ordering, lying to save an innocent person's life (#7) comes out as MORE sinful than lying to protect someone's cash (#6) or someone's chastity (#8), which strikes basically everyone I've shown it to as backwards. I even tried it on my intro ethics students and half of them assumed I was misremembering it.
