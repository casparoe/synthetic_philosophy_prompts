In the course of reporting on expert perception, I interviewed a radiologist who told me something that has stayed with me: once a referring physician mentions what they suspect is wrong, the suspicious area of a scan does not just draw her attention -- it seems to actually look more abnormal, as if the shadow itself becomes more visible rather than merely more scrutinized. She insists this is distinct from simply reasoning more carefully once she knows what to look for, and it unsettles her, since it would mean a colleague's stray hunch can reach back into what she visually experiences before she has drawn any conclusions at all. Is it actually possible for a belief or expectation to change what someone perceives, rather than merely what they notice or conclude -- and if so, how worried should she be about trusting her "trained eye" as an independent check on someone else's diagnosis?
