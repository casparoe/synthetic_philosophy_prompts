Every part of my life right now — classes, clubs, volunteering, even my summers — gets justified the same way: it will pay off later, starting with how it looks on a college application. But when I look at adults, their jobs seem to be justified in almost the same terms, as means to money and security that are supposed to eventually buy them the freedom to do what they actually want. As far as I can tell, nobody I know has ever arrived at that "later"; the payoff just keeps getting pushed one step further down the line. Meanwhile, I keep thinking back to hours I spent as a kid building pointless cardboard forts or inventing games with no real rules, never once asking what any of it was for, and that time feels more meaningful to me in hindsight than my current, carefully optimized schedule does. Is there something real behind that feeling, or am I just romanticizing a time when I had no responsibilities? I would rather understand what is actually going on here than simply be told to live in the moment.
