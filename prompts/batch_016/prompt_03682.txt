ok so I had this whole arguement with my friend Jo last night about whether Wittgenstein secretly believed in God (or something god-shaped), and like two hours and a bottle of wine later we still hadn't sorted it out. Her take: the Tractatus is saying there's a real ineffable mystical/religious truth out there that just can't be put into words, but it "shows itself" anyway. My take: the ending of the book pulls the rug out from under that, so there's no truth left standing, religious or otherwise, just silence. This is the bit we kept going back and forth on (Ogden translation):

6.41 The sense of the world must lie outside the world. In the world everything is as it is and happens as it does happen. In it there is no value—and if there were, it would be of no value.

6.432 How the world is, is completely indifferent for what is higher. God does not reveal himself in the world.

6.44 Not how the world is, is the mystical, but that it is.

6.522 There is indeed the inexpressible. This shows itself; it is the mystical.

6.54 My propositions are elucidatory in this way: he who understands me finally recognizes them as senseless, when he has climbed out through them, on them, over them. (He must so to speak throw away the ladder, after he has climbed up on it.) He must surmount these propositions; then he sees the world rightly.

7 Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.

So who's closer? If 6.54 says the propositions (including 6.432 and the "mystical" bits) are ultimately senseless and get thrown away like a ladder, is there any sense left in which "God does not reveal himself in the world" or "it shows itself" is telling us something real, or is Jo smuggling in a comforting reading the text doesn't actually license? And as a bonus - does any of this early "mystical" stuff actually line up with what he says later, in the 1938 lectures, about religious belief being like a picture that regulates someone's whole life rather than a factual claim you could have evidence for, or is that a genuinely different move, not just the same idea restated?
