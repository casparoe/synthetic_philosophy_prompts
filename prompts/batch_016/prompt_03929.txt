Okay, this is going to be a bit of a rabbit hole but bear with me. Ever since that Butlin, Long, Bengio, and a small army of other co-authors "indicator properties" report on AI consciousness made the rounds a while back, i've been down a real hole on higher-order theories of consciousness, since HOT theory is one of the ingredients they use to build their checklist for whether a given system might be conscious. I brought it up at our local ACX meetup and mostly got blank stares, so now I'm bringing it here instead. The thing that's been nagging at me is the "targetless HOT" problem: on Rosenthal's version of the theory, a mental state is conscious just in case you have a suitable, non-inferential higher-order thought (HOT) to the effect that your in that state, and since the HOT and the first-order state it's supposedly about are meant to be distinct existences, nothing stops the HOT from firing off with literally no first-order state underneath it - basically a pure hallucination of being in pain with no pain-state anywhere in the system. Rosenthal, and apparently Weisberg too, and Lycan for the higher-order-perception version, all just bite the bullet here: yep, still a conscious experience of pain, it's just a HOT misrepresenting a state that doesn't exist. I even saw that Lau and Rosenthal went looking for actual empirical cases of this, which honestly made the whole thing feel weirder to me instead of more legitimate.

My intuition is that this bullet-biting is a lot worse than the standard "perceptual illusions misrepresent the world and are still real experiences" move that higher-order theorists like to lean on to make it sound like business as usual, because in ordinary illusions there's still some first-order state doing representational work, whereas in the targetless case you get the full qualitative character out of what's supposed to be a two-place relation where one of the two places is just empty. I can't fully cash out why that disanalogy should matter so much though, and it clearly doesn't strike people like Rosenthal himself as a big deal, so maybe I'm missing something.

Can you build the strongest possible case that targetless HOTs are a genuinely fatal problem for higher-order theories in general -- not just Rosenthal's two-state version specifically, but Lycan-style higher-order perception too -- rather than a mildly weird commitment the theory can just shrug off? I really want you to engage with the "it's basically just like an illusion" defense directly and show exactly where the disanalogy is supposed to bite, rather than just asserting that the whole setup feels intuitively icky.
