ok this has been rattling around in my head since I reread Shadows of the Mind. Penrose's argument is basically: for any consistent formal system F thats strong enough to do arithmetic, Godel gives you a sentence G(F) thats true but unprovable in F, and *we* can see that G(F) is true just by reasoning about F from the outside, so whatever we're doing when we do math can't just be F running as an algorithm. When I actually work through the logic it seems pretty airtight, which as far as I can tell would mean no fixed algorithm (so no Turing machine, so no amount of scaling up a transformer) could ever fully replicate what a human mathematician does. But I've been reading LW/ACX stuff on AGI timelines for like a decade now and I never see anyone bring this up, people just debate takeoff speed and alignment like computability isn't even a live constraint. Is everyone just ignoring a real result here, or is there a hole in the argument that I'm missing?
