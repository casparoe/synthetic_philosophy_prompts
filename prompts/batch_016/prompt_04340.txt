In their "Embedded Agency" research program, Scott Garrabrant and Abram Demski argue that standard models of rational agency, from Bayesian decision theory to idealized constructions such as AIXI, tacitly assume a "Cartesian" picture in which the agent stands outside, and is strictly larger than, the environment it models. An embedded agent, by contrast, is a proper part of the world it acts in and reasons about, and can never contain a complete model of that world within itself.

Please compare these two conceptions of rational agency, focusing on (a) the Löbian obstacle to an agent's trusting its own future reasoning, and (b) the problem of logical, as opposed to purely empirical, uncertainty. In each case, explain why the difficulty resists being absorbed into the Cartesian picture as merely one more form of environmental uncertainty. Finally, defend a view: does embeddedness impose a genuinely new limitation on any formal theory of rationality, or does it essentially restate, in formal dress, an older philosophical problem about self-reference or the limits of self-knowledge?
