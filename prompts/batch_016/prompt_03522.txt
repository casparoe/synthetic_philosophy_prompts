Hey — I've been working on a piece about the new wave of embryo-screening startups (the ones letting IVF patients rank embryos by polygenic risk scores for things like diabetes, or even predicted height and IQ), and when I interviewed a bioethicist for it she kept steering the conversation toward this Parfit puzzle I'd never really sat with: the "non-identity problem." I went and reread the relevant chapter of Reasons and Persons afterward and now I'm a little stuck on it. The thing that messes with my head is that in the classic versions of the case — the 14-year-old girl who has a baby now instead of waiting a few years so the child would have a much better start in life, or a government picking a resource-depletion policy over conservation — nobody technically ends up worse off, because a different decision would've resulted in different people existing entirely, not the same people existing in better shape. So whoever "suffers" can't really complain, since the only alternative to their crummy-but-worth-living existence was never existing at all. And yet almost everyone's gut, mine included, says the girl (or the depleting government, or — per my actual article — a clinic that implants an embryo it knows will develop a painful heritable condition when a healthier one was sitting right there) still did something wrong. I definately didn't expect a decades-old thought experiment about resource policy to end up being the crux of a story about IVF, but here we are.

Could you write me a dialogue that really gets into the weeds of this, between two philosophers who are equally sharp and don't let each other off easy — maybe set at a bar after a bioethics conference panel? One is a hardline person-affecting theorist willing to bite the bullet all the way: if literally nobody ends up worse off than they otherwise would have been, nothing has been done wrong, full stop, however strange that sounds. The other thinks that's nuts and wants to vindicate the common-sense verdict, but has to actually explain what the wrong-making feature is if it isn't harm to some identifiable victim. I want real back-and-forth — each one responding to the other's specific move rather than just restating their opening position — and if it comes up naturally, I'd love for them to get dragged into how this connects to the "procreative asymmetry" (no duty to create happy people, but apparently a duty not to create miserable ones) and the Repugnant Conclusion, since I gather you can't dodge nonidentity for long without running into those too. Doesn't need a tidy resolution — a real impasse is a totally fine ending.
