In my philosophy of mind seminar, we've been discussing how multiple realizability is used to motivate functionalism over mind-brain type-identity theory. Putnam's original argument, extended by Fodor into a general case for the autonomy of psychology from neuroscience, treats multiple realizability as decisive evidence that mental states must be characterized functionally rather than identified with any single physical realizer. Jaegwon Kim, however, argues that multiple realizability actually supports only "local," structure-specific reductions rather than blocking reduction altogether, since a multiply realized property is arguably just a disjunction of its realizers and so cannot figure in genuine causal-explanatory laws. Could you compare these two uses of the multiple realizability argument and assess whether Kim's local-reduction strategy succeeds in undermining the functionalist case for the autonomy of psychology? Justify your answer.
