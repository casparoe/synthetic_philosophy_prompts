I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and something from a recent piece has stayed with me. I moderated an in-person panel on a disputed psychology finding, where the exchange was careful: people conceded points and visibly changed their minds. When I wrote the panel up afterward, the online reaction to that same material was different in kind—more moralizing, more confident, and oddly impersonal, as if no one in particular were answerable for it, even though some of the sharpest comments came from people who had been in the room. Engaging with it only intensified things, and within days the whole episode dissolved, its energy simply reattaching itself to an unrelated controversy. It's tempting to conclude that people just behave worse online, but I suspect something more structural happens to an opinion once it passes through publication and a comment thread. Is there a real pattern here, and does it change how I should think about responding to reactions to my own work?
