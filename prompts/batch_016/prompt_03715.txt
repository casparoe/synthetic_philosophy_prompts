Okay, this one's a bit different from what I usually ask. I spent 35 years as a physicist before retiring, and lately I've fallen into a philosophy kick -- mostly economics and epistemology stuff, which I never touched professionally.

At my niece's engagement party last month, her fiancé (does some day-trading on the side) and my old grad-school friend Marek (a quant at some fund now) got into a whole argument about whether its even rational to trade on your own analysis. Marek's opening move: say you think a stock's underpriced and go to buy it -- ask yourself why the person selling it to you, who you have to assume is just as rational as you and sees the same public info, is willing to take the other side at all. If everyone's rational and the only reason anyone ever trades is private information, nobody should agree to the trade, because your wanting to buy is itself a signal the seller should read as a warning and back out. He called this "the no-trade theorem," though I might be butchering the name.

The fiancé said that's obviously wrong since people trade constantly, so whatever theorem Marek's citing must just not describe real markets. Marek agreed real markets don't seize up like that, but said its ONLY because most volume has nothing to do with private information -- pension funds rebalancing, people needing cash -- and his own stock-picking only works by trading against people like that. The fiancé shot back that this makes Marek's whole "smart money" career sound like picking off retirees' 401k trades, which is an ugly thing to be smug about. Marek just shrugged, said basically yes, and added that the scarier part is the fiancé has no way of knowing, on any given trade of his own, whether he's the smart money or just the sucker supplying liquidity for someone smarter.

Nobody at the party could referee this, and neither can I. Could you go move by move through the exchange and tell me, for each point, whether it's basically right, half-right, or off base, and why -- and also tell me whether this "no-trade theorem" thing is an actual result or Marek was just dressing up a hunch?
