I am an elected member of my country's national legislature. During debate on a bill to legalize medically assisted dying for terminally ill patients, I explained that I oppose it because my faith teaches that only God has the authority to end a life, which is my genuine reason for voting no. A colleague later told me that invoking religious belief this way was inappropriate for a vote that will bind constituents of many different religions and none, and suggested I rely instead on reasons such as the risk of coercing vulnerable patients. I do not wish to misrepresent my actual motivation, yet I recognize some force in their objection. Were they right that this kind of reasoning is out of place in an official vote, and if so, does that mean my real reasons simply do not count?
