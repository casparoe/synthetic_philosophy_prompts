I've been meditating on and off for like 6 years now, and somewhere along the way I got really into the anatta / "no-self" idea - not just as a vibe but the actual argument, like how "you" are supposedly just the five aggregates (body, feelings, perceptions, mental formations, and consciousness) chugging along together with nothing extra running the show behind them. Honestly it helped a lot with my anxiety, cause if there's no fixed "me" then theres less of a "me" for bad stuff to happen to, if that makes sense.

Recently though a friend who actually reads philosophy (I never went to uni for any of this, I just read suttas and dharma talks on my own) told me that a guy named Derek Parfit landed on a pretty similar "no fixed self, just a bundle of psychological connections" view from a totally different angle, and that he used it to argue that since your connection to your 80-year-old self is way weaker than your connection to yourself tomorrow, it might not even be irrational to care less about that future person and just live it up now. That freaked me out a bit since I literally just opened a retirement account and started forcing myself to exercise "for future me." It also made me remember that somewhere in the old Buddhist texts there's a king asking a monk something like "if there's no self, who actually inherits the karma from what I do today," and the monk answers with some kind of flame-passed-between-lamps thing, but I honestly can't remember how that's supposed to settle it one way or the other.

So here's what I'm actually asking: what are the most important implications of the no-self view (you can pull from the Buddhist side, the Parfit side, or both) for how much weight I should rationally put on my own future well-being? Does taking anatta seriously actually give me an excuse to slack off on stuff like saving money and staying healthy, or is there a good reason within that same framework for why it wouldn't? I'd rather not get the standard "just stay present, don't worry about it" answer - I want to know if there's a real tension here and what actually resolves it.
