So this has been bugging me for a while: I've spent way too much time in old Overcoming Bias and LessWrong comment sections, and I keep noticing that people invoke Aumann's agreement theorem in two totally opposite ways. Camp one treats it as almost a moral rule -- like, if you and someone you consider a genuine epistemic peer have talked a disagreement through and you're still not converged, that itself is evidence somebody (maybe you) is being irrational. Camp two basically shrugs the theorem off as a cute math result with zero practical bite, since real humans never actually satisfy its preconditions (common priors, common knowledge of each others exact posteriors, etc). I don't think I've ever actually seen these two camps go toe to toe on the object level rather than just talking past each other, so I want you to stage that fight properly.

Write a dialogue between two old friends from a rationalist meetup, Priya and Tomasz, who've had a running disagreement for months over some forecasting-type question they keep making small side bets on (pick whatever's plausible, doesn't matter). At some point Priya invokes Aumann head-on -- something like "we're both honest, we both think the other one's smart, we've hashed this out for hours, by Aumann we should've converged on a number by now, so what's actually going on?" -- and I want Tomasz to come back with real substance rather than a vague "well humans aren't perfect Bayesians." Get him into the actual weeds: whether differing priors are really doing the work Priya thinks they are, whether "common knowledge" of somebody's exact posterior is even the sort of thing two people talking in a bar can achieve, the gap between the original static theorem and the later result about what happens if you just keep repeating your updated numbers back and forth, and any legitimate reasons a person might have for not fully deferring even to someone they respect. Priya shouldn't fold, though -- I vaguely remember Robin Hanson having some move about how having a different prior from someone else isn't automatically a free pass, since the difference itself calls for an explanation -- so give her real comebacks along those lines instead of letting Tomasz just win by attrition.

Keep the two of them evenly matched the whole way through: I want genuine back-and-forth where each line responds to the specific thing the other person just said, not two prepared monologues stapled together, and please don't have it resolve cleanly with one side clearly right, since I genuinely don't know which of them I agree with.
