ok so i've read through the evidential cooperation in large worlds stuff a bunch of times now (back when oesterheld first wrote it up it was still called MSR) and there's a step in the argument that never quite clicks for me. the selling point is supposed to be that it works even when none of the correlated agents are modeling each other or even aware of each other's existance -- unlike the usual acausal trade / twin prisoner's dilemma setup where you're basically assuming everyone's simulating everyone else. but if nobody's thinking about anybody in particular, why would my choice to cooperate be any evidence about some random alien civilization's choice, rather than just uncorrelated noise? "we're both rational" clearly isn't enough on its own, since rational agents can land on totally different answers. so what's actually generating the correlation here, and how would you even estimate how strong it is for agents you know essentially nothing about?
