A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about an unresolved tension in Vienna Circle politics. Carnap and Neurath both held that unconditional value judgments are cognitively meaningless, yet both helped write the Circle's 1929 manifesto, which claims an "inner link" between their scientific world-conception and movements to reorganize economic and social life along planned, rational lines. My friend argued that Neurath's own way of relieving this tension — replacing monetary calculation with "calculation in kind," so that planners lay out whole rival plans in rich, incommensurable detail for citizens to choose among — quietly smuggles value judgments back onto the scientific side of the ledger. I argued instead that Carnap's thinner picture, on which science can only spell out conditional means-ends relations, cannot explain why their philosophy should have any special affinity with socialist planning rather than with any politics whatsoever.

Write a dialogue in which Carnap and Neurath try to settle, using only their own actual doctrines, how much of a planned economy's design science can legitimately determine and how much must be left to non-cognitive decision.
