ok so for AP Gov we have this extra credit thing where you find a "primary-ish source" about democracy and write a reflection on it, and i went down a total rabbit hole and found this super obscure 1943 essay by Simone Weil called "On the Abolition of All Political Parties." She apparently wrote it in London while working for de Gaulle's Free French, like a few months before she died, and her whole argument is that literally EVERY political party (not just the corrupt or extremist ones) is totalitarian by nature, because of three things all parties supposedly do: they manufacture collective emotion, they pressure individual members to fall in line instead of using their own judgment, and their real bottom-line goal is just unlimited growth for its own sake. Her proposed fix isn't "reform the parties" or "add more small parties so no one dominates" — its literally: get rid of the whole concept of political parties.

Here's the thing that's bugging me though. We're also reading chunks of Rousseau's Social Contract in AP Euro right now (the chapter on whether the general will can be wrong, with the Lycurgus/Sparta example), and it seems pretty obvious that Weil is basically running with his idea that "partial associations" corrupt the general will. But Rousseau actually gives himself two different escape routes for that same problem: either you have zero partial associations/factions at all (his Lycurgus ideal), or, if that's not realistic, you make sure there are a ton of small, roughly equal ones so their differences cancel out (he credits that one to Solon and some Roman lawgivers). Weil doesn't clearly take either option — she wants zero political parties specifically, but from what I read she was totally fine with people forming other kinds of small groups, like discussion circles around little intellectual magazines, which seems like its own kind of "partial association" by Rousseau's own logic.

So could you actually write a real comparison of these two views instead of just summarizing them next to each other? Specifically: does Weil's "abolish parties" stance make her MORE radical than Rousseau (since she's rejecting his "many small equal factions" compromise), or actually LESS radical (since she's only targeting parties specifically, not partial associations in general)? And is that a real inconsistency in her position, or is there an actual good reason parties are different in kind from other group activities that would justify singling them out? Please don't just land on "it's complicated both ways" — commit to a specific reading of how her view relates to his and defend it.
