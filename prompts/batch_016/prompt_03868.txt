Ok so I've had this idea rattling around for a possible blog post and want a sanity check before I make a fool of myself. Been re-reading the old MIRI tiling agents paper (Yudkowsky & Herreshoff, the one about the Löbian Obstacle) and it lays out two failure modes for an agent trying to trust it's future self without being able to predict exactly what that future self will do (what they call Vingean reflection). First there's the Löbian obstacle itself: an agent that only trusts proofs can never prove that its future self, running the same proof system, will only take "safe" actions, so it's stuck before it even starts. Second there's the procrastination paradox: an agent that just trusts its future self unconditionally ends up perfectly happy to defer the goal forever, since future-it will surely handle it.

My pet theory, which I genuinely can't tell is insightful or just a pun on "trust," is that these two failure modes are basically a clean formal model of two very recognizable human patterns: anxious over-planners who can never pull the trigger because they can't verify in advance that future-them will execute right (that's the Lobian one), and garden-variety procrastinators who keep pushing things to tomorrow-me (procrastination paradox, obviously). I don't think it's a coincidence, since both cases are about an agent embedded in time that can't fully simulate the version of itself it's trying to coordinate with.

Does this analogy actually hold up, or am I just galaxy-braining a coincidence in terminology? Where's the weakest link if I tried to write this up properly? And is there already better work out there, rationalist-adjacent or academic, making this same connection that I should just read instead?
