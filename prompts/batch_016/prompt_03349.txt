This is something from my own job that's been bugging me (I mainly cover science and ideas stuff). Editors always drill into you that every claim in a piece needs a source. But fact-checking a recent story about a nutrition study, I kept asking 'ok, but how does THIS source actually know that,' and every answer just pointed to another source, which had its own sources behind it — a couple times two sources even ended up backing each other up in a circle. Made me wonder if a 'well-sourced claim' is kind of an illusion: theres no real bottom to it, just an endless chain, or a loop we pretend isn't one, or we stop at some source for no principled reason and call it good. Is there an actual way out of that, or do journalists (and everyone else, tbh) just have to live with picking an arbitrary stopping point?
