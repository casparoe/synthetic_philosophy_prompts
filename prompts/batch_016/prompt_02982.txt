Retired chemist here, got into philosophy books this past year and it's dredged up an old memory I can't shake. For my first big paper back in the 90s, about 4 or 5 out of 60-odd data points came out way off from what the theory predicted, and I quietly left them out of the writeup, chalking it up to "probable contamination" even though I'd never actually nailed down a cause — mostly I just didn't want to loose the clean result. Nobody ever blinked at it and the paper did fine. I've told myself for thirty years it was just an ordinary judgment call every researcher makes, but it still nags at me, and I don't think a research-ethics rulebook is really the point here. I never technically lied about anything I did report, so is there some actual principle that tells me whether what I did was wrong, or am I just an old man manufacturing guilt out of nothing?
