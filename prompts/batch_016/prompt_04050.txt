Like, the Fregean move where concepts are "unsaturated" so a predicate and an object just click together without needing some further relation to glue them — that's supposed to stop the regress before it even gets started, because the concept was never a free-standing third thing that needed connecting in the first place. But then there's the whole "concept horse" mess, where the second you try to say what an unsaturated concept even is, the phrase you use to refer to it turns it into an object, and the theory seems to eat its own tail. And then on the other side you've got people like Armstrong who just bite the bullet on the regress and say fine, it's there, but its benign, like the truth-regress (p, it's true that p, it's true that it's true that p...) — states of affairs come first and are basic, particulars and universals are just abstracted out of them after the fact, so don't ask what glues them together because that question already has the order of explanation backwards. Both of these answers feel unsatisfying to me in a similar way and I honestly can't tell anymore if that's because they're secretly the same move in different clothes or genuinely different strategies.

I want real back-and-forth, not each side just restating themselves: at some point the unsaturation defender needs to actually deal with the concept-horse worry instead of dodging it, and the benign-regress defender needs to answer the charge that "don't worry, it's benign" is a refusal to explain rather than an explanation. Bonus if by the end they manage to pin down whether they're disagreeing about the metaphysics itself or about what should even count as an explanation here — but don't force a tidy resolution if there genuinely isn't one.
