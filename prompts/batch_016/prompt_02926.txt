Back in 2014 Aaronson wrote a post showing IIT implies a big enough grid of XOR gates (or his Vandermonde-matrix decoder example) has way higher Φ than a human brain -- his "Cheetos test," basically: if your theory says a DVD player is more conscious than you, that's a reductio. Tononi's reply (literally titled "why Scott should stare at a blank wall and reconsider") bit the bullet all the way, with four separate arguments for why the grid really is conscious: (1) common sense has been wrong before (geocentrism etc), so "counterintuitive" isn't automatically disqualifying, (2) it just follows from his phenomenological axioms, (3) it's backed up empirically -- IIT correctly predicts the cerebellum barely contributes to consciousness despite having ~4x the cortex's neuron count, because it lacks the lateral connectivity that drives up Φ, and (4) if you actually introspect while staring at a blank wall, "empty" 2D space turns out way richer than it seems, which is evidence 2D structure itself carries that richness.

Aaronson clearly thinks he wins this one, and honestly so do i. so convince me otherwise: pick whichever ONE of the four arguments seems least bad to you and actually defend it for real, strong enough to survive Aaronson's specific comebacks (he calls the cerebellum move a double standard, and asks why the phenomenology argument doesn't apply just as well to a 1D rope). don't just summarize both sides, commit to a position and argue it like you actually believe it.
