So I've been down a bit of a Bayesian epistemology rabbit hole after re-reading Abram Demski's old Radical Probabilism sequence on LW (the one where he talks about how Bayes' rule is really just a constraint relating your conditional probabilities, and doesn't by itself tell you what update rule to use unless you already know how you'd react to evidence — the "rigidity" thing). That sent me back to Richard Jeffrey's actual book, and his classic example of sizing up a cloth by candlelight: you can't become certain its green, but your confidence shifts around between green/blue/violet anyway, without ever hitting probability 1 on anything. Jeffrey uses that case to motivate "probability kinematics" (aka Jeffrey conditionalization) as a genuine generalization of ordinary conditionalizing, for evidence that doesn't come packaged as some proposition you learn for certain.

I brought this up with a friend of mine who's a much more old-school Bayesian than me, and he wasn't buying that it's doing any real philosophical work — his line is that you can always push the uncertainty back a level and say the agent really did conditionalize, just on some maximally specific proposition about their visual experience (whatever the candlelit retina/brain state amounts to), and that Jeffrey's rule is at best a convenient shortcut for that, not a substantively different picture of rational belief change. I don't think that's quite right but I had a hard time putting my finger on the problem with it, especially since he can just keep making the hypothetical experience-proposition more fine-grained whenever I object to it.

Could you write me a dialogue between the two of them — call them the Strict Conditionalizer and the Jeffrey Conditionalizer — arguing this out with reference to the candlelight case, where they're both genuinely trying to figure out whether this is a substantive disagreement about the structure of rational belief or just a notational dispute? I'd like the Strict Conditionalizer to get pushed on what that hypothetical experience-proposition actually is and whether we have any real epistemic access to it, and I'd like the Jeffrey Conditionalizer to have to grapple with the fact that Jeffrey updates aren't commutative in general (the order in which you update can change where you end up), since that seems like a genuine cost of giving up strict conditionalization. Neither one should just be a mouthpiece who loses — I want them both bringing their A-game.
