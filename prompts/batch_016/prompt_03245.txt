Random question about Bayesian stuff that's been bugging me. I'm building a little app on the side that tracks predictions (log a claim, log a confidence %, see how it pans out), and I fell down a rabbit hole reading about Dutch book arguments. From what I can tell, if your confidence levels don't follow the probability axioms, someone can construct a set of bets you'd each accept individually but that guarantee you lose money no matter what happens - basically a free exploit. If your numbers do follow the axioms, no such exploit exists. So isn't the whole trick to having rational beliefs just making your probabilities internally consistent (sum to 1, conditionals line up, etc)? Once that exploit is closed, haven't you essentially guaranteed your beliefs are accurate and well calibrated too?
