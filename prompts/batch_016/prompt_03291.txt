So I had this long argument with my friend Theo last night about free will that kind of fizzled out unresolved. He brought up a thought experiment he said was from a philosopher named Pereboom (not sure I've got that right) - basically you compare a guy whose brain gets directly rewired by a team of neuroscientists so he ends up wanting to kill someone, to a totally ordinary guy who ends up with those exact same murderous desires just from his own upbringing and circumstances, under plain old determinism, no scientists involved at all. Theo's point was that compatibilists have no principled way to say the rewired guy isnt responsible while the ordinary guy is, since neither one chose the stuff that made them who they are either way. I feel like there's obviously some real difference between deliberately rigging someone like that and just being a normal product of your own causal history, but I couldn't actually explain why in the moment and he kind of steamrolled me. Can you build the strongest possible case that there IS a principled line compatibilists can draw between those two kinds of cases?
