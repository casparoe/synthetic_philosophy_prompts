A few months ago I read about how researchers used an automated theorem prover to formally verify that Gödel's ontological argument is logically valid, which led me to look further into the objections philosophers have raised against it. The one I have not been able to fully understand is the "modal collapse" problem: apparently, Gödel's axioms about positive properties do not merely secure God's necessary existence but also force the conclusion that every true proposition whatsoever is necessarily true, erasing the distinction between what merely happens to be true and what has to be true. From what I can tell, this is not simply a quirk of Gödel's own 1970 notes, since the same collapse shows up in Dana Scott's tidied-up version of the proof as well. I understand why this is considered an unacceptable cost, but every source I have found simply states that the collapse follows without walking through the actual steps. Could you explain, in plain language rather than modal-logic notation, how the premises actually generate that result?
