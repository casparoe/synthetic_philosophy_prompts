In Knowledge and Its Limits (2000), Timothy Williamson argues that no non-trivial condition is luminous: there is no condition such that, whenever it obtains, one is thereby in a position to know that it obtains. This anti-luminosity argument — built on a margin-for-error principle governing states that can be discriminated only inexactly, illustrated by his example of gradually feeling cold — is then redeployed against the KK principle: the thesis that knowing that p puts one in a position to know that one knows it.

Write an essay assessing whether Williamson's argument succeeds in refuting KK. Reconstruct the reductio at its core, then present and evaluate the strongest available objection to it, whether by challenging the margin-for-error/safety principle itself or its specific application to iterated knowledge. Weigh these considerations against each other, but do not end on a non-committal note: defend a specific verdict on whether the KK principle survives the challenge.
