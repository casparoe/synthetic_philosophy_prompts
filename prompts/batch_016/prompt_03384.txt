In my formal epistemology seminar this term, we've spent the last few weeks working through Bostrom's material on anthropic reasoning and self-locating belief, and I've decided to write my term paper on the disagreement between the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA). As I understand it, SSA says you should reason as if you were a random sample from the observers who actually exist within your reference class, while SIA says you should additionally weight hypotheses by how many observers they predict, since a hypothesis on which many observers exist gives you correspondingly more chances to be among them. My professor's favorite argument against SIA is the presumptuous philosopher: two cosmological theories are empirically tied except that one implies a trillion times more observers than the other, and a philosopher who accepts SIA can declare the bigger-universe theory a trillion times more probable before the physicists even run their decisive experiment, which feels like settling an empirical question from the armchair. But when we covered the classic Doomsday argument and the reference class problem, along with cases like the Incubator and the Adam-and-Eve "serpent's advice" scenario, several of us came away thinking SSA has just as many skeletons in its closet, just less flashy ones.

For the paper, I don't want to simply lay out both positions and leave it there; I want to actually defend one. Could you write an essay that weighs the strongest considerations for and against each assumption (feel free to draw on whichever of the thought experiments mentioned above are useful, or others you think are relevant), and then reaches and defends a specific verdict about which view we should adopt when reasoning about our own existence and location in the world -- SSA, SIA, some hybrid or qualified version of one of them, or neither? I'd like the essay to actually commit to an answer and defend it against the strongest objections, rather than concluding that the matter is simply unresolved.
