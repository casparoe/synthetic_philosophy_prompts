I write novels for a living (three out, currently late on a fourth), and last month a small regional press talked me into being one of five judges for their annual debut-fiction prize, mostly because it sounded like a nice break from my own manuscript. It was pretty informal: each of us ranked the four shortlisted books 1st through 4th, and the coordinator turned those rankings into points (4 for a first-place vote, 3 for second, down to 1 for last) to get an overall winner. Book A won comfortably that way. But at the Zoom debrief afterward, one of the other judges mentioned, almost as a throwaway comment, that if you just looked at head-to-head prefernces — among judges who ranked A over B versus those who ranked B over A — Book B was actually the one more of us preferred. Then, out of curiosity, someone quietly dropped the book that had obviously come in dead last (nobody was ever going to give it the prize) and redid the points tally on just the remaining three, and the winner flipped again, this time to Book C.

Nobody cheated and nobody strategically misranked anything, we all just wrote down our honest opinions, and three completely reasonable ways of adding up the exact same five ballots gave three different winners. It's such a low-stakes, silly situation, but its been gnawing at me for weeks, in that way things do when I'm supposed to be plotting a novel instead. Part of me keeps assuming there's one true, fair winner hiding in our ballots and we just used bad arithmetic to find it. But another part of me suspects that's wishful thinking, and that this kind of flip-flopping is just what happens once you've got more than a couple of options and more than one person voting, no matter how carefully you count. Which is it? And if it really is unavoidable, is there at least a least-bad method I should push for next year, or does picking any ranking system just mean smuggling in a value judgment about which kind of unfairness I'm more willing to live with?
