okay so i've been going down an effective altruism rabbit hole for like two months now (blame the 80,000 hours podcast) and i just learned about "moral uncertainty" - this idea that since nobody can actually be sure which ethical theory is correct, you shouldn't just pick utilitarianism or kantianism and go all in on it, you're supposed to spread your credence across a few theories and let that shape what you actually do. the version that really got stuck in my head is the "moral parliament" thing (i think Bostrom started this?) where each theory you have some credence in gets seats proportional to that credence, and then they basically negotiate and vote on what you should do, kind of like a coalition government except the parties are utilitarianism and Kantian ethics instead of, like, actual political parties.
