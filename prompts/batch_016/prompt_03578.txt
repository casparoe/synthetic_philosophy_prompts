For most of my working life I was an experimental physicist, measuring radioactive decay rates and reporting confidence intervals without ever stopping to ask what "probability" truly refers to. Since retiring I have been reading through the philosophy of probability, and I find that every interpretation I try founders on the same difficulty: the single, non-repeatable case.

Von Mises's frequentism defines probability only as a limiting frequency within a collective, so when I ask for the probability that one particular atom decays in the next minute — rather than the decay rate over some large ensemble — I run straight into the reference class problem: that atom belongs at once to indefinitely many reference classes (this isotope, this sample, this run of the experiment), each of which might ground a different frequency, with nothing in the theory itself to say which is relevant. Popper introduced propensities largely to rescue single-case talk of exactly this kind, tying probability to the generating set-up itself — paradigmatically, a quantum-mechanical preparation — rather than to a sequence of trials. But Humphreys showed that propensities, being irreducibly causal and time-directed, cannot in general be inverted by Bayes' theorem the way any genuine probability must be, which leaves it unclear that propensities can satisfy the probability calculus at all. A thoroughgoing subjectivist has no trouble assigning a credence to the single case, but then owes some account — along the lines of Lewis's Principal Principle — of why that credence should answer to an objective chance rather than merely expressing personal opinion, on pain of collapsing the very distinction between "the" probability of an event and someone's guess about it.

Write an essay that works through this three-way bind and defends a definite verdict: is there an interpretation of probability that can coherently underwrite talk about the probability of a single, unrepeated event, or should we conclude that such talk is simply confused? Commit to an answer rather than concluding that each interpretation merely "has something to recommend it."
