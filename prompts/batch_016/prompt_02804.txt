Spent about 35 years as a field biologist before retiring and getting hooked on philosophy of science (better late then never). Been stuck lately on the Gould & Lewontin spandrels paper from '79, the one going after the "adaptationist programme" and "just-so stories" using the arches in the San Marco basilica as the analogy. Funny thing is, toward the end they actually claim Darwin himself would've been on their side -- they cite his own bits on correlation of growth and insist he was genuinely pluralist about explaining nature's complexity, more than the strict adaptationists give him credit for. So say you could actually sit Darwin down in 1979 and walk him through the paper -- which camp do you think hed truly land in, the neo-Darwinian adaptationists or Gould and Lewontin's pluralists? Try to ground your answer in what he actually wrote rather than just asserting it, since I've seen smart people confidently claim both.
