quick one for a piece i'm noodling on about what 'function' even means in biology. phil of bio has this split between etiological accounts (a trait's function is whatever it was historically selected for) and Cummins-style causal-role accounts (function = whatever the trait currently contributes to some capacity of the system, no history needed). the case that keeps bugging me is Swampman -- a freak lightning strike that assembles a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a person out of swamp muck, zero evolutionary history. on the etiological view his heart has no function at all, since nothing selected it for anything, even though its pumping blood just fine, same as yours or mine. a lot of the etiological replies i've read just bite that bullet -- fine, technically no 'proper function,' our intuitions are tracking something else. can you argue the etiological side WITHOUT taking that easy way out? i want the strongest real case that etiological accounts aren't actually embarrassed by Swampman, not just a shrug about intuitions being unreliable.
