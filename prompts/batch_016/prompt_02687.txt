I'm a cognitive science PhD student working on Bayesian models of belief revision, and I've recently been reading the formal epistemology literature on updating under uncertainty for background. As I understand it, Richard Jeffrey's probability kinematics was developed precisely to handle situations in which an agent's evidence fails to confer certainty on any single proposition, by taking a directly given, less-than-certain probability assignment over some partition as the input to updating, rather than requiring that P(E) = 1 for some learned proposition E. Given that this is exactly the problem Jeffrey conditionalization was designed to solve, I'm struggling to see why van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin case is still treated as an open puzzle in the literature rather than as a routine application of the rule. There, an agent learns only that, conditional on being in Red territory, she is three times more likely to be in the Headquarters area than in the Second Company area -- isn't this just a Jeffrey shift on the four-cell partition {Red HQ, Red Second, Blue HQ, Blue Second}? I would be grateful if you could help me see what I'm missing here.
