Within the rationalist blogosphere it's practically received wisdom that Solomonoff induction dissolves Goodman's new riddle of induction: 'grue' loses out to 'green' simply because encoding it requires a costly reference to some particular time t, so a complexity-weighted prior favors green automatically and non-arbitrarily. I've become skeptical that this really settles anything, since Kolmogorov complexity is only defined relative to a chosen universal Turing machine, and the invariance theorem guarantees only that two machines' complexity assignments differ by some constant depending on the pair — a constant that can be made arbitrarily large by choosing a suitably idiosyncratic machine, including one that makes 'grue' primitive and 'green' the costly concept. Please argue that Solomonoff induction doesn't actually solve the new riddle of induction but merely relocates it into an unprincipled choice of reference machine, and that any principled way of favoring one machine as more 'natural' than another would have to smuggle back in something like Goodman's own appeal to entrenched predicates.
