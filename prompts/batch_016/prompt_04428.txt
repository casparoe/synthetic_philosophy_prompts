Went to a concert with a friend last night and afterward we ended up in a two hour argument about what music even is. I was defending Schopenhauer's take that instrumental music is totally different from the other arts — painting, poetry, sculpture etc are all just copying the Platonic Ideas, but music skips the Ideas entirely and gives us a direct copy of the Will itself, which is supposedly why it can move us without ever depicting anything in particular. My friend thinks thats mystical overreach and that Hanslick basically settled the question back in the 1850s: music is nothing more than "tonally moving forms," self sufficient sound patterns, and any emotion we hear "in" the music is just us projecting onto it. Neither of us budged. Could you write a short dialogue between a committed Schopenhauerian and a committed Hanslickian actually hashing this out, where each one has to respond to the other's strongest point instead of just restating their own view?
