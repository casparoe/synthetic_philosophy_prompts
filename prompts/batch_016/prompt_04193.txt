After a career in experimental gravitational physics (including some hardware work for the Gravity Probe B frame-dragging mission), I've spent my retirement working through the philosophical literature on space and motion, and I've grown impatient with treatments that stop at Newton's bucket and Leibniz's shift argument. What interests me now is Julian Barbour's 'best matching' program: a relational mechanics that compares successive configurations of the whole universe without presupposing any background frame, and which reproduces the bucket's spinning water only when the universe's total angular momentum is zero. Its descendant, shape dynamics, recovers general relativity itself by trading the four-dimensional spacetime manifold for an evolving conformal geometry plus a single global 'York time.' It strikes me that this relationism buys its victory at the price of a new holism, since best matching has to solve a constraint over the entire instantaneous universe just to say how any one part of it is moving — which sounds less like eliminating absolute structure than relocating it.

Write me a dialogue between a substantivalist and a committed relationist in the Barbour tradition that argues out exactly this trade-off. The relationist should have to defend best matching's holism and its zero-angular-momentum assumption against the charge that it smuggles absolute structure back in; the substantivalist should have to say what is left of 'absolute space' once their own commitments are pared down to the bare metric field. Have them genuinely engage each other's arguments rather than talk past one another, and don't let either side come away with an easy win.
