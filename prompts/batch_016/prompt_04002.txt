A friend and I spent an evening arguing about Quine's "gavagai" scenario and never resolved one point at the center of it. She maintained that the indeterminacy of translation is really just underdetermination of theory by evidence applied to the special case of language: rival translation manuals are simply empirically equivalent theories, no different in kind from any two physical theories compatible with all possible evidence. I insisted that Quine meant indeterminacy of translation to be a stronger and stranger thesis than ordinary underdetermination, but when pressed I couldn't reconstruct where exactly the extra strangeness was supposed to come from, or whether it holds up. Could you compare the two ideas carefully: underdetermination of theory by evidence, and indeterminacy of translation? In particular, is indeterminacy of translation simply a special case of underdetermination, or does it rest on some further, independent claim -- and if so, what exactly is that claim, and is it defensible?
