So I had this whole argument with my friend last night that went way longer than it shouldve. We were talking about the Turing test (she works in ML) and she brought up that Turing, in the actual 1950 paper ("Computing Machinery and Intelligence"), raises ESP as one of several objections to his own imitation game -- and takes it pretty seriously: he says the statistical evidence for telepathy at the time was basically overwhelming, worries a psychic contestant could just read the interrogator's mind (like correctly naming a card they're thinking of) and unfairly beat the machine, and "fixes" this by sticking everyone in a "telepathy-proof room." I said who cares, ESP isn't real, so it's a harmless historical curiosity that doesn't touch whether the imitation game is actually a good test of thinking. She said I was missing the point -- the fact that he has to answer a threat like this by sealing off yet another possible information channel shows the test was never measuring "thinking," only "indistinguishability given whatever channels we've remembered to block so far," and that's a real structural problem with defining thought behaviorally, not just a footnote. I said that's just what good experimental design always looks like, you patch confounds as you find them and that doesn't discredit the method. We went back and forth for ages and neither of us budged.

Write a dialogue between two philosophers of mind who actually dig into this properly -- one pressing my friend's case that the telepathy-proof room exposes something ad hoc in Turing-style operationalism about thought, the other defending my case that it's just ordinary experimental control and doesn't undermine the test at all.
