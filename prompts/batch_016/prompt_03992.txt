As a PhD student in philosophy of biology, I've grown dissatisfied with popular treatments of the units-of-selection debate that stop at 'selfish gene' slogans. Write a dialogue between a committed gene selectionist and a multi-level selection theorist, equally matched in sophistication, who disagree over a concrete case: an alarm call that lowers the caller's own survival while raising the fitness of nearby kin. The multi-level theorist argues that partitioning the Price equation into within-group and between-group covariance components shows group selection is doing genuine explanatory work; the gene selectionist responds that this partition merely redescribes an inclusive-fitness account, yielding identical predicted allele frequencies either way. The two should engage directly with each other's formal argument rather than simply restating their preferred vocabulary, and the dialogue should end with them trying to pin down whether any substantive (as opposed to merely verbal) disagreement survives the formal reconciliation.
