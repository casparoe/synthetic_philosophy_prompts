I'm a philosophy major, and this term I'm taking a course on formal epistemology. This week's unit was on Bayesian conditionalization: you hold some credence in a hypothesis, you learn a piece of evidence with certainty, and you update by conditionalizing on it. My professor kept stressing that the whole framework assumes the evidence itself becomes known for sure. That struck me as a strong assumption at the time, and it's been nagging at me ever since something happened this weekend that doesn't seem to fit the mold at all.

At a party on Saturday, I told my friend Priya that I wouldn't be able to make her birthday dinner next week. She said, "Oh, that's totally fine, no worries," before someone pulled her into another conversation. In the moment, I registered her tone as maybe a little flat rather than genuinely breezy, but the music was loud, I'd had a drink, and the whole exchange lasted only a few seconds, so I'm honestly not confident about what I actually perceived. If I had to put a number on it, I'd say I'm now about 70 percent confident her tone was flat and 30 percent confident it was as breezy as the words themselves suggest. Before Saturday, I would have said there was only about a 15 percent chance Priya was annoyed with me about anything (we'd had a minor disagreement online the week before that never quite got resolved). Thinking it through further: if I were sure her tone had actually been flat, I'd put the odds she's annoyed at something like 75 percent; if I were sure it had been genuinely breezy, I'd still leave it at around 10 percent, since people say "no worries" for all kinds of reasons. (I recognize it's a little ridiculous to assign made-up numbers to a gut feeling about a friend, but I've been buried in problem sets all week and apparently can't turn that habit off.)

Here's what I can't work out: I never actually became certain that her tone was flat; I just have a shaky, partial impression that I'm not even sure I trust. Given that, how should I revise my belief that Priya is annoyed with me? Would it be a mistake to simply pick whichever reading of her tone seems more likely and conditionalize on that, as though I actually knew it for certain? And is there a more principled way of handling a case like this, where what I'm unsure about is the evidence itself, rather than the thing the evidence would normally bear on?
