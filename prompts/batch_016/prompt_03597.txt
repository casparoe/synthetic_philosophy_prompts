As a longtime reader of the rationalist blogosphere, I'm used to free-will threads eventually washing up on Frankfurt-style cases, but a dispute in my in-person reading group last week got more technical than the usual online rehash, and I'd like an outside adjudication.

We'd been discussing the classic Jones/Black setup: Jones decides on his own to do something, but Black, a neuroscientist monitoring his brain, would have intervened to force that very decision had Jones shown any sign of deciding otherwise. Since Black never actually has to act, Frankfurt concludes that Jones is responsible even though he couldn't have done otherwise -- refuting the Principle of Alternative Possibilities (PAP).

Round 1: Nadia said the case works as advertised -- whatever grounds Jones's responsibility is plainly untouched by the mere presence of Black's contingency plan, so responsibility and having open alternatives must come apart.

Round 2: Marcus objected that an alternative survives even here: Jones could have exhibited the prior neural sign that would have triggered Black's intervention, and this "flicker of freedom" is itself an alternative possibility, however thin -- so PAP's defender need only relocate to it rather than concede.

Round 3: Nadia replied that the flicker is the wrong currency: an alternative that Jones has no voluntary control over exhibiting can't be what makes the difference between his being responsible and not, so invoking it doesn't rescue PAP -- it just finds some difference between the scenarios and stipulates that it's morally significant.

Round 4: Marcus came back with what he called the dilemma defense: either the prior sign is causally sufficient for Jones's decision, in which case the story has quietly smuggled in determinism and begs the question against a libertarian defender of PAP, or the sign isn't sufficient, in which case it could occur without the predicted decision following, so Jones's decision was genuinely avoidable after all -- either way, the case can't do the work it needs to for anyone who isn't already a compatibilist.

Neither conceded. Could you go through the four rounds and say who has the better of it at each one, rather than just picking an overall winner, and then tell me whether there's a real crux underneath all this that neither of them has actually surfaced?
