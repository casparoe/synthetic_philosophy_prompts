I'm a high schooler who has spent a lot of this past year reading decision-theory posts on AI safety, and I keep getting stuck on the "surrogate goal" idea as a defense against acausal blackmail. As I understand it, an agent that expects to be threatened by another agent that can predict or simulate it without any direct causal contact deliberately commits to caring about some cheap, otherwise meaningless goal exactly as much as it cares about its real values, so that a rational threatener targets this decoy instead of anything the agent actually values. What I can't see is why this doesn't just relocate the vulnerability rather than remove it: if adopting the surrogate goal is itself a strategic move made because a threat is expected, why couldn't a sufficiently sophisticated threatener simply threaten the agent's real values unless it drops the surrogate-goal trick, so that its commitment to defending the decoy becomes the new pressure point? Could you walk me through how defenders of the surrogate-goal proposal try to block this kind of regress, and tell me whether you think their response actually succeeds?
