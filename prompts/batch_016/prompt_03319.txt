I have been reporting on free will and divine providence, and Molinism is the piece of the puzzle I cannot quite get past. As I understand Luis de Molina's account, God possesses "middle knowledge" of exactly what every possible free creature would do in every possible circumstance, a form of knowledge that is fixed logically prior to God's decision to create anything at all. But if the truth about how I would act in any circumstance I might ever face was already settled before I existed, then God's choice of which world to actualize seems to lock in my entire biography just as completely as Calvinist predestination does. What, then, is the real philosophical difference between the two positions, and why do Molinists maintain that middle knowledge rescues a freedom that predestination is supposed to destroy?
