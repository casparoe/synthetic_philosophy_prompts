ok so I've been kind of obsessed this summer with this fight between "adaptationists" and the "spandrels" people in evolutionary biology, and I honestly can't tell who's right. not a scientist or anything, just been reading a bunch of Gould essays and going down evo-psych youtube holes.

as I understand it: Gould and Lewontin's complaint (the "spandrels" name comes from those leftover triangle shapes you get for free when you put a dome on rounded arches, that someone later painted mosaics onto) is that biologists — evo psych folks especially — are way too quick to assume every trait exists because natural selection built it for some specific job, then just cook up a plausible sounding "just-so story" for why its adaptive. they even had a name for the extreme version, "Panglossian," like Dr Pangloss from Candide who thinks everything's for the best. Their point is a ton of traits might just be side effects of how the organism happens to be built, or a byproduct of selection acting on some totally different trait, that maybe gets co-opted for a new use later but wasnt "designed" for it in the first place.

then the other camp (Dawkins, Dennett, that whole crowd) basically says G&L are attacking a strawman that no serious adaptationist actually believes, and that treating "eh, maybe its just a spandrel" as always on the table is a get-out-of-jail-free card that can kill off perfectly good research questions before they even get asked.
