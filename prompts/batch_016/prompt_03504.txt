I'm currently enrolled in a graduate seminar on the philosophy of mind and the early history of artificial intelligence, and this week's assignment was to read Alan Turing's 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" in its entirety, rather than the heavily excerpted version that circulates in most undergraduate anthologies (which usually stops after the description of the imitation game itself). I was genuinely unprepared for section 6, where Turing works through nine objections to his own position, the last of which is "The Argument from Extra-Sensory Perception." Rather than dismissing ESP out of hand, Turing treats it as a serious empirical threat to the test: he remarks that the statistical evidence for telepathy seemed "overwhelming" to him, sketches a scenario in which a telepathic human contestant could out-guess a machine at identifying the suits of playing cards, and proposes resolving the whole difficulty by putting the human and machine competitors into a "telepathy-proof room." He then adds the further wrinkle that if the machine's answers depend on an internal random-number generator, that generator itself might be susceptible to the interrogator's psychokinetic powers, which, if anything, muddies the picture further rather than resolving it.

I've been assigned to open Friday's discussion, and given how bewildered (and, frankly, amused) my cohort was by this section, I'd like to start with a joke rather than diving straight into exegesis. Could you write me one short joke that turns specifically on this part of Turing's argument — the telepathy-proof room, the card-guessing statistics, or the psychokinetic random-number generator — rather than a generic "humans versus robots" joke? For it to actually land with people who've read the section closely, the humor needs to depend on the real structure of Turing's reasoning (e.g., that he treats ESP as an empirical dispute to be handled with experimental controls rather than rejecting it as obviously false), not just be a throwaway line about ghosts or psychics.
