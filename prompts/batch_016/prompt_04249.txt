Been rereading Leibniz's mill passage from the Monadology and realized it's basically the same move Scott Aaronson made against Tononi's Integrated Information Theory. Leibniz says: blow up a machine that supposedly thinks to the size of a mill, walk around inside it, and all youll ever find is parts pushing on parts, never a perception. Aaronson's version is that IIT's math lets a big boring network of XOR gates wired up as an expander graph score a higher phi (and thus be "more conscious") than an actual human brain, and Tononi just bit the bullet and agreed that it would be. Where it gets complicated is that Leibniz wasn't just some generic anti-materialist on team Aaronson here, he was a committed panpsychist, monads all the way down, even a rock has some dim confused perception going on. So would he actually side with Aaronson that the expander graph result shows IIT is broken, or would he say IIT stumbled onto a genuinely Leibnizian conclusion (matter is shot through with faint perception) using exactly the kind of mechanical explanation he'd already shown was impossible? Work through how he'd actually reason it out, not just which side he lands on.
