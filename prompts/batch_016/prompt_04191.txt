At a wine tasting, two friends of mine disagreed about a bottle neither had yet tried. (1) Marcus called it 'excellent' on the strength of a critic's 96-point score, arguing that we routinely trust pure testimony in every other domain -- a country's population, a chemical's boiling point -- so refusing the same trust to a critic's palate is an arbitrary double standard. (2) Elena objected that aesthetic verdicts differ: a wine's being excellent partly consists in how it strikes a competent taster, so one cannot believe that it is excellent, in the relevant sense, without undergoing something like that encounter oneself. (3) Marcus replied that the critic's score is itself the product of a competent palate's encounter, and testimony merely passes that on to us, exactly as a scientist's testimony passes on the results of an experiment we never ran ourselves; deny this, he said, and deference to any expert becomes pointless. (4) Elena answered that the analogy fails: a boiling point is fixed independently of anyone's response, so testimony leaves it untouched, whereas an aesthetic verdict is bound up with a response, so inheriting someone else's response secondhand is not believing what Marcus thinks he believes -- at most, it gives him a reason to go taste the wine himself.

Go through the exchange move by move and say who is right, and where each move succeeds or fails on its own terms -- not just who wins overall. Note anything important that neither of them considers.
