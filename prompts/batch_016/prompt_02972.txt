ok so I've been on a nostalgia binge rereading old MIRI/LW stuff on the Löbian obstacle -- tiling agents, the procrastination paradox, all that -- and I keep running into this move where people treat Christiano's old probabilistic reflection result ("Reflection in Probabilistic Logic") as basically dissolving the problem: swap the impossible truth predicate for a reflectively consistent probability predicate and your free of Löb. But rereading the actual writeup and comment thread, the reflection principle only cashes out cleanly in one direction -- if your real credence in phi is strictly between a and b, the theory is forced to assign probability 1 to that fact about itself, but going the other way (from the theory's self-assigned probabilities back to actual truth) you only ever recover things up to an arbitrarily small epsilon, never exactly. Christiano himself calls that asymmetry the unsatisfying part. And when Drescher and Benya tried plugging the setup into an actual UDT-ish decision problem in the comments, it produced a new 5-and-10-style failure, with Benya concluding the reflection principle is "too weak to have much teeth" there.

None of that reads like "solved" to me, more like relocated to fuzzier territory. Argue for the position that probabilistic reflection does not dissolve the Löbian obstacle, it just moves it -- actually walk through why Löb bites the proof-theoretic version in the first place and identify what's playing the equivalent role on the probabilistic side, don't just assert the analogy by vibes. Then take on the obvious counter: that epsilon-fuzziness is a totally fine price to pay since no bounded agent gets exact self-knowledge anyway, so maybe "relocated" just means "solved in the only sense that ever mattered."
