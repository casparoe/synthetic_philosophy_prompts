I've been reading LessWrong and Astral Codex Ten for close to a decade now, and one thing that has always felt load-bearing in that whole scene is the "map is not the territory" picture of belief: credences are supposed to track some mind-independent reality, calibration is scored against what actually happens, and updating on evidence is justified because, done well, it brings the map closer to the territory over time. I even keep a small forecasting habit going on Metaculus partly for the calibration practice. For a philosophy reading group I've recently been working through Rorty -- Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature, then Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity -- and I keep getting stuck on how much of that whole picture his antirepresentationalism is actually supposed to threaten, as opposed to being merely a difference in vocabulary.

The specific move that trips me up is the one he borrows from Davidson: the world causally impinges on us and produces beliefs, but nothing in the world thereby "makes" a belief true in the sense of standing in some representational relation of fit to it; justification, on this view, is entirely a matter of how a belief coheres with our other beliefs and sentences, never a matter of checking belief against some unconceptualized reality directly. Rorty is adamant this isn't idealism -- he grants that the world is out there causing things whether or not anyone describes it -- he just denies that truth is out there in that same sense, since only sentences, which are human creations, can be true or false in the first place. He also has this notion of a "final vocabulary": the words a person or community uses to justify its beliefs and practices, which cannot themselves be given any further non-circular justification. What I can't work out is whether, on his view, the rationalist vocabulary of credence, calibration, and truth-tracking is simply one more final vocabulary that happens to be useful for some purposes (forecasting, engineering, and so on) without being any closer to reality than a rival vocabulary would be, or whether something more corrosive is supposed to follow for a community that treats getting-it-right as close to a terminal value.

Taking Rorty's antirepresentationalism seriously on its own terms, rather than dismissing it upfront as some kind of relativism, what would you say are the most important implications for someone who actually practices rationalist-style epistemics day to day -- Bayesian updating, calibration training, forecasting tournaments, and so on? I'm not looking for a general primer on Rorty; I have the basics down. I want to know what, concretely, is supposed to change. Does his view just rename what careful Bayesians already do, under a less metaphysically loaded description? Does it undercut the rationale for scoring forecasters against eventual outcomes, or is that practice untouched because it only ever needed the causal story rather than a representational one? Does it give a principled reason to care more about which epistemic vocabulary helps a community solve its actual problems, and correspondingly less about "getting reality right" as some further, separate achievement? I'd rather you work through a handful of candidate implications and say which ones actually survive scrutiny than list everything that might loosely be associated with pragmatism.
