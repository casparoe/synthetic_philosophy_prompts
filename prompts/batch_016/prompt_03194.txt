hey so I'm working through the personal identity chapter of my diss (on Parfit and the teletransporter stuff) and got stuck rereading Reid's old objection to Locke. here's the passage, from his 1785 essay against the memory theory:

"There is another consequence of this doctrine, which follows no less necessarily, though Mr. Locke probably did not see it. It is, that a man may be, and at the same time not be, the person that did a particular action. Suppose a brave officer to have been flogged when a boy at school for robbing an orchard, to have taken a standard from the enemy in his first campaign, and to have been made a general in advanced life; suppose, also, which must be admitted to be possible, that, when he took the standard, he was conscious of his having been flogged at school, and that, when made a general, he was conscious of his taking the standard, but had absolutely lost the consciousness of his flogging. These things being supposed, it follows, from Mr. Locke's doctrine, that he who was flogged at school is the same person who took the standard, and that he who took the standard is the same person who was made a general. Whence it follows, if there be any truth in logic, that the general is the same person with him who was flogged at school. But the general's consciousness does not reach so far back as his flogging; therefore, according to Mr. Locke's doctrine, he is not the person who was flogged. Therefore the general is, and at the same time is not, the same person with him who was flogged at school."

so obviously this is a transitivity failure aimed at Locke's raw memory criterion. but Parfit's dodge is to swap in psychological continuity, defined as overlapping chains of strong connectedness, which ends up transitive basically by construction (you just chain the chains together). my question is whether that move actually earns him anything, or if its a bit of a shell game -- since connectedness itself, the relation thats actually doing the work whenever Parfit tells us how much reason we have to care about the guy stepping out of the teletransporter (or a future self decades out), is NOT transitive and can go missing in exactly the brave-officer way. so does the overlapping-chains fix really rescue the view from Reid, or does it just relocate his problem onto connectedness, which was arguably the notion we cared about in the first place? feels like this should already be a solved problem somewhere in the lit but I keep going in circles on it.
