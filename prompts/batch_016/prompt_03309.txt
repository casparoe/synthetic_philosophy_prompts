So this term I'm taking a Philosophy of AI seminar, and this week we did a close read of Turing's original 1950 paper, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence." Everyone always talks about the imitation game itself, but almost nobody mentions that near the end, in his list of objections, there's literally a section called "The Argument from Extrasensory Perception," where Turing says the statistical evidence for telepathy is "overwhelming" and takes seriously the worry that a good telepathic human contestant could beat a machine at the game (he even makes up stats for it, like the guy correctly naming the suit of a card 130 times out of 400 vs the machine's random 104). Theres also this whole bit where, if the machine uses a random number generator to answer, the interrogator might mess with it via psychokinesis, and his actual proposed fix for all of this is to just stick everyone in a "telepathy-proof room." My prof mentioned that a bunch of Cambridge philosophers around Turing's time were genuinely really into psychical research, so it apparently wasn't totally out of nowhere for him, but my study group was still kind of losing it when we first read this section.

It doesnt need to be more than a page or two, but I'd like actual characters and some real dramatic tension in it, not just people reciting the philosophy at each other.
