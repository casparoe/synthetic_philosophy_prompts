Our philosophy department is rewriting its bylaws, and someone proposed a "safeguard clause": any future amendment only counts as valid if we can show, using the bylaws' own current standards of reasoning, that decisions made under the amended version will still be sound. Seemed like harmless due diligence at first, but when I tried to actually apply it to a real proposed change, I realized we can't certify that for anything but trivial edits -- to vouch in advance that our future selves' calls (under new rules, in cases nobody's thought of yet) will be trustworthy, we'd basically need to already know what they'll conclude, which kind of defeats the point of trusting a future process to handle stuff we can't foresee. A colleague says I'm way overthinking some boilerplate language, and that any reasonable person can just eyeball whether an amendment's fine. Is there an actual bootstrapping problem here, or am i missing something obvious?
