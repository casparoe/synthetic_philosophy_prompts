In the Philosophy of Right, Hegel argues that war is ethically necessary because it prevents the stagnation and moral corrosion that a prolonged peace would breed in civil society, and that risking one's life for the state is the actual realization of freedom, not mere self-preservation. He even treats the invention of the gun—which made martial courage more impersonal and mechanical than the older courage of hand-to-hand combat—as no accident, but as an expression of the modern principle that subordinates the particular individual to the universal. Extending this logic, would Hegel regard contemporary drone warfare and autonomous weapons systems—in which combatants on one side face little or no personal risk—as simply the next stage in the abstraction of courage he already welcomed, or would he see the disappearance of genuine risk as crossing a threshold beyond which war can no longer perform the ethical function he assigns it? Defend your answer with reference to his specific arguments, not his general attitude toward war.
