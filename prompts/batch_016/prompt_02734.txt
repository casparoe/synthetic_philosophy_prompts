Ok, this has been bugging me since I saw it argued on a couple different rationalist blogs/forums: Good's theorem proves that for a bayesian agent facing a decision, the expected value of free evidence is never negative -- worst case you just act like you never saw it, so learning true things can only help you in expectation, never hurt you. Given that, I dont get why the same crowd still takes 'information hazards' seriously as something an individual should worry about for their own sake -- like people who decline genetic testing for untreatable conditions -- instead of just relabeling 'info other people might misuse against me' with a scarier-sounding name. Shouldn't the theorem settle that deliberately staying ignorant of something 'for your own good' is never actually the rational move, just a comforting story people tell themselves after the fact?
