Having followed MIRI's and the LessWrong community's work on formalizing reasoning under logical uncertainty for some years, I have never seen a clean side-by-side treatment of two of the more fully worked-out proposals: Scott Garrabrant et al.'s logical induction, and the earlier reflective oracle framework due to Fallenstein, Taylor, and Christiano. Both are meant to let a reasoner assign sensible, non-dogmatic credences to sentences it has not yet proven or refuted, including sentences that reference the reasoner's own beliefs, but they reach that goal by very different routes: one via a computable sequence of trader-driven markets whose guarantees only bite in the limit, the other via a single fixed-point oracle whose existence is established non-constructively. Compare the two approaches with respect to computability, their handling of self-reference, and their resistance to exploitation. In your judgment, which comes closer to a genuine solution to the problem of logical omniscience? Justify your answer.
