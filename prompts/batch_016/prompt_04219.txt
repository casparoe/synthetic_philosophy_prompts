I've been reporting on psychology's replication crisis, and two of my sources — a Bayesian statistician who thinks the field should retire p-values entirely, and a frequentist psychologist skeptical that statistics was ever the crisis's real cause — had an email exchange along the way that never made it into the piece. Paraphrasing:

(1) The Bayesian: The real defect in null-hypothesis significance testing (NHST) is optional stopping — labs peek at incoming data and stop once p dips below .05, which inflates the true false-positive rate well past the nominal 5%. A Bayes factor avoids this: built from the likelihood of the data you actually collected, not the sampling plan you didn't follow, it's indifferent to when or why you stopped.

(2) The frequentist: True, but that buys less than you think, since nothing stops a researcher from trying several priors, outcome definitions, or covariates until one combination yields a satisfying Bayes factor. You haven't eliminated researcher degrees of freedom, just relocated them somewhere harder to audit — a self-serving prior can still look principled.

(3) The Bayesian: Fair, but that's what preregistration is for, and it burdens both frameworks equally. The real asymmetry is that a preregistered Bayesian analysis gives the identical posterior whether you peeked at 20 participants or 200, provided the reason you stopped was unrelated to the data. A preregistered significance test still has to fix its sample size and comparisons in advance, because its error-rate guarantees are defined over hypothetical repetitions of the entire sampling plan.

(4) The frequentist: That "guarantee" just restates what you're dismissing as irrelevant. Frequentists fix the plan in advance because they're tracking how often the method would mislead us across repeated use — a property of the procedure, not of this dataset. Call that irrelevant to "evidence" if you like, but the replication crisis was precisely the discovery that certain procedures were misleading us at far higher rates than advertised. Retiring the one framework built to notice that isn't curing the disease — it's losing the ability to detect it.
