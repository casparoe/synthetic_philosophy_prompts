Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have finally had time to read philosophy of science properly, and I keep returning to Popper. His picture of scientific progress as a sequence of falsified conjectures replaced by bolder, better-tested ones matched my own experience in the lab. I was glad to learn that he did not leave the notion of a theory being "closer to the truth" as mere metaphor: he gave it a precise formal definition, comparing the truth-content and falsity-content of rival theories, which should let us say rigorously that, for instance, general relativity has more verisimilitude than Newtonian mechanics, even though neither is exactly true. Given that Popper settled this back in the 1960s, I am puzzled why philosophers of science still write papers debating how to measure a theory's closeness to the truth, rather than simply applying his definition. What am I missing?
