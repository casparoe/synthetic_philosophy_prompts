Ok, weird one, but it's been bugging me all week. At a family reunion my aunt told this story about our great-grandfather supposedly stealing a horse before the family emigrated - big scandal, nobody had heard it before. I asked how she knew, and she said cousin Pete told her; turns out Pete says he heard it from her. So really its just the two of them backing each other up, no outside source at all. My uncle says that's fine, because the story fits with a bunch of other stuff we already believe about the family (they left in a hurry, never talked about the old country, etc), and that fit is what makes it credible. My cousin Dana thinks that's nonsense - she says you need at least one piece of evidence that doesn't just rest on somebody's say-so (a letter, a record, anything), or the whole thing is groundless. Then my other cousin Theo said it doesn't even need to bottom out anywhere - we should just keep turning up more corroborating details forever, and that open-endedness is itself what would make believing it reasonable. Everyone's looking at me to referee since I'm supposedly "the philosopher" of the family, but honestly I work on phil of language, not this stuff. Who's actually got the best of this argument, if anyone?
