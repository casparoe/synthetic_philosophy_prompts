Got into a whole argument with my friend last night about whether omissions can actually cause things — started because our housesitter forgot to water my basil while we were away and it died. I said not watering it is obviously why it died; she said no, an absence isn't a real thing, so it cant be a cause, that's a category error, only actual events do causal work.

Then she pointed out that if "would've prevented it" is enough, then everyone who didn't water it also caused it to die — my mom, the mailman, randoms in another country. Thats clearly too much. But the fix (it only counts if she had a duty to water it, or if that was the normal/expected thing) means whether something's a "cause" depends on social norms, not on what physically happened, which feels wrong for something that's supposed to be an objective fact about the world.

Write me an essay that actually settles this: are omissions ever genuine causes, or is "causation by omission" just loose talk for something else? Walk through the real options and land on an actual verdict, don't hedge. Maybe 500-700 words.
