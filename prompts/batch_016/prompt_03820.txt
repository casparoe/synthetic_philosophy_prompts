I've spent enough years reading LessWrong, Overcoming Bias, and similar forums that I instinctively look for the "crux" whenever people I know argue past each other, but a dispute from a meetup two weekends ago has resisted my best efforts to locate it. Some background: a regular in our local rationalist-adjacent friend group, Odell, has spent something like a year complaining, both in person and in the group chat, about "slate voting" in the polls we use to pick books for our reading group — a few people coordinating in advance to all upvote the same pick so it wins regardless of what everyone else prefers. Two weekends ago, someone dug up an old chat log showing that Odell organized exactly this kind of slate himself, two years back, before most of the current members had joined. When it came up over drinks, two other friends, Priyanka and Marcus, ended up arguing for a good twenty minutes about whether the revelation should change how the group treats Odell's ongoing complaints. I mostly listened, and I'd like a more careful outside read than either of them reached, since my impression is that philosophers working on "standing to blame" have already mapped this terrain more precisely than anyone managed at the table.

As best I can reconstruct the exchange: (1) Marcus said the chat log settles things — Odell has no standing to complain about slate voting anymore, since you can't hold other people to a norm you've broken yourself. (2) Priyanka replied that this conflates two separate questions: whether slate voting is bad, and whether it's currently happening, has nothing to do with Odell's history, and using his past to dismiss the complaint is just a tu quoque. (3) Marcus said he wasn't disputing that slate voting is bad or that it's occurring, only that Odell specifically has forfeited the right to be the one objecting to it — anyone who'd never done it could say the identical sentence and be completely in the clear. (4) Priyanka asked why a two-year-old incident, predating most of the current group, should permanently disqualify someone from ever raising the issue again, and added that Marcus's talk of "standing" struck her as a more respectable-sounding way of saying "you don't get to talk." (5) Marcus said that wasn't fair: the issue wasn't the old slate itself but the fact that Odell had spent the past year complaining while letting everyone assume he'd always played it straight, never mentioning his own history until he was caught — that silence, not the original vote-stacking, was the real hypocrisy. (6) Priyanka said that if concealment was the real complaint, it should have evaporated the moment Odell owned up to it, which he did, that same night, in one sentence — yet nothing about the current slate voting, or the soundness of his reasons for opposing it, changed at that moment, which suggests standing was never doing much independent work.

Neither of them gave ground, and I don't think either position is silly. Could you go through moves (1) through (6) and say who has the better of it at each step, rather than just declaring an overall winner? I'm especially interested in whether Marcus's move in (5) actually answers Priyanka's charge from (4) that his talk of "standing" is just a respectable-sounding dismissal, or whether it merely relocates the same objection onto a different fact. I'd also like to know whether Priyanka's closing move in (6) shows that the standing objection wasn't doing much real work, or is fully compatible with Marcus having been right all along. Finally, is there a consideration that both of them are overlooking?
