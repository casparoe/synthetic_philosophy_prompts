Since retiring from my physiology lab I've gotten into reading philosophy for fun, and it's dredged up something from my career I never really resolved. For decades I used animals in experiments, justifying it by figuring the suffering of a few rats or rabbits was worth it for real health benefits to way more people. But I'd never accept that same logic with humans - nobody would let me run an experiment that harms a handful of people even if it definately saves a much bigger number down the road. A former grad student of mine says I can't have it both ways: either animal suffering counts the same as human suffering in the calculation, or I owe a real reason it doesn't. Been chewing on it for weeks - is there an actual principled answer here, or was I just kidding myself all those years?
