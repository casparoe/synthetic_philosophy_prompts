Aquinas holds that a private individual may licitly kill a tyrant who has seized power through usurpation, yet insists that a ruler who acquired power legitimately and then governs tyrannically may be deposed only by public authority, never by a private person acting alone. But Aquinas also holds that a ruler's authority is justified only insofar as it serves the common good. Write an essay on whether these two commitments are genuinely consistent: if a legitimate ruler's title depends on his governing for the common good, doesn't a decisive turn to tyranny strip him of that title and assimilate his case to the usurper's, thereby undermining the very distinction Aquinas relies on to forbid a private individual from killing him? Weigh the strongest considerations on both sides, and don't settle for a non-committal answer -- defend a definite conclusion.
