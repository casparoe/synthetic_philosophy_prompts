i came across this old essay called 'Epilogue' by Frank Ramsey (the guy who did a bunch of foundational math/philosophy/economics work and then died at 26). His whole argument for why he's not bummed about being a tiny speck in a huge universe is basically: stars are big but they can't think or love, so size just isn't that impressive. He even extends this to time, saying the far future cooling down and everything dying doesn't really matter much because its 'present value at compound discount' is basically nothing. Except in his actual economics papers he argued the opposite - that discounting future generations wellbeing is 'ethically indefensible' and just comes from a failure of imagination, which is basically the exact argument longtermists use today for why we should care so much about the astronomical number of potential future people. So is Ramsey contradicting himself, or is there a way to square his 'don't sweat the vast future' attitude with his own case against discounting? Genuinely curious what you think he'd actually say if you sat him down with a longtermist today.
