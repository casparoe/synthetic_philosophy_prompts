So this started as a work problem and turned into a genuine rabbit hole for me, curious what you make of it. I'm a mobile engineer at a small startup, and our PM wants our entire onboarding + settings flow to be 100% icons, zero text anywhere, because we're launching in something like 40 countries at once and localizing all the copy into 40 languages is expensive and slow. Her pitch was basically "pictures are universal, everyone reads them the same no matter what language they speak, so lets just skip translation entirely." For simple nouns this is genuinely fine — camera icon, bell icon, gear icon, nobody's confused.

Where we keep faceplanting is anything that needs actual and/or/not logic instead of just objects. Like: "you won't get notifications unless location AND bluetooth are both on," or "show this badge only if the user is NOT a paying subscriber." We've tried a red slash through the bell icon for "off," we've tried smooshing two icons together with a plus sign for "and," we tried the standard circle-with-a-line-through-it over the subscriber icon for "not" — and in every round of user testing people misread this stuff constantly. The slashed bell reads as "notifications are broken" way more than "notifications are off." The mashed-up icon reads as some brand-new third setting instead of "these two together." Almost nobody spontaneously reads our "not" symbol as negation without us explaining it to them first, even though that same symbol works fine for stuff like "no smoking." Our designer keeps insisting we just haven't landed on the right icon yet and wants another sprint. I mentioned it to a friend who does UX research and even she didn't have a real answer, just more icon suggestions.

Three sprints in, I'm starting to suspect this isn't a "haven't found the right icon" problem at all — like maybe images are just bad at this specific job no matter how clever you get, the same way you basically can't draw an "unless." I googled around and only found generic icon-design tips, nothing that explains why this keeps happening. So: is there something real and unavoidable going on here, or are we just not creative enough yet? And if it's real, is there any actual way around it short of caving and putting text back in? (I know basically zero philosophy so if this is some well-trodden thing, feel free to tell me I'm reinventing the wheel.)
