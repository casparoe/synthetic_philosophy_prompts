Anyway, two people in my discussion section, Theo and Leah, got into a real argument after class last week over the classic Frankfurt case: Black the neuroscientist wants Jones to kill Smith and has rigged up a device that will force Jones to do it if Jones shows any sign of wavering, but Jones ends up deciding to do it entirely on his own, so the device never actually has to kick in. Frankfurt's point is that Jones is still morally responsible even though, because of the device, he literally couldn't have done otherwise — which is supposed to be a problem for the "principle of alternative possibilities," the idea that you're only responsible for what you could've avoided doing.

I wasnt part of the argument, just half-listening while I packed up my bag, but here's roughly how it went (numbering the moves since I want to ask about specific ones):

(1) Theo: this settles it, PAP is false. Jones has zero alternate possibilities and we still judge him responsible — that's literally the whole point of the setup.
(2) Leah: not so fast, there's a dilemma here. Whatever "sign" tips Black off that Jones might waver either guarantees the wavering or it doesn't. If it does, then determinism is just true in this story and you've assumed the very thing you needed to prove; if it doesn't, there's a split second where Jones could show the sign and still not go through with it, which is a real, if small, alternate possibility.
(3) Theo: fine, grant that flicker exists. It's a flicker in some neural sign Jones isn't even aware of, not an alternate way of deciding whether to kill Smith. If a possibility that thin can save PAP, you've kept the letter of the principle and gutted the whole point of it.
(4) Leah: sure, but then you owe me a principled account of how "thick" an alternate possibility has to be before it counts, and I'd bet any line you draw lands exactly where it needs to for Frankfurt to come out right. That's not a real cutoff, that's just tuning the definition to fit your conclusion.

They both had to run before anyone won, and its been bugging me since. Could you go through moves (1) through (4) and say who's got the better of the argument at each step — not just declare an overall winner — and flag anything both of them seem to be missing?
