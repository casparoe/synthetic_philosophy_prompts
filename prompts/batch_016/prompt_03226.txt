The dogmatism paradox, originated by Kripke and relayed in print by Gilbert Harman, runs roughly as follows: if I know that h is true, I thereby already know that any future evidence against h would have to be misleading, and so I seem entitled to discount it before I've even encountered it. This has always struck me as sitting in curious tension with the LessWrong notion of conservation of expected evidence -- the idea that you shouldn't be able to predict, before actually seeing a piece of evidence, that it will move your credence in some particular direction. Write a joke whose punchline turns on this tension. It shouldn't work as a generic joke about stubbornness or confirmation bias; it should only really land for someone who understands why "I already know I'm right, so anything that looks like counter-evidence must be misleading" is a genuine epistemological puzzle, not just a personality flaw.
