In 1929 Carnap co-signed the Vienna Circle's manifesto together with Otto Neurath, aligning himself with what historians call the Circle's left wing, which sought to extend the scientific world-conception into the planning of economic and social life. In the socialist calculation debate that followed, Neurath argued that a planned economy could dispense with market prices altogether, coordinating production through direct calculation in physical units; Mises and Hayek countered that this was doomed by the impossibility of aggregating dispersed knowledge without a price mechanism. Carnap himself was no economist, but he shared Neurath's confidence that scientific method could be extended into social planning, alongside his own commitments to verificationism and to what he called the principle of tolerance—choosing between rival frameworks on grounds of fruitfulness rather than dogma. Given all this, what do you think Carnap would have made of recent claims that large-scale computation and machine learning finally make this kind of non-market planning feasible?
