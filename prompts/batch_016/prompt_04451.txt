Write a dialogue between two philosophers of anthropic reasoning — one who accepts the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and one who accepts the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) — set just after a 'presumptuous philosopher' has interrupted a team of physicists who are about to run a costly experiment to decide between two cosmological theories that are otherwise equally well confirmed: one on which the universe contains a trillion trillion observers, and one on which it contains a trillion times more. The presumptuous philosopher has just announced that the experiment is unnecessary, since our own existence already settles the matter overwhelmingly in favor of the larger universe. Have the SIA proponent defend this verdict and the SSA proponent resist it, with each pressing the other on the standard costs of their position — bare presumptuousness on one side, the doomsday argument and the reference class problem on the other — and let neither side simply concede the other's framing of who counts as a relevantly similar observer.
