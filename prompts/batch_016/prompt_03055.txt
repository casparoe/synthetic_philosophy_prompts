For the past couple of years I've volunteered as a mentor for at-risk teenagers, on the assumption that steering even one kid toward a better path is obviously worth doing. A friend recently raised an objection I can't shake off: whatever good I do for that teenager doesn't stop with them, since their life will go on to affect countless other people, who will affect countless others in turn, for generations, in ways no one could ever trace. Since there's no way to know the net effect of all that, she argues I have no real basis for believing my mentoring does more good than harm overall, however positive the immediate effect looks. I find this hard to answer, yet it also seems to prove too much, since the same logic would apply to almost any good deed, from a small act of kindness to a major life decision. Is she right that this uncertainty undermines my reason for mentoring, or is there a flaw in her argument?
