So I spent most of my career as a field biologist -- sticklebacks mostly, with some beetle population work toward the end -- and retired about four years ago. Since then I've finally had the time to work through all the philosophy of science I always meant to read, and last week I fell down a real rabbit hole on Karl Popper. Turns out that for years he argued natural selection wasn't actually a testable scientific theory at all, just a "metaphysical research programme," because to him "survival of the fittest" was basically circular -- the fittest just are whichever ones survived, so the whole slogan collapses into "survival of the survivors." Then, weirdly, he got up at the first ever Darwin Lecture at Cambridge in '77 and recanted the whole thing, saying he'd changed his mind and that natural selection really is testable after all. I've been a working biologist my entire adult life and it never once occured to me that what I was doing might not count as proper science, so this whole saga has been bugging me more then I expected.

What I'd love is a dialogue out of this rather than a straight explanation -- two philosophers who genuinely disagree, hashing it out after a seminar, maybe over a pint. One of them thinks Popper had it right the first time around, and that the recantation was too hasty; the other thinks he had it right the second time, and that the tautology worry always rested on some kind of confusion. I don't want either one just reciting talking points past the other, though -- the "it's testable" side needs to actually answer the circularity charge head-on (does defining fitness some other way than "whoever happened to survive" really dissolve the problem, or just push it back a step?), and the "it's a tautology" side needs to seriously engage with whatever concrete predictive successes get thrown at them, rather than just repeating the slogan. Neither one should be an easy win for the other. It doesn't need to end tidily either -- I'd rather it stop wherever two smart, honest people would actually get stuck.
