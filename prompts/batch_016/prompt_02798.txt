I've been reading about the worry that a future superintelligent AI might "acausally" threaten people today—for example, by simulating and punishing copies of us in order to influence how we behave now, even though there's no causal link between us and it. The proposed defense that keeps coming up is the "surrogate goal": you get yourself, or an AI acting on your behalf, to genuinely care about some arbitrary and otherwise meaningless outcome—say, preventing an absurdly specific arrangement of objects that would almost never occur naturally—so that any threat gets redirected toward that decoy goal instead of toward what you actually value. Since whatever the threatener destroys under this scheme was never something you cared about in the first place, adopting a surrogate goal seems to make an agent essentially blackmail-proof, with no real downside. Given that, why do researchers in this area still treat extortion as a serious unsolved problem, rather than as something already handled by just having every AI adopt a throwaway surrogate goal?
