Ok so I've fallen down a bit of a rabbit hole this week: superdeterminism. I first ran into it through those old "cosmic bell test" experiments, the ones where physicists used light from quasars billions of years old to pick the measurement angles in an entanglement experiment, specifically so that nothing in the recent past could sneak in and correlate the detector settings with the particles. I'd always assumed superdeterminism was a fringe, slightly embarrassing idea, but the more I read the less obvious that is.

For a piece I'm working on I called up two physicists and got two completely confident, completely opposed reactions, with basically zero engagement between them. One said superdeterminism gives up something he called "measurement independence," and that this basically wrecks the logic of controlled experiments, since you could always wave away an inconvenient result by claiming a hidden conspiracy between the measuring device and the thing being measured. The other said that objection is question-begging: if the universe really is deterministic all the way down, everything is already correlated with everything else via a shared causal past anyway, so singling out this one correlation as uniquely conspiratorial just smuggles in an assumption of "free" experimental choice that was never justified in the first place. Neither one actually responded to the other's point, they just said their bit and got off the phone.

Could you write me the conversation I wish I'd gotten instead? A real dialogue between a committed superdeterminist and a physicist who'd rather give up locality or realism, where they actually engage with each others strongest arguments rather then just restating their own. Neither should be the obvious loser - I want both of them to be the smartest possible version of their view, actually landing blows and having real comebacks. Something like 12-16 exchanges back and forth should do it, and please don't let it end with one side just running out of things to say.
