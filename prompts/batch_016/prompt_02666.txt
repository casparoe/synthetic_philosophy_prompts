Over winter break, I had a long conversation with my grandmother that I still haven't been able to shake. She's deeply religious, and she's completely certain that when she dies, she'll be reunited with my grandfather, who passed away a few years ago. A little too curious for my own good, I pushed her on how she could be so sure, and pointed out that there isn't really any evidence either way. What surprised me was that she didn't respond the way she does when we disagree about ordinary things, like whether a certain route is faster or whether it's going to rain on Saturday. In those cases she'll lay out her reasons and admit she could be wrong. Here, she just seemed to think the question missed the point somehow, without ever getting defensive about it.

At the same time, I don't think she treats "we'll be reunited" as some comforting metaphor she doesn't really mean. It's clearly not a throwaway belief: it shapes how she talks about the future and what she prays for. But it's also strange that she still grieved my grandfather so intensely, for so long. If she really thought about his death the way she'd think about him being away on an unusually long trip before they saw each other again, you would expect the grief to have looked at least a little different. So she isn't treating this belief quite like an ordinary factual claim she happens to be very confident about, and she isn't treating it as an open question either. When I brought this up with a couple of friends, they told me I was way overanalyzing one conversation with my grandma, but it has genuinely been sitting with me since. Is she just avoiding a question she doesn't have a good answer to, or is asking for her evidence somehow the wrong way to engage with what she believes in the first place?
