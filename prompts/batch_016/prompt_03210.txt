As a journalist who writes about how scientific findings get communicated to the public, I keep running into disagreement over what actually entitles someone to assert something as fact. Please compare Timothy Williamson's knowledge norm of assertion — one may assert that p only if one knows that p — with Matthew Weiner's rival truth norm, which drops the knowledge requirement and simply requires that p be true. Test the two views against Jennifer Lackey's "selfless assertion" case. A devoutly creationist elementary-school teacher privately rejects evolution, yet tells her class that modern humans evolved from Homo erectus, because she takes it to be her duty as a teacher to present what the evidence supports — even though, by her own lights, she neither believes nor knows the claim to be true. Which norm classifies this assertion correctly, and does whichever one fares better here pay for it with a distinctive weakness elsewhere? Adjudicate between the two theories and justify your verdict.
