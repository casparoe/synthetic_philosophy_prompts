George Berkeley famously argued that matter has no existence apart from being perceived, so that a physical object is ultimately nothing more than a bundle of ideas with no independent substance behind it. I was surprised, then, to find that the same philosopher devoted an entire treatise, The Querist, to arguing that a nation's currency need not be backed by gold or silver, since money, as he put it, is really just a "ticket or counter" for reckoning power over other people's labor. If nothing physical has any independent substance behind it in his system to begin with, surely the question of what "backs" a currency should have struck him as confused rather than as a serious matter of state policy worth working out in such detail. So why does he treat it as the latter, and what does that suggest about how seriously he actually took his own metaphysics?
