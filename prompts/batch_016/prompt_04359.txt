In my philosophy of science seminar, we have been contrasting Karl Popper's falsificationism with the logical positivists' verifiability criterion of meaning. As I understand it, Popper essentially retained the positivists' underlying project while substituting a new criterion: where they held that a statement is cognitively meaningful only if it is verifiable, he held that a statement is cognitively meaningful only if it is falsifiable. On this reading, when Popper dismisses theories such as the Marxist theory of history or Freudian psychoanalysis as unfalsifiable, he must be classifying them as literally meaningless, not merely as unscientific. Am I misunderstanding something here? If not, I do not see why standard histories of philosophy of science insist that Popper's project was fundamentally different from that of the Vienna Circle, rather than treating him as simply offering a technical refinement of their meaning criterion.
