I'm preparing next week's meeting of a graduate seminar on classical Chinese philosophy of language, devoted this term to the School of Names (名家). We've already worked through the opening gambit of Gongsun Long's "White Horse Dialogue" (白馬論) — "horse" names a shape, "white" names a color, and whatever names a color cannot be what names a shape, hence "a white horse is not a horse" — which by now is old hat to my students. What continues to give everyone trouble, myself included on a bad day, is the dialogue's final exchange, which comes after several rounds in which sophist and objector have gone back and forth over why a brown or black horse can "answer to" a request for a horse but not to a request for a white horse.

Here is the exchange, in the original together with my own working translation:

有白馬,不可謂無馬者,離白之謂也。不離者有白馬不可謂有馬也。故所以為有馬者,獨以馬為有馬耳,非有白馬為有馬。
Objector: "That one has a white horse cannot be said to be that one lacks a horse — but this only holds once 'white' has been split off and set aside. Without that split, having a white horse cannot be said to be having a horse. So whatever entitles us to say 'there is a horse' is horse taken just by itself — not 'there is a white horse.'"

白者不定所白,忘之而可也。白馬者,言白定所白也。定所白者,非白也。馬者,無去取于色,故黃、黑皆所以應。白馬者,有去取于色,黃、黑馬皆所以色去,故唯白馬獨可以應耳。無去者非有去也;故曰:「白馬非馬」。
Sophist: "'White,' left to itself, is not tied down to any particular thing it is the white of — that can simply be left unspecified. 'White horse' states that the white has been tied down to a particular bearer, and white so tied down is no longer the same as white. Now 'horse' rules nothing in or out by color, so a brown or black horse can equally answer to it; 'white horse' does rule in and out by color, so brown and black horses are excluded by their color, and only a white horse can answer to it. What rules nothing out cannot be equated with what does rule something out. Hence: 'a white horse is not a horse.'"

What I would like help with is the logical anatomy of the sophist's closing move, and how it relates back to the objector's talk of "separating" (離) white in the preceding turn. The sophist seems to be running together two claims that do not obviously entail one another: a metaphysical one, about "white" being indeterminate until "fixed" (定) onto a bearer, and a semantic one, about "horse" and "white horse" excluding different colors. Fung Yu-lan read the whole dialogue as a piece of Platonic realism, on which "white horse" and "horse" simply name two distinct universals; Chad Hansen rejected this in favor of a mereological reading, closer to later Mohist logic, on which the terms name part/whole compounds rather than intersecting properties. Could you reconstruct, as charitably as you can, how each of these readings would make sense of this particular passage, say whether "fixing" and "separating" are doing the same work or different work across the two turns, and conclude which reading, if either, actually licenses the sophist's stated conclusion, "a white horse is not a horse," as opposed to the far weaker claim that "white horse" and "horse" are merely non-synonymous terms?
