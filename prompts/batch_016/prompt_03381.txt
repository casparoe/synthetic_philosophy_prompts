quick decision theory one: parfit's hitchhiker. you're dying of thirst in the desert; a driver will only give you a lift into town if she predicts you'll pay her $100 once you get there, and once you're actually in town, paying does nothing further for you. the usual take (especially in rationalist/decision theory circles) is that this shows causal decision theory is broken, since CDT says dont pay once you're safe, and real rationality requires becoming disposed to pay even though its against your narrow interest in the moment -- people call this rational irrationality. i want you to argue against that.

make the strongest case that the CDT agent who refuses to pay in town isn't irrational at all, and that the standard "but you'd want to be the kind of agent who pays" move doesn't actually rescue the in-the-moment choice -- it just quietly switches to a different question (what policy to commit to beforehand). engage directly with mcclennen's resolute choice and gauthier-style constrained maximization, dont just say "well FDT/UDT solves it" and call it a day.
