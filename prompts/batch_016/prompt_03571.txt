I teach a graduate seminar on philosophy of mathematics, and every single year, once we get to Skolem's paradox, the same thing happens: the students read the Lowenheim-Skolem material, skim Bays' SEP entry, and by the end of the week everyone's converged on "oh, its just a scope confusion between internal and external quantifiers, there's no real philosophical puzzle left once you're careful" — and honestly it bugs me how quickly everyone lets Skolem himself off the hook. He didn't think this was a boring technical footnote; he thought it showed something substantive, namely that "uncountable" isn't an absolute property a set just has, but only ever holds relative to a model.

Here's what I want from you: make the strongest case you can FOR Skolem's own relativist reading — i.e., that the existence of a countable model of ZFC which itself satisfies "there exists an uncountable set" (say, the powerset of omega) shows that notions like countable/uncountable are irreducibly model-relative, not absolute facts about sets in the way classical Platonists assume. Don't give me a neutral both-sides summary, actually argue the position. In particular I want you to engage with, and try to defeat, the standard rebuttal: the one where people say the whole "paradox" trades on an equivocation between "M satisfies the sentence 'x is uncountable'" and "x is really uncountable," so that once you're careful to distinguish internal from external claims about bijections (noting that "there's a bijection between x and omega" just isn't absolute across models), the puzzle dissolves and there's nothing left for a relativist to hang their hat on. I've always suspected that rebuttal is too quick, but I've never been able to say exactly why, so give it a real shot. Feel free to bring in Putnam's permutation argument or the broader model-theoretic worries about realism if they're genuinely useful, but try to keep the main thread on the narrower cardinality case rather than sliding into the whole internal-realism debate — that's a seperate rabbit hole for another day.
