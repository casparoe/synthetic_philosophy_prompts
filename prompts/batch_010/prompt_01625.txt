I'm designing a contract resolution system for a legal technology platform, and I've encountered a problem that I'm not sure how to approach. Consider this scenario: a commercial lease requires the tenant to maintain "high quality standards" for their retail storefront, but the contract never defines what that phrase actually means. The landlord argues that the tenant is in violation—the storefront's interior design is outdated, the fixtures are worn, the overall presentation doesn't convey premium quality. From a literal reading of the contract text, the landlord's position seems defensible. Yet I'm troubled by this conclusion. The tenant was operating a modest neighborhood business, not a luxury boutique, and presumably both parties understood that distinction when they signed the lease.

Here is my core dilemma: should a contract resolution system interpret agreements strictly according to their written words, or should it attempt to infer what the parties probably intended? The literal approach seems clear, but it creates a tension with what the agreement appeared to be fundamentally about. There is an obvious gap between the specific words used and the apparent intent behind them. When a rule's strict interpretation conflicts with its underlying purpose, which principle should prevail? I suspect there is a more principled answer to this question than I'm currently seeing.
