Kurt Gödel died in 1978, over a decade before Roger Penrose published *The Emperor's New Mind* and *Shadows of the Mind*, in which he deploys the first incompleteness theorem to argue that human mathematical understanding outstrips any single formal system, eventually tying this claim to putative quantum processes in microtubules. Gödel's own unpublished remarks, notably the 1951 Gibbs lecture, gesture toward a disjunctive thesis about mind and mechanism, yet he stopped well short of endorsing anything like Lucas's later argument. Drawing on Gödel's mathematical Platonism, his skepticism of formalism, and the cautious disjunctivism of the Gibbs lecture, how do you think he would have assessed Penrose's argument? Would he have accepted the anti-mechanist conclusion, and would he have regarded the appeal to quantum indeterminacy as a plausible physical basis for noncomputable cognition, or as an unmotivated addition to the logical core of the argument?
