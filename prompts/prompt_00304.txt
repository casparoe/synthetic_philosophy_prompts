Leibniz's mill argument in the Monadology insists that no arrangement of mechanical parts, however intricate, could ever produce perception, since inspecting the gears of such a mill would reveal only parts moving other parts, never the unity of experience itself. Functionalism's multiple realizability thesis, by contrast, holds that mental states are individuated by their causal-functional role and so could in principle be realized in silicon as well as neurons. How would Leibniz likely have assessed this thesis if confronted with a contemporary large language model that reproduces human-like functional organization? Would he treat such a system as a mere aggregate incapable of genuine perception, or does his account of monads and pre-established harmony leave room for functional isomorphism to matter? Defend your answer with reference to his views on matter, aggregates, and simple substances.
