Two people in my study group got stuck on a nonidentity case. A couple is choosing between two embryos: one would become a child with a genetic condition that makes life a bit worse but still worth living, the other a different, healthier child. They implant the first. One said they didn't harm the child, because if they had chosen the other embryo, that particular child wouldn't exist, so there's no counterfactual where the same child has a better life. The other said that's a nonstater, because harm can be assesed time-relatively: once the child exists, she has extra burdens, and we can ask whether she was brought into existence with a worse condition than she could have had. The first person replied that "could have had" is doing all the work, since the child's identity depends on the exact gametes, so you're just comparing her to someone else and calling it harm. The second person said identity isn't the only thing that matters; it can be bad to create a person with avoidable burdens even if no prior person is worse off. They asked me to referee. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step -- not just who wins overall -- and what both are missing?
