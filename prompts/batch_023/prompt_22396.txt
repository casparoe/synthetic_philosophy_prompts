I'm revising a chapter on the metaphysics of dispositions, and a recent discussion has left me confused about the fink/mask distinction. If a fink is a disposition whose stimulus removes the target disposition before the target can manifest, while a mask merely prevents manifestation without removing the disposition, why would anyone think finkish cases are an objection to Humean manifestation-condition analyses of dispositions at all? Since the fink is itself just another disposition, and the target disposition is simply not present at the relevant time, doesn't that mean the case actually supports the idea that dispositions are stable enough for Humean accounts, provided we simply restrict attention to dispositions that survive their own stimuli?
