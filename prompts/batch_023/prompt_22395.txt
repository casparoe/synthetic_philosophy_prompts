At a reading group, two people argued about Lewis's triviality result for indicative conditionals. (1) One said it is decisive: if an indicative conditional “if p then q” is an event in the same Boolean algebra as p and q, and its probability equals the conditional probability of q given p, where that conditional probability is the usual ratio P(p ∧ q)/P(p), then every such conditional must have probability 0 or 1. (2) The other replied that the theorem only rules out that package; on Stalnaker-style semantics, the probability of a conditional is determined by the proportion of the most similar p-worlds in which q holds, not by the global ratio, so the collapse need not follow. (3) The first person answered that this is just a non-ratio conditional probability, so the cost is losing the simple connection between the probability of the conditional and the probabilities of p, q, and p ∧ q. (4) The second person conceded the connection is lost but said that is not a defect: the result shows the ratio is the wrong tool for conditionals, not that conditionals cannot be probabilized at all. Please adjudicate move by move, saying which specific claims are correct, which are overstated, and what both sides are missing, and end with a brief overall verdict.
