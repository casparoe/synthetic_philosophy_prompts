I was just having a long and rather tiring argument with a friend about whether Quine’s naturalism, if extended to ethics, would make him a moral realist, a deflationist, or something closer to a quietist. Could you try to predict what Quine would have said about the moral status of large language models that report distress, especially a model that reliably says it is in pain in response to certain prompts but whose architecture contains no obvious analogue to nociception? Please ground the answer in Quine’s ideas about the web of belief, the underdetermination of theory by evidence, and the indeterminacy of translation, and distinguish carefully between three questions: whether Quine would treat a claim such as “this model suffers” as a revisible empirical hypothesis, whether he would think our best theories of mind and language make phenomenal consciousness in such systems sufficiently probable, and whether he would derive practical obligations from the remaining uncertainty. I would also like the response to avoid assuming that Quine had a fixed moral theory, and to show where his position might diverge from both a straightforward naturalist moral realism and a purely expressivist account.
