I'm taking a graduate seminar on predictive processing, and two classmates had a dispute after a reading on the free energy principle that I couldn't settle. One of them, Maya, said that the free energy principle is not merely a metaphor for Bayesian brains but a unifying principle: a system that persists does so by minimizing variational free energy, and because perception and action both serve that objective, the “self” is simply the system’s hierarchical model of the variables inside its Markov blanket. Her interlocutor, Daniel, replied that this collapses two different questions: (1) learning a model that makes sensory data probable, and (2) using that model to select actions that minimize expected free energy. He argued that the self-model is circular, because the model is judged by how well it minimizes free energy, yet the free-energy calculation already presupposes a partition of the system into a Markov blanket and an environment, and that partition is what makes the model a model of a self. Maya answered that the Markov blanket is not chosen by minimizing free energy; it is fixed by conditional independence in the statistical model, and the apparent circularity is no worse than ordinary Bayesian updating, where the posterior that updates the prior is itself obtained from the prior. Daniel conceded that local Bayesian updating is not vicious, but said that the blanket is model-relative: the same physical system can be described with different conditional-independence structures, so the free energy principle needs a prior that already fixes the self/environment split. He added that if the principle is just a thermostat maintaining a set point, it may be useful but it no longer explains the kind of selfhood or agency the literature often claims to explain.
