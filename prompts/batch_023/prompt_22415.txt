I am grading a final examination for a graduate seminar in metaphysics. The question was: “Explain Max Black’s two-sphere thought experiment and its significance for the identity of indiscernibles. In your answer, say whether the example still threatens the principle once relational properties are admitted.” One student wrote the following: “Black’s example involves two perfect spheres in an otherwise empty universe, separated by one foot. They are alike in all intrinsic respects. The identity of indiscernibles, if understood as a claim about qualitative identity, is threatened: the spheres are distinct yet qualitatively identical. Introducing relational properties seems to help the principle, since each sphere has the property of being one foot from the other. But this only gives them a common relational property; it does not supply a property possessed by one and not the other, so the spheres remain indiscernible. The challenge therefore survives unless one counts properties that refer to the spheres themselves as distinct individuals, which would make the principle depend on haecceities.”

Please grade this answer out of twenty and assign a letter grade. In your feedback, comment point by point on the student’s claims: indicate which parts are accurate, which are imprecise, and which involve a step that does not follow. In particular, assess whether the student correctly characterizes Black’s example, the force of the challenge, and the role of relational properties.

Do not merely supply a corrected essay; treat this as an exam evaluation. I would like the comments to be specific enough that I could use them directly in a grading memo, rather than a general impression of whether the answer is “good” or “weak.”
