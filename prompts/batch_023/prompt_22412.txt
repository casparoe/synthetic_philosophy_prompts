I’m a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I’m preparing a feature on whether time is “real” in the way everyday intuition suggests. I keep returning to J. M. E. McTaggart’s 1908 essay “The Unreality of Time,” because I want the piece to do justice to the presentist/eternalist divide without collapsing it into a simple “moving now versus static block” slogan. I understand the broad shape of the debate, but I’m stuck on a passage where McTaggart distinguishes the A-series from the B-series and then argues that the A-series cannot be coherent.

The passage I’m having trouble with is his claim that “the present is not a property of an event, but a relation between an event and a reference-point,” and his related claim that “the passage of time is the change in the A-series.” I’d like help unpacking what he means by the “reference-point” here. Is he saying that an event is present only relative to itself, or present relative to some observer, perspective, or other event? And why does he think this relationality makes the present unstable rather than merely indexical? I’m especially interested in how this point bears on presentism: does McTaggart’s charge depend on the idea that a genuine “now” would have to be an absolute property, or is he trying to show that even a perspectival present cannot be part of the objective temporal structure?
