I’m not a philosopher, but I’ve been trying to work out whether voting is rational when a single vote is almost never decisive. Suppose there is a close election in which the probability that my vote is decisive is about one in ten thousand, and the stakes are serious but not life-and-death. Please write an essay on whether voting is rational in that situation. Weigh the expected-value argument that voting is irrational, the collective-duty argument, the expressive or identity-based argument, and the common claim that I should vote because if everyone thought their vote was not decisive, no one would vote and the worse outcome would be more likely. The essay should determine whether that common claim is a valid argument or a mistake about individual action, and it should end by defending a conclusion rather than remaining neutral. If you conclude that voting is rational, specify what kind of reason makes it so; if you conclude that it is not rational, say what should replace it as a practical norm.
