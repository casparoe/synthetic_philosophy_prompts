I've been trying to make sense of the absent-minded driver problem, but not the toy version where someone just says “there’s a 1/2 chance it’s the first exit, so what do you do?” I mean the version where the driver has a memory block and, whenever she comes to an exit, she cannot tell whether she is at the first or second one, but she knows she is following some fixed policy p for whether to exit at an exit. I keep seeing two different answers floating around. One treats the situation as a one-shot decision: at the exit she should compare the expected value of exiting versus not exiting, using her current credence about which exit she is at. The other treats it as a planning/diachronic problem: she should choose a policy before the trip that maximizes the overall chance of making the correct exit, and the “rational” action at the exit is just whatever the policy says. Can you compare these two ways of framing it?

I’m especially stuck on the probablity part. If the policy is “exit with probability p,” doesn’t the probability that the exit she is currently at is the first one depend on p, because if she had already passed the first exit she would only be at the second if she didn’t exit there? I want a concrete calculation, maybe with p = 0, 1/2, and 1, and an explanation of why the one-shot framing can look wrong or misleading. Also, is this really a problem for Bayesian updating, or is it just that the unit of rationality is the whole strategy rather than each individual decision?
