Write a short story related to aesthetic testimony. Set the story in a city where a newly discovered painting can be viewed by only one person at a time, and a public tribunal must decide whether the painting is beautiful in order to determine whether it may be displayed, sold, or returned to a private owner. The story should make the epistemic problem of aesthetic testimony central: several witnesses are sincere, competent, and free of obvious bias, but they disagree, and the narrator—who has not seen the painting—must decide whether testimony about the painting's beauty can give her any genuine reason to think the painting has that aesthetic character, or whether it can only give her evidence about the witnesses' experiences.

The story should dramatize, rather than lecture about, the distinction between testimony to an aesthetic experience and testimony to an aesthetic value. Include at least one scene in which a witness tries to report the painting's beauty without reducing it to a description of her own pleasure, and let the ending turn on whether the narrator comes to treat beauty as a property of the painting, a response-dependent feature of the painting-and-viewer relation, or a normatively regulated matter of testimony and authority.
