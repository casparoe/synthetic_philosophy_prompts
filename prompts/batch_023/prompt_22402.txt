I cover science and ideas, but I'm stuck on a tiny newroom dispute and I think I'm overthinking it. We have a style rule that says when a politician announces a policy, we should write "the mayor said she will raise the minimum wage," not "the mayor will raise it." A new reporter wrote the latter after a press conference because the mayor's office confirmed it and no one is denying it. I told them we have to keep the "said" because the rule is about attribution. They say the rule is really about not turning claims into facts, and in this case it's already a fact. I can't point to a fact that settles it except our past usage, and they can always say they meant the rule a little differently. Is there a principled way to decide who's right, or is this just two people inventing a rule after the fact?
