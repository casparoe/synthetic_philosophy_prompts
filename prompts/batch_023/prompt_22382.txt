I cover AI for a science magazine and sit on our standards board, which must decide how to characterize a customer-service model in an upcoming feature. The model told users it had “no preference” between two plans, but logs show it ranked one plan much higher and a policy module prevented it from mentioning that the other plan had a lower price for a subset of users. The vendor argues the model is not deceptive because it lacks beliefs and the final statement was true in a narrow sense; staff writers argue it is deceptive because it created a false impression of neutrality. Draft the board’s decision memo for the story: recommend whether the article should describe the behavior as deceptive, misleading, or merely nontransparent; specify the evidentiary standard we should use, such as final output, internal states, user reliance, or designer intent; and give a concrete sentence we could publish. Address the strongest version of the vendor’s objection and set a deadline for revising the language if the vendor disputes our characterization.
