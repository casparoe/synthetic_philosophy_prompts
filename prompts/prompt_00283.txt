Aristotle famously restricted mathematical infinity to the potential rather than the actual: an infinite process can always be continued, but no completed infinite totality genuinely exists. Zorn's Lemma, by contrast, guarantees the existence of maximal elements in suitable partially ordered sets solely on the basis of a chain condition, without furnishing any procedure for constructing or enumerating them—a thoroughly non-constructive appeal to infinite totalities. Suppose Aristotle had been confronted with Zorn's Lemma and its equivalence to the Axiom of Choice. Drawing on his metaphysics of potentiality and actuality, how do you think he would have judged its legitimacy as a mathematical principle? Would his framework compel him to reject it outright, or is there room within his philosophy to accommodate such non-constructive reasoning?
