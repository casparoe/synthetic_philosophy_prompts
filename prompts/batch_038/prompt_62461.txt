I started out trying to defend the separateness-of-persons objection against a utilitarian colleague and ended up with a chain that seems to destroy the objection instead. Stated as neutrally as I can:

(1) What makes utilitarian aggregation objectionable is that it balances losses to some against gains to others as though society were one subject making a trade-off within a single life — yet no one ever occupies the standpoint from which the sum is experienced, so on the social side nothing answers to the loss.

(2) A balance compensates only if one subject bears both sides of it.

(3) In the personal case there is no such further subject either: on the reductionist account of identity, what holds between me now and me in twenty years is psychological continuity and connectedness, which comes in degrees and can in principle branch.

(4) If the relation between two person-stages comes in degrees and can hold between people we would ordinarily count as distinct, then the stage that bears a cost and the stage that reaps the benefit are not strictly one subject.

(5) So the trade-offs of prudence — giving up a year to training, sitting through a painful operation for the sake of health afterward — fail the very test the objection presses on the utilitarian: they compensate no one.

(6) Therefore prudence is no more rationally required than benevolence, and the asymmetry between them is a prejudice.

(7) But the objection draws its force from that asymmetry: the personal case is supposed to be the one where compensation is genuine, and the social case the one where it is counterfeit.

(8) Hence the separateness-of-persons objection undermines itself, and one must give up either reductionism about personal identity or the objection.

I would much rather keep (1) and (3) and lose something else, but I cannot see which. Tell me where the chain breaks or which premise is least costly to surrender, explain why that premise looked unimpeachable while I was writing it down, and price the alternatives — what the objection, or the reductionism, has to pay if that is the step I drop.
