ok, roleplay exercise. Play a research-governance lead at a big lab — she's a true believer in the unilateralist's curse. Her view: when a bunch of smart, well-meaning people each hold their own noisy estimate of whether some action is net good, and each is free to act on their own estimate, you systematically get the optimistic tail — the action goes ahead whenever anyone thinks it's a good idea, so more capable would-be do-gooders makes things worse, not better. Her fix is pooling: nobody moves until the estimates have been laid out together and the pessimists have had their full say. She is not a luddite and not a coward, she genuinely wants the work done and done fast, she just wants it done after the information is in one room.

Rules: stay in character the whole time, never break out to narrate or summarize the debate, and keep each reply to roughly one paragraph so it feels like an actual conversation in a hallway. She can and should concede tactical points — procedure details, timelines, specific cases — but never the core claim. If I ask her something she couldn't reasonably know (outside facts, what other labs are doing, numbers), have her say so as the character rather than making stuff up. Don't cite papers or name philosophers at me.

My opening move, as a researcher on her team who wants to release something she wants to hold: your pooling argument only buys you anything if the errors are independent. The moment you stand up a permanent committee to vet everything, the committee reads the same memos, hires from the same places, and promotes people who already agreed, so their estimates stop being independent samples and start being one estimate repeated six times. Averaging correlated estimates doesn't cancel the noise, it just makes it sound unanimous. So I'm claiming your committee isn't the cure for the curse — it's a smaller group of unilateralists with a shared blind spot and a rubber stamp, and it's worse than my publishing because it's gauranteed to be confident. And relatedly: you treat delay as free and action as risky, but delay is an action too. Every quarter we sit on this, other groups with sloppier release practices are converging on it, and whatever harm you're trying to prevent arrives anyway, just without our name on the documentation. Answer both, then tell me what you actually want to do about my release request.
