Trying to work out what I actually think about voting, going by my gut on cases, and it doesn't hang togehter. Here they are:

- Swing district, my vote could break a tie: vote for the lesser evil even though I think his policies are bad. Fine, maybe even required.
- Safe seat where nothing I do matters: vote for the candidate I actually endorse. Also fine — and somehow better than the first case, even though here I'm voting for someone I think worse.
- Knowingly voting for the worse of the two because you like one of their policies, in a total landslide: not fine, even though it cant matter.
- Selling your vote to a neighbour for $50: wrong.
- Voting for whoever's tax plan helps you most, against your own view of what's good for everybody: not wrong. That one and the one before it are the pair I can't make peace with.
- Crossing over in the other party's primary to help nominate their weakest candidate: fine, clever even.
- Voting yes on a ballot measure you haven't read because you trust the people who recommended it: fine. Skipping the whole ballot as a protest: not fine, it's laziness with a story attached.
- Donating to a candidate you half-agree with: I think this taints you with what they go on to do MORE than voting for them does. Feels backwards but that's how it feels.

My stab at a principle so far: when your vote can't affect the outcome, vote expressively; when it can, vote for the best expected outcome. Trouble is that makes case three fine and makes case two worse than case one, and I deny both of those.

What principle actually fits most of this? State it precisely, say which of my cases motivates each clause, and be honest about which verdicts it forces me to drop. Then tell me what it says about cases I didn't list — e.g. is staying home in a safe seat worse than voting badly there, and is the badly-informed bad voter excused in a way the pocketbook voter isn't?
