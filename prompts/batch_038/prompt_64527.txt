I'm writing the spec for a welfare metric we'd use to rank farmed-animal interventions by how much good they do, and the whole team keeps assuming "pleasure" is one thing you can put on a single scale and add up across species and across time. Before I commit to that, I want the requirements list: what does a theory of pleasure actually have to deliver to be usable this way? Give me the phenomena it has to explain (a warm bath and finally cracking a proof both count as pleasant and don't feel remotely alike), the distinctions it can't collapse — wanting versus liking, pleasure versus absence of pain, being pleased by an activity versus being pleased that something is the case — the empirical stuff it can't contradict, like hedonic adaptation and the fact that most of our subjects will never report anything, and the hard cases it has to get a handle on: perverse pleasures, being pleased about something false, the experience machine. For each, say why it's a genuine requirement here and not just something one camp happens to care about, and tell me which single requirement knocks out the most candidate theories. And if some subset can't all be satisfied at once — I suspect needing one uniform scale and needing the value of a pleasure to track its object pull against each other — say so bluntly, because that would sink the project. Skip the survey of existing views, I'll do that after I know what I'm asking of them.
