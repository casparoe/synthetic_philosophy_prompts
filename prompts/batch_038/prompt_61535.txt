Setup, briefly: everyone agrees perception and inference are in. Then Nyāya says four, adding upamāna — the gavaya case, where you're told a wild ox is like a cow, later you see one in the forest and thereby learn what the word picks out — and śabda, testimony. Buddhists say two. Jains say three. Kumārila's Mīmāṃsakas say six, adding arthāpatti (Devadatta is fat but doesn't eat by day, so he must eat at night) and anupalabdhi for absences, where they think you need a whole separate source just to know the pot isn't on the floor and Nyāya thinks you just see that. Nobody has moved in fifteen hundred years. Every reduction argument and every anti-reduction reply is on the table and known to both sides, and the "how many are there" question just gets restated by each new generation.

My friend's diagnosis, which I want you to actually stress-test rather than politely agree with: the number question was never a real question, because the camps individuate pramāṇas differently and never admit it. Dignāga counts by kinds of object — two kinds of object, particulars and universals, so two means of knowing, closed. Nyāya counts by whether the resulting cognition could have been produced by some other pramāṇa, i.e. irreducibility of the knowledge-event. Kumārila counts by distinct epistemic job, what gets you out of a bind that nothing else gets you out of. On his reading "there are four" and "there are two" are both true relative to their own criterion, so it's a taxonomy fight wearing an epistemology costume. Then he went further and said the real split underneath is metaphysical: Nyāya treats knowledge as a produced episode, a quality of the self brought about by a causal chain, so provenance is what's interesting and you individuate by cause; Buddhists deny the self and treat cognitions as momentary events, so what matters is whether the cognition tracks its object, and you individuate by object. Counting is downstream of that.

I said that was too neat, because the gavaya case looks like a substantive fight with a right answer to it: can testimony transmit what a word means to somebody who doesn't yet know what it means? Both camps claim to handle the guy in the forest and they disagree about that, which doesn't look like a difference in how you're counting units. He said I was smuggeling in the Nyāya criterion. Unresolved, obviously.

So diagnose it. Verbal dispute, different questions under one label, genuinely substantive but hard, or ill-posed? Use the actual reduction moves and the actual replies as evidence for your diagnosis, not as scenery. Is his criteria story right, or does it explain too much — could you run the same "they're just counting differently" move on any disagreement you like? Does the metaphysics-downstream step survive my forest objection, and did either of us win that exchange? And then tell me what would count as progress here: a distinction someone could draw, a case that comes out the same way under all three criteria, a change of question — or argue straight out that no progress is possible and why. That last one is more useful to me than a hedge.
