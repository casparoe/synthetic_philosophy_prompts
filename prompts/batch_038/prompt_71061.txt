I sit on the investment committee of a small endowment, and next month we must decide whether to retain a manager whose largest winning position was built on reasoning he cannot now reconstruct and which our own analysts judged unsound at the time. Half the committee holds that the outcome settles the matter; the other half holds that we would simply be rewarding luck. Before I let either side invoke epistemology, I want the specification written down.

Write the requirements that any account of why knowledge is worth more than mere true belief must satisfy to be usable in a decision like ours. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Among the things the account must cope with, please make sure the following appear: the familiar swamping difficulty — that once a belief is true, its having been produced reliably seems to add nothing to its value; the ancient concession that a true belief serves exactly as well as knowledge for as long as it lasts, with the difference located in stability rather than in present usefulness (say whether your list requires an account to preserve that concession or to overturn it); cases in which knowing is worse for the knower than remaining ignorant; true beliefs produced by processes that would have gone wrong in very nearby situations; and the distinction between what a state is worth to the person in it and what it is worth to a group that relies on him.

One requirement I want tested hard: whether the extra value of knowledge is available only in hindsight. If it is, can it do any work at all in an assessment that is necessarily made before the bet resolves?

Structure the answer as: **Purpose**, one paragraph on what you take the account to be for; **The requirements**, numbered, each with its motivating case; **Non-negotiable or camp preference**, a short pass marking each; **The unsatisfiable subset**, if there is one — that is the part I most want; **The most exclusionary requirement**, a sentence or two; **Contenders in brief**, three or four standard answers scored against the list. Nine hundred to twelve hundred words. No citations or reading list — I can find the sources; it is the criteria I lack.
