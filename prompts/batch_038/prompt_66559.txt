Ran into a weird argument at work. We refactored a long-running service: same data, same endpoints, mostly the same code, but new artifact, new name, new repo. Question was whether the on-call history carries over, and one of the senior guys said no — "the statue isn't the clay, same matter, different object" — meaning we need two separate entries in the service registry.

I'm an engineer, zero philosophy background, but I'm pretty sure that one-liner is carrying way more weight than it can hold. So what are the actually-distinct claims someone could be making with "the statue isn't the clay"? I can think of a few — that the statue and the lump are literally two objects in the same place at once, that they differ in what could happen to them (clay survives getting squashed, statue doesn't), that identity depends on how your counting things, that it's just vague — but I don't know which of those are truism, which are real metaphysical commitments, and which are just false.

Then the part I actually care about: does ANY of those readings license giving one continuously-running thing two registry IDs, or is he just dressing up a policy preference? And which reading do you think he's reaching for?
