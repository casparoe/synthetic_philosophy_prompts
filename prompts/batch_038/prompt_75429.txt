ok so I'm a junior and my philosophy club does this monthly thing where we each write a fake document and everybody has to guess what real debate it's secretly about. mine is the situationism vs virtue ethics fight — the one where social psych experiments are supposed to show character traits aren't real, or at least aren't the global cross-situation things aristotle-flavored people think they are. I want to do mine as a customer service chat transcript.

setup: a boutique online retailer that sells virtues as literal products. you order something like "courage, practical grade" and it ships. a customer wants a refund because their courage stopped working. the support agent denies the claim — and every reason the agent gives has to map onto a real finding or a real argument from the situationist literature. the hurry manipulation with the seminary students, the dime in the phone booth and the cookies mood studies, the old classroom-cheating experiments where kids were honest in one setting and not another, the low cross-situational consistency numbers, that kind of thing. a specialist should read the warranty exclusions and go "oh that's the phone booth one" without the piece ever naming a researcher, a study, or a year. no citations, no bibliography, the agent cites internal company QA protocols instead of journal articles.

it needs to include:
- the transcript itself, around 700 words, deadpan throughout, timestamps are fine
- an itemized warranty-exclusions section that the agent pastes into the chat partway through, where each separate exclusion encodes a distinct situationist move — global vs local traits, situation features doing the causal work, mood and time pressure counting as causes, "character" as a folk category, whatever else you want in there
- an escalation to a supervisor who takes the harder line than the first agent
- a closing satisfaction survey with one free text box at the very end

the part I care most about: don't make it a one-sided dunk on virtue ethics. the customer can't be a rube. their comebacks have to be actual replies virtue ethicists make — habituation, virtue as a skill you get better at through practice, practical wisdom being about perceiving what THIS situation calls for rather than applying a rule, and the point that of course helping varies with hurry because the hurry changed what the situation required. and somewhere the piece should surface one genuine problem FOR situationism, like that if traits are really that situation-bound then the retailer's whole "buy yourself a better character" business model is self-undermining, or that telling customers they have no character might make them behave worse. end it so a reader genuinely can't tell who won the argument.

the jokes should come from the philosophy and the psychology being exactly right, not from wacky product names — tho a couple of dry product names are fine. and please don't wink at the reader anywhere, keep it straight-faced the whole time. sry this is so long, I just have a specific thing in my head.
