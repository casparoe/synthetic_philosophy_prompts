(3) So for any actual outcome O there is a huge set of nearby starting conditions, differing from the real ones only in trivial respects, that lead to outcomes nothing like O.

(5) Therefore no explanation of a specific historical outcome is adequate. History can describe, not explain.
(6) And this isn't a special problem about the past. The same reasoning runs on any specific outcome of any amplifying process: the exact ordering of a shuffled deck, the exact landfall point of a hurricane, the exact 537-vote margin in a close election.
