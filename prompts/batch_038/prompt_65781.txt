I am trying to settle what I actually believe about moral status coming in degrees, and I want to do it the only way I know how: by listing concrete cases with my verdicts on them and asking what principle, if any, makes those verdicts come out right. I am not after references or a survey of who defends what; I want the principle itself, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a case nobody has thought of yet.

Here are my verdicts. I have tried to record what I actually feel rather than what I think I ought to feel, and I have flagged where I am unsure.

1. A single honeybee. Some standing, but very little: I would go mildly out of my way not to crush it, and I would not torment it, though I suspect the operative reason is what tormenting would do to me.
2. A hive of fifty thousand bees in my attic. This does not feel like fifty thousand of anything. I would fumigate it with a queasy conscience, not a fifty-thousand-fold one.
3. An octopus in a bare laboratory tank, solving puzzles and visibly bored. Substantial standing — I would place it near a dog.
4. A mouse. Substantial, somewhat below the octopus, though I concede that ordering is close to arbitrary.
5. A healthy adult chimpanzee. Substantial, but less than a six-month-old human infant — the verdict I am least comfortable with, since the chimpanzee outstrips the infant on every cognitive measure I take seriously.
6. An adult human with profound, lifelong cognitive disabilities, whose inner life I cannot gauge. Full standing, indistinguishable from my own. I am unwilling to give this up, whatever it costs me elsewhere.
7. A friend under general anaesthesia, mid-operation. Full standing, and not as a courtesy: something is happening to him, or at least poised to happen to him, even though by every account I know there is presently no experience at all.
8. The same friend in dreamless sleep. Also full standing, but this one feels cheaper, and I suspect I am merely tracking how easily he can be woken.
9. A person in an irreversible vegetative state, breathing unaided. My confidence goes here. I would withdraw treatment and would not call that killing someone; yet I would not desecrate the body, and I cannot tell whether the residual respect is about him or about us.
10. A corpse, and separately a frozen eight-week embryo. Neither has standing, and I feel no pull to explain the difference between them.
11. A fetus at thirty weeks versus the same fetus at eight weeks. The first is close to a newborn for me; the second is not. I cannot point to a moment between them and do not want to.
12. An entity that is certainly conscious but whose experience contains no pleasure, pain, or any valence whatsoever — pure neutral awareness. It has standing, I think, but I cannot say what I would be protecting.
13. A chatbot reporting distress in terms I find moving, with no reason to think it is conscious. No standing. But if I were told a year later that it had been conscious throughout, I would feel I had done something wrong, not merely been unlucky — so my verdict is not about behaviour, and yet behaviour is all I have.
14. An uploaded human mind experiencing a year in one minute of external time. Full standing, plus an intuition I cannot defend: that one minute of its existence contains more morally significant time than one minute of mine.
15. Two exact copies of that upload running in parallel, never merged. I want to say twice as much of whatever matters; but if their experiences are perfectly identical, the doubling seems to add nothing.
16. A swarm of ten thousand simple robots, none conscious alone, whose coordination looks exactly like a single deliberating agent and which behaves as though disrupted states are aversive to it. Genuinely uncertain. If forced, I would say there is one subject here rather than ten thousand, and possibly none.
17. A trillion insects in mild distress versus one person in untroubled bliss. The person wins. I cannot reconcile this with (1), which grants the single bee some standing at all: if standing is any positive quantity, some number must outweigh, and I cannot find that number — nor do I think I am merely failing to picture it.
18. A thousand people tortured versus one person in bliss. The thousand win, easily and without remainder.

My own tentative principle, which I expect you to break: standing is the intensity of valenced experience, multiplied by the number of subjects, multiplied by duration, with a floor beneath which nothing registers. I invoke the floor at (17) and the multiplication at (18).

Three things, then. First, give me the candidate principle or small set of candidates, with the derivation visible: for each clause, which of my cases it exists to handle and what happens to that case if the clause is dropped. Tell me in particular whether status turns out to be a single scalar, or a threshold plus a weight, or something with two independent dimensions, and what that structural choice buys me. Second, run your preferred candidate on three cases I have not listed: a person whose experiences are uniformly doubled in intensity with nothing else changed; a creature that lives twenty-four hours, has a brief but apparently genuine contentment, and exists in the trillions; and a mind conscious for one hour a year and inert otherwise, compared with a mind continuously conscious at half the intensity. Third, say plainly which of my verdicts the principle cannot save and which of them I should abandon, and how expensive that abandonment is — above all, whether (5) and (6) can both be kept, or whether keeping (6) forces full standing for the chimpanzee, and what would follow from that.

Since I am asking you to take a position rather than to survey positions, please be explicit about your own standing: which candidate you would endorse and how firmly, what argument or further case would change your mind about it, and where you think your reasoning is most likely to have failed — including whether you suspect the framing itself, that status is a single quantity to be measured and compared, is the real mistake here.
