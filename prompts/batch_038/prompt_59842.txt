could you write me an agony-aunt column for a local paper — the reader's letter and the reply, about 550 words all in?

The letter is signed by the chair of a parole board, a thoroughgoing retributivist: she sentences people to what they deserve and never minds the consquences. She's just clocked that the man she's kept inside for thirty-odd years remembers nothing of the offence, has none of the temper or beliefs that led to it, and is by every measure a different person — so she asks whether she's been punishing somebody who doesn't exist, and whether that makes her a jailer of the innocent.

Make her articulate and sincere, not a strawman: she should be able to explain why she once kept a man locked up during a riot outside that his release would probably have ended, and mean it. The agony aunt replies in breezy consequentialist terms, and her advice should be perfectly coherent and quietly monstrous — she never once engages with the identity problem, and offers exactly three numbered suggestions. The laughs have to come from getting the penal theory right (desert against deterrence, proportionality, the gap between justifying the practice and justifying this particular sentence) so that a philosopher of punishment chuckles rather than winces. No real philosophers named, nothing cited.
