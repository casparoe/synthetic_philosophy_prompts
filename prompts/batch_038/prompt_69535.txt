I work on probabilistic modeling, and most of the last several years of my work has been on deep latent-variable models: variational autoencoders and the identifiability results surrounding them, posterior collapse, reparameterization, exponential-family observation models, and the general distinction between a parameter that is recoverable from the data-generating process and one that merely indexes an equivalent redescription of it. My physics stops at undergraduate quantum mechanics, and I have no philosophy of science. I am trying to understand the ψ-ontology debate in quantum foundations — the argument over whether the wavefunction is a state of the world or a state of knowledge about the world — and I keep reaching for analogies in my own field, which I suspect are partly misleading.

The framework as I understand it: Harrigan and Spekkens classify hidden-variable models according to whether the quantum state is ontic or epistemic, calling a model ψ-ontic if "every complete physical state or ontic state in the theory is consistent with only one pure quantum state" and ψ-epistemic if "there exist ontic states that are consistent with more than one pure quantum state" — equivalently, a question of whether the distributions over ontic states associated with distinct pure states have disjoint or overlapping supports. The no-go theorem of Pusey, Barrett, and Rudolph is then said to rule out ψ-epistemic models, conditional on an assumption of preparation independence: systems prepared independently have independent physical states. On the other side, there are ψ-epistemic constructions, Spekkens' toy theory most prominently, that reproduce a restricted fragment of quantum statistics, together with arguments from preparation noncontextuality that push in the ψ-epistemic direction.

My tentative translation, which I want you to stress-test rather than merely elaborate: the ontic state λ is a latent variable; a preparation fixes a conditional prior over λ; a measurement is a decoder yielding the outcome distribution given λ; ψ-ontic means the latent is identifiable from the output distribution and ψ-epistemic means it is not, so that one and the same observable behaviour is compatible with several latents; and the PBR theorem is an identifiability result whose force depends on an independence assumption about the prior, in the manner of assuming factorization across independent samples.

Please carry out the translation properly and then give me a residue report.

(a) Restate the definitions, the theorem, and the ψ-epistemic escape routes in my vocabulary, being precise about where the structural match genuinely holds: what corresponds to the support of a distribution over latents, what corresponds to a decoder, and what in my vocabulary, if anything, corresponds to an ontic state's being a state of the world rather than a posit internal to a model.

(b) Say explicitly whether my tentative gloss survives. In particular: is "ψ-ontic equals identifiable" correct, or does it smuggle something in? Non-identifiability in my field is normally treated as a defect to be repaired by adding structure — auxiliary variables, non-Gaussianity, side information, multiple views — whereas overlap of λ-distributions is presented in this debate as the attractive option. Is that a difference of substance or only of valence?

(c) Itemize the residue: which claims carry over cleanly, which carry over only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all. I especially want the preparation-independence assumption examined — whether it corresponds to a factorized prior over independent data points, or whether it presupposes something about the physical separability of preparations that has no model-theoretic analogue — and the "knowledge" in ψ-epistemic: knowledge held by whom, given that on my usage a non-identifiable latent encodes no one's knowledge of anything.

(d) End with a judgment rather than a shrug. Does the residue show that my vocabulary is missing a distinction — something like the difference between a model's internal bookkeeping and the structure of the world — or that the ψ-ontology debate is largely a redescription of familiar facts about non-identifiability, elevated into metaphysics?
