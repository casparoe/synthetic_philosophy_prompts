I am drafting a paper on spectrum arguments and want to use my colleague R as a case study, because her verdicts are unusually well documented and unusually hard to systematize. Here is the record, each item affirmed to me more than once.

1. One person tortured for fifty years versus a billion people each given a one-hour migraine: the second is worse, all things considered.
2. Take a chain running from the torture to the migraines in which, at each step, intensity falls imperceptibly and number rises imperceptibly. For any adjacent pair, the later member is *better* than the earlier — she insists on "better," not "no worse than." She has affirmed this for a dozen sampled pairs, including pairs at both ends of the chain.
3. She holds that transitivity is constitutive of "better than," that she retracts none of her pairwise verdicts, and that she cannot explain how all of this is compatible with (1).
4. An eighty-year life that is very good throughout versus the same life plus one further good second: the longer is better, but she would not bet on it, and she would not fault a committee that chose the shorter.
5. Two job candidates, one of whom is by stipulation very slightly better on the only relevant dimension: she would flip a coin, and she does not regard that as a failure of rationality.

The case I intend to put to her is a money pump. An agent offers her the entire chain as a sequence of swaps: she begins holding the torture outcome and is offered each successive outcome in turn, every swap costing her one dollar, until she holds the billion migraines; the agent then offers to walk her back to the start on the same terms.

Predict what she says and does. Will she accept the swaps, and if she refuses, which of her own verdicts does she surrender, and how can she justify that given (3)? Then tell me which view best explains the whole record: a Parfit- or Chang-style appeal to imprecision or parity, on which (2) overstates the relation; an epistemicist reading, on which one step in (2) is false though she cannot locate it; a context-sensitivity reading, on which (1) and (2) were never answers to a single question; a Temkin- or Rachels-style intransitivism that she disowns but is nevertheless running, in which case she simply pays; or a view on which choice need not track betterness, or on which she can bind herself against the sequence. Say what each predicts about the pump, then commit to a best guess. Finally, propose one further case — one she has not already ruled on — that would discriminate between the two readings you find most plausible, and say whether (5) is evidence for either.
