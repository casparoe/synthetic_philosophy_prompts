I'm preparing a seminar on the ontology of generations and have run into a pattern in the popular and consulting literature that I can't attach to any term I know. Suppose a cohort label is introduced with traits that are asserted rather than measured — that people born within a given window are distinctively disloyal to employers, or distinctively entrepreneurial. The label then circulates through journalism, HR practice, and advertising; members of the cohort begin describing themselves in those terms, including on surveys administered under the label; and later writers cite those surveys as independent evidence that the traits were present before the label arrived. The classification is thus credited with having discovered a real kind, when what it did was produce the warrant for itself. What interests me is the way the loop closes through the evidential record: the reactive effect is not merely a side consequence but is retroactively read as prior justification. There are familiar neighbors — the general thought that some classifications change what they classify, and the older one about predictions that bring themselves true — but in both of those the question whether the kind is genuine stays open, whereas here the reaction is what settles it. Does what I've described have a name? If so, tell me how well the term fits and where my description outruns it; if not, coin one, define it, and draw its edges. I'd want the boundaries especially: does the phenomenon cover a stereotype that becomes true of a group without having been caused by the label, or a market category that shapes the products it sorts, or ordinary reliance on self-reported attitudes? Please don't stop at a label — the carving is the point.
