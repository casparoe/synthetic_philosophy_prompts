Consider a toy model of doxastic justification: the warrant a belief inherits from an inferential chain is the product of the warrant of that chain's terminal support and a retention factor r < 1 for each link traversed, and where several chains support the same belief, its warrant is the greatest of theirs (or the sum, if you prefer that aggregation rule). Score foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism on the fraction of an ordinary empirical belief's warrant that survives in the limit under this model — a number between 0 and 1 for each, plus an overall ranking — and identify which structural features do the real work in your scores: depth of chain, the presence of cycles, the number of independent supporting chains, or the availability of revisable starting points.

Then assess whether the model is neutral between the three or quietly prejudges the contest. Which of them could deny multiplicative decay without special pleading? And can infinitism be rescued in a principled way — letting r approach 1 as reasons accumulate, or treating warrant as additive across infinitely many chains — or does every such rescue concede that the infinite chain as such transmits nothing, collapsing the view into a species of coherentism?
