I am reconstructing Berkeley's master argument — the passage in the Principles in which he invites the objector to imagine trees in a park, or books in a closet, with nobody by to perceive them, and then insists the objector has shown only that he can frame ideas in his own mind — and I have run into a knot I cannot untangle. I would like an explanation of the conceptual machinery, not a survey of the literature and not a reading list.

Here is my apparatus. Let `C(s, p)` mean that mind `s` conceives that `p`, where conceiving is treated as an act with propositional content; let `E(x)` mean that `x` exists; let `P(x)` mean that `x` is perceived by some mind. The materialist's target content, for some sensible object `t`, is `C(s, E(t) & ~P(t))`. The argument I want to examine is:

- **(M1)** If it is possible that `p`, then it is possible for some mind to conceive that `p`.
- **(M2)** It is not possible for any mind to conceive that `E(t) & ~P(t)`.
- **(M3)** Therefore it is not possible that `E(t) & ~P(t)` — no sensible object can exist unperceived.

Berkeley's support for (M2) is twofold: that the objector has merely framed ideas of trees while *omitting* to frame the idea of a perceiver, and omission is not the conceiving of an absence; and the ideational constraint that whatever is conceived is an idea, and an idea is by nature perceived.

Please work through the following, using my notation.

1. Explain why the argument requires the direction of (M1) that I have written — possibility entailing conceivability — and why the far more familiar converse principle, that conceivability entails possibility, is no use to Berkeley here. What kind of account of our epistemic access to modality would underwrite the premise he actually needs, and could a materialist accept that premise for ordinary empirical cases while rejecting it in this one without special pleading?

2. Distinguish three claims that (M2) might be taken to express: that no mind can conceive *of* `t` that it exists unperceived; that no mind can entertain the content `E(t) & ~P(t)`; and that any content which asserts of the act entertaining it that it is not being entertained is thereby false in that act. Explain why the third is trivially true but seems to do no metaphysical work at all, given that self-refuting contents can perfectly well be possible contents — consider something along the lines of "no one is now entertaining this thought." Then say what would have to be added to get from the act-relativity of the third claim to the modal conclusion in (M3), and whether the first claim fares any better or simply relocates the problem.

3. Suppose the ideational constraint is doing the real work, converting the act of conceiving into a perceiving and so making (M2) true. Now bring in Berkeley's doctrine that we apprehend minds and their operations by notion rather than by idea. Explain what happens to the argument if notion-based apprehension counts as a way of conceiving, and explain the materialist's rejoinder: that his unperceived tree was never offered as a sensory image but as something notionally grasped, a posit whose content is fixed by the explanatory role it plays in accounting for the order and regularity of our ideas. Is that rejoinder a legitimate deployment of Berkeley's own resources, or an equivocation on "conceive"?

4. Draw the line between defeating the materialist's counterexample — showing he has not performed the feat he claimed — and establishing the positive thesis that nothing sensible can exist unperceived. Explain why the omission reply appears to secure only the former, and what the argument would look like if the two were properly separated.

5. Finally, set out in my notation the strongest valid reconstruction you can produce, identify the single premise a Berkeleyan should be least reluctant to surrender, and state whether the residual dispute is substantive or turns in part on the word "conceive."

If my formalization misrepresents Berkeley at some point — if, for instance, the conclusion he wants is not what (M3) says — say so explicitly and correct it rather than working within my frame.
