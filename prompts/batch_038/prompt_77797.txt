Three hours last night arguing with a friend about Gödel, Escher, Bach and I still can't tell who won. Her line: the move from a formula that encodes its own unprovability to a mind that's aware of itself is a metaphor Hofstadter smuggles in as an argument — coding gives you a sentence a formal system cant prove, and nothing about that licenses the claim that a brain doing something structurally similar thereby has an I. I don't buy it, but I couldn't say what would settle it, and thats the part bugging me.

So take the strange-loop account as my position and map how exposed it actually is. What would count against the claim that a self is the same kind of thing as a self-referential Gödel sentence? Rank the defeaters, fatal versus merely awkward, and say which ones a committed defender would themselves concede as damaging rather than wave away.

Work through these in particular:
- the left-hand side problem. The theorem needs a specific, recursively specifiable system with enough arithmetic, plus consistency. Nobody can point to the corresponding system for a person — so what exactly is the analogy pairing up with?
- the diagonal construction is mechanical and happens inside the system. What is this capacity to step outside that a person supposedly has and a machine lacks — and if a machine can run the same step, is the analogy dead or does it just relocate?
- iterating the step: build a system that asserts the consistency of the previous one and keep going along a computable ordering. Does that rescue the view or drain it of content?
- what if self-models turn out to be largely confabulation, doing no causal work in the behaviour they claim to explain? Does the loop survive that, and would it be a different kind of failure from the ones above?
- the deflation worry: if a self-model is allowed to be partial and approximate instead of exact like the coding is, isnt that just what any information-processing account already says? What does the tangled hierarchy add that a plain functionalist story doesn't?

Then give me an honest verdict: does the view risk anything at all, or has it been adjusted until it's compatible with every possible outcome — a redescription of consciousness rather than a theory of it? If it has, say whether thats fatal for a claim of this kind or just what such a claim is.
