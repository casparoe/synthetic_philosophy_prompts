I'm TAing a political philosophy seminar on freedom and I want to hand out a case that actually makes Pettit's non-domination bite, because every stock example (kindly master, benevolent despot, 1950s housewife) my students just wave off with "well, obviously that situation is bad." So: construct a thought experiment in which Berlin's negative liberty and a positive/self-mastery account both say the person is free, and republican non-domination says they aren't. Constraints: contemporary realistic setting — a workplace, a family, a clinic, whatever, but no slavery and no dictators; one paragraph, under ~150 words; the would-be dominator never actually interferes, makes no threats, and sincerely and reliably tracks thier interests (so the unfreedom can't be smuggled in via cruelty or bad outcomes); and the person has to be fully autonomous in the positive sense — no compulsion, no addiction, no distorted second-order desires, they reflectively endorse their choices.

Then do the checking work. (a) Say what each of the three accounts says and why. (b) Explain what seperates domination from mere dependence here — is it the arbitrariness of the power, the absence of an exit, or something else? (c) Name the one feature of the case doing the work. (d) Tell me whether adding a written, costless, one-day-notice exit clause collapses the case or just weakens it. (e) How would a Berlinian who thinks non-domination is just negative liberty with a longer time horizon attack your case? Also flag whether Pettit's eyeball test gives the verdict you want. American English please, and don't bother with citations — I've read the books, I just need the case to work.
