I spent thirty-eight years in condensed matter physics, mostly doing neutron and X-ray scattering on ordered solids, and have only taken up philosophy seriously since retiring. My reading group is currently working through the argument associated with Rietdijk and Putnam that special relativity defeats presentism: observers in relative motion disagree about which distant events are occurring now, both descriptions are equally correct, and so if the events simultaneous with me are real, so too are those simultaneous with you — and by chaining such disagreements one apparently sweeps in the whole of past and future.

The member who presented the argument leaned throughout on the popular "loaf" picture: spacetime is a loaf of bread, each inertial observer slices it at a slightly different angle, and therefore the whole loaf exists at once, so that anyone insisting only one slice is really there has mistaken the orientation of their own knife for a feature of the bread. He took the picture to license two conclusions: that temporal passage is an illusion, and that the metaphysical question is settled by physics.

I have a candidate replacement drawn from my own working life. A crystal is a single object, yet one may index it along any of various families of lattice planes; which family one chooses is a matter of orientation and convenience, not a fact about the stone, and no family is more real than another. On this telling, spacetime is the crystal and a simultaneity convention is a family of lattice planes.

Please audit both analogies, and give me the following:

1. A correspondence table for each — source term mapped to target term — including, explicitly marked, those features of the source that have no counterpart in the target.
2. The disanalogies, separated into those that genuinely threaten the intended use (establishing that the four-dimensional whole is already there, and so doing metaphysical work rather than merely illustrating a formalism) and those that are superficial and can be set aside.
3. A statement of what each analogy smuggles in. In particular, does the loaf's status as a completed object sitting in an embedding space presuppose the eternalism it is offered to prove? Does my crystal fare any better on that count, given that a crystal is also a finished object with a determinate structure?
4. A verdict on whether the presenter's conclusions survive the audit, and whether my version improves matters, makes them worse, or merely relocates the difficulty to a different piece of imagery.
5. If you believe a better analogy exists for this argument, name it and sketch it in two or three sentences; if you believe no analogy can carry this weight, say so plainly.

Finally, address briefly whether the situation changes when one passes from special relativity to the curved spacetimes of general relativity, where a global inertial slicing may not be available at all.

Please write in American English and keep your answer to a maximum of 800 words, tables included. I would rather have precision than encouragement: where you are uncertain about a point of physics or of the dialectic, flag it instead of filling the gap.
