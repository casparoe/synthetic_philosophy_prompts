ok so this has been eating at me for like two months and I can't figure out what I'm supposed to think about it.

I'm a junior. I'm good at chem and bio, like weirdly good, and last year our school's after-school tutoring program got cut when the district lost a chunk of funding, so a bunch of freshmen were just failing. I started staying late on tuesdays to help them. Free. Nobody asked me to, there was no form, no volunteer hours, no award, I just did it because it felt dumb not to.

Word got around. Now it's like 14 kids every tuesday. Two other juniors show up sometimes but mostly it's me and I'm the one who knows where the practice tests are. In the fall our counselor put it in the school newsletter and called it "our peer tutoring program" and my name is on the spring sign-up sheet, and she told some scholarship committee that I *run* it, which I don't, I just show up.

Here's the thing. I got a job. Saturday shifts at a garden center, and I actually need the money because my mom's hours got cut in January. Tuesday after school is the only extra shift they'll give me. So I have to stop the tutoring. There's no version where I do both.

When I told my counselor she went really quiet and then said "well, we'll figure something out," in a voice that meant we won't. The freshmen have their midterm in three weeks. One of the kids texted me "wait are you actually leaving??" like I broke a promise to him. I never promised anything. I never signed up for anything. I did this because it was extra and I wanted to.

But I feel worse than I would if I had actually made a promise and broken it. My dad says I'm not responsible for a thing I volunteered for, and that the school is using me as an excuse to not pay a real tutor, and honestly he might be right about that part. My best friend says that once people depend on you it doesn't matter what you originally agreed to, and "but it was extra!" isn't a defense.

So — is it actually wrong for me to quit, or do I just feel bad? And how did doing something that nobody could have demanded of me turn into something I'm now expected to do and get treated like a jerk for not doing? If I'd never started I'd be completely in the clear, which basically means I'm worse off for being the one who helped. Does that mean I shouldn't have started? Do you tell people not to do the good thing because of how it ends?

Also small thing but I keep thinking about it: do I have to go tell the scholarship people that I don't run the program? I didn't lie. I just didn't correct her.
