I am drafting the internal rules by which a mid-sized worker-owned firm will distribute its annual surplus among members, and before I get to the mechanics I want to settle which properties of persons could ground a claim to a larger share. I have assembled a small framework of my own, and I would like you to apply it as stated rather than to substitute a better one.

Six candidate grounds of desert:

1. Exertion — the hours and intensity of labor actually supplied.
2. Marginal product — the measurable contribution of one's work to the firm's revenue.
3. Improvement — the gain one produces relative to one's own starting capacity, so that a slow worker who has nearly doubled their competence scores highly.
4. Opportunity cost borne — what one has given up elsewhere (a foregone higher wage, deferred training, risked savings) in order to be here.
5. Transferable capacity built — investment in skills, routines, and knowledge that remain available to the firm and to coworkers after the individual leaves.
6. Judgment relied upon — being the person whose decisions others in the firm act on and are therefore exposed to.

Three tests, each scored from 0 (fails outright) to 10 (survives unaided):

A. Attribution — does the ground track something of which the agent is the right sort of author, and does it survive the familiar objection that even the willingness to exert oneself is a product of fortunate circumstances and endowment, without collapsing into the conclusion that nothing at all is deserved?
B. Measurement — could a third party assess it at acceptable cost, without the assessment being cheaply gamed and without distorting the activity being measured?
C. Reciprocity — could those members whose labor makes the exercise of this ground possible accept the resulting inequality without that acceptance compromising their standing as equals in the firm?

What I want back:

- A score for each of the six grounds on each of the three tests, as a grid. No ranges, and no ties within a test unless you state explicitly that the tie is real rather than a refusal to choose.
- A tier list of the grounds by overall standing, with one sentence per ground identifying which of the three tests does the decisive work for it.
- A single number: the fraction of a fixed surplus pool that should be distributed in proportion to the top-ranked ground, with the remainder divided equally among all members. Defend the number in three sentences or fewer.
- Anywhere you think two of my grounds are not genuinely distinct from one another, say so, propose the merger, and report what it does to the ordering.

Please be committed: I want your scores and your fraction, not a survey of what different traditions would say. Then tell me which single entry in your grid you regard as most likely to be wrong, and what consideration would overturn it. Finally — and I mean this — if you think the framework is defective, if one of my tests is mis-specified, if a ground is unintelligible as I have stated it, or if you think ranking grounds of desert is a confused enterprise in a way that no ranking could respect, say that plainly at the top of your reply rather than scoring politely. I would much rather have that objection than a tidy grid I cannot trust.
