I teach a philosophy of mathematics seminar and for the week on set-theoretic foundations I want to hand out something that's actually funny rather than a joke-on-a-stick. Can you write me a parody help-center article for a fictional company that sells Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory as a subscription service, where the Axiom of Choice is an optional paid add-on? I'm picturing: a title, a short "is Choice right for me?" intro, a tier comparison (base ZF, ZF + countable choice, ZF + dependent choice, full AC Enterprise), five or six FAQ items, a "known issues" box, and a see-also footer. Around 700 words.

The target is the working mathematician's breezy assumption that Choice is a harmless convenience, plus the marketing voice of foundations talk generally — not any real person or school. So the humor has to come from getting the mathematics right: that Choice, Zorn's lemma and the well-ordering principle are sold as three products but turn out to be the same product; that Tychonoff needs the full plan while Hahn–Banach gets by on a cheaper one; that you can pick from infinitely many pairs of shoes without the add-on but not from infinitely many pairs of socks; that enabling it silently ships non-measurable sets and one sphere becoming five; that the add-on is independant of the base system, so support can't tell you whether you actually have it or are just not noticing; and that some competitor is running a model where all sets of reals are measurable. A set theorist sitting in the room should laugh rather than wince, and a first-year philosophy student should still get the shape of it.

Keep it deadpan — the company never concedes anything is wrong, nothing steps outside the format to explain the joke, and the objections should be felt through the fine print rather than stated. Begin with the article itself, no preface or commentary.
