Ok so this has been rattling round my head since Saturday and I couldn't work out who was winning, so I thought I'd get a second opinion. I was at a dinner party and two friends of mine ended up in a proper argument about dreams. Neither is a philosopher by trade — well, one sort of is.

Dev works nights in a sleep lab (he's the one who does the waking-people-up-and-asking-them part of the studies) and Marta is halfway through a philosophy PhD, though not on this topic. What kicked it off was Dev telling a story about himself: he fell asleep on the sofa, a bottle smashed in the kitchen, and he woke straight up reporting — instantly, in detail — a long dream about being at a shooting range which ended with a gunshot going off. He said he'd seen that a hundred times at work, and his conclusion was that the dream can't really have been running for all that time; it must get assembled at the moment of waking, with the real noise stitched into the ending. So, he said, dreams aren't experiences you have while asleep at all, they're more like a memory that gets manufactured on the spot and handed to you already finished.

Then it went like this:

1. Marta said that's a non-sequitur: even if the report gets polished at the moment of waking, that says nothing about whether something was happening before then. You can't remember nothing.
2. Dev said the eye-movement experiments cut against her, not for her — the ones where lucid dreamers signal with pre-arranged eye movements and the signals turn up at the right clock times. He said that shows brain activity tracking time, and activity isn't experience: nobody has ever caught a dream in the act, only a brain and a report.
3. Marta said by that standard nobody has ever caught a waking experience either, since all you ever get there too is a brain and a report. If the rule is "not real unless it can be observed independently of the report", his rule destroys waking perception as well.
4. Dev said there's a difference in kind: waking reports can be cross-checked against the world and against other people's testimony, whereas dream reports can't be checked against anything, so there's no fact about whether they match some earlier episode.
5. Marta said fine, but then his own claim has no fact to match either — "the dream was inserted rather than lived" is meant to be a discovery about what happened, and on his own account nothing could make it true or false.
6. Dev said that's just verbal bullying; a false memory can be implanted and it's still false, and philosophy doesn't get to rule that out by definition. He added that people report half-hour dreams after five minutes of REM, and that if you add up what people say they dreamed in a night it's way more than the two hours or so of REM they actually get, so the durations in reports simply aren't trustworthy.
7. Marta said he's sliding between two claims: that dream reports are bad evidence about duration (probably true) and that there was no experience at all (doesn't follow). She also said the stimulus-weaving stuff is evidence for her side — a heart monitor beeping and turning into an ambulance siren inside the dream looks like an experience that's live and tracking the world, not a tape.
8. Dev said weaving happens at insertion time too: whatever does the assembling reads what's coming in through the ears right at that moment, which is exactly what his own bottle-smashing story shows.
9. Marta said that makes his view unfalsifiable, since any sign of a live experience just gets redscribed as assembly-time stitching.
10. Dev said so what, unfalsifiable isn't the same as false, and she still hasn't given him one single case of a dream verified while the sleeper stayed asleep.

Can you go through this and tell me who's right about what? For each of the ten moves I'd like a rating — clearly right, mostly right, mixed, mostly wrong, clearly wrong — with a couple of sentences justifying it, and then a verdict on who came out ahead overall and whether anything in the exchange actually settles the question. On top of that, please sort the moves into ones that are empirical (a better sleep lab could in principle decide them), ones that are conceptual, and ones that are just the two of them using the word dream for different things; and flag anywhere one of them quietely switches between the report and whatever episode, if any, the report is supposed to be of. I'm aware this is an old argument that gets re-run a lot, but I don't want a potted history of it or a reading list, I want you to judge the moves as they were actually made here. And if you think one of them is making a mistake that neither of them noticed, say so bluntly. British English please.
