You're going to help me write a funding proposal. Quick background, because it matters for the idea: I'm not an academic and never will be. I sat on my city council for two terms, got knocked out in a primary, and for the last few years I've been volunteering with a local group that pushes for citizen assemblies and sortition — picking some of the legislators by lottery instead of electing them. I'm also weirdly sympathetic to the term limits crowd, who most of the people I work with treat like the dumb cousin of their idea.

Here's the thing I've been turning over on my own, and I want you to actually think hard about it before you start writing, not just tidy it up. Basically everybody in the sortition debate frames it as a bet on whether ordinary people are competent enough to govern. But when I was in the room, the difference between the career people and the rest of us wasn't skill. It was that they had a next job to protect and I didn't. So maybe the active ingredient in sortition isn't the lottery at all — maybe it's that the job is a dead end. No re-election, no future, no career to manage, nobody to impress for the next round. And if that's the real mechanism, then term limits remove the future too while keeping elections, which would mean they buy you a big chunk of what sortition is supposed to buy you, at a fraction of the fight. On the other hand maybe the lottery is doing something completely different — changing *who* gets in rather than what they do once they're in — and the two reforms aren't substitutes at all. I'm not sure wether that second story actually holds together.

I want to pitch a foundation for a six-month project, part time, just me plus a small stipend, ending in one workshop and a written report. Write the proposal.

Please write it in American English, and use exactly these section headers in this order, with roughly these lengths:

- **Working through my idea** — about 150 words. Poke at my half-formed thought before you build on it. Where is it confused, where is it promising, and is there a sharper version of it than the one I gave you. Don't flatter me and don't just agree.
- **The question** — about 200 words. State it precisely enough that it could come out one way or the other. If you think my framing needs to be split into two separate questions to be answerable, split it and say why.
- **Why the current answers don't settle it** — about 250 words. What the sortition people say, what the election-people say, what the term limits people say, and why each of them has already talked past the question instead of answering it.
- **How I'd actually do the work** — about 400 words. Month by month. What I'd be reading and thinking about and writing, what the workshop would be for, and how a non-academic with no research staff makes progress on something conceptual rather than just collecting opinions.
- **What a result looks like, and what failure looks like** — about 250 words. What would count as actually having answered the question instead of restating it, and what would make me say the project definately failed. I want the failure conditions to be real, not decorative.
- **Scope check** — about 150 words. Honest assessment of what could make this too big or too ambitious for six months part time, and what I should cut or narrow to fit.

No bibliography, no literature review, and please don't name specific books, articles, or authors — I can't check any of it and I'll add references later if I need them. Talking about positions, arguments, and camps in general terms is completely fine. Aim for around 1,400 words total.
