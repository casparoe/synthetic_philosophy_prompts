Roommate of mine is a pretty sharp amateur apologist and last night he defended the kalam's first premise — the one about whatever begins to existing having a cause — by just writing ex nihilo nihil fit on the whiteboard, like that closes the deal. When I said okay but what about quantum vaccuum fluctuations, things popping into being with no apparent cause, he said the vacuum isn't nothing so it isn't a counterexample. Then later he used the exact same line to prop up the Leibniz contigency argument as well. I left feeling like I'd watched a shell game but I can't say where the pea went.

Can you unpack the sentence for me? I want the distinct theses it could be asserting, varying what nothing amounts to (absolute non-being, no material substrate, no earlier state of the world, no efficient cause, no explanation), what comes from means (having a first moment of existence vs. being rearranged out of pre-existing parts vs. being continuously sustained), and whether the thing is supposed to hold inside the universe or of the universe as a whole. For each reading, tell me whether it's near-analytic, an empirical generalization, or a big metaphysical bet — and whether it's true. Then say which one he actually needed for the premise, whether the vacuum-isn't-nothing move rescues that reading or just makes it unfalsifiable in a suspicious way, whether the slogan does any work at all for an argument that wants explanations rather than causes, and whether it can even apply to the beginning of the universe if time itself starts there, since then there's no earlier nothing for stuff to fail to come out of.

Two more things. I've got a hazy memory this maxim is ancient and was originally pointed the other direction, at creation — that there's some permanent stuff nobody could have made. Is that right, and does it change what the line is worth as a premise? And is the pop-science the universe came from nothing crowd using it in yet another sense, so that half the argument is just verbal? Sort the readings into a list, from barest platitude to most contentious, and mark which one each side of that debate is really leaning on.
