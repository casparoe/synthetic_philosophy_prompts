I'm a first-year history PhD student and yesterday in a methods seminar we were going round on the Late Bronze Age collapse — the palace economies, the Sea Peoples stuff, some climate record that seems to show a long dry stretch right around then. I asked what I thought was a pretty basic question: if you had to say what actually did it, was it the climate, the incursions, or the fact that the whole interdependent trade system was one bad harvest from unravelling? A senior student said, flatly, that the collapse was overdetermined and my question didn't have an answer, the room nodded, and we moved on. I left feeling like I'd been mugged by a technical term.

My problem: 'overdetermined' now gets thrown around our department constantly and I've noticed it seems to mean at least three different things depending on who's saying it — sometimes 'any one of those factors alone would have been enough', sometimes 'you need them all together, none was sufficient by itself' (which sounds like nearly the opposite to me?), and sometimes something much vaguer along the lines of 'it was complicated, don't be reductive.' Someone also told me the word originally came from psychoanalysis, about dream elements having several sources at once, and I have no idea whether thats right or whether it's even the same notion being borrowed.

So can you lay out the distinct claims 'the collapse was overdetermined' could actually be making? I want the full menu, not your favourite reading: for each one, what it commits the speaker to, whether it's a platitude no historian would deny, whether it's substantive and arguable, whether it's just false of most large historical events, and what evidence you'd need to establish it. My suspicion is that the strong reading has a bar nobody has ever actually cleared, and I want to know whether I'm right about that. Then tell me which of these readings, if any, licenses the move that was made in the seminar — which ones would genuinely make my 'but which one did it?' question defective rather than merely hard. And if the honest answer is that none of them do, say what my question was really asking, because I have a hunch that 'which was the cause' isn't a piece of causal metaphysics at all and that the whole exchange was a category slip. Lastly: flag what word people *should* be reaching for in each of the weaker cases, so I can tell next time whether someone's using a term of art or just waving. No bibliography and no reading list please — I want the disambiguation, not the literature.
