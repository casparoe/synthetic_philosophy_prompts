So: I run training for the volunteers on a peer-support text line. Not a crisis line, not therapy. Our users are mostly articulate people in their 20s and 30s who overthink everything and are strangely bad at introspection — i.e. exactly the demographic you'd expect. Roughly 200 chats a week across a rota of ~30 volunteers with zero clinical background, each of whom gets one page of SOP. I write the SOP.

The current page says: reflect the feeling back, don't problem-solve, escalate if there's risk of harm. It's producing two failure modes and I am tired of both.

1. About half our volunteers are closeted rationalizers. Someone types "my boss is definitely going to fire me" and they go straight to "what's your evidence for that?" Sometimes that's exactly right. Most of the time it lands as dismissal and the person ghosts mid-chat.
2. The other half have overcorrected into pure validation. Someone says "I know the spider is harmless, I *know* it, and I'm still terrified," and the volunteer just mirrors it back for forty minutes, which leaves the person feeling like they've been talking to a very warm wall.

I've read enough of the philosophy-of-emotion literature to be dangerous — James on emotions as feelings of bodily change, Nussbaum and Solomon on the judgment side, then Roberts and Tappolet and Prinz arguing they're more like perceptions (of value, or of your own body). What strikes me is that these aren't just rival metaphysics, they imply three different *first moves*: re-appraise the judgment, treat the seeming as non-doxastic data you register rather than dispute, or work on the body/labeling directly. I've been calling them the three levers. I'm sure that mangles the actual views — tell me how.

What I want is a decision procedure a volunteer can run *inside* a live chat in under three minutes: the questions to type, in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, what each answer routes to, where the procedure dead-ends and where it hands off to a senior volunteer or a clinician (and mark which of those handoffs are risk/values calls rather than factual ones). Then:

- which distinctions from the three theories have to survive compression onto one page, and which you're knowingly throwing away
- the hard cases it must handle: the recalcitrant emotion, a mood with no object, grief, shame-about-being-angry, someone whose body says panic while their construal says nothing in particular, and the person who is *right* to be afraid — where the emotion is tracking something real and the fix is upstream of them
- at least one case where the procedure tells me something I won't like. My prior is heavily on the perception side and I'm clearly motivated to route everything to "don't argue with it," so assume I'll flinch
- what the procedure flatly cannot do

Invent three realistic transcripts yourself rather than asking me for them, covering different routes, including one where the right answer is "none of the three levers, stop and escalate."

Format: the SOP in prose, numbered, then a JSON array with one object per case using exactly these fields — `case_id` (string); `surface_report` (their words compressed to one sentence); `deciding_question` (which step's answer settled the route); `route` (one of "judgment", "perception", "feeling", "escalate"); `first_move` (the literal next message the volunteer sends); `forbidden_moves` (array of strings — things that would make it worse); `why_not_the_other_routes` (string); `escalate` (bool); `escalation_trigger` (string or null); `volunteer_confidence` ("high"/"medium"/"low"); `procedure_failure_mode` (string or null — the way this case is one our SOP will still get wrong in practice).
