And the student's answer:

> Plato's late dialogue the *Laws* is a conversation between an unnamed Athenian Stranger and two older men, the Spartan Megillus and the Cretan Cleinias, and it's the Athenian doing the arguing rather than Socrates, which matters because he's sketching a second-best city instead of the one in the *Republic*. He opens by pointing out that the Spartan and Cretan institutions — the common meals, the gymnastics, the carrying of arms — are all arranged with an eye to war, which means they train citizens to hold out against pain and fear but do nothing whatsoever about the opposite danger, being beaten by pleasure. His central claim is that a lawgiver who trains only against pain has trained half a citizen, since the worst kind of defeat is the one a man suffers at his own hands.
>
> To fix this he uses the same method the Spartans already use for courage. They expose boys to frightening things in graded doses until they stop panicking; wine, the Athenian says, works the same way, because wine is a kind of drug that produces the opposite of the state you want to end up with — it makes a man bold, loud and shameless. Given under supervision, with a sober president of the drinking party in charge, it's a cheap and pleasant way of testing whether a citizen can hold on to his sense of shame while his soul is being pushed the other way, and of building up the courage he'll need to resist. That's why the gift of Dionysus — which Plato says was given to mankind out of spite, to drive them mad — turns out to be valuable: the danger is exactly the point.
>
> He also treats wine as a remedy for old age, which is why the rules are graded by age: no wine at all before eighteen, since young bodies are still developing and drink would damage them; moderate drinking until thirty; and after forty a man may drink freely at the common meals. His model of the good drinker is the Socrates of the *Symposium*, who can drink everyone under the table with no visible change in his behaviour, so the legal restrictions are really just training wheels for men who haven't yet acquired that immunity.
