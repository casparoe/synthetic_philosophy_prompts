Every year we put out a long-range regional outlook — 20 years, infrastructure and energy — and it goes to the board. Last cycle we got torn apart in review. So now I have two people telling me my job is nonsense, from different directions, and I don't have the training to adjudicate it.

Here's what I want. Rather than argue about the genre in the abstract, I pulled nine sentences straight out of our last outlook. I want you to triage them against Popper's critique.

1. No economy that has crossed 55% urbanisation has ever fallen back below it. The region crosses that line around 2031, so the direction of travel after that is settled.
2. Economies move agrarian → light manufacturing → heavy industry → services. They climb that ladder in order, and the ones that try to skip a rung stall out.
3. We can't tell you the year. But by 2050 the three family-controlled port conglomerates in the region will have lost control of their terminals, because the structural logic of containerisation runs against them.
4. Coal-fired capacity is now more expensive than solar-plus-storage in four of our six markets. Absent a policy reversal, closures continue at roughly the rate observed since 2019 through the early 2030s.

6. Resource-dependent regimes don't outlive their founding generation. The last one standing in the region falls before 2040.
7. Our 2045 grid numbers assume the region has deployed, at scale, the long-duration storage tech that is currently in pilot in two advanced markets.
8. Fertility below replacement has never recovered in a large economy. Treat a shrinking regional workforce in the 2050s as a certainty and build the port plan around it.
9. We have published a downgrade warning on the sovereign for six years running, and each publication makes the downgrade likelier — our own forecast is one of the mechanisms producing the thing it predicts.
