so me and my roommate burned like two hours on the absent-minded driver problem last night (the one where the guy cant tell the two exits apart — 1 if he takes the first, 4 if he takes the second, 0 if he blows past both) and we got absolutely nowhere. His take: the driver should flip a weighted coin before he leaves the house, take-the-first-exit with probability 5/8, and this is basically the sleeping beauty thing in a trenchcoat, i.e. 5/8 is just his credence that hes at the good exit. I said no to both parts but im not sure I can actually defend either one.

On the coin: one flip at home cant do what he wants, because if it comes up keep-going hes guaranteed to miss both and eat a 0, so his expectation is only 5/8, which is worse than just always exiting at the first junction he sees. On the credence: I said the driver has literally zero evidence distinguishing the two exits, so his credence has to be 1/2 each, which means 5/8 isnt a belief about anything. And more basically I said mixing should never help anybody — randomizing just averages over outcomes you could have had by picking one of them, it cant manufacture information, so a randomized plan beating every deterministic plan has to be a sign the setup is incoherent rather than a discovery.

So where exactly do I go wrong? Is the 5/8 a probability of something, and if it isnt, what is it doing that a coin flip at each exit can beat both pure plans?
