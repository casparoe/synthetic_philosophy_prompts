You're a professional philosopher who works on Wittgenstein's *On Certainty* and hinge epistemology, and who also writes comedy. Both halves of that matter for what I want.

I'm a third-year PhD student and I've been asked to contribute something to our department's annual anthology of silly prose. I want to do a parody of one of those building-compliance documents: a "Hinge Inspection and Maintenance Schedule" issued by some invented regulatory body for the world-picture of an ordinary dwelling. Around 600–700 words, formatted like the real thing — a header with a document reference number, numbered inspection items with tick-boxes, two short sidebars, and a sign-off block at the bottom.

The humour has to come from getting the epistemology right, not from generic bureaucratic absurdity. Please build in at least: an item on checking hinges for wear which quietly makes clear you can't test a hinge without already relying on it; a two-tier certification scheme separating the kind of certainty that makes "I know" the wrong thing to say from the kind that doesn't; a clause on what to do when a hinge has shifted so that the previous inspection is void — its the riverbed business; something about hinges that hold fast only because the neighbours' hinges hold them fast; and a procedure for satisfying the inspector modelled on Moore holding up his hands, which the form treats as entirely reasonable and even rather brisk. Don't name the objections. The reader should come away uneasy about relativism, and about whether this sort of entitlement counts as epistemic at all, without either issue ever being mentioned.

The target is the assurance — the form's and Moore's alike — and it should stay genuinely unclear which of the two the reader is meant to distrust. Deadpan throughout, no winking, no joke-explaining.

Two hard constraints: no verbatim quotations from *On Certainty* and no numbered remark references, since I can't check them and I don't want paraphrase dressed up as quotation. Just paraphrase freely.

Start by brainstorming six candidate inspection items, one line each, then commit to your favourites. After the document, add one line telling me which item you cut and why. British English spelling throughout. My supervisor will read this, so make sure a hinge epistemologist laughs rather than winces.
