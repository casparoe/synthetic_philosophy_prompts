write me a parody of a newspaper death notice / obituary, full obituary conventions and all — headline, "of [town], aged X", the predeceased-by and survived-by paragraph, service details, "in lieu of flowers" — except the deceased is a pure strategy in a finite normal-form game that has just been killed off by iterated elimination. Around 550-650 words. The target is the discourse of "solving" games by dominance and the confidence that elimination gets you somewhere, not any real game theorist, so invent all the mourners.

beats I want hit, all delivered straight, no winking:

- the deceased hung around for years being merely weakly annoying, and was finally done in not by any single pure strategy but by a mixture of two of its own siblings. the obit should make it obvious to a lay reader how a weighted combination can be uniformly better when no single option is, but it must not turn into a lecture or a worked example with numbers in the middle of the sentences.
- the order of the procession was mercifully irrelevant — someone in the family should be comforted by the fact that it makes no difference who was carried out first — and there should be a pointed contrast with the service being held across town for the weakly dominated, where nobody can agree on the order and the guest list comes out different depending on who goes first.
- an awkward paragraph on the fact that the deceased will never be played with positive probability at any Nash equilibrium, which the family has asked the paper not to dwell on; and a weakly dominated cousin who *is* part of an equilibrium sends flowers and everyone has feelings about it.
- the genuinely sad bit, and the one I care most about: whether this death was justified depends on how many players were at the table. In a two-player game, strictly dominated (even by a mixture) means it was never a best response to anything anybody could believe, and the death is unanswerable. But this game had three players, so the family insists the deceased was a perfectly sensible choice for anyone who didn't suppose the other two were coordinating, and the coroner can only say the death certificate required letting them coordinate. Get that asymmetry exactly right — it's the whole point — and let it land as grief, not as a footnote.
- closing note that none of this would have happened unless everyone present knew everyone was rational, and knew that they knew, and so on forever, an assumption nobody at the service could actually vouch for; and that the surviving set, though much reduced, still hasn't settled on a single outcome, so the funeral was in a sense only preparatory.

keep it deadpan throughout, no parenthetical asides explaining the game theory, no footnotes, no author's-nudge at the end. someone who teaches this material should recognise every beat and think it fair; someone who's never seen a payoff matrix should still find it funny as an obituary.

one typo or two is fine in my instructions here, obviously not in the piece.
