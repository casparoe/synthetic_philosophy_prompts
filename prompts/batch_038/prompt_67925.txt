Right, so I'm in year 13 doing an EPQ on free will and blame, and I've been watching loads of videos and reading whatever I can get my hands on, and there's a move I keep seeing that I don't have a word for.

The setup first. Someone says you can only be to blame for something if you could actually have done otherwise. Then someone else tells a story where you couldn't have: there's a neurosurgeon monitoring you who would step in and make you do the thing if he saw you starting to hesitate, but you never hesitate, so he never lifts a finger, and you do it entirely on your own. They say, look, you're defintely still to blame in that one, so the could-have-done-otherwise rule is wrong.

And the reply that comes back is almost never "no, he isn't to blame". It's: that story doesn't count. Either because the person who built it built it to get that verdict, and you could fiddle the details until the intuition flipped, so all it really shows is that you can design a story with whatever answer you wanted already baked in. Or because nobody's sense of fairness was formed by situations with neurosurgeons lurking about, so asking what we'd say about one doesn't tell you anything about blame at all. And everyone treats that as a proper answer rather than a dodge. Both camps do it. I've seen the exact same thing with runaway trams — no one's moral thinking was built for trolleys, so a trolley case can't teach you anything.

So does this have a name? Its not the same as saying the example begs the question, and it's not the version where people argue the case can't even be built coherently (either the minder is doing something, in which case it's him choosing, or he isn't, in which case you could have done otherwise) — I know that's a separate objection. This one is more like: the case is too weird and too made-to-order to be evidence about anything. If there isn't a proper term, can you make one up and define it, including when the dismissal is actually fair and when it's just someone refusing to engage with a counterexample they don't like? And give me a couple of borderline ones so I know where the edges are. Don't bother with a reading list, I can't get hold of the papers anyway — I just want the concept carved up properly, not a label.
