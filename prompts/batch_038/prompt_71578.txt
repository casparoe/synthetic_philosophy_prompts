I am assembling a set of questions on which I want numbers rather than surveys of opinion, and I would like you to treat the following as such a question.

Consider this proposition, which I will call P:

P: There is at least one identifiable internal state of some currently existing large language model for which a teleosemantic account assigns a determinate and non-trivial content — where teleosemantic means content fixed by a history of selection for effects of a kind that contributed to the success of the system bearing them, read together with the state's downstream consumers; where determinate means the account selects one content rather than leaving the standard rivals (the small dark moving dot; the fly; the retinal shadow) equally admissible, and so does not simply re-inherit the disjunction problem in full; and where non-trivial means the content so fixed is not merely the input-side correlate that a purely informational account would assign anyway, so that the selection history and the consumer relation are doing real work.

Please give me:

1. Your number for P, to whatever precision you think is honest.

2. The decomposition: the sub-questions on which P depends, each with a rough probability and one or two sentences in support. I would expect the list to include at least the following — the probability that teleosemantics is the right account of content even for biological systems; the probability that training a model on a prediction objective constitutes selection history of the relevant kind, rather than a design process whose products inherit content only derivatively from their designers; the probability that there exists a suitable consumer with a genuine success condition, given that the candidates include the model's own downstream layers, the human reader, and whatever reward or evaluation signal shaped the later stages of training, and that these need not converge on the same content; and the probability that the content fixed by the winning combination survives causal intervention. Add any terms you think I have missed. Then show how the terms combine into your number, and justify the combination rule you use given that several of these terms are plainly not independent of one another.

3. The term you are least confident in, and the specific evidence or argument that would move it most.

Two further instructions. First, I want to know whether the interpretability toolkit changes the arithmetic and not merely the rhetoric. Representations in a trained model can be probed, ablated, patched, and steered, and competing content hypotheses can be tested against the model's own behavior under counterfactual inputs, in a way that thought experiments about frogs cannot replicate. State explicitly whether you take that asymmetry to raise or lower the relevant probabilities, and by roughly how much. Second, suppose P turns out to be false. Say what that failure would show, assigning probabilities to the three readings I can see — that it counts against teleosemantics as an account of content anywhere, that it counts only against these models having underived content, and that it counts against nothing at all because the case is simply not one the theory was built for — and fold those into your answer.

No citations or references are wanted; I am after the structure of the reasoning. For calibration: my own figure is about 0.35, arrived at almost entirely by heavily discounting the selection-history term, and I suspect that I have imported a derivativeness intuition without examining it. Tell me whether you think my number is too high or too low, and on which term.
