I am drafting an essay for a module on Warring States philosophy in which we have to bring the Mencius–Xunzi dispute over human nature to bear on a case neither author could have anticipated. Suppose a state introduces an opt-out bioenhancement programme: an intervention administered in early childhood that reliably amplifies a person's spontaneous distress at the suffering of others, while leaving reasoning, instruction and judgement entirely untouched. How would Mencius and Xunzi each rule on it, and on what grounds?

Please tie the predictions to their distinctive machinery rather than to a generic contrast between optimism and pessimism about human nature. For Mencius, consider whether magnifying the sprouts short-circuits the work of extension that he thinks they require, and how the parable of the farmer who ruined his crop by pulling up the shoots to help it grow bears on a deliberately designed intervention. For Xunzi, consider whether an inborn amplification of the very responsiveness he takes nature to lack could possibly count as the deliberate activity that only ritual, teachers and long habituation can produce — or whether it is, on his view, a counterfeit of it.

Finally, tell me where each position is most strained by this case, and which of the two has the better resources for insisting that amplified empathy is not yet virtue.
