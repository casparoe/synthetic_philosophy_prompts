my brother died in april and the book that got me through it, weirdly, was I Am a Strange Loop. not the strange loop stuff so much as the other idea in there: that a person is a pattern, and pieces of that pattern get installed in everyone who knew them at whatever resoultion each of them happened to take in, so that dying means the sharpest copy is gone while the blurry ones keep running and keep doing things. he would have hated being consoled. i don't want consolation.

so: can you write me a pantoum about that? six stanzas, give or take one. the part i actually care about is the inheritance. in a strict pantoum lines 2 and 4 come back verbatim as lines 1 and 3 of the next stanza; i want them near-verbatim but decaying. those recurring lines should be his phrases, the dumb habitual stuff a person says a thousand times without meaning to — 'check the mirror twice', that register — and every time one comes back a word should be swapped or a tense should slide or the punchline should land one beat early. visible enough that reading aloud you notice something's off. not so much that it turns into parody. then the final stanza should pick up lines 1 and 3 of the first one the way the form closes, and that should be the one time a phrase comes back completely intact, with it clear that the intactness is my doing now and not his.

hard constraint: no vocabulary from the theory. no patterns, symbols, information, substrate, copies, resolution, loops, none of it. the idea has to ride entirely on the repetition and on what my body is caught doing — i want one moment, maybe stanza 3 or 4, where i catch myself making his gesture and it lands as funny and awful at once, because his habits are running my arms. unrhymed is fine, don't force a rhyme scheme. don't be warm at the end, and don't explain the poem afterwards.

someone who's read the book should be able to say the poem got the view right — that being in another head comes in degrees, that lower fidelity isn't a failure of the copy — and someone who's never heard of it should just find a good sad poem about a brother.
