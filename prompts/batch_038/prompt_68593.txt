So — weird family situation that's turned into a research project. My sister quit her job in March and now sells birth chart readings online, 90 euros a pop, and my dad (retired structural enginer, extremely concrete-minded) has taken it as a personal insult. Dinner last week turned into a shouting match: he called the whole thing medieval garbage, she called him a closed mind, I sat there eating potatoes.

I went looking for middle ground and fell down a hole on Kepler — not the planetary motion part, the astrology part. Turns out the guy cast horoscopes for money basically his whole life, including one for a general, and also wrote things arguing against the theologians who wanted to bin astrology entirely *and* against the working astrologers, because he wanted to strip the practice down to just the geometrical angles between planets and throw out the signs and the houses. Nobody at that table would recognise that position, which is exactly why I want to hand him a document instead of a lecture.

Here's what I want: a parody regulatory code, formatted like a modern professional-standards document — numbered clauses, deadpan legalese — issued by an invented licensing body for astrological practice, which codifies Kepler's reform programme as if a government had actually adopted it. About 750–850 words. It stays in character the whole way: no winking at the reader, no "obviously none of this is real," no footnotes, no bibliography, no real titles or dates. I want to print it out.

Things it has to actually do, because these are the jokes:

- A preamble/scope section insisting the practice is hereby *preserved and made respectable*, not abolished, on the grounds that banning a field because it has charlatans in it would oblige you to ban a whole list of others — medicine, law, whatever else the reformer would have to throw out. Let the list escalate slightly and get increasingly inconvenient for him.
- A schedule of permitted claims restricted to the angular relationships between heavenly bodies at a given moment — and which also legislates several *brand new* angles into existence, derived from dividing the circle into harmonic parts, on the grounds that the traditional set is incomplete. The new angles should be mandatory. The people in legal trouble are the ones who ignore them.
- A schedule of prohibited apparatus, all of it treated as unlicensed practice: the personality meanings of the twelve signs, the topics assigned to the houses, dignities and exaltations, retrograde character material, answering a specific question from the moment it was asked, and picking auspicious dates.
- A mandatory client-disclosure clause requiring the licensee to state in writing, before payment: that a good share of predictions come true by luck; that much of what lands well is the licensee's own knack for reading a person sitting in front of him; and that clients remember the hits and forget the misses.
- A hardship exemption permitting nativities to be cast for wealthy patrons and military men on the grounds that the treasury is behind on the licensee's salary. Make this clause blatantly self-serving and totally unembarrassed.
- One dry clause about the licensee's relatives not being presumed to be witches on account of his trade. No explanation, no follow-up.
- A grandfathering provision for practitioners already established, an enforcement and penalties section, and a short FAQ appendix.

The engine of the piece is that by the final clause almost nothing is left standing, but the document never notices and keeps congratulating itself on having rescued the field from its enemies. The FAQ answer to the question "what may a licensed practitioner actually tell a client?" is the punchline and should be extremely short — weather, essentially.

It has to be legible to someone who's never heard of any of this, i.e. my dad, but built right enough that a historian of science reading it would know exactly whose programme it is. Dont put any real person's words in quotation marks anywhere; invented clause text and FAQ answers are fine, of course.
