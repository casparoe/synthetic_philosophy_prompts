"A refugee, legally speaking, is anyone forced to leave their country because of war, persecution, or conditions that make life unliveable there — poverty and climate disaster count too these days. The key document is the 1951 UN Refugee Convention, which does two jobs: it defines who qualifies, and it obliges signatory states to take in a fair share of them. Once you qualify you can claim asylum in whichever signatory country you prefer, and that country has to process your claim. What you can't do is travel past a safe country to get there — the convention says you must claim in the first safe country you reach, so someone who flies into London having transitted elsewhere is a queue-jumper and technically in breach. The one absolute rule is non-refoulement: a state can't send you back somewhere you'd be persecuted, but that only bites once you're physically on its soil, which is why turning boats around at sea is legal."
