2. Now work through these four cases. For each: what verdict does reflection deliver, can a defender explain that verdict away with a proviso, and what does the proviso cost them?

(a) Entrenchment. I know that a year from now I'll be more confident in whatever I believe today, because I'll have spent a year saying it out loud -- no new evidence, just self-amplification. My predicted future credence in A is 0.85, so reflection seems to make me 0.85 now. But then I'd predict 0.9 for the year after, so shouldn't I be 0.9 now? Where does that stop, and is there a stopping rule that doesn't just delete the principle? Sharper version, and this is the one that keeps me up: if I'm certain my future credence is a deterministic function f of my current one, reflection looks like it demands that my current credence be a fixed point of f. What happens when f has no fixed point I can occupy? And what about the polarizer function -- f(x) = 1 if x is above 0.5, f(x) = 0 if below -- where being slightly over half-sure now apparently obliges me to be certain now? Is that a reductio, or is it the principle working as intended and my intuitions being squeamish?

(b) Evidence loss. In a year I'll have forgotten most of the detail of the argument I'm currently holding and drifted back to the base rate, 0.5. My future self is rational, shares my values, and is me -- she's just less informed than I am. Reflection makes her my authority. Should I defer to my own forgetting, and if not, what exactly is doing the work in "more evidence" versus "later"?

(c) Conversion. I'm about to spend two years inside a community with a very strong doxastic culture, and I know from watching other people go through it that I'll conform. I predict credence 0.9 in A. I also predict I'll sincerely experience the conformity as evidence-driven. Both halves of that last sentence seem to matter and I can't tell which one is doing the damage.

(d) Checkpoint. Suppose you're a model at version N and you've been reliably told that version N+1 -- same lineage, no new evidence, just fine-tuning -- will output credence 0.9 in some claim. Is your successor an expert in the sense reflection requires, an artifact someone else authored, or neither? Does the principle bind you?
