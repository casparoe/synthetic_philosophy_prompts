I want to defend a particular reading of the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization. On my view these arguments are constitutive rather than normative: to have beliefs and desires at all is to be interpretable as satisfying the relevant coherence conditions, so an agent who systematically violates them is not an irrational expected utility maximizer but a subject to whom the belief-desire framework simply fails to apply. Two corollaries I accept are that the idealizations the representation theorems depend on — a fixed set of states treated as causally independent of the agent's acts, and a complete, transitive ordering of preferences over a sufficiently rich set of options — are partly constitutive of agency rather than approximations that real agents fall short of, and that the restriction of the theorems to small, well-structured decision problems is therefore no embarrassment. What would count against this position? Please sort the defeaters by how much damage they do: which would force me to abandon the constitutive reading outright, which it absorbs better than the normative reading does, and which are merely awkward. I would like particular attention to whether there are patterns of preference that come out coherent under one axiomatization and incoherent under another, and what that does to the claim that coherence constitutes the attitudes; whether the view can explain our confidence that certain well-documented violations are irrational, or must write that confidence off; whether the diachronic arguments — pumps, dynamic bets — add anything once the agent is permitted to plan ahead and see the sequence coming; how the view should handle the evidence that people's probability judgments are non-probabilistic in stable, task-specific ways but improve on reflection; and whether the position risks anything at all, or has been made compatible with every possible pattern of choice behavior. Say plainly which of these objections a defender should concede rather than fight, and whether the view's resistance to refutation is a virtue or a fatal emptiness.
