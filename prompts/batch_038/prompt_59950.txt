Analyze the two Irish referendums on the Treaty of Lisbon (2008 and 2009) as a test case for the concept of 'the will of the people' as populist theory uses it. I want the analysis to run in both directions: what the concept commits us to saying about this episode, and what the episode does to the concept.

The facts you need, so you don't have to reconstruct them. The Treaty of Lisbon was signed in December 2007. Because of a 1987 Irish Supreme Court ruling, Ireland could not ratify it without a constitutional amendment, and every amendment goes to a referendum; Ireland was the only one of the 27 member states to put the treaty to voters, the others ratifying in parliament. On 12 June 2008 the amendment was defeated, 53.4% No to 46.6% Yes, on a turnout of 53.1%. The European Council then agreed a set of guarantees to Ireland concerning taxation, military neutrality, and certain ethically sensitive constitutional provisions, together with a commitment on the composition of the Commission. On 2 October 2009 the same amendment, re-proposed, passed with 67.1% Yes on a turnout of 59% — a swing of roughly twenty points. The intervening period included the collapse of the Irish banking system and a severe recession, with the same government in office throughout. Ireland had done the same thing with the Treaty of Nice (rejected 2001, approved 2002), and Denmark had done it with Maastricht (rejected 1992, approved 1993 after opt-outs).

Please be disciplined about the mapping before you evaluate. Who is the relevant people here: the Irish electorate, the Irish voters who turned out, the 66.6% majority of a 59% turnout, or some European demos that never got asked? What is the object of the will: the treaty text, the treaty-plus-guarantees package, ratification, or the identity of the decision-maker? Set out what each candidate account of popular will — Rousseau's distinction between the general will and the will of all, the aggregative versus the deliberative or will-formation conceptions, and Riker's social-choice argument that a coherent popular will does not exist because of cycling and agenda control — implies about which of the two results, if either, expressed a will.

Then take seriously two distinctions I want pressure-tested rather than merely entertained. First, the question-identity problem: was October 2009 the same question asked again, in which case one of the two outcomes misdescribes the people's will, or a different question, in which case neither is authoritative about the other and the phrase 'the people changed their minds' is doing illegitimate work? Second, the second-order reading: the No vote as a protest against the government and the process rather than a preference about the treaty, which would make the 2009 result anomalous, since the protest motive should have strengthened after the banking collapse rather than evaporated. Say which reading survives and why.

Finally, the reverse direction. Is the two-vote pattern a confirmation of Riker's arbitrariness thesis, an anomaly for populism's claim of a homogeneous people whose will elites obstruct, or evidence that 'the will of the people' functions as a claim to authority rather than a description? Does the repetition across Ireland, Denmark, and later cases matter? And be explicit about where these frameworks fall silent — on the standing of the 46.6% minority, on the absence of any turnout threshold, and above all on the fact that roughly one percent of the Union's population held an effective veto over a decision affecting 500 million people whose own representatives ratified it without consulting them. If a theory cannot locate the demos in that situation, say so plainly instead of stretching it to fit. No bibliography; argue from the concepts.
