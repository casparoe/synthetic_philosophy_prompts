I want a dialogue about taxing wealth transfers that avoids the two versions I keep encountering, in which one of the parties is a strawman.

Setting: a kitchen table, three siblings dividing a parent's estate.

One character is a luck egalitarian who has given up on taxing estates and wants instead to tax each heir's cumulative lifetime receipts from gifts and bequests. On this view the objection to inheritance is not the donor's act of giving but the heir's accumulated unearned advantage, and the three-way split in front of them is exactly what a cumulative base is designed to catch.

The other defends the freedom to bequeath, but has also shifted ground: they concede that the dead have no rights and that nothing is taken from the deceased, and argue instead that a bequest is a form of the donor's own consumption — the satisfaction of a project that outlasts them — so that a receipts tax taxes the donor's choices by a different route. They add that partiality toward one's own children is a ground project rather than a bias to be corrected.

Give each of them at least one argument the other cannot answer, keep them responsive to what has actually been said rather than to a paraphrase of it, and leave the disagreement live at the end.
