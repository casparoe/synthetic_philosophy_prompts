I'm putting together a handout for a masters-level seminar on societal collapse (Bronze Age palatial systems, Classic Maya, Rapa Nui — the usual trio) and I want a decision tree that sorts students into positions on what "collapse" even is, before they ever get to argue about which cases had one.

Please build it out of questions rather than names: five to seven branch nodes, each a yes/no or three-way question a student could answer without knowing where it leads. I want the branches to cover (a) what the thing-that-collapses actually is — the palace administration, the resident population, the elite culture, the standing level of sociopolitical complexity, or the whole adaptive system; (b) whether the word commits you to a judgment that the change was bad, and bad for whom (the scribes who lost their patrons vs. the farmers who stopped paying tribute, say); (c) whether the change counts as reversible, and over what timescale; (d) whether you owe a story about why the society failed to cope, or whether describing the drop in complexity is already the whole explanation.

Every leaf gets a name — use the standard one if it has one (Tainter-style diminishing returns on complexity, Diamond-style self-inflicted ecocide, resilience/reorganization views, the "collapse is a category our sources impose" deflationists) or write "unnamed" and say who could plausibly live there — plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Then mark any combination of answers that nobody occupies and say why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of leaves your questions genuinely can't seperate, and say whether some case could.

Close with a line on how confident you are that the branches are exhaustive and mutually independent, and name the single leaf placement you're least sure of.
