Two people who run the volunteer side of a food pantry near me got into it at the intake table and I couldn't work out who's actually right. Context: they get one fixed pallet of groceries a week, it serves maybe 200 households, and there are two other pantries in the county that they dont coordinate with at all.

(1) Ray says the only defensible policy is a hard cap — serve first-come-first-served until the pallet's gone, close the doors, and deliberately don't advertise. His reasoning is the lifeboat thing: make the pantry generous and well-known and the line grows, the regulars get less, and you end up helping nobody properly.

(2) Dana says that's arbitrary. Closing at 11 means whoever has a car and knows the hours eats, and the person working two shifts goes hungry. She'd rather switch to appointment slots sorted by need, and she says Ray's "don't advertise" rule is really just excluding the least-informed people, who are usually the worst off.

(3) Ray says the need-based system ratchets: every time you soften the rule, more households build their budget around the pantry, the claimant population ratchets up and never comes back down, so per-household rations drift toward nothing. Softening is one-way.

(4) Dana says the ratchet only works if demand is basically unlimited, but the number of people in this county who need groceries is fixed-ish — there's only so many of them. So softening doesn't manufacture new claimants, it just redistributes the same people over time and across the calendar.

(5) Ray says even granting a fixed headcount, the composition changes: people stop keeping any buffer of their own, so the first month the pallet comes up short they're worse off than if the pantry had never opened. Dana says that's a speculative empirical claim about dependency being made to carry a moral conclusion, and he hasn't got the evidence for it.

Neither budged. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right at each step — grade (1) through (5) individually, since I suspect several of them are half-right rather than clean wins — and then tell me what both of them are overlooking? I don't want a general essay on famine relief or world hunger, just the adjudication of what these two actually said.
