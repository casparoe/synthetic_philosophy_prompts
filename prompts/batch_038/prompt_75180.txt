so eight months ago i moved back to the town i grew up in to look after my dad — vascular dementia, and these days he doesn't reliably know who i am, some mornings he thinks i'm my brother. i'm 38 and i've basically stopped having a career. someone lent me a book on Confucian role ethics and it's the first thing i've read in years that described my life instead of arguing with it, so i've sort of assembled a position out of it plus whatever i already believed, and i want it poked at hard before i say any of this out loud to an actual person. here's where i am:

1. there's no me underneath the roles. i'm not a free-standing individual who happens to be a son and a neighbour and a wage-earner — those relations are what i'm made of. strip them all out and there's nothing left to point at, no core, no chooser standing nowhere.

2. everything i owe anybody i owe in virtue of a role i'm actually in. nothing is owed to people just because they're people, and i don't think rights talk survives without the free-standing individual i just denied in (1).

3. which is why that bit in the Analects — the one where the father steals a sheep and the son covers for him and that's offered up as what uprightness actually looks like — reads to me as correct rather than as a scandal. partiality isn't a bias to be corrected out of morality, it's the shape morality has.

4. my dad is exactly as much of a person now as he was six years ago, and what he's owed from me has gone up if anything. his not knowing me changes nothing about the weight of it, and anybody who thought the weight tracked his capacity to recognise me would be being monstrous.

5. the forms matter. the sunday visit, the phone call at the same time every evening, doing it flat and resentful on the days i don't feel like it — because i think the feeling follows the practice, not the other way round. but i also think going through the motions with genuinely nothing behind them is failing at the role rather than doing it well. sincerity is what i'm aiming at, not performance.

6. my brother hasn't been home in eleven years and isn't coming. i don't think that's a lifestyle choice i merely happen to dislike, i think something is wrong in him for it. but if a role is a thing you do, and he hasn't been doing it for a decade, then on my own account is he even a son any more? and if he isn't, what exactly is he failing at? i honestly can't tell whether the answer is "he isn't, and my anger about it is a hangover from the individualism i claim to have dropped" or "of course he still is."

7. you can get out of a role when its wrecking you or when the role itself is unjust. i walked away from the congregation i was raised in and i don't think i owe it anything now.

so: which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? rank the real ones by severity rather than giving me a list where everything is equally bad, and assume some of these are perfectly compatible — i'd rather you said that than padded the audit. for each genuine tension tell me what has to go and what it costs to lose it, and be blunt about whether the cheapest fix is cheap in principle or just cheap for me because it lets me keep living exactly the way i'm already living. and then tell me whether there's a version of this package that holds up at all, or whether i've just dressed up ordinary guilt in somebody else's vocabulary.
