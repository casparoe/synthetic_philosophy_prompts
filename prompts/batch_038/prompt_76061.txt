Two thirds through a novel and I've started to worry the book doesnt know what it thinks about being alive, which is a bit of a problem when a third of the cast might not be. Here's my gut, case by case, as it stands in the draft.

The reef-thing that eats once a decade and grows a metre a century: alive, no question, even though no character ever sees it do anything.

The dunes in the second half: they hold their shape against the wind, repair themselves after a storm, and throw off daughter dunes downwind that grow into their own — not alive. It's sand doing what sand does, even though it ticks every box I'd tick for the reef.

The bare replicator that drifts inert until it lands in a host and then copies furiously: not alive on its own, alive while it's inside somebody. Same thing, different answer depending on where it is, which I know is dodgy.

The woman who uploads and goes on arguing with her sister for two hundred years: alive. The weather-simulation her machine runs off as a waste product, which spins up self-maintaining eddies nobody designed and nobody watches: not alive. Same silicon, same kind of process.

A crystal that grows, feeds, and shatters into seeds: obviously not alive.

And the city — it metabolises, it repairs, it remembers in its street plan — I have the characters say it isn't alive, but I write every one of its scenes as though it were, and I can't tell if that's a contradiction or just prose.

Is there one principle under all of that, or am I running two and not noticing? I'd like it stated as precisely as you can, with a note on which of my verdicts is buying which clause, and then an honest word on which verdict you'd have to overturn to keep the principle simple rather than baroque. Then give me your call on two I haven't settled: a creature stitched together from dead tissue that starts metabolising under its own steam, and a swarm where every individual member fails every test but the whole passes them. Please don't hand me the usual survey of viruses, mules and that self-sustaining-chemical-system-capable-of-evolution line — I want the principle, not the literature. And tell me how confident you are in what you land on, plus the single point you're least sure of.
