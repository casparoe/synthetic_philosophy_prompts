A familiar account of weakness of will runs through the shape of the discount function: because discounting is hyperbolic rather than exponential, an agent's ranking of a larger-later against a smaller-sooner reward can flip as the sooner reward draws near, and akrasia just is that flip happening. Notice what the account needs in order to generate a flip: a spread between the times at which the competing payoffs land. When all the relevant payoffs land at the same moment, the discount factor applies equally to both options and cancels out of the comparison, so the ranking is the same under any strictly increasing discount function, hyperbolic or exponential.

With that in mind, stipulate a class of cases I will call *spreadless*. In a spreadless choice, the two options' entire payoff profiles — every pleasure, pang, cost, and consequence the agent cares about — are located at the same time, or within a window so short that no discount function of any shape could distinguish them. Yet by stipulation the agent judges one option better all things considered and takes the other.

Write an essay (roughly 1,200–1,800 words) on what spreadless cases, if they are possible, show about the discounting account of akrasia. Weigh at least the following three positions against one another:

(a) spreadless akrasia is possible, so the discounting account leaves a substantial slice of the phenomenon untouched and is at best incomplete;
(b) no actual choice is spreadless — the fine temporal structure of any real payoff stream is what constitutes the phenomenon, so the objection has stipulated away its own subject matter;
(c) the account can absorb spreadless cases by redescribing them at ever finer temporal grain, but only at the price of becoming compatible with any behavior whatever, and so of ceasing to explain anything.

Be precise about the conditions under which the familiar reversal result does and does not obtain, including where (b)'s appeal to fine grain becomes unfalsifiable if it does.
