Just came out of a four hour arguement with a friend about left and right and my head is spinning, so I want you to play her side properly instead of being nice to me.

Here's the view I've talked myself into. The usual options are that left/right is one axis with real content at both ends, or two axes, or a meaningless historical accident with a stubborn name. I think it's real but radically asymmetric: the left has positive content — a substantive commitment, roughly to equality, that actually generates programs — while the right has no positive content at all. The right is purely reactive. It's whatever is arrayed against the left's current proposal, and it only holds together as a coalition because there's a left standing across from it. And the stronger version, which is the one I actually wanna defend: there is no right-wing political philosophy that isn't parasitic on a prior left proposal. Burke needed the French Revolution to become Burke. Anti-communism needed communism. The whole counterrevolutionary tradition is named after the thing it's countering.

Her counter, which I couldn't dislodge: the right has its own positive commitments — order, hierarchy, particularity, the standing of inherited institutions, inequality as a fact to be accommodated rather than remediated — and my thesis only looks compelling because I'm sampling from 1789 onward in one corner of the world. Worse, she says my view can't distinguish the right from any opposition movement, because the left was reactive too, against feudalism, against capital. So either reactivity is universal and my asymmetry collapses, or it isnt the thing doing the discriminating.

So: what would count against me?

- Rank the defeaters, fatal down to merely awkward, and for each say what the historical record or the conceptual structure would have to look like.
- Which cases are genuinely hard for me — stable hierarchical conservatisms, European or not, pre-1789 or not, that weren't answering to anything left-wing — and which only look hard until you redescribe them? Then tell me how much of the record I can absorb by redescription before that becomes a tell rather than a reply.
- What does my view predict that the symmetric view doesn't? Things like: what happens to right coalitions when the left threat recedes, the ratio of polemic to constructive work in right intellectual life, the right ending up defending yesterday's left program as tradition. Flag which of these could actually be checked and which are only checkable in principle.
- Whether I've qualified the thesis into unfalsifiability. And if I have, whether that's fatal here — it's an interpretive claim about a tradition, not an empirical theory, so maybe redescription is the job rather than a bug. Honest answer, not flattery.
- Which defeaters a committed defender would concede as counting against us and which they'd wave off, and where you think I've already crossed the line into waving.

No bibliography and don't tell me to go read anybody. Cases and arguments, described in your own words.
