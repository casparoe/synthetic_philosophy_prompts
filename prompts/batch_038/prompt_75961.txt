I was at a reading group last Thursday and two people got into a long arguement about Dennett's creature taxonomy — the Darwinian / Skinnerian / Popperian / Gregorian ladder — specifically about where large language models sit on it. They went around in circles and I left more confused than when I arrived, so I'd like a proper referee report.

For context: Dev is a CS postdoc, Marianne is a computational neuroscientist. Neither of them was strawmanning, as far as I could tell. Here's roughly how it went.

(1) Dev: the ladder is really a hierarchy of control architectures, and it's ordered by how much of the policy gets fixed where. Darwinian creatures have policies written into the genome. Skinnerian ones can revise a policy online from reward and punishment. Popperian ones carry an internal model of the environment, so they can run candidate actions in simulation before committing to them. Gregorian ones get their models from an external store of artifacts built by other minds — writing, notation, tools, accumulated technique. On that reading an LLM is Gregorian nearly by definition: it was built by ingesting the external symbol store of an entire species, which is exactly the Gregorian criterion.

(2) Marianne: at most it's Skinnerian, and arguably it doesn't even get that. Its weights were selected by a training process rather than by anything it did, it doesn't update at all once deployed, and it never selects an action, so there's nothing an internal model could be *for*. Calling it Popperian or Gregorian is just pasting our labels onto something that lacks the whole embedded, stakes-having life that gave those labels their content in the first place.

(3) Dev: but it does simulate. Producing a continuation is rolling out a model of how things go, and bad rollouts get abandoned partway through. And on the world-model point, there are now plenty of probing results where you train a network purely on game transcripts it never sees rendered, and it grows something like a readable internal representation of the board that you can also perturb causally and change the network's moves. If that isn't a Popperian world model, the term has no content left.

(4) Marianne: then the taxonomy collapses, and that's my actual objection. If ingesting external symbols is enough for Gregorian, a library catalogue is Gregorian. If rolling out continuations is enough for Popperian, a physics simulator is Popperian. Your version turns the ladder into a checklist that any sufficiently large statistical model ticks, so it stops discriminating anything. The whole point of the Gregorian rung was supposed to be that the creature uses tools it didn't invent to widen what it's able to think at all — and you cant test that from the outside. If you can't test it, the rung isn't a hypothesis, it's a compliment.

(5) Dev: fine, then add a rung. Retrieval-augmented systems are a different kind of thing — they don't generate or transform the symbol store, they index and re-serve it. Call them Archivist creatures, sitting between Skinnerian and Gregorian. The ladder survives by being extended, which is what a decent taxonomy does when it meets a case nobody anticipated, and the fact that you can't tell an LLM from an Archivist without an argument about what's going on inside it is your epistemic problem, not a defect in the taxonomy.

(6) Marianne: a ladder that grows a new rung every time it fails to classify something isn't a taxonomy being extended, it's a mood being defended.

So: please adjudicate this move by move. For each of the six, tell me who's right and how right, and don't just grade the conclusion — grade the inference, because I suspect some of these moves have a defensible point inside a bad argument. Then tell me what both of them missed. My own hunch, which I couldn't defend at the time, is that they're fighting about whether the ladder is a claim about mechanisms or a claim about evolutionary history, and neither of them ever says so out loud. I'd also like to know whether Dev's Archivist proposal is a real improvement or exactly the escape hatch Marianne accuses it of being — and whether "Popperian" as applied to a system with no actions and no stakes is a coherent use of the term or just an equivocation between simulating and simulating-in-order-to-act.

Format: give me a table with these columns, in this order —

- move: the number, 1 through 6
- who: Dev or Marianne
- asserts: the move's claim compressed into one clause
- verdict: exactly one of clearly right / mostly right / mixed / mostly wrong / clearly wrong
- strongest counter: the best available response to this move, one sentence
- load-bearing: yes or no — does the overall outcome depend on this move, or is it decoration

After the table, roughly 300 words on what neither of them addressed, then a single line naming who came out ahead overall and on what basis.

And be upfront about your own footing here: say which of your six verdicts you'd be most likely to revise, what argument or piece of evidence would flip it, and whether you think a neutral referee in this dispute is really just smuggling in their own priors about what counts as having a mind. If there's a step where you're reasoning from a gloss of Dennett's categories rather than from anything you'd stand behind as precise, flag that step instead of papering over it.
