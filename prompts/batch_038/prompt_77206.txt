ok so I do trust & safety at a mid-size discussion site — local news and politics, couple million monthly actives, basically reddit-shaped — and last quarter we shipped a context-note system cribbed from community notes, with one change of our own that i now think is doing way more philosophical work than anyone on the team realized. a colleague keeps telling me it's "just crowd moderation with extra steps" and doesn't commit us to anything, and i can't articulate why she's wrong. help.

how ours works: any user over a karma threshold can attach a proposed context note to a post. the note only goes live if it collects enough helpful ratings, and — the bridging part — our ranker refuses to surface a note unless it's rated helpful by people whose rating history puts them on the opposite side of the issue from the note's author. we infer each rater's "perspective" from their past ratings, so a note that only delights people who already agree with each other never shows up no matter how many of them there are. the thing we added is what the team calls the conversion requirement: a note has to get atleast one helpful rating from someone who upvoted the very post the note is attached to. plus a penalty rule — if a note collects enough cross-perspective unhelpful ratings it gets killed and the author loses rating privileges for 30 days.

so what i want is a writeup of what this design actually presupposes. my hunch is it's committed to something like: when people who've never agreed about anything both accept a claim, you should move toward that claim, and how far you move doesn't depend on which side had better access to the facts in the first place. is that the right reconstruction, what's the standard name for that view, and what would have to be true for it to be right? does counting a cross-perspective helpful rating as evidence commit us to treating the two sides as roughly equally reliable about the disputed claim, or could the system be defended on seperate, weaker grounds — e.g. that bridging only shows a note isn't partisan, not that it's true? and here's the part i genuinely can't resolve. i read somewhere a distinction between communities that merely insulate you from other voices and communities that actively train you to distrust outsiders, where distrust of the other side is a feature members are taught in advance, along with the warning that any correction will come from an enemy. under that second setup the conversion requirement looks self-defeating: a note survives only if it's acceptable to precisely the people conditioned to reject anything from outside, and then our penalty rule punishes whoever wrote a true note. is the whole scheme incoherent, or only incoherent given assumptions about who our raters are that might not hold? and if we dropped the view it currently assumes, what would we have to change — kill conversion, weight raters by track record, let moderators override? start with your bottom line in about three sentences, then the reconstruction, and please keep the whole thing under 700 words, i want to circulate it before a meeting rather than read a paper.
