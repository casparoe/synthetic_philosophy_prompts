Not a philosopher — I do behavioural ecology, frogs mostly — but I read philosophy of mind on the side and there's a move I keep seeing that I can't find a name for.

Setup, roughly as I understand it. Suppose you think what a signal means is fixed by how it got there: what it was selected for, what the mechanism was recruited to detect, what reinforced it. Now take a case where the environment has moved on, the original trigger is rare or gone, and something else reliably sets the signal off, with the receivers responding to that other thing. You get a fork: does the signal still carry the old content, or the new one? I know that fork is a real and well-known dispute. That's not what I'm asking about.

What I keep noticing is what happens *before* people get to the fork. They seem to settle the verdict they want first and then pick which history is the content-fixing one accordingly — and the picking never looks arbitrary, because both histories are genuinely there and both stories are tellable. Two instances:

- Someone who wants to insist the frog's tongue-flick is really about flies, and not about any small dark thing that moves, will lean hard on the fact that selection is still running, that the ongoing economy of reinforcement is what matters.
- Someone arguing that a state in a machine that got wired up by accident, with no design history behind it, nevertheless genuinely means something, will insist that what matters is the role the state has been playing and being relied on inside the system since it got there.

Same structural choice, opposite verdicts, and in each case the history that gets priviledged is the one that yields the conclusion the author wanted on some further question (does content need a designer, can meaning be natural at all). Nobody flags it, beacuse each individual choice is defensible on its own and there's no independent handle on which history is the right one to consult.

So: is there an established concept for this — selecting among candidate content-fixing histories after you already know which answer you want? I know it sits near the disjunction problem and near the Swampman-type cases, and that the theories in this area get called etiological or proper-function theories, but those are names for the underlying puzzle, not for the argumentative practice around it. And if there's no name, can you coin one and define it tightly enough that I could tell it apart from (a) ordinary special pleading, and (b) the perfectly legitimate business of using intuitions about what a signal means as evidence for a theory of content? The (b) boundary is the part I actually care about — how do you tell when the intuition is doing the evidential work honestly and when the history-selection has been quietly reverse-engineered from it?

Bottom line first, please: one line saying either "yes, it goes by X" or "no, nothing established." Then the rest in 400 words max.
