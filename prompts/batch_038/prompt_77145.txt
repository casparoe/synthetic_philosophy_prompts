spent like four hours arguing with a friend last night (he does alignment work, I mostly just read a lot) about process vs outcome supervision, and at some point he said my side wasn't a position, it was a mood. That stung, so I want an actual audit rather than a lecture. Here's what I hold:

1. What we care about is the result, so outcome supervision is the only kind that's anchored to anything real. Process rewards are learned guesses about whether a step looks right, and anything learned like that gets Goodharted the second you optimize hard against it.
2. But you can't train long chains of reasoning on outcome-only signal — the reward lands at the very end and there's no way to know which of forty steps did the damage. So you need step-level feedback.
3. Humans can reliably judge whether an individual step is correct; checking is way easier than producing, and that's why process supervision is more scalable than people give it credit for.
4. The domains where any of this matters most are exactly the ones with no cheap ground-truth check on the final answer, so outcome supervision there has to come from human preference over finished outputs.
5. Chain-of-thought traces are largely post-hoc rationalization. I don't think the trace is the computation.
6. Process rewards should be trained on the policy's own on-policy traces, not on traces from some stronger model, becuase otherwise you're just distilling somebody else's reasoning style.

Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only look contradictory until you say what job each claim is doing. Rank the real tensions by severity instead of listing them flat, and for each one say what I'd have to give up and what giving it up costs me. Then tell me whether there's a single revision that cleans up most of the damage at once. Be blunt — I'd rather find out from you than from him.
