I audited a joint seminar last term run by our philosophy of science group together with a philosophy of biology group. One session concerned Popper's changing assessment of Darwinism over his career, and near the end two participants — I will call them Miriam and Daniel — got into an argument that ran through several rounds without either conceding anything. I took notes at the time and have reconstructed the exchange as faithfully as I can below, with the moves numbered.

1. Daniel opened by saying that Popper's original objection was that the principle of natural selection is close to tautological, since fitness is defined by actual survival and reproductive success, so the principle amounts to saying that those who survive are those who survive. He then pointed out that Popper withdrew the objection in the mid-1970s, conceding that the theory is testable, though difficult to test — and that a few years later he went back on the withdrawal, describing Darwinism instead as a metaphysical research programme rather than a strictly testable theory. Daniel's conclusion was that the vacillation is itself the datum worth attending to: a demarcation criterion that returns three different verdicts on the same body of theory within a few decades, with no corresponding change in the biology, is not tracking anything in the world.

2. Miriam replied that this misreads Popper's vocabulary. For Popper, she said, "metaphysical" is not a term of abuse; he spent his career rejecting the positivist equation of the metaphysical with the meaningless, and he repeatedly argued that unfalsifiable but fruitful programmes — atomism was his stock example — have advanced science more than premature testability has. Testability also comes in degrees on his account and can increase as auxiliary hypotheses accumulate. So the later characterization is neither a demotion nor a reversion: it is a claim about a framework, whereas the retracted tautology charge was a claim about a principle. Two objects, two verdicts, no contradiction.

3. Daniel said that the distinction rescues the criterion only by emptying it. If the framework is itself never testable and only the hypotheses generated within it are, then the framework has no content beyond being a name for a family of testable claims; and any theory whatsoever can be immunized by assigning the tested parts to its descendants and the untestable residue to the "programme." He added a second charge: that Miriam was sliding between two different objections. That natural selection is tautological is a semantic claim about how a term is defined; that it is unfalsifiable is a methodological claim about the absence of potential falsifiers. The two come apart in both directions — a principle can be non-tautologous and yet untestable, and a tautologous schema can sit inside a highly testable research programme. Popper conflated them from the start, Daniel said, and that is why his verdict kept moving.

4. Miriam granted the distinction but said it cuts against him. What made the tautology charge tempting, on her account, was never a definition; it was an observation about the order in which evidence arrives. Adaptive explanations are frequently constructed after the outcome is known, so the attribution of function is calibrated to the result. Popper had precisely the notion needed for this, she said: what matters is whether a prediction was made in advance and was risky. On that reading his late position is not a weakening at all — it says that Darwinism is valuable as an engine for generating risky predictions, which is what he always valued metaphysical programmes for.

5. Daniel answered that Popper had then conceded the substance to the biologists who accused him of misreading them, and had merely re-clothed the concession in his own terminology. He put a challenge: name one empirical finding that would have led Popper to say that Darwinism had been falsified rather than revised.

6. Miriam said the honest answer is that there is no such finding, and that this is a feature of frameworks generally rather than a defect peculiar to Darwinism. She said Daniel's challenge, pressed consistently, would demolish Newtonian mechanics as well, since no single experiment can falsify a theory without assumptions about initial conditions, instrument reliability, and background hypotheses absorbing the blow.

I would like a point-by-point adjudication of this exchange. Specifically:

(a) Rate each of the six moves on a scale running from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a brief justification of each rating. Where a move bundles more than one claim, rate the claims separately rather than averaging them.

(b) Rule on the framework/descendants distinction as such. Is it a principled deployment of resources Popper independently had, or is it an ad hoc rescue of the sort Popper himself condemned — he had a well-developed account of immunizing auxiliary hypotheses and of the stratagems by which theories are protected from refutation, and the question is whether Miriam's move qualifies as one of them. Note that the answer may differ depending on whether she is interpreting Popper or defending the criterion.

(c) Assess Daniel's tautology/unfalsifiability distinction on its own terms, including his claim that the two come apart in both directions. Does the distinction do the work he needs it to do in this argument, or only in some other argument he has not actually made?

(d) Decide whether Daniel's falsifier challenge in move 5 is fair, and whether Miriam's Newtonian reply in move 6 disposes of it or merely changes the subject. Address explicitly whether the two cases are relevantly alike, given that one concerns a law-like theory with quantitative predictions and the other a historical framework applied to particular lineages.

(e) Here is something that struck me as an auditor and that neither of them raised. Both spoke throughout as though "the theory" were a single thing, but there seem to be at least three separable levels: a general template linking trait, environment, and reproductive outcome; particular filled-in instances of that template with specific trait–environment assignments; and the auxiliary apparatus used to fix those assignments and to reconstruct past conditions. Please say at which of these levels Popper's criterion gains any purchase at all, whether the verdict differs across levels, and — if it does — whether that difference explains his shifting assessments. Then say whether such an explanation exonerates him or indicts him: does a criterion whose verdict turns on which level the analyst happens to be considering still function as a criterion of demarcation?

(f) Conclude by stating which of the two is more right overall, and whether anything in the exchange generalizes — that is, whether it exposes a difficulty for falsifiability as a demarcation criterion as such, a difficulty only for its application to historical sciences, or no real difficulty at all.

Two constraints on your answer. Please adjudicate the arguments as actually reported rather than reconstructing either participant into a stronger position than the one they took. And please work from the exchange itself; I am not looking for references or further reading, only for your judgment on the reasoning.
