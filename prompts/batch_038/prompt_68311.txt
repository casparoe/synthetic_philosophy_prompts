I've been doing the exercise from Hofstadter's *Le Ton beau de Marot*, only I'm not translating Marot himself — I wrote a ten-line French dizain as a practice source, addressed to a girl who's ill, ten syllables to a line, rhymes ababbccdcd, and then translated it under the rule that both the count and the scheme have to survive exactly. English version is done. What I can't do is say what rule I actually followed, because my stated one keeps failing.

My stated one, which I've been calling positional fidelity: a feature of the source counts as preserved only if it turns up in the same line number, not merely somewhere in the poem. Plus a scoring rule — sound and sense get separate tallies, so a saved rhyme can never pay for a lost nuance or vice versa, no trading across categories.

The decisions, with my gut verdict on each:

1. Line 2: source says "la main", I needed a syllable, wrote "the pale hand". Not fine. It costs nothing semantically and it's true of the poem's world, but I've marked it as a cheat.
2. Line 6: source "sur vous" left me two syllables short, wrote "upon you" instead of "on you". Fine. That's the same mechanism as (1) — filler bought to make the count — and I've given it the opposite verdict. This is the pair I keep circling.
3. Line 4 ends on "vers", which in French is verse, toward, and worms all at once. I gave up on line 4, rendered it flat, and put a worms/verse pun in line 8, where the source had nothing clever at all. Fine — the pun is still in the poem once, and line 8 could afford it.
4. Line 9 has a pun on the girl's name and I simply dropped it, no compensation anywhere. Also fine, because keeping it would have wrecked the c-rhyme. But (3) and (4) look like the same choice made differently: there I paid to relocate, here I paid nothing.
5. Lines 1 and 3: I rejected "love"/"move" for that slot as a rhyme you only see. Not fine. Lines 2 and 4: I used "again"/"rain", which only rhymes if the reader says "agen". Fine, no hesitation.
6. Lines 6 and 7: couldn't find a pair, used "light"/"light". Not fine, logged as a failure — even though the source opens on "Douceur" and closes on "douceur" and I rendered that as "Rose" (flower) opening and "rose" (past tense of rise) closing, which I think is the best thing in my whole version. Identical word twice: praiseworthy at the frame, disgraceful at 6/7.
7. Line 3: I kept the French inversion and it reads as fake-archaic. Not fine. Line 7: I inverted ordinary English word order to save the c-rhyme. Fine.
8. The source echoes "douceur" at lines 2 and 9, no rhyme relation, just an echo. I wrote "sweetness" and "gentleness" to avoid repeating myself. Not fine — I killed the echo. But I use near-synonyms freely elsewhere, e.g. "ill" and "unwell" for "malade", and that's fine.

Two cases I genuinely haven't decided. My source has a deliberate stumble at line 7, nine syllables instead of ten; I regularised it to ten in English, and now I wonder whether a translator owes it to the original to keep the limp. And my frame in (6) is an upgrade — the source repeats one word, I found a homograph pair that does more work — and I don't know if I've earned the right to make the poem better than it was.
