I'm trying to work out whether "left" and "right" actually pick out anything, going by my own gut calls on particular positions rather than by what people say the labels are supposed to mean. Here's my list.

- Raising the minimum wage: left. Cutting capital gains taxes: right. Easy ones.
- A universal basic income paid in cash, funded by scrapping in-kind welfare programs: I call it right — it's the old free-market move — even though basically everyone who wants it today is on the left.
- Tariffs to protect domestic manufacturing: right when a nationalist wants them, left when a union wants them. Same policy, and I hand out two different verdicts depending on who's holding the sign.
- Abolishing minimum lot sizes and parking requirements so more housing gets built: honestly can't place this. It reads like deregulation and property rights, which is right-wing language, but the people pushing it are left.
- Banning abortion: right, obviously — except its a massive expansion of state power over private life, which is supposed to be what the right objects to.
- Decriminalizing drugs: left. Also a removal of state coercion.
- School vouchers: right. Universal pre-K: left. But a childcare subsidy that follows the kid to whatever provider the parents choose feels left to me too, even though it's structurally a voucher.
- Carbon tax with an equal per-person dividend: left. Cap-and-trade where the permits get handed free to the utilities that already exist: right. An economist friend tells me I have that backwards.
- Restricting immigration to protect the wages of low-skilled native workers: right, but the argument is explicitly about protecting the worse-off, which I thought was the left's whole thing.

My tentative principle is roughly: the left wants to use collective power to flatten hierarchies of status and money, the right wants to protect markets and inherited custom from that flattening. But abortion and drugs blow a hole in it, and protectionism maybe does too.

Is there a principle — one axis, two, whatever it takes — that actually delivers these verdicts instead of just most of them? I want a committed answer, not a menu of options: pick the account you think is right and argue for it. Then tell me what it says about three positions I didn't list (mandatory national service, a law requiring workers to elect half the board of every large company, and letting people sell food out of their home kitchens in a poor neighborhood without a license), and which of my verdicts you'd force me to give up.

Please use these sections, in this order: **Bottom line** (3-4 sentences); **The principle** (~200 words, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a case I've never seen); **Running it over my list** (~300 words, flagging the two or three verdicts it strains hardest on); **The three new cases** (~200 words); **What you'd make me drop** (~150 words).
