I run a 400-acre row crop operation outside Kearney, and my brother-in-law Dale does financial planning in Omaha. Twice in the last few years I've had to choose between a safe option and a gamble put up by the same equipment dealer, and both times I'm pretty confident I picked right. Dale made a spreadsheet afterward and told me my two choices are flat-out contradictory, and that if he felt like it he could arrange a run of trades that would bleed money out of me every single time I faced a decision shaped like that. I didn't really follow the second part, but it stuck with me and now I keep thinking about it when I'm signing stuff.

Here's the two decisions. Spring of '22 the dealer was clearing out a demo fleet and offered me either a straight $40,000 rebate on the order, guaranteed, no strings, or a "mystery" version where I had a 10% shot at a $250,000 rebate, an 89% shot at the same $40,000 I could've just taken, and a 1% shot at nothing whatsoever. I took the guaranteed forty grand and did not lose one minute of sleep over it. Then last fall a different rep from the same company had a leftover promo where I could either take an 11% chance at $40,000 with an 89% chance of zero, or a 10% chance at $250,000 with a 90% chance of zero. I took the 10% swing at the quarter million, and I'd do it again. Dale says those two picks can't both be rational: he says the 89% chunk of the first deal was identical in both options, so it ought to cancel out, and once you cancel it what you're left staring at is exactly the second decision, where I went the other way. So either I was wrong in the spring or I was wrong in the fall, and probably he thinks I was wrong both times.

The thing that bugs me is that when he walks through it on paper I sort of see the shape of what he means, but standing there in the dealership the two situations just dont feel like the same situation. In the first one there's a version of events where I walk away with nothing and have to explain to my wife and to the bank why I turned down free money for a lottery ticket, and that version of events felt like it was really on the table, like it was mine to avoid. In the second one, going home empty was already the likely outcome whichever way I turned, so nothing felt like it was being given up, it was just a question of how big the upside ticket was. I'm not trying to be cute about this or defend myself reflexively, I genuinely think I'd make both calls again tomorrow. So: is Dale right that I'm being irrational, or is there something legitimate in how I'm responding to those two setups that his spreadsheet is missing? And if he is right, how far does that spread -- because I make calls like this constantly, on seed contracts, on whether to hedge the fall delivery, on whether to patch the grain dryer or replace it outright, and I'd like to know if I should stop trusting my gut across the board or just in some narrow cases.
