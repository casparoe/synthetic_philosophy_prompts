Spent three hours last night arguing with a friend about mathematical platonism versus nominalism and we got absolutely nowhere, which is maddening because we agree on everything checkable. He's the nominalist, and his line is that the indispensability argument died the moment Field showed you can rewrite at least chunks of physics — Newtonian gravitation anyway — without referring to numbers or sets or functions at all, so math is a convenient shorthand and if it's dispensable in principle the platonist never had an argument, only a habit. My line is that the argument was never really about whether you could grind the math out of the theory, it's about whether the math is doing genuine work when it helps explain and confirm things — like when you explain some regularity by citing a symmetry in the mathematics rather than any physical mechanism, or when the mathematical machinery is what gets the novel predictions to come out — and there's definately something there the nominalist owes us an account of. He also conceded-ish that the reconstruction trick may not extend past classical physics, something about quantum mechanics needing Hilbert spaces in a way that isn't cosmetic, but he calls that unfinished business, not a concession.

Here's the thing: we agree on every concrete claim about numbers, every equation, all the physics, and neither of us thinks the other is confused about any of it. So my hypothesis is that this is mostly a verbal dispute about what "there are" is doing in each of our mouths, with a residue — the bit about explanatory indispensability — that's either a real metaphysical question or an aesthetic preference in a trenchcoat.

Which is it? Is the disagreement verbal, substantive, or two different questions wearing one label — his being about whether we need a non-mathematical story for why the math tracks the world, mine being about what makes an explanation explanatory? And what would actually count as progress: a distinction that splits the issue cleanly, a worked example that discriminates between the two, a formal result about what the nominalizations can and can't buy you, or just both of us admitting we can't say what we're disagreeing about? I want the diagnosis, not another tour of the positions — I've read that tour.
