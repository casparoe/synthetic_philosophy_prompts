My roommate is on a neuropsych rotation and came home describing pain asymbolia: people with damage around the insula who say they feel the pinch perfectly well — they can locate it, rate its intensity — but don't mind it. Apparently some joke about it while it's happening. Her brother, who reads a lot of philosophy stuff, said that settles the question: pain isn't bad in itself, badness is just an attitude we take toward a sensation, since you can have the sensation with the badness switched off. She said that's backwards. Asymbolics don't withdraw, they let the damaging thing keep going, they get hurt and don't notice — so the case shows their pain-response is broken, not that pain's badness is optional. He said she's sliding between two things: the tissue damage is bad for them, fine, but harm isn't what makes pain hurt. She said then he owes an account of why pain is the paradigm case of a bad experience at all, and that a theory on which this exact feeling could be perfectly fine to have has lost touch with the phenomenon. He said a theory where "bad" just means "I dislike it" can't explain why we should ever dislike anything either, so they're both stuck and neither would concede. I honestly couldn't follow half of it. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall — and point out anything both of them are definately missing?
