So I want a case analysis, and I'd rather you be rigorous about the mapping than give me the usual summary of the controversy.

The episode: the UK's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Bill, autumn 2021. As I have it — correct me if I've got the order wrong — the bill as introduced defined "animal" as vertebrates only, so all invertebrates were out. There was a committee-stage vote in mid-November 2021 where the government whips opposed an amendment that would have brought lobsters, crabs and octopuses in, and the story that came out of it was that ministers planned to boil lobsters alive, which the government then said was nonsense, there was never such a plan. About four days later a government amendment went in adding all decapod crustaceans and all cephalopod molluscs to the definition, right after a government-commissioned review out of LSE reviewed something like 300 studies and concluded those groups should be regarded as sentient. The Act got Royal Assent in spring 2022. What the Act actually does is set up a committee inside an executive agency that can write reports on whether government policy has had all due regard to how it might adversely affect the welfare of animals as sentient beings; the committee makes recommendations, government responds, and the Act explicitly says those recommendations must respect provisions and customs relating to religious rites, cultural traditions and regional heritage. It also doesn't reach policy in devolved areas. Meanwhile cephalopods had already been protected in laboratory research for years, and the practices the review recommended against — eyestalk ablation in shrimp broodstock, declawing, live sale to untrained handlers, boiling without stunning — remained legal. Switzerland banned boiling lobsters alive back in 2018; the UK didn't.

The lens I want: the precautionary principle applied to animal sentience, in the specific form where the claim is that when there's credible but non-conclusive evidence of sentience in a group, policy should treat that group as sentient rather than wait for certainty. The UK episode looks like it settled status and said nothing at all about weight, and then handed the whole thing to a procedural due-regard mechanism rather than to any substantive prohibition.
