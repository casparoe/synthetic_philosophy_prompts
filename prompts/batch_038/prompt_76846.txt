I'm moderating an adversarial collaboration on whether dreams are experiences, and the two camps have been talking past each other for years. Camp A holds that a dream report is a report of something that phenominally happened during sleep — dreams are, at least sometimes, immersive experiences you were in, roughly hallucination-like in kind. Camp B holds the Dennett-style line: what we call a dream is a confabulation inserted into memory at or near waking, so there was never an experience there to report. One wrinkle I want built in: Camp B is actually two different arguments — Dennett's causal/cognitive one, and the older Malcolm-style conceptual point about what a sentence like "I dreamt that p" even commits you to — and the joint statement should not let those two lean on each other or collapse into a single "anti-experientialist" position.

Draft the document both camps could put their names to:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides accept — about what the empirical data actually are, about what would need explaining either way, and about what "experience" has to involve for the disagreement to be substantive rather than a quibble over the word.
2. A disagreements table: each row a precise statement of one disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position, with a column marking which camp thinks that particular row is merely verbal.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would genuinely move at least one side — and for each, a specific case or piece of evidence both camps would agree bears on it (prearranged eye-signal reports from lucid sleepers, drift across repeated retellings, reports from what subjects call deep dreamless sleep, whatever you judge does the work), with each side's prediction written down in advance of the results rather than after.
4. An explicit ruling on whether the dispute over "experience" is verbal, and if only part of it is, exactly which part and why.

Keep it neutral throughout — if I can tell one side got the easier phrasing anywhere, I'm sending it back.
