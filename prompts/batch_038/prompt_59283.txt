Was up way to late last night arguing with my friend Dan about whether the Church was just being dense when it went after Galileo. Every time I tried to say it wasn't a simple mix-up he'd drop the same line: 'the Bible teaches us how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go' — a saying he swears Galileo himself repeated approvingly from some cardinal. His take is that scripture was never making claims about astronomy to begin with, so there was literally nothing to fight about and Galileo was in the clear from the start. When I said the real question is what it would even take to justify reinterpreting a passage like the one where Joshua stops the sun, he said the slogan makes that question empty, and then read out part of Galileo's letter to the Grand Duchess Christina as proof: the bit about how 'the holy Bible and the phenomena of nature proceed alike from the divine Word.'

I don't think he wins with that but I can't say why. Can you pull apart what the slogan could actually be claiming — the separate theses hiding in it, from the total platitude up to the really strong version — and for each say whether it holds up and whether it gets Dan his conclusion? Then tell me which version Galileo himself actually held, and whether the letter passage he read out backs him or backs me.
