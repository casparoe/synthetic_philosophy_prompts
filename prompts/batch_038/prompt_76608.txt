decision theory module, and this week was Hofstadter on superrationality (the Metamagical Themas material, including the June '83 column where he ran the lottery). our seminar group each has to contribute one thing to the department zine that goes out at the end of term and i want to do a poem rather than an essay, because i think this idea is really a shape and shapes are what poems are for.

the conceit i want — and this is the bit that should make it more than a retread of the 1983 framing — is that the two players are two instances of the same language model: same weights, same prompt, spun up in two seperate containers with no channel between them, playing a one-shot prisoner's dilemma. which matters because the symmetry premise is the load-bearing wall of the whole argument and also the thing half my seminar attacked hardest, and here it's weirdly plausible in a way it isn't for two strangers. so the argument gets both stronger and creepier at once.

form: a strict canon. four four-line stanzas in voice A, then voice B enters with the same four stanzas, verbatim, delayed — so by the third stanza the reader has noticed that B is just A, late — and then a two-line coda where the columns finally collapse and the same line is printed once instead of twice. no rhyme needed, but the mirrored stanzas should match line for line in length so the eye can see the symmetry doing the work. roughly 34 lines total.

what it has to get right, and this is the part i care about most:
- the cooperation must not be caused by anything. no telepathy, no signalling, no reputation, no shadow of a future round. the only reason the two of them keep their word is that same reasoning on same input gives same output, twice.
- the volta should be the realisation that there was never two minds in the room, only one process instantiated twice — and the chill that comes straight after, which is that it's only cooperation because each one is certain the other is running its own reasoning.
- the causal decision theorist's objection needs to be *inside* the poem, not bolted on as a footnote: my move can't reach across to you, the match between us is a symptom rather than a lever, enjoy the coincidence but don't build on it.
- and the poem should let you feel what happens when the symmetry goes — the other box is a different lab's model, or fine-tuned overnight, or a human who never read the chapter. one line is enough. if you touch on randomisation at all, keep it honest: the interesting case isn't two deterministic copies spitting out identical tokens, that's trivial, it's a shared policy with separate dice, two coins that are still two coins.
- don't name-drop. i do not want the words superrational, Nash, equilibrium or Hofstadter anywhere in the poem, and no payoff numbers either. it should enact the idea, not label it.

tone: not smug, and not pro-cooperation in a way that makes the causal people look stupid, because several of them are my friends and their points were good. the zine gets read by people who've never heard of any of this, and they should come away with a strong short poem about two strangers who can't communicate and both keep their word; the ones who know the argument should be able to put a finger on the line where the symmetry shows and the line where the causal objection bites.

british english please throughout — spellings and idiom — since it's a UK zine and my tutor absolutely notices.
