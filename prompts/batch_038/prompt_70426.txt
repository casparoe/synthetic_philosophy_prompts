I am auditing a graduate seminar on personal identity and the metaphysics of mind uploading, and my reading group has now spent two sessions on the gradual-replacement argument: the claim that if one neuron at a time is replaced by a silicon module that is an exact functional isomorph of it, then, because there is no moment at which anything detectable happens, experience must be preserved throughout and the person survives. The load-bearing step is usually defended in a Chalmers-style way — if the qualia were fading while the functional organization stayed fixed, the subject would eventually notice the mismatch and say so, and such a report would itself be a functional difference between the organic and the artificial case, which by hypothesis there cannot be; so the fading hypothesis is false. My friends and I keep trying to construct a counterexample in which the subject's phenomenology really does fade away to nothing while she remains, by construction, functionally identical to her former self and never once notices, but every version we have produced cheats in an obvious way: the technicians sedate her, or edit her memories, or program the modules to answer that nothing has changed regardless of what is going on, or simply announce at the end that she is now a zombie without explaining why nothing along the way was detectable. I would like you to build a version that does not cheat, because I suspect the case can be made and I want to see exactly what machinery it requires and where the machinery is vulnerable.

The specifications are these. First, replacement proceeds at a rate of roughly one neuron per day over a quarter of a century, each module being an exact functional isomorph of the neuron it replaces — same input-output profile relative to its neighbors, same plasticity rules, same conduction latencies to within tens of microseconds. There is only ever one subject: no copying, no branching, no duplicate bodies, so that this is not covertly a fission case, and no exotic physics beyond the isomorphs themselves. Second, she is awake, healthy, and living an ordinary life throughout; she is not drugged, hypnotized, sleep-deprived, or amnesic, and nothing whatsoever is done to her memory or to her concepts. Third, she is enrolled in a demanding self-monitoring protocol: daily structured phenomenology reports plus periodic forced-choice discrimination tasks aimed precisely at the kinds of loss in question (saturation of reds, fine hue discriminations, the felt presence of imagery, the vividness of remembered scenes), and she is strongly motivated to report degradation honestly, both by a substantial payment and by her own stake in the outcome. Fourth, at the end of the sequence her phenomenology is gone entirely: she behaves, reasons, discriminates, and reports exactly as her organic counterpart would, and there is nothing it is like to be her. Fifth, at no point has she had any detectable reason to think anything had changed — her reports, her discrimination performance, and her dispositions are, at every stage, exactly what they would have been in the world where nothing faded. Sixth, and this is the real constraint, the mechanism delivering that fifth condition must not be deception, drugging, memory editing, conceptual impoverishment, inattention, or stipulation. It must be structural: the point has to be that the introspective channel through which a change would be noticed lies inside the very region being replaced, so that there is never an unaffected observer within the system whose verdict could diverge from the output of the modules. Call this the co-replacement constraint. Finally, build the paired control case, identical in every respect except the order of replacement, in which the self-monitoring apparatus is replaced last while everything else is already silicon; there she should catch the discrepancy, precisely because organic introspection is still looking at silicon experience. The pair must isolate exactly one variable: the order of replacement relative to the reporting machinery.

What I want back is the following. (i) The two scenarios, no more than about a page in total, told concretely rather than schematically — pick a day in the middle of the sequence and describe what she does, what goes in the journal, what happens in that week's discrimination session. (ii) A step-by-step demonstration that the five conditions are jointly satisfied rather than merely declared: trace the causal path from the fading of a given quale to the absence of a report, and identify the point at which a divergence from the no-fading world would have to appear and why it cannot appear there. (iii) A statement of which premise of the argument fails under co-replacement, formulated explicitly as a premise, and a verdict on whether the failure sits in the claim that fading would be noticed, in the claim that noticing would manifest as a functional difference, or in the inference from those two claims to the impossibility of fading; where you refer to the argument, please work from my paraphrase above rather than reconstructing anyone's original wording. (iv) The corresponding demonstration for the control case, showing why the same premise survives there and hence why the argument goes through, together with what the two cases jointly establish about the reach of the argument — in particular, whether co-replacement shows the argument to be false, or merely to have a narrower domain than its defenders assume. (v) An honest diagnosis of which single feature of the co-replacement case is doing the work, what happens if that feature is weakened or removed — for instance if only half the introspective channel is replaced, or if module latencies drift by a millisecond over the years — and whether the case then collapses back into one of the cheating versions I described. (vi) The strongest reply available to a defender of the original argument, which I expect will be that an exact functional isomorph simply cannot differ in phenomenal character, so that a case stipulating both perfect isomorphism and total fading is not a possible case at all; assess whether that reply is an argument or a restatement of the conclusion, and say what an independent reason for accepting it would have to look like. You do not need to cite anything or supply a bibliography — I have the primary texts and it is the reasoning I am after.
