So I've been roped into refereeing an adverserial collaboration between two people I know. One is a hardline easy-knowledge skeptic: he takes the old gas-gauge case — driver reads the gauge all trip, then concludes from those readings that the gauge itself is reliable — as showing you can't keep both closure and justification that comes free from a source, something has to go. The other thinks that's overkill: bootstrapping can hand you real knowledge about the source, it's just worthless knowledge, knowledge you couldn't use to convince anyone or to license more trust than you started with.

They've been talking past each other for months, partly because they keep arguing about the toy case. Here's the real one I want the document built around: my team runs a model that flags possible sepsis cases in a hospital. There's no clean labeled dataset, so the reliability figure we publish is computed by comparing the model's outputs against chart reviews that clinicians only ever performed on patients the model had flagged. So the number that tells us the model is trustworthy is produced by a procedure that assumes it is. Both of them agree that loop exits. What they don't agree on is what it costs us.

Draft the joint statement the two of them could actually sign. I want: (1) a short numbered list of claims both sides accept, about the hospital case and about the underlying epistemology — they have to be substantive, not "both sides think evidence matters"; (2) a table of the disagreements that survive, each row worded so the side holding it would sign that wording, with a column saying whether the row is a genuine dispute or just the two of them using 'justified' differently; (3) three cruxes, sub-questions where an answer would actually move at least one of them, and for each one the argument or case both would accept as bearing on it plus each side's prediction written down in advance.

Also be explicit about which parts of this are settled and which are live disagreements among people who actually work on the problem. Don't slip one camp's view in as the consensus, and where you're making a judgment call rather than reporting a shared position, mark it as such.

Hard cap: 600 words including the table. If you run out of room, cut agreed claims, not predictions.
