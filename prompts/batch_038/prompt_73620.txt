I work on developer tooling, and there's a pattern I keep running into that I can't find a word for. Some properties of an engineer behave like ordinary attributes: you can store them on their record and they stay true when nobody is looking. Others don't. Whether someone is the person you go to about the payments service isn't a fact about them plus a fact about the codebase; it exists entirely in a web of standing dispositions among their colleagues to route problems to them, and it is graded rather than binary — there is no threshold at which they flip from not-it to it.

Two features are what make me suspect this isn't just a relational property in the trivial sense. If the routing stops — a long leave, the team turns over — the property decays out of existence. Nothing is revoked; it lapses. And the colleagues' dispositions don't merely track it, they partly produce it: someone who is uniformly ignored loses the thing itself, not just the accurate description of it. I suspect the same structure reaches well past job roles, down to whether someone counts as a full adult member of a community at all.

Is there an established name for a property of this kind? I'd like the nearest candidates set against it — ordinary extrinsic properties, properties that consist in how people respond to something, statuses that hold only because a group collectively treats them as holding, and views on which personhood itself is constituted by one's relationships — with an account of how mine differs from each, which fits best, and where the edges fall. Does a reputation qualify? A debt? Being loved? The label matters less to me than the carving, so if nothing fits, define a term and give its boundary cases.
