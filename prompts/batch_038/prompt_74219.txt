I've run a volunteer bike-repair co-op out of a rented garage in our town for six years. Fourteen of us, open Thursday evenings, free parts for kids' bikes, that kind of thing.

Last spring somebody filmed one of our sessions and the video got maybe two million views. Practically overnight we picked up around 900 people in our online discussion group who have never set foot in the garage. Two things happened after that and I can't get a handle on either.

One: we needed to start charging $5 for parts to cover rent, so we put it to a vote in the group. 4,100 people voted, 78% against. The eleven of us who actually work Thursdays were unanimously for it. Our treasurer announced afterwards that the community had decided. I have personally met maybe six of those 4,100. Some of them joined that morning, voted, and were gone by Friday.

Two: a journalist wrote us up around one line from the video, and now I get emails daily from people who are certain they know what we are. One flew in from three states away and was angry we wouldn't let him film inside. Another lives in the Netherlands, has never visited, and puts out a weekly digest of our threads that about 12,000 people read. And honestly alot of the writing about us uses the exact same handful of words now — community, grassroots, authentic — including from people who've never talked to anybody here.

My gut says something went wrong structurally, not just growing pains or "you need better moderation." But when I try to explain it I sound like a crank complaining about the internet. My co-founder thinks I'm being precious: those votes came from real people with real opinions, and if I only count the views of people who physically show up, we're just a clique protecting itself from being outvoted.

Is he right? Is there any principled difference between 4,100 people who vote and eleven who show up, or am I mostly bothered that I stopped being one of the eleven who matter? Not looking for governance tips, definately not another set of bylaws — I want to know whether the thing I'm sensing is actually there.
