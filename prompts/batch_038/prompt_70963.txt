Thirty-eight years running detectors, and I've only started reading philosophy properly since I retired, so forgive the amateur framing. Thing that still bugs me from the lab: we had a decay mode whose rate everybody quoted as roughly 3 in a thousand events. A postdoc told me that since I know that figure, my confidence for any single event should just be 0.003, and that my own logbook — six firings in the four hundred events I personally counted over the years — shouldnt budge it, on the grounds that it's like knowing a coin is fair and then making a fuss over a run of heads. Fine, except that figure was never derived from theory, it came from other people counting events exactly the way I was, so if my counts arent allowed to touch it, what ever gave the number its authority in the first place? Separately, I'd happily bet my pension on it firing next week from pure forty-year feel, and I know a gut isnt data. Are those two reasons for going against the number really the same kind of thing, and where's the principled line?
