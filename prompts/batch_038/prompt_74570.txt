Write a short story of no more than 1,000 words, set in the claims department of a property insurer, in which the operative question is whether a single 71-hour weather event — one that contained, somewhere near its middle, a lull of roughly six hours of calm, clearing skies, and rising barometric pressure — was one storm or two. Because the policy carries a separate deductible per storm, the answer decides whether the claimant is made whole or ruined; because the predicate storm has no boundary fixed in hours of calm, nobody in the office can say what that answer is.

Dramatize two opposed convictions, held by two named employees, without ever presenting them as theories or attaching labels to them. One holds that there is a definite hour — the thirty-eighth, the forty-fourth, it does not matter which — at which the first storm ended and the second began; that this is as much a fact as the date the claim was filed; and that no instrument, however fine, could ever locate it, since the ignorance would survive perfect data. The other holds that there is no such fact to find, that the file is settled only when someone with the authority to settle it draws the line, and that a good deal is nonetheless settled independently of any drawing: that there was at least one storm, that sixty hours of hurricane-force wind is not no storm at all, and that either it was one storm or it was not one storm stands firm whether or not anyone will ever be able to say which half holds.

Constraints: the words vague, vagueness, borderline, sorites, precisification, epistemic, and bivalence must not appear anywhere in the story. Someone who works on these questions should be able to recognize both positions from the events and dialogue alone, and should find each given in its strongest form rather than as a straw man; neither side may win, and the ending must not adjudicate between them. Include at least one piece of paperwork — an internal memo, a letter to the claimant, an underwriter's marginal note — whose exact wording turns on the dispute. You may echo the question in the adjuster's private life if it earns the space.

The final line must be a single sentence containing an either/or that the reader can accept as true while accepting that neither of its halves can ever be established. Reply with a title and the story only: no preface, no author's note, no explanation of which position was which.
