There's an early warning project run out of a Holocaust museum's genocide prevention center with a university partner — every year they publish a statistical risk assessment ranking 160-odd countries by their likelihood of seeing an *onset* of mass killing over the next two years. Three things about the design bug me.

One: the variable being predicted is "mass killing," not genocide. The word genocide does no work anywhere in the model. The event is defined by deliberate killings of noncombatant civilians crossing a threshold within an episode, with nothing in the definition about intent to destroy a group, or about which group, or about the group as such.

Two: but the predictors are saturated with group structure — whether the ruling elite is drawn from a distinct communal group, whether the state systematically discriminates against a group, regime type, armed conflict, prior episodes. So the inputs are all about groups and the output is a body count.

Three: because it predicts onset, a country where the killing is already happening drops out of the risk ranking and goes into a separate ongoing bucket.

So — what theory of genocide does this apparatus presuppose, and what theory of what's wrong with it? Does building it this way commit the institution to thinking the distinctive wrong just is very large scale civilian killing, so that Lemkin's picture — genocide as a coordinated plan to disintegrate a group, which is why it's its own crime rather than a big massacre — is a distinction with no predictive or moral cash value? Or could they believe all of Lemkin and still model it this way for purely measurement reasons?

I'd want the strict-vs-comfortable line drawn: what does the practice actually *require*, and what does it merely sit well with? Is there any moral commitment in here at all, or is it entirely epistemic — coding reliability, tractability, getting a target variable that survives peer review?

And then the consistency questions. Can you run group-based predictors against a group-free target without smuggling the group back in through the back door — and if the group keeps doing the explanatory work, what was gained by deleting it from the definiendum? Does publishing a ranked list of named countries while refusing to use the accusatory word presuppose that quantification can be done from outside the politics of naming, that a number isn't a charge? And what conception of "risk" lets the places where the harm is presently occurring score as not at risk — is that just an artifact of forecasting, or a view about what an early warning system owes?

Last bit: suppose you rebuilt the thing around the legal concept instead — intent to destroy a protected group in whole or in part. Tell me what would concretely have to change, and which of those changes is the one that breaks the project rather than merely inconveniencing it. My suprise-free guess is that it's not the data.
