I run exhibits at a small county museum and i'm stuck on a wall text about why our mill town went from 14,000 people in 1970 to under 6,000 today.

The old label, writen by the guy before me, is a story. It follows one family: he works the looms, she takes in sewing, the kids leave one at a time on the Greyhound, the plant shuts in '82, the house is a duplex now. Visitors read that one all the way through, and it's the thing they mention to me afterwards.

We hired a consultant and her report says it has to come down. She says the label describes one family's experience and never explains anything, and she wants a panel in its place listing the causes — automation, the interstate bypassing us, the trade deals of the '90s, the pension buyouts — with a number attached to each. My board likes hers because it sounds checkable.

Here's what I can't settle. She keeps saying the family story is an illustration, not an explanation. But when I read the story I feel like I get why the town emptied, more than I do from her bullet points. One of my volunteers offered a truce: the story shows what it was like, the list shows why. I'm not convinced those come apart here — the family in the story is the one where the plant shut, after all.

So: is she right that a list of causes is the only thing that counts as explaining this? And whichever way that goes, which panel actually belongs on the wall?
