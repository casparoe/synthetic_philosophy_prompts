I've just finished a chapter on other minds in a trade book on consciousness written by a cognitive scientist, and it summarizes Wittgenstein's private language argument as follows:

"Picture a man who keeps a diary, marking a sign S each time a certain sensation occurs, and who fixes the meaning of S by concentrating on the feeling so that he will recognize it again. The trouble is that he has no independent way of checking whether he is applying S to the same thing twice: memory is the only check on memory, so whatever seems right to him is right, and that means there is no fact of the matter about whether today's S-sensation is yesterday's. A language whose words stand for private inner objects is therefore impossible. The beetle in the box makes the same point from the other direction — nobody can look into anyone else's box, so the beetle drops out of consideration entirely and might as well be nothing at all. Meaning is use, and use is public; so there is no such thing as the private inner theatre that philosophers of mind have been worrying about. That is why the argument does not answer skepticism about other minds but dissolves it: there was never any question about other people's inner states, because there are no private inner states of the kind the skeptic posits."

My instinct is that the diary case is described fairly but that the last two sentences overreach, and I have also heard it said that Wittgenstein emphatically did not deny that we have pains. Please audit this paragraph sentence by sentence, separating what a Wittgenstein scholar would accept, what is a defensible simplification, and what misrepresents either the argument or its target. Address in particular whether the beetle-in-the-box remark licenses the eliminativist reading attached to it here, whether the argument's aim is really to establish that there is no fact of the matter about a speaker's own sensations, and whether the closing claim about dissolving rather than answering skepticism follows from whatever parts of the summary are accurate. Please write your answer in American English.
