Please answer as a philosopher who works at the intersection of aesthetics and epistemology, and think the matter through properly — including a short private brainstorm of candidate labels — before committing to one. I am not after a survey or a reading list; I want the carving.

I am a novelist, and across two decades of blurbs, advance copies and book-club correspondence I keep meeting a pattern I have no name for. Two instances. First: the same novel went out to two reading groups, one behind a cover line praising its cool restraint, the other behind a cover line praising its fevered urgency. Each group afterwards cited the same pages as evidence of the quality it had been promised, and did so in the register of perception — you can feel how withheld the sentences become here — rather than in the register of trust in the blurb's author. Second: after a rave review of a contemporary whose work had always bored me, I returned to a passage I had skimmed for years and was genuinely moved; I still cannot tell whether the verdict let me see something in the prose or installed something in me that I then reported as seeing.

So a verdict received in advance does not merely change what people say. It seems to change what they find themselves attending to, and to furnish them with particular, citable grounds they could not otherwise have produced. Is there an established concept for this? I suspect no exact one: it is not plain deference, since the subject claims and appears to have first-hand perception, and it is not obviously mere confabulation, since the enriched experience may be real. Tell me the nearest established notions and precisely how my description differs from each; where the edges lie (does the effect survive when the advance verdict concerns a non-evaluative feature, such as the presence of a twist, rather than a value?); and, if nothing fits, coin a term with a definition and boundary cases. Then say what turns on the naming — whether appreciation manufactured this way can count as knowing the work, and whether the distinction matters for how much weight criticism deserves. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely disputed among people who work on this, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
