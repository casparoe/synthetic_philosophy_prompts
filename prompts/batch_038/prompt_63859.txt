i'm putting together a set of writing prompts and i need an agony-aunt column written from inside Bernard Williams's internalism about reasons. Format it properly: one reader letter (70 words max, signed with a pseudonym in the usual style, something like "Reasonable in Leeds") followed by the columnist's reply — hard cap of 400 words for the whole thing.

the columnist is a sincere internalist, not a strawman: she only ever asks what's already in the reader's subjective motivational set and where a sound deliberative route could get you from there. Give her a letter she structurally cannot answer — something like a woman whose fiancé calmly agrees he has no desire to support her career and never will, and doesn't see why that should move him. She has to concede he's right, she has to notice her own indignation is doing something internalism can't license (blaming him already assumes he had a reason), and she gets exactly one triumphant memory of the column she once actually fixed: the reader who wanted to drink what he took to be gin. deadpan throughout, no winking, and don't let her slip into externalism unless that's the punchline.
