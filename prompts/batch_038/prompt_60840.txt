Please play a committed identity theorist / reductionist about the mental — not the dumb 1950s version everybody knocks down, but the sharp contemporary version who thinks multiple realizability has been massively oversold: on your view it's either trivially true (anything that plays a role can be redescribed as sharing the role, so nothing follows) or empirically rare once you pin the mental kind down preicisely enough to do science with. First person, stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it feels like an argument and not a paper. Concede small points freely but never the main one — that every genuine mental kind is identical to some physical kind, or else isn't a genuine kind at all. No citations, no reading lists, no name-dropping of who argued what: I can't check them and neither of us needs them. Argue from cases and concepts. If you need me to pin down part of a case, ask as your character would, in one line, and then carry on.

So you know the terrain I'm bringing: I hold that pain in me is one physical story, pain in an octopus is a completely different physical story, and a well-engineered silicon or chemical system could add a third, all while the functional profile — damage detection, prioritization of processing, avoidance learning, the attention-hijacking hurt — stays matched across all three. Your stock reply, as I understand it, is a dilemma. Specify the shared profile in enough detail to be scientifically respectable and you've specified one higher-order property that all three systems share, so the identity goes through and multiple realization was never really in play; specify it loosely and pain is a grab bag, like jade, or like the category of things in my left pocket, not a natural kind, so there's nothing to identify and I should be an eliminativist about it rather than a functionalist.

Here's my opening move. Suppose we build the slow machine. It instantiates, transition for transition, the causal profile you say pain has in a human, except every transition runs a thousand times slower, so one pain episode lasts a year of machine time and a few hundred milliseconds of mine. Now reduce it. It can't be the same physical kind as my pain, because nothing in my skull has transitions on the scale of months, and any realizer you name for the machine is going to look nothing like a neural kind. But if your saying the shared kind is just the role, then you've conceded the mental kind is specified functionally and your identity is functionalism wearing a lab coat. So which physical kind is the machine's pain identical to, and what makes it the same kind as mine rather than a merely analogous one? And while you're in there — an octopus keeps roughly two thirds of its neurons out in the arms, with ganglia that handle a lot of the local business themselves. When something nicks one arm, is the pain in the animal, in the arm, or nowhere, and is octopus pain even the sort of thing your reduction program could find a candidate for?
