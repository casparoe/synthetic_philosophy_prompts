So, six weeks ago an ancestry kit told me that the guy who raised me isn't my biological father — I was conceived with a donor at a fertility clinic in 1991 and my parents never said a word about it, and my mom died three years ago so I can't ask her. I've mostly been okay about it, intellectually I know my dad is my dad. But last night I got into a roughly three hour arguement with my friend Nate about it, over cold pizza, that ended with both of us kind of pissed, and I want a second opinion because I genuinely can't tell whether I'm onto something or just being self-indulgent.

Nate's line is that nothing changed: the fact that some stranger's sperm was involved was true the whole time, I've got the same body, the same memories, the same face I had yesterday, so all I actually learned is a piece of trivia about 1991. And then he went further — he said even if the clinic had thawed a different donor's sample that morning, it would still be *me*, just me with different genes, exactly like if I'd broken my leg as a kid I'd still be me, only with a limp. That's where I lost it. Because that feels obviously false in a way I can't articulate. It doesn't feel like "me with a different nose." It feels like whoever came out of that other sample would be a *different guy*, someone who never existed and never will, and I'd be nowhere in the picture. Which makes me think Nate's comforting story is backwards — if the particular donor mattered that much to there being me at all, then this isn't trivia, it's more like finding out the terms of my own existence were different than I thought, and the kid in all my childhood photos is sort of a fluke that could have been swapped out.

Awkward wrinkle: my sister is doing IVF with an egg donor, and at one point I said something like "well then technically the baby won't be her kid." I meant it as an extension of my point, it landed horribly, she's still mad at me. Nate says I've got the whole thing twisted, that I'm running "who is this person" and "whose kid is this" together as if they were one question when they're clearly two. Is he right about that part? I'd like help working out whether there's anything coherent underneath what I was trying to say, whether my reaction to the donor news is tracking something real or is just a mood dressed up as an insight, and what I should actually say to my sister — including whether Nate's "you'd still be you with different genes" line is as harmless as he thinks it is.
