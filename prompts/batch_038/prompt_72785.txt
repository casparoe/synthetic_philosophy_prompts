ok so here's a thing I keep chewing on and I want a real answer, not the obvious one.

Orwell wrote that 1946 essay about English prose — the one where he sorts bad writing into dying metaphors, operators or verbal false limbs, pretentious diction, meaningless words, and argues that political language is mostly euphemism because politics is mostly the defence of the indefensible. His causal story is a loop: language gets ugly and inaccurate because thought is foolish, and then the resulting slovenliness makes it easier to think foolishly. And his cure is individual — the sincere writer who refuses the pre-assembled phrase, because insincerity is what wrecks clarity.

Now suppose you could hand him a machine in 2026 that turns out fluent, varied, grammatically spotless prose on demand, prose nobody intended. Work out what he'd actually conclude. Some specific places I want you to push:

- every diagnostic category in that essay is keyed to prose that *sounds* bad. smooth, well-turned, hollow sentences don't fit the scale at all. would he think his categories need extending, or does this kind of text just fall outside what the essay was about? and what happens to the insincerity claim when there's no insincere speaker behind the sentence to begin with — is that worse for him or simply not a case he was considering?
- he thought the loop was self-reinforcing but escapable by effort. what does he make of ready-made phrases being generated fresh each time, so that the cliché is never detectably a cliché? does the loop still close the same way, or is it now closed with the human slack taken out of it?
- everyone assumes the AI stuff vindicates Newspeak, but Newspeak worked by deleting vocabulary and narrowing the range of thought. make the case both ways and tell me which one he'd actually buy, and whether he'd have any patience for the idea of building plain-speech norms into the tools themselves.

I want you to say what he'd concede, what he'd reject as people misreading him, and — most importantly — the one or two spots where his framework genuinely has no slot for the new thing and he'd have to invent a concept rather than extend an old one. Don't just say he'd be appalled, assume he'd be appalled and then get to work.
