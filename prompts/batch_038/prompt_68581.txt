I'm putting together a session for my metaphysics reading group on de re modality and I want a case that runs the opposite way to the standard line. The usual story is that counterpart theory makes de re possibility cheap — there's always some barely-similar counterpart out there, so nearly anything is possible for an object — while transworld identity saddled with essences makes it restrictive. I want a thought experiment where that ordering flips: the transworld identity theorist has to affirm a de re possibility that the counterpart theorist, working with the counterpart relation that's actually salient in the context, has to deny.

Constraints: the object should be an ordinary middle-sized thing — an artifact is fine, no persons, no fission or fusion, no ships or statues. The transworld identity theorist in the case has to be someone who takes cross-world identity as primitive rather than grounded in qualitative similarity, and so no essentialist about material or origin; but the possibility they affirm must be a qualitative one, not a bare swapping of roles between two qualitatively identical worlds, or I've just handed them the case by stipulation. The salient counterpart relation should come out of the speakers' ordinary practical interests, not something reverse-engineered to get the verdict. Scenario one paragraph max, realistic setting.

Then do the work: state exactly what each theory says about the de re claim and why, name which feature of the case does the work, tell me what happens to the case if you pull that feature out, and give me the best objection from a counterpart theorist who thinks I've cheated — that I've gerrymandered the counterpart relation or stacked the context — plus whether there's any principled reply.

The point of the exercise is to show my group that the real disagreement isn't about how permissive de re modality is but about whether it's grounded in qualitative character at all. So a case that just announces the verdicts instead of generating them from the two frameworks isn't any use to me.
