I run a little reading group for engineers at my company — we do mostly AI-safety-ish stuff, nobody has a philosophy degree — and I got volunteered to make a one-page decision tree on van Fraassen's Reflection principle. You know the idea: if you're certain your future self will put credence x on some claim, you should put x on it now too. Half the room thinks you should basically always trust your future self and half thinks that's insane, and im pretty sure they aren't disagreeing about the same thing.

So build me the tree, 6 to 8 branch questions max so it fits on a page. Every node has to be a concrete situation someone could answer without knowing which view it leads to — for example: your future self will be more confident and you have no idea why; the belief gets formed at 3am at the end of a bad on-call week; they'll have seen evidence they can't remember the details of by then; the change comes from something you can't imagine from where you're standing (a kid, a diagnosis, whatever); they'll be just as informed and just as careful as you and still land somewhere else. Leaves get the name of the position if one exists, or "nobody holds this" if the cell is empty, plus one line on what that view costs you. Where you mark a cell empty, say whether it's empty because the view is incoherent, just unappealing, or genuinely unexplored.

Then flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate, and define any term you use that a coder wouldn't already know.
