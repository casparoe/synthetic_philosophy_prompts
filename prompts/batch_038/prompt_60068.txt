I am a retired cell biologist. For thirty-five years I worked on receptor signaling: ligand binding, affinity and specificity, coupling to downstream effectors, knockouts and rescues, and the routine observation that a receptor's significance changes when it is expressed in a different cell type. Only lately have I turned to philosophy, where I have run into the quarrel over whether intentionality is ever original or must always be derived — Searle's room against Dennett's wandering coin detector, whose internal state means "quarter" in one country and "quarter balboa" in another with nothing in the device itself altered.

Restate that quarrel in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, then give me a residue report, claim by claim: what translates cleanly into binding, specificity, coupling, and cellular context; what survives only with distortion; and what has no expression at all.

Two things I want settled explicitly. First, my working hypothesis is that Searle's original intentionality has no counterpart in signaling whatsoever: the nearest candidate is binding specificity, and specificity is a physical relation with a measurable affinity constant, not a meaning. Confirm or correct that hypothesis, and tell me whether correcting it would cost me anything I care about as a biologist. Second, suppose I grant the teleosemanticist's move that content is fixed by what a trait was selected to detect. In my field that is very nearly a truism. Does adopting it collapse the original/derived distinction, on the grounds that a selected function is no more intrinsic to the receptor than an engineered one — or does it quietly smuggle back the very thing Dennett denies?

Do not import my framework's presuppositions and label them translation; wherever the two come apart, say so and say which side is giving way. Open with your verdict on the two questions in a single paragraph, then the translation and residue report. No more than 800 words.
