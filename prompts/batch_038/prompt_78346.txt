Suppose a physicist hands you an inflationary model that passes every ordinary observational test, but once you fix a measure over spacetime regions it predicts roughly 10^40 fluctuated observers with exactly our kind of orderly, law-abiding memories for every ordinary observer. I want a number on this: is the model thereby emprically ruled out, or is the Boltzmann brain worry mostly a misapplied typicality assumption we should shrug off? Give your credence in ruled out, then decompose it — how much hangs on typicality reasoning being legitimate in cosmology at all, how much on there being a unique right way to count observers, how much on the extra wrinkle that a Boltzmann brain's own reasoning would be self-undermining so it couldn't trust the argument that produced the model, and whether stipulating that the freak observers have our exact memories actually neutralizes the usual reply that they'd have chaotic experience. Rough probability for each term, how you combine them, and which one you're least confident in — plus what would move it most.
