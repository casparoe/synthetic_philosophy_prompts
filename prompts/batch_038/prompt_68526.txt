For thirty-seven years I ran mesocosm studies: columns of lake water walled off from the lake, stocked, manipulated, sampled. Everyone in my discipline knew that such an enclosure can be impeccably controlled and still tell you nothing about the lake, because the wall alters what it encloses — it excludes the top of the food web, it permits a biofilm that never forms in open water, it fixes a founding population that no natural population resembles. Since retiring I have taken up philosophy, and it has struck me that thought experiments are mesocosms. The verdict an intuition pump elicits is a reading taken inside an enclosure, and the substantive questions are which enclosures are honest and which merely manufacture artifacts of the wall.

I have been reading around the controversy over whether thought experiments carry evidential weight, and I cannot reliably tell which disagreements in it are real and which are people using the word intuition for different things. I run a monthly reading group of retired colleagues — all scientists, none of us trained in philosophy — and I would like to bring them a decision tree that sorts us. Please write one, walkable in about twenty minutes.

Requirements:

1. Eight or nine branching questions. Each must be either a short concrete case with two or three possible responses, or a claim the reader can accept, reject, or refuse to judge. No question may contain the name of a position, a philosopher, or any term ending in -ism. I want the tree to sort us by what we say about cases, not by what we call ourselves.

2. Draw your cases from the familiar stock — division of a person into two, wholesale physical duplication, someone given every physical fact about a phenomenon without ever having encountered it, a word translated between two linguistic communities, trade-offs of the trolley kind — but feel free to invent your own where the stock ones are now too celebrated to elicit a naive response. That contamination is itself something I want the tree to handle.

3. The following joints must appear somewhere in the tree, though you may reorganize them. Whether a pump fails in its construction or in its reader: I want the case that never gets the possibility in front of you distinguished from the case that gets it in front of you and is then misjudged. Whether verdicts survive the adjustment of incidental knobs — order of presentation, size of the numbers, affective colouring, whose point of view you are given — and whether instability is disqualifying or merely informative. Whether a case too familiar to yield a clean reading should be redesigned or its readers' verdicts discounted. What the verdict is a reading of at all: a possibility, a concept, the extension of a word, a principle, or nothing beyond a disposition to say something. Whether a pump can be reconstructed as an explicit argument, and what remains over if it can. Whether some readers are simply better instruments than others, and whether that can be established without appeal to the conclusions those readers reach.

4. At each leaf, give the position a short descriptive tag of your own devising; do not import the labels from the literature, and do not cite anyone. Among the leaves I want at least one on which pumps are evidentially respectable and the bad ones are bad because of how they were built rather than who read them, and one on which the whole practice yields no evidence about anything outside our dispositions to speak. Then mark every cell in the space your questions open up that no position occupies, and say for each whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or merely because nobody has got round to it.

5. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate: readers who could answer identically all the way down and yet belong in different places. Do not paper over these.

6. For each leaf, one sentence on the cost of living there — the consequence its inhabitants would least like to own.

Be decisive about where the joints are; I am not interested in hedged cartography. If one of my six branches turns out not to be a genuine joint — a distinction without a difference, or two distinctions wearing a single coat — cut it, and say what you have put in its place and why. And if you think the mesocosm framing is a poor one, that thought experiments fail in ways with no counterpart in an enclosure experiment, or that importing my discipline's standards of external validity already settles the very question the tree is meant to sort, tell me that plainly at the top before doing anything else. I would much rather have the objection than a courteous compliance.
