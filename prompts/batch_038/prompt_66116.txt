ok so I run my school's environmental club and next month we're launching a campaign to get people to actually sort their lunch trash right (compost / recycling / landfill), plus we have to post about it on the club instagram for like six weeks. After some drama last year with another club's signup drive, student council made every club hand in a short writeup on how we plan to talk to people, so I wrote down the rules I actually believe about where persuasion stops and manipulation starts. My co-president read it and said at least two of them can't both be true, but she wouldn't tell me which, and now I don't trust my own list. Can you do the check for me?

Here's what I wrote:

1. The line is about mechanism. If you hand someone reasons and let them weigh them, that's persuasion. If your method works by getting around the weighing part, it's manipulation. Fancy packaging or a nice tone doesn't change which one it is.
2. I use a test I made up: could I tell the person exactly what I'm doing and why, ahead of time, and have it still work? If it only works because they dont know the plan, it's manipulation.
3. Making people feel something is fine. A true story about a real consequence, or genuinely scaring someone about a real risk — legitimate, as long as what I say is true and actually relevant to the decision.
4. Rearranging the situation so the good option is the easy one is fine too, as long as it's out in the open and someone can opt out in about ten seconds. Defaults, signs, putting the compost bin first in the line.
5. It's also fine — arguably good — to exploit known biases when what I'm pushing is the thing the person already says they want. Half my school tells me they care about climate and then forgets every single day. Posting at the hour I know their willpower is bottomed out, or framing a missed signup as losing something, isn't manipulating them, it's lending them willpower they already asked for. I call it borrowing someone's better self.
6. Manipulation can't plant a motive out of nowhere. It only steers desires that are already there, so the person is still the one acting — I'm never the author of what they do.
7. Being on the receiving end still feels like a real wrong though. If I found out a company had done number 5 to me, I'd be angry even if I ended up doing the exact thing I would've done anyway.

Tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and rank them by how bad they are instead of just listing them all as equally broken. Also tell me which ones only look like they conflict but dont, because I suspect I've been worrying about the wrong pair. For each real tension, what's the cheapest edit — and what does that edit cost me specifically for the trash-sorting campaign, like which tactic I'd have to drop? If one of the seven has to go entirely, say which, but don't assume I already know which one it is. I don't need sources or a reading list, just the audit.
