my brother does growth at a short-video company and has latched onto Tolstoy's What Is Art? as a defense of his job. his pitch: Tolstoy says art is infection — a feeling passes from maker to receiver, the strength of the infection is the only measure of how good a work is apart from its subject matter, and the artist's sincerity is what drives it most. so completion rate and shares are just instruments measuring what Tolstoy could only introspect: a clip that gets watched to the end and forwarded is more infectious and therefore better art, and a cynically optimized clip should betray its insincerity in the drop-off curve. he thinks that makes engagement analytics a branch of aesethetics.

audit this. what maps onto what in the disease model — pathogen, host, route of transmission, virulence, dose, R0 — where does the mapping break, and which features of infection-as-epidemiology is he importing that Tolstoy's account doesn't actually have? i want the disanalogies that genuinely threaten his conclusion kept separate from the decorative ones, a verdict on whether the conclusion survives, and what analogy would do his work better if there is one. then tell me what would change your verdict: what would he have to show for the metrics reading to carry real Tolstoyan weight, and where is your own reasoning here most likely to be wrong?
