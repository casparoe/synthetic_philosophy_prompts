Been reading the persistence debate on and off for about a year now (amateur, day job is nowhere near philosophy) and there's a structural feature of it I can't find a name for. I want to know whether I've just missed the term or whether the term doesn't exist.

Here's what I keep seeing. Somebody offers a case that's supposed to be decisive — the road that's straight in one stretch and curved in the next, the person who had straight hair as a kid and curly hair now, the lump of clay that survives being squashed into a ball when the statue doesn't. One side says: easy, the object has different properties at different times because it has different temporal stretches, and the statue/clay business is two worms sharing stretches. The other side says: no, the object is entirely there at each moment and has the property relative to that moment, and the statue/clay business is two things in one place. Then — and this is the part — each side says of the other's story that it's just their own story in different words. The endurantist says relativizing to times is exactly what you'd get if you took temporal stretches seriously but wouldn't say the word. The four-dimensionalist says "wholly present at t" is just "the temporal part at t" with a hat on it. And as far as I can tell they're both right about the translatability, atleast for every case that's actually been proposed. Nobody ever produces an example where the other side's vocabulary genuinely runs out.

So there's a permanent tie at the level of cases. But the striking thing is that neither side concludes the dispute is empty. They both grant that anything one can say the other can translate, and they both still think there's a hard fact about which way of describing the world is better — more natural, carves at the joints, whatever — and that *that's* where the disagreement really lives. The disagreement has migrated from the cases up into the choice of description language, and it's still supposed to be a real disagreement about the world rather than about words.

Does that configuration have a name? I can see several near-misses and I want to know which, if any, is the right one, and where exactly the thing I described sits relative to each:

- a merely verbal dispute, where nothing is at stake — except here both sides insist something is at stake and they seem to have a candidate for what it is;
- underdetermination of theory by evidence — except there's no shortage of evidence, the two accounts are equivalent on all the evidence in principle, not just on the data we happen to have;
- "notational variant" in the old sense — which is what each side calls the other, but it's being used as a jab inside a live dispute rather than as a verdict that the dispute is finished;
- something like a disagreement about which concept to adopt, where the interesting wrinkle is that both sides deny that's what they're doing.

Please don't just hand me a label, that's the easy bit. What I want is the carving: what exactly is this phenomenon (a dispute that's intertranslatable at the level of every possible case but claimed to be substantive at the level of which description is fundamental), what is it *not*, and where are the edges — at what point does it stop being this and start being a plain verbal dispute, and at what point does it turn into ordinary theory choice between empirically equivalent rivals? If there's no established name, coin one, define it, and give me a nearby case that falls outside it so I can see the boundary. And tell me whether there's an exact parallel somewhere else — I have a vague suspicion the philosophy-of-science literature has the same shape of argument under a different label, but I don't know it.

No bibliography needed and don't worry about references, I'm not chasing down papers. I just want to know whether I've rediscovered something or invented something.
