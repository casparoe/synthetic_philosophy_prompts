I TA a distributive-justice seminar whose convenor has never said which metric of equality she holds; she only rules on cases, and I'm trying to reverse-engineer her view for my prospectus. Her verdicts so far: a woman who cannot walk is owed more than an equal per-capita share, because the standard bundle buys her far less mobility than it buys her neighbours; a man who spent thirty years cultivating a taste for rare wines, needs five times his neighbours' income to be equally contented, and would not give the taste up if you paid him, is owed nothing extra; a woman passed over for the training her brothers received, who now sincerely says she wants nothing she lacks, is owed something notwithstanding her contentment; and a man who joined an ascetic community in middle age and flourishes on very little does not thereby forfeit any part of his share.

Next week's case is this: a woman who, since a childhood illness, takes markedly less pleasure out of everything than her brother takes out of an identical bundle. She would not have chosen the temperament, but she has stopped minding it, reports no dissatisfaction, and asks for nothing.

Predict her verdict. I have tried fitting the first four cases to Dworkinian resourcism and to the capability approach, and both seem to swallow all four whole, so tell me what each actually predicts here and whether some third candidate fits the record better than either. Then say which of the surviving rules you would bet on and why, and give me one further case that would separate them, together with what she would have to say about it on each.
