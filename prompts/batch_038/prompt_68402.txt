I am a novelist working on a book set in a northern English county borough in 1974, and I need a piece of fiction written from inside my material rather than about it. The borough council has installed a mainframe in the basement of the municipal buildings — the staff call it MORTMAIN — and given it a single, apparently modest remit: to maintain the definitive map of public rights of way. It decides which footpaths, bridleways and green lanes are to be recorded as public, which are to be extinguished for disuse, and which diversions are to be approved when a landowner asks to move a path out of a field being developed. A rights-of-way officer, a woman in her fifties who has walked every one of those paths since she was a child, must countersign each recommendation before it takes legal effect.

The difficulty I want the machine to suffer is the frame problem, and I want it dramatised, never diagnosed. Please write a short story of roughly 1,600 to 2,000 words that satisfies the following, and then append a note of no more than 300 words.

1. The story must open on the technical difficulty — the sheer impossibility of writing down everything that remains the same when one thing changes. Some clerk must be obliged, in the ordinary course of a working day, to type in a clause to the effect that nothing else has altered, and the accumulation of those clauses should become physically present in the room.

2. It must then slide, without any character remarking on the slide, into the philosophical difficulty: the machine's inability to know which of the many true things about a footpath matter. The two should feel continuous in the reading, and I want to be told afterwards whether they genuinely are.

3. Two further scenes should stage the qualification problem and the ramification problem respectively — the one where the council cannot finish listing what might prevent an action from working, and the one where a single approved diversion keeps generating consequences it was never asked about. A proposed diversion through a field, with a housing estate on one side and a parish boundary, a school walk and a drainage ditch entangled in it, would serve. Do not name either problem in the fiction.

4. Formal constraints. Third person, close on the rights-of-way officer; the machine speaks only through documents, punched-card listings and printouts rendered typographically within the story. Each time MORTMAIN is asked a question, its reasoning should appear as a nested parenthetical aside, and these asides should lengthen through the story until the prose itself begins to look like the affliction, after which the final movement should be written in short, unadorned sentences. Do not use the words frame problem, relevance, commonsense or artificial intelligence anywhere in the fiction.

5. Avoid the stock furniture of this subject entirely. No ticking explosive, no child wandering into a road, no machine standing motionless in a burning room while it enumerates the consequences of consequences, no scientist shouting at a terminal, no closing image of the machine being switched off. If it helps, treat the whole thing as an administrative tragedy in the manner of a provincial council minutes book.

6. The officer's competence should be the story's silent counter-argument: she knows which of the true things about a path matter, and she does not appear to have arrived at that knowledge by any procedure the machine could be given.

In the appended note, please write in British English and tell me three things: which scene carries which of the three problems; precisely where in the story the technical version hands over to the philosophical version, and whether that handover is a real continuity or a pun I have exploited for effect; and whether the officer's fluency should be read as a solution to MORTMAIN's predicament or as an entirely different kind of thing that no amount of further clauses could ever reproduce. If you think my premise cannot bear the weight I am putting on it, say so in the note rather than quietly softening the story.
