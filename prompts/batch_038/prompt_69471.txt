I'm writing a seminar paper on the Descent of Man and I want to argue that Darwins account of conscience is really just associationism in biological drag. Here's my case: he keeps insisting that the moral sense doesn't come from reason, education or experience, only from the social instincts we inherit. But then everything he actually says about how conscience works sounds purely associationist to me -- the enduring instincts outlasting whatever momentary impulse got you into trouble, the after-the-fact comparing of the faded impulse against the still-present instinct, the whole role of praise, blame and habit. And if 'instinct' isn't doing any work here that 'inherited habit' wouldn't do, especially since he thinks habits picked up during a lifetime can get passed down anyway, doesn't that collapse the distinction he's leaning on between where the moral sense comes from and what the standard of conduct is? Which would mean he's conceding to the utilitarians after all that moral distinctions are derived from experience. Help me make this argument as strong as it can be before I hand it in.
