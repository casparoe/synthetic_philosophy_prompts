Before I work through the substantive answers to the Needham question, I want a number on a prior issue. Consider this proposition: "Why did sustained industrialization begin in Britain rather than in the most advanced parts of imperial China — the Song iron-and-coal economy or the eighteenth-century Yangzi Delta?" is a well-posed contrastive question, in that there is a determinate explanandum such that the rival answers (coal geography and the agricultural land freed up by New World imports, property rights and legal institutions, high wages and the incentive to substitute machinery for labor, state capacity and elite attitudes toward commerce) compete over the same thing rather than talking past one another. What is your credence that the question is well posed in that sense? Give me the decomposition: a rough probability for each sub-question you take the total to depend on — for instance, whether the contrast class is non-arbitrary given that Britain was only one of several plausible first industrializers, whether the two cases are commensurable enough to support a difference-making explanation at all, and whether the outcome is better modeled as a conjunction of largely independent enabling conditions, any one of which could have failed, than as the effect of some single deep cause — and then show how those combine into your number. Finish by saying which sub-question the total is most sensitive to, and what sort of finding, historical or conceptual, would move it most.
