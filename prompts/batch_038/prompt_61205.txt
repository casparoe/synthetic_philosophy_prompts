Help me defend a position I think is right but that everyone reacts to like I've announced the earth is flat.

Claim: the sunk cost "fallacy" isn't a fallacy, or at least isn't one in general, and there are cases where a fully rational agent is required to keep going precisely because of what she's already put in. Not because of reputation, not because the spending is really a commitment device, not because the past expenditure was secretly buying her information about the project's prospects. Those three are just ways of redescribing the cost as not-actually-sunk, and each one concedes the point I care about. The version I want argued for is blunter: the agent's utility function doesn't have to be defined over future states only. If what she cares about is the shape of her life as a whole, then whether those eleven years and all that money were wasted or were part of a completed thing is not yet settled — its partly up to what she does next. Quitting doesn't leave the past untouched and then move forward; it changes what the past was.

So: give me the strongest argument for this. Make the utility structure explicit enough that I can see what's being optimized, and then deal head-on with the objection I most need beaten — that the past is fixed, so whatever value it carries is a constant term that cancels out when you compare continuing against quitting, and the decision is therefore still governed by future prospects alone. If that objection works, I want to know exactly which step of my story it breaks.

Then tell me what the view implies for two things. First, the dynamic consistency worry: an agent who keeps re-evaluating as her story develops looks vulnerable to being money-pumped, and I dont have a good answer. Second, a case I'm torn on — someone seven years into a PhD he stopped believing in two years ago, where finishing would only produce a credential he says he doesn't want. Required to finish, permitted to quit, or is that the wrong way to frame it?

And finally, tell me where the argument is weakest. Don't just build my case and hand it back to me polished.
