**The setup I have in mind.** Let *Kp* abbreviate the claim that p is known, and let ◇ be read as unrestricted metaphysical possibility. The derivation in question runs from:

- A1 (factivity): Kp → p
- A2 (conjunctivity): K(p ∧ q) → Kp ∧ Kq
- A3 (knowability): ∀p(p → ◇Kp)
- A4 (a normality assumption about ◇, operative in the step from ¬K(p ∧ ¬Kp) to ¬◇K(p ∧ ¬Kp))
- A5 (classical logic, including the final step from the negation of the existential to the corresponding universal, plus the assumption that the sentence *p ∧ ¬Kp* denotes a single proposition falling within the range of the quantifier in A3)

to the conclusion that every truth is known, via the instance of A3 applied to the conjunctive sentence, A2, A1, and reductio.

**The position I hold.** I am inclined to accept the strong reading of this result: that it is a sound derivation from premises the verificationist cannot plausibly reject, and hence a genuine refutation of the thesis that all truths are knowable — not a technical curiosity about the interaction of a quantifier with a modal operator, and not a reductio of some optional extra assumption smuggled in by a hostile formalization. On my view, the standard escape routes (restricting the range of propositions quantified over, temporalizing or indexicalizing the possibility operator, treating the knowability operator as hyperintensional so that substitution of logical equivalents fails, retreating to intuitionistic logic, or denying that verificationists were ever committed to A3) either do not work at all or work only by surrendering something the verificationist needed A3 for. In short: there is no stable middle position between realism about truth and the claim that all truths are known.
