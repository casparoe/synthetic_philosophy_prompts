I am preparing a paper for a centenary panel on Michael Dummett whose theme is his political rather than his technical work. Dummett died in December 2011, which means that every development now most likely to test his position on immigration postdates him by a decade or more. I need a reconstruction, not a eulogy.

Here is my own précis of the position, which you should treat as fallible and correct wherever I have got it wrong. In his 2001 book On Immigration and Refugees, and in earlier writings on race in Britain, Dummett treats the state's claim to exclude as a standing presumption against which any particular restriction must argue, rather than as a default prerogative. He regards the distinction between the economic migrant and the refugee as morally consequential, but not as licensing the treatment of the latter as someone who has jumped a queue that no one offered him a place in. He holds that resistance to immigration in Britain has been substantially an expression of racism, and that the churches, including his own, were complicit largely through silence. And he gives weight to a special obligation on the part of former imperial powers toward the peoples of the territories they ruled. He argues throughout as a Catholic rather than as a libertarian: his conclusions concern duties owed, not liberties enjoyed.

Against that background, tell me what Dummett would have made of each of the following, taking them in turn.

1. The Windrush scandal as it emerged in 2018, together with the hostile environment policy announced in 2012 and given statutory form thereafter, under which long-resident Commonwealth subjects who could not produce documentary proof of a status nobody had ever asked them to prove lost employment, housing and medical treatment.
2. The United Kingdom's Rwanda partnership: announced in 2022, held unlawful by the Supreme Court in 2023 on the ground that the receiving state could not be treated as safe, and then rendered lawful by an Act of Parliament declaring that it was safe.
3. The two-tier statutory distinction introduced in 2022 between asylum seekers who arrive by sanctioned routes and those who arrive irregularly, along with the accompanying official vocabulary of legal and illegal arrival.
4. Brexit, in the specific respect that free movement was terminated by a bare-majority referendum in which most resident citizens of other member states had no vote, and that net migration subsequently reached record levels through non-European routes.

For each case: state the verdict you believe he would have reached; identify which of his premises is doing the work, so that I can see whether the conclusion is load-bearing or merely decorative; and classify the inference as following from something he actually wrote, as an extrapolation from his principles to a case he never considered, or as genuinely undetermined by anything in the record. Where the record runs out, say so plainly instead of filling the gap with what a theologically literate British philosopher of his generation would plausibly have said.

Two harder requests follow. First, name at least one position on these questions where his principles would have put him out of step with the people who now invoke his name: somewhere he would have been colder, more demanding, or more willing to concede something to the state than contemporary advocates of open borders are prepared to be. I have no use for a reconstruction in which he turns out to agree with me about everything. Second, tell me whether his technical philosophy buys anything at all in this domain. Two candidates present themselves. One is his verificationism, the requirement that the content of a claim be tied to conditions under which we could recognize it as established, which may or may not have something to say about a legal status constituted in practice by paperwork rather than by facts. The other is his work on voting procedures, which may or may not bear on the legitimacy of settling such questions by simple majority. Assess both honestly; if either is an opportunistic appropriation of a logician's reputation rather than a genuine application, say that it is, and say why.

Please do not supply a bibliography, a reading list, or citations, and do not put words in his mouth in quotation marks. I want his position paraphrased and argued for.
