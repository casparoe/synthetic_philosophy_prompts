I'm chairing the Q&A at a departmental workshop and need help with questions. The guest defends what I'll call the stance-first account of Strawson's reactive attitudes — she's real enough, but let's not put a name into something that'll get forwarded around, so just work with the position as I've sketched it here.

Roughly, she thinks Strawson has the order of explanation backwards. On her view the reactive attitudes aren't feelings caused by beliefs about somebody's quality of will; they're the *form* that second-personal engagement takes. To resent someone is to address a demand to them, not to register a fact about them. Two further commitments you'll need in order to write good questions: (i) she holds that the objective stance can only be taken wholesale, never piecemeal — dipping into it in the middle of a relationship isn't a cool-headed therapeutic option, it's either self-deception or a change of subject; and (ii) she holds that guilt isn't the mirror image of resentment, because you can't address a demand to yourself in the relevant sense. She's also a very smooth talker, and her stock escape is to say that whatever objection you've just raised is only a complaint about one particular attitude, not a problem for the account of what the attitudes are.

So: give me eight to ten questions for the Q&A, each with a short follow-up underneath. For the follow-up, first spell out in a line or two the reply you think she'll actually give, then write the question that bites on that reply. Constraints:

- no warm-ups, no career stuff, nothing she can answer by summarising her own view for five minutes;
- at least one question on how a demand-first account explains fittingness at all — why the demand is apt rather then merely expressive — since that's the place the standard Strawsonian story (resentment answers to ill will, or to a lack of due regard) does work her view seems to have no room for;
- one on blame directed at people you could never have addressed anything to: a Roman emperor, a long-dead benefactor, a dissolved corporation. What is the demand being made, and to whom;
- one on the piecemeal-objectivity ban, run through a concrete case: a hospice nurse who deliberately adopts a clinical, managerial stance toward an abusive patient so she can keep doing her job well. She has to pick — the nurse is being corrupted, is deceiving herself, or is simply doing something other than blaming, and none of those sound good;
- one on the mute resenter: someone with all the reactive dispositions who never voices, signals or acts on any of it. If resentment is address, has she blamed anyone? Push her to say whether unvoiced resentment is silent address or no address at all;
- one on guilt and self-blame, and one on the psychopath / severely impaired case — where she has to say whether her view makes responsibility a matter of who we *choose* to treat as capable of recipricating, and if so why that isn't just arbitrariness dressed up as an account;
- about half the room hasn't read Strawson, so every question needs to be followable without jargon, while the content still being hard. Plain phrasing, sharp target.
- end with a single question asking what would have to turn out to be true for her to abandon the account, phrased so that "well, more work needs doing here" doesn't get her off the hook.

Finally: if you think one of the lines I've specified is actually a weak question given her position, say so in a sentence and swap in a better one rather than dutifully writing it.

Please write the whole thing in British English.
