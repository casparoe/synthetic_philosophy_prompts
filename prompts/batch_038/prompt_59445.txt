**The question** (word limit 600, worth 15% of the module):

Nozick's utility monster. (a) State the thought experiment and the conclusion Nozick draws from it. (b) Explain why this is supposed to be a problem about the aggregation of welfare rather than a problem about inequality, or about the interpersonal measurement of utility. (c) Give one reply a utilitarian could make, and say whether you think it works.

**The student's answer:**

Nozick's utility monster, from Anarchy, State, and Utopia, is a being who gets far more utility out of every unit of a resource than anyone else does. If an apple gives me one unit of pleasure it might give the monster a hundred, so a utilitarian who wants to maximize the total has to keep shifting resources toward it, and in the limit has to accept that the rest of us should be fed to it, because the monster's gains outweigh everything we lose. Nozick's point isn't that the monster is wicked — its a being that isn't doing anything wrong, it's just experiencing a lot. The point is that the theory has no vocabulary for saying these resources are already theirs, since the only question it can ask is how much utility there is in total.

(b) This is why the objection targets aggregation and not equality. Nothing unfair has happened in the ordinary sense: the monster didn't cheat, and the distribution it produces isn't unjust by any patterned criterion, it's just extremely unequal. The complaint is that utilitarianism adds welfare across persons as though all of it belonged to a single subject who is being made better off, so a thousand people losing everything can be outweighed by one being's enormous gain. That's exactly the structural complaint Parfit makes with the repugnant conclusion, which is really just the utility monster applied to population size instead of to a single individual — in both cases the theory is committed to a large total built out of lives that are terrible for the people living them.

(c) A utilitarian could reply by giving up on totals and going in for average utilitarianism instead. If what matters is welfare per head, then pouring everything into one creature doesn't automatically raise the average, so the monster loses its grip. I don't think this reply works, though, because it never answers Nozick's real point, which is that no way of adding up welfare can respect the fact that there is a separate someone the adding up is happening to.
