A friend and I spent an hour last night arguing about whether a source can vouch for itself, and we ended in a stalemate, so I would like the options laid out properly. These are the verdicts I am confident about; please tell me what principle, if any, they add up to.

1. My fuel gauge has never been checked against anything. Last week I saw it read a quarter tank, concluded the tank was a quarter full, and concluded from that that the gauge is accurate. No warrant gained.
2. I suspected my phone's random-number generator was rigged. I drew ten thousand digits, found each appearing roughly a tenth of the time, and concluded it is fair. Warrant gained.
3. A coin that came up heads sixty times in a hundred flips is biased toward heads. Warrant gained.
4. I tracked a horoscope column for a year, scoring each prediction myself and generously, and concluded it predicts real events. No warrant gained.
5. I reverse-engineered a decoding rule from a ciphertext and adopted it because the text it yields is fluent English. Warrant gained — though I would feel differently if the same rule had also supplied the fluency test.
6. A mood diary: each morning I predict the day's dominant mood, each night I record what it was, and after a year the match rate is ninety percent, so I conclude I know my own moods reasonably well. Warrant gained, with some unease.

My friend's position is that I am rationalizing: 2 and 4 are the same procedure, and 3 only looks different because I happen to like its conclusion. My own suspicion is that what matters is whether the inferred property is constituted by the source's outputs — fairness, bias — or merely correlated with something independent of them, as accuracy and predictive success are. But that appears to give the wrong verdict on 5, where fluency is manufactured by the very rule under test, and perhaps on 6 as well.

Give me one principle, or a small set of candidates, each stated precisely enough to work as a decision procedure; for each, name the case that motivates every clause, deliver a verdict on a case I have not listed, and say candidly which of my six it cannot accommodate. End with your own judgment on whether my friend is right that 2 and 4 collapse. Please write in American English.
