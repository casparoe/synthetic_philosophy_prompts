ok so I've been going around in circles on the Pasadena game and I think I might have found where the standard argument goes wrong, but tell me if I'm just being dense.

My setup, which I think is basically the Pasadena gamble: you flip a fair coin until it lands heads. If heads first shows up on toss n, you get paid (-1)^(n-1) * 2^n / n dollars. So +2 if it's heads straight away, -2 if heads comes on the second toss, +8/3 on the third, -4 on the fourth, and so on. The line I keep reading is that this gamble has no expected value because the series blows up. But when I actually write it out I get (1/2)(2) + (1/4)(-2) + (1/8)(8/3) + (1/16)(-4) + ... which is just 1 - 1/2 + 1/3 - 1/4 + ..., and that converges to ln 2, about 0.69. So the value isn't missing at all, it's sitting right there. And the rearrangement objection looks like a red herring to me — nobody is going to physically reorder coin tosses, the flips happen one after another in a fixed temporal order, so the sum is the sum. ln 2, done.

Then I tried slowing the payoffs down to dodge whatever's left, since I figured the original trouble was that the payouts grow too fast: heads on toss n pays (-1)^(n-1) * 2^n / n^2 instead. Same move gives me 1 - 1/4 + 1/9 - 1/16 + ... = pi^2/12, roughly 0.82.

So, three things:

1. Is my "the series doesn't actually blow up" point a real hole in the argument, or am I misdescribing what the argument ever claimed? If it's the latter, what is the actual complaint about this gamble, stated precisely enough that I can check whether my two versions fall under it?

2. Does the n^2 version genuinely escape it, or is it the same trap wearing nicer numbers? And is "the payoffs grow too fast" even the right diagnosis of what needed fixing — because both of my games have payoffs that get arbitrarily large, so clearly that can't be the whole story.

3. Concretely: if someone offered me either gamble against a guaranteed 65 cents, which one do I take, and does the answer come out differently for the two? Work through what you'd want to check before letting a number like 0.69 or 0.82 be compared against 0.65 at all — I keep feeling like there's a catch here and I can't pin down what it is.
