So a friend of mine is writing a dissertation on hypocrisy and I've been freeload­ing off her reading list for about a year now, mostly at night. I don't do philosophy professionally — I work in compliance at a hospital, which is probably why one particular pattern keeps snagging on me at work and I've never been able to find a word for it. I want to know whether it's a recognized thing in the literature or whether it's just an idiosyncratic itch I have.

The setup is this. Someone condemns another person, pretty forcefully, for doing X. And this person has genuinely never done X. Not once. No cover-up, no selective memory, no "well that one time didn't count" — their record on X is spotless. So they aren't a hypocrite in the normal sense, because there's no mismatch between what they say and how they've actually behaved. But it turns out the spotless record is an artifact of the fact that they were never in a situation where X was tempting, or even available. They grew up somewhere it didn't come up, or the stakes never got high enough to matter, or somebody else always ate the cost so they never had to choose. And sometimes you get to see the counterfactual resolved, because they change jobs, or the situation finally arises, and they do X within a month — or they say something out loud that makes it obvious they'd have done it in a heartbeat given half a chance. The condemnation isn't coming from a fraud. It's coming from somebody who was never tested.

Two real ones from my life. A physician I worked with used to be very loud at our M&M conferences about a certain kind of chart-cutting-corners, and she was personally impeccable about it, genuinely, no complaints ever. But she'd spent her whole career in a department where three senior partners read everything before it left the building, so there was never a single moment where the shortcut would have bought her anything. Six months after she moved to a place with no such review, her notes were worse than anybody's on staff. Nobody thought she'd been lying before, exactly. It just retroactively made all those speeches feel like they'd been free. Second one: a guy in my book group goes hard on people who cheat on their partners. He's faithful, always has been — he's also been with exactly one woman for twenty-two years, is completely gone on her, and has said out loud that he has no idea what he'd do in other circumstances, and I don't think he's fishing for compliments when he says it.

Here's the part I can't name: what, exactly, is wrong with the blaming in these cases? It isn't insincerity. It isn't whataboutism — nobody's deflecting anything. And it doesn't seem to be the ordinary hypocrisy complaint, since that whole complaint looks like it depends on your having done the thing, or at least doing it now. It's more that clean hands which never cost you anything don't seem to earn you anything, though I'll admit the intuition wobbles under pressure, because presumably we'd still rather have the clean-handed person do the blaming than the dirty-handed one, however each of them got that way. So does this have a name? I've heard moral luck brought up in connection with the driver who runs over a child through no fault of his own, and I keep seeing the phrase standing to blame, and I assume those are neighbors, but neither one looks like it's this. I'm not after just a label — I want the carving. What is the phenomenon precisely, what's the nearest established concept, and how does it differ from plain hypocrisy, from a general loss of standing, from moral luck, and from the old virtue-ethics point that doing the right thing isn't the same as being the kind of person who'd do it anyway? And where are the edges: does it still bite if the person would genuinely have held up under pressure but was simply never asked? Does it matter whether an outsider can tell they're untested, or only whether they are in fact untested? And does it get worse if they eventually do X, or is the eventual doing a separate problem that just reveals what was already true? If there's really no term for it, coin one for me and tell me which cases fall just inside and just outside it.
