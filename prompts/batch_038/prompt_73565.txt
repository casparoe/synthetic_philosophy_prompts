So I run a tiny podcast that is basically forecasting-nerd catnip — prediction markets, calibration, base rates, the whole lineage going back to the old rationalist blog days. Next month's guest is a mathematical biologist turned macrohistorian: not Turchin himself, but someone who's spent about fifteen years fitting structural-demographic models to long-run data and who is a committed defender of the secular-cycles picture. She's sharp and she's pleasant, which is the problem, because I do not want forty-five minutes of her patiently re-explaining what elite overproduction means.

Write me nine interview questions, plus for each one a single line telling me the reply she'll reach for and the follow-up that makes that reply cost her something. It's audio, so plain English and no equations, and no generic softballs about how she got into this stuff. Also don't tack on a reading list at the end, I've got the books.

The pressure points I want covered, roughly in this order:

- The 2010 Nature letter saying the coming decade would be one of growing instability in the US and western Europe. She likes the forest-fire framing — deadwood piles up, you can't say what the spark will be. I want a question that separates the risky part of that claim from the safe part, and asks what the 2020s would have had to look like for her to call it a miss.
- The instability index itself. Riots, strikes, assassinations, terror events pulled out of a cross-national time series. Is she measuring instability, or is she measuring record-keeping and press coverage, and how would she check?
- Elite overproduction. How it actually gets operationalized — aspirants per position, advanced degrees, top-end wealth share — and how you tell it apart after the fact from the boring story that times were hard so people were angry. Does "counter-elites" do real work, or is it a relabeling device?
- The phase-shift move she and the Korotayev crowd make: if two variables oscillate out of phase, a flat same-year correlation doesn't falsify anything. I think there's a real dynamical-systems point in there and I also think it's a universal escape hatch. Make her say which data shape would rule the model out.
- Where the period comes from. Is the roughly fifty-year rhythm emergent from parameters fixed on independent grounds, or tuned until the peaks line up? What's the difference between a dynamical model and a curve fit wearing a story?
- Seshat. How much of the moralizing-gods result survives a re-coding, who arbitrates when specialists disagree about a code, and what the "natural experiment in history" design assumes about cases being independent.
- The n=1 problem. One realization, no controls, nonstationary systems. She came out of population ecology where you get replicate systems — ask what transfers and what doesn't.
- Genre guilt. Kondratiev waves, Strauss-Howe fourth turnings, Spengler. What's the difference in kind rather than in rigor, and why should my audience not file her with those?
- Reflexivity, plus the 2020 claim with Goldstone that unrest persists regardless of which party holds power until someone actually moves on inequality. Any calm decade can be deferred rather than falsified, and a public forecast aimed at elites may change the thing it forecasts. Get her to say whether that's a feature.

Two of those I want flagged as the cruxes — the ones worth burning ten minutes on if we run long — and I want one question somewhere in the middle that concedes something real to her side, because I actually think the mechanism story is better than the skeptics allow and I don't want the hour to sound like a prosecution. Close with her committing, on the record, to one concrete probabilistic claim about the next decade with a resolution date she'd let us score.
