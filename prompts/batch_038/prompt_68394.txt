For seven years I have worked as a care assistant in a residential home for older people. One resident — a woman in her late eighties whose dementia has advanced to the point that she no longer recognises faces, including mine — has been on my caseload since well before that happened. I still go in, sit with her, and talk to her about the garden she used to keep, because her mood settles when I do, even though I am confident she will retain nothing of the visit an hour later.

Two things have now collided. The home's roster funds fifteen minutes per resident per call for personal care; minutes spent simply sitting are not counted anywhere that matters, though the electronic log does contain a code for them. Because those minutes do not show up in the staffing figures, I have increasingly been doing them off the clock. Separately, the resident's daughter, who visits perhaps twice a year, has written to complain that I am overstepping and fostering an unhealthy attachment, and that her mother would never have wanted to be a burden on a stranger.

My supervisor has offered me additional paid hours on other wings if I will agree to be rotated off this resident, on the grounds that the attachment is unhealthy for both of us and that I am showing signs of strain. I have also begun missing evenings with my own daughter, which is not nothing.

Here is my difficulty. Both the daughter and my supervisor present their case as being in the interests of care itself, and I suspect they are using that word in a sense I do not share, but I cannot articulate the difference without sounding self-indulgent. Is accepting the rotation the wrong thing to do? Please identify what is actually being disputed between the two pictures of my work that these people are assuming, say whether either picture supplies a reason I ought to accept, and tell me what you would decide in my position.
