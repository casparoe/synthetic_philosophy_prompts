I've been reading on my own about the money troubles of the American Revolution, and the thing I keep returning to is George Berkeley's *Querist*. He holds that money has no intrinsic value whatever, that "the true idea of money, as such, be not altogether that of a ticket or counter," that it is useful only "so far as it stirreth up industry, enabling men mutually to participate the fruits of each other's labour," that value turns on demand relative to plenty so that less money circulating briskly is equivalent to more circulating slowly, that a people could go on rating, buying, and selling in paper even if the bullion were gone entirely, and — the part that interests me most — "whether arbitrary changing the denomination of coin be not a public cheat."

I'd like you to run the Continental dollar through that theory. The facts I have in mind: Congress began emitting paper in 1775, each note carrying a printed promise that the bearer would one day receive a stated number of Spanish milled dollars; Congress had no power to tax and could only requisition the states; it emitted something on the order of two hundred million dollars over five years; the notes slid from par toward worthlessness, wrecking soldiers' pay and army supply; and in March 1780 Congress called in the old emissions at forty to one against new bills. "Not worth a Continental" became proverbial.

So: on Berkeley's account, what exactly went wrong here — a failure of paper as such, or a failure of something the notes lacked? Do his claims about demand, plenty, and velocity actually generate the observed pattern of depreciation, or do they run out at an issuer with no coercive power to make anyone want its tickets, a war that destroyed the goods the tickets were supposed to command, and a retroactive revaluation that looks very much like his "public cheat" committed by a body that had no other way to pay its army? Then tell me what the theory is silent about, whether his own remedies — paper credit, a national bank, manufactured wants to spur labor — would have helped or hurt in 1776, and whether this episode is a case his framework anticipated, one it quietly assumed away, or a genuine anomaly in treating money as nothing but a counter.
