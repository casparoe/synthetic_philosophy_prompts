So I keep getting pulled back into the ENCODE fight from around 2012, and I want to work through it as a philosophy-of-function case rather than a genomics case. The facts as I have them: the consortium reported that something like 80% of the human genome takes part in at least one biochemical event — gets transcribed, bound by a protein, chromatin-marked — in at least one cell type, and they were willing to call that "functional." A lot of molecular evolutionists pushed back that this is a bait and switch on the word: function means selected effect, the fraction actually under selection is more like ten percent or less, and 80% is impossible anyway given the deleterious mutation load we'd all be carrying.

Run that episode through the etiological vs. causal-role distinction. Specifically: which account was each side actually working with, and were they having one disagreement or two different ones that got tangled? Could ENCODE's data in principle have settled it, or was the quarrel verbal from the start — and if it was verbal, why did both sides behave like it wasn't? Then tell me where the mutation-load argument is doing philosophy in population-genetics clothing rather than population genetics, and whether it begs the question against the causal-role people.

One sub-case I'd like you to handle explicitly: suppose some stretch really is reproducibly bound or transcribed in one cell type, but it isn't conserved and knocking it out costs the organism nothing. The causal-role account seems forced to call that functional, which makes me wonder whether it can say anything at all is non-functional. Does that problem bite here, or is it a red herring? Finish with what the whole mess does to the two accounts — vindicates one of them as the general theory of function, shows that function attribution is irreducibly interest-relative and neither account can absorb that, or reveals that both were being asked a question they were never built to answer. If you think one of the accounts is just silent on what the ENCODE people were asking, say so and say why rather than making it speak.
