we just finished the Kuhn unit in my phil of science seminar and i want to close with something weird instead of another summary handout. Write a sestina about the redistribution of "planet" between Ptolemy and Copernicus, where the six end-words are just the nouns that get sorted into new kinds — sun, moon, earth, star, planet, heaven. The permutation should do the taxonomic work: same six terms coming back every stanza but no longer sitting in the same categories, so by the third or fourth stanza you can feel that the sun has quietly changed sides. Then let the envoi land Kuhn's later, tamer point — the two vocabularies are locally untranslatable, there's no one-to-one map, but you can still learn the other list and compare the worlds, you just can't carry yours over wholesale. A Kuhn scholar should recognize the argument rather than just the name-drop, and someone who's never read him should still get a poem about the sky getting refiled.
