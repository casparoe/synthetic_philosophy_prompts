I want a thought experiment engineered to a specification, not an illustration of a problem I could describe to you myself.

**Purpose.** I am testing whether panpsychism about fundamental physics can support claims about the ethics of suffering without first solving the combination problem. The case should exhibit a single physical act on which four doctrines — all of them compatible with every fact stated in the scenario — issue four different verdicts, such that on one doctrine the act removes every trace of morally relevant suffering and on another it removes nothing whatever. The verdicts must be derived from the doctrines; a scenario that states or hints at the answer fails.

**Physical setting** (your choice, within these limits): a laboratory system whose constituents may be stipulated to be micro-experiential — a trapped-ion array, an ultracold-atom lattice, or comparable apparatus. The only departure from actual physics permitted is an explicitly stated psychophysical mapping from structure to phenomenal character; no new particles, fields, or forces, and no equipment beyond what such a laboratory could plausibly build. An engineer faces a binary choice: retain the system in a *bound* configuration, in which the constituents compose a single unified subject whose experience consists in a sustained, mild, unremitting aversive state with no offsetting goods and no way of being relieved while the configuration persists; or shift it to an *unbound* configuration, in which composition fails.

**What must be held exactly fixed across the two configurations:** the number of constituents; the intrinsic character, intensity, and valence of each constituent's micro-experience; the system's entire functional organization, including whatever the fourth doctrine below treats as sufficient for a unified conscious subject; and every fact an outside observer could in principle measure. Composition — the bonding relation, and nothing else — is what changes.

**The four doctrines**, to be taken as stipulated rather than as any philosopher's actual position:

1. *Subject-first*: unified subjects are the only moral patients; phenomenal character that is not a state of any subject is not suffering and carries no weight.
2. *Constitutive*: a subject's experience is constituted by the micro-experiences that compose it, so the bound configuration contains no disvalue beyond the sum of those micro-experiences.
3. *Additive*: macro- and micro-experiences are distinct episodes, all of which count, so the bound configuration contains both.
4. *Functionalist*: phenomenal character is fixed by functional organization alone; bonding relations that make no functional difference make no phenomenal difference.

**Deliverables, in this order, within 600 words total:**

- The scenario: one paragraph, at most 150 words, containing no moral vocabulary, no report of anyone's intuitions or reactions, and no indication of which doctrine is correct.
- A section headed *Verdicts*: for each doctrine, the moral status of the shift (obligatory, permissible, pointless, wrong) **and** the description under which the act falls — relief, partial relief, rearrangement, annihilation, or nothing at all. For the additive doctrine, give the arithmetic explicitly in terms of the number of constituents. If any two doctrines deliver both the same status and the same description, say so and explain whether the construction has failed to separate them.
- A section headed *What does the work*: name the single stipulation generating the spread; state what the case collapses into if that stipulation is withdrawn; and flag every auxiliary assumption you had to make to reach the verdicts — for example, that micro-experiences can be individuated and counted at all, or that a bonding relation can vary while function is held fixed — saying for each whether it is doing hidden work.
- A section headed *The attack*: the strongest objection a defender of whichever doctrine fares worst could raise against the construction itself rather than against its conclusions, targeting the coherence of the stipulations.

Do not cite, name, or recommend any literature; the four doctrines as stated are all the theory you need.
