Take the Kony 2012 thing and run it through Kierkegaard's account of the press and the public from Two Ages / The Present Age. Recap of the episode, in case it matters: March 2012, a 30-minute film from Invisible Children about Joseph Kony's LRA goes from nothing to roughly 100 million views in under a week, with a celebrity relay doing a lot of the lifting. Then within days a much sharper counter-current — Ugandan bloggers and journalists saying the LRA had been out of northern Uganda for years and was down to a few hundred fighters, that US advisers were already deployed, plus noise about the org's finances and politics. Then the April 20 Cover the Night street-postering event, which drew far fewer people than the view count implied, and about ten days after release the director Jason Russell has a public breakdown on a San Diego street, filmed by passersby, and gets hospitalized. Kony was never caught.

Here's the version of Kierkegaard I want used and then pushed on. His public is not a crowd you could ever assemble: no room holds it, it never acts as a public, it never repents as one, and only the press can produce it, because the press alone addresses everybody at once while nobody is actually present. What it does to the individuals it collects is leveling — the exceptional and the passionate flattened into an average, action displaced by chatter, i.e. reflection that has run out of passion.

So map it. Who or what plays the press here, and does it matter that a feed has no editor — a responsibility gap, call it, where the theory's target used to sit? Which features does leveling genuinely explain: the speed, the relay, the deflated poster night, Russell being eaten by an abstraction he built? And be straight about where it strains. Real money came in, a Senate resolution passed, the AU moved troops — is any of that chatter, and if the theory has to say yes, has it just become unfalsifiable? Also, the critics were themselves a public assembled by the same machinery, so does Kierkegaard have room for a counter-public, or does leveling only ever run one way? Close with whether Russell's breakdown is real confirmation or just a suspiciously tidy one, and whether you'd bet the same analysis on the Corsair affair that turned Kierkegaard himself into a street-mocked caricature in 1846.
