I'm rebuilding my lecture on the lottery and preface paradoxes and I want to teach them as one puzzle wearing two costumes. In both, an agent piles up beliefs that are each perfectly rational on their own but can't all be true, and in both the clean diagnosis is that the agent never really held them outright at all — just had high credences — so nobody in either story comes out irrational. And it seems to me that once you've said outright belief is nothing but credence above a threshhold, the two paradoxes stop being paradoxes: they collapse into the much tamer question of where to set the bar, maybe contextually. So whatever substantive work is left is a matter of tuning rather than principle.

You're an expert epistemologist, so think this through properly — start with some brainstorming before you commit to a line.
