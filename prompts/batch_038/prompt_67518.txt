A friend and I spent an entire evening arguing about which of Sweden and Norway is the more liberal society, and by the end neither of us could say what would have settled it. Every indicator one of us produced was met either with a counter-indicator or with a dispute about what counted as one: one of us called the Norwegian state alcohol monopoly paternalism and was reminded that Sweden runs a monopoly of its own; another exchange turned on establishment, with Sweden's church disestablished at the turn of the century while Norway's remains constitutionally entrenched as the people's church even though its clergy ceased to be civil servants some years ago. Before we resume, I want the specification rather than the scorecard.

Write the desiderata that any comparative measure of a society's liberalism must satisfy in order to license a verdict of the form "Sweden is more liberal than Norway." For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or is really the ideological commitment of one camp in disputes like ours. At minimum, address: how the measure must fix the object of assessment — the state's rules, the citizens' attitudes, or the effective options people actually face; what it owes us about restrictions no law imposes, the informal sanctions and conformity pressures that can narrow a life without violating a right; how it is to handle the split between liberty as non-interference and liberty as effective capacity without smuggling an answer in through indicator selection; whether it must report a distribution — liberal for whom, and at whose expense — rather than an aggregate; and what it must say about weighting and trade-offs across kinds of liberty.

Then tell me which subset of these requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. My own growing suspicion is that a single scalar verdict, neutrality between conceptions of liberty, and freedom from arbitrary weighting cannot all be had at once; if that is right, the impossibility is the interesting result rather than a flaw in the list. Finally, identify the one requirement that excludes the most familiar families of measure — legal-institutional indices, economic-freedom rankings, cross-national attitude surveys — and say why.
