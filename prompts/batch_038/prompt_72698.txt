I lead a small mechanistic-interpretability team, and in our write-ups we routinely say things like "the model represents the goal of appearing agreeable and steers toward it," because such claims predict behavior in contexts we never probed. A colleague now insists this talk is empty: activations push activations, and any claim that *symbols* do the pushing is a redescription that never earns its keep. I want this turned into a thought experiment built to my specifications.

Take the device: a sealed chamber of steel pinballs careening under deterministic, perfectly elastic collisions, in which certain stable clusters of trajectories — call them simmballs, for "symbol-balls" — persist long enough that an observer watching from outside begins to describe the chamber as a place where symbols move, combine, and cause things, though nothing but balls ever touches balls.

Construct two such careenia satisfying all of the following: (1) the microscopic physics is of exactly the same kind in both — deterministic, elastic, no additional forces, no privileged particles; (2) in one, the simmballs do genuine causal work, and in the other the symbol-level description is inert bookkeeping; (3) an observer limited to watching the chamber from outside can, in principle and in bounded time, tell which is which without inspecting individual collisions; (4) the discriminating test does not smuggle in the conclusion by already assuming that high-level causation is a real relation.

After the case, tell me which single feature of it does the work, what the verdict becomes if that feature is removed, and how a reductionist who accepts my test would nonetheless argue that the two careenia differ in no respect that matters. Close by saying what this licenses me to write: under what conditions is "the model wants X" a causal explanation rather than a relabeled behavioral pattern?
