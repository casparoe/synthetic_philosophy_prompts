I'm putting together a short talk for a metaphysics reading group on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, and I've built an argument that I'm now pretty sure is too strong, but I cant see where it breaks. Here it is.

(1) If a and b are genuinely distinct, then whatever joins them is distinct from both — a joiner that just was one of the things joined wouldn't be joining anything.
(2) So joining a and b requires a third item, call it R.
(3) But R is itself distinct from a and b, so by (1) joining R to them requires a fourth item, and then a fifth to join that one, and so on without end.
(4) So a and b never actually get joined: an endless stack of joins is never finished, and a joining that never finishes never starts.
(5) Nothing in (1)–(4) mentions thought, meaning or propositions — it's just about distinct items and what it takes to unite them.
(6) So the same reasoning shows that a set is never united to its members, the words of a sentence never amount to something meaningful, atoms never amount to a molecule, and the notes I strum never amount to a chord.
(7) But there are sets, meaningful sentences, molecules and chords, so something above has gone wrong.
(8) And if I patch it by denying (1) — insisting the joiner isn't a relatum of the same kind as the joined, that it's unsaturated or otherwise not the sort of thing that needs a further joiner — then the patch works just as well in the proposition case, so the regress turns out to prove nothing about propositional unity either.

So either (6) is false and thought has some feature that sets and molecules lack, or (8) is false and the regress really does bite on propositions while leaving everything else alone. I keep flip-flopping between those and both feel like special pleading. Tell me which step you'd actually reject and why — I'd most like to keep (6) and (7) since I'm not about to stand up and tell the group there are no molecules, but I'll give them up if that's what it takes. And please dont just survey the options and hand me a menu: commit to one rejection, say plainly what it costs you, and tell me what you'd say to someone in the room who pushes on the other horn.
