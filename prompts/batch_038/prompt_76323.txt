so I help put together a tiny philosophy-of-physics zine, and for the next issue I want a parody of one of those laminated public notices taped to the counter at a municipal land-registry office.

Setting: the city of Einsteinburg. One neighborhood, officially designated The Hole, contains no houses, no roads, no people, no matter of any kind — just surveyed parcels of land on the books. Overnight the registry has re-issued every parcel description in town under a smooth shift: each lot's legal description now sits two blocks east of where it used to, so that the bare point of land our complainant calls Lot 412 now carries the description that used to belong to Lot 414. Nothing she can see, touch, or measure has changed. Her fence is still on the same side of the same creek as her neighbor's well. She has come in to file a takings claim on the grounds that her property was moved out from under her.

Deliverable: the Deeds Office's official customer-facing FAQ notice, 650–750 words, deadpan bureaucratic register, with a form number, an ordinance citation (invent both — no real statutes, no real cases, nothing a reader might go looking up), headings, a hours-of-operation line, and at least one footnote. The office's policy should be flat and unapologetic: two filings that differ only by a relabeling of the parcels are the same filing, no fee is owed, and the office does not adjudicate which labeling is the true one.

The jokes have to come from getting the philosophy right, not from the word "parcel" being inherently funny. I want a reader who knows the debate to be able to tell you actually understand it:

- that this isn't the office pleading ignorance. it's not "we just don't know which parcels got shifted" — the notice should make clear the office thinks there's nothing further to know, and should be careful about how different those two positions are.
- that the office only recognizes registered coincidences — this well at that corner, this fence along that creek — and has no procedure whatsoever for a bare point of land with nothing on it.
- that the indeterminacy is confined to The Hole and its future; the past, and everything with matter in it, is settled on the books, which is exactly what makes the shifted filing admissible in the first place.
- a fair hearing for the other side: a complainant's sidebar where the residents who hold that the parcels exist in their own right, prior to and independently of what's built on them, can state their grievance in their own words — and where the office concedes they aren't obviously crazy, just expensive.
- at least one jab at the office's own smugness. the satire should cut both ways a bit; the policy should be defensible and also faintly absurd as a way to run a city.

Do not use any of the technical vocabulary of the debate anywhere in the notice itself. no "diffeomorphism," no "substantivalism," no "haecceity," no philosopher's name, dead or alive. The whole thing has to work as a municipal document that a reader with no physics would find merely tedious and that a specialist would find excruciating.

American English, please. Format: the notice first, then one section titled "What I was actually satirizing" (~200 words) mapping the bits of the piece onto the real moves in the arguement, and closing with a single honest sentence about something the piece oversimplifies or gets wrong for the sake of the bit.
