A couple of us on a small AGI safety team have been going back and fourth for months about whether we should deliberately build surrogate goals into a system's terminal value structure — a standing disposition to preserve some other agent's goals, including the goals of an agent that could threaten us acausally (blackmail via simulated copies, torture threats, that kind of thing), as a sort of preemptive peace offering. One camp says that's the only sane hedge, because if the threat landscape is real then refusing to hold any surrogate goals is just gambling that it isn't. The other camp says the threat landscape is way more speculative than the hedge assumes, that an attacker's model of us has to be accurate enough to detect compliance and that's doing enormous unargued work, and that a surrogate goal is a built-in handle for manipulation — an attack surface we'd be handing over on purpose, plus a component that has to survive optimization pressure as terminal rather than quietly instrumental.

Write the joint statement both camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. We have maybe eighteen months before a capability milestone where this stops being an abstract question, and I want to know whether the fight is worth more effort or whether it mostly dissolves.

Structure it like this: first a numbered section of claims both sides can actually sign — including on the mechanics, like what an acausal threat requires in terms of the attacker's model of us and whether there needs to be any channel at all by which the attacker learns whether we complied, and whether a surrogate goal can stay terminal under optimization. Then a disagreements table, each position phrased so that its own side would accept the wording without wincing. Then the cruxes, ordered by how much settling each one would move the other side, and for each crux the argument or thought experiment both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance so they can be scored later.

Also flag which parts of this you think are verbal rather than substantive — I suspect some of it turns on what "threat" is allowed to mean when there's no causal channel behind it, and on whether preserving another agent's goals counts as a concession or as something the system would do anyway for independent reasons. For those, say what a non-arbitrary resolution would look like, if there is one. Don't referee who's right, and don't give me a reading list — I want the document, not the bibliography.
