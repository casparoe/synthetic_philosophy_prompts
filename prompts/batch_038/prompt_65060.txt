ok so for my rationality-over-time seminar we get to hand in a creative piece instead of the usual essay, and I want to write a short story about money pumps — but not the textbook one where some guy prefers A to B to C to A and a bookie skims a penny at each step. I want the diachronic version, where the person's preferences are perfectly sane at every single moment and the pump works only because they change in a predictable way.

here's the setup I've landed on. The story is set in a port town where everyone's tastes turn with the monsoon — two seasons, two selves, and the flip happens on published dates, so its nothing mysterious, it's more like a tide table. Because everybody knows the turn is coming, there's a whole trade built around it: brokers who walk you through a sequence of swaps, each one of which you genuinely want at the moment you make it, taking a small cut each way, so that by the end of the season you're holding exactly the bundle you started with and you're poorer by the fees. My narrator is one of those brokers. Their client this season is a woman who has bound herself in advance and refuses to trade at the turn at all, and the story is mostly the long conversation between the two of them.

what I need out of it:

- the accounting has to actually work. pick three or four concrete goods, lay the swaps out in order, and make sure a reader with a pencil can verify that every step was the thing she wanted then and that the loop closes on her original bundle.
- a scene where the broker explains, in plain talk with no symbols or diagrams, why knowing you'll want to trade it all back later doesn't save you if you want the trade right now — and where that explanation visibly fails to land on her.
- the self-binding has to cost her something real and concrete, and the story should not resolve whether she made the right call. I want the reader to come away unsure whether she's the disciplined one or the one being robbed by her past self.
- first person, past tense, 1300-1500 words, no philosophers named, no seperate lecture-y asides explaining the theory to me.

Can you write that? feel free to swap out the goods or the town if something else makes the mechanics cleaner, but keep the pump strictly diachronic — no circular preferences at any one time.
