I spent forty years in a national metrology laboratory, and since retiring I have been reading formal epistemology with the attention I once reserved for instrument calibration. The question that grips me is what an agent ought to do when the observation itself arrives blurred — a reading only half resolved, an endpoint I am not sure I witnessed — rather than crisp and certain.

Please build a decision tree that carries a working scientist from the admission "my new evidence is not certain" to a named updating policy. Every branch node must be a question the scientist can answer from the inside, without knowing which label the branch leads to: for instance, whether the experience leaves the prior probability of the observed proposition recoverable at all; whether it picks out a partition of possibilities that was already there or forces the partition itself to be redrawn; whether the agent can say what she would have judged had the observation come through clearly; and whether she treats her prior as a fixed input or as itself revisable. Each leaf should be a policy — strict conditionalization, Jeffrey conditionalization, Lewis's imaging, the weighted-averaging response on which no single posterior is privileged, and radical probabilism understood as a refusal to legislate the update — plus one further leaf for a policy that seems coherent but that essentially nobody defends, with a diagnosis of whether it is empty because it is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or because it has simply not been explored.

Two further requirements. First, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot in principle separate; I suspect Jeffrey conditionalization and imaging agree over some region of cases, and I would like to be told precisely where they part company and by which kind of example. Second, drive the whole instrument with laboratory cases rather than coloured surfaces glimpsed at dusk: a chromatogram peak I cannot tell whether it is one component or two, a titration whose colour change may or may not have occurred at the moment I looked away, a colleague's report of a result I only partly trust, and one case in which the noise in the apparatus has itself changed since my prior was formed.
