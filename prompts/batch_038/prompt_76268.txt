She's been extremely consistent about which things she'll call diseases and which she won't — two years of seminar plus one paper — but I still can't tell what she's actually running. She insists she's a naturalist and she's allergic to anything that smells like normativism, and yet half her verdicts look normative to me.

Her track record so far:

- Male pattern baldness: not a disease, not even close. Treatable, medically managed, plenty of men are wrecked by it — doesnt matter.
- Presbyopia in a 55-year-old: not a disease. Her line is that it's not a malfunction, it's the schedule.
- Congenital deafness in a woman raised in a signing community, no interest in an implant, says she isn't missing anything: still a disease, and she'll go twenty minutes on why the woman's own assessment doesn't settle it.
- Live kidney donor, a year out, eGFR 55, runs marathons, feels great: not a disease. He's a donor, not a patient.
- Born with one kidney, never knew it: she called this a borderline case she didn't want to spend a seminar on, which is as close to a non-answer as she ever gives.
- Moderate depression that's kept a guy from applying to two jobs he wanted: disease, obviously.
- Blood pressure sitting at 150/95, no symptoms, doesn't care, no life plans touched: disease, and whether she minds is beside the point.
- When the cardiology societies lowered the blood-pressure cutoff, she wrote that nobody had gotten sicker overnight — but that the adults parked at 135/85 had been borderline all along and the panel had just stopped pretending otherwise.
- And in print she's said both that harm has no role in the analysis of disease and that a condition nobody is worse off from isn't a disease. As far as I can tell she's never addressed the tension.

New thing she's been asked to rule on for a symposium: a 27-year-old woman, incidental finding on a pre-op screen. She has a variant that leaves her with roughly a fifth of typical activity of a plasma enzyme that breaks down one of the muscle relaxants used in general anesthesia. Never had a symptom, every other number normal, no biomarker off. In ordinary life it does literally nothing; it matters only if she's given that particular drug, and she has an elective ACL reconstruction booked for March.

So: predict her verdict. Then give me the candidate analyses that are actually consistent with everything above — name the real positions, don't just say "she's a naturalist" — and say what each one comes out as on this woman. My own hunch is that she's running a statistical-subefficiency account with an ability clause smuggled in somewhere, and I want you to tell me whether that hunch survives contact with the list. Then name the single further case that would best separate the live options. And whatever she says, tell me what she's now committed to about BRCA1 carriers and about APOE4, and whether that's a line she'd want to hold. Two follow-ups I especially want: if she says disease, which of those two in-print sentences is she giving up, and if she says not-a-disease, what does she do with the deafness case — since there she was adamant that how a condition bears on the person's own life isn't what decides it.
