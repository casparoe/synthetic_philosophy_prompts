Topic: when is a region actually allowed to leave a country. Here's my verdicts and they don't fit together.

- Scotland 2014: London agreed to let them vote, huge turnout, they voted no about 55-45. Legit, obviously. But I also think they were entitled to ask again after Brexit, since Scotland voted to stay in the EU and the whole deal changed. So a no doesn't seem to be forever in a way that a yes would be, and I can't tell if that's principled or just me liking them.

- Catalonia, October 2017: Spanish courts said the referendum was unconstitutional, they ran it anyway, turnout was maybe 40%, almost everyone who did vote said yes, then they declared independence and it fell apart in about a week. Not legit. I don't think that declaration created any right at all.

- Quebec 1995: legal vote, lost 50.6 to 49.4 with basically everybody voting. Thing is, if the yes side had won by that same half percent I don't think they'd have been entitled to just walk out. They'd have to negotiate, and I think the rest of Canada could have demanded a second, clearer vote. A 0.6% margin on a complicated question doesn't feel like the will of a people to me.

- Kosovo 2008: no referendum at all, straight up unilateral declaration, Serbia never agreed, a bunch of countries recognized it right away and others still won't, including some in the EU. And I think this one WAS legitimate. Which is the exact opposite of how I judged Catalonia. Best I can do is that Kosovo had gone through ethnic cleansing and years of UN administration, so maybe it's about the oppression rather than the process. But I don't know if that's a real principle or a rationalization.

- South Sudan 2011: referendum written into a peace deal after a long civil war, something like 99% yes, became a country. Obviously legit.

- Bougainville 2019: voted about 98% for independence in a referendum Papua New Guinea agreed to but that isn't legally binding, and years later they're still not a country. I think they've earned it, but I genuinely can't tell you whether that vote obliges PNG to let them go, and that gap is what's driving me crazy.

- Falklands 2013: voted like 99.8% to stay British and I think that settles it, Argentina has no case. But most of the people voting are descended from settlers Britain put there, so apparently I'm fine with a settler population deciding its own status here and not fine with something similar somewhere else.

- Iraqi Kurdistan 2017: held an independence referendum, overwhelming yes, Baghdad rejected it and moved troops into the disputed areas including Kirkuk. I think they had a right to hold it AND I think holding it was a bad mistake. That doesn't feel like a contradiction but maybe it is.
