I sat in on the last session of a consciousness workshop last week (I'm not in the field, I was there for the free lunch and my sister's poster) and two of the speakers got into it by the coffee urns afterwards. I wrote the exchange down as best I could from memory because I want to know who actualy won and I can't tell. One of them — I'll call her M — works in the integrated information theory camp. The other, R, is a computational/ML type who's skeptical. Roughly in order:

1. R opened with what he called the unfolding argument. Take any recurrent network, he said, and unroll it in time — replace the feedback loop with a long stack of copies of the same layers, purely feed-forward. It implements exactly the same input-output function. But on IIT the unrolled version has essentially no Φ, because nothing in it acts back on itself, while the recurrent one can have a lot. So IIT says two functionally identical systems can differ in consciousness. And since everything science can measure is function — inputs, outputs, lesions, perturbations, recordings — the theory's central claim is unreachable in principle. His line: IIT is either false or its not a scientific theory of consciousness at all, it's a claim about something no experiment could ever get at.

2. M said that's not an objection, it's a description of the theory. IIT never said consciousness is a function of inputs and outputs; it says consciousness is intrinsic cause-effect power — what a system does to itself, from the inside, in the state it's in right now — and the whole method is to unfold a system's causal powers and see what it specifies. The unrolled copy has the same external function and no intrinsic power over itself. So all the unfolding move shows is that IIT and functionalism disagree, which nobody disputes. She called it an argument that functionalism is incomplete, not an argument that IIT is false.

3. R said fine, but that makes it unfalsifiable, which is worse rather than better. And he added the practical point: Φ can't actually be computed for anything remotely brain-sized — people can't do exact Φ for a few dozen logic gates without approximations, and the number depends on the whole state of the system plus which subset of units you settle on as the complex in the first place. Untestable in principle, unusable in practice.

4. M said in-principle uncomputable is not the same as unconstrained, and pointed at what she called the theory's track record: IIT predicts the cerebellum contributes little to consciousness even though it holds most of the brain's neurons, because its circuitry is feed-forward and modular; it puts the action in the thalamo-cortical system; and the line of work where you perturb the cortex with TMS and measure how complex the echo is, which people now use on unresponsive patients, came out of it. A bedside tool, she said, not metaphysics.

5. R said every one of those is still a functional prediction — a claim about which circuits subserve which functions — and a functionalist makes all of them too, with fewer assumptions. The only prediction that's distinctively IIT's is that a system can be functionally identical to a conscious one and be dark inside, and that's exactly the one she's conceded can never be checked. His summary: the testable content isn't IIT's, and IIT's own content isn't testable.

6. M said accept that and it still costs something real. If IIT is right, then anything built as pure feed-forward computation — however good it gets at talking, reporting, insisting it has feelings — has no experience whatsoever, and simulating a brain on ordinary hardware produces nothing either. She said that's a substantive claim about what's sitting in front of us right now, a reason to care about physical implementation rather than software, and functionalism can't give it to you.

7. R said now she's using the uncheckable claim to license a practical stance while insisting the uncheckability doesn't matter. And he pressed his original point: her own example shows the extra Φ in the recurrent network does no work in explaining anything that network does. It doesn't change the outputs, the behaviour, or the reports. So Φ is explanatorily inert — epiphenomenal by construction — and she demonstrated it herself.

8. M said epiphenomenal with respect to output, yes, and IIT is comfortable with that. On her telling, reports and behaviour are produced by feed-forward machinery that reads out what the conscious complex specifies; the experience isn't a link in the causal chain to the mouth. She said the fact that Φ never shows up in the input-output function is precisely why there's a hard problem, and complaining about it is like complaining a theory of colour experience is unscientific because it doesn't change the wavelength measurements.

9. R said that's a confession dressed as a defense, and that the demand he's making isn't functionalism smuggled in as a criterion of respectability — it's just what it means for a claim to be about anything. If two situations are indistinguishable by every possible observation and differ in none of their consequences, saying they differ is saying nothing.

They went around on 9 once more and I lost the thread. My sister thinks M won because R never answered her about the cerebellum and the TMS work; some of R's people think R won because M openly agreed Φ has no observable consequences.
