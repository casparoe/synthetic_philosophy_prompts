I spent most of last night arguing with a friend — a physicist, and a combative one — about whether special relativity forces the future to be fixed. His line was the familiar one usually called the Rietdijk–Putnam argument: two inertial observers who pass one another at even a modest relative speed have differently tilted planes of simultaneity; over intergalactic distances that tilt amounts to years; and if what is wholly real for one such observer is no less real for the other, then events we would describe as lying years in the future are already real, hence already settled, hence there is nothing open about the future and no sense in which anyone's deliberation makes a difference. I think the last two steps are non sequiturs, but every time I said so he accused me of refusing to take the geometry seriously, and I have to admit I could not produce a concrete case that pinned down my objection. I am now running a reading group for a mixed audience — philosophy graduate students, two engineers, nobody with more than undergraduate physics — and I would like to settle the matter with a constructed example rather than with competing assertions.

So: design me a thought experiment to the following specifications. I want a single scenario, no longer than a paragraph, set in flat spacetime, involving two inertial observers who pass one another at an ordinary and technically achievable relative speed, and one distant event that is spacelike separated from both of them at the instant they pass. The scenario must make the argument go through completely: the geometry together with the premise that neither observer's simultaneity relation is privileged must jointly force the conclusion that the distant event is already real, in the sense of being as much a part of reality as the observers' own here-and-now. But that very event must at the same time be genuinely, objectively undetermined — not merely unknown to us, and not "indeterminate" only in the thin textbook sense in which a measurement outcome is said to be undefined until observed. I want a macroscopic, recordable event with a well-defined single-case chance, something on the order of a coin flip, whose outcome is a matter of objective indeterminism in the world your scenario describes. Please also avoid the standard dressings — no alien fleets, no distant galaxies with names I have heard a hundred times, no pedestrian crossing a street — and rule out faster-than-light signalling, exotic matter, and any collapse mechanism that would require a preferred frame. State the numbers you are relying on: the relative speed, the distance, the resulting simultaneity offset, and an explicit check that the offset is large enough to place the event beyond the reach of any causal influence from the moment of passing.

After the scenario, show me that it meets the specifications rather than asserting that it does. Specifically: (i) say which premises of the argument your case uses and which it declines; (ii) locate exactly where "already real" and "already fixed" come apart in the case — state the fact about the world your scenario posits that renders the event real but unsettled, and explain why that fact is compatible with the Lorentz invariance you have assumed, given that whether the event has happened at all is itself frame-dependent; (iii) identify the single feature doing the work, the thing whose removal would collapse your scenario back into my friend's fatalist version, and describe precisely what happens when it is removed; and (iv) give him the best reply available. I expect that reply to run roughly as follows: if the outcome is already real, then there is a determinate fact of the matter about it now, and a determinate fact now is hard to square with a genuine fifty percent chance, so either the chance goes or the reality does. Press that objection as hard as it will bear and tell me honestly whether your case survives it, or whether the specifications turn out not to be jointly satisfiable after all. If they are not, I would much rather be told so plainly, with the reason, than be handed a case that quietly cheats by stipulating the verdicts instead of generating them. One practical constraint: nobody in the room will have looked anything up beforehand and I am not asking for a bibliography, so please give me no references, no citation lists, and no attributed quotations — if you need to invoke a named position, describe it in your own words instead.
