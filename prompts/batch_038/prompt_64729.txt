ok so we're doing the fall of Rome in my history class and my group built this argument and our teacher read it and said it "proves too much" and then wouldn't explain, which is infuriating. here it is:

(1) A state is nothing over and above its institutions — its law, its tax system, its army, its cities and roads, its official language, its religion, its administrative map.
(2) So asking whether Rome fell is just asking whether those things went away.
(3) They didn't. Roman law kept getting applied and copied, the church ran on the old imperial map (a diocese is a Roman administrative unit), bishops picked up the civic jobs the magistrates used to do, Latin stayed the language of law and learning for like a thousand more years, the new kings ruled Roman-style and wanted Roman titles, and the eastern half just kept being a Roman state for another thousand years.
(4) So nothing that made Rome Rome actually disappeared. It transformed. There was no fall.
(5) And nothing in there is special about Rome — institutions get inherited and adapted, never made from scratch, so for any state, its institutions survive into whatever comes next.
(6) Which means no state has ever ended. The Soviet Union didn't fall, the Ottoman Empire didn't fall, nothing that ever existed has ever stopped existing.

I don't accept (6). Something obviously happened in 476 and the USSR obviously ended — my grandparents lived through that part. But I also can't find the broken step, because (1) through (5) all sound fine when I read them out loud and (5) really does look like it follows from (1) and (2). Is the problem (3), like am I only listing the institutions that survived and quietly skipping the ones that vanished? Or is (1)/(2) sneaking something in about what it takes for a thing to be the same thing? Or is (5) just overreaching?
