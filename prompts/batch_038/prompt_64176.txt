So I finally wrote out my whole Sleeping Beauty position in one place, because our meetup is doing an argument night on anthropics next week and I keep catching myself saying different things in different comment threads. The setup is the usual one — fair coin Sunday night, Beauty woken Monday whatever the outcome, woken again Tuesday only if tails, amnesia drug so she can't tell how many times she's been up. Here's the package, and I want to know if it hangs together:

(1) On waking, P(Heads) = 1/3. Elga's side, not Lewis's. And the numbers that go with it: P(Monday) = 2/3, P(Heads | Monday) = 1/2, P(Monday | Tails) = 1/2.
(2) My reason for thirding isn't really the indifference business, it's counting: over a thousand runs of the experiment you get about five hundred heads-awakenings and about a thousand tails-awakenings, so "what kind of awakening is this one?" has a one-in-three answer from the inside. I think the same engine gives the right verdict in a God's-coin-toss style case too — heads means ten people get created, tails means one, you find yourself existing, you should say 10/11 heads.
(3) Credence is a function of total evidence and nothing else, and I'm not willing to drive a wedge between credence and choice — your number is what you'd bet at. So if the bookie settles only one wager per run of the experiment no matter how many times she's woken up, Beauty's number is *still* 1/3 and she should still pay up to two-thirds of a dollar for a dollar on Tails.
(4) I reject the presumptuous-philosopher move — the one where counting observers hands you near-certainty in a cosmology purely because it implies more people in it — as obviously absurd. Nobody gets to be that confident about physics on that kind of basis.
(5) I also reject the Doomsday argument, and I don't think (2) costs me anything there.
(6) The halfers at my meetup keep saying 1/3 violates Lewis's Principal Principle, since the coin's chance is 1/2 and credence is supposed to track chance. I think they've just misread it: the principle only pins credence to chance when you're lacking inadmissible evidence, and self-locating information is exactly the inadmissible sort.
(7) Their other complaint is that sitting at 1/2 on Sunday while being certain you'll be at 1/3 on waking is diachronically incoherent. I don't buy that either — I knew on Sunday that I'd wake up unable to tell which awakening it was, and that's not the situation reflection principles were ever meant to govern.

So: audit it. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look bad, and for each real one, what has to give and what the cheapest revision costs. Rank them by severity instead of dumping them in one undifferentiated pile, and tell me which single change would clean up the most — and for the ones you think are fine, say briefly *why* they're fine, so I can tell you actually checked rather than rubber-stamped half the list. I'm deliberately not telling you which of these I'm most attached to, because I honestly don't know, and that's the point of asking. American English please, and skip the bibliography — I've read the threads, I want the reasoning rather than the references.
