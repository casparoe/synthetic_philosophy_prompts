I am preparing a reply for a philosophy-of-time seminar at which the Rietdijk–Putnam argument will be presented as settling the question in favour of eternalism. I do not want the argument restated and I do not want the familiar objections recited; I want a case built to specification.

**Purpose, which fixes what will count as cheating.** The case is meant to establish what the argument proves when every quantity appearing in it is one that actual human beings could occupy, arrange, or measure. A construction that secures its verdict by stipulating a separation or a relative speed that nothing in the story could instantiate fails, however elegant it looks.

**Constraints.**

1. Two human inertial observers who coincide at a single event, that coincidence fixing the shared present from which everything is reckoned.
2. Their relative speed must be one a person can sustain on foot. State the figure you use.
3. Every event whose reality is at issue must be located inside the solar system.
4. The case must grant the argument all of its premises: Lorentz invariance, the absence of a dynamically privileged rest frame, the relativity of simultaneity, and the transitivity of the co-reality relation. Whatever defeat you engineers must be kinematic. It may not come from a presentist declining a step, from an appeal to consciousness or observation, or from anyone's noticing anything.
5. The target event must be one about whose temporal order inertial frames can genuinely disagree. If the event you first think of is not of that kind, the case fails; say so and repair it rather than proceeding.
6. Scenario prose: three hundred words at most. The demonstration that follows may run as long as it needs.

**Deliverables.**

1. The scenario, containing one concrete event whose status is in question — an engine ignition, a decay in a detector, the closing of a shutter — at a stated distance, occurring a stated interval after the coincidence event as measured in the rest frame of the first observer.
2. Four numbers for that event: its separation; the interval by which it lies in the first observer's future; the width of the window within which frames can disagree about its order at that separation; and the shift its time coordinate undergoes on the second observer's simultaneity slice at your chosen walking speed. Then state plainly whether the argument reaches the event.
3. The inversion. At your walking speed, how far away must an event be for the tilted slice to reach one second into the first observer's future — and one day, and one year? Compare each of those distances to the scale of the solar system and to that of the nearest star, and draw the consequence for the argument's reach.
4. The load-bearing quantity: the single product on which that reach depends, together with a general statement of the ranges of it for which the argument delivers a future event and for which it delivers nothing an agent could care about.
5. A contrast case differing from the first in exactly one parameter, everything else — the observers, the speed, the coincidence event, the premises granted — held fixed. Identify the parameter and confirm the remainder is unchanged. Then perform the same one-parameter variation in the other direction: retain the solar-system scale and lift the speed restriction to something physically realisable, though not humanly occupiable.
6. The eternalist's best reply, in its strongest form. I anticipate that it will be that confining the argument to frames human beings can occupy is arbitrary, since the physics quantifies over all inertial frames and the universe contains real objects moving relativistically with respect to us. Give that reply, and then specify exactly what further premise a presentist would have to supply — a premise, not a refusal — for the restrictions in constraints 2 and 3 to be principled rather than ad hoc.
7. Your verdict: is this a counterexample to eternalism, or a limit on what this particular route to eternalism can prove? Which does it become when each of the two caps, speed and distance, is lifted separately? Finally, identify the point in your own scenario at which a careless reader would be tempted to import the conclusion, and say whether you have blocked it.

No exposition of the argument, no catalogue of standard objections — the transitivity complaint, the appeal to a distinguished frame, the retreat from co-reality to a relation indexed to a single worldline — and no bibliography. I want the construction and the arithmetic.
