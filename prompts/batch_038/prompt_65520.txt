ok so I'm in an archaeological theory seminar this term and also drafting my dissertation chapter on a small Bronze Age settlement, and there's a convention in site reports I can't stop noticing. Whenever an object or a deposit can't be given a practical function, the write-up calls it ritual. Reports say things like "of uncertain function, possibly cultic," "ritual deposition," "symbolic," "probably ceremonial." My own feature is a pit sealed under a floor with something like 40 fragments of deliberately smashed clay figurines, some cattle bone, and basically no domestic refuse — and my supervisor's draft of the section calls it a foundation deposit showing household ritual practice and a shared belief in protective spirits. My reader's one comment was that we're inferring way too much from way too little.

I'm less interested in whether my pit is actually ritual than in what the classification habit itself is committed to. So: what view of evidence about other people's minds does it take for granted when unexplainedness gets treated as a positive indicator rather than just a gap? Does labelling something ritual even assert anything about beliefs, or only about patterned behaviour — and is it possible the practice is doing the second thing while everyone reads it as doing the first? Which rival positions does it rule out without ever arguing against them (the processual line that prehistoric belief is just not recoverable, the more interpretive line that meaning is constituted in practice rather than inferred from objects behind the practice)? And is it internally coherent that the identical figurine type gets called a discarded cult object in a rubbish pit, a child's toy in a house, and a votive in a pit under a floor — three different mental states read off the same object with only context varying?

Then tell me what my chapter would have to look like if "ritual" had to be earned from something other than the absence of a mundane explanation — what would actually license a claim about belief as opposed to a claim about repeated action, and is there any such evidence available in principle for a pre-literate site. Please keep clearly separate what's settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested between schools; I do not want one tradition's assumptions handed to me as the consensus of the field.
