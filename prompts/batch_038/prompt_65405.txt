Carnap died in 1970, so he never saw the current revival of the socialist calculation debate, and I'm trying to decide whether to build a seminar week around it. The case I want him to react to: a policy outfit has proposed a public planning authority that runs continuous input-output optimization over national supply chains, fed by firm-level transaction data, with a published objective function set by parliament. Their pitch is that the old objection — no center can ever gather and process dispersed knowledge — is now an engineering problem rather than a principle, and they point out that the biggest retailers already operate internally planned economies larger than many mid-century states.

Predict, as concretely as you can, what Carnap would make of this. Three things I'm after: (a) which of his commitments pull him toward enthusiasm and which pull the other way — the tolerance principle, his non-cognitivism about value judgments, the explication program, the socialism he shared with Neurath and Hahn; (b) what he'd say back to a Hayekian who insists the proposal is a category mistake rather than a hard computation; (c) whether he'd treat the choice of objective function as an external/pragmatic framework decision or as an empirical matter that science can settle.

Don't give me a balanced tour of the options. Commit: tell me which of his commitments you think wins out and what he'd actually sign onto, and then flag the single place where you think your prediction is most likely to be wrong.
