I'm helping run a small working group — five people, two years, mostly political theory plus one anthropologist — whose job is to land on a conceptual account of corruption that an aid agency could actually apply when deciding whether a program it funds has been captured. The standard furniture is on the table: office-based definitions (corruption as the misuse of an entrusted public office for private gain), whether the concept is universal or just the Western public/private split wearing a trenchcoat, where gift-giving, hospitality, and kin obligation stop being politics and start being bribery, whether private-sector capture counts at all.

But the specific thing I want you to weigh is a proposal one of us keeps pushing: that the wrong-making feature is concealment, not the transfer. On this view a patronage job handed out openly, with the network acknowledged and voters able to punish it at the ballot box, isn't corrupt even though it's a textbook misuse of an office — while a fully legal, fully disclosed lobbyist dinner might be, if the real deal is hidden from the people the office is supposed to serve. Call it the visibility account. The obvious objection is that disclosure just launders the transfer, or that a corrupt official who discloses is simply one who isn't scared; I don't need you to referee that, though I'd want to know if you think it's fatal.

So: which questions should eat our two years? Give me an ordering of four or five candidates, with "is the visibility account right?" included if you think it deserves a slot, and defend the order. For each one, say whether settling it constrains what the others can look like, whether it's tractable for five people with no fieldwork budget, and what would make you demote it. I care less about whether you like the visibility account than about whether it's the right crux or a distraction from one that matters more — and please flag anything on the list above you'd cut outright as a waste of a year.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate the question back at me.
