Give your credence, then decompose it — how much turns on whether harm must be understood counterfactually, as leaving someone worse off than they would otherwise have been; how much on whether wronging someone requires harming them at all; how much on whether a corporate agent can remain the same responsible party across two centuries; how much on whether a claim can pass to descendants who never personally suffered it; and how much on the impossibility of constructing a credible comparative baseline, which may defeat the claim in practice rather than in principle. A rough probability and one line of justification for each term, plus any term I've left out that does real work, then show how you combine them. Finish by saying which term your final number is most sensitive to, what argument or evidence would move it, and which of these sub-questions you suspect my colleagues are actually divided on when they talk past one another about whether the students of today were ever harmed.
