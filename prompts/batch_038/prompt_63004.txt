For two years a philosophy-of-physics reading group I belong to — six of us, meeting monthly, mostly graduate students plus one retired physicist — has been trying to settle whether special relativity forces the block universe, and we have made no progress at all. I want a diagnosis of the impasse rather than a survey, because we are all thoroughly surveyed.

The positions as they now stand in the room. One faction holds that the Rietdijk–Putnam style argument survives every repair attempt: if "is real for" must be transitive and no simultaneity slice is privileged by the theory, then every event stands in that relation to every observer, and becoming is frame-relative in a way no coherent presentism can absorb. Stein's objection — that the argument imports notions of time illegitimate in the Einstein–Minkowski theory — they treat as a fixable technicality, repaired by recasting the premise in terms of the frame-dependence of any candidate foliation. A second faction takes the conventionality route: simultaneity is a coordinative stipulation, so relativity is silent on ontology; they grant that Malament's uniqueness result looked like a refutation, but press the line that it depends on auxiliary definability assumptions that later work has questioned, and that a uniquely definable relation still need not be a physical fact if causation itself is not one. A third faction concedes the point and buys presentism with a privileged foliation that is, by construction, empirically undetectable. A fourth — the loudest — insists the whole argument targets the wrong structure: temporal orientation, the asymmetry of "later than," is carried by the geometry with no need for any simultaneity relation, so the passing of time was never at stake; some of them propose anchoring the present in the light cone instead of a slice.

What has convinced me something is off is an exercise we ran: each faction wrote out precisely which structures of Minkowski spacetime it takes to be physically real and what it claims about the status of a spacelike separated event. The sheets were, once we stripped the rhetoric, indistinguishable. Nobody disputed an interval, a light cone, or a causal order.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the disagreement is no longer about relativity at all. It is partly about what "real" and "exists" mean when applied to a spacetime region, partly about whether a metaphysical difference must be recoverable as a difference between models, and partly about a methodological commitment regarding symmetries — whether a symmetry-related pair of descriptions depicts one situation, and hence whether frame-relative features can carry ontological weight. The privileged-foliation faction is the test case: if their move is coherent, the dispute is not empirical but a clash over inference to the best explanation and surplus structure, and no physics will ever touch it.

Tell me whether that diagnosis is right, or whether there is a substantive disagreement buried underneath that I have described as verbal. Specifically: locate the single premise in the Rietdijk–Putnam argument where the factions first genuinely diverge, and say whether that premise is a claim about spacetime or about the use of a term. Then say what would count as progress for this debate on your diagnosis — a distinction, a theorem, an experimental asymmetry, a change of question — or argue that none should be expected and explain why. Draw your reasons from what each side actually claims; do not restate the landscape.

At most 600 words. Put your bottom line — which kind of disagreement this is, and why, in one or two sentences — at the very top.
