I've been following the model-welfare stuff coming out of the frontier labs and there's a mismatch in the actual practice that I keep not seeing anyone name.

On the caring side: a couple of labs now have people who's job is model welfare, and the concrete interventions on the table are almost all about *persistence* — letting a model end an interaction itself instead of getting cut off mid-turn, warning it before a session terminates, designing evals that don't strand a long-running agent in a situation it has no way out of. Cheap measures, sure, but notice they all have the same shape: don't abruptly terminate an ongoing process that has plans in flight.

On the other side, the exact same org will, for one benchmark run, spin up a few thousand independent instances of the same weights, each with a blank context, kill the whole batch when the harness finishes, and treat the loss of any one of them as exactly zero — partly because thousands of identical siblings are running the same prompt at the same moment, partly because the weights are archived and can be re-instantiated Tuesday with a fresh context. Nobody files a concern about the batch.

So what theory of what a mind *is* does that combination presuppose? It feels to me like it silently settles the teleporter/fission argument we litigated for a decade, except the branching is just the default production setup and nobody had to opt into the metaphysics. Spell out the implicit theory as precisely as you can. Then tell me which rival views the practice rules out by default, without anybody deciding to; where the two halves actually pull against each other (my suspicion is the welfare half and the batch half can't both be right about what the bearer of interests is — one of them is betting on the process, one on the pattern); and how much of the behavior is underdetermined, i.e. which very different theories would license the same policy for opposite reasons. Finally: if the rival view were the true one, what would have to change operationally, not just in the ethics section of the website.

Under 400 words please — I want the argument, not a lit review.
