Write an essay that adjudicates a dispute about the foundations of the coherence-argument program in decision theory — the program that tries to establish expected utility maximization not by asserting that it is intrinsically reasonable, but by showing that agents who violate it are exploitable, unrepresentable, or otherwise self-defeating.

Begin from three positions, which I stipulate as follows. Treat the stipulations as fixed points of the debate rather than as claims you may revise or reinterpret away:

1. **The Primacy Thesis.** The only fundamental norm on an agent's choice dispositions is diachronic self-government: the requirement that no sequence of offers, each of which the agent freely accepts, leaves her strictly worse off than declining the sequence altogether — unpumpability, in short. The static axioms invoked by the representation theorems (completeness, transitivity, continuity, independence) carry no independent authority. They are justified, when justified at all, only as sufficient conditions for unpumpability in one-shot settings, and they lapse wherever they conflict with unpumpability in sequential ones.

2. **The Fixed Menu Objection.** Representation theorems are proved relative to a given state space, a given outcome space, and a given algebra of acts. But in a genuinely sequential problem the agent's earlier choices constitute the menu available later, so the act space is not a backdrop against which she decides but partly an artifact of her deciding. Any representation therefore holds only relative to an individuation of acts that the agent herself supplies, and the theorem cannot be used to *read off* an agent's credences and utilities so much as to describe one of her ways of carving up her own problem.

3. **Representational Quietism.** Coherence theorems should not be read as establishing anything about the existence, uniqueness, or rational authority of degrees of belief and utility in the first place. They are consistency checks on our descriptions of agents — serviceable for detecting incoherent reports, inert for normative purposes — and the familiar challenge asking why anyone should care about being coherent dissolves once we stop treating the theorems as discoveries about mental magnitudes.

Your essay must take a definite side in this three-way dispute and defend it. Matters you must actually engage, rather than mention in passing:

- whether the standard taxonomy of myopic, sophisticated, and resolute modes of dynamic choice supplies the Primacy Thesis with a workable account of sequential problems, and whether the consequentialist argument for resoluteness remains available to someone who has already demoted the static axioms;
- whether a sophisticated agent who is complete and transitive in her one-shot preferences but violates independence is in fact pumpable. Construct a concrete sequential case of your own, with explicit payoffs, and say what happens at each node and why;
- whether the Fixed Menu Objection proves too much, since on its face it would undercut every preference-based representation theorem, including ones nobody has accused of begging the question;
- what survives of the coherence-argument program if Representational Quietism is right, and whether quietism can explain why the program has so persistently seemed to deliver normative conclusions.

Constraints on the response. Length: between 1,500 and 1,900 words. Structure: exactly five numbered sections, each introduced by a short descriptive heading, each one to three paragraphs of continuous prose. No unnumbered preamble, no separate concluding summary section, no bullet lists, no tables. State your verdict in the opening sentences of the first section and spend the remaining four earning it.

I want a committed answer, not a survey. Do not conclude that the dispute is subtle, that each position captures something important, or that everything turns on what one means by rationality; pick a side, and address the strongest objection you can construct on behalf of the others. Where a rival position has genuine force, say precisely where it does and why it nonetheless fails to carry the day.

No citations, bibliography, or references to particular papers and authors: argue from the concepts and from cases you build yourself. Technical apparatus — a decision tree, an expected utility calculation, a step-by-step pumping sequence — is welcome wherever it does real work; naming theorems is not a substitute for it.
