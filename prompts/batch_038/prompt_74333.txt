Running a small philosophy podcast, and my guest next month defends the view that we literally perceive other people's emotions — that seeing someone's grief isn't a matter of reading their behavior and inferring a hidden inner state, the grief is right there in the face and the posture. She's not a behaviourist, she doesnt think the emotion is identical to the expression, and she also holds that the perceptual capacity itself gets trained up by immersion in stories and narratives about why people do what they do. I want about ten questions that don't let her just perform the view for forty minutes.

Please make sure the list includes:

- one pressing the line between perception and very fast inference: once you concede the capacity is learned from narratives, what is left of the word direct?
- one built on dissociation cases — display rules, flat affect, people born unable to move their facial muscles, where the evidence about whether they recognize others' emotions is genuinely contested. I want her to commit to a prediction rather than shrug that it's an empirical matter.
- one on a purely textual interlocutor, say a chatbot with no body at all, that gets grief exactly right. If she says we can perceive mindedness in the prose too, give me the follow-up that stops perceive from stretching until it covers any reliable sensitivity whatsoever.
- one asking why the problem of other minds feels so gripping in the first place if perception really hands us other minds, plus the unfalsifiability charge that comes with answering that the skeptic is doing philosophy instead of looking.
- one on the asymmetry with self-knowledge: if it's perception, why is it so much worse than my access to my own states, and what does the usual reply about not being able to walk around to the far side of a person actually explain?
- and one asking what finding would make her abandon the view.

For each question give me the follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, not to a strawman, so I can read them as a pair. No filler about what drew her to the topic.
