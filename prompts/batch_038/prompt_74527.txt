I am about to circulate a workshop paper on the transitivity of betterness, and before doing so I would like a consistency audit of the position I actually hold rather than comments on the prose. My commitments are these.

(1) Betterness is not in general transitive. I accept the spectrum-style cases in which every adjacent step from a severe, concentrated harm towards a milder, more widely spread one is an improvement, while the two ends of the spectrum are ordered the other way round.

(2) This is not a vagueness phenomenon. I do not think the comparisons in question are indeterminate, or that anything turns on borderline instances of worse; I take the failure of transitivity to be a structural feature of the betterness relation itself.

(3) I think each outcome in my cases has a determinate quantity of badness, fixed by the harms it contains and how those harms are distributed across people, and that betterness tracks that quantity.

(4) My illustration involves three options. P: a hundred people are killed. Q: a thousand people each lose a hand. R: a billion people each suffer a brief, mild pain. I judge Q better than P and R better than Q, since in each of those pairs the harm is milder and only spread more widely; but I also judge P better than R, since at that scale the sheer aggregate of trivial harms outweighs the loss of a hundred lives.

(5) I reject incomparability: any two outcomes stand in exactly one of the relations better than, worse than, or exactly as good as.

(6) For choice, maximality suffices: rationality requires only that one select an option that nothing in the feasible set beats. I deny that immunity to money pumps is an independent requirement of rationality.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be given up and what the cheapest revision would cost me elsewhere in the paper. Where a pair merely looks problematic, explain why it dissolves. I am not after a survey of the literature or a general defence of transitivity. Please write your answer in British English.
