You are a set theorist who also works in the philosophy of mathematical practice — someone equally at home with the hierarchy of choice principles strictly below AC and with the question of what working mathematicians are entitled to assume. Please think carefully and show me the work before you settle on an answer: I want to see the candidate principles you considered, the specific ledger entries that refute or motivate each clause, and why the runners-up fail, not just the eventual winner. Do not pad the response with a bibliography or a survey of the independence results; I already know the standard story.

Here is my situation. I am drafting a dissertation chapter on the epistemic status of the axiom of choice, and for several months I have kept a private ledger of my own verdicts about which choice-flavored principles I am willing to invoke in a proof. Two friends have been telling me opposite things — a category theorist who thinks I am merely squeamish, and a constructive mathematician who thinks I have not gone nearly far enough — and I have realized I cannot articulate what my ledger adds up to. The entries, in the order I wrote them down:

- Countable choice for arbitrary families of nonempty sets: accept. A universe in which a countable union of countable sets might fail to be countable is not a universe I want to inhabit.
- Dependent choice: accept, without enthusiasm. It buys me Baire category and it lets me build sequences by recursion, and I would rather not lose either.
- Full choice, equivalently the well-ordering principle or Zorn's lemma: reject. Banach–Tarski is the reason. A principle that licenses cutting a solid ball into five pieces and reassembling two copies of it has not disclosed something surprising about space; it has refuted itself.
- Every vector space has a basis: accept, and I use it constantly — extending independent sets, dual bases, dimension. I have since learned this is equivalent to full choice over ZF. I want to keep the verdict anyway and I cannot explain how.
- That there is a non-measurable set of reals: reject. I would like every set of reals to be Lebesgue measurable, and I gather this is consistent with dependent choice relative to a suitable large cardinal. I am aware that Vitali's construction only selects from countably many cosets of the rationals, which is precisely the countable choice I accepted two entries ago. I do not know what to do about that.
- The ultrafilter lemma, equivalently the Boolean prime ideal theorem: accept. It is all I need for compactness in first-order logic and for Hahn–Banach, and I have been assured it does not well-order the reals, so it feels safe. I accept Tychonoff for countable products of copies of the unit interval but not for arbitrary products, since the general version is equivalent to full choice.
- That every set can be linearly ordered: accept. That every set can be well-ordered: reject. These feel categorically different to me even though I know the first follows from the second.
- Selecting one from each of infinitely many pairs of shoes: unproblematic, there is a rule. Selecting one from each of infinitely many pairs of socks: unproblematic when the pairs are sets of reals, not unproblematic in general.
- That every infinite set has a countably infinite subset: genuinely torn. It feels as though it should need no choice at all, yet I am told there are models containing infinite sets with no countable subset whatsoever.

My tentative principle is this. I have been calling a choice principle tidy if, whenever it asserts the existence of a selection, that selection could in principle have been made by a rule fixed in advance from the data of the problem — pick the left shoe, pick the least element under a given well-ordering, pick the point nearest the origin — and I accept exactly the tidy principles plus whatever else is strictly required by theorems I actually use. Two remarks from a seminar have unsettled me about it. First, accepting full choice would not alter a single arithmetical truth, since every model of ZF has an inner model of ZFC with the same natural numbers; so if my objection to AC is that it gets something wrong, the objection has no purchase at the level of arithmetic, and I am not certain it has purchase anywhere else. Second, I gather that in the model where dependent choice holds and every set of reals is measurable there is no free ultrafilter on ω at all, hence no nontrivial ultraproducts indexed by ω and, I take it, compactness must fail somewhere above the countable — and I am the teaching assistant for a model theory course in which both are routine tools. My ledger may therefore be demanding a universe in which my own teaching is impossible.

What I want from you is reflective equilibrium performed on request, in four parts. First, a principle — or a small set of competing candidates, if you think my verdicts genuinely admit two incompatible best systematizations — stated precisely enough to be checked against a case it was not designed for, with every clause tied to the ledger entry that motivates it. Second, the verdict your principle returns for cases I have not listed: the axiom of determinacy as a wholesale replacement for choice, plus two further choice-flavored principles or theorems that you judge diagnostic and that I have overlooked, chosen so that at least one of them splits my ledger rather than sitting comfortably inside it. Third, an explicit statement of which entries your principle cannot accommodate; where you think an entry must be abandoned rather than merely strained, say why it must go, and identify which of my verdicts you would sacrifice to buy the simpler principle and which you would keep at the cost of a baroque one. Fourth, an account of what the Banach–Tarski entry is actually objecting to, given that Banach–Tarski is a theorem rather than a falsehood: is that entry tracking truth, justification, ontological cost, or something else entirely, and can any single principle distinguish the pieces of a Vitali set from the pieces of a Banach–Tarski decomposition, or must a coherent position treat them alike and let my fifth entry fall with my third?
