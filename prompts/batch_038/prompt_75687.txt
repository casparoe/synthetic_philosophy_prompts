I am a second-year graduate student in a metaphysics seminar whose last three sessions have been devoted to a single question we have failed to settle: must grounding be well-founded? That is, granting the familiar logical profile of grounding — a strict partial order, irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive, holding between facts and underwriting the "in virtue of what?" question — must every descending chain of grounds terminate in facts that are themselves ungrounded, or could reality be derivative all the way down, each fact fully grounded by further facts, with no fundamental level anywhere? The seminar has split roughly down the middle, and I have been asked to circulate a position paper in advance of next week's meeting, one that both camps would recognize as engaging their best reasons rather than caricaturing them. Please write that paper: an essay of roughly two thousand words that sets out the controversy, argues its way through it, and ends on an explicit verdict. It will be read aloud to the whole seminar, so it should be self-contained enough for someone who has not been following our discussion, but it should not spend its length re-explaining what grounding is.

I want it to move past the standard standoff — on one side, the intuition that an infinite regress of grounds leaves everything derivative and hence leaves the derivative character of the world itself unaccounted for; on the other, the observation that infinite regresses are not automatically vicious, since each link in such a chain may discharge exactly the local explanatory burden the well-foundationalist wants discharged at the bottom. Beyond that standoff, three things in particular need to be confronted. First, what I have been calling the mirror objection: if each fact derives its content from the fact below it and there is no first, original fact, then the situation is like an infinite corridor of mirrors each reflecting the next, in which no mirror holds an image at all; the infinitist replies that this smuggles in the assumption that whatever is transmitted along a chain must originate somewhere. Say whether that reply works, and, if it does not, whether the objection proves too much — for instance, whether it would equally condemn an infinite sequence of causes, or a chain of testimonial reports with no first witness, neither of which most of us regard as a fatal predicament. Second, what one of my classmates calls the limit-point proposal: that the well-foundationalist's real demand is not for an ungrounded fact at the bottom but for the chain to have a determinate terminus, and that a terminus might be supplied by a limiting structure rather than by a member of the chain — the way the sequence 1, 1/2, 1/4, ... has an infimum that none of its members attains. Is that coherent when grounding is supposed to hold between facts, or does it quietly reintroduce a fundamental level through the back door? Third, whether the question is well-posed at all: if there is no single generic grounding relation but only a family of specific determination relations — set membership, proper parthood, realization, token identity, the determinable/determinate relation — then well-foundedness has to be asked about relation by relation, and the answers may diverge, since atomless gunk is a perfectly respectable model of endless descending chains of proper parts, while standard set theory forbids descending membership chains by axiom and non-well-founded set theory permits them. Address whether this fragmentation dissolves the controversy, merely relocates it, or leaves the general question intact.

Two further requirements. Build the essay around one worked example of a putative infinite descending chain of grounds and return to it throughout, so that the abstract arguments get tested against a case; tell me what your example has to have, and has to lack, in order to do that work. And do not close on a both-sides shrug or with the remark that everything turns on how one means by "fundamental." A qualified verdict is acceptable — something like: a bottom level must be assumed for grounding to play role X but not role Y, or: the demand for termination is legitimate only on a determination-flavored conception of grounding and not on an explanation-flavored one — but whatever you conclude, argue for it. You need not cite or name sources; I am after the argumentative work rather than the apparatus, and I would rather you spend the space on objections and replies.
