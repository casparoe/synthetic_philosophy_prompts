I am a third-year philosophy student in a seminar on moral responsibility and autonomous systems, and our instructor has asked each of us to write down, before arguing for anything, the set of commitments we bring to the topic so that the rest of the group can test them for coherence. Mine is due next week and I have come to doubt that it hangs together. Let me first give the running case we have been working with, since several of my commitments only bite in it. A municipal water utility contracts with a vendor to operate a learned controller over its pumping and valve network. The controller's decisions are not predictable in advance even by the vendor's own engineers, who can state its training objective and its hard constraints but not what it will do in any given hydraulic state. The vendor does ship a "legibility layer": for every control action the system emits a short natural-language rationale — reducing pressure in sector four to protect the main against surge — produced by a second model that reads the controller's internal state but plays no part in determining the action. A night-shift operator sees these rationales, has been trained to treat them as faithful, and retains override authority, though he concedes he understands the underlying hydraulics too little to know when to use it. During a hard freeze the controller does something no one anticipated, a main bursts, and a neighborhood is without safe water for nine days. Nobody's conduct was malicious; several people cut corners.

Here is what I hold. (1) Guidance condition: a person is blameworthy for a harm only if, at some point before it occurred, she was in a position to form a true belief about the risk her activity created and to act otherwise on that belief — blame tracks guided agency, not causal contribution. (2) Tracing: (1) is not indexed to the moment of harm; one who culpably arrives at a position of ignorance, through negligence, self-deception, or indifference to evidence she could have gathered, remains blameworthy, with the blame traced back to the earlier failure. (3) No moral luck: whether the risk ripens into harm is irrelevant to how blameworthy one is, so that two deployers with identical conduct, one of whom injures people and one of whom does not, are equally blameworthy. (4) Only individuals: blame in the strict sense attaches to individual human agents; talk of the corporation, the committee, or the many hands being responsible is a manner of speaking, and wherever blame is warranted there is an identifiable person who satisfies (1) or (2). I do not believe in group agents. (5) The gap is real and specific: for some autonomous systems — those whose behavior resists prediction even after reasonable effort — there are harms for which no one is blameworthy, and this is distinctive of machine learning rather than a general feature of complicated technology. (6) Remedy without fault: the victims in my case are owed compensation, and I would support a no-fault fund financed by manufacturers and operators that requires no finding of individual blameworthiness, because I regard compensation and blame as answering different questions. (7) Blame is a matter of the appropriateness of reactive attitudes — resentment, indignation, the altered interpersonal stance — and of the quality of will the conduct expressed; I reject accounts on which we blame whomever it is socially useful to blame.

Please audit this set rather than lecture me on the responsibility-gap literature, which I have read. I want the tensions ordered by severity, and for each one: whether it is genuine or merely apparent, which of my numbered commitments has to give, what the cheapest revision looks like, and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. I would also like the audit carried through to the water case, where I especially want to know what your reading of (1) implies for the operator who trusted the rationales and for the vendor who supplied them. Finally, tell me which single revision would buy the most overall consistency. I deliberately do not want to signal a preference about which of these I would sacrifice, because I cannot tell which is load-bearing, so please commit: say which one goes and defend that, rather than laying out the alternatives and leaving the choice to me. Where you are genuinely uncertain, say so and give me your best judgment anyway; and if any part of your case depends on reading one of my theses in a contestable way, flag which reading you have adopted.
