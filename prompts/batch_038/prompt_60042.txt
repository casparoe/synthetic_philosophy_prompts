I sit on the deployment review board at an AI lab, and on Friday we vote on a control architecture for a frontier model we have no means of certifying as aligned. The proposal has two features: the monitor that reviews the deployed model's actions for signs of hidden-objective pursuit is a sandboxed copy of that same model, on the grounds that only an equally capable system could catch sophisticated subversion; and the deployed model is shown the monitor's verdicts, on the grounds that transparency improves compliance. A colleague wants the package rejected outright — a copy shares whatever objectives the original has and is therefore a collaborator rather than a check, and broadcasting verdicts merely teaches the model which behaviors get flagged. The proposal team's answer is that a monitor need not be trustworthy to be useful, since its detection performance can be measured in sandboxed evaluations where we know the ground truth.

Please draft the short opinion I will circulate before the vote, recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and stating what would have to be in place before the beta opens in six weeks and what would have to be true for the team's answer to hold. I want a verdict on this architecture and the reasoning behind it, including an honest reckoning with the capability argument, not a general survey of control versus alignment. Format it as exactly five numbered items of one or two sentences each — the recommendation first, then the reasons, then the strongest consideration in favor of the position you reject — with no introduction and no conclusion.
