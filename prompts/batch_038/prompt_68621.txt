ok so for my senior project in AP world history I'm building something for my class, because we've argued all semester about whether societies "collapse" and I'm pretty sure half the class isn't even talking about the same thing. Some people mean the government, some mean the people, some mean the big monuments. I want a decision tree / sorting quiz that makes them commit to what they care about before they get to use the word collapse.

Can you write it? Like 8 or 9 questions, each one a concrete situation instead of a definition — something like "the palace stops keeping records, but the same families keep farming the same fields and the village houses get bigger — is the thing you were tracking still there?" — with two or three answer options each, and leaves at the end that name the position somebody lands in. Some leaves should be positions people actually defend, and if a combination turns up that nobody holds, just label it "unnamed" and say in one sentence why the spot is empty (incoherent, unappealing, or nobody's thought about it yet).

The splits I already think matter, add or throw out as you see fit: what exactly has to survive for it to not count as a collapse (the population, the state, the elite culture, the language, or how well the average person eats); what the baseline is (the peak, the century before, their neighbors, or just what outsiders expect); how fast it has to happen and whether a slow slide counts at all; reversibility — if the cities fill back up 300 years later was it a collapse or an intermission; and whether we're judging from what elites left behind or from skeletons and house floors, since I suspect some famous "collapses" are partly just the writing stopping.

Also flag any two leaves your tree can't actually tell apart, and any combination of answers that seems impossible rather than just unoccupied.

Then run three cases through it and tell me where each lands: the Classic Maya lowlands, Rapa Nui (I read that the really big population crash there maybe wasn't until Europeans and slave raids showed up, but don't take my word for it), and the end of the Bronze Age palatial systems around the eastern Mediterranean. Design the tree so it still works if the archaeology on those gets revised — I don't want it to depend on anybody winning an empirical argument.
