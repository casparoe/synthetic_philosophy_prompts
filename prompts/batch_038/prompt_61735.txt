I did a master's in communications engineering and I work on physical-layer stuff — channel coding, LDPC and polar codes, rate adaptation, link budgets. I know Shannon cold, or at least the parts that get used in a real system. Biology is a hobby: one undergrad course a decade ago, plus whatever I pick up from a friend who's a postdoc in comparative genomics. Last month he said something at a bar that I've been chewing on ever since. I'd asked him whether it's literally true that DNA is a code, and he said yes, then said no, then said it depends which sense of "information" you mean, and I came away with the impression that his field has been going around in circles on this for fifty years and never landed it.

So here's what I want: take the biology-side debate about genetic information and restate it in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can manage. Let me give you the positions the way I've got them from him plus a couple of overview pieces I skimmed — and correct me where I've garbled something, because I've almost certainly garbled something. (1) People say the DNA-to-protein mapping is a code in something like the engineering sense: a fixed four-symbol alphabet, three-symbol words, a lookup table onto twenty-odd amino acids plus start and stop markers, and the table is nearly universal across life. (2) People also say the table isn't random — that codons one mutation apart tend to specify chemically similar amino acids, so the code is somehow error-tolerant against point mutations and misreading — and they say this as though it were a designed property of the code rather than an accident of history. (3) Then there's a claim I don't get at all: that genes carry "semantic" information and not just Shannon information, and that this is the special thing about biology, something about the codon-to-amino-acid assignment being arbitary the way a protocol is arbitrary, and about that arbitrariness needing an explanation in terms of some convention-like arrangement rather than in terms of physics and chemistry. (4) And the pushback, which I think I half follow: that there's no sender and no receiver here, that the machinery doing the "decoding" is itself built under the specification it's supposed to be decoding, so the bootstrapping looks nothing like a link; and that the genome is only one causal resource in development, with the egg's cytoplasm, epigenetic marks and the environment doing work that a comms engineer would insist has to be inside the message if the message is supposed to determine the output. (5) Plus a pile of exceptions my friend mentioned: the table isn't quite universal, some organisms read a stop codon as an amino acid depending on what surrounds it, and which synonym you pick changes how much protein you get even when the amino acid is identical.

Translate all five into coding-theory terms, one at a time, and for each be explicit about how far you had to stretch: which of my terms map cleanly and onto what, which only work if you quietly redefine them, and which claims have no expression at all in my vocabulary — is there anything in the biology debate that literally cannot be said about sources, channels, encoders, decoders, redundancy, synchronization and protocols, no matter how hard you push? Don't just rename their words and hand them back to me; I want to be able to catch you smuggling an assumption across the border. Then the residue report proper: for whatever failed to translate, tell me whether my framework is missing something real — some category Shannon deliberately excluded and biology turns out to need — or whether "code" is doing poetic shorthand work here and the argument is thinner than it sounds from the inside. As a test of the whole exercise, give me one sentence I could say to a room of engineers that states "DNA is a code" defensibly, and one sentence I should refuse to say on pain of being called sloppy. And last: say where you think your own translation is shakiest, and what would change your mind about the verdict — which consideration, if I brought it back to you, would make you say the residue is real and my vocabulary genuinely can't hold it?
