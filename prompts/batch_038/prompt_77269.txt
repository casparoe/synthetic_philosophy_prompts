I write a long-form column on the methodology of the sciences for a general-interest magazine, and I am drafting a piece whose argumentative spine is a claim I find persuasive but intend to have properly attacked before it goes to print. The claim, stated as sharply as I can manage: the replication crisis was at bottom a crisis of inference rather than of incentives or measurement. The apparatus of null-hypothesis significance testing — dichotomized decisions, a structural asymmetry between the null and the alternative, no way to accumulate evidence that an effect is absent, and a test statistic whose reference distribution assumes a sampling plan that researchers demonstrably did not follow — is what produced the literature of fragile and inflated findings. The field's partial migration toward Bayesian machinery is therefore not a stylistic preference but the substantive correction. On this view, preregistration, registered reports, data sharing, and larger samples are useful hygiene that could sit equally well inside either paradigm, while the change in how evidence is quantified is the load-bearing reform.

Argue against it from the inside, and tell me what would have to be true for it to be false. Specifically:

- Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each say what the world or the evidence base would have to look like. I want the ones that bear on the paradigm attribution itself, not a general survey of the crisis.
- Take up at least these candidates. Whether selection and publication bias operate on which data reach the analyst at all, so that a more coherent calculus applied to a filtered sample simply inherits the filter — and how far making the likelihood selection-aware genuinely gets you. Whether the celebrated tolerance of Bayes factors to the stopping rule survives contact with how data are actually chosen, analyzed, and reported. Whether explicit priors relocate researcher discretion rather than removing it, given sensitivity to the scale of a default prior on effect size. The error-statistical objection that a method can be internally coherent and still fail to detect its own mistakes, since a Bayes factor is not an error probability and no severity assessment attaches to it. Whether low measurement reliability and construct drift mean that failed replications carry almost no information about inference at all. Whether apparent failures are really genuine but context-bound effects, which would put heterogeneity and moderation, not the paradigm, at the center. And whether the timing cuts against me: the pathologies of significance testing were described in detail decades before the crisis broke, and some fields using the identical statistical toolkit turned out to be far less fragile.
- For each, say which a committed advocate of the Bayesian-remedy view would concede as counting against them and which they would dismiss, and on what grounds — including the family of retreats of the form that this is Bayesian analysis done badly.
- Tell me what my position predicts that the incentive-only and measurement-only accounts do not, and which of those predictions could in principle be checked, ideally by comparing disciplines rather than by re-reading psychology's own record.
- Give me a straight verdict on whether the claim has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that is a genuine defect for a methodological thesis or a legitimate idealization, given that no discipline implements any paradigm cleanly.
- Close by saying where you would land: defensible as stated, defensible only in a weakened form (state the form), or not defensible.

Two notes on my own susceptibilities. I am least moved by thought experiments internal to one philosophy of statistics, since either side can win those by fiat. I am most moved by cross-field comparisons and by findings whose interpretation does not depend on which paradigm the reader already accepts; flag which of your defeaters are of that kind. And I would rather be handed a claim I can print with an honest hedge than a claim I cannot print at all.
