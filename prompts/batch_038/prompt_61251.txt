A friend of mine is obsessed with sphexishness — the Hofstadter word that comes from the wasp that leaves its paralyzed cricket at the mouth of the burrow, goes in alone to check, comes back out and drags it in, and starts the whole routine over from the beginning if you nudge the cricket a few inches while it was inside. She applies the label to all sorts of things and I'm trying to work out what her actual test is. Her verdicts so far:

- The wasp: sphexish, obviously.
- A thermostat: sphexish. This suprised me, since it copes with any weather you throw at it — but she says it'll happily heat an empty house all week.
- A chess engine that plays on twenty moves past the point where its position is dead lost rather than resigning: sphexish.
- A firefighter's drilled procedure, run without a second of deliberation in a real fire, which he breaks off halfway through when the floor starts sounding wrong under him: not sphexish.
- A grandmaster who's played the same opening for 25 years, gets caught in a prepared novelty, loses, and spends the next month rebuilding the line: not sphexish.
- A concert pianist who plays a memorized movement flawlessly while her head is full of an argument she had at lunch, correcting every slip as it happens, and who'd stop dead if the piano started smoking: sphexish. Her line was that the music isn't coming from her.

The case I actually want her answer on is this. A guy I know checks every lock and window in the house twice before bed. He's wide awake doing it — he notices which windows his wife already latched and skips those, changes his route when a kid is asleep in the hallway, and would drop everything instantly if the smoke alarm went off. He can explain at length why the ritual protects nothing, has resolved to stop a dozen times, and says tonight, like every night, he simply can't: he gets into bed and something gets him back up. One exception — when his sister stays over he skips the whole thing without the slightest struggle.

Is he sphexish on her account? Give me your prediction, but I mostly want the machinery: what rule is she actually running, which other rules fit all six of her verdicts just as well, and what does each of those say about the lock-checker? Where they disagree, tell me which one you'd bet on and why, and describe the further case that would settle it between them. Also: should the sister detail carry any weight at all, or is it a red herring?
