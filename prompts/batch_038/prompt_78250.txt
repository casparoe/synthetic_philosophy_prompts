I'm a dental hygienist outside Phoenix and I have zero philosophy background, so please bear with me. This started because my sister is a vet tech and she said something about how a dog can't tell the difference between a slightly worse injury and a slightly less bad one, which got me thinking about pain comparisons in general, and then about that old example I half remember from a podcast where someone asks whether it's better to give one person an hour of really extreme agony or to give a billion different people a mild headache that lasts a month. I've been filling a notebook with this stuff on my commute for like six months and I've built an argument that I think is airtight and also completely insane, and I can't figure out which. Here it is.

(1) If two bad experiences differ so little that no one who had both of them could tell you which was worse, then neither one is worse than the other.
(2) Start with an hour of the worst agony you can picture. Now imagine a long series of cases stepping down from there to a single one-second pinprick, where each case differs from the one before it by so little that nobody who lived through both could pick out the difference.
(3) So going down the series, no step is worse than the next step.
(4) If no step is worse than the next one, then no earlier step is worse than any later step — because if some earlier step were worse than some later step, there would have to be a first place going down where the worse-than relation kicks in, and (3) rules that out.
(5) So an hour of the worst agony is not worse than a one-second pinprick.
(6) The exact same reasoning run upward gives me that a one-second pinprick is not worse than an hour of the worst agony.
(7) When neither of two things is worse than the other and neither is better than the other, the two are equally bad.
(8) So an hour of the worst agony is exactly as bad as a one-second pinprick.
(9) That's obviously false, so something above is false, and (1) is the one I'd blame. But if (1) is false then there are pairs of experiences where one really is worse even though nobody who had both could tell, and I can't see how that could be a fact about the experiences at all, or how anybody could ever know it.
