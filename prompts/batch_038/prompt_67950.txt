I'm the welfare rep for my department's student society and I need to settle an argument before Sunday's committee meeting.

One of our committee members — I'll call him J — got reported to us by three different first-years for following them home after socials and hanging around outside their buildings. Nothing was ever filed with the university, it all came straight to us. We asked him to step down. I drafted the notice:

"J has been asked to step down from the committee after three first-year members told us he followed them home and waited outside their buildings. If something like this has happened to you, come and talk to me."

Our media officer rewrote it as:

"Following a review of conduct concerns, changes have been made to the composition of the committee. The society remains fully committed to the wellbeing and safety of all its members."

Her argument is that mine is reckless: J never went through any formal process, there are only three first-years who could possibly fit that description so my version identifies them anyway, and we could get in serious trouble. I think she's probably right about the legal bit. But reading her version makes me feel bad in a way I can't quite explain — not just that it says less. It's like the sentence turns us into a certain kind of organisation. And it doesnt actually warn anybody: J will just join another society, and the new first-years arriving in September won't have a clue.

My flatmate thinks I'm making the whole thing up. Her view is that these notices are all boilerplate, nobody reads them, and the words dont do anything either way, so just publish whatever the media officer wrote and stop losing sleep over it.

So: is her rewrite genuinely worse, or am I inventing a problem? And is there a third version we could put out that doesn't identify anyone but also doesn't dissolve into fog? My seminar this term is on language and politics and I keep feeling like its relevant to this, I just can't connect the dots.

Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no introduction and no conclusion. First two should tell me what's actually going on between the two drafts, last three should be what I argue for on Sunday.
