I am a first-year doctoral student in philosophy of science, and this semester I joined an interdisciplinary reading group on judgment and forecasting that mixes philosophers, statisticians, and two people who build prediction systems for a living. The group's convenor is excellent on most things, but he has a verbal tic I have come to find exasperating: whenever a proposal departs from classical Bayesian orthodoxy, he leans back and says, "If you can be Dutch-booked, your beliefs are irrational — that's all there is to it." He clearly takes this to be a conversation-ender rather than an opening move, and because he is the senior person in the room, nobody pushes back. I want to push back properly, and to do that I think I need to know how many different claims are packed into that one sentence.

Two recent episodes are what finally bothered me. In the first, a statistician suggested that for thinly evidenced questions — she was thinking of something like whether a given treaty would be ratified within a decade — it may be better to work with a set of probability functions, or with belief functions that are not additive, rather than forcing a single sharp number; the convenor replied that such an agent is by definition exploitable and therefore irrational, and the discussion stopped. In the second, I asked whether an agent whose numbers violate additivity simply because it cannot compute that two hypotheses it entertains are mutually exclusive — a tired human expert, or a fitted model, or anything with limited processing capacity — is irrational in the same sense as a gambler who gets cleaned out by a bookie. His answer was that it is the same thing: coherence is coherence. I suspect he is running together several distinct theses, some of which are little more than arithmetic and some of which are controversial claims about what rationality even is, but I cannot yet say which is which, and I certainly cannot say which of them his two dismissals actually require.

Could you lay out the precise claims that sentence could be making, as a numbered menu running from the trivial to the radical rather than a single recommended interpretation? For each entry, give a verdict tag — mathematical theorem, platitude, substantive but defensible, genuinely contentious, or false — and state what has to be assumed for it to hold: what it takes for credences to amount to betting quotients one would accept, whether utility must be additive or stakes small, whether the agent must be willing to take every package offered, whether the bookie must know the agent's mind, whether the propositions need determinate truth values, and so on. I would also like you to say explicitly, for each of the two episodes above, which reading the convenor would have to be relying on, whether that reading is actually available to him, and what the honest reply is if it is not. Two further things matter to me. First, please distinguish the synchronic claim about one's credences at a time from the diachronic claim about how they ought to change, and tell me whether the slogan silently imports the second, including whatever extra premises a dynamic book argument needs. Second, please address the objection that guaranteed monetary loss is a practical rather than an epistemic defect, and whether recasting the argument in terms of accuracy instead of money changes any of the verdicts — noting, if it does, which verdicts it rescues and which it makes worse. Throughout, I need you to separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on these arguments: where the literature has a standard result, say it is a result; where competent people disagree about whether the argument establishes anything normative at all, or about whether non-additive and imprecise frameworks really escape exploitation, say that they disagree and give me the shape of the disagreement rather than presenting your own preferred side as the consensus. Where a verdict depends on a technical claim whose status is disputed, flag that dependence explicitly so I know which of my replies to the convenor would be resting on sand.
