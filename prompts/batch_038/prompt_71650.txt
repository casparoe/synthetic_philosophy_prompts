**Draft: The Granularity Ratchet**

Ordinary Bayesian conditioning requires the agent to become certain of some proposition. Much perception doesn't work that way: looking at a shirt under bad light, I don't come to believe *it looks teal*, I merely shift toward it. Jeffrey's generalization takes as input a new probability distribution over a partition E_1, …, E_n of the agent's space of possibilities, and holds the agent's conditional credences fixed, so that P_new(H) = Σ_i P_new(E_i) · P_old(H | E_i). The constraint that those conditionals stay put — the rigidity condition — is what makes the update determinate rather than underconstrained, and when the new distribution puts all its mass on a single cell the rule collapses back into ordinary conditioning.

Two formal features matter for what follows. First, iterated certain conditioning commutes: conditioning on A and then on B lands you in the same place as conditioning on B and then on A, provided everything stays well-defined, because you have simply conditioned on the conjunction. Second, iterated Jeffrey updates do not commute in general: shifting on one partition and then on another typically yields a different credence function than shifting on the second and then the first. Meanwhile, redundant updates are harmless — feeding the rule the distribution you already hold is an exact no-op, and so is updating on a coarsening using the probabilities your current function already assigns to those coarse cells.

The objection I want to press is that this asymmetry is fatal. Experience doesn't arrive pre-sliced into one partition; it arrives as a continuous drift. Every few minutes I take a coarse snapshot — *greener than teal-ish, less so than outright green* — and update on it. Each snapshot leaves my conditional credences untouched by stipulation, so nothing corrects anything else along the way; the drift just accumulates. And because the updates fail to commute, where I end up is a function of the mesh size and the temporal order of the snapshots I happened to take, not of the endpoints.

So consider two agents with the same total experience of the shirt. One glances at it every few minutes and takes a hundred coarse snapshots; the other looks away, then looks back once at the end and takes a single update. Identical inputs, different terminal credences. That, I claim, is a reductio. A rule that permits this is not a norm of rational belief at all; it is a description of one psychological pathway through an experience, and the pathway is doing the normative work.

The culprit is rigidity. Rigidity is not a claim about probability; it is an assumption about how experience bears on hypothesis, and it is false precisely when the partition terms are vague. If the cells are *teal-ish*, then P_old(H | teal-ish) is not a stable quantity that could be held fixed across time, and the finer I slice experience to buy precision, the more the conditionals themselves move. Either way the rule has nothing determinate to preserve.
