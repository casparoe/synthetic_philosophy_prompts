I'm one of nine people on the rules committee of a volunteer fire department — not a firefighter, I do the books at a hardware store, I only got on because nobody else would. Here's the situation I need help with.

Our bylaws have a line that as far as anyone can tell hasn't changed since the 1920s: no apparatus leaves the township without the chief saying out loud, in person, that it can go. Not a radio call, not a text, not the county dispatcher's mutual-aid assignment — the chief, standing there, saying yes. The minutes from the decade its probably from are gone (basement flood in the '70s, plus big gaps before that), and nobody alive has heard an explanation.

Half the committee wants it struck, and their argument isn't dumb. Last spring an engine sat in the bay for eleven minutes waiting on a chief who was at a dentist appointment, while a structure fire a mile past our line got an immediate mutual-aid assignment from dispatch. That's in the incident log, in writing, with times. The other half — mostly the older members — say the rule has outlived five chiefs and every kind of pressure to drop it, and that we don't get to call it junk just because we can't find the reason. They can't say what the reason is either. Nobody can.

But here's what's actually bugging me, and its the part I want the memo to dig into, because I don't have the words for it and I've been turning it over for months. We have a standing practice that before we repeal any old rule, somebody has to go looking for why it was made in the first place. We call it a fence audit, after that story about not tearing down a fence until you know why somebody built it. Sounds careful. Except I've sat through four years of these and I've noticed the audit almost never turns up anything — because the rules whose reasons did get written down are usually the ones that got modernized already, and the ones nobody remembers are unremembered precisely because nobody wrote anything down. So we search, we come up empty, we note "no documented purpose" in the minutes, and we vote to strike. The thing that was supposed to make us careful is really just a machine for producing a formal record of our own ignorance and then counting that record against the rule.

And the evidence is lopsided in a way that has nothing to do with whether the rules are any good. When we delete something we get to watch what happens next, so we generate evidence. When we keep something, nothing visible happens — the fire that didn't happen never shows up in a log, the fight between two companies that nobody had never gets written down. So our procedure manufactures reasons to repeal and never manufactures reasons to keep, and after enough years of that everybody starts to genuinely believe the old stuff was never doing anything, which is exactly what the record looks like whether or not its true.

So please draft the memo I can hand round before next month's meeting. I need a recommendation on this specific rule — strike it, keep it, or some third thing — with what actually happens and by when. And the reasoning has to be something the eleven-minutes-is-unacceptable people can answer back to, not a sermon about respecting tradition. Take their side at full strength; that delay is real and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. I'd also like the memo to propose how the committee should handle undocumented rules going forward, because this exact fight comes back as a different rule every couple of years. And if you think my diagnosis is wrong — if the audit practice is fine and I'm just pattern-matching on four years of sitting in a folding chair — say that instead, and tell me what better explanation there is for what I've been watching.
