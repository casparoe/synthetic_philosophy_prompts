Set theory seminar, and someone ran this argument that starts out as a case against CH having a truth value and ends up proving way too much. I cant find the leak.

(1) If two candidate set-theoretic claims are each consistent with everything we're entitled to believe, and neither is reachable by any method of justification we actually accept — proof from ZFC, intuition about the iterative conception, fruitfulness, whatever — then there is no fact of the matter which of them is correct.
(2) CH and its negation are exactly like that: any countable model of ZFC has an inner model (L) in which CH holds and a forcing extension in which it fails, and the ordinals and the arithmetic come along for the ride untouched.
(3) So there's no fact of the matter about CH.
(4) Same again for anything forcing can flip in both directions — the Suslin hypothesis, ♦, the size of 2^ℵ₁, the whole Easton menu.
(5) And nothing in (2)–(4) used anything distinctive about forcing. What did the work was just that both sides are unreachable. Unreachability of any kind should carry the same conclusion.
(6) If ZFC is consistent then Con(ZFC) isn't provable in ZFC, and if ZFC is inconsistent then Con(ZFC) is refutable. So Con(ZFC) is independent of ZFC either way, hence unreachable by proof.
(7) So there's no fact of the matter whether ZFC is consistent.
(8) But of course there is: either some finite string of formulas is a ZFC-proof of 0=1 or none is, and it had better be none, becuase people have been looking for the better part of a century.
(9) Hence (1) is false.

I don't want (9). I'd like to keep (1), since without something like it I don't see how to defend agnosticism about CH at all, and (8) isn't up for grabs. (6) is basically a theorem and (5) looks like a harmless generalization of what (2)–(4) actually did. So which step goes, why did it look innocent on the way in, and what does giving it up cost — in particular, is there any principled line between the independence in (2) and the independence in (6), or is (5) right and (8) has to go after all?
