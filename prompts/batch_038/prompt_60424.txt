I spent forty years earning my living from photons that had already been filtered many times before they reached me. A survey limited by a detection threshold does not sample the sky; it samples the subset of the sky bright enough to register, and it samples that subset unevenly, over-representing the intrinsically luminous at every distance — the bias we used to name after Malmquist and spend our careers correcting for. What I took from that work is not a mistrust of data but a discipline: you do not despair of a filtered sample, you characterise the filter. You write down a selection function, you find a calibration sample where you can, and you are scrupulous about distinguishing the case where the data are systematically distorted from the case where they simply contain no information about the quantity in question. The first is corrigible in principle; the second is not, and the honest response to it is to say so rather than to speculate.

Since retiring I have been reading in the philosophy of history, and I keep meeting the maxim that history is written by the victors, deployed to support a conclusion I find alarming: that because every surviving account was produced from within an interested perspective, and because the perspectives that survived belong to the powerful, there is no neutral standpoint from which to say that one account of the past is better supported than another — only accounts differently situated. My instinct is that this is a selection-effect argument wearing philosophical clothes, and that I already own the right tools for it. I also know the occupational hazard in that instinct, so I would like the analogy audited rather than flattered.

Here is how I have been using it. The past is the population; the archive is a magnitude-limited sample of it; the filter is what made an event likely to be recorded, likely to survive, and likely to be attended to; silence in the record is missing data, and the question is whether it is missing at random or missing informatively; correction requires a selection function and, ideally, some analogue of a complete nearby sample from which the intrinsic distribution can be inferred. There is a further wrinkle from a story I tell undergraduates about armouring aircraft: the damage pattern on the machines that came back is systematically unlike the damage pattern on all the machines that were hit, and the informative part of the data is precisely where the survivors are undamaged. Applied naively, that would suggest the historian should look hardest at the periods and places from which nothing survives at all.

Against this, three things I have noticed in the historical literature seem to complicate the picture, and I want them taken seriously rather than folded in. First, the filter is evidently not one filter but three, with different mechanisms and, I suspect, different correctability: what was noticed and recorded at the time; what physically survived afterwards; and what later generations chose to read, edit, and canonise. The maxim is invoked as though it governed all three, but it seems to describe only the third, which is the one that least affects the content of the documents themselves. Second, the composition of the archive does not look much like a triumphal narrative; it looks overwhelmingly like bureaucracy — tax rolls, court records, correspondence about supplies — material produced for purposes that had nothing to do with glory and which therefore leaked facts their authors never intended to convey. Third, the celebrated cases cut both ways: the most admired account of the Peloponnesian War was written by an Athenian general who had been exiled for losing a city, and whose city lost the war.

What I want from you is an audit, not a summary of the debate about relativism. Specifically:

1. A correspondence table. For each element of my source domain — detection limit, selection function, intrinsic luminosity distribution, calibration sample, missing-at-random versus informatively missing, deconvolution given a known instrumental response — state what plays that role in historiography, and mark whether the correspondence is structural or merely verbal.

2. The disanalogies, sorted. Some differences between the two cases will be awkward but survivable; others will defeat the inference the analogy is meant to license. Give me a criterion for telling these apart, and apply it. I suspect the crucial disanalogy will turn on whether the filter is random or intentional, so tell me whether a designed instrument is easier or harder to characterise than an accidental one, given that modelling a censor requires evidence about the censor's incentives and that this evidence is itself filtered.

3. Whether a calibration sample exists even in principle, and what could serve as one: archaeology against text, independent traditions, comparative cases, the material record. Say whether any of these escapes the filter or merely duplicates it.

4. Whether the archive's silences are a bias problem or a power problem, and what follows for each. I want to know whether the pessimistic conclusion is even the right shape: that we cannot know, or that there are particular questions about which the surviving sample contains no information, and about which we can know that it contains none.

5. A verdict on both inferences — the relativist's and mine. Say plainly whether my optimism is carried by the analogy or by something independent of it, and if you think I am reasoning backwards from a preference I would rather keep, say so and show me the step where the preference enters.

6. If the analogy fails, propose a better one. My own candidate is taphonomy: in the fossil record the earliest and latest known appearances of a lineage fall systematically inside the true range of its existence, sampling is patchy in time and space, and yet disciplined inference about the timing of extinctions is still possible. Tell me whether that is a better source domain than survey astronomy, and what it costs.

I am not asking for references or a reading list — I have plenty of those and they are not what is missing. I am asking for the reasoning, with the verdicts argued rather than announced.
