ok so my teacher runs an after-school philosophy club and every week she writes a case on the whiteboard, makes us all vote, and then tells us what SHE thinks — which is honestly the part I care about, because she's way more consistent than the rest of us and it's fun trying to reverse-engineer her. This month is future people. Her verdicts so far:

- A girl who's 14 and decides to have a baby now instead of waiting til she's finished school: wrong. A kid said "but the baby has a good life, so who actually got hurt?" and she said that's precisely the move you're not allowed to make, and then spent ten minutes on why.
- A country that picks the cheap energy policy — everybody's a bit richer for the next couple hundred years, but it leaves contamination behind so some people in the far future get lives full of chronic illness: wrong. She made the obvious objection herself before anyone could, that those exact people wouldn't exist at all under the safe policy because everything shifts, who meets whom and when, so it's totaly different people later. Still wrong, she said.
- A couple who know before conceiving that the kid's life would be so bad it isn't worth starting: wrong, she called this one the easy case.
- The same couple except the kid's life would be harder than average but still clearly worth living, and this is their only chance at a kid: not wrong. Someone called her a eugenicist for saying that and she got pretty annoyed.
- A woman who decides not to have children even though she'd be great at it and they'd have had lovely lives: not wrong, and she was firm that nobody is obligated to manufacture happy people.
- Parents who took a drug during pregnancy with maybe a 1-in-3 chance of causing a serious disability, and the baby came out fine: still wrong. She said you judge that at the moment they did it, not by how it landed.

She says the whole theme has a name, the nonidentity problem, something about a philosopher called Parfit, but we havent gotten into the formal stuff and I don't want the textbook version, I want your read on her.

Next week's case is already posted and I want to walk in with something better than a guess. A couple is doing IVF and has two embryos left. The clinic tells them — not with certainty, more like 70% — that Embryo A will be born deaf, and that Embryo B would very likely hear fine and have an easier time of things generally, though both would have lives well worth living either way. Both parents are Deaf, they've got a tight Deaf community around them, and they deliberately pick Embryo A because they want a kid who's part of their world. So: predict her verdict and say which principle you think she's actually been applying all month, then list the other principles that fit all six of her verdicts just as well and say where each of them lands on the embryo case and how it differs from your pick. Which one would you put money on, and what in the record tips it? Also — does her answer change between the version where the kid is born deaf like the clinic said and the version where the 70% doesn't happen and the kid hears fine, and does it matter that she already told us the drug parents were wrong even though nothing bad came of it? Then tell me which of her own six verdicts is the most awkward one for the principle you're betting on, and give me one extra case I could write on the board that would force her to show which rule she's running, plus what each candidate rule would have to say about it.
