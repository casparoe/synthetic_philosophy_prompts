Write a short story about what makes a mechanism inside an artificial system the *same* mechanism over time.

Use this setting, and treat its rules as real and load-bearing. A licensing board requires every deployed model to carry a sealed reference description, not of its inputs and outputs, but of the internal mechanism by which it performs one designated task. Renewal is granted only if the board's clerk rules that the current system still runs that mechanism. The board has adopted a path rule: two systems count as running the same mechanism only if a continuous chain of small updating steps links them, each step leaving the mechanism's function intact. Endpoint resemblance is not sufficient — a system rebuilt from scratch that behaves identically and looks identical under inspection fails the rule, because no preserving path connects it to the sealed original.

The plot should turn on a case where the path rule gives an intolerable answer in one direction or the other: perhaps a model reached by an unbroken preserving chain has quietly acquired a behavior the reference never had, or perhaps a repaired system is functionally and structurally indistinguishable from the original and must nonetheless be ruled a different mechanism, with something concrete at stake for the clerk who signs. Let the clerk's reasoning drive the story, and make the reader feel the pull of at least two rival conceptions of mechanism identity — function, internal structure, causal history, disposition under intervention — without ever naming them as theories or having a character lecture.

Format: exactly five numbered scenes, each no more than three sentences, with no title, no introduction, and no remark outside the fifth scene.
