Nobody in the room is a Davidson specialist, which is part of my problem.

My view, roughly — this is the thing the objections should actually target, not generic charity complaints:

I think the standard reading of the Conceptual Scheme argument gets the shape of the result wrong. People read it as a global reductio: two schemes that are untranslatable would have to be mostly-false-about-the-world schemes, that's unintelligible, so there's only the one scheme and the dualism of scheme and content dies. I argue the argument is local. What Davidson actually establishes is that *radical* divergence is unintelligible at the point of entry — you cannot begin to read someone as a speaker except by maximizing agreement over a minimal observational core. Charity is an entry condition, not a standing constraint on the whole corpus. After entry, agreement can drift arbitrarily far and there's no principled bound on the drift that interpretation itself supplies. Three consequences I want to defend: (i) Davidson's optimism — belief is constitutively mostly true — only holds at the moment of entry, so it does not license any anti-skeptical conclusion about a speaker's beliefs taken as a whole; (ii) the scheme/content dualism survives in a weak form, since schemes exist but are not separable from content by any interpretive procedure, which is a different claim than the one usually attributed to him; (iii) triangulation does no work my entry-condition doesn't already do, so the later social stuff is redundant machinery. And I claim the prior theory/passing theory distinction in the Derangement paper is a concession *to* me — it's Davidson giving up global charity without noticing.
