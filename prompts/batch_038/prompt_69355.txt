I help run a reading group where the tracking account of knowledge gets glossed the same way every year, and this term the handout did it again: a belief that tracks the truth is like a well-functioning smoke alarm, whose entire job is to sound when and only when there is a fire, so the counterfactual that the alarm would have stayed silent had there been no fire is just what "good alarm" means, with the converse counterfactual — that it would have sounded had there been a fire — supplying the other half of the same disposition. The handout then turns the picture against Nozick. Its argument runs: a detector's report ought to carry whatever the detected fact entails, since an alarm that sounds for a kitchen fire is thereby telling you the house is not fire-free; so an account on which you can know that the animal in the pen is a dog while failing to know that it is not a cleverly disguised mule is not describing a detector at all, and the failure of closure is a defect rather than a consequence.

Please audit the analogy. Specifically:

1. Give an explicit correspondence table: what plays the fire, the sounding, the wiring or mechanism, the installation and its environment, the alarm's detection range, and — the entry I most want — what corresponds to the nearest worlds in which the fact fails to obtain.
2. Separate the disanalogies that actually bear on the handout's argument from the merely decorative ones, and say for each of the load-bearing ones what it costs the argument.
3. Check whether the smoke-alarm picture really reproduces the mule verdict, or whether it has been quietly pointed at a case in which closure does hold. If the latter, say what the alarm scenario would have to look like to be a true analogue — a prankster with a smoke machine, an alarm whose trigger condition is finer or coarser than the content it reports — and whether that repaired version still supports the handout's conclusion.
4. Address the place the analogy is always silent: how the mechanism gets specified. Whether the relevant apparatus in the counterfactual situation is the alarm's wiring, or the grandmother's eyesight as against what her relatives tell her, is what fixes the verdict, and there is no inspection panel on a belief-forming method. Say whether this is a problem the analogy merely hides or one it genuinely helps diagnose.
5. Say whether knowledge of necessary truths has any analogue in the picture at all, and if you think there is a better source domain for tracking than an alarm, name it and say what work it does that the alarm cannot.

End with a final line beginning "Verdict:" stating in one sentence whether the handout's conclusion — that closure failure shows the tracking account is broken rather than correct — survives the audit.
