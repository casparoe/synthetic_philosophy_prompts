I am convening an adversarial collaboration in my graduate seminar over whether moral status comes in degrees, and I want the joint statement the two camps ought to be able to sign. The gradualists hold that phenomenal consciousness itself admits of degree, that moral standing tracks it, and that there is accordingly a real ordering running from mammals through cephalopods and insects down to systems with no standing whatsoever. The threshold theorists hold that standing is binary, attaching to a discrete capacity — being the subject of a life that can care about its own continued existence — so that whatever has it possesses something unconditional in value rather than a mere price, and whatever lacks it has no claim of that kind however flexible its behaviour.

Draft the document. First, a numbered section of claims both camps can sign: about the comparative distribution of nociception, learning, and behavioural flexibility across taxa; about what moral status is for in practical reasoning; and about our evidential situation with respect to other minds. Second, a table of the residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own camp would accept the wording as fair, with the nearest formulation the opposing camp could accept noted alongside. Third, the cruxes — sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side — and for each, a case or body of evidence both camps accept as bearing on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance of the result.

Three cruxes are mandatory: whether partial standing can function as a constraint at all, or only as a weight in an aggregation; whether gradualist weights permit trading one threshold-level being against sufficiently many marginally sentient ones, and whether that verdict is a reductio of gradualism or a cost it can absorb; and whether any threshold can be drawn that survives the sorites-style pressure of induction into and emergence from general anaesthesia. Close with the section I most want — which strands of this dispute both camps should now concede were merely verbal, and the disambiguation that dissolves them.
