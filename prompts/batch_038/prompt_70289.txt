So I run a small meetup that's basically ex-rationalist-blog people — everyone knows what a Bayes factor is, nobody has touched analytic metaphysics since undergrad — and Goodman's grue keeps coming up. The riddle itself lands instantly: every emerald anyone has examined before t was both green and grue (grue meaning green if examined before t, blue otherwise), so the same evidence that licenses "all emeralds are green" equally licenses "all emeralds are grue," and nothing about the evidence picks between them. What I've noticed is that my crowd always treats Goodman's actual answer — the entrenchment stuff, where a predicate earns its projectibility from its track record of past projections — as a shrug-and-change-the-subject dodge. And maybe it is a dodge, but I think there's a much better joke available if you take entrenchment completely literally, as a mechanism rather than as a consolation prize. If projectibility is a function of how often a predicate has been used and how well its past projections went, then projectibility is a resource, and resources can be purchased by anyone with a big enough budget.

Can you write me a pitch deck from a boutique "semantic branding" agency that sells entrenchment-as-a-service? About 700-800 words, formatted as slide titles with two to four bullets each, plus a short cover note at the top in the voice of the account exec. Client can be invented — I like something with a real financial stake in what happens at t, e.g. a consortium of lab-grown gem producers and an underwriter selling color-change policies, but use whatever makes the deck funnier. Deadpan throughout, no winking, and the agency should be sincere and genuinely competent: the joke has to come from the fact that their plan is a legitimate implementation of the criterion, not from them being bad at their jobs. A specialist should be able to tell the author knows exactly what entrenchment is and is deliberately taking it at its word; someone who's never heard of grue should still be able to read it as a normal corporate deck.

Beats I'd like hit, roughly in this order but feel free to rearrange:

- a brand architecture slide treating grue and bleen as a two-product family, with the time-index handled correctly and the agency flatly refusing to treat it as a defect — it's a feature, it's what makes the product differentiated
- budget line items where each one is tied to a mechanism that would actually move the metric: corpus seeding, n-gram placement, getting grue into preschool picture books early because early entrenchment compounds, a paid "projection farm" of contractors uttering grue-generalizations at scale, maybe licensing a community of first-language speakers on an island
- a KPI slide where the metric is literally the count of past projections, and where the deck is forced to concede that even total success only helps inductions run after the campaign closes — so the grue-speakers who've been laughed at for decades get zero retroactive vindication — followed by the spin on why that's still worth the fee
- a bootstrapping slide that stays cheerfully oblivious to the fact that you entrench a predicate by projecting it and can only project it if it's already entrenched; don't name the circularity, just give the slide chicken-and-egg-is-a-moat energy
- a competitive-response slide about the mirror campaign: if green-and-blue can be re-entrenched on demand, and if from inside a grue-first language it's "green" that looks like the gerrymandered disjunctive one, then a price war literally decides which generalizations count as lawlike. Have the agency treat that as unremarkable market dynamics
- an incumbent-PR rebuttal slide where the competition keeps smearing grue as positional and heterogeneous and appealing to natural kinds; the agency handles this the way agencies handle smears, never once conceding it's a philosophical argument

- a portfolio slide with one flagship success (an engineering discipline whole-cloth adopting a predicate about copper conducting electricity) and one spectacular failure (a client who wanted a projectible predicate for the class of people who happen to be in a given room, all third sons, burned the entire budget and got no lawlikeness for it)
- a closing risks slide covering what happens if t actually arrives and the emeralds come out blue: deliverable, client-satisfaction problem, or upside event for the underwriter — plus a note on scope creep from the client asking about bleen as well

Two housekeeping things: don't cite anything, no reading list, and keep real philosophers out of the deck entirely — it should read like it comes from a world where nobody ever framed this as a paradox, only as an addressable market.
