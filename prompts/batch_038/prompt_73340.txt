**No Common Thread: Attentional Endorsement and the Disjunctivist Turn in Hedonism**

Start with the heterogeneity problem. A warm bath, the first cold beer on a hot day, a proof finally clicking, schadenfreude, the first two miles of a run, being teased by someone you love, a good stretch after sitting too long. Introspect and you do not find one hedonic tone running through these like a thread; you find a bunch of wildly different feels, and the word pleasure looks like it's doing a family-resemblance job rather than naming a property. Felt-quality theorists respond that the tone is faint and attention-transparent, or that there are several hedonic tones rather than one — but pluralism about tone already concedes most of what the problem alleged. Attitudinal theorists go the other way and take pleasure out of feeling altogether: on Feldman's view it's a propositional attitude, being pleased that p. Desire-based accounts like Heathwood's say what makes an experience pleasant is that you intrinsically desire that it continue. Both of those purchase unity at a real price: they make pleasure much less obviously a feature of experience, and they struggle with creatures who have pleasure but few concepts.

My move is to stop asking what pleasant experiences share as an ingredient, and ask instead what they share in how they relate to the subject's own attention. Proposal: an experience is pleasant to the degree that attending to it tends to sustain and intensify the attending. Self-ratifying attention. Call this attentional endorsement. Note that the state is second-order — its object is my attention to the experience, not the experience itself, which is why nothing needs to be common across the experiences that get endorsed. Unpleasantness is not the absence of endorsement but a stuck loop: attending that keeps trying to redirect and is prevented from doing so, which is I think what makes pain awful rather than merely noticed.

Some of what I want the view to explain: intensity without a quantity of felt quality (intensity is just loop gain); why we can be wrong about what we enjoy, since the loop is diachronic and something that self-ratifies for ten minutes can collapse over an hour (the rich dessert, the third glass, the person); why pleasures resist being lined up on a single scale. That last one cuts against me — if hedonism needs a common measure to aggregate welfare, a view that predicts incommensurability is not obviously a friend of hedonism, and I currently just admit this.

The framing I want to borrow is perceptual disjunctivism. The disjunctivist denies that veridical perception and the matching hallucination have a common component, and denies that the good case should be analysed in terms it shares with the bad case. I want to say something parallel: pleasant experiences are not a natural kind picked out by a shared ingredient, and the search for that ingredient is the mistake, not the failure to find it. The nice thing is that I get to keep the felt-quality theorist's insistence that pleasure is in experience without inheriting their introspection story, and keep the attitudinal theorist's structural insight without demoting feeling.

Worries I already have, in my own order of severity: (a) is this just Heathwood with attention swapped for desire, and if not, what would a genuine separating case look like; (b) guilty pleasures and masochism, where the loop runs but I disavow what I'm enjoying — is disavowal a third-order state that breaks the loop, or does the case simply break the view; (c) background pleasure, the music you're plainly enjoying while your attention is elsewhere, which looks like a counterexample at the level of the definition and not a quibble; (d) whether animals and infants get endorsed at all, since endorsement may want attentional capacities they lack; (e) whether paragraph four does any work.
