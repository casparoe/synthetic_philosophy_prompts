I'm on my city's naming and monuments commission — we handle petitions to rename streets, schools, parks and to take down statues. We've never actually written down a standard, we just vote, and next month's case looks like it's going to split us bad. I want to know what rule our record adds up to before I walk in there.

Our record so far:

- We kept the street named for the 1890s mayor who also ran the mill everyone knows was a hellhole. The minutes basically say the conditions were standard for the period, that he was honored for getting the waterworks built and not for the mill, and that no descendents of his workers had ever come to us about it.
- We renamed the school that honored the superintendent who ran the segregated system. He'd gone further than state law required — he closed the Black schools the statute would have let stand — and the people who got pushed out of those schools, plus their kids, brought the petition themselves. Two members floated a plaque as a compromise and lost 5-2, the majority saying a plaque records something while a name honors it.
- We kept the park named for the naturalist, even though his field journals describe Indigenous people in ways that are just awful. But the journals were never published in his lifetime, the plant catalog he's honored for has nothing to do with them, and the objections came from a national organization rather than from anybody here. We added an interpretive panel.
- We took down the statue of the hospital benefactor. Her money came from a patent tonic that her own company's letters show she'd been warned was killing people, and the hospital got built directly out of those profits. Families of the patients still live here and they're the ones who asked.
- We declined to rename the esplanade named for the shipping magnate who brought in strikebreakers whose men beat pickets. Legal at the time, and he was honored for deeding the waterfront to the city, not for the shipping business. Honestly tho the thing that carried the room was that the name is printed on federal navigation charts.
- Split 4-3, we kept the courthouse named for the judge who wrote the 1920s opinions upholding racially restrictive covenants and who also built this city's legal aid society. Majority: the covenants were lawful and broadly endorsed back then, legal aid is what he was commemorated for, and no organized group had asked. The three dissenters said that missed the point entirely, without really saying what the point was.

The new one: a middle school named for a doctor who in the fifties and sixties opened the first community mental-health clinic in the region and is still locally beloved as the guy who brought psychiatry out of the asylum. Under a state sterilization statute that was on the books and that most of his professional colleagues supported, he authorized procedures on dozens of patients without anything you could call consent. The survivors and their children are organized now, they're local, and they filed the petition. The clinic is where the procedures happened, and the per-procedure state payments were a real chunk of what kept the clinic running. The school building is being replaced anyway so rebranding costs us nothing. And one more fact I keep going back and forth on: locally almost no one knows about the sterilizations.

So — which of our precedents is the closest analog to this one, and what should we vote if we want to be able to look at our own record afterwards? My hunch is we've been running some mix of judge-them-by-their-own-era and did-anyone-actually-harmed-and-still-here-ask-us, but I don't think that's the whole thing and I'm pretty sure those two stop agreeing next month. Lay out the rules our record is compatible with, including ones I haven't thought of, and say which of them the school case actually splits and what each one predicts. If the record genuinely doesn't decide it, tell me what principle we'd be adopting with each possible vote, whether a panel is on the table this time or whether the superintendent vote already foreclosed that, and what a future case would have to look like to finally tell these rules apart.
