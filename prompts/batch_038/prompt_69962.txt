Longtime reader of the usual rationalist blogs/forums. Twice this month I've watched "this is the best of all possible worlds" get used to shut an argument down, in opposite directions. Once in a thread about global suffering, where the point was that if its really the best possible world then working to fix things is futile at best and arrogant at worst — like we'd be claiming to know better than whatever process arranged the status quo. Once in an AI-risk thread, where someone used the same line approvingly, as a reason to relax: every previous panic turned out fine, so the universe is presumably on our side again. I know it's Leibniz's slogan and that Voltaire took a swing at it, but neither thread was doing history of philosophy, they were just doing something with the sentence.

I couldnt say what's wrong with either use. Can you break it into the distinct precise claims it could be making — best for whom or by what standard, best over what scope (all of history vs some region of it vs right now), best out of which set of alternatives, and whether "best" even picks out anything if the alternatives are infinite with no top element — and sort them into undisputed platitudes, substantive-but-defensible, and false-or-empty? Then tell me which reading, if any, actually licenses the quietist use and which licenses the complacent one. A list I can paste back into the thread would be great.
