I'm in the second year of a philosophy PhD, and my area paper is on classification in chemistry and the life sciences. Over the last few months I have kept running into a configuration I cannot name, and I'd like to know whether it has an established one. Here is the clearest instance I have. Two research communities use the same substance term, each with a well-confirmed inductive profile for it, but the profiles are not coextensive and neither is derivable from the other. Take "metal." One community fixes it by chemical behaviour — electropositivity, cation formation, basic oxides — and the other by electronic structure, where the mark of a metal is a partially filled band, so that resistance rises with temperature. There are materials that satisfy one profile and not the other: graphite conducts along its planes through delocalised electrons, in exactly the way the second profile demands, and yet nobody in either community counts carbon among the metals. There is also a standing penumbra in which the two communities' own words for the borderline are used almost interchangeably in interdisciplinary writing without being the same class at all — arsenic, antimony and bismuth are semimetals in the band-theoretic sense, while silicon and germanium are metalloids to a chemist and semiconductors to a physicist. What strikes me is not that the criteria diverge, since plenty of useful terms are vague, but that nobody treats the divergence as a problem. Results secured under one profile get cited in work using the other with no translation and no caveat; both criteria are taught in the same graduate course as though they picked out a single thing; and when someone proposes splitting the term, the proposal is dismissed as pedantry — correctly, so far as I can tell, because the two profiles are useful to each other in a way they would not be if they were attached to different words.

A second, briefer instance, to show that this isn't a quirk of one field. "Hormone" was originally fixed by a delivery criterion — a chemical messenger secreted into the bloodstream by a ductless gland, acting on a distant target — and is now used by endocrinologists, neuroscientists and plant physiologists under at least three criteria: paracrine and autocrine signalling, neurosecretion, and plant regulators produced by nothing glandular whatsoever, one of which is a gas. Again the term is not experienced as ambiguous, and again findings migrate across the gap in both directions.

I want the general shape of the thing, and I want to know whether the literature already has it. Some near neighbours I'm fairly sure it isn't: ordinary homonymy or polysemy, because these communities are not merely tolerating a shared sound but doing joint epistemic work through it; Kuhnian incommensurability, because communication is not failing and no theory is displacing another; and a term awaiting reduction in the way "heat" awaited statistical mechanics, because nothing here looks like a pending identity and neither profile has any prospect of eliminating the other. I have also heard "boundary object" used in science studies for something adjacent, but my impression is that it describes the pragmatic function of an entity shared across communities while staying neutral on the question I actually care about, which is whether the shared term answers to one joint in nature or to two that practice has fused.

So: is there an established concept for a kind term whose extension is fixed by two partially overlapping but non-nested inductive profiles, held jointly by different communities, without anyone treating it as ambiguous? If there is, tell me which candidate fits best and exactly where my description strains it. If there are several near neighbours — I suspect at least a couple of familiar moves in the metaphysics of natural kinds come within striking distance — tell me what each is designed for and how the configuration I've described differs from each, because the differentiation is what I need, not the labels. If nothing fits, coin a term and define it properly: conditions for a kind term to fall under it, and, more important to me, the boundary cases that have to be kept out, i.e. what the adjacent configurations look like that are not this one (mere vagueness, genuine ambiguity, a reduction waiting to happen, two profiles where one nests inside the other, a disjunctive grouping nobody expects to support induction). And close by telling me what you think this implies for the joints question: does a term of this kind show that there is no single joint where it cuts, that there are two joints with one word doing double duty, or that the joints question is being posed at the wrong level? A one-word answer would be useless to me; what I'm after is the carving.
