I work on reward models for a living — preference data pipelines, scalar RMs, the whole RLHF stack, plus enough eval-side paranoia about reward hacking and overoptimization that I dream in Goodhart's law. I'm fluent in that vocabulary and basically illiterate in the history-of-philosophy one; I've skimmed Adam Smith but wouldn't trust myself to explain him to anyone. So here's what I want.

Take Smith's impartial-spectator machinery in The Theory of Moral Sentiments and restate it, as faithfully as you can manage, in the vocabulary I actually use: annotators, preference pairs, inter-annotator agreement, aggregation functions, reward model as a scalar-valued function over outputs, KL penalty against a reference policy, calibration, distribution shift, process vs outcome rewards, the critic being internalized by the policy. And I don't mean the pop version where "the impartial spectator = society's reward model, done." I mean the specific moving parts, each one translated on its own terms:

- Sympathy as Smith actually describes it — an act of imagination, a first-person simulation of what I'd feel in your situation, not contagion and not a poll of bystanders. He writes that by the imagination we place ourselves in his situation, we enter as it were into his body, and become in some measure the same person with him; that it's by changing places in fancy with the sufferer that we come to be affected by what he feels. What is that operation in my vocabulary? Is it a policy rollout? A counterfactual prompt? Something my stack has no word for at all?
- The asymmetry between the two directions of adjustment: the spectator tries to raise his feeling toward the agent's, the agent tries to bring his down to what the spectator can go along with, and propriety lives at the meeting point. Translate that. Tell me whether it survives as anything like a two-sided calibration problem or whether it collapses.
- Smith's distinction between the judgment of propriety (is this affection proportioned to its cause) and the judgment of merit/demerit (are the effects on others praiseworthy or blameworthy) — two separate evaluations that my framework tends to smash into one scalar. What does each become?
- The pleasure of concord as its own motivator, including the weird bit where we want our sentiments echoed even when we already hold them confidently, and where agreement feels better for being shared. Reward models don't enjoy anything. Does that kill the translation or just relocate it?
- General rules of morality, formed by induction from lots of observed cases and then used as a corrective to what Smith calls the misrepresentations of self-love — i.e. the trained prior overriding the in-the-moment evaluation. That one feels like it should map, so tell me exactly where the map is too clean to be honest.
- The man within the breast: an internalized critic that can itself be corrupted, plus Smith's insistence that the relevant standard is what a well-informed and unbiased spectator would feel, not what actual spectators do feel, since actual crowds get distorted by custom, fashion, and faction. That's a conditional, not a marginal. Say so if my framing is leading you.
