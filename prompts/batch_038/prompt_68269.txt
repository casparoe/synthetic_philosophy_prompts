I'm writing a novel and I've got an evolutionary biologist reading for accuracy — she's been ruthless about one word in partcular, which behaviors actually count as cooperation and which ones don't. So far: vampire bats sharing blood with unrelated roost-mates, yes. Cleaner fish picking parasites off bigger fish, yes, and she made a point of noting the client can swim off if the cleaner takes a bite of mucus instead. Ants herding aphids for honeydew, no — she called it farming. Mycorrhizal fungi trading phosphate for tree sugar, no. Birds and meerkats where the helpers are last season's offspring, no, that's just nepotism. Chimp hunts where the meat goes disproportionately to whoever groomed the hunter, yes. Hunter-gatherer camp meat sharing, yes.

Now the species I invented: when a forager comes back empty, other members of the band feed it, at real cost to themselves and mostly to non-relatives. A forager who got turned down last time can and does turn down that specific individual in return. Nobody else gets involved — no ganging up on freeloaders, no punishment beyond refusal. And the food doesn't have to come back from the person you fed; anyone in the band might feed you later. I've deliberately left it undecided whether they remember individuals or whether the refusal is just a vaguer gut reaction to a familiar scent.

So: will she call it cooperation? More usefully, what are all the rules that fit her seven earlier verdicts, what does each one say about my creatures, which would you bet on and why, and what further case would settle it — because I have to decide how smart these things are before chapter four.
