I cover democracy and elections for a science/ideas desk, and something about the way we draw the electorate has been nagging me. Nearly every democracy lands on the same weird set of lines: 18 is the magic number almost everywhere (a handful go to 16, and at least one Gulf state sits at 25); someone who has lived, worked and paid taxes in a country for twenty years on a permanent-resident visa usually has no say in national elections at all, though in a fair number of places they do get a local vote; convicted prisoners are locked out completely in some democracies and voting from inside their cells in others; and citizens who emigrated forty years ago and have sent nothing home but postcards often keep their ballot for life — Britain only dropped it's 15-year cap on expat voting a couple of years back, while Denmark cuts you off after about two.

I want to know what theory of political membership all this adds up to. Explain it the way you would to an editor who's smart but doesn't have the literature in their head, and argue rather than annotate — no reading lists or citations, I can't check them anyway.

Sections, in this order:

- **The practice** (~150 words): state cleanly what the pattern of lines actually is, including where they pull against each other.
- **What it presupposes** (~400 words): reconstruct the implicit theory of who belongs to the demos. Is the vote treated as a right that tracks competence, as something owed to those the state coerces, as a stake in the outcome, or as an incident of membership? For each line separately — age, residence/citizenship, imprisonment, emigration — say which criterion it's really tracking.
- **What it rules out** (~250 words): which rival accounts of the boundary does the practice quietly foreclose, and would adopting one of them force changes to things we currently treat as obvious?
- **The seams** (~250 words): are the three or four lines consistent with one criterion, or several different criteria stapled together? Point at the sharpest internal tension and say whether there's any principled way to keep all of them.
- **Underdetermined** (~200 words): where does the practice fail to choose between theories that would all license it just as well?
- **If we swapped the theory** (~250 words): take the most plausible rival criterion and say what election law would have to look like under it — who gets in, who gets kicked out, and what the obvious objection to the new system is.

One more thing I can't shake: whoever draws the boundary is already standing inside it, and gets picked by the people the previous boundary let in. Fold that in wherever it belongs.
