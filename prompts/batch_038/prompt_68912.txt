I am preparing the closing exercise for a graduate seminar on explanation in the historical sciences. We have spent two weeks on the question of whether "Why Britain and not China?" is a well-posed question at all, given that the contrast can be fixed at wildly different scales: Britain against the Yangtze delta around 1750; Britain against the Qing empire taken as a whole between 1400 and 1800; northwestern Europe against East Asia around the year 1000; and Eurasia against the Americas in the broader rise-of-the-West register. The claim I want the students to test is that each candidate explanation of the divergence does its real work at one of these contrasts and quietly loses most of that work at the others, so that ranking the candidates requires first fixing the explanandum.

Please score the seven candidates below on that claim. For each, give (i) a contrast-stability score from 0.0 to 1.0, where 1.0 means the explanation retains essentially its full force across all four contrasts and 0.0 means it says something only at one of them; (ii) the single contrast at which it is best posed; and (iii) two sentences of justification, one of which must state what the explanation is committed to about when the divergence began, since a late-dating account cannot carry the weight an early-dating one is asked to carry.

1. Coal: the depth, location, and water-borne accessibility of British seams relative to the delta's distant ones (Pomeranz).
2. New World colonies as an ecological release: the imports of fiber, timber, sugar, and grain that freed British land from having to produce them (Pomeranz).
3. Relative factor prices: British high wages and cheap energy rewarded labor-saving, energy-using machinery, while Chinese cheap labor and dear energy did not (Allen).
4. The high-level equilibrium trap: a market-efficient, labor-abundant economy with no profitable margin left for mechanization (Elvin).
5. Political fragmentation and interstate rivalry in Europe set against unified imperial governance in China, including the turn away from oceanic expansion.
6. Credible constraints on sovereign predation after 1688–89: secure property, funded public debt, and developed capital markets (North and Weingast).
7. The Industrial Enlightenment: useful knowledge and the coupling of natural philosophy to artisan practice (Mokyr).

State your weighting rule for converting these into an overall ranking before you give the scores, then produce the ranking and say in one sentence per candidate why it lands where it does. Finally, identify any candidate that is in your judgment not an answer to the divergence question at all but an answer to the Needham question about where modern science arose, and explain what is lost when the two explananda are run together. No bibliography or list of sources is needed; the attributions above are the ones I want you to work from.
