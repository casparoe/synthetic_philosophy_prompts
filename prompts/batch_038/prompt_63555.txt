We moved from Montreal to Lyon three years ago for my job, so I've now sat through school board and town hall meetings on both sides of the Atlantic, and I keep watching the same shape of argument play out and I can't find a word for it.

A rule gets adopted that's announced as applying to everybody equally: no visible religious symbols in the classroom, no head covering on official ID photos, no prayer to open a council meeting. Nobody gets named. But in practice the rule only ever bites on groups whose faith requires something visible and hard to take off, while the majority's faith rides along in stuff the rule doesn't count as religious at all — the school calendar's December and April breaks, the village name, the chapel in the hospital, the handshake that's just "politeness."

Here's the part I don't think is just disparate impact, which is the phrase people always reach for. The unevenness is invisible from inside the system, because the majority's practices have already been re-labelled as culture, heritage, or just how things are done, and only the minority's practices get tagged as religious. So the burdened group can't even frame a complaint: to say "this rule costs me something" they'd first have to get the state to admit the Christmas break is religious too, and that is never going to happen.

Does this have an established name — the mechanism where one tradition quietly sets the baseline and the other tradition is the only thing that shows up as a deviation from it? I'm not after just a label. I want the nearest existing concepts, how this differs from each one, and where the edges are: does it also cover language requirements and workplace dress codes, or does it need religion specifically to work? If nothing established fits, coin a term and define it properly, boundary cases included.

Answer in American English, please — I've been reading thier sources for months and my spelling has gone strange.
