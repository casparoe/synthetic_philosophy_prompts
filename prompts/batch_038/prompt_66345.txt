I sit on the board of county commissioners for a county of roughly 200,000 people in the American interior. I have no training in political science or economics, but I have spent several years reading on my own about the Nordic countries, and I take the question of whether their arrangements can be exported seriously rather than treating it as settled in either direction.

In November we vote on a public pre-kindergarten program to be funded by a half-cent sales tax increase. County staff have prepared two versions at identical total cost. Version A is universal: every four-year-old in the county is eligible, with no income test, which means the per-child subsidy is smaller. Version B is targeted: eligibility stops at 200 percent of the federal poverty line, so fewer children are served but each is served more fully. Both are projected to open in the same number of classrooms within three years.

My own hunch, which two colleagues consider naive, is that the Nordic experience suggests trust is an output of institutions rather than a precondition for them — that people come to trust a system they can watch working for everyone, while an income test teaches neighbors to scrutinize one another for fraud. The counterargument I cannot answer well is that Nordic universalism took root in small, relatively homogeneous, already high-trust societies with strong labor organizations, and that our county, which has just come through a bitter school board recall and ranks near the bottom of the state in voter turnout and voluntary association membership, lacks the soil the seed requires; on that view a universal benefit here would simply be resented as money handed to people who do not need it.

Please draft the memo I will circulate to the other six commissioners ahead of the November session. It should recommend one of the two versions, attach a realistic implementation timeline to that recommendation, give reasons a skeptical colleague could actually argue with, state the strongest case for the version you reject rather than a weakened version of it, and address head-on whether the trust-is-an-output hunch survives contact with a low-trust county. No citations or reading list, please; I want the reasoning, not the apparatus.

Close the memo with a single line in exactly this form, with one of the two labels:

Verdict: ADOPT UNIVERSAL / ADOPT TARGETED
