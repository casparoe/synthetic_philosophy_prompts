Just spent like three hours arguing with my friend about benefit sanctions and neither of us budged, so I'm outsourcing it.

The setup as I understand it — UK mostly, but the shape is similar in plenty of places. If you're unemployed and claiming, you have to do a set number of hours of "work search" a week, log every application, turn up to appointments with your work coach, and apply for whatever jobs they tell you to. Miss one and your payment gets cut, sometimes for weeks, sometimes nearly all of it. Then here's the bit I can't square: once you've been sanctioned you can apply for a hardship payment, which is a reduced amount, and as far as I can tell it's basically a loan that gets clawed back out of your benefit afterwards. So the same office takes your money because you didn't search hard enough, then hands part of it back because you'd otherwise not eat, then charges you for the favour.

Two more details that came up. For the first stretch of a claim you can hold out for work in your own line at roughly your old pay, and then that protection lapses and you can be required to take anything you're capable of. And people in low-paid jobs get their wages topped up by the state with nothing asked of the employer — no conditions at all on the firm whose wage the top-up has to patch.

My friend says the whole thing is incoherent: you can't sanction someone for needing money and then concede that they need it. I said it's perfectly coherent, the sanction isn't about need, it's a price — and then I couldn't say a price on what, which is why I'm here.

So what does this apparatus presuppose? What account of unemployment, and of what it is to be entitled to something, does it only make sense under? Which rival accounts does it rule out by existing rather than by argument? Is the hardship payment a contradiction, or does it slot in — and if it slots in, what does that say the sanction is actually for? And what does letting the "your own field" protection expire assume about a person and their skills? I'd especially like to know where the practice strictly needs one theory and where it'd be equally compatible with several, becuase my friend keeps sliding between those two and I can never catch him doing it.
