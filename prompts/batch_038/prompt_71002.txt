I am a commissioning editor for a documentary strand on modern history. Perhaps a third of the treatments that reach me are built on a single responsibility claim — that some state, leader, or decision started a given conflict — and the Cold War is the most common, though I see the same shape for 1914 and for several post-colonial wars. I have about an hour per treatment, I am not a specialist in any of these literatures, and if I pass one, development money is committed and the film's central claim becomes something a large audience will take as settled. I want a screening procedure I can run on any treatment whose spine is a "who started it" claim.

The procedure should be a numbered sequence of questions, each with what counts as a sufficient answer drawn from the treatment's own materials, what a failing answer looks like, and whether that failure is curable in the edit or fatal. It should make me test whether the question the film asks is well posed before testing the film's answer: whether what is being asserted is causal contribution, but-for necessity, or culpability; whether the film fixes the onset of the thing it says was started; whether the actors are states, leaders, bureaucracies, or systems, and whether the film changes level mid-argument; and whether the film's rival accounts are represented strongly enough to be beaten rather than waved off. It should distinguish failures of evidence from failures of framing, say where the procedure must hand the question to a specialist referee because a non-specialist cannot settle it in an hour, mark the steps that are judgment calls wearing a procedural costume, and state plainly what the procedure cannot do.

Then run it on three treatments I actually hold: (1) one arguing that the Soviet leadership planned the division of Europe from 1943 onward and that Western policy was reactive throughout, resting on internal planning documents and the memoirs of officials who served that government; (2) one arguing that nobody started it — that a bipolar security dilemma made the conflict overdetermined — whose centrepiece is a montage in which both sides take structurally identical steps; (3) one arguing that American economic and atomic diplomacy started it, which is the treatment I find most persuasive and which I expect the procedure to fail anyway.

Give the procedure as a numbered list. For the three runs, give a table with these columns: treatment; verdict (develop / develop with conditions / refer to specialist / reject); first procedure question failed, by number; why the treatment's answer is insufficient; curable in the edit (yes/no); what would have to be added or cut to pass. End with three sentences on the procedure's limits — which treatments it will wrongly kill and which it will wrongly pass.
