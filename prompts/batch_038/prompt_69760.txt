Our interpretability subgroup has gotten stuck in an argument I can't break with reasoning, so I want to break it with a sorting instrument instead. The fight is over what it takes for two things to count as "the same mechanism." One of us says a monitor is tracking the same mechanism across training runs if it lights up on the same feature direction, full stop — implementation details are irrelevant. Another says identity is strictly causal-circuit identity: same components doing the same causal work in the same order, or else it's a different mechanism and your transfer claim is worthless. A third says the question is ill-posed until you fix which behavior or capability you're individuating relative to, so "same mechanism" can come apart by interest. Each of them thinks the other two are being sloppy, which is the classic sign that we're applying different criteria without noticing it.

So: write me a quiz, call it something like "which criterion of mechanism identity do you actually hold?", that we can run in our Friday reading group — six people, two with basically no ML background, one extremely stubborn. Eight to ten items, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping patterns of answers onto named criteria. Use the sort of cases we actually fight about: two independently trained models where the same-looking circuit shows up at a different depth; one feature picked out by two different sparse autoencoder dictionaries trained on the same activations; a model that keeps producing the right output after fine-tuning but where patching shows a completely different set of components is now responsible; a trigger-conditioned sleeper-agent-style behavior that stays invisible to a monitor that fires reliably on the honest version of the same behavior; a capability you can elicit on demand but never localize; a circuit that gets the right answer for what looks like a spurious reason; a superposed feature sharing neurons with a dozen others so you can't say where one mechanism ends. Phrase every item so someone can answer on intuition without knowing where their answer lands — nothing of the form "do you think mechanisms are individuated by function?" And no reading list, no citations, no named papers; we'll argue from the cases.

Two things I especially want. First, build the space before you fill it: lay out the axes you think actually seperate the positions — function versus causal architecture, type versus token, intrinsic versus interest-relative, all-or-nothing versus similarity-graded, whatever else belongs there — then show which combinations are occupied by a recognizable view and which are empty, and for each empty cell say whether nobody is there because the position is incoherent, because nobody would want it, or because nobody has thought of it yet. Second, flag the pairs your instrument can't tell apart; I'd much rather be told that item 4 and item 9 collapse onto the same criterion than find that out mid-meeting. Close with one short paragraph on which criterion, if any, licenses the transfer claim we actually care about: that a monitoring result from an earlier checkpoint or a smaller model tells you something about the deployed model.
