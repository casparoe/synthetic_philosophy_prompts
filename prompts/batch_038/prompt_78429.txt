Think carefully before you write. Actually, start with a short brainstorm: list like 8 real, non-strawman problems with drawing the boundaries of a voting group, then circle the 4 or 5 that would be funniest as condo-association paperwork, and only then write the piece.

background on me, since it matters: i'm not an academic, i run a small hvac outfit. but i sit on my condo board, and last month we spent two and a half hours arguing about whether the owner in 4B — who rents his unit out and lives three states away — gets to vote on the motion about whether renters get to vote. nobody noticed we were going in a circle until my wife did, in the car afterwards. i've been low-key obsessed ever since, because it seems like every time you try to fix who's in the group, you need a group to authorize the fix, and there's no bottom. definately not a problem you can solve at a tuesday meeting.

so here's what i want: a **parody HOA election packet**, about 700 words, deadpan bureaucratic voice throughout, as if it's being mailed to unit owners by a very tired recording secretary.

Structure it like a real packet:

1. a cover memo from the secretary
2. the agenda for the annual meeting
3. minutes of the *previous* meeting, where the vote tally was itself disputed and the motion to table the motion was tabled
4. **Proposed Bylaw Amendment 7-C**, numbered clauses, defining who constitutes the electorate — each clause should quietly require a further clause
5. a ballot question the recipient cannot legally answer unless they've already been determined to be a voter under 7-C
6. a sidebar: "Why can't we just let everyone affected by a decision vote on it?" — answered helpfully, with the tally of eligible voters that resulted from trying it, and why the meeting was postponed

A couple of beats i'd love in there: the original plat map was signed by the developer in 1974 and nobody can verify the signature or find his heirs; there's an owner who moved out in 2011 but has never been removed from the roll; a proposal to extend the franchise to "all parties with a demonstrable interest," which turned out to include the town downstream, a future buyer in 2061, and the herons in the retention pond; and at least one kid.
