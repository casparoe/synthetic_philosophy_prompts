I'm not a philosopher — I run the data platform team at a mid-size logistics company. Somewhere along the line I became the guy who decides what our company will permanently know about its own past, and it's started to bug me, so I wrote down what I actually believe and I want to know whether it holds together.

My list:

1. The event log is the record. If we can't reconstruct something from it, then for any practical purpose it didn't happen. I don't trust postmortems written three weeks later, tribal knowledge, or anyone's memory of a standup.
2. The log should be neutral. We append what happened, we don't try to guess what will turn out to matter. Keep the schema as thin as possible and let future people do the interpreting.
3. Data minimisation is a moral obligation, not just a compliance checkbox. If we don't need a field we don't collect it — and over the last year I've had us drop a bunch of fields and purge some of what we'd already been keeping.
4. Silence isn't neutral. Which workers, which shifts and which kinds of events show up in the log at all is a function of what we built the system to notice, so the gaps encode the priorities of whoever designed it rather than anything about the world. I've made coverage a first-class metric — what fraction of a given site's or shift's activity produces any event at all — and we now deliberately instrument the groups that come out thin.
5. Nobody gets to curate. No manual edits, no retroactive relabeling of old events, no special role with write access to history, because whoever holds that ends up owning the past.
6. The archive isn't a closed loop. There are independent traces — paper manifests, carrier systems, weighbridge readings, physical stuff — that in principle let you check whether the log's picture of some period is skewed, so the record can be corrected from outside itself.

What I want from you:

- Tell me which of these genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do. My team keeps insisting 4 and 5 are flat-out contradictory; I don't think they are, but I want to know if I'm fooling myself there.
- Specifically: can 2 and 4 both survive? And is 3 vs 4 a real conflict or just a question of how much you collect?
- For each real conflict, what's the cheapest thing to give up, what does giving it up cost me elsewhere in the list, and is there a single revision that fixes more than one?
- Rank them by severity, don't just list everything as equally bad.
- And seperately: is there something 1 commits me to that I'd be pretty unhappy about once you connect it to the others? I have a vague sense there is and I cant pin it down.

I'm deliberately not telling you which one I'd drop. I want your honest read on where the load-bearing problem is.
