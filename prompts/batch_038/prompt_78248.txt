I am one of three student members of my high school's Academic Integrity Committee, and I need a short written opinion to circulate before Thursday's vote. The administration wants to pilot a system this spring: when a teacher suspects that a submitted paper was written by an AI, or that one of its sources was invented, two chatbot systems would be assigned to argue opposite sides of the charge, and a panel of three students with no expertise in the paper's subject would read the transcript and vote. The vendor's case is that non-experts can still reach correct verdicts this way, because a side that is telling the truth can always expose the other's errors in plain language, and because the school has no realistic way of getting a qualified expert to examine every accusation. Two of us are uneasy: the contested papers often turn on long, dense chains of reasoning or on references the panel cannot look up, and it seems far easier to produce a confident, complicated-sounding case than to check one, so we may simply be rewarding fluency, length, and tone rather than truth.

The memo should recommend adopting the pilot, adopting it under stated restrictions, or declining it, and should attach a concrete timeline for whatever it proposes. Please specify any restrictions so that we could actually enforce them — which categories of case the system may and may not be used for, what the student judges must be instructed before reading a transcript, what happens on a split vote or on appeal, and what the pilot's first-semester review should look at. Then give the strongest version of the vendor's argument and say what survives it, and state plainly which kinds of case my recommendation would get wrong and how the committee would find out that it had. Please do not rest anything on studies, statistics, or sources we could not check ourselves; the reasoning has to stand on its own.
