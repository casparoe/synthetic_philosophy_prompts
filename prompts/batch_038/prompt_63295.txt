So me and my friend (molecular biologist, insufferable about it) argued until 2am about the typogenetics material in Gödel, Escher, Bach — specifically whether the DNA/Gödel-numbering thing is an actual isomorphism or just a really seductive metaphor. My position: it's actual. In both cases a single string gets used in two totally different modes — interpreted, to build something, and copied, uninterpreted, as a template — and in both cases the regress-stopping trick is the same trick: the encoding winds up appearing inside the thing it encodes, so the system gets to mention itself. I think that's one structural pattern wearing four costumes (Gödel numbering, von Neumann's self-reproducing automaton, a Quine, a compiler that compiles itself), not four loose analogies that happen to share the word "code."

Her position: I'm pattern-matching on surface vocabulary. Her best shots, as I remember them:

1. Genetic coding is an arbitrary lookup table read by machinery — the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, the ribosome — and the sequence doesnt specify that machinery in anything like the way arithmetic specifies facts about numerals. Gödel numbering does its work because arithmetic is rich enough to *represent* statements about syntactic manipulation; a codon table can't represent anything, it just maps.
2. DNA isn't *about* itself. No content, no semantics. So "self-reference" is carrying the whole load and it's simply false on the biology side — what you actually have is causal self-templating, which is a different phenomenon with a similar-sounding name.
3. She also turned the RNA world against me, which I didn't expect. A ribozyme whose sequence determines its own catalytic behavior looks like it deletes the external interpreter, which ought to tighten the isomorphism — but she says it deletes the code at the same time, so there's no encoding left to be isomorphic to.

What I want from you: argue against me, straight, no diplomacy. What would have to be true for my view to be wrong? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be explicit about which ones a committed defender of the isomorphism would themselves concede as biting — I don't want strawmen, but I also don't want a dunk. Does my view predict anything that the it's-just-a-metaphor view doesn't, or is it compatible with absolutely everything? Because if it's compatible with everything, that's the real problem, and I want to know whether the two-modes move has quietly insulated it into vacuity. Finally: give me the minimum a case would have to have to count as a genuine instance of the pattern, stated tightly enough that some real candidate fails it. I'd much rather get a crisp criterion that excludes something than a fuzzy one that includes everything.

And please keep the settled part seperate from the contested part. I'm aware there are people who treat genetic information as information in a fairly strict, semantic sense and people who think that's all loose talk imported from communication engineering — tell me which of those is closer to consensus among people who actually work on the question and which is a live dispute, and don't hand me a contested claim dressed up as the standard view.
