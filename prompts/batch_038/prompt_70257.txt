A friend and I argued past midnight about whether philosophy makes progress, and we now suspect we were talking past each other rather than disagreeing. We have agreed to run an adversarial collaboration; please draft the joint statement we should be able to sign.

His position: absent convergence of informed opinion on an answer, everything else — sharper arguments, mapped dead ends, better formulations — is churn rather than progress, and the analogy to non-convergence in the foundations of physics fails, because physics as a whole is disciplined by prediction while these philosophical disputes have no comparable external check. Mine: progress consists in the shrinking of the space of positions one can hold while remaining a serious contender, plus the rising cost of holding the survivors, and convergence is not the only sign of tracking a subject matter — the continuum hypothesis is a case where the best-informed disagree permanently and yet few deny that mathematics progresses.

I want the document itself: a numbered list of claims both of us can sign, including on what would count as a philosophical question's having been settled; a table of the residual disagreements, each phrased so that the side holding it would accept the wording without feeling caricatured; and the cruxes — the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one of us. I suspect one crux is whether a position can be permanently eliminated or only suppressed: he will say the verificationist criterion of meaning came back disguised as anti-realist semantics, and I will say what came back is not what was refuted. For each crux, name a case, argument, or body of evidence both of us would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance, including what two administrations of a large survey of professional philosophers' views, a decade apart, would have to show for either of us to concede.

Please write in American English, and close by telling us where your drafting is most likely to have failed: which crux you take to be merely verbal, which agreed claim is quietly doing work for one side, and what consideration would change your mind about each of those judgments.
