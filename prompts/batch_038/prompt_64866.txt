I keep losing the same argument with a friend who's doing a PhD in history of science, and I think I need it reframed before I can make progress on it. The topic is periodization: whether labels like "the Middle Ages," "the Renaissance," and "the Scientific Revolution" pick out real joints in the past or are mostly backfilled by people who wanted to be on the flattering side of a line. The concrete stuff in play, as far as I've got it: the Italian humanists basically invented a dark in-between era as a way of casting themselves as restorers of antiquity; the continuity people point out that the fourteenth-century Parisians already had a theory of impetus and the Oxford crowd already had the mean speed theorem, so nothing really "begins" in 1543 or 1687; the rupture people say something genuinely different happened in how nature was conceived and you can point at it; and then there's the separate embarrassment that the phrase "Scientific Revolution" is a couple centuries younger than the thing it supposedly names. My friend thinks I'm a naive realist about eras. I think he's doing a mote-and-bailey between "these labels are useful handles" and "these labels are findings about the world." Both of us are talking past each other and neither of us has the vocabulary to say what we're actually disagreeing about.

So here's the ask. Don't give me a historiography lecture and don't name-drop. I'm a release engineer — eleven years of semver policy, deprecation windows, LTS branches, changelog generation, and RFC threads about whether a given change counts as breaking. Translate the periodization dispute into that vocabulary as faithfully as you can: version numbers versus diffs, breaking versus non-breaking changes, who is entitled to cut a release, how changelogs get written and by whom, what a bisect can and can't locate, tags applied to commits long after the fact, the difference between "we shipped v2" and "v2 happened." Two constraints I care about a lot. First, don't stretch the engineering vocabulary until it's just decorative — if the mapping only works because you've quietly redefined "breaking change," say so. Second, don't smuggle in your own verdict about the historical debate under cover of the metaphor; I want to be able to tell which parts are translation and which parts are you having an opinion.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want: which claims in the debate carry over cleanly, which only carry over with distortion, and which have no expression at all in release-engineering terms. My guess is that the untranslatable part has to do with history having no maintainer, no release branch, no one who cut the tag, and consumers who never saw the old API — but I want your version, not mine. And end with a call: does the leftover suggest the historians' disagreement is less substantive than it sounds, or that my vocabulary is missing a concept it ought to have?

Format: exactly five numbered items, two or three sentences each, no intro paragraph and no wrap-up at the end. Roughly two items on what translates, two on what distorts or fails to translate entirely, one on your verdict about the residue and who's right in the argument.
