My father died in the spring, and among other things he left eight hundred thousand dollars in trust to fund scholarships at the small college he attended. The deed is deliberately open: the money is to be used for the education of students there, and no schedule is specified, so it falls to me as trustee to decide whether we spend a substantial part of it over the next decade or leave it invested and let a much larger sum reach students fifty or eighty years out.

My brother, who manages money for a living, argues for the second course. His case runs like this. At five percent a year in real terms, a dollar left alone becomes roughly fifty dollars in eighty years, so waiting multiplies many times over the good the trust can do. On top of that, we do not know whether the college will still exist, or still need help, and we have every reason to expect that people alive in 2105 will be considerably better off than we are. Taken together, he says, these justify valuing a benefit to one of them at somewhat less than the same benefit to a student enrolling next fall, and he thinks four or five percent a year is a generous way of doing it.

Two things about this bother me, and I cannot tell whether the bothering is insight or a failure of nerve. First, the upshot seems to be that a student who enrolls in 2105 counts for less than one who enrolls today, and I cannot see what earns her that reduction other than her distance from me. Second, I am sixty-one, and I notice that I want this money to do something while I am still here to see it; I suspect that at thirty my brother's arithmetic would have looked self-evidently right to me. Does that self-suspicion disqualify my first objection, or is there something defensible in wanting the consequences of your choices to land inside your own lifetime?

The money is competently invested and the administrative questions are settled, so I am not looking for portfolio advice. What I want is a verdict on the reasoning: whether his argument holds, which of the considerations he has bundled together actually carry weight and which do not, and — if he is wrong about some of them — where the weight goes instead. I am wary of motivated reasoning on both sides of this, including my own.
