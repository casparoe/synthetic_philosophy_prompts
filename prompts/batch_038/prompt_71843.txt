So our department podcast (four regular listeners, one of whom is my advisor) is recording a live episode during the spring grad workshop, and the guest is a philosopher who defends the double-halfer line on Sleeping Beauty — the view that Beauty's credence in heads is 1/2 both before she goes under *and* again on each awakening, with the thirder intuitions explained away by the fact that on tails her decisions get made twice instead of once. I've done the reading and I know the Elga/Lewis exchange cold, so I don't want a single question that just invites her to re-run that debate for an audience that's mostly seen it already. What I want is around ten questions, and for each one a sentence or two predicting the answer she'll actually give, plus the follow-up keyed to that specific answer rather than to a strawman. The episode is 45 minutes in a room with terrible acoustics, so the questions need to be short enough to be heard once.

There are a handful of pressure points I definitely want covered. First, her update rule: if her credence doesn't budge on awakening, what does she think is happening epistemically when Beauty finds out she's awake right now — does she have a general account of what self-locating evidence can and can't do, and what becomes of the Principal Principle in her picture? Second, a variant I rigged up for our reading group and want to spring on her: heads means one awakening on Monday in a red room; tails means two awakenings, Monday in a red room and Tuesday in a blue one, memory erased as usual. Here "the room is red" doesn't look like a no-information proposition the way "I'm awake" does, and running it through ordinary conditionalization I get 2/3, which is the thirder's number. I want a question about what principled difference she can point to between conditionalizing on the wall color and conditionalizing on the fact of being awake — and a follow-up for whichever way she jumps, since if she says the color is centered too I want to know what's left of ordinary conditionalization anywhere, and if she says it isn't I want to know why her halving survives the moment the setup stops being perfectly symmetric. Third, the thousand-tails version: a thousand awakenings on tails, one on heads, and she still has to say 1/2 — is that a bullet she bites cheerfully, and what does she tell a grad student who reports that her view feels insane here. Fourth, the delayed-flip version where the coin is tossed after she's asleep rather than before, which my friend swears makes halving obviously right and which I think makes it harder to explain why her number is the same in both orderings. Fifth, the money questions: if she's offered a bet on heads at every awakening, and she knows in advance that tails will hand her two bites at it, what should she precommit to on Sunday night, and is there any combination of bets across awakenings that guarantees her a loss — with a follow-up on whether "the bets land on different centers" is an explanation or a restatement. Sixth, what credence is even *for* on her account, if it can diverge from rational betting dispositions this systematically without either one being wrong. Seventh, whether the view travels: Doomsday-style reasoning, the cosmological measure problem, Everettian branching — does double halving tell a cosmologist to ignore observer counts, and if so what breaks? And save the last slot for the question about what argument or observation would actually make her abandon it, with a follow-up in case she answers with something unfalsifiable.

One more thing. She has a verbal tic — whenever a betting or frequency argument comes at her she says some version of that's a fact about the decision problem, not about the credence. I don't want to ambush her or turn this into a gotcha fest, I like her work. But I'd like at least two of the questions phrased so that reaching for the tic commits her to something she'd rather not own on tape, and I'd like you to flag in a line which questions those are and what the commitment is. Also please don't spend any slots on biography, on how she came to the problem, or on softballs about whether the puzzle is overdiscussed — everyone asks that one and she has a canned answer for it.
