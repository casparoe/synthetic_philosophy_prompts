I have spent more time than I care to admit on the argument about whether Easter Island is a parable of self-inflicted ecological suicide or a story of competent adaptation to a marginal environment that was then wrecked from outside, and I have come away genuinely unable to tell whether the two sides disagree about the archaeology or only about what the word collapse is licensed to mean. The same thing happens with the Terminal Classic southern Maya lowlands, where the abandonment of the south coincides with the florescence of the north, and with the eastern Mediterranean around 1200 BCE, where some polities disappear, some are destroyed and rebuilt smaller, and some simply stop being interesting to the sources. I would like the specification before I read one more candidate theory.

So: write the desiderata for a concept of societal collapse. Treat this as conceptual engineering rather than historiography — what would a definition have to deliver for anyone to be entitled to assert that society S collapsed over interval t? I want eight to twelve requirements. For each, give the case or consideration that motivates it, a rough note on how one would operationalize it in principle, and a verdict on whether it is non-negotiable for any adequate concept or merely the preference of a particular school (the diminishing-returns-on-complexity economists, the resilience and adaptive-cycle ecologists who treat sudden release as one phase among several, the Late Antiquity historians who insist on transformation rather than decline, the scholars who take descendant communities' own framings as data, the cliodynamic coders). At minimum the list must deal with: which unit is supposed to collapse — a polity, a settlement system, a population, a tradition of practice, a ruling stratum; along which dimension loss is measured, since complexity is not a scalar and monumentality, administrative record-keeping, craft specialization, trade radius, and per-capita nutrition can move in opposite directions; against what baseline the loss is measured, given that the pre-collapse peak is chosen by the analyst; and over what observation window, since a case that is a collapse on a fifty-year window can be a relocation of the center of gravity on a five-hundred-year one.

Additional constraints:

1. The concept must be usable as a coded variable in a comparative dataset: applicable to non-literate societies on the strength of survey, palynological, faunal, and mortuary evidence alone, assignable to a dated interval, and decidable by someone who has not read the entire specialist literature on the case. Where a requirement makes coding impracticable, say so and say whether that disqualifies it.

2. It must yield a defensible verdict on four cases, and I want the verdict stated plus what it costs you to give it: the Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean around 1200 BCE; the Terminal Classic southern Maya lowlands; Rapa Nui; and, as a deliberate stress case, since it is inside living memory and saturated with texts that describe it in exactly this vocabulary, the Soviet Union and its successor states between 1989 and 1991.

3. Then apply it to a case I am stipulating rather than arguing about: a polity known only from settlement survey and pollen cores, in which the administrative centers and the monument-building stop within roughly two generations, occupied area falls by about half, per-household faunal assemblages get measurably richer across the same interval, and there is no evidence of warfare, epidemic, or abrupt environmental change. What should the concept say, and which of your requirements does the answer hang on?

4. Taboo the words collapse, fail, decline, and civilization in the statement of every requirement. If a requirement cannot be stated without one of them, treat that as evidence it is doing no work and either repair it or drop it.

5. State explicitly how the concept is meant to separate what people call collapse from de-urbanization, political fragmentation, the relocation of a capital or a ceramic tradition, ordinary dynastic turnover, and statelessness that agents choose by walking away from a taxing authority. If some of these cannot be separated in principle, that is a finding, not a defect in your answer.

6. I most want the joint-unsatisfiability analysis. My own suspicion is that rate-sensitivity — the demand that the loss be fast, on pain of the term ceasing to discriminate — cannot be held together with both the demand that a case where ordinary people's material conditions improved not be classed as a catastrophe and the demand that the concept be statable without reference to anyone's self-understanding, since illiterate societies leave us none. Tell me whether that triple is genuinely unsatisfiable, or find a worse one. For every unsatisfiable subset, say which member should be dropped or weakened, and what the resulting concept is then for.

Close with a short section identifying which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate accounts, and whether any of the requirements in circulation is argued for on rhetorical grounds only — that is, whether anyone would defend it if it were not entailed by their preferred case study.

No reading list and no citations, please: I cannot check them and I would rather have the cases and the distinctions. Do not rank existing accounts either; I will do that part myself once I know what I am supposed to be asking of them.
