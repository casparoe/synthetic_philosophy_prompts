For about four years my friend group has had the same argument on repeat and I've basically given up on winning it — but I want to understand *why* it's unwinnable, if that's even the right description. Nobody in the group studied this stuff. We're a nurse, two software people, a guy who repairs boat engines, and me (I do dispatch for a trucking company). It started because one of us read a thought experiment somewhere and brought it to the diner: there's a button that gives one randomly chosen person five years of the worst torture you can imagine, and a second button that gives a dust speck in the eye to more people than there are atoms in the observable universe — mildly annoying for a few seconds, once each, never again, no other effects on anyone's life. You have to pick a button and then defend the pick. We've done maybe a dozen variants since (kidney stones instead of torture, paper cuts instead of specks, spreading the torture over a thousand people instead of one) and the split is identical every single time.

Here's what each side actually says, because I think the diagnosis has to come out of that rather than out of abstractions. Marcus, on the speck side, says that if a speck is bad at all then it has *some* amount of badness, and amounts add, so refusing to add them isn't a moral position, it's just being bad at large numbers — his line is roughly that you can't call something bad and then say a billion billion trillion of them isn't worse than one torture, that's not a view, that's a failure to do arithmetic. Priya, on the torture side, says she has physically held people through real pain and there is no sense in which any pile of itchy eyes becomes a person being destroyed; her line is that you can't build a torture out of dust, and that pushing the speck button means treating one person's five years as a rounding error, which she thinks is the actual wrongness and has nothing to do with totals. Then there's Dave, who's sort of on the torture side but with an argument I can't shake. He says: start with the torture, then trade it for four years and eleven months of torture for that person plus one speck for somebody else — clearly an improvement, nobody in the group denies it. Keep taking steps like that. Each one is an improvement, and yet at the end you're at the speck button, which Marcus says is worse. So either one individual speck somewhere made the difference between better and worse, which is absurd, or "better than" just doesn't chain together the way Marcus assumes it does. Marcus says that's a cheap trick — if every step improves things then the endpoint is improved, that's what "improve" means, and Dave is basically arguing that a thousand grains of sand don't make a pile.

My own hypothesis, which I want you to attack rather than flatter: I don't think they disagree about how bad a dust speck is. Everyone agrees it's trivial. I think they're answering two different questions under one heading — Marcus is asking which of two states of the world contains more bad stuff, and Priya is asking what it's permitted to do to a particular person — and the four-year stalemate is just what happens when two questions share a sentence. Or maybe it's even simpler and more embarrassing: maybe one side thinks a speck has a tiny nonzero weight and the other thinks it has exactly zero weight, and absolutely everything else they say follows mechanically from that one unstated premise, so we've been having a huge argument about a sentence nobody ever said out loud. So: which is it, or is it something neither of those? Is this a real substantive disagreement, a verbal one about "worse," a hidden disagreement about how comparisons have to work as a formal matter, or just two sets of gut reactions that can't be argued about? Use what they actually say, not what positions like theirs are supposed to say.

And then the part I care about most: given whatever you diagnose, is there anything that would count as progress here — a distinction, a case we haven't tried, a way of restating it, a result about how "better than" has to behave — or is this the kind of deadlock where the honest move is to drop it and go bowling? If there's a single crux the whole thing turns on, name it and tell me which way you think it goes. Also, I'd like a straight answer on whether it's legitimate to think one of them is simply *wrong*, as opposed to operating from bedrock intuitions that just differ. I don't want a list of books or philosophers to go read, I want your own take on the structure of the disagreement.
