ok so this is suddenly very real for me and I want someone to tell me whether I'm reading Lakatos wrong, because I'm about to put this reading in a response-to-reviewers letter.

I'm a postdoc in a computational social science group. We've spent two years extending an agent-based model of labour market matching. The reviewer's main complaint across two rounds is basically: your core assumptions can't be falsified, and every time the model gets a known stylized fact wrong you bolt on another mechanism with another free parameter, which is textbook ad-hocery. My PI's answer in the hallway was to wave at Lakatos — we're a research programme, the assumptions are the hard core, the bolt-ons are the protective belt doing its job, done deal, go cite him. I read the MSRP stuff over the weekend (skimming, not gonna lie) and I think I have a much stronger version of that argument, but I want it checked first.

Here's what I've got. Lakatos says the hard core is irrefutable by the methodological decision of the people running the programme, and the negative heuristic just bans aiming modus tollens at it. So the core was never in the business of being falsifiable, which makes the reviewer's central charge a category error — you can't fault us for protecting what everyone in the framework agrees is protected. That leaves the belt as the only assessable part, and the only question there is whether the programme is progressive, i.e. still growing and still absorbing anomalies. Ours is: the mechanisms we added were each motivated by data from outside our own fits before we tuned anything, and last year the model produced a non-monotonic U-shape relating posting frictions to within-firm wage dispersion that nobody in the field had claimed, and it showed up in new administrative data. So on Lakatos's own test we're progressive, and the reviewer is a naive Popperian who thinks one counterexample kills a theory. There is a rival search-and-matching macro model people use, but its advocates havent engaged with our framework at all, so I figure that doesn't count.

Where I'm unsure, and please be blunt:

- Does progressive just mean not-yet-falsified? Because if the core can't be refuted by decision, and a belt theory only gets dropped once a better rival programme turns up, then it looks like literally nothing can ever count against a programme, which I doubt is what he means.
- Our added mechanisms were independently motivated, but they were also added right after a specific failure surfaced. Is that still ad hoc on his account, or does independent support settle it? And does one unexpected success that later checks out make the whole sequence progressive, or is that too small a sample?
- Can you even call a programme progressive in real time? Something I read suggested the appraisal only works decades later with hindsight, in which case my entire letter is pointless and I should say something completely different to the reviewer.
- Is progressive/degenerating a property of our programme on its own, or does it only mean anything relative to a rival? If the latter, what are we supposed to do when the rival camp just ignores us — is the reviewer entitled to sit and wait indefinitely?

Tell me where I've got him wrong and what I should actually be claiming in the response.
