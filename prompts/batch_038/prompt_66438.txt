I am preparing a position statement for a seminar on language and cognition, and before I commit to it in writing I would like an audit of the commitments below. They are views I actually hold, arrived at piecemeal over several years of reading; I am not able to say in advance which of them I would abandon if forced, and I would rather not be told merely that the set is "in some tension." Please treat the set itself as the object of analysis.

1. Determinism in the strong sense is false. Any concept expressible in one language is in principle graspable by a speaker of another, given enough exposure, explanation, or paraphrase. No thought is permanently sealed off by the absence of a word or of a grammatical category.

2. Nonetheless, weak relativity is a real and measurable phenomenon rather than an artifact of how findings get described: the language one grows up speaking leaves detectable traces on non-linguistic cognition.

3. My paradigm evidence is color. Speakers of languages that lexically split a region of color space discriminate within that region faster or more accurately than speakers whose language leaves it undivided; the advantage is attenuated or abolished when participants carry out a concurrent verbal task; and effects of a related family appear in description and similarity-judgment tasks involving grammatical gender. I read the interference result as showing that language is recruited in the act of discriminating — that is, that what speakers see, and not only what they subsequently call the stimulus, differs across languages.

4. The mechanism I find most credible is the one that confines relativity to online event construal: language shapes thought chiefly in the moment when a speaker is preparing to describe, remember, or judge an event in language, and what results are habits of attention rather than standing alterations to the cognitive system.

5. At the same time, I take languages that obligatorily encode cardinal-direction frames of reference to demonstrate something stronger. Their speakers maintain accurate orientation in nonverbal pointing and array-reconstruction tasks in which speakers of relative-frame languages fail badly, and I regard this as a difference in cognitive capacity, not merely in disposition.

6. I do not think any of the above is threatened by evidence that the cortical language system is dissociable from reasoning — intact problem-solving in people with severe agrammatic aphasia, and the imaging dissociations between language-selective regions and multiple-demand regions. Language can shape thought without being the medium in which thought is carried out.

7. I am skeptical of several celebrated case studies. I do not accept that speakers of an anumeric language are incapable of representing exact quantities, since the follow-up work suggested the original demonstrations turned on task design; I do not treat the claim that a particular Amazonian language lacks embedded clauses as settled; and I set aside the mid-century material on Hopi temporal expression in light of the later linguistic reanalysis of it. Methodologically, I regard describing another language's categories in the vocabulary of one's own as an error to be policed.

The deliverable is an audit, not a survey. Identify which pairs or clusters genuinely cannot be held together and which only appear to conflict; order the genuine ones by severity; and for each, state what would have to be revised and what that revision would cost elsewhere in the set. Then name the single change that would resolve the most, and say what I would lose by making it. Where one of my premises assumes a claim that is contested among people who work on this, flag it explicitly and separate the parts of the picture that are broadly settled from those that remain live disputes — I would rather learn that an item on my list is a minority position than have it silently treated as background consensus. If you think one of my readings of a finding is simply wrong about what the finding showed, say so rather than reasoning within my framing. No references or reading list, please: argue from the structure of the positions.
