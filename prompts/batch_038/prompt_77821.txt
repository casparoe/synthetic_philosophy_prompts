I was at a reading group on theodicy and two people got into it over Leibniz's claim that God created the best of all possible worlds. Ren went first: (1) the thesis can't come out false, so its not saying anything — Voltaire pointing at Lisbon wasn't a counterexample so much as a demonstration that "best" has stopped doing work. (2) Omar said that's a category error: the claim is comparative and about whole worlds, not slices of one, so you can't score it off an earthquake any more than you can score a symphony off one bad bar. (3) Ren said fine, but then we can never actually compare whole worlds — we don't have the alternatives to inspect — so the claim is evidentially inert either way, and only God could check it, and God isn't publishing. (4) Omar said there is a criterion and it's not arbitrary: the best world maximizes variety of phenomena under the simplest set of laws, and it's not additive, so "just add one more happy person" doesn't obviously beat it, because that rewires everything downstream. (5) Ren's closer was two pronged: even that ordering might have no maximum at all (Aquinas already held God could always make a better universe than the one he made), and if there is a unique best, then God's choice is necessitated — which is the Spinozism Leibniz wrote the book to escape. Neither of them budged.

Rate each of the five moves from −2 (clearly wrong) to +2 (clearly right), with a sentence of justification per move, then say who's more right overall and name one thing neither of them raised that would change the verdict. Hard cap: 400 words.
