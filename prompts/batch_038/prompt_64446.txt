Trying to work out whether my reactions to uncertain judgement add up to anything, and I'd rather go by the cases then by any theory I've read. Here's where I land:

Two ventures that, as far as I can tell, have the same chance of working — one backed by fifteen years of data on similar things, one resting on a single analogy and a smart person's hunch. I take the first, and I don't think it's because I rate it likelier. I can't say it's likelier. Something else is better about it, and I don't have a word for the something.

Some questions I don't think have a ranking there to be found: the chance of a big war in twenty years versus the chance some technology exists by then. I can't order them, and I don't think more thought would let me. I don't take that as a defect in me.

When I've thought as hard as I can and stalled, I do what the people around me are doing, and I think that's legitimate — last move standing, not a failure of nerve. But put my savings on that same judgement and no, then I won't act at all.

A forecast quoted to three decimal places makes me trust it less. "Roughly a third, and I could be way off" makes me trust it more. I can't defend that and I don't want to give it up.

Then the one that breaks the pattern: something everyones analysed to death, I avoid it precisely because it's well understood, and I'd rather hold the thing nobody has looked at. Which looks like the exact reverse of my first verdict.

And I'll happily do expected-value arithmetic on a whiteboard with numbers I invented, and it genuinely moves me — but ask me to bet the house on the same number and I refuse.

Is there one principle that generates all of these, or most of them, precise enough that I could actually test it against a new case? Say which clause each verdict needs. And if simplicity costs me a verdict, tell me which one to drop — I'd rather lose one than keep a contraption with six dials on it.

The case I keep flip-flopping on: a friend wants me to go in on something where there's almost no data at all, but he's spent two years inside it and has a strong feel for it. Does his evidence count for me, when all I actually have is his word that he has it?
