So I'm a junior and somehow I got roped onto my school's Elections Committee — seven of us, mostly seniors, plus a teacher advisor who doesn't get a vote. Every spring we run student body president for like 1100 kids, first past the post, and it's always a disaster. Last year two candidates from the same club split basically the same voters and the winner had 36%, and half the school thinks he's a joke. This year the committee has to actually choose a new rule instead of punting again, and I got volunteered to write the memo everyone reads before Thursday's meeting. It's supposed to recommend something, not just summarize options, and I'm kind of in over my head.

Here's the state of play. On the table: keep FPTP, ranked choice (instant runoff), approval voting, Borda count, and one senior wants sortition — an actual lottery among the candidates — on the grounds that Arrow's theorem proves every voting system is rigged, so we might as well be honest and pick randomly. He also argues that since Arrow shows no ranked system can be fair, the fair move is to just keep FPTP because the ballot already exists and it's the simplest thing to explain. Another member says approval voting escapes Arrow's theorem entirely because it isn't a ranked system at all, so that settles it, we should do approval. Our advisor keeps saying we're all being sloppy about what the theorem does and doesn't say, but she won't just tell us. To get some data we ran a practice poll in homerooms with four candidate names we made up (Marcus, Priya, Dana, Theo) and had 600 students rank all four:

- 180 students: Marcus > Priya > Dana > Theo
- 150 students: Priya > Marcus > Dana > Theo
- 130 students: Dana > Theo > Priya > Marcus
- 90 students: Theo > Dana > Priya > Marcus
- 50 students: Theo > Priya > Marcus > Dana

Two other things that matter for the decision. Our bylaws say the winner needs "a majority of votes cast," which nobody has ever actually gotten under FPTP and everyone has quietly ignored for years — but if we switch systems people might start enforcing it. And practically: our ballot is a Google Form that can't do a real ranked ballot without some ugly workaround, and the district wants any change to the ballot format filed 60 days out, which is about five weeks from now, so if my recommendation needs a brand new form we may just not have time.

Can you draft the memo for me? What I want in it: (1) actually run that practice-poll table and say who wins under each rule — FPTP, IRV, Borda, approval (you'll have to say what you're assuming about how many candidates a voter approves, since we never specified it), and whether there's a Condorcet winner in there at all. Show just enough of the arithmetic that the seniors can check you, because I'm pretty sure the results are not what the committee assumes. (2) A straight answer to the sortition guy: what Arrow's theorem actually rules out, which of its conditions is doing most of the work, and whether "it's all arbitrary so use a lottery / keep FPTP" follows from it at all given what our own poll numbers look like. (3) Whether the approval-voting person is right that it dodges the theorem, and what it costs us anyway — strategy, ballot comprehension, the majority-of-votes-cast bylaw, all of it. (4) The strongest honest case for whatever rule you end up telling me not to pick, taking the five-week deadline seriously rather than waving at it. (5) Two or three criteria the committee could re-use next year without me in the room. Roughly two pages, clear recommendation up top, plus a fallback option in case the 60-day thing kills the first choice. Sorry this is so long, I just want to walk in Thursday with something that holds up.
