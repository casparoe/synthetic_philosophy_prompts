1. Marcus said the flying man is basically Descartes' cogito, just like six hundred years early. Strip away everything about the body you could be unsure of, what's left is your own existence, and both of them use that to conclude the mind isn't the body. Same argument, same conclusion.

2. Dana said they're not structurally the same at all. Descartes needs the doubt as a method and what he gets out of it is certainty. Avicenna's flying man isn't doubting anything, he's just a guy who gets created all at once already grown, floating in calm air with his limbs spread out so they don't touch each other, eyes covered, no sound or smell or anything. He'd affirm that he exists, and he wouldn't affirm his body or his limbs. So the payoff is about awareness, not certainty. It's a claim about what your attention is aimed at, not a metaphysical claim.

3. Marcus said if that's all it is then it's way too weak for what Avicenna actually wants, which is a soul that's a seperate thing from the body and can survive it. And anyway the setup doesn't hold up: a real person floating there would still feel their heartbeat and their breathing, and their inner ear would tell them which way is down. So the premise that he gets no information about his body is just false.

4. Dana said the heartbeat point helps Avicenna rather than hurting him, because feeling a thump in your chest isn't the same as being aware that you have a heart, an organ with a shape and a location. You can have raw inner feels and still not affirm that your body exists. And on the other point she said the flying man was never meant to prove substance dualism by itself. It's a device for noticing that your self-awareness isn't assembled out of body-awareness, and that's the input his real arguments need, like the one about how the intellect can't be a bodily organ because an organ is locked into one kind of thing and so it couldn't take in everything else. Marcus said then the thought experiment is just decoration, and bad decoration, since it invites exactly the dualist reading Avicenna can't pay for. Neither of them budged.
