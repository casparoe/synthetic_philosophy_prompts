Write me a short story, maybe 1000 words, about a small town whose planning commission applies a really strong precautionary principle: a project gets rejected unless the proponent can show the chance of an irreversable harm is effectively zero, and the size of the benefits is legally inadmissible — you're just not allowed to weigh them at all. Here's the wrinkle I want the plot to turn on: a junior staffer realizes the same rule can be turned around on the commission's own refusals, because declining to build things has irreversible costs too, and nobody can show those are zero either. So the rule, applied honestly, condemns every option on the table including doing nothing.

Put the argument inside an actual public meeting and make everyone in the room reasonably smart — the chair should have a genuinely good reply, not a strawman. End it unresolved: the rule shouldn't come out obviously vindicated or obviously destroyed. Keep the setting totally mundane (a culvert, a grain elevator, a dialysis clinic), and no sci-fi, no pandemic, no evil bureaucracy, no AI.
