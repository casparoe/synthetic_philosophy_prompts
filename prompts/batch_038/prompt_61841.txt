For the coming year I plan to read interpretations of the French Revolution, and I would like to know in advance what to demand of one. My reason for reading is a specific question: was the Terror already contained in 1789 — in the Revolution's ideology, its social conflicts, the institutional forms it began with — or did it happen to the Revolution, produced by war, accident, and decisions that could have gone otherwise? Write the desiderata for an interpretation that would actually settle that question: the features of the events it must account for, the distinctions it must not collapse, the results it must be consistent with, with the historical case behind each item. Some cases I have in mind: the crisis was opened by aristocrats, and the Third Estate's claims were framed universally rather than as the interest of a class; the Revolution changed hands repeatedly between 1789 and Thermidor, and each turnover needs explaining rather than assimilating; the king's flight, the war, and the levée en masse stand between the founding principles and the guillotine; most of those executed were not nobles, and much of the worst killing was civil war against peasants; the Revolution destroyed precisely the institutional forms a rising bourgeoisie is supposed to have wanted, while the administrative centralization it is credited with creating both preceded and outlasted it. Make the list considerably longer than these, and tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out candidates.

Then sort the list: which items are genuine constraints on any adequate interpretation, and which are the settled preferences of one camp? I suspect at least one of the standard demands — that an account name the class whose interests drove events, or that an account show the Terror developing out of Enlightenment principle — is a commitment rather than a constraint. Relatedly, does an interpretation owe me a mechanism for the general claim that revolutions devour their children (successive purges that narrow the coalition, power concentrated with no exit, the enemy defined as internal), or is that merely a description of this case, and what would the difference show?

Most importantly: is any subset of the desiderata jointly unsatisfiable? If answering my question requires giving up something else one is entitled to demand of an interpretation, I want that argument laid out, because then my question is malformed rather than merely contested. Close with a few sentences on how the social, the political-ideological, and the contingency-first families of interpretation fare against the list — no survey of historians and no bibliography.
