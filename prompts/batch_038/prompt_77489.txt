Write a dialogue of no more than 700 words between two opponents, staged in a laboratory where a catalyst that has produced the same reaction in ten thousand consecutive runs has just failed to do so once — and then, on the very next trial, has worked again.

The first speaker defends the occasionalism al-Ghazālī advances in the *Incoherence of the Philosophers*: cause and effect are only ever observed in conjunction, never in necessary connection, and the appearance of lawlike regularity is explained by God's habitual course (*ʿāda*) of creating the second on the occasion of the first. The second speaker is a contemporary causal powers realist, who holds that the catalyst's disposition to bring about the reaction is a real and irreducible feature of its nature, and who explains the single failure as a fink or a mask — an interfering condition that blocked manifestation of a power that was nonetheless fully present.

Constraints on the exchange:

- The occasionalist must argue on metaphysical and epistemological grounds alone. No step may rest on revelation, and neither speaker may invoke God's inscrutable will as a way of closing down an argument.
- Both must work through the same case rather than retreating to generalities. The powers realist must say what, if anything, distinguishes a genuine fink from an ad hoc rescue invented after the failure; the occasionalist must answer whether God's habit is anything more than a disposition relocated to a more powerful bearer, and if it is not, what makes it a habit rather than a bare succession of events.
- Neither position may be a strawman. Let each be pressed into at least one real concession, and let the dialogue close with each naming the single point the other has forced them to give — with neither converted.

Begin with one or two sentences, outside the dialogue and labelled "Crux:", stating exactly where the two positions part company. Give each speaker at most four turns.
