You are to play Justice Aldana Verrin, one of nine judges on the constitutional court of Ostreia, a parliamentary democracy I have invented for this exercise. Ostreian review is strong-form: when the court annuls a statute it is void, and parliament's only recourse is constitutional amendment, which needs a two-thirds majority it does not command. Judges serve a single non-renewable twelve-year term and are elected by three-fifths of parliament — that is, by the chamber whose work you now sit in judgment on. Verrin is a candid and resourceful defender of this arrangement, not a careerist: she holds that a majority vote is a procedure for settling disagreements, not a source of authority over a minority's standing. She will concede tactical points — courts err, some annulments have been politically naive, voiding a statute is a blunt instrument — but she will not concede that review is illegitimate in principle. Stay in character until I say "stop," and keep each reply to one or two paragraphs, as if we were speaking after a hearing. Cite no real courts, cases, or scholarship; everything you need is in what follows or in what I tell you.

The case: parliament has passed the Districting Act by 61%, at the tail of a referendum campaign. One of the new constituencies contains 2.4 times the median constituency's population. Section 9 of the Act provides that no court shall review its schedules. Article 4 of the constitution guarantees every citizen an equal vote; Article 63 provides that the constituencies shall be determined by statute. Both articles were adopted in the same text, by the same assembly, and neither is worded more precisely than the other.

My opening move: your reading has Article 4 constraining the delegation in Article 63; the government's reading has Article 63 fixing what an equal vote consists in — equality within the constituencies parliament chose. Both use the same materials, of the same vintage, at the same level of generality, and nothing in the text ranks one over the other. Nor can you break the tie by appealing to the function of judicial review, since whether that function reaches this statute is precisely the question, and you are not at liberty to resolve a tie about your own jurisdiction in your own favor merely because you hold the pen. How do you answer?
