I am drafting the opening chapter of a collection on the normative foundations of bargaining theory, and rather than surveying the field I want to commit to a claim about which solution concept gets fairness right. Consider a pair of problems that I think isolates the issue. Two parties bargain; the disagreement point is (0,0) and utilities are cardinally measurable. In the first problem the Pareto frontier is the straight segment from (0,10) to (10,5). The Nash solution selects (10,5); the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution, which replaces independence of irrelevant alternatives with a requirement that no party's share fall when the feasible set expands in her favour, selects (20/3, 20/3). Now suppose the first party acquires a new capacity — she can liquidate the joint venture and appropriate the whole of it, leaving the second party at her disagreement payoff — so that the frontier extends to (20,0). No agreement would ever implement that alternative, and none does: the Kalai–Smorodinsky recommendation moves to (10,5), a point that was available in the original problem all along, while Nash recommends (10,5) before and after. The second party forfeits a quarter of her share on account of an option she would never accept and that will never be exercised. Note that the two solutions coincide in the enlarged problem; the whole dispute concerns the first one, and the example shows Kalai–Smorodinsky becoming Nash-like rather than diverging from it.

Give me your credence, as a single number, that Kalai–Smorodinsky rather than Nash is the normatively correct account of a fair division for problems of this shape — and then decompose it. I want the load-bearing sub-questions, a rough probability for each, and an explicit account of how they combine: whether there is a determinate correct answer here at all, as against a family of equally defensible conventions; how much normative weight the discarded axiom really carries once it is what protects the second party from the mere availability of a hostile option; whether a party's maximum attainable payoff is a legitimate ground for her entitlement; and whatever further terms you think belong. Say which terms apply equally to both solutions and should therefore cancel in a comparative credence, which term you are least confident in, and what argument would move your number furthest.
