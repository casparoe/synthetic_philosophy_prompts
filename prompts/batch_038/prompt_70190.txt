so i've been reading about path dependence on and off for about a year now — mostly the stuff about why we're stuck with the QWERTY layout and why VHS beat Betamax — and without meaning to I seem to have assembled a whole position. A friend who actually studied this in school told me to write it all down so she could point out which bits don't fit, and I want a second opinion before I hand it over. Here's the list:

1. History really is path dependent. There are forks where two or more outcomes were both live, an early accident picks one, and then switching costs make it stick. It genuinely could have gone otherwise.

2. My test for whether something counts: would the same outcome have shown up anyway if you rewound and replayed? If it would have, it wasn't path dependent in the interesting sense, it was just a coincidence about timing.

3. But I also think most of the famous examples are shallow. Fine, a different video format might have won, but a video format was coming. Replay the tape and you get functionally the same thing under a different brand. So contingency is real but usually not deep.

4. Technology ratchets. Individual techniques get lost here and there, but the underlying know-how doesn't un-happen — over the long run it's one-way, and humanity doesn't permanently lose ground.

5. Institutions are nothing like that, and I think they're where the actual lock-in lives. A set of rules can keep running for centuries after the conditions that produced it are gone, and that's most of why some places are rich and some are poor.

6. On method: I think you settle these questions by comparison. Find two places that were basically the same at some point, watch them take different forks, and that's your evidence.

7. Applying test (2) to the case I care about: democracy would have arrived anyway once you get enough wealth and literacy — but that wealth and literacy landed where they did for reasons I wouldnt call inevitable at all.

So — which of these actually pull against each other, and which ones only look like they do? I'm especially unsure about (4) and (5): it feels like I need an argument for the asymmetry I haven't come up with, or else one of them is just special pleading. And I have a nagging feeling (6) is unusable given the rest, but I can't say why. Please don't take my word for where the trouble is though, and don't build the answer around assuming I'd sacrifice any particular item — I honestly have no idea which one I'd drop.

What I want is an audit: rank the real tensions from worst to mildest, say for each one exactly what has to give, and what the cheapest repair costs me (what I lose by making it). Then tell me which single revision cleans up the most at once, and whether my test in (2) is even a well-defined question the way I've stated it.
