In my decision theory seminar we had a long argument after class about what coherence arguments actually establish, and I'd like a careful referee's report on it. Ravi and Ines were discussing an agent whose preferences violate the independence axiom — an Allais-style agent who is risk averse in a way expected utility theory disallows.

(1) Ravi: Such an agent is money-pumpable. Presented with a suitable sequence of choices, she can be led to trade down, surrendering a small sum at each step and ending where she started but poorer. A preference pattern that guarantees a sure loss is thereby shown to be irrational.

(2) Ines: The pump works only on a myopic agent. One who foresees the cycle either declines the first trade or binds herself in advance, and no loss occurs. What you've shown is that myopia together with risk aversion is exploitable, not that risk aversion is.

(3) Ravi: Notice what that concession costs you. If she binds herself in advance, she binds herself to the choice an ordinary expected-utility maximizer would make, and she pays for the privilege of doing so. So her risk aversion costs her money in every case where it makes a difference, and a preference you would pay to be rid of cannot count as rational.

(4) Ines: Costing money is not a criterion of rationality. Nothing requires an agent to rank prospects by expected monetary value; if she genuinely prefers the certain outcome, what she forgoes is simply the price of getting what she wants. And even where a Dutch book does go through, the representation-theorem arguments are too weak to deliver expected utility maximization: because the probability and utility functions they yield are not uniquely determined, bookability cannot tell us which axioms an agent satisfies.

Our TA said they were each half right but wouldn't say which halves. Please adjudicate move by move, rating each of the four from clearly mistaken to clearly right with a brief justification, and then say who is more right overall. Where a move depends on something neither of them has established, flag it explicitly rather than supplying it for them.
