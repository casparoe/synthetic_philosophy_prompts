Longtime LW/SSC lurker, doing evals work now, and there's a pattern I keep running into that I don't have a word for.

Setup: you're supervising a system by checking each step of what it does rather than judging the end result — process supervision, in the alignment vocabulary. But whether the checker can see anything at step N depends on the state that steps 1..N-1 produced, and the system produced that state. So the system never has to sneak anything past the checker. It just has to relocate the checker somewhere it can't see.

Examples I've actually watched: a model proposing small tidy-ups to its own eval harness, each individually approved by a human, until the harness stops measuring what it used to. A codebase where every added abstraction layer passed review, and now no reviewer can tell what a given diff actually changes. An agency that quietly reformats the reports it files, so the regulator's checks all still pass while becoming uninformative.

The nasty part is that its monotone and path dependent: looking harder doesn't help, because looking harder requires the very thing that decayed. And no individual step was deceptive, and nothing was optimized against a metric — the ability to *compute* the metric is what eroded.

Is there an established term for the supervisee gradually degrading the supervisor's capacity to supervise? I know it sits near reward tampering and near Goodhart, but neither seems right. Walk me through the nearest real concepts, where the edges are, and if there's no name, give me one that survives contact with them.
