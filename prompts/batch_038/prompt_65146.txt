I am an MA student in a philosophy-of-science seminar this term on logical empiricism and social planning. For the closing session each of us has to circulate a one-page position profile: our own commitments, stated in the idiom of the figure we have been working on, which the rest of the group then attacks. I have chosen Carnap, and I want my profile to be a serious reconstruction of a Carnapian political philosophy rather than a straw-man technocracy. Before I circulate it I would like a rigorous consistency audit. Please take on the role of a philosopher of science who knows both the Vienna Circle's social and political context and the contemporary debates about value-free science, expertise, and planning. Think carefully: this is an audit, not a sympathetic summary, and I would rather you found one real problem than praised the view.

Some background on what I am *not* claiming. I accept the standard correction that Carnap was no unpolitical positivist: he came out of the youth movement, joined the Independent Social Democratic Party after the First World War and wrote for left-wing papers and circulars, belonged to the left wing of the Circle, co-signed its 1929 manifesto, and in America supported the civil-rights and anti-war movements. My profile is an attempt to say what a political philosophy looks like if it is built out of his philosophical resources. Here it is:

1. **Tolerance about frameworks.** The choice among conceptual frameworks — including the choice to conduct a debate in the language of the empirical sciences rather than in some other idiom — is a practical decision, not a theoretical discovery. Frameworks are not true or false; they are more or less fruitful for given purposes. Philosophy accordingly issues proposals and conventions, not prohibitions.

2. **Internal versus external.** Inside a framework, questions have determinate answers, settled by the framework's own methods; for the scientific framework those methods are observation, measurement, and calculation. Questions directed at the framework as a whole are not theoretical questions at all but proposals to adopt a linguistic form, and the most that can be said for or against them concerns the practical advantages of adopting them.

3. **Anti-metaphysics, in the strong version.** Metaphysical theses — about the inner essence of being, about the reality of values as such, about the spirit of a people or the meaning of history — are not false but cognitively empty, and logical analysis dissolves rather than refutes them. I take this demarcation to be delivered by logic itself, not to be one framework's preference asserted against another's.

4. **Non-cognitivism about values.** Value judgments express a volitional stance, not beliefs; there is no science of correct ends. Psychology and sociology can describe what people value and explain how they come to value it, but nothing they find can justify an end.

5. **My politics.** I am a democratic socialist. I think a coordinated, planned economy under democratic control of the plan is both feasible and preferable. What makes Carnap's project politically important to me is the ambition, already visible in the *Aufbau*, of reconstructing cultural and social concepts on a basis common to all investigators: if questions about housing, health, education, and production can be formulated in a language every party shares, then disagreement about them stops being a collision of worldviews and becomes a question of evidence and calculation that can in principle be settled.

6. **No derivation.** My socialism is not derived from, and cannot be supported by, my philosophy. Philosophy clarifies concepts and compares means; ends are set by practical decision. Asked why plan at all, the honest answer is that this is a question about the form of life one wants to lead, not about what is the case.

7. **Tolerance as a political commitment, not only a methodological one.** Letting others keep their frameworks, and refusing to coerce them in what are really framework choices, is part of the program; I expect a society organized around a scientific world-conception to be freer and less coercive than one organized around metaphysical or nationalist worldviews. At the same time, a plan is binding: it determines for everyone how collective resources are used.

What I would like from you:

- Begin with a short brainstorm, before any verdicts. Go through the pairs and list every combination that could conceivably pull against another, labelling each a genuine tension, a merely apparent tension, or no tension at all, with a clause saying why. I want this spelled out so I can see you have surveyed the whole space rather than stopped at the first problem that occurred to you.
- Then rank the genuine tensions by severity, worst first. For each: state precisely what collides with what (which words in which commitment do the damage), what would have to be given up or weakened, the cheapest available revision, and what that revision costs me — and whether the cost is philosophical, political, or both.
- Tell me explicitly which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection and why. I expect several classmates to attack precisely those, and I want to know in advance that the attacks fail. If one dissolves only at a price, name the price.
- Do not flag everything as equally worrying; a useful audit discriminates. An answer that treats all seven commitments as mutually suspicious is worse than useless to me.
- Say which single revision would clean up the most, and whether what remains is still recognizably a Carnapian position or has quietly become something else — and if the latter, what it has become.
- If any of the seven is not something Carnap could have held as I have stated it, flag that in a sentence and give the nearest version he could have held. Keep this minimal: I do not want a lecture on his intellectual development, and I have no use for a bibliography, a reading list, or page references. I am working offline and want your reasoning, not your citations.
- I do not know where the trouble is, and I deliberately do not signal which of these I would be most willing to abandon. Please do not read my ordering as a ranking of my attachment.
