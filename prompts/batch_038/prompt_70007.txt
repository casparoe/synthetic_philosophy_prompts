I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine — mostly the afterlife of old arguments in current culture — and I just burned about a month reporting a piece on how medieval Islamic occasionalism keeps resurfacing on the wellness internet, i.e. people who listened to one podcast episode about al-Ghazālī and now talk about "surrendering control" and "nothing makes anything happen" as though it were a self-care protocol. The piece got killed. My editor's note said it was thorough but emotionally inert, which is fair, it read like a lecture with jokes bolted on afterwards. But the material is genuinely funny and I hate wasting it, so I want to redo the whole thing as satire and let you do the heavy lifting. What I want is a fake lifestyle-blog post, around 800 words, in the voice of an invented homesteader-influencer (pick her name and her aesthetic — sourdough, woodstove, four kids, an Etsy shop, a paid "surrender circle" community) who has adopted Ash'arite occasionalism as her actual parenting and home-management philosophy. She genuinely holds it: fire doesn't burn cotton, knives don't cut, bread doesn't nourish, the sun doesn't rise the earth — every effect is created directly by God on the occasion of what we carelessly call a cause. Then, appended: a short "update" post from three weeks later, about 200 words, and a comments section with exactly six entries.

The comedy has to come from the theology being right and her being wrong, not the other way around, so please hold to this. Her metaphysics should be stated accurately, including the canonical fire-and-cotton example from the seventeenth discussion of al-Ghazali's Incoherence of the Philosophers, God's habit (ʿāda) as the thing that makes the world predictable, the possibility of a break in that habit (khāriq al-ʿāda, miracle), and the fact that what's denied is *necessary* connection rather than regularity — she should have absorbed the vocabulary perfectly and the argument badly. Her error has to show up in the texture of her own post rather than in any authorial wink: she insists nothing causes anything while setting the kettle on, latching the gate, checking the smoke alarm, and describing all of it as "letting go." I want at least one dead-on moment where her view and ordinary causal talk make zero difference to anything she expects to happen, and where she's asked to name one prediction her metaphysics changes and can't. And give me a modest-proposal engine: she announces a 30-day "occasion fast" in which her household stops doing anything that presupposes a cause — no locks, no firebreak around the woodpile, no sunscreen, no timer on the stove — pitched completely straight-faced so the reader does the recoiling; nobody actually gets hurt on the page, the dread should be anticipatory. The comments carry the objections: one who argues in an Avicennan direction that if nothing causes anything then medicine, prudence, responsibility and knowledge all collapse (this is Averroes's charge against Ghazali, but paraphrase it, do not put a quotation in his mouth); one reader who actually teaches kalām and very gently explains that she has inverted the doctrine into the thing the doctrine was meant to forbid; one person only there about the affiliate links; one troll; one true believer signing up for the circle.

Two rules about tone. Deadpan throughout — a reader should be able to tell exactly what's being ironized without the narrator ever signalling. And the target is the influencer flattening and its monetisation, never Islam or al-Ghazali himself; a specialist in Islamic philosophy should read it and think the author understands the position and finds the satire fair. Don't invent any verbatim quotes from historical figures, paraphrase everything. After the blog post, the update and the comments, give me a JSON object with two keys. `moves`: an array with one entry per joke you actually landed, each entry having `beat` (a few words locating it, no need to quote yourself), `target` (whose practice or discourse is being needled), `doctrine_at_work` (the specific philosophical point doing the work — e.g. ʿāda, necessity versus regularity, khāriq al-ʿāda, the knowledge objection, zero predictive difference), `changes_any_prediction` (boolean — true only if the beat depends on the view actually altering what someone expects to happen), and `fair_to_the_doctrine` (one sentence on why a specialist would or wouldn't object). `risks`: an array of `{"risk": ..., "mitigation": ...}` naming every place the piece could slide from mocking her into mocking the theology, and what you did to stop it.
