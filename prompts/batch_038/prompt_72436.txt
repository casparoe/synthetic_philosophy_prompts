I'm auditing a philosophy of mind seminar and we've spent two weeks on the timing-of-consciousness material — color phi and the two ways you can tell the story about it. So: two lights, different colors, different positions, flashed in sequence inside the right window, and people report one dot that travels across and changes color partway. The change obviously can't have been seen before the second light came on, so either the subject really did have an experience of a moving dot that then got rewritten in memory before it could ever be reported (Orwellian), or the whole thing got assembled and reconciled before any of it became conscious at all (Stalinesque). Our professor's line, which I find annoyingly persuasive, is that these two aren't just hard to tell apart but permanently indistinguishable: the two camps supposedly agree about all the brain mechanics and about what the subject will say, and they differ only about whether to label a given spot in the pathway pre-experiential or post-experiential. Any probe you bring in to settle it is itself a report, and reports come downstream of the point where the two stories have already merged. My seminar partner goes further and says it's a category error rather than an empirical question, which I think is a cop-out.

So I want you to build a thought experiment that breaks it. Design a paradigm — describe it as an actual lab procedure, with stimuli, timings in milliseconds, and what the subject is told to do — in which the Orwellian and Stalinesque accounts of the same illusion genuinely diverge in observable behavior. Here's what counts as cheating, since that fixes what I'm after: you can't stipulate the verdict. I don't want a case where you just assert that the subject consciously saw the first color at time t and then forgot it; the behavioral difference has to fall out of mechanics each model is independently already committed to. Constraints: color phi itself is off the table, and so is any variant that merely fiddles with the gap between the lights or adds a third light — the discriminator has to be structural, not parametric. Everything has to be ordinary psychophysics: a screen, a button box or an eye tracker, a normal undergraduate subject pool. No imaging of "experience itself," no memory-editing drugs, no TMS, no implants. And the subject's output has to be something a human can actually produce — a verbal report, a forced choice, a key press, a saccade. One hint, which I think is the whole ballgame but feel free to ignore it if you can find a better hinge: the two models only merge if *every* channel that could carry the pre-edit content out to the world is itself downstream of the edit. If there's a channel with a shorter latency than the reporting channel — one that gets hold of the content before the rewriting and commits an output that the rewrite can't retract — then the Orwellian model has something available to it that the Stalinesque model structurally cannot produce. So be explicit about which channel leaks, and about the timing budget that makes it leak.

Then show me the design works, section by section, roughly these lengths: **The paradigm** (~300 words — stimuli, onset/asynchrony timings, the task, described well enough that I could hand it to a lab manager); **Stalinesque prediction** (~150 words — trial by trial, what the subject does and when); **Orwellian prediction** (~150 words — same, plus exactly where the two curves split and by how much you'd expect); **The hinge** (~120 words — name the single feature doing the work); **Dennett's reply** (~200 words — steelman what a multiple-drafts defender says about why the case still begs the question against them, i.e. that it smuggles in a finish line and treats the leaky channel as if it were the one true stream; don't strawman this, it's the objection I actually have to answer in seminar); **Kill switch** (~80 words — what I'd have to remove or equalize for the case to collapse back into ordinary color phi); **Would it run?** (~120 words — the confounds that would sink it in practice, and an honest call on whether this is an executable experiment or only a conceivable one). No references or bibliography, please, I just want the design and the reasoning.
