Before I write it I want the spec: what any account of emergence has to deliver to be usable in that room.

Give me the requirements — the cases it has to handle (including one where the macro law is derivable in principle but the derivation is about as long as just running the system, and one where a macro property is underivable only because the macro variable was gerrymandered), the distinctions it must not collapse, the results from physics and biology it can't contradict — with the motivating consideration behind each one. Then tell me which requirements are actually non-negotiable and which are camp preferences: my hunch is that "must exhibit multiple realizability" and "must license downward causation" are both the latter, but argue it, dont just assume it.

Most of all: is any subset jointly unsatisfiable? I want to know whether novelty, causal efficacy, nothing-over-and-above-ness, and causal closure at the micro level can all be held at once, and if not which one has to go and what you lose by dropping it.

End with five lines scoring Nagelian reduction, the Anderson more-is-different line, grounding-based accounts, and Wilson-style accounts that make emergence out to be ontological independence from a base you're nonetheless metaphysically dependent on. Five lines, not a treatise.
