I divide fixed pots between two claimants whenever a settlement fund or an insolvent estate cannot cover what both are asking for, and I would like to know whether my judgments add up to a rule. Here is what I told people, in the order the files came in.

1. Pot $100k. Ann claims the entire $100k and Ben claims the entire $100k, each insisting the asset was promised to them alone. I split it 50/50.
2. Pot $100k. Ann claims $60k, Ben claims $90k. I gave Ann $40k and Ben $60k: the shortfall should fall in proportion to what each is asking for.
3. Pot $100k. Ann claims $20k, Ben claims $90k. I gave Ann her full $20k and Ben the remaining $80k. Her claim is small enough that the pot all but covers it, and I saw no reason to shave it.
4. The same file as (2), reopened: Ben's lawyer amends his claim to $500k, adding punitive items that everyone in the room agrees will never be paid out of this pot. I held the split at 40/60, on the ground that a claim which cannot be honored should not move a single dollar.
5. Pot $100k, each claiming the whole of it, but Ann holds a secured position worth $20k: absent a settlement the file goes to court, she recovers that $20k, and fees consume the rest, so Ben walks away with nothing. I gave Ann $60k and Ben $40k — the $80k genuinely in play split down the middle.
6. As (5), except that Ben too would come out of litigation with $10k. I gave Ann $55k and Ben $45k.

A colleague tells me that (2) and (3) are the Nash and the Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining solutions wearing plain clothes. Test that rather than assume it, and then give me:

- the rule that best fits the six verdicts, stated so precisely that it outputs numbers for any pot, pair of claims, and pair of fallbacks — together with an account of which verdicts it cannot keep, and what in each case forces the choice;
- whether any rule can keep both (2) and (3), or both (2) and (4), while also never leaving money in the pot, treating like claimants alike, and giving the same answer whether I quote the figures in dollars or in euros. If no rule can, I want that impossibility stated as the result, with the two conditions that do the damage identified by name;
- what your rule says about a file I have not decided: pot $100k, Ann claims $30k and holds $10k of security, Ben claims $80k with no fallback.
