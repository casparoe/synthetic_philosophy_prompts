I write a science-and-ideas column and next month I get 40 minutes with a cognitive scientist whose whole thing is Dennett's ladder of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian and Gregorian creatures — except he thinks the ladder collapses. His argument: Gregorian-ness isnt a further upgrade in the head at all, it's just Skinnerian learning pointed at other creatures' artifacts, so the only real rung is the Popperian one, and his criterion for it is which thing gets destroyed when a guess goes wrong, the guess or the guesser. He also says language models prove the point, since they're Skinnerian systems shaped by Gregorian material rather than Gregorian creatures themselves. Write me eight to ten questions that get past his rehearsed answers. I want at least one on where New Caledonian crows and octopuses land on a ladder with only two rungs, one on whether the guess-vs-guesser criterion can actually be applied to a bee or a chimp rather than just asserted, and one pressing him on the worry that since literally everything alive is Darwinian and most things are Skinnerian, his one real line is doing all the work by stipulation — plus a follow-up for each keyed to the answer he's likeliest to give (he tends to wave off objections by saying the taxonomy was never a claim about what's inside the skull). End with one question about what observation would distinguish his two-rung picture from the four-rung one.
