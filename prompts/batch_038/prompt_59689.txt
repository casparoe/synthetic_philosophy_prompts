I am drafting the framing section of a survey on the metaphysics of causation, and I want a number rather than a tour of the literature.

Fix the target proposition as follows. Call two accounts of causation *genuinely distinct* when there exists at least one fully specified case in which the two, asked about the same concrete goings-on, return opposite verdicts on whether some item c caused some item e — where "the same concrete goings-on" means the case has been described without smuggling in either account's proprietary apparatus. I want your credence, on a 0–1 scale, that interventionism (roughly, the Woodwardian view on which causal claims are claims about what would change under interventions, with causation tracked by invariance under manipulation) and Lewis-style counterfactual analysis are genuinely distinct in that sense, rather than two notations for a single theory.

Here are two cases I constructed for the purpose. Please assess each on its own merits before combining anything.

1. *The inseparable cam.* One cam in a stamping press does two things per cycle: it drives a die onto a blank, and it opens a valve flooding the chamber with hot gas. The coupling is exhaustive by stipulation — no arrangement of this machine, and no machine of any design whatsoever, drives the die without flooding the chamber or floods the chamber without driving the die. The blank warps if and only if pressure exceeds a threshold and the gas is present; either factor alone leaves it flat. The question posed to both theories: did the driving of the die cause the warping? My conjecture is that the counterfactual account has a determinate answer here — yes, since the warping depends counterfactually on the driving — while the interventionist's answer turns on a choice of variable set that the case itself does not settle, and that a coarse-grained variable ("cam cycle") and a fine-grained pair ("die pressure," "chamber heat") license different responses. Tell me whether that conjecture is right, whether one of the theories in fact has no answer at all, or whether the case is a dud.

2. *The fixed constant.* Consider the claim that the value of the cosmological constant being what it is caused galaxies to form when they did. Attempt an explicit description of what an intervention on that value would be, if there is one, that is not simply a restatement of the counterfactual dependence already in play.

Now the decomposition. Assign a rough probability to each of the following, with one or two lines of justification per item, and then tell me how you combine them — they are manifestly not independent, so either give the functional form you are actually using or describe the holistic adjustment you are making in its place:

(a) that "intervention" admits of a characterization that does not itself lean on causal or counterfactual notions;
(b) that there is a non-trivial class of perfectly legitimate causal claims — cosmological, historical, or concerning properties that admit of no manipulator even in principle — for which no intervention is describable;
(c) that a variable-relative answer differs in content from an event-relative answer about the same goings-on, rather than merely redescribing it;
(d) that invariance under intervention is doing truth-conditional work at all, as opposed to stating a norm about the quality of explanations.

Close with three things: which of (a)–(d) your final number is most sensitive to, and by roughly how much; what argument, if you ran into it, would move your credence furthest and in which direction; and, if neither of my cases does the work I am asking of it, a better case of your own construction.

Finally, state how confident you are in the credence you settle on — not in the reasoning generally, but in the number itself — and single out the one point in your argument where you think you are most likely to be wrong.
