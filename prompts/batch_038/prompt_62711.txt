A friend of mine who works in trust and safety keeps arguing that we should treat misinformation literally as an epidemiology problem. Her pitch: a claim is the pathogen, people are hosts, shares and quote-posts are the vector, you can estimate an R0 for a given story, there are superspreaders, prebunking is the vaccine, deplatforming is quarantine, and you can even do contact tracing on a rumor. She leans on it hard to say that taking content down isnt censorship in any loaded sense, it's just public health — neutral, technical, like culling infected stock. She also thinks the analogy earns its keep by making predictions: that selection on transmissibility alone should push claims to get more contagious and less accurate over time, the way virulence trades off against transmission, and that exposure to a weak variant should confer cross-immunity to the strong one.

I'm broadly on her side politically but I think the comparison is carrying more weight than it can bear. Can you build the correspondence table properly — pathogen, host, vector, incubation period, asymptomatic carrier, herd immunity, virulence, vaccine, quarantine — and then tell me which disanalogies actually matter for the use she's putting it to versus which ones are just noise that anyone pressing the analogy could concede? In particular: does it quietly assume the host is being harmed, and that there's some healthy default state the population returns to once you clear the infection? Neither of those seems obviously true of belief. And then the real question — does "it's public health, not censorship" survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy supplying the whole conclusion? If it breaks, is there a better source domain that would support intervention without smuggling in the neutrality?
