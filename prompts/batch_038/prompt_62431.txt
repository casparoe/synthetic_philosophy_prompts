So my partner and I got married last spring, and for some reason that sent me down a rabbit hole reading arguments about what marriage actually *is*, including all the ones from before it was legal here. Ten-plus years of this stuff and the thing I can't get over is: the legal question got settled and the argument didn't stop at all. It just relocated.

Roughly, as I understand it, there's two camps. Camp A says marriage is a specific kind of thing with a built-in shape — a permanent, exclusive, comprehensive union of a man and a woman, ordered toward having and raising children, where fidelity and permanence aren't matters of taste but part of what the institution is. On that view the state doesn't invent marriage, it recognizes it, and it can recognize it badly. These people call their own position the conjugal view and call the other side revisionist, which already tells you something. Camp B says marriage is a human institution whose content has always been set by what societies use it for — a publicly recognized commitment between two consenting adults, whose point is the mutual care and stability it produces, and whose definition can and should change as our reasons for having it change. On that view there's no deep fact about what marriage is underneath what we've collectively decided to do with it.

Both sides know every argument the other side has. Nobody moves. My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis and probably half-baked: they aren't arguing about the same object. Camp A treats marriage like something with a function it can have or fail to have — like a heart, or a protocol spec somebody wrote — while Camp B treats it like an identifier we can rebind to whatever arrangement we choose, like a typedef. If that's right, the whole fight is downstream of an argument nobody's actually having, about whether things like money, marriage, and property have purposes independent of human intentions, and everything else is noise.

Is that the right diagnosis, or am I talking nonsense? Sort out which of these it actually is: a substantive disagreement about whether social institutions have essences or functions; a verbal fight over "is" and "for"; two different questions sharing one label — one about what marriage has done across cultures, one about what the state should recognize and promote; a hidden empirical question about effects on children and on the norms of the institution that both sides wave at but nobody treats as the real crux; or bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for. Then tell me what would count as progress, if anything — a distinction, a reframing, some evidence, a different question — or why no progress should be expected. And be explicit about where my API analogy is smuggling in metaphysics that doesn't belong in it.

One more thing: I write backend code for a living and have zero philosophy training, so please seperate out what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's still live and contested. If the experts disagree, say who disagrees about what instead of dressing up one side's view as the consensus answer.
