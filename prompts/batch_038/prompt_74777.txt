Just spent two hours on this with a friend and I've lost the the thread, so I need a third party. He thinks the following argument settles Humeanism about laws, and I can't tell whether it's a real refutation or a sleight of hand. Best reconstruction I can manage:

(1) Humeanism says the laws are nothing over and above a systematization of the total pattern of particular matters of fact — the mosaic comes first, and the laws are whatever the best deductive summary of it comes to. Nothing governs anything.
(2) So which laws are the laws of our world is fixed by the whole mosaic: two worlds that differ anywhere in the mosaic are worlds that can differ in their laws.
(3) Now consider a counterfactual about one electron: if its position at noon today had been a millimeter to the left, what would have followed?
(4) What makes that question worth asking is that we hold the laws fixed and let the downstream facts vary — same laws, different electron, different consequences.
(5) But by (2), any world where that electron is a millimeter to the left differs from ours in the mosaic and so differs in the laws, at least slightly. So on Humeanism there is no reading of (3) on which the laws stay put: every counterfactual, however trivial its antecedent, turns out to be a counterfactual about a change in the laws.
(6) A theory of laws that can't hold the laws fixed across any counterfactual isn't a theory of laws at all, since that holding-fixed is the entire job laws are supposed to do.
(7) Therefore (1) is false: there has to be something over and above the mosaic that stays put when particular facts don't.

Where does this go wrong — or, if it doesn't go wrong anywhere, which premise should the Humean actually be willing to give up, and what does giving it up cost them? I'd rather keep (4) and (6) than (1), since (1) is the thing I'm trying to defend, but I don't want that preference doing any work. And if the same reasoning would go through against some other claim of the form "X is nothing over and above the total pattern of such-and-such facts," tell me, because I have a feeling it would and I cant work out whether that's a reductio of the argument or a genuine discovery about supervenience.
