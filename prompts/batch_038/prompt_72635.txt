Right, I'm trying to pin down my own view on the agricultural revolution before I write anything public about it, and I'd like a proper consistency check rather than a lecture. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Foragers were broadly affluent — fewer hours of work, more varied diets, better bodies than the early farmers who came after them.
2. The switch to farming was a mistake: it made ordinary people worse off for millenia.
3. But nobody actually made that mistake. Each step (settling down, tending, then cultivation) was locally sensible, and I don't buy any story about a conquering farmer class forcing it on anyone.
4. What really mattered was storage, not cultivation — you only get entrenched inequality once a surplus keeps, and once it keeps someone can sit on it.
5. Forager societies were seriously egalitarian, and I'm not in the business of romanticising them either.
6. I don't trust the ethnographic analogy: today's foragers are the leftovers, pushed into deserts and rainforest, so you can't read them back into the past.
7. And I'm not sorry farming happened in the sense that I wouldn't trade modern medicine for anything.

Which of these genuinely pull against each other, as opposed to just looking awkward standing next to each other? I also want to know where I'm committed to something only because of evidence I can't personally check, so flag those separately.
