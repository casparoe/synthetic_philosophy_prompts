Our cognitive science reading group wants a diagnostic decision tree for the four-way scheme that sorts minds by what does the selecting: Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Please build it out of the logical space rather than out of the four labels — no more than six branch points, each phrased as a question an investigator could settle by observation or intervention without knowing which category it leads to, such as what becomes of the animal's repertoire if it is raised in isolation from its kind, whether a response it has never displayed can be reinforced into it, whether it behaves as though it has already tried an option before trying it, and whether it depends on devices, notations, or techniques that other individuals left lying around.

Then place these cases on the tree and say what each placement costs: an aphid; a pigeon on a variable-ratio schedule; a corvid that caches food and recovers it months later; an octopus, whose arms appear to solve local problems of their own; an ant colony that does things no ant does; a reinforcement-learning agent that was never evolved and has no body; a large language model trained on nothing but human writing; and a six-month-old infant.

I also want the corners of the space that nothing occupies — a Gregorian creature that is not Popperian, a Skinnerian one with no Darwinian ancestry behind it, whatever else you can construct — together with a verdict on each: empty because incoherent, because unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, name any pair of cases your tree cannot actually separate, and say whether the fault lies in the cases or in the taxonomy itself. Please answer in continuous prose, as you would in a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and describe the branches in sentences rather than drawing them.
