I'm giving a year to Carroll's tortoise, but before I read another proposed dissolution I want the specification, because the candidates I've seen declare success by incompatible standards. Write the list of requirements that any account of what licenses a deductive step must meet in order to count as a solution to the regress rather than a restatement of it.

For each requirement, give the consideration behind it and a concrete test. The list should include at least the following: a re-run test — whatever is nominated as the stopping point (a disposition, a normative status conferred by communal practice, a rule built into grasping the concepts involved), why can't the tortoise simply ask for the warrant for that, in the same tone it used before; a finite-mind constraint — the stopping point must be available to a thinker who cannot survey the consequences of what she already accepts; an error constraint — the account must leave room for someone to be wrong about which inferences her own commitments license, where being wrong is not merely a matter of a missing premise; the notebook datum — the tortoise grants each new conditional in turn and never grants the conclusion, so any account that explains inference by appeal to such granting owes a story about what granting D or E was; and two tests from the other side — a rule that is sound but strikes no thinker as compelling, and a rule that everyone finds compelling but is not truth-preserving.

Then tell me which of these are non-negotiable and which are prejudices of particular camps; whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable, since that would be the result I most want to see; and which single requirement disqualifies the most candidates. A few sentences scoring the three familiar families — dispositions, social-normative statuses, and meaning-constitutive rules — against the list is welcome. No citations; I'll add those myself.
