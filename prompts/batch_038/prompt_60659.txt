ok so I've been chewing on this for months and I want a story out of it, not an explainer.

Me and my brother get into it every holiday. He's convinced generations are just real — boomers are like this, gen z is like that, you meet someone and you can tell within five minutes. And I'm like, that's horoscopes with birth years, you drew a line somewhere arbitrary and now you're surprised the people on either side of it came out different. Except here's the part that wrecks my own argument: sometimes the cohort thing seems to actually do something to people. Graduate into a bad year and your wages are lower for twenty years, and that isn't a personality quirk, that's a fact about when you were 22. So maybe a generation isn't a kind of person at all — maybe it's more like a weather event that happens to a bunch of people at once. The event's real, the crowd that got soaked is real, but there's no such thing as a boomer soul sitting inside any of them. I can't get from there to anywhere though, and I dont know how you'd ever check which of us is right. That's the knot.

Write me a short story that works on the knot. But I don't want a realist thing where coworkers of different ages annoy each other, because that always collapses into jokes about phones and music. Here's the setup I want instead:

An unnamed country has a government office whose actual legal job is to draw the lines between generations. Not describe them, not study them — set them. And the lines have teeth: your cohort decides your school intake, your service year, which pension scheme you land in, possibly who gets called up. So somebody sits in a room and rules that everyone born before midnight on a certain date is one thing and everyone born after is another, and real lives bend around that decision.

Main character is a clerk in that office. Mid-level, not a hero, has done it for years, mostly bored. The plot: they're redoing an old boundary, and there's a queue of cusp cases — people born within a day or two of the line, petitioning to be moved to the other side. Make one petitioner the emotional center of the story, somebody with a genuinely strong reason (held back a year, family crossed a border, whatever you like) and give them a rival in the queue whose reason is much worse but somehow just as human.

And the creepy part I want in there, handled lightly and never spelled out: the clerk slowly notices that once a boundary is officially drawn, the data for the two new groups starts fitting the line better than it did before. Like the measurement tightens around the cut. Is the office's classification actually making the kinds, or are the clerks just counting differently now that a rule exists, or is it nothing at all — don't answer it. The clerk shouldn't know either.

Ground rules: invent the cohort names, don't use boomer/millennial/gen z anywhere inside the story, keep the worldbuilding dull and bureaucratic (forms in triplicate, a queue numbering system, a broken heater, a supervisor who only cares about throughput), and no character gets to make a speech explaining the philosophy. End on something small and human, not a reveal. Around 1500 to 2000 words.
