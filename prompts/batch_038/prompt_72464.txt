I run an argument-night at a bookshop once a month, about fourteen people, mostly not academics but they read, and next month we're doing Tolstoy's What Is Art?. I don't want the usual "he trashed Shakespeare therefore he was an idiot" forty minutes, so I've put together a scorecard of objections to the infection theory and I want the room to vote on how much damage each does before I show them mine. I need yours first, so I've got something to push against.

Here they are the way I've worded them for the handout:

1. **Channel.** Infection only takes if the receiver is already disposed to catch the feeling, so what the theory is really measuring is a fit between work and audience rather than a property of the work. Same piece, two people, one infected and one bored: which one gets to decide whether it's art?

2. **Sincerity is private.** Tolstoy makes sincerity the decisive condition of quality, but sincerity isn't visible from outside, and a forger who convinces everybody is indistinguishable from the sincere one. Either the criterion collapses into "it worked on us," or it's a claim about souls that no critic can check.

3. **Output refutes the author.** The theory ranks a village lament, or a parable about forgiveness, above most of what the theory's own author wrote, and he seems to have accepted the ranking. So either he's right and his life's work is second-rate, or he's wrong and the theory has just condemned its best piece of evidence.

4. **Form goes missing.** Nothing in the account explains why the transmission has to happen through this particualr arrangement of words or sounds rather than another. If all that matters is that the feeling arrives, a summary of the story should be as good as the story.

5. **Bad infection.** Lots of works transmit feeling with total clarity and sincerity and unite their audience around something vile. Tolstoy has a content filter for this — the religious perception of the age, the brotherhood of everyone — but the moment he needs it, the definition of art and the evaluation of art have come apart: the vile work is art, just bad art, which is not at all what the infection story sounded like when he first told it.

6. **Instrumental music.** A good deal of music transmits no feeling anyone could name before or afterwards, yet music is supposed to be the paradigm case of infection.

Score each one on two axes, 0 to 10: damage to infection theory as a *definition* of art, and damage to it as a *criterion of value*. Both numbers for every objection, then a single ranking of the six by overall damage, with a sentence or two each on what makes it land or not land. Then tell me which one I should put in front of the room first for maximum damage, and which one sounds devastating to amateurs but is actually the easiest for a Tolstoyan to answer — I want to be able to spring that one on them.

Last thing: give me a fraction, your honest estimate, of the chance the room walks out thinking the theory is worth keeping in some modified form rather than binning it.
