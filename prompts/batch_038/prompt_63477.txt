A friend and I spent most of an evening arguing about money pumps, and I have given up on settling it by argument. Her position: a pump is only ever a symptom of incoherent preferences — if your ranking over final bundles is well behaved, no sequence of individually acceptable trades can walk you back to where you began with less in your pocket. Mine: the same signature can be produced by an agent whose ranking is perfectly transitive at every moment, provided the person deciding at each step is in effect a different decision-maker who cannot bind the ones who come after, so that what fails is not coherence but foresight and commitment.

I teach an upper-level seminar on practical rationality — 18 students, one 75-minute session — and I am willing to lose about twelve dollars in quarters to the class in real stakes. Design a trading game in which the students run into the pump themselves before anyone names it; the terms money pump, Dutch book, and intransitive should not be spoken until the debrief. The mechanism should be exactly the one we were fighting over: each offer must look like a clean local gain when viewed on its own, and at least one arm of the class must be able to escape the loss only by committing in advance, so that the room can see whether the damage came from a defective ranking or from an inability to bind one's later self.

Give me exactly five numbered items, with no introduction or conclusion, each of one or two sentences — the offer sequence in item 2 may be a compact table if that reads better:

1. Materials, room layout, and how the class is divided into arms.
2. The exact sequence of offers and prices, with arithmetic a student could check on paper.
3. The commitment device available to one arm only, what it costs, and when it must be bought.
4. The debrief, mapping particular moments of the game onto the two rival diagnoses, saying which of us the room will conclude was right, and giving the student who refused the first trade something to say for herself.
5. What predictably goes wrong the first time I run it, and the one feature of the phenomenon this game cannot reproduce, which I will have to supply by hand.
