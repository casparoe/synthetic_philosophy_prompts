Here's my half-baked idea. The standing-to-blame debate has spent nearly all its oxygen on how you *lose* standing — hypocrites, tu quoque, pots and kettles — and I count at least three rival diagnoses of what actually goes wrong: something like (a) blaming commits you to a standard you're not holding yourself to, so the hypocrite's blame is internally inconsistent; (b) blame from a hypocrite is bad evidence, they've blown the credibility that made their blame worth hearing; (c) blaming someone for what you do yourself is a way of refusing to treat the two of you as moral equals. People adjudicate between these by trading intuitions about cases of losing standing, and honestly the intuitions are mushy — everyone agrees the hypocrite is doing *something* wrong and they disagree about what.

But the three accounts make sharply different predictions about what it takes to get standing *back*, and I don't think that question gets much airtime. On (a), sincere regret should restore it instantly and for free. On (b), you need a track record, because your future behaviour is what makes your blame informative again, and that can't be talked into existence. On (c), you have to submit to the demand you're making of the other person, which might mean eating the blame or the sanction yourself. Then there's the workaround I keep seeing in real life: get someone with clean hands to deliver the blame for you. If the defect is in the act, outsourcing cures it; if it's in the relationship, outsourcing makes it worse, since now you've also manipulated a third party.
