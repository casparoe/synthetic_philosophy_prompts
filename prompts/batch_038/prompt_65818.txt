I'm setting the take-home final for an upper-level seminar on agency and mechanism, and I need one essay question on sphexishness that can't be answered by just retelling the digger wasp story back at me. Hand the students a concrete spec to reason about — say, a hierachy of control routines in which each level can suspend the level below it and select among its alternatives, stacked arbitarily deep — and make the question turn on whether that nesting actually buys non-sphexishness or merely relocates it one level up. They should have to take a stand on whether flexibility is a property of the system alone or of the system-plus-environment (nothing ever goes wrong for the wasp inside the niche it actually lives in, after all), and on whether sphexishness has any bearing at all on the free will debate, given that wiring a noise generator into the wasp wouldn't make it one jot less sphexish.

Deliverables: the question itself, about 150 words, with the spec stated precisely enough to be argued against; a model answer of roughly 600 words; and a marking scheme totalling 20, built out of five must-hit moves — marks for each, what seperates an A answer from a B answer at that point, and one line on how students typically fumble it. 90 minutes, closed book.
