ok so slightly weird one, bear with me. I'm a backend engineer, zero philosophy background, but I've been reading up on Henry Sidgwick — the Victorian ethics guy who also co-founded and ran that Society for Psychical Research, i.e. the ghost-and-telepathy investigating club.

Here's the setup. I own the experimentation platform at a consumer app company, ~40M monthly users. Every feature ships behind an A/B test: pre-registered hypotheses, assignment from a seeded hash, hardware RNG, fully automated logging, and nobody on the team is allowed to look at the data until after the analysis freeze. So no file-drawer problem, no p-hacking, no grad student who really needs the result to be there.

I keep daydreaming about pointing that machinery at a precognition test. User plays a 200-trial guessing game, their guess gets locked and hashed before the target is drawn from the hardware RNG, and we collect a few million trials a day for free. I could run it for a year and publish whatever comes out, good or bad.

So, imagining Sidgwick alive and sitting across the table from me:

1. What does he actually say? Not "he'd be cautiously interested" — I want the specific objections and the specific credit, keyed to what he thought was wrong with the evidence for telepathy and mediumship in his own day. Was his problem a shortage of trials and better controls, in which case my platform is the answer, or was it something about testimony, observers, and conditions that a cleaner automated experiment doesn't touch at all? Would he even think my design is aimed at the right question?

2. He and his colleagues did at least one thing that looks a lot like what I'm describing — a big statistical sweep of ordinary people's reports rather than seances with a few famous mediums. Where would he put my app study relative to that, and would he think the numbers settle anything or just relocate the problem?

3. Now say it comes back positive: 51.3% hits against a 50% baseline over ~400M trials, and it survives twelve months of hostile replication attempts from people trying to kill it. Does Sidgwick get what he was after? My understanding is that psi results weren't the end goal for him, that there was something bigger he was hoping to get out of the whole enterprise. Tell me what the gap is between "statistically overwhelming weirdness" and whatever that thing was, and whether he'd say the result closes it, partially closes it, or doesn't touch it.

4. Bonus: how would he file the two phenomena I keep hearing about — effects that shrink the more you study them, and experiments that only work for certain experimenters and not others?

Feel free to push back on the premise of the whole thing if you think he'd laugh at it.
