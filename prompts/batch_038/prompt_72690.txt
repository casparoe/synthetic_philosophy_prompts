I'm TA-ing a senior bioethics seminar this term — we spent two weeks on the therapy/enhancement distinction (mostly the normal-functioning line of argument, plus the stuff about giftedness and hubris), and the prof has asked me to write a practice essay question for the revision session next week, partly so people stop panicking that the final will just ask them to summarise the debate. I want a question they genuinely can't answer by reciting the distinction, and I think I've got the seed of a case that does the job, but I'd like you to build the actual assessment around it.

Here's the case. A teaching hospital supplies its residents with free modafinil-type wakefulness drugs during 24-hour shifts. Measured against a well-rested adult, the drug pushes sustained attention and working memory above the species-typical range, which looks like enhancement; measured against a resident at hour twenty of a shift, it restores them to normal, which looks like therapy. The twist I want the question to turn on: the sleep deprivation is produced by the very institution handing out the drug, and shift length is fixed by the residency program's accreditation and coverage requirements, so the hospital can't just shorten the shifts and make the problem disappear. Also stipulate that the drug is safe, non-addictive, and chemically identical to one sold over the counter to well-rested undergrads as a study aid. So the classification of one and the same intervention seems to depend on a background condition the provider controls — and could be flipped at will by changing the schedule, or the reference class, or both.

The question should push students on what a non-manipulable baseline would have to look like, and on whether a distinction relativized to a chosen reference class can still do the two jobs people want it to do: drawing a funding line for a public insurer, and marking a difference in kind between healing and something else. It needs to be answerable in about 45 mintues and worth 25 marks. Please give me (a) the question exactly as it would appear on the paper, (b) a model answer of around 600 words in continuous prose, and (c) the marking scheme as exactly five numbered criteria, each one or two sentences saying what the criterion is, how many of the 25 marks it carries, and the specific mistake that should lose marks there. The five criteria are what should separate an A from a B, so each one has to name a move a top answer makes that a merely competent answer skips — e.g. noticing that the two jobs the distinction is asked to do can come apart in this case, or that fixing the background conditions is partly blocked by the stipulations. No intro, no wrap-up, no tips on running the revision session: just the question, the model answer, and the five numbered criteria.
