For thirty-eight years I worked on the stochastic kinetics of reacting systems: master equations, path probabilities, first-passage times, and the perennial gap between what an ensemble average says and what any single trajectory does. I retired three years ago and have taken up decision theory as an amateur, where I have met a puzzle I cannot get out of my head, and I want to know whether the vocabulary I actually think in can hold it.

The puzzle is the absent-minded driver, introduced by Piccione and Rubinstein in 1997. A driver sets out along a road with two intersections, X and then Y. Exiting at X pays 0; exiting at Y pays 4; continuing past both pays 1. The essential feature is that on arriving at an intersection he cannot tell X from Y and cannot remember whether he has already passed through one, so whatever rule he adopts applies at both, with a fresh randomization at each visit. Sitting down beforehand, he computes that continuing at each intersection with probability 2/3 — equivalently, exiting with probability 1/3 — maximizes his expected payoff, at 4/3. But once he is at an intersection the arithmetic seems to shift: conditional on being at an intersection at all, he is at X with probability 3/5 and at Y with probability 2/5, since he reaches Y only by having continued at X, and exiting on the spot now yields an expectation of 8/5, which exceeds what his own plan delivers. The literature calls this a paradox about imperfect recall, and one standard resolution, due to Aumann, Hart and Perry, is to treat the driver as a collection of two agents who take one another's behaviour as given.

Please restate the whole business in my language, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report. In particular:

1. Cast the road as a state space with a single coarse-grained observation cell containing both intersections; the driver's plan as the dynamics that generates a measure over trajectories; and the payoff as a functional of where a trajectory terminates. Rederive both of my numbers, 2/3 and 4/3, in that notation, and state explicitly what the coarse-graining has destroyed.

2. Say what the action-stage calculation is in my terms. Those weights of 3/5 and 2/5 are conditional on an observation whose statistics are produced by the very dynamics being evaluated. Tell me whether the quantity the driver computes at the intersection is an average over trajectories at all; if it is an average over some other ensemble, identify that ensemble and say whether it corresponds to any arrangement that could physically occur, or only to a counterfactual one.

3. Tell me whether the self-consistency the resolution demands is a fixed-point problem of a kind I already know — mean-field theory, self-consistent screening, that family — or structurally different from it, and if different, precisely where the analogy fails.

4. Then the residue, item by item: which claims in this debate translate cleanly into my vocabulary; which can be carried over only by bending one side or the other; and which have no expression in it whatsoever. I have in mind especially "he has acquired no new information," "the plan binds him," "he is one agent and not two," and the word rational, which appears to be doing work that consistent does not do.

5. Close with your verdict. Is the residue a defect of my framework — physics having no room for something that selects its own dynamics and is afterwards answerable to the selection — or is it evidence that the decision theorists are quarreling over a bookkeeping error about which measure to condition on, and draping it as a paradox?

Two conditions. Do not quote the papers at me and do not append a reading list; I own the originals, and I want your reconstruction rather than your references. Work entirely from my description above, and if that description is mistaken on some point that bears on the argument, correct it before you translate anything. Above all, keep me honest: if at any stage you find yourself importing a decision-theoretic assumption and labelling it a translation, say so at that point rather than letting it pass silently into the physics.
