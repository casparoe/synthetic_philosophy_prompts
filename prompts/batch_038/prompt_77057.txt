I am drafting a chapter arguing that costly-signaling theory generalizes from animal displays to human institutions, and in support of it I have built an argument whose conclusion I could not defend in public. I would like a diagnosis rather than encouragement.

The argument, step by step:

(1) A signal is honest — reliably correlated with the underlying state it is taken to indicate — only if sending it costs a signaler who lacks that state more than it costs a signaler who has it. Cost is what makes dishonesty unprofitable.

(2) Reliability is therefore monotonic in the relevant cost differential: the more it costs the unqualified to go through with the display, the more informative the display is.

(3) Cost, on this account, is currency-neutral. Anything the signaler genuinely gives up — money, time, pain, sleep, health, forgone opportunities, social standing — counts toward the differential, and one currency may be substituted for another at the signaler's own exchange rate.

(4) Institutions that gate membership or credential on ordeals — hazing, brutal qualifying examinations, unpaid apprenticeships, strict dietary and sexual codes, celibacy, tithing at a fixed tenth, basic training, initiations — are signaling systems of exactly this kind.

(5) So the reliability of such a gate rises with the wastefulness of what it demands. This is why strict, demanding religious communities retain members better than permissive ones, and why a doctorate earned through an ordeal is a better credential than one earned by coursework.

(6) Consequently an institution designing a filter should maximize the cost of entry, and there is no upper bound on the cost that improves reliability. In particular, requiring each applicant to destroy a large sum of money — burned in public, with no skill, endurance, or knowledge involved — is a strictly better filter than requiring a year of supervised work, because the destruction is more purely costly: it buys the applicant nothing, and so cannot be quietly enjoyed by someone faking.

(7) It follows that cheap substitutes for costly gates — notarized pledges, third-party verification, credit and criminal records, references, probationary periods — cannot be honest signals at all, since by (1) they lack the requisite differential cost, and so every institution that adopts them has adopted a filter that selects for nothing.

I cannot accept (6) or (7). (6) implies that burning money outperforms relevant work as a test of competence, which is absurd on its face and which my own case material appears to contradict. (7) implies that contract law and background checks are inert, which is simply false.

I would much rather keep (1) than surrender it: the differential-cost condition is load-bearing for the entire chapter, and if it fails as a necessary condition I have to start over. If the argument must be trimmed, I would prefer to trim (3) or (6).

Please tell me: which single step or transition, if rejected, stops the argument at the least cost to the remainder; whether the failure you identify is a matter of invalid inference or of a false premise, since the two call for different repairs; why that step looked unobjectionable when I wrote it, including any ambiguity or slide in the notion of cost that it exploits; what rejecting it costs me elsewhere, and in particular whether (1) survives intact, survives weakened, or goes; and what (6) would have to become in order to be true — that is, under what conditions wastefulness really does outperform usefulness in a filter, if any. Then rank the competing exits from this argument by how much of the chapter each one destroys, and if more than one step independently breaks the chain, say so and identify the more principled point of failure rather than merely the first one.

Write in American English. You may draw on the formal signaling literature as you understand it — the handicap debates, the distinction between indices and handicaps, cheap-talk models, the role of the receiver's response rule — but argue from the structure of the steps above rather than from authority, and do not supply a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I want the reasoning, not the references.
