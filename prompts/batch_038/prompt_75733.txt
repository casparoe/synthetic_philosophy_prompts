I'm circulating a position statement at an alignment workshop and would like a consistency audit of my own view before I defend it in public. My commitments on defining low impact without first solving value specification:

(1) The central claim: avoiding side effects is a problem that can be posed and substantially solved without a full specification of value. Impact minimization is prior to, and independent of, knowing what we want.

(2) Impact is baseline-relative: it is the difference between the state distribution the agent actually brings about and the one it would have brought about had it taken a null action — never started, or been switched off at the outset. Selecting that baseline is a technical modeling decision, not a normative one.

(3) What makes a difference from the baseline count is not that it alters something we care about, but that it closes off futures. The penalty should be defined over the agent's own capacity to bring about a broad, arbitrarily sampled set of states of the world — how much harder each of a large collection of auxiliary goals becomes. Because those goals are arbitrary and equally weighted, no value function enters the measure.

(4) Trivial effects must carry zero penalty: rearranging molecules nobody would notice, shifting a pebble a few centimeters. A measure that scores these alongside genuine harms is not cautious but paralyzed.

(5) The penalty enters the objective as a term traded off against task reward at a fixed exchange rate, so the agent acts whenever the task is worth more than the impact it causes.

(6) Even so, some effects are absolutely off-limits — irreversibly destroying the only record of something, permanently disabling human oversight — and no quantity of task reward may buy them.

Which of these pull against each other, and how badly? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally damaging. I expect at least one pair that looks contradictory but isn't, and I'd rather you say so plainly than pad the list. For each genuine tension, give the minimal revision that removes it and what that revision costs me — in particular, whether it forces a concession on (1), since that is the claim the whole statement is built around. Also say what my view predicts about agent behavior that a frankly value-dependent impact measure does not, if the difference is behaviorally detectable at all. Finally: if I could revise only one commitment and buy the most consistency with it, which should it be? Don't try to guess which one I'd be most willing to surrender; I want the audit, not a prediction about me.
