I'm retired — thirty-five years doing low-energy scattering experiments at a national lab — and I've only recently gotten into philosophy of science, mostly for fun. Our little reading group of ex-engineers and ex-physicists keeps tangling itself up over underdetermination, so I'd like you to write us a decision tree: start at "you grant that more than one theory can fit all the data" and walk down to a named position.

Nodes should be questions a person could answer without knowing where they lead — no "are you a constructive empiricist?" Keep it to about six or seven branch points. I want these leaves at a minimum: Quinean holism, van Fraassen's constructive empiricism, the Laudan–Leplin deflation, Stanford's unconceived-alternatives argument, and the flat denial that any genuine equivalence ever obtains (give that last one a name if it has one). Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate, and list the answer-combinations that come out unoccupied, with a line each on whether they're empty because the view is incoherent, because it's unattractive, or because nobody's bothered to go there yet.

Then run two cases down the tree and show me the path. First, phase-equivalent potentials in inverse scattering: same phase shifts, hence same cross sections at every energy, different radial shapes — the equivalence is exact, but it only survives as long as you leave out bound-state information and off-shell behavior. Second, one of my own that I think is the more interesting: a detector whose efficiency curve is uncertain by a couple percent, theory A needing no correction, theory B needing a correction that sits squarely inside that band, and no amount of better calibration ever settling it because the calibration is itself model dependant. Tell me whether these two land in the same place, and if not, which node does the separating.

Last thing: redo or annotate the whole tree for the stricter reading where equivalence means equivalence to all possible evidence rather than to the evidence we can actually go and get. My hunch is that half the leaves collapse together under that reading and the real fight moves somewhere else — say so plainly if I'm wrong.
