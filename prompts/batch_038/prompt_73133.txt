Construct a thought experiment to test a specific claim about competence and comprehension — the claim, familiar from the inversions of reasoning that Darwin's and Turing's work invite, that a system can be reliably excellent at a task while understanding nothing about it, with comprehension at most a downstream report on a process that never needed it. I want the case built in the direction people rarely build it: not a system whose competence survives the absence of understanding, but a system whose competence is destroyed by acquiring it, where the destruction is not a matter of processing cost, distraction, hesitation, or second-guessing, but of the understanding itself underdetermining or corrupting the action it is supposed to guide.

Constraints:

- A real professional setting — a craft, a clinical judgment, a negotiation, an edit, a diagnosis — with no science-fictional apparatus, no homunculi, no uploaded minds, and no recognizable paraphrase of Searle's room.
- Two matched agents, identical in training history, opportunity, and every disposition you are willing to specify, differing only in whether the understanding is present, so that the difference in performance follows from that one variable alone.
- At most two paragraphs for the scenario itself.
- The mechanism of failure must be stateable entirely in terms of what the agents do and what happens to the work, not in terms of what it is like to be them.

Then demonstrate that the case meets the specifications: say exactly what the understanding consists in, and why having it makes the agent worse rather than merely slower or less confident. Identify which feature of the case does the work, and what happens to the verdict if that feature is removed. Finally, give the strongest reply available to someone who thinks the design is a cheat — that what you have supplied is not understanding but a false or overly explicit theory of the task, and that a genuine understanding would have improved performance rather than degraded it — and say honestly whether that reply defeats your case or merely relocates the dispute.
