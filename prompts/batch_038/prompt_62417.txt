So, quick background: my husband and I are being assessed at Newcastle for mitochondrial donation. I carry a mutation that killed our first son before his second birthday, and the plan is they take the nucleus out of some healthy donor's egg and put mine in, so the baby ends up with her mitochondria and our DNA. I've been going through the rules and the leaflets they gave us and something isn't adding up, and I want someone to work out with me what the whole setup actually assumes.

Here's the bits puzzling me. The regulations insist the nuclear DNA has to come from my egg specifically — they name me in the approval — but the egg it gets dropped into can be any suitable donor's, and the donor stays completely anonymous. Our child could never find out who she is, not even at 18, whereas a kid in this country conceived with donated sperm or a donated egg can get the donor's name from the regulator once they turn 18. Our child is also legally not related to the mitochondrial donor at all, nor to any other children born using her eggs. The official reasoning as far as I can make out is that giving mitochondria is supposed to be more like giving blood or a kidney than like giving an egg. Meanwhile I got my DNA tested over Christmas and the company has told me my direct maternal line is defined by exactly the mitochondrial DNA that would be swapped out, and my genetics team treats my condition as a family history running down my mother's side — which is definately not how the fertility people talk about it. And they keep telling us the baby will still be our own genetic child, as though swapping the mitochondria can't make it a different person.

So: what theory of where a person comes from does all this presuppose? Someone on a forum mentioned an old argument that you couldn't have come from a different sperm or egg, and it struck a nerve. Is that sort of assumption actually load-bearing for these rules, or could they run on something more mundane — traceability, not wanting hundreds of kids turning up on one woman's doorstep, keeping the donor pool supplied? I'd like you to separate what the regime strictly commits us to from what it merely sits comfortably with, say whether the anonymity rule and the insistence on using my egg are consistent with each other, and whether there's any coherent view on which mitochondrial DNA counts as my maternal lineage for ancestry and medicine but not for parenthood. Then tell me what would have to change — the consent forms, the counselling, the whole three-parent baby framing, the right to know at 18 — if the view it presupposes turned out to be wrong, and what it would look like instead if you replaced it with one where origins matter by degrees. That last bit matters to me because a small amount of my own mitochondria gets carried over whatever they do, so the child wouldn't be purely anyone's.
