I want the desiderata first, before I read any of the actual proposals: what does a rule for how much to stake on a repeatable, multiplicative bet have to deliver? Give me the properties any adequate account of bet-sizing has to have, each with the case or consideration behind it. I'm after the ones people tend to skip -- what it says when the bet can only be taken once, when the horizon is long but finite rather than infinite, when you dont know the true odds and only have an estimate of them, whether it has to be dynamically consistent (what you'd stake now vs. what you'd stake after a win, with no reason to want to revise the plan), and what it owes someone who says they don't care about their wealth in 40 years, only about the next decade. The bar I'm setting is "good enough to hand to something that actually has to survive," not "an elegant account of rationality in the abstract."

Settle one thing along the way. Some people claim that maximizing the long-run growth rate of your wealth is a utility-free standard -- that it makes subjective utility functions unnecessary because it falls out of the dynamics of the gamble itself. Others say that's log utility wearing a trenchcoat. Genuine fork or verbal dispute, and which of your desiderata decides it?

Then flag any pair of requirements you think can't both be satisfied, and name the single one that eliminates the most candidate rules. Don't survey the literature unless some particular entry is doing work on some particular desideratum.
