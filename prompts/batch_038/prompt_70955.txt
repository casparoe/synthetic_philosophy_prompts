Two members of a reading group spent half an hour arguing about al-Ghazālī's fire-and-cotton case and then asked me to referee. I couldn't decide, so I'm passing it on. The exchange went as follows.

(1) Nadia opened by saying the dispute is verbal. Al-Ghazālī keeps every regularity — cotton still burns on contact with fire, and we predict it just as well as before. All he drops is the claim that the connection holds by necessity rather than by a settled custom of God's, and that changes nothing about the world.

(2) Omar said it is not verbal but modal, and modality is where the content lives. For al-Ghazālī the fire is inert and does nothing at all; the burning is explained only by God's creating it then and there. That is what makes miracles intelligible — God can withhold the burning with no change in the fire — and a causal realist cannot say that.

(3) Nadia replied that if God creates burning whenever fire touches cotton, this is the realist's thesis with an extra clause attached. The regularity, not the divine act, does all the predictive work, and no observation could ever tell the two views apart, so the metaphysics is idle.

(4) Omar said idle is not the same as empty. The claim is untestable but not vacuous: it rules out a class of explanations, such as that cotton burns because burning belongs to fire's nature, and ruling out explanations is substantive work. Besides, that the future will resemble the past is untestable too, and nobody calls it vacuous.

(5) Nadia pressed that this concedes her point. Al-Ghazālī still owes an account of why the custom holds rather than lapses, and if the answer is that nothing makes it hold, he has admitted a brute regularity — exactly what the realist says about causation. The positions collapse into each other.

(6) Omar said they collapse only if you assume a view is substantive just in case some observation could distinguish it, which is the standard al-Ghazālī's side rejects. And the collapse isn't total: the realist must take nature's uniformity as brute, while al-Ghazālī traces it to a rational agent who keeps His custom.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the six steps from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and justify each rating in a sentence or two. Then say who is more right overall, and identify what both of them are missing — including whether either has correctly characterized what al-Ghazālī is actually denying.
