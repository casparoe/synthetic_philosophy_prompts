Here is the relevant passage:

> Ellsberg's urn is usually described as the experiment that refuted subjective expected utility, and the description is fair. One urn, ninety balls: thirty are red, and the other sixty are black or yellow in a proportion nobody tells you. Offer someone a hundred dollars if they draw red, or a hundred dollars if they draw black, and they take red — it is the one number they were actually given. Then offer a hundred dollars for red-or-yellow, or a hundred for black-or-yellow, and the same people take black-or-yellow. Write down what those two choices imply about their beliefs and you get a contradiction: no assignment of probabilities to the three colors makes both choices optimal, at any utility function and at any stake. The sure-thing principle, the load-bearing axiom of Savage's edifice, is what fails — and it fails for a reason that has nothing to do with risk. These people are perfectly willing to gamble; what they refuse to do is gamble against an unknown.
>
> Two consequences follow, and both are usually missed. First, the standard repair job — put a prior over your priors and treat ambiguity as simply more probability — buys nothing. The moment you take expectations, the second layer collapses back into a single first-order probability, so a decision maker with second-order beliefs behaves exactly like an ordinary Bayesian. If you want ambiguity aversion, you have to give up additivity outright. Second, this is not a bias to be corrected out of people. It is the right response to missing information, and it is the response our models ought to have: when you cannot characterize the data-generating process, hedge against the worst case over the distributions you cannot rule out. The theorists who wrote down those axioms were describing what a careful agent does, and Ellsberg showed that careful agents are precisely the ones who decline to guess. Note also that a person whose choices cannot be represented by any probability at all can be pumped for money by anyone patient enough to offer them the right sequence of bets — which is exactly the exposure a robust objective removes.
