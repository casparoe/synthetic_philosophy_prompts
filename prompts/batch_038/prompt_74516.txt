I am a third-year philosophy PhD student co-coordinating a pre-registered, multi-site replication-and-extension study of the side-effect effect: the asymmetry whereby a large majority of participants judge that a corporate chairman who announces he does not care about harming the environment and only wants profits thereby *intentionally* harms it, while a much smaller minority, presented with the matched help version of the same scenario, judge that he intentionally helps it. Before we lock the protocol I have to hand my advisor a specification of what an explanation of this effect must deliver — not a survey of the explanations on offer, which I have already read, but the criteria those explanations are being asked to satisfy. Every paper I have worked through simply assumes its own success conditions: the ones treating the asymmetry as an artifact of probe wording think a theory succeeds by dissolving it, the ones treating it as evidence that moral evaluation contaminates descriptive concepts think a theory succeeds by tracing the contamination further, and nobody states which of these ambitions is a genuine requirement and which is the preference of a camp. Two of my lab-mates are refusing to run the study at all until someone tells them what would count as an adequate account, so the specification needs to be self-standing.

Write that desiderata list, with the purpose fixed as follows: an adequate theory is one good enough to serve as the theoretical core of a pre-registered experimental program, which means above all that it must license predictions made in advance about vignettes nobody has tested, and must say where the asymmetry should shrink, vanish, or reverse rather than only where it appears. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as non-negotiable or as belonging to a particular camp — the conceptual-analysis program, the pragmatic camp, the "it is all performance error" camp, and so forth. Please sort them into the categories that actually matter here: the phenomena to be explained, including the ones people forget, such as the absence of any comparable asymmetry for the chairman's main goal and the way the pattern travels to neighbouring ascriptions like "was in favour of" or "decided to"; the distinctions a theory may not collapse, such as competence versus performance, a concept versus one judgment applying it, the valence of an outcome versus the agent's own attitude toward that outcome, and blame versus intentional action; the empirical results it must be consistent with, spanning developmental, cross-linguistic, and clinical findings along with order and framing effects and effect sizes that are modest and sometimes unstable; and the philosophical prices it must be willing to pay, for instance to the role "intentionally" plays in explaining action. Interleave four variants we drafted ourselves for the battery and which I could not find in the literature, saying for each which requirement it probes and what pattern a candidate theory should predict before seeing any data: (1) *Cost-to-self* — the program will damage the chairman's own health twenty years hence, and he says he does not care about that and only wants this year's profits; (2) *Exogenous indifference* — the standard harm case, except that his indifference to the environmental damage is stipulated to be a documented consequence of a degenerative condition affecting his evaluative responses, of which he is unaware; (3) *Agentless probe* — the harm case, but the test question names no agent and asks only whether the environment was intentionally damaged; (4) *Avoidable cost* — the harm case, except that the chairman had commissioned a report showing the damage could have been prevented for a fee smaller than the profit increase, and he declined to pay it.

Then give me the two things I most want: which single requirement eliminates the most contenders, and whether some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. I have a suspicion that a theory which explains the asymmetry by having moral evaluation infect the application of a descriptive term cannot simultaneously preserve the intuition surveys that generated the data as evidence about that term — that the account undercuts its own evidential basis — and I would like to know whether that tension is real or dissolvable, since if it is real, the list itself is a result. Close with no more than a line or two per family of explanation (moral-infusion, deep-self or expression-of-character, missing-competence-condition, pragmatic-implicature, blame-seeking, affective-artifact) naming the requirement each fails first. No bibliography, no citation list, no suggested reading; a table is fine for the requirements themselves.
