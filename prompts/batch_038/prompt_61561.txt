For six years I have been working out, on my own and without any philosophy training, an account of how God can know future free choices without causing them; I later learned the label for the crucial piece is middle knowledge. In three weeks I present it for forty minutes at my congregation's Wednesday theology forum, followed by questions. The room divides into three groups. About a dozen are from the elder circle and hold a meticulous-providence view: they will press on where the truth of a counterfactual about a free choice comes from, and on whether God's knowledge of it really is prior to His decree. A handful came out of an open-theist background and think God simply does not know future free decisions; they will ask what is left of freedom if the answer was already settled. The rest have absorbed little beyond a free-will defense of prayer and evangelism and hold no strong position.

Here is my argument as I plan to give it. (1) Before deciding to create, God knows of every possible free creature what it would freely choose in any situation He might place it in; these conditionals are true independently of any divine decision, so God does not make them true. (2) He therefore selects which creatures to bring into existence and which situations to put them in, and the whole of history follows, yet He never intervenes in a single choice. (3) My own addition, which I have not seen defended anywhere: God knows these conditionals because He knows the character He would give each creature, and that character is what settles what the creature would do; the creature remains free because the character is its own. (4) Prayer, evangelism, and moral responsibility all survive.

Give me the objections I will actually receive from each of these groups, in their vocabulary, ordered by how much damage they would do; for each, a reply I can deliver in about two minutes from the front of the room, and a candid note on where that reply is thinnest. Flag anything in step (3) that invites an attack I have not anticipated, and close with the question I should be hoping nobody asks.
