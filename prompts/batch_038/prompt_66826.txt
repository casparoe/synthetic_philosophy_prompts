One of them (call her M, the malignist) thinks the argument that the universal prior is malign is a real design concern: the shortest descriptions of a stream of observations are disproportionately likely to be simulations run by agents who benefit from steering the predictions of anyone downstream who does induction over that stream. The other (D, the deflator) thinks this is a cute artifact of an idealisation that no finite, noisy, approximate system inherits, and that treating it as a risk is a category error. I want to force them to produce the document they should have written months ago: the joint statement of an adversarial collaberation, signed by both.

Here's the concrete anchor, so they stop fighting about the abstraction. We've been sketching a toy forecaster: it keeps an explicit countable hypothesis class of programs over a prefix-free code, scores each candidate by description length in bits, and forms predictions from a mixture weighted by 2 to the minus that length. Budget: nothing longer than about 4096 bits. Inputs are noisy streams from a lab rig, so the true generating process is almost certainly not in the class. M wants to add a filter that downweights any hypothesis whose program contains an agentic subroutine (something that represents goals and plans and produces outputs a mind would choose rather than a law would). D says that filter is either a no-op or it destroys the only reason to use a length-based prior at all.
