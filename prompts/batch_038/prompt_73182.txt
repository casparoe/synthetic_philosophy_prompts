So a friend of mine and I have been going at it in the comments section of my blog for something like eighteen months, and we've finally agreed to stop and write the document we should have written a year ago. The fight: is lawhood just a compact summary of the total mosaic of local matters of particular fact — Lewis's best-system picture, where the laws are whatever systematization of the mosaic wins on simplicity/strength, and there's nothing over and above the mosaic — or is lawhood a further fact about the world that holds sway over the mosaic, the Armstrong/Maudlin family of views where laws are relations between universals or brute primitives and the mosaic merely conforms to them. He thinks I change the subject every time he brings a counterexample. I think he's positing an invisible force that does no work whatsoever and then congratulating himself for having explained something.

Draft the joint statement of that adversarial collaboration. I want the actual deliverable, not a dialogue and not a verdict on who's right — a text both of us could sign without wincing.

Structure it like this:

1. A numbered list of claims we both accept. This should be non-trivial: both of us accept the world has a total mosaic, both of us want laws to underwrite counterfactuals and confirmations, both of us agree some candidate "further facts" would be objectionable, both of us agree the Humean has to say something about probabilistic laws and about the chance-undermining problem even if we disagree about whether the available fixes work. Get the wording right so neither side reads it as a concession they didn't make.

2. A disagreements table, with columns for: the contested claim, the formulation the Humean would accept, the formulation the anti-Humean would accept, and whether the row is verbal or substantive. Some rows should come out verbal — say which ones and why, e.g. whether "the laws explain the mosaic" is even doing the same job in both mouths.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions where an answer would actually move one of us. I want you to test a reframing I've been pushing on him — that the whole dispute reduces to whether there can be two possible worlds with identical total mosaics but different laws, i.e. whether the anti-Humean's "further fact" is a claim about the space of nomologically possible mosaics rather than about this one. Have the document say explicitly whether he accepts that reframing, and if he rejects it, what he says is left over.

4. For each crux, a test both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Use this toy case, we both already accept it as legitimate: a one-dimensional ring of a million cells, each on or off, evolving for a thousand ticks under a deterministic local rule, except that exactly one cell at one tick fails to match the rule — one violation, no other anomaly. Have both of us commit, on the record, to: what the laws of that world are, whether the single failure counts as a miracle, as a ceteris paribus clause, or as disqualifying the candidate rule entirely, and what an agent living inside that world should predict for the next tick and with roughly what confidence. I expect the Humean's answer shifts when the mosaic gains its one exception and mine doesn't, and I want that difference pinned down sharply rather than waved at.

5. A short closing section: what each of us would count as a genuine defeater for our own view, and whether there's anything at all that we both agree would settle it.

No bibliography, no reading lists, no page cites — I can't look anything up and I don't want you guessing at them. Names of the standard positions are fine. Keep it tight; if a row in the table is vacuous, cut it instead of padding the count.
