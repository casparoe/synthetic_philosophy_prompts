I'm TA-ing a first-year graduate seminar on persistence this term and it's gone sideways in a way I think is actually interesting. Two of the students — one a committed endurantist who's perfectly happy to say objects are multilocated, i.e. exactly located at each moment of their career and wholly present at every one of them, the other a worm theorist who thinks that's just incoherent and that objects are spacetime worms with temporal parts — have spent ten weeks talking past each other. Neither of them ever actually states the other's view in a form the other would sign, and the professor has asked me to put together a handout that's basically the document they should have produced months ago. So: write the joint statement that these two could both put their names to after a genuine adversarial collaboration, not a strawman version of either side.

Here's what I need in it. First a numbered section of claims both can sign, and I want these to be substantive rather than trivial — stuff about what the location vocabulary is doing (what 'wholly present at t' even means if you're not allowed to use 'has all its parts at t' in the analysis), what would have to be true for the temporary intrinsics problem to be a real problem at all, whether both sides owe an account of when a series of momentary stages adds up to one thing, what they agree physics does and doesn't settle for them. Then a disagreements table: for each remaining disagreement, the statement in neutral wording, plus how each side would phrase its own position in the words it prefers, plus a flag saying whether you think that particular row is a genuine disagreement or one that's really about words — and if it's verbal, which words are doing the work.

The cruxes are the part I care most about, and I have some guesses I'd like you to work with rather than replace. One is the lonely homogeneous disc case: two perfectly homogeneous discs, alone in the universe, one spinning on its axis and one sitting still. Each of my students insists this refutes the other, and I suspect the real crux underneath is whether that scenario is metaphysically possible at all, and whether instantaneous temporal parts can carry velocity in the first place or whether you need extended ones for the spinning/still distinction to be representable. A second crux is whether 'exists at' can be vague, and what the sorites-style argument from the vagueness of composition actually establishes — does it force unrestricted diachronic composition and hence four-dimensionalism, or does it only show that the endurantist needs a story about precision somewhere? A third is whether the whole dispute is partly about how well our quantifiers carve reality. For each crux I want the case or argument both sides agree bears on it, each side's prediction written down in advance (so we can go back in a year and see who moved), and what each side would accept as a reason to abandon their own view rather than dig in. Don't bother with a bibliography, we'll add citations to the handout ourselves — I just don't want invented ones.
