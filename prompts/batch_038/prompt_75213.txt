I run a small reading group on philosophy of cosmology, and before we argue about whether string theory is confirmed I want the bar written down: what does an account of non-empirical confirmation have to actually deliver before it counts as doing something other than rebranding theoretical virtues under a fancier name?

The wrinkle I want the list built around, because nobody in the group ever raises it: say a theory racks up non-empirical support over a couple of decades — no serious rival has surfaced, bits of it hang together in ways nobody designed in, the standing arguments against it keep dissolving. Then someone produces a rival that covers all the same ground. Did that accumulated support evaporate, or is it still on the books, just misleading? An adequate account has to pick, and has to pick without making earlier support either weightless or unrevisable — otherwise it isn't confirmation of anything, it's a mood.

So, the requirements: which cases it has to get right, which distinctions it can't collapse (start with confirmation versus justification, and with "there are no alternatives" as a claim about the space of theories rather than about how hard people looked), and what it has to stay consistent with about how science revises its own mind. Tell me which of these are non-negotiable and which are really just one camp's taste, and flag any pair you think can't both be met. Especially: can the monotonicity problem and the demand that the support be non-trivial be satisifed together, or does meeting one cost you the other?

Don't bother scoring existing views against the list — I'll do that live in session.

Format, in this order: "What the account is for" (~150 words), "Non-negotiable" (~600 words, one requirement per paragraph with the case behind it), "Camp taste" (~250 words), "The squeeze" (~300 words) — argue for the impossibility, don't just assert it.
