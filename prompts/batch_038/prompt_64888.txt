I'm a postdoc in ecology, 34, and I have to decide something in the next month that my advisor thinks is obvious. Option A is a normal three-year project on how a certain ground beetle handles drought — four papers, probably, and a real shot at a lectureship. Option B is taking over a long-running monitoring dataset, something like sixty years of counts from a chain of lakes, that nobody wants because the guy who built it retired and the funding is a mess. B won't produce anything publishable for eight years, maybe ten. Its actual value is that someone in 2075 will be able to ask questions of it that we can't even formulate yet.

My advisor says B is career suicide and he's probably right about that part. But that's not what's got me stuck.

What's got me stuck is a thought experiment I ran on myself last week. I imagined being told, defintely and by someone I fully trust, that a rock hits in 2080 and takes out everything — I'd be long dead either way, my parents would be dead, nobody I love would live to notice. And I noticed option B go flat instantly. Not tragic, just... empty, like filling out a form nobody will read. Whereas option A felt almost exactly as worth doing as it did before. Same pattern with a bunch of other stuff: learning Portuguese, planting walnut trees at my folks' place, scanning and writing up my grandmother's letters. All flat. But "finish the beetle paper" — unaffected.

Then my housemate tried to break the experiment with his own version, and I still can't answer him. He said: forget the rock. Suppose instead that in 2080 everything survives — people, cities, the species, all fine and happy — but every record and every memory of the past gets wiped, so the people alive in 2085 are just like us in every respect except they have no connection at all to anything anyone did before. He said if B still feels empty in that version, then whatever my reaction is tracking, it isn't future people existing. I couldnt tell him whether it does feel empty, and that's the part that bugs me, because I genuinely don't know what my own hesitation is pointing at.

So two things. First: what should I actually do about the job? Second, and more importantly — is my reaction to the rock evidence that B is the worse choice, or is it evidence about something in me that I should be careful about trusting? Because there's clearly a difference between what survives the rock test and what doesn't, and I can't tell if that difference tracks value or just tracks me. A feels more robust, but I can't shake the worry that "robust under extinction" and "worth doing" are pulling apart in a way that says something unflattering about A rather than something damning about B.

And if the two versions of the thought experiment really do come apart, tell me which one I should be taking seriously and why.
