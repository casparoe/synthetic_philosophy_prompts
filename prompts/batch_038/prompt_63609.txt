So, three weeks from now I'm giving a colloquium talk at a history department — I'm technically in a philosophy program but the talk is about historical method, and I want to walk in prepared because my thesis is going to annoy pretty much every corner of that room in a different direction.

The thesis. I think the standard objectivity fight — Ranke's old formula about showing how it essentially was on one side, Carr and Hayden White on the other — is stuck, and it's stuck because both sides assume the thing you assess for objectivity is a whole narrative or a whole book. I want to argue you should assess it one level down instead, at what I'm calling warrant-bearing claims: claims you can't deny without simultaneously undermining the documentary practices that make your own research possible. So "this memorandum was produced by this office in this year and entered this registry" is warrant-bearing in a way that "the memorandum shows the leadership intended X" plainly isn't. My claim is that there's a real and non-trivial objectivity at that lower level, and that White's tropic analysis never touches it, because tropes and emplotment operate on the narrative shape. But — and this is the part I need help defending — the lower level isn't a Rankean foundation either. It's institutional. It's made out of habits of citation, of finding aids, of who was allowed to write things down, not out of the historian's self-abnegation. I also want to say something that'll sound arrogant: that warrant is partly self-applying, because the same practices that warrant claims about the past also warrant claims about the practices themselves.

The room: maybe 25 people. Mostly social historians. Two cultural historians who cite White approvingly and will hate being told their scepticism is confined to a level. One economic historian who does quantitative work and thinks this whole debate is navel-gazing. A couple of grad students on colonial archives, one of whom is writing about how the archive was produced by the people doing the violence. No philosophers at all. I get 40 minutes and 20 of questions.
