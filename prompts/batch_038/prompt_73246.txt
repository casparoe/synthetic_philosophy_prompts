I am the instructor for an upper-level undergraduate course in the philosophy of mind, and I would like a second opinion on one final-exam answer before I finalize grades. Below are the question exactly as it was posed and one student's response, unedited. Please grade the response and write the comments that justify the grade.

QUESTION 4 (25 marks). Douglas Hofstadter's careenium is an imaginary slab of substance whose microscopic constituents — he calls them simmballs — are spherical particles, each bearing a symbol. When a simmball is triggered, it careens through the slab, colliding with other simmballs and triggering them in turn. The slab admits of two descriptions: a careenium-level one, given in terms of particles, trajectories, and collisions; and a symbol-level one, given in terms of concepts activating concepts, a wish leading to an intention, and so forth.

(a) [8 marks] Explain what the careenium is meant to show about the relation between these two levels of description.

(b) [9 marks] Explain how the thought experiment bears on the following worry: if every event at the careenium level already has a sufficient cause at that level, then causal claims made at the symbol level are either false or merely redundant.

(c) [8 marks] Say whether the careenium actually answers that worry, and defend your verdict.

STUDENT ANSWER.

(a) The careenium's point is that one and the same set of events can be described truly at two different scales, and that neither description is a shorthand for the other in the way a plot summary is a shorthand for a novel. At the careenium level there are only particles and impacts. At the symbol level there are meanings that trigger one another: the simmball marked CAT sets off the simmball marked MOUSE not because of any law about cats and mice, but because this slab has been so arranged that the triggering relations among its particles line up with the relations we would call association. What makes the symbol level describable at all is that alignment; were the simmballs to careen at random there would be no stable patterns to name, and the higher-level vocabulary would have nothing to attach to.

(b) The worry is the familiar causal exclusion worry: if the micro-level is causally closed and complete, then a high-level cause either duplicates a micro-cause or adds nothing, and systematic double-causation is something no one should accept. Hofstadter's answer, as I understand it, is essentially the answer Jaegwon Kim gives: concede the premise, and conclude that the high level has no independent causal powers. The careenium level does all the pushing. The symbol vocabulary survives only as a convenience, a way of talking about enormously complicated arrangements of simmballs without listing every collision. Hofstadter differs from Kim chiefly in tone: he wants us to keep the symbol talk and to take it seriously as a manner of seeing, while admitting that strictly speaking it is the simmballs that do the work.

(c) I do not think this amounts to a concession, and the reason has to do with the direction in which determination runs. If a symbol-level description is true exactly when the corresponding arrangement of simmballs obtains, then that arrangement does not so much explain why the description is true as get singled out by it as the relevant arrangement. In a slab arranged the way the careenium is, the state of the symbol-level pattern fixes what the simmballs do next: it is the pattern, not the individual particles, that carries the information making the next triggering happen. That is genuine downward causality, and it means the careenium is not closed under careenium-level physics after all; the symbol level contributes causal force that no sum of collisions could supply. The objection that this double-counts the causation misses the point, because the two levels are not two sets of events but one set of events, and one set of events can be driven from the top as readily as from the bottom.

There is a residual difficulty I should note. Since a simmball bears a symbol, and a symbol is the sort of thing whose content is fixed for someone, the careenium appears to presuppose an interpreter who already possesses the concepts the simmballs stand for. If that is right, the thought experiment cannot be used to explain where concepts come from; it can only illustrate what happens once they are present. Hofstadter is alive to this, and treats the careenium as a device for getting clear about levels rather than as a complete account of meaning.
