My uncle and my cousin argued through most of a four-hour drive about whether famines are ever properly called crimes rather than disasters, and I couldn't referee it, so here's the exchange.

Uncle opened with Sen's entitlements point about Bengal in '43: there was no collapse in overall food availability, so the deaths trace to who could command food, and a policy is a thing people did. Cousin said that's a non sequitur — showing a famine had political causes doesn't make it a crime; governments fail to stop bad things constantly, and if a bad monsoon plus rigid wartime priorities plus relief that never arrives adds up to murder, the word has been stretched past use. Uncle went to Ireland: grain kept being shipped out while people starved, so that wasn't a failure to prevent, it was ongoing extraction. Cousin said the export line is overplayed — the tonnages were small next to the shortfall, they dropped off after '46, and by '47 Britain was shipping maize in, so Ireland doesnt give uncle his clean case of active taking. Uncle said fine, take the Great Leap: inflated harvest reports, procurement quotas set against those fictional numbers, grain collected anyway, and peasants barred from going where the food was. Cousin agreed that one looks different but said it proves the distinction that matters isn't natural-versus-political, it's bystander-versus-funneller — did the state let a harvest failure do its work, or did it move food away and lock the exits. Uncle said that's decoration: in all three the state had options it didn't take, and choosing not to feed people you could feed is choosing that they die. Cousin said no, what makes something a crime is that the agent's decision made the difference between living and dying, and where a harvest failure would have killed a lot of them anyway, the decisions changed the distribution and the timing but not the bare fact. Neither budged an inch.

Go through it move by move and say who's right at each step — not just who wins on the whole. Then tell me what neither of them raised that would actually shift the argument.
