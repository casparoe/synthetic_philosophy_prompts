Setting the final for a 3rd-year political theory course on secession and self-determination: 2 hours, one essay question, 25 marks. I want a question nobody can pass by reciting remedial-right-only vs. primary-right theories. The crux should be recursion — if a people inside a state can leave, why can't a people inside the leaving unit leave too? Quebec all but seceded in '95 and the James Bay Cree ran their own vote days earlier on staying in Canada (96% for staying); the same thing haunts any Catalan exit, for Barcelona or the Val d'Aran. Either you allow the recursion, in which case no secession ever completes because the map keeps fracturing, or you halt it somewhere, in which case you owe a principled stopping rule that isn't just "whatever tier happened to be a sovereign state in 1945."

Make the question self-contained — describe the case inside the prompt so no marks go to trivia recall — and make it demand both a stopping rule and an account of what that rule costs the people it excludes. Then give me the question as it would appear on the paper, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 that names the moves a first-class answer makes that a competent-but-unremarkable one misses, plus three or four specific errors that should cost marks (treating it as merely practical, appealing to majority consent in a way that begs the question, that sort of thing).
