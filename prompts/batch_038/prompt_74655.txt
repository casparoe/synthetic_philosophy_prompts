I trade on a play-money forecasting site for a class, and I got wrecked last month in a way that's stuck with me. There was a market on whether the US would be in a recession by a certain date, and I went long because basically every indicator I could read pointed at contraction — jobs revisions, the yield curve, soft retail sales. Then the market resolved NO, because the rule pointed at a business-cycle committee at a private research outfit and that committee hadn't declared anything inside the window (turns out they can take the better part of a year). Same pattern with the election markets: they don't pay out on certified results, they pay out on whether a designated news agency has called the race, which is a judgment made by a small desk at some point in the night. And when people complain, half the platforms say the written rule and its named source decide, technicalities included, while the other half have a fallback telling the resolver to go with the spirit of the question or common-sense consensus if the letter breaks down. My site does both at once, in different corners of the rules doc.

So what does any of this presuppose? My instinct is that resolving on the source rather than on the event is a kind of operationalism — the recession just *is* the declaration, the winner just *is* the call — but I can't tell whether that's the real commitment or me over-reading a bit of plumbing. Reconstruct the view of facts and of measurement the practice needs, and be careful to separate what it strictly presupposes from what it merely sits comfortably with. I can see an argument that it commits nobody to anything metaphysical and is really a view about adjudication: named official sources are cheap to check and hard to fake, whereas "what actually happened" is contestable and invites manipulation, so maybe its an implicit positivism about who gets to settle disputes and nothing more. Tell me which reading the details actually favor, and mark where the practice is underdetermined — several philosophies licensing the identical rulebook.

Then the part I actually care about: is the package coherent? The same people who insist these markets are epistemic instruments — dispersed information aggregated into a price more accurate than any expert's judgment — are the ones telling you the price is a forecast about a press release rather than about the world. And the futarchy pitch my prof likes, where the legislature sets the goals and markets pick the means because prices forecast better than committees do, seems to need the price to be about real national wellbeing and not about whatever index an agency happened to publish, or else you've built a very expensive machine for optimizing the index. Say what theory of measurement, or of testimony and authority, would let someone hold both ends at once, if any does. And finally: if you swapped report-resolution for resolve-on-the-truth, what would the institution have to turn into — who does the finding, what does that cost, and is the thing still a market at the end of it?
