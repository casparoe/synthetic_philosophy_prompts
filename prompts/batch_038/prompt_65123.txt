Backend engineer, zero philosophy coursework, but I read Lewis's Do We Believe in Penal Substitution? on a whim and got stuck on one bit. He says we're of two minds about handing punishment off: if the penalty is a fine, we're perfectly happy to let somebody else pay it, but imprisonment or death can't be transferred no matter how willing the volunteer. Lewis died before community bail funds and crowdfund-your-fine campaigns were a thing, so what would he make of an arrested person whose bail gets wired in by an non-profit run by people who have never met them? On his own account, is that defendant still being punished, or has the punishment genuinely been substituted? What I'm most curious about is whether closeness between offender and substitute does any work for him here — the fund's donors do think of themselves as standing with the defendant as members of one community, which feels like it could be exactly the kind of case he was reaching for, or nothing like it at all. Please reason from his actual argument rather than from general intuitions about whether money bail is fair.
