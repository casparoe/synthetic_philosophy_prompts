Last night I spent three hours arguing with a friend about a working relationship I have been in for seven years, and I came away unsure which of us is right. I would like a careful answer, because I have to act within the next month.

The arrangement. I am a freelance conference interpreter, as is the person in question — call him M. We work in a specialty with perhaps forty practitioners in this region, and nearly all of our work reaches us by word of mouth inside that circle. For seven years M and I have had a standing understanding: when one of us is double-booked or turns a job down, we pass it to the other, and we split the fee on the engagements where the client insists on two of us in the booth. We also share a workshop, on a lease we both signed, with a deposit that is returned only if we both sign off on the condition of the place at the end. A recording and transcription rig of mine — worth roughly four months of my income — has stood at his end of that room for two years, because it suits him to use it there.

The trouble. In the spring he missed a handover that cost me a client; it emerged later that his father had been hospitalized, he telephoned me from a corridor to say so, and he was scrupulous for six months afterwards. In the autumn he missed another, offered no explanation then or since, and took that client onto his own books a few weeks later. In January he declined, with very little reason given, to cover a booth for me during a week I had a family commitment, after I had covered for him twice in the same season. I have said nothing about any of it. Twice before I absorbed a loss without comment, and both times the behaviour continued or worsened, so I no longer believe that letting it pass buys anything. There is one further wrinkle I keep turning over: in our second year I botched a handover of my own, through simple incompetence. He never mentioned it, then or later, and I do not know whether that was forgiveness or bookkeeping.

The complication. My partner has been posted abroad and I am leaving in fourteen months. This is not a secret: M knows, the circle knows, and my name comes off the local listings next year.

My friend's case. He says the understanding only ever held together because each of us expected further dealings with the other; that once both men can see the calendar, nothing is left to hold it together; and that the only thing my conduct between now and my departure can affect is what the other thirty-eight people conclude about me. Therefore: end it now, state plainly and in the open what happened with the autumn client, and accept the loss on the fourteen months. He adds that a reputation for pushing back is worth nothing unless one has actually pushed back at least once, and that I am paying real money for a reputation I do not have.

Where I resist. I do not accept that the fourteen months are worth nothing — those referrals are a fifth of my income, and I would rather earn them than spend them making a point. Nor do I accept that the two missed handovers are the same event: one arrived with a telephone call from a hospital and was followed by half a year of reliability, the other arrived with nothing and was followed by a theft. My friend calls this sentiment, says the second tells me everything I need to know about the first, and insists that any arrangement of this kind rots from the end backwards once the end is visible.

What I want from you. Tell me which of us has the better of it, and then be concrete:

- Should I raise the autumn client at all, and if so with him, with the circle, or with both?
- Should I keep passing him work for the next fourteen months, stop passing it, or keep passing it while withdrawing elsewhere?
- Is there any version of a written agreement — a penalty for a missed handover, a dated schedule for returning my rig — that would genuinely help, or does putting it in writing destroy whatever is holding this together?
- What do I do about the deposit and the machine, given that both of us must sign at the end and he knows perfectly well that I need his signature?

I am not after references or the names of studies. I want your judgment and the reasoning behind it, including anything in my own account of events you think I have got wrong or am conveniently forgetting.
