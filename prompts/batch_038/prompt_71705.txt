Anti-realists then say this kind of spread is what you'd expect if there were no moral facts to track — more like disagreement about which foods are disgusting than about arithmetic.

Here's the wrinkle I want you to chew on. Suppose the anthropology gets sharper and the big differences turn out to live inside individual heads rather then between cultures: take the same person, prime a kin/community frame in one condition (you're deciding about your cousin) and an anonymous one-shot market frame in another (you're deciding about a stranger you'll never meet again), and you reproduce most of the cross-cultural spread within one subject on one afternoon. Which frame gets applied is set by cues the person doesn't endorse and can't easily reason their way out of.

Does that help or hurt the case for moral objectivity? I can see it both ways and I want you to come down somewhere. On the other hand it might take away the realist's main escape hatch, which is that disagreement would wash out under better information and reflection; cue-sensitivity doesn't wash out when you inform people, it just moves with the cue.
