I've spent fifteen years building OWL ontologies — classes, individuals, subsumption axioms, reasoners — and no time at all reading metaphysics, so what I want is a translation, not a lecture. The position to be restated in my vocabulary is realism about properties: that when many particular things are all red, there is one thing they share, and that this shared thing is more than a name or a habit of grouping. Please carry over the realist's three options for where the shared thing lives — outside the particulars and independent of them, in each of them, or only as a concept formed afterward by a mind that sorts them — the nominalist's denial, and the regress worry that if the many red things and the thing they share are alike in being red, you seem to need yet another thing over the lot of them.

For each, report what lands cleanly in class/instance/axiom terms, what had to be bent to fit, and what has no expression at all in my vocabulary. Then give me the residue report. Specifically: does the fact that a reasoner cheerfully admits classes with no individuals, and treats two class names as interchangeable whenever they happen to pick out the same individuals, settle the question in the nominalist's favor as I have assumed — or does it merely build into the framework a decision the realist is disputing? I want an honest verdict on whether the residue exposes the realist's claim as confused or exposes my vocabulary as missing something, and I want the translation not to smuggle description-logic assumptions in under the name of translation.
