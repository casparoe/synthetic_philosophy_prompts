I'm a fourth-year philosophy of mind PhD student and I co-host our department's small podcast. Next week's guest is a philosopher who has spent the last few years arguing that the sphexishness business has been badly misused in debates about agency. The background she always starts from is the digger wasp — genus *Sphex* — that paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the mouth of its burrow, goes inside to check that the coast is clear, comes back out, and drags it in; if you nudge the cricket a few inches while she's underground, she drags it back to the threshold and runs the entire routine again, and she'll do this indefinitely. Her positive view, which she calls the recovery-band account, goes roughly like this: sphexishness isn't binary and isn't a missing ingredient, it's a measure of the range of perturbations an agent can absorb and still get back to its goal; the wasp's band is just very narrow and ours is very wide; and a wide band requires nothing beyond more and better-tuned subroutines, so there's no leftover gap for anything non-mechanical to fill. She's an out-and-out compatibilist about it. She also likes to bring up large language models as a test case, on the grounds that they have a suspiciously wide band inside language and essentially no band outside it. Her signature move when you press on whether any of this is about *freedom* is to say the wasp isn't unfree, it's just narrow — competence, not liberty.
