kind of an odd one. my ethics reading group spent the whole month on spectrum arguments — the Temkin stuff about whether "better than, all things considered" has to be transitive — and next week is the last session. i want to open it by reading something that performs the argument instead of describing it, because the bit that never landed in discussion is that the intransitivity isnt a vagueness trick. it comes from a different consideration taking over as you move down the spectrum: intensity dominates the adjacent pairs, quantity dominates the endpoints, and that's what lets every handoff be right while the chain lies.

here's the spectrum i want you to use, it's my own, not lifted out of the book. Rung one: two years awake under something enormous that's slowly closing, being crushed. From there every rung halves the pain and doubles the length, nine rungs down, until the last one is a grain of sand in your shoe — the faintest irritation there is — for four million years. (yes, nobody lives that long, we already had that fight, it's a thought experiment.) At every adjacent pair the halving is clearly worth the doubling: nobody sane takes the more intense one. But ask about the two ends directly — the press, or the grit for four million years — and i think most of us take the press. that's the reversal the poem has to make you feel without ever explaining it.

form: nine four-line stanzas, one per rung, chained — the last line of each stanza repeated word for word as the first line of the next, so the handoff literally *is* the comparison being passed along. first eight do the handoffs, ninth is the endpoints. no meter required, rhyme welcome only if it doesn't force anything. put the turn around stanza five or six: the linking line has to change register there, quietly, out of the language of how bad and into the language of how much, so a reader feels the criterion swap without being told. and the ninth stanza's final line must not loop back to the opening line the way a crown would — the chain just stops. those last two lines should state the endpoint verdict flatly, contradicting everything the handoffs just licensed.

please don't use the words spectrum, transitive, intransitive or "all things considered" anywhere in the poem; keep it physical, keep it concrete. someone who knows the literature should be able to point at the turn stanza and say why the poem refuses to close. someone who doesn't should just get a good poem about pain and arithmetic.

then one section after it, titled "notes on the chain," maybe 200 words: where you put the turn, what you did to the linking line there, why you ended it the way you did. dont summarize the philosophy back at me.
