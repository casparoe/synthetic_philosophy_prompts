So I manage a small counter-service coffee place that sits across from a big teaching hospital, which means we get a lot of out-of-town and foreign visitors, and I've gotten weirdly bothered by how our little bundle of rules hangs together. Let me lay it out and you tell me what theory of obligation we're actually running on, because I don't think anyone here could articulate one.

First: wages. I'm pretty sure the way it works in my state is that the restaurant can pay serving staff a couple dollars an hour and count the tips toward the rest of the minimum wage, and if somebody's tips come up short in a week the employer is supposed to cover the gap. Our menu says tips are appreciated but never expected. Meanwhile we have a tip-out rule where the baristas hand a slice of their tips to whoever worked the register, so the cashier's take-home partly depends on how generous the customers of a different employee happened to be. And when the owner floated getting rid of tips entirely — raise prices 20%, pay everyone a flat rate, no more card screen asking for 15/20/25% — the staff voted it down, because the good tippers more than make up the difference.

Second: the line. We run one queue to two registers, next available till. Somebody cutting in gets treated as a genuine moral offense, not a faux pas — last week a woman got shouted at for drifting in front of a guy who'd stepped out to grab a napkin. But walk to the window and you can see the same customers crossing the street against the light when there's no traffic, and nobody reacts to that at all. Both are just conventions we could have set up differently. One of them carries blame and one doesn't, and I can't tell what the difference is supposed to be.

Third: the visitors. When a tourist from somewhere without tipping leaves nothing, the staff don't say he didn't know, they say he's cheap. One of them asked why he should pay an extra 20% for a coffee he picked up himself, and a coworker basically told him when you're here you do it our way. So the norm seems to bind people who never agreed to it and didn't grow up with it, and not knowing about it doesn't seem to excuse anybody.

So: reconstruct the implicit theory. Is the tip functioning as a gift, a wage, a debt, or something else, and can one thing be all of them at once? What has to be true about conventions for line-cutting to be a wrong and jaywalking to be nothing? Does the practice assume that local norms morally bind visitors? Tell me what our setup strictly presupposes versus what it merely happens to fit with, and flag the parts where a bunch of different theories would license exactly the same behavior. Then tell me what would actually have to change — in the wage structure, the queue, the way we talk about tourists — if we swapped the presupposed view for the most plausible rival one.

No headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables please. Just continuous prose, like you're writing me an email about it.
