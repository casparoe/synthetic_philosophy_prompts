I'm drafting a paper on standing to blame with a senior colleague, and I have been running my examples past her. Her verdicts so far, with her margin notes:

- A technician who has himself been quietly padding calibration logs and has never admitted it, denouncing a postdoc for padding calibration logs: no standing.
- A co-author who designed the padding, then self-reported, accepted a sanction, and signed the correction, and who now says the PI knew and approved all along: standing.
- An emeritus professor who falsified data thirty years ago, made restitution, and has spent two decades arguing against the practice: standing.
- A star graduate student with a spotless record who insists publicly that everyone drops inconvenient runs, that this is how the field works, and that the postdoc is simply a fool for getting caught: no standing.
- A philosopher at another university, spotless record, no connection to the lab, who publishes an op-ed about the case: no standing — she wrote "what business is it of his?", and on the next line, "he has never run an experiment in his life."

The example I now need her to rule on is this. A postdoc who, under deadline pressure, has this year been excluding inconvenient data points from her own analyses. She has told no one, and she has not told herself it was permissible either: she takes what she did to be exactly as bad as what she is about to condemn, and she has begun drafting a retraction of her own poster, though she has not yet sent it. At lab meeting she tells the PI that dropping inconvenient runs is falsification and has to stop.

Predict her verdict. Then do the harder part: lay out two or three principles that would account for all five of her earlier rulings, show which of them her record cannot yet separate, say what each predicts about this case, and commit to one prediction with reasons — including which of her margin notes you are leaning on and how much weight the ambiguous fifth ruling can bear. Finally, tell me what the paper would be committed to if I wrote up each version: what claim about blame in general we would be adopting either way, and what single further case would tell the candidate principles apart. Please state how confident you are in the prediction and in the principle you favor, and single out the step in your reasoning you are least sure of.
