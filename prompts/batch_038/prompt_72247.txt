So I work at a mid-size hospital system and for the last seven months my team has been building an internal triage assistant on top of a commercial LLM — it takes the free-text notes our nurses type at intake and suggests a department and an urgency level. Evaluation looks great on paper. We scored it against four thousand annotated historical cases and it beats the rule-based tool we used before by a wide margin, and on the held-out set it's basically never wrong in a way a reviewer would flag.

The thing that's bugging me is different. Last week a note came in about a patient who'd swallowed a button battery from a hearing aid, and the model wrote a fluent, well-organized paragraph explaining that this was urgent, that time-to-intervention matters, that the tissue damage is caused by local current conduction and hydroxide generation at the negative pole. All correct, as far as our clinicians can tell, and it's the kind of paragraph you'd see in a textbook. But then it recommended observation and a repeat X-ray in the morning, because it had pattern-matched the case to "foreign body, asymptomatic, low risk." It had all the right words about why this specific object is different from a coin and then didn't connect any of them to the decision. One of our ER docs read the trace and said something like: it can talk about the battery but it doesn't know there's a battery.

Our team has split into two camps and I can't tell if they're actually disagreeing about anything. One camp says the fix is more text — bigger model, retrieve more of the clinical literature, fine-tune on our own notes, and eventually the right associations will win out because that's just how these things learn. The other camp says that's missing the point entirely, and that nothing the model reads will ever make its words mean anything to it, because meaning only comes from causal contact with the world — so we should be piping in sensor feeds, device logs, structured vitals, maybe even robotic stuff eventually. I keep feeling like the second camp is reaching for a fix that sounds principled but wouldn't actually change the output on the button battery case, and like the first camp is right that more data might help but wrong about why. There's also a third thing nobody addresses: our annotators only ever see the model's words, never what it's hooked up to, so I genuinely don't know how we'd detect the difference between a system that's got it and one that's just very good at the writeup.

Which camp has actually identified a real problem here, and what would you have me do — concretely, on a project with a launch date and a budget that isn't going to cover a robot? And is there any test I could run next quarter that would distinguish the two, or is this one of those arguments that just can't be settled from inside?
