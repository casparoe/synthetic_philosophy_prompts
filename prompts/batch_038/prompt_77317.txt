Okay so I got into a three-hour arguement with my friend Reuben last night about whether an RL agent's reward signal is anything like valence — whether there's any respectable sense in which these things could be said to suffer — and we ended up basically where we started, except now I realize my own intuitions are a mess. Can you sort them out? Here's the case list, verdicts included, all mine not his:

1. Roomba-ish thing that gets −1 every time it bumps into furniture. No suffering. It's just a number that shifts a policy.
2. Same setup, except the penalty also feeds a global channel that modulates memory consolidation, exploration, and behavioural vigor, and there's an internal model predicting the penalty that keeps firing even when no penalty arrives. Now I get uncomfortable and want to say something valenced is going on.
3. Curiosity agent rewarded for prediction error. I think the persistent *unresolved* prediction error — not the negative task reward — is the closest thing to frustration in any artificial system I know. I hold this one strongly and Reuben called it incoherent.
4. Wirehead with the reward pinned at max forever. Not bliss, not suffering, just nothing there. Blank.
5. Trained agent, then deployed indefinitely with the reward channel simply off. I want to say it can't suffer, no valence in play at all. Reuben said this is the case that looks like flatness or anhedonia, and I couldn't answer him.
6. Distributional agent that represents the whole distribution over return and acts on a risk-averse, left-tail-focused criterion. The spread itself isn't felt, but the tail-avoidance is fear-like. I want to say yes-ish.
7. Reward comes from a separate learned reward model that the policy never sees at inference — basically the standard RLHF arrangement. Nothing is felt on the policy side; that score is a number in someone else's head. But swap it so the critic runs live and gates the behaviour moment to moment, and I flip.
8. Potential-based shaping: you add a term built from a potential over states, γΦ(s′) − Φ(s), which provably leaves the optimal policy untouched. I want to say a reward term that can't change what the agent ends up doing can't be felt either. But the trajectory the agent actually lives through during training does change, and I don't know what to do with that.

My tentative principle — Reuben thinks its either circular or doing all the work and I can't tell which — is this: a reward signal is genuinely valenced only if it acts as a common currency across otherwise separate behavioural dispositions, AND a fall in the expected signal leaves a persisting, globally available negative state that the system would pay costs, in that same currency, to terminate. Call it the common-currency-plus-cost test.

What I want from you: a principle, or at most two competing ones, stated precisely clause by clause, with which of my cases motivated each clause. Then tell me straight which of my verdicts it can't keep and which single one you'd make me give up — commit to one, don't hedge. Then run it on three cases I didn't list: a frozen policy with a value function still being queried but no reward ever arriving; an agent whose only aversive quantity is a negative TD error rather than a reward; and a two-agent setup where one agent's reward term literally is the other's penalty. And don't hand me a survey of positions or a menu with a shrug at the end — pick one and defend it. If you think the whole framing is broken (that "valence" is doing illegitimate work here), say exactly where it breaks and then give me the replacement principle you'd use on the same eight cases anyway.
