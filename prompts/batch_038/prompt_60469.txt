I'm setting a design exercise for a graduate seminar on causation by omission, and I need the case built to specification rather than adapted from the stock examples.

Purpose, since it fixes what counts as cheating: the case is meant to pressure role- and norm-based accounts of omission-causation — accounts on which a failure to act causes an outcome only when the agent's acting was what their office, function, assignment, or undertaking called for — by confronting them with a bystander who is neither remote, nor ignorant, nor unable.

Specifications. One ordinary contemporary setting (a municipal pool, a building site, a hospital floor — your choice), rendered in a single paragraph, with no science fiction, no exotic physics, and no appeal to anyone's inner life beyond what an onlooker could observe. The case contains two agents, both of whom fail to intervene, and a harm that follows; either intervention on its own would have prevented it.

Required verdicts: (i) a simple counterfactual-dependence account must come out saying that both failures caused the harm; (ii) the role-based account must say that only the role-holder's failure caused it; (iii) a contrastive account must get no escape, in that the salient contrast-event and contrast-effect are the ones your own description fixes, and dependence holds relative to them.

Constraints that keep the design from being easy: both agents are on the scene, know the facts that make intervention necessary, and can intervene at trivial personal cost, so the usual remoteness, ignorance, and inability moves are unavailable; and you may not smuggle the verdict in by describing the bystander's inaction in language that already carries causal weight.

After the scenario, show the work. State the counterfactuals relied on for each agent and why each holds; identify precisely which norm or role is present for one and absent for the other; name the single feature of the case doing the work and say what the verdicts become without it; and give the role-based theorist's best reply — for instance, that trivial-cost rescue itself generates a norm, so the bystander falls inside the account after all — together with the revision you would make to block that reply.
