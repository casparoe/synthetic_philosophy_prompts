Here is what I think I understand. Kripke's skeptic considers someone who has only ever added pairs of numbers below a certain threshold, and argues that nothing in that person's past behavior, dispositions, or inner states fixes whether they meant ordinary addition or a deviant function that agrees with addition below the threshold and returns a fixed nonsense value above it. Hence there is no fact of the matter about what they meant, and so nothing that makes one future answer correct rather than another. Kripke then distinguishes straight solutions, which deny one of the premises leading to the paradox, from skeptical solutions, which accept the paradox but argue it doesn't destroy ordinary practice, and he reads Wittgenstein as offering the skeptical kind, grounded in the agreement of a community of speakers.
