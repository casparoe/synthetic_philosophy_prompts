You are a philosopher of science who specializes in the metaphysics of laws of nature and who has argued both sides of the Humean/anti-Humean debate at length. Please think carefully and write as a committed advocate rather than as a surveyor of the literature: I want a case made, not a map drawn. Before producing the argument itself, spend a short section brainstorming — list two or three genuinely distinct argumentative strategies available for the thesis stated below, say which you judge most promising and which you will set aside, and say why in each case.

Here is the thesis. For a paper I am revising, I have been working with a regimented variant of the Mill–Ramsey–Lewis best-system account, which I will call the compression-first account. Fix a language whose non-logical vocabulary consists solely of the perfectly natural properties and relations. A candidate system is a set of axioms in that language together with whatever probabilistic apparatus it needs, and a system is admissible for a mosaic M if its consequences determine every fact of M up to an error tolerance ε — where ε is not fixed from outside but is itself read off the probabilistic generalizations that the winning system entails. Admissible systems are then ranked neither by a weighted sum of simplicity and strength nor lexicographically by strength, but by compression ratio: the number of bits required to specify M directly in the privileged language, divided by the number of bits required to specify the system's axioms together with its ε. Laws of nature are the theorems of the ratio-maximizing admissible system.

Thesis T: once laws are construed this way, the two standing objections to Humeanism lose their force — namely, that the account inverts the direction of explanation, since on it particular matters of fact account for the laws rather than the laws for the facts, and that it makes the laws objectionably dependent on our descriptive interests, since simplicity is a measure of how we like to write things down. More aggressively, and this is the half I most want pressed: the anti-Humean cannot construct a rival compression-first account at all, because the second-order necessitation relation posited by Dretske, Tooley, and Armstrong is either redundant — it adds bits to the specification of a world without adding compression — or inexpressible in a framework whose sole currency is pattern. Please make the strongest case you can for T, and in doing so engage at full strength with at least the two best anti-Humean replies: that necessitation is required to ground counterfactuals and the modal robustness that a merely maximal regularity cannot deliver, and that compressibility is itself an intensional, interest-relative notion, so that a compression-first Humeanism has not escaped the mind-dependence worry but simply relocated it inside the scoring function. State each reply as its proponent would before responding to it.

One epistemic request about the standing of your own answer. Keep clearly separate (i) what is common ground or near-settled among people who work on this topic — including which premises Humeans and anti-Humeans are both happy to grant — from (ii) what is genuinely contested, and do not allow a contested position, including T itself and including my stipulated apparatus, to be presented as though it were the consensus view. Where you think my definition of the compression-first account is ill-formed or does unearned work — the self-referential clause fixing ε and the choice of a ratio in place of a weighted sum are the two places where I expect trouble — say so explicitly rather than quietly repairing it and arguing from the repaired version. Close with a brief note identifying the one or two steps of your own argument that a well-informed opponent would attack first, and an honest assessment of how confident you are that those steps survive.
