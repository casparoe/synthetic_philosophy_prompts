Last night a friend and I argued for four hours about whether memetics is a theory or merely a vocabulary, and we got nowhere, chiefly because we could not agree in advance on what would settle the question. He is a fairly orthodox memeticist: on his view, once you can point to something that is copied with tolerable fidelity, varies from copy to copy, and differs in how likely it is to be copied again, you have everything a Darwinian explanation needs, and every further demand is just the human sciences insulating themselves from being explained. My objection was that this makes the label so cheap that it fits the diffusion of a slang term, the spread of a rumour, a child picking up a word, and the generational improvement of a kayak or composite bow design all equally well — which is to say that it fails to separate the cases where cultural change accumulates into something adaptive from the cases where it merely churns. We ended up trading examples: playground games, which change constantly and get better at nothing, against the ratcheting of tool and instrument traditions; and the guided variation problem, which he thinks smuggles intention back in through the door and I think he is simply declining to look at.

Before I try to adjudicate any of it, I want the specification rather than the positions. I have been asked to write a review essay that applies a single standard of adequacy to three rather different books on cultural evolution, and I cannot do that without knowing what the standard is. So: write out what a theory of cultural change has to deliver in order to count as a genuinely Darwinian account rather than a Darwinian idiom. That means the phenomena it must explain — with cumulative adaptive complexity, not mere spread, as the central explanandum — the level at which its explanations must be pitched, the distinctions it cannot collapse, the empirical results about transmission it must be consistent with, and the conditions under which it must be able to come out false about a particular domain, since a framework that cannot fail to be Darwinian about anything is not making a claim. Attach to each requirement the case or consideration that generates it: the fidelity and discreteness question; the guided variation objection; the content, prestige and conformity biases documented in the psychology of cultural learning, and whether a theory must absorb them or is refuted by them; the difference between a variant that is copied from an ancestor and one that is independently reconstructed by a learner from other materials; and the question whether cultural lineages are particulate at all, or whether population-level thinking can do the work without discrete units.

Then do the sorting work I actually care about. Tell me which requirements follow from the explanatory job itself and which are contingent commitments of one camp — my suspicion is that bounded, recombinable, gene-like units are baggage rather than necessity, since the argument that replicators are not needed for cumulative adaptive culture strikes me as having landed, but I want that examined rather than assumed. Tell me whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable: I suspect that high copying fidelity, undirected variation, and the production of cumulative design cannot all be had once the generation of variants is treated as the work of minds with goals, learning resources, and developmental scaffolding, and if that is right the impossibility is itself the result, and I want it stated precisely rather than gestured at. Tell me also whether being a Darwinian population is all-or-nothing or comes in degrees, so that some cultural domains can be more Darwinian than others, and if the notion is graded, say which features of a domain move it up or down the scale and which of the three books' subject matters would land where. Finally, identify the single requirement that excludes the most candidates, and then score briefly — a few lines each, no more — an orthodox memetic account, a dual-inheritance or population-thinking account, and the developmental view on which the interesting explanatory work happens inside the black box of variant generation rather than in any subsequent sorting step. Keep the scorecard short; the list is what I want.
