Just got out of a ~3 hour argument with a friend about pure time preference and my brain wont shut up, so I'm dumping it on you instead.

Context: I'm on a small program committee that gets to fund three one-year projects — one person each, no team, no data collection, basically a desk and a year — on the ethics of discounting the far future. Philosophers and economists can both apply. My job is to propose what those three slots should go to, and I want to walk in with something better than "the usual controversies, again."

The argument, since its what triggered this. My friend's position is that pure time preference isn't a moral claim at all. On his telling, a nonzero delta is a commitment device: a way for the present generation to refuse to be dictated to by the sheer arithmetic of how many future people there will eventually be. He calls it the sovereignty reading — we discount not because future welfare counts for less, but because setting the pure rate to roughly zero means handing policy over to a headcount that doesn't exist yet and can't reciprocate. My counter was that this just relabels status quo bias, that it can't explain why the rate should be small rather than large, and that it collapses the second you notice the same logic licenses discounting the claims of large groups of living people too. He said that's a feature, not a bug. We did not resolve it and I'm still annoyed.
