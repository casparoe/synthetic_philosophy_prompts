Can you build me an instant-runoff profile that produces the no-show paradox — a voter who is strictly better off not turning up at all? Constraints: exactly three candidates, no more than a dozen voters, no ties at any count, and each of the three has to have real first-choice support (no straw candidate who's mathematically dead from the start). Dress it as something ordinary, like a society picking between three conference cities, and give the ballots as a table of types.

Then run both counts round by round, and check these for me:

- Could the same voter get an outcome at least as good by showing up and truncating instead — ranking only their favorite and stopping there? If truncation makes the abstention framing redundant, just say so, I'd rather know.
- Does the paradox need two or more people staying home, or could a single voter pull it off? Argue it, don't just assert.
- Does it survive doubling every ballot type?

Then tell me which structural feature of the profile is doing the work and what happens to the case if you remove it, and situate the whole thing: is this an instance of Gibbard–Satterthwaite, a cousin of it, or something G-S is silent on? And what does it cost the usual pitch for IRV that you can always vote your conscience without triggering the spoiler effect?
