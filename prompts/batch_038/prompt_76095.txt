I spent thirty-five years designing laboratory instruments and writing their documentation, and only since retiring have I taken up Thomson's lamp and the wider literature on supertasks. I should like a parody product datasheet, of not more than 600 words, for a commercially available Thomson lamp, composed in the deadpan register of genuine instrument documentation and using the usual section headings in the usual order: overview, technical specification, installation, operating instructions, troubleshooting, safety, warranty and liability, regulatory compliance.

The humour must come from getting the thought experiment right rather than from absurdity. I would expect the datasheet to cover: the actuation schedule that converges on noon; the absence of any final logged switching event, and the consequent impossibility of answering a customer who asks how many times the switch moved; a state register whose value at noon is undefined — explicitly not to be reported as either both or neither — despite the complete history at every earlier instant; a continuous-output dimmer variant offered as an upgrade on the grounds that its limit exists; at least one safety warning grounded in what the schedule would physically demand of the mechanism; and a compliance statement certifying every instant strictly before noon while remaining silent about noon itself.

My target is the assumption that a full specification of a system's behaviour at all earlier moments settles what it does at the limit. Please do not state that target, do not wink at the reader, and append no explanatory note: an engineer should be almost able to order one, and a philosopher of mathematics should be the one who laughs.
