Two people I know, Ruth and Owen, argued for an hour about hallucinations, after Ruth mentioned that her grandmother spent her last weeks seeing visitors who weren't there and describing their coats and faces in detail.

(1) Owen said that during those experiences there was something the grandmother was aware of — a figure that, from where she sat, had a grey coat and a moustache. Nothing of the sort was in the room, so what she was aware of must have been an inner, mental item she was directly acquainted with. (2) Then came his real move: from her own point of view those experiences were exactly like really seeing someone, and if two experiences cannot be told apart from the inside, they have to be the same kind of thing. So even when she looked straight at her daughter, what she was directly aware of was an inner item, with the daughter known only by inference from it. On his account we never actually see the world.

(3) Ruth said that failing to tell two things apart is a fact about the person, not about the things: a convincing fake diamond is indistinguishable from a real one to her, but that does not make the real one a fake, nor does it make her examining it the same event as being fooled. So the good case and the bad case need not share anything at all. (4) Owen: then say what they do share, because from the inside they are certainly alike, and she cannot leave that alikeness with nothing behind it. (5) Ruth: the bad experience merely seems, to the person having it, to be of the same kind as a real perception, and that is the whole of it. (6) Owen: that is not an explanation, it is the thing that needed explaining, said back louder.

Ruth's parting shot was that the same reasoning applied to remembering would show that a vivid false memory leaves you acquainted only with an inner image and never with the past, which she took to be absurd; Owen said memory is obviously a different matter and that she was changing the subject.

Please work through the exchange step by step and tell me which of (1) to (6) hold up and which fail, and whether the memory point lands or genuinely changes the subject. Then say who comes out better overall, what the strongest version of each side's position would be, and whether there is something neither of them raised that would settle the matter.
