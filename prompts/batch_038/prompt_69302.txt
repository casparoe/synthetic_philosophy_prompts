You are a political philosopher who specializes in the ethics of official contrition and who has also spent years working as a legislative drafter. Write a satirical document: the final internal draft of a state apology, prepared for an invented country, for a wrong committed roughly sixty years earlier by a regime the current government loudly repudiates. Invent both the state and the wrong, but keep the wrong structurally ordinary — a forced relocation, a coerced assimilation programme, a state-run sterilization campaign, something of that kind. Use no real country, no real named event, and no real living official.

The form is fixed: exactly five numbered clauses of the apology text itself, each two to four sentences in the actual register of official contrition, each immediately followed by one bracketed marginal note from whichever ministry has just intervened (Office of Legal Counsel, Finance, Foreign Affairs, Heritage, the Speechwriter's office). No preamble, no title, no closing comment — just the five clauses with their marginalia, about six hundred words in total.

The humour has to come from the drafting, never from an aside to the reader; stay deadpan throughout. Each clause should make a genuine problem in the theory of state apology do the comic work, and no two clauses should carry the same one: whether a successor government can apologize at all for the acts of a regime it insists was never legitimate; whether descendants can accept an apology on behalf of people who are dead and were never asked; how far agentless construction ("errors occurred," "practices took place") can be pushed before nothing has been admitted; what an apology that expressly disclaims legal liability and purports to close the matter is doing as a speech act; and whether an apology that costs the treasury nothing can be an apology rather than a receipt. The marginalia are where the negotiation shows, so that the document reads as a single text being progressively emptied out by the offices required to sign it.

Two constraints on internal consistency. First, the state never actually concedes an ongoing obligation, and whenever a clause drifts toward one, a marginal note must retract it in the same breath. Second, exactly one clause should come out accidentally correct — a sentence that says what an unqualified apology would have to say — and the note attached to it should strike that sentence, giving the reason in budgetary or procedural rather than philosophical terms. Do not name or allude to any philosopher, book, or real apology; the argument should be legible from the drafting alone. Before writing, work out in your head which distinction each of the five clauses will carry, then draft only the document.
