our reading group on heuristic arguments meets one last time this week and i want to read something aloud at the end of it — 16 lines, a pantoum, strict-ish.

quick framing in case its relevant: what we've been circling all semester is the estimate you get by chopping a hard question into factors and multiplying them out, on the presumption of independence — that the factors don't conspire. it feels like knowledge and there's no probability space anywhere in it. the number is prime or it isn't.

form: four quatrains. lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and in the final stanza the poem's own first line returns as line 2 while the poem's third line closes it. repeats verbatim — punctuation can shift if it absolutely has to, but no rewording, because the point is that the line you inherited is the line you stopped examining.

what i want the form to do: let the two inherited lines in every stanza be the presumption itself, the thing nobody re-derived, and let the two new lines each time be the fresh factor you think you've just checked. so the poem keeps feeling like progress and is really only carrying the same unearned assumption forward. when the loop closes, the assumption is literally the last thing said, which is what the argument looks like from the outside: supported by nothing but itself.

content to work in, roughly in this order:

1. the case where it works. the density of primes near n: multiply (1 - 1/p) over the primes up to the square root of n and you get the right shape, off by about twelve percent — a constant, not noise. independence is nearly true, and what's left over is a systematic tilt rather than an error that averages out. don't name the constant or any theorem, just let it be a small wrongness you cant shake.

2. the case where it breaks. sieve theory has a barrier: counting arguments of exactly this flavor provably can't tell a number with an even number of prime factors from one with an odd number, so on their own they cant isolate the primes at all. there the factors aren't approximately independent, they're in cahoots, and taking more of them into account buys precisely nothing. someone who knows this material should be able to put a finger on the line where it enters.

3. if there's room left, the formalization worry: the probability isn't over worlds, it's over a reference class of problems the estimator gets applied to, which turns "presume independence" into a claim that the estimator is calibrated on that class — and then the honest question is what makes a class the right one.

constraints: no proper nouns, no names of people, theorems, or papers. rhyme isn't required and you shouldn't force it. it has to survive being heard once in a room, so no line that only pays off on the page. and please don't resolve the tension in the last stanza or explain the joke — we still use the estimate, thats the awkward part.
