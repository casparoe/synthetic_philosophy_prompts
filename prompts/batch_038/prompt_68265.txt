Recently I ran into the Condorcet jury theorem, which as I understand it says roughly: if every voter is individually more likely to be right than wrong, and they vote independently of one another, then the majority verdict is more reliable than any single voter, and it keeps getting better as you add people, approaching certainty. My team has basically turned that into a slogan. Whenever we can't agree on whether a config change caused an outage, somebody says "lets just take a majority vote, more eyeballs means more accuracy," and everyone nods and we do it.

I want to push back with a designed counterexample, and I want it built to order rather than something I have to hand-wave about. The point is I'm writing a short internal doc, so the verdicts need to fall out of the numbers themselves. If the case only "works" because I declared it works, it's useless to me.

3. The correlation between people's judgments has to come from a single ordinary shared upstream input that everyone reads — one dashboard, one summary doc written by a single person, one log stream — not from people copying each other's votes or talking before they vote.
