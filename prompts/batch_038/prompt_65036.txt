I'm vice-chair of the hardship panel at our council — small discretionary fund, nothing to do with DHPs, just money the authority sets aside itself. It's capped, and we've spent two thirds of it with four months of the year to go. Seven decisions so far, and I'm trying to work out whether we're running one rule or several:

- care worker on zero-hours, shifts axed for six weeks, four years' service, no savings: paid the rent shortfall in full.
- man who handed in a steady warehouse job to do a plumbing course and ran out of money halfway through it: refused. Minute reads "the fund isn't a bridge for career changes", but the vote was 2–1 and the dissenter's reason was just that he'd done it to himself.
- woman who didn't declare a lodger, got an overpayment and a sanction: paid £430 of rent arrears direct to the landlord, refused the fuel element. Two kids in the house, landlord had started proceedings.
- man with severe depression, private rent £90 a month above the LHA rate, so a fresh shortfall every month as far as the eye can see: refused. Minute reads "a payment that has to be repeated isn't a resolution". The chair's covering note says the real worry was that saying yes would turn us into the borough's rent-gap filler.
- nineteen year old who left a violent home with a bin bag of clothes, no work history, five weeks from his first UC payment: paid a deposit and a month's rent.
- woman who turned down two job offers because the hours clashed with the school run, then found work on her own: paid bus fares and the first week's childcare.
- man overpaid about £900 of UC by the department's own error, now having it clawed back out of his standard allowance and £600 short: refused. Chair's note: we can't insure everyone against the DWP's mistakes, there are four hundred of these in the borough.

Next week's case. Man in his fifties, casual security guard, main carer for his adult son. Sanctioned thirteen weeks after missing four mandatory work-focused interviews, two of which landed on days his son's respite placement fell through. The sanction has now run it's course, he's back on full UC, and the employer he's been casual with for years has given him contracted hours from week five. He wants £610 for arrears that built up entirely during the sanction period. The landlord hasn't served notice. On the face of it nothing here recurs — the son now has a full-time placement that's meant to be permanent, the sanction is spent, the job is contracted. Two members have already declared. One says the arrears were caused by the sanction and the sanction is the department's business, not ours, so paying makes us a compensation scheme for every sanction in the borough and a seperate appeals system nobody voted for. The other says he missed those interviews for a reason any of us would have, and that if we refuse we're bolting our own conditionality on top of theirs.

What should we do if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which of the seven is the closest analogue, and which one is really doing the work — and if our record genuinely underdetermines this, what principle are we adopting with each possible answer? I'd also like to know whether you think the chair will vote with her own covering notes or against them, given she's written both of the ones above. Then tell me which case you'd want her to bring us next that would settle which rule we're actually on, because I suspect it isn't the one I'd pick.

Bottom line first, please: pay or don't pay, in your opening sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble and don't read the case back at me.
