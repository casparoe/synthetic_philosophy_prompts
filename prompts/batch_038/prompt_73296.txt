I am a second-year PhD student in economics, working in normative public economics and social choice. My working vocabulary is Bergson–Samuelson social welfare functionals, admissibility conditions on individual information bases, Arrow-type impossibility results, Harsanyi-style aggregation, mechanism design, and Mirrleesian optimal taxation with incentive constraints. I have read A Theory of Justice closely and sympathetically, but I have no real training in philosophy, and my supervisor's view is that anything I cannot re-express in my own formal apparatus I have not yet understood.

For the past year I have been trying to formalise the difference principle in a dynamic setting: an index of primary goods held by a representative member of the worst-off group, to be maximised over feasible institutional arrangements subject to incentive constraints on the more productive, so that departures from equality are admissible only where they raise the long-run prospects of that group relative to every feasible alternative. Alongside it I have been treating the veil of ignorance as an impartial-observer device, on the model of Harsanyi: an agent who has an equal chance of occupying any position in society and who ranks institutional schemes by the expected value of her own evaluation of them.

What I want is a systematic translation, and then an honest residue report. Restate Rawls's argument — the two principles and the serial priority of the liberty principle over the difference principle, the veil of ignorance and why the parties are said to have no probability information at all rather than equiprobable information, the choice of a primary-goods index rather than a welfare metric, the restriction of the principles to the basic structure rather than to particular allocations or transactions, the publicity condition, and the strains-of-commitment argument for preferring a principle the parties can actually stick to — into the vocabulary described above, as faithfully as you can manage. Then tell me, section by section:

- which claims translate cleanly, and where the formalism is genuinely illuminating rather than merely decorative;
- which translate only with distortion, and exactly what the distortion consists in. I am especially interested in whether representing the serial priority of liberty as a lexicographic constraint silently converts it into a very large weight, and whether modelling the parties as expected-utility maximisers with an unusual information set misrepresents Rawls's insistence that the situation is one of uncertainty rather than risk;
- which have no expression at all in my framework. My suspicion is that publicity, the moral powers, and the claim that the principles govern institutions rather than outcomes all fall out;
- where, precisely, Rawls departs from the Harsanyian reconstruction. If an impartial observer with equal chances and expected utility ends up ranking schemes by total welfare, the translation must identify which step of my set-up he would reject, and whether the veil is then doing any independent work or is merely a label attached to a leximin rule.

Then give me your verdict on the residue: does it show that social choice theory is missing something it could in principle recover — rights as protected choices, procedural constraints on the information basis, a distinction between institutional rules and outcome patterns — or does it show that once translated, the difference principle is a familiar constrained-maximin criterion with the surrounding moral machinery doing no further work? Please flag, as you go, every point at which the formalism forces an interpretive choice that Rawls himself did not make, and do not present those choices as discoveries. I would also like to know whether the primary-goods index survives contact with the usual objections from the capability side, since in my framework the metric is simply a parameter that must be defended rather than assumed.

I do not need references or a reading list; I need your own reconstruction. Finally, please be explicit about your confidence: state how sure you are of each major claim in the residue report, and single out the one point where you think you are most likely to be wrong, whether through a misreading of Rawls or through an over-generous assessment of what my vocabulary can absorb.
