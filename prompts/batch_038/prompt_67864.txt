You are a philosopher of physics who has spent years brokering adversarial collaborations in quantum foundations. Think the matter through carefully before drafting: begin by sketching, in a few lines, the strongest version of each camp's position that the other cannot dismiss as a straw man, then produce the deliverable.

Two camps have been talking past each other for decades about what Bell's theorem established.

Camp A holds that Bell's locality condition — the requirement that probabilities for events at one wing, conditional on a complete specification of everything in their past light cone, be unaffected by the setting or outcome at the spacelike-separated wing — simply is the physical content of relativistic locality; that the theorem assumes no determinism and no hidden variables beyond what the experimental arrangement already supplies; and that the observed violations therefore establish genuine nonlocality in nature.

Camp B holds that what the theorem refutes is a norm on explanation — the demand that spacelike-separated correlations be screened off by a common cause in the overlap of the past light cones — rather than a physical locality constraint; that quantum theory obeys no-signalling and relativistic quantum field theory obeys microcausality; and that reading nonlocality off a failed explanatory demand is a category mistake.

Draft the joint statement both camps could sign after a good-faith collaboration. I want: a numbered section of agreed claims (what the formalism says, what the loophole-free tests do and do not license, what the theorem's premises actually are); a table of the surviving disagreements, each row giving the topic, the disagreement phrased in wording Camp A would accept as its own view, the same in Camp B's wording, and a verdict on whether the disagreement is substantive or verbal; and the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp. I suspect the cruxes include whether outcome-dependence without parameter-dependence amounts to nonlocality, and whether a theory that preserved local causality at the price of measurement independence would count as rescuing locality; propose others if you see them.

For each crux, state a case, argument, or piece of physics both camps would accept as bearing on it, and record each camp's prediction in advance. At minimum, both must address this stipulated test case: a theory is produced that reproduces every quantum prediction up to the Tsirelson bound, is Lorentz-invariant at the observable level, satisfies measurement independence and parameter independence, and violates outcome independence. Say what each camp is then committed to saying, and whether the case exposes the dispute as verbal.

Argue from the physics and the logic; do not cite literature, name papers, or supply a reading list. Write the whole response in British English.
