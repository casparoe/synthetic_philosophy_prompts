I have just come out of a four-hour argument with a friend — she teaches behavioural ecology, I build machine-learning pipelines for a freight company and read philosophy of mind on commuter trains — and I suspect we spent the last two of those hours defending positions neither of us actually holds. The occasion was Dennett's ladder of creature types, which we were both prepared to accept in outline: creatures whose competences are fixed by selection, creatures whose own history can modify them, creatures that can test a hypothesis internally instead of in the world, and creatures that import externally designed tools into their thinking. What I cannot place anywhere on that ladder is a configuration I keep encountering in my own work, and my friend insists I am describing something that already has a perfectly good name. So I want the thing stated more carefully than either of us managed at half past midnight, and then adjudicated.

The configuration is this: something with no capacity whatsoever to improve itself nevertheless raises the ceiling on what a learner downstream of it can achieve, not by supplying solutions and not by supplying tools, but by constructing the distribution of problems the learner will meet. In one of our training setups there is a module whose parameters are frozen within the first hour of a run. Its entire function is to emit situations for a second module that does all of the learning — situations pitched so that they are neither trivial nor hopeless for the learner as it currently stands. Over several weeks the lineage becomes markedly more competent at the target task, while the frozen module ends exactly as limited as it began: nothing in it changed, it changed what the changing thing had to face. I think the same shape appears elsewhere, which is why I don't think it is an artefact of software. The meerkat work, for instance: helpers hand pups prey that becomes progressively harder to deal with as the pups grow, responding to the pups' own begging, so that each juvenile is presented with a graded sequence of problems matched to what it can currently manage. Or tandem-running ants, where one individual paces and redirects its progress to a follower, effectively controlling the rate at which the other ant's information is acquired. Or, in a register with no biology in it at all, the retiring machinist who spends his final year building jigs and fixtures that make no sense for his own hands and every sense for the apprentice's.

My friend's objection is that I have merely relabelled three things we already have words for, and that each of the three covers my case. Her first candidate is niche construction or ecological inheritance: organisms modify their environments and descendants inherit the modifications. My answer is that the standard examples change what the environment rewards or what selects for which genes, whereas here the reward is untouched and the only thing that has been engineered is the order and difficulty of the learner's encounters — and, crucially, the builder does not inherit or exploit its own construction, which is why the beaver dam and the termite mound feel to me like different cases rather than paradigm cases. Her second candidate is the Gregorian creature, on the grounds that I have simply split one Gregorian across two individuals. My answer is that the Gregorian description is about taking external artefacts into one's own cognition, and my frozen module has no cognition to extend and could not use its own output even if it had. Her third candidate is teaching, and here I am least sure of myself, since teaching is often defined in terms of a tutor modifying its behaviour in the presence of a naive pupil; but the frozen generator emits the same stream whether or not anyone is listening, which seems to me to be a real disanalogy rather than a quibble. So: does this configuration have an established name, and if it does, which of the existing concepts actually subsumes it and which of my three disanalogies dissolves on inspection? If it does not, I would like you to say where it sits relative to the four creature types — a fifth kind, a way of being Popperian or Gregorian by proxy, or a category mistake on my part — and then coin a term with real boundaries: what would count, what would not (the beaver dam, a gene that biases its own developmental environment, a textbook, a parent who only ever praises effort), and what evidence would show that the category earns its place. I want the carving, not the label; a name alone will not settle anything between us.
