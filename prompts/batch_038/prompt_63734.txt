Here's the actual claim I'm defending, which is a bit spicy: entropy increase doesn't explain the arrow of time, it just restates it. The standard story is that the microscopic laws are time-reversal symmetric (I've got a slide on the reversibility objection from Loschmidt and the recurrence objection from Zermelo, so I can handle those), and the asymmetry comes from the universe starting off in an absurdly low entropy state — the Past Hypothesis. My argument is that this buys you an asymmetry only by positing a one-off boundary condition that nothing can test, and it still doesn't explain why memories, records, causation and our ability to intervene all line up pointing the same way. So instead: the real asymmetry is counterfactual. You can hold the past fixed and wiggle the future; you cannot hold the future fixed and wiggle the past, because that would require fixing an insanely specific microstate that you have no handle on. So the arrow is grounded in what agents can actually do, and the Past Hypothesis isn't a law of physics at all — it's a summary of our commitment to that counterfactual asymmetry, dressed up as cosmology. I also want to claim that Boltzmann brains give an extra reason to distrust entropy arguments about our own past, since a fluctuation universe makes memory unreliable, whereas my account grounds reliability in what we can do rather than in what we happen to remember.
