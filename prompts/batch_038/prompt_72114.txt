In a general-interest essay on the science of happiness I read the following attempt to explain how hedonism survived Bentham:

"Bentham held that pleasures differ only in amount, never in kind, which is why he could say that, quantity being equal, a children's game of push-pin is worth as much as music or poetry. Mill's repair was to add quality: on his account the pleasures of the intellect feel different from the pleasures of the body, not merely stronger, and which pleasures those are is settled by asking people who have tried both, since they invariably prefer a dissatisfied human life to a satisfied animal one. Because the difference sits inside the experience itself, the usual complaint that pleasures are heterogeneous — that the satisfaction of finishing a proof shares no felt ingredient with the comfort of a hot bath — simply misses Mill's target, since he never claimed the experiences resemble one another. The price of the repair is that Mill has to concede a standard of value over and above pleasure, which is why his critics conclude that qualitative hedonism is not hedonism at all, and why the test of competent judges persuades only those who already rank intellectual pleasures above bodily ones."

I came away thinking Mill is really a perfectionist in hedonist clothing, though I suspect the essay got me there by a detour. Please go through it claim by claim: what would a Mill scholar accept as it stands, what is a defensible simplification, and what misrepresents either Mill or the objection he is answering? In particular, tell me whether the essay has run together two things a hedonist keeps distinct — something about how pleasures feel and something about what makes them valuable — and what changes if they are pulled apart. Then say whether the closing conclusion survives that correction: if qualitative hedonism is not thereby shown to be non-hedonistic, what does the heterogeneity problem actually threaten, and how might a hedonist who does not think of pleasure as a single sensation still answer it?
