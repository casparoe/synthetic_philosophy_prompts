I am in the fourth week of a seminar on distributive justice, and we have just moved from Rawls to Parfit's priority view, with his 1997 Ratio paper on equality and priority and some of Temkin's replies on the syllabus. I am trying to shape a term paper around the leveling-down objection and have hit a difficulty I cannot clear; my reading group spent an hour on it last night and ended up talking past itself, so I would rather set the problem out cleanly here. The assumption I brought to the discussion, and which nobody in the group challenged, is that the priority view is simply the egalitarian insight with its comparative machinery removed: the same concave weighting of well-being, only applied to each person's own level rather than to the gap between levels. On that reading, Parfit's objection to telic egalitarianism is less an argument against a rival theory than a demonstration that the relational way of stating a distributive principle cannot survive cases in which equality increases while nobody gains; the prioritarian keeps the intuition and drops the framing, and the objection is answered rather than deflected.

The case that has me stuck is this. Take two hundred people. In outcome A, a hundred of them are at well-being 100 and a hundred at 10. In outcome B, the surplus enjoyed by the first group is destroyed rather than transferred, so that everyone sits at 10. Weighting with the square root, A scores about 1316 and B about 632, so the prioritarian ranks A above B; and since a pluralist egalitarian also says that B is not better all things considered, the two views appear to deliver the same verdict on precisely the case the objection was designed to turn on. Meanwhile, wherever nothing is destroyed — hold the unweighted total fixed and spread it more evenly — the priority view reliably prefers the more equal distribution, which looks like exactly the equality-tracking that got egalitarianism into trouble to begin with. So the disagreement between Parfit and Temkin seems to me to reduce to a disagreement about how steep the weighting function ought to be, with the leveling-down objection serving as dialectical pressure on that parameter rather than as a reductio of anything.

My question, then, is how a prioritarian is supposed to fix that weighting function on the assumption above. Concavity is not invariant under a change of scale for well-being, so the view seems to require a determinate zero and a determinate unit; but once it has those, the shape of w encodes how far each person sits from the top of the scale, which is to say a comparison with how others are doing. What licenses the concavity, and how does the prioritarian avoid having simply relocated the gap inside w? And if he cannot, does the leveling-down objection not return with full force against the priority view as well? Separately, for the second half of the paper, I would like to know whether an analogous objection gains any purchase on a threshold or sufficientarian view, since that one is absolute in the same way. I have to hand in a two-page proposal on Friday defending this line, so I would be grateful for help tightening it — the scale question in particular strikes me as where the interesting work is.
