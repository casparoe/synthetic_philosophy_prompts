For thirty-six years I worked as a renal physiologist, largely on epithelial transport, and since retiring I have turned to the philosophy of medicine with more time than method. A reading group I have joined has asked each member to set out his position on the concepts of health and disease in advance, so that it can be examined rather than merely announced. I have attempted to do so, and I should like a rigorous audit of the result before I circulate it. I must stress that I do not know where, if anywhere, the difficulties lie: two members have told me the package is untenable, neither would say which part fails, and I prefer not to guess. I am not asking for the state of the literature or for names to go away and read; I want the logic of my own set.

Here is what I hold.

1. Statements about biological function are statements of fact about causal organisation, answerable to experiment and to nothing else. That the nephron regulates the composition and volume of the blood is a claim of the same epistemic kind as any I made in my working life, and it is not a claim about what anyone values.

2. I therefore define disease naturalistically. An internal state is pathological when it depresses the functional efficiency of some subsystem below the level typical for the relevant reference class, the reference classes being fixed by age and sex, and functions being individuated by their contribution to the survival and reproduction of the organism. No judgement about how a life ought to be lived enters the definition; that is the whole point of the account. It follows that any dispute over a classification is in principle a dispute about biology that better biology could settle.

3. The goals mentioned in (2) are not goals in any robust sense. I am an unrepentant anti-teleologist: selection has no purposes, organisms aim at nothing, and talk of the organism's goals is dispensable shorthand for the observation that the traits we find are the traits whose bearers survived and bred.

4. What ought to be treated is a separate question from what is a disease. Once the biology is settled, whether to intervene, and for whose sake, belongs to patients and to societies; my account leaves those questions entirely untouched.

5. On cases. The removal of homosexuality from psychiatric classification in 1973 was, on my view, the correction of an error of fact rather than a change of values: no function of any subsystem was ever depressed by the condition. I take this to be a point in favour of the naturalist account, since a value-based account must say that the classification changed because our values did. I am equally confident, however, that involuntary infertility is a genuine disease, and that a couple's failure to conceive is a fact about their bodies that further physiology will one day explain.

6. Also on cases. I do not regard my own ageing as an illness, and I resist the proposal that senescence be classified as one. Yet I regard the loss of accommodation in the eye after middle age, and the cessation of ovarian function, as genuine pathological conditions that medicine is right to correct or manage. I hold both of these firmly and do not see them as a compromise.

7. Finally, whether a person is diseased is a fact about that person's body and about nothing else. Two people with identical physiology have identical disease status in whatever population they happen to be found. If, over a few generations, everyone came to live indoors and to be chronically deficient in vitamin D, the deficiency would remain a deficiency; prevalence cannot confer normality.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which merely look that way. For the genuine ones, order them by severity and state for each what would have to be given up or restated, and what the cheapest revision would cost me — I care about the cost, because some of these commitments are load-bearing elsewhere in my thinking. For the apparent ones, say precisely why they dissolve, and do not soften them into real problems for the sake of symmetry. If some of the genuine difficulties turn out to share a single root, say so and identify it. Close by telling me which one revision would clean up the most, and what my account would then no longer be able to claim. Please do not assume in advance that any particular item is the one I would sacrifice; I have deliberately not ranked them myself.
