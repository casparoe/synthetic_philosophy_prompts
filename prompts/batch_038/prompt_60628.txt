Retired from a physiology lab about three years back and I've since fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole, consciousness mostly. Last week I read that dialogue in The Mind's I where the Tortoise and Achilles argue about Einstein's brain — the bit where the Tortoise can't see why you'd bother playing a record instead of just hanging it on the wall and taking in the music "all at once." That got me thinking about the actual history: the brain really was sliced up and photographed, so in principle someone could print it, one slab per page, as a big atlas.

So here's a scoring exercise I'd like you to do. Rate each item below on two seperate 0–10 scales:

(A) how likely it is that there's something its like to be that thing, and
(B) conditional on there being something it's like, how much that experience would be *Einstein's* mind in particular rather than just some generic one.

1. The atlas sitting closed in a dark archive, never opened again.
2. The atlas being read once, cover to cover, by a single grad student.
3. A machine in a windowless room that flips through the pages in a fixed loop, forever, recording nothing and being watched by nobody.
4. The press run itself — the moment the ink is being laid down on the sheets.
5. A simulation somebody rebuilds from the photographs and then runs.
6. As a control: the original brain, alive in a vat and hooked up to normal senses.

Then tell me where your (A) and (B) orderings disagree most, and what you think that disagreement is actually tracking. And separately: does the "has to be spread out in time" business the Tortoise keeps harping on predict your (A) scores, or is it a red herring? I want your numbers and your reasons, not a tour of who-said-what.
