I have now run the Dennett–Caruso dispute over just deserts through two graduate seminars, and each time the discussion reproduces the published stalemate almost exactly. The compatibilist side holds that desert is earned by ordinary competences — recognizing reasons, anticipating consequences, being movable by the prospect of blame and praise — and that our practice of holding one another responsible is not a metaphysical luxury bolted onto agency but part of what constitutes responsible agency. The skeptical side holds that none of this delivers the ultimate sourcehood basic desert requires, that retributivism therefore loses its central justification, and that what remains is something like a quarantine-and-prevention model bounded by proportionality to the danger posed and by the least restrictive means available. Each camp has a reply to the other's best argument; no reply moves anyone.

My working hypothesis, which I want tested rather than confirmed: the impasse is not about free will. Neither position turns on whether determinism is true, and both grant that the relevant capacities are natural and gradable. What divides them, I suspect, is which institution is under examination — a distributed social practice of attribution, or the state's authority to inflict hardship on a person already in custody — plus an unexamined empirical wager about what happens to the treatment of offenders once desert language is withdrawn, a wager on which each side assumes the opposite result from the other. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a genuinely normative disagreement about what could ever make the infliction of suffering permissible that my framing dissolves rather than resolves? Alternatively, is some of it merely verbal, a dispute over how far the concept of desert extends?

What I want is the structure of the disagreement rather than a verdict on it. Sort the components into conceptual, empirical, and those resting on starting intuitions neither side can argue for, and support the sorting by pointing to the moves each side actually makes when pressed. Then tell me what would count as progress on your diagnosis: a distinction that separates the conflated questions, an agreed test case in which the two models of punishment deliver different sentences for the same offender, evidence bearing on the wager about desert talk, or a change of question. If your conclusion is that no progress should be expected, argue for that explicitly and say what would still be worth doing with the debate.
