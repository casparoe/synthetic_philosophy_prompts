I work on regulatory impact analyses at a state agency — we mostly follow the federal template — and one piece of the standard furniture has started to bother me. When we cost out a rule that cuts emissions, every ton of CO2 avoided gets credited with a dollar figure. EPA's late-2023 update puts that at roughly $190 a ton at the central 2 percent discount rate, and the number falls out of an integrated assesment model that projects GDP and emissions forward, turns them into temperature, turns temperature into a smooth percentage-of-GDP damage curve calibrated mostly on a handful of sectors (agriculture, labor supply, energy demand, some health outcomes), and then discounts all of it back to today. Damages the literature can't monetize — ecosystem collapse, tipping points, conflict and migration — get left out, and the agency says plainly that its estimate is therefore probably an underestimate. In the very same documents, though, the same agency monetizes a statistical life for the particulate co-benefits at north of ten million dollars per person, plus asthma ER visits and lost workdays. Then the number does real work: if the rule's abatement cost per ton lands under it, the rule pays for itself; if it lands over, somebody has to make a different kind of argument entirely.

So what theory of value does this practice presuppose, and how much of that is actually load-bearing?

A couple of things I'd specifically want you to get at. First, is the discounting separable from the rest — could you run the identical machinery at a zero pure time preference and have it presuppose somthing different, or is the discount rate just a parameter inside a deeper commitment? Second, the tons are fungible across sectors and borders, which seems to assume that what matters is the atmosphere's ledger and not who emitted or whether the emitting activity should have existed at all. Third, and this one might not belong here at all: the whole apparatus assumes we can harm people who don't exist yet, but exactly which people will exist in 2150 depends on the policy choices being costed. Does the practice have to ignore that, or can it run with the problem live?
