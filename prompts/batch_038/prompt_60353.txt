So I volunteer a few hours a week at a small grantmaking group, and at our last strategy session the new program officer kept reaching for a triage analogy. Choosing between causes, she said, is like being the medic at a mass casualty scene: you don't go to the worst-off first, you go to the ones you can actually save, and you tag the hopeless cases expectant and walk past them without feeling guilty about it. She used that to argue we should basically rank causes by tractability and let importance fall where it may, and that anyone who wants to fund the most important-sounding but least workable causes is just being squeamish.

Can you audit the analogy for me properly? I want the correspondence laid out explicitly — a table is fine. What in the casualty scene is supposed to be importance, whats tractability, what if anything is neglectedness, what plays the role of the fixed number of medics and stretchers, who's the medic and who's the patient, and what would tagging a whole cause area "expectant" actually consist in given that these aren't identifiable individuals.

Then separate the disanalogies that genuinely undercut her argument from the ones that are just decoration. Two I keep circling and can't resolve: first, does the source she's importing even have a slot for neglectedness, or does the three-factor framework quietly lose one of its legs when you run it through a triage lens — in a casualty scene nobody is more or less crowded, everyone's equally untreated until someone gets to them. And second, a triage officer has to take the available medicine as given, whereas how tractable a cause is seems like something a funder can partly change over time by paying for research and tool-building, which would make the expectant tag self-fulfilling rather than a discovery about the world. Are those load-bearing or am I overreading?

Finally: does the walk-past-the-worst-off conclusion survive once all the disanalogies are on the table, and if the analogy can't carry the weight she's putting on it, what should she be reaching for instead — and does the replacement license the same conclusion or a different one?
