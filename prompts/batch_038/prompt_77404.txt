ok so this is for my world history class but it turned into kind of a philosophy question and my teacher said i could go down the rabbit hole, so here goes.

there's an argument i keep hearing in slightly different versions. i heard it in a youtube video about Troy, then from a guy on my school's debate team, and i'm pretty sure the same move gets used about the exodus and about king arthur. it goes like: before writing, stories travelled by word of mouth, and word of mouth is basically a game of telephone. whisper a sentence down a line of 30 kids and by the end it's garbage, so whatever homer wrote down is just garbage that happened to get frozen in ink. conclusion: there's no actual history inside the legend, only the legend.

my teacher wasn't having it and she gave three reasons, which i'm going to put here so you can check whether she's right or just right-ish: (1) in telephone each kid hears the message exactly once, but a poet grows up hearing hundreds of performances of the same story before they ever perform it, so it's not one copy of one copy of one copy; (2) the whisper chain is linear, but at any given moment there are hundreds of people telling the same story in the same region who can correct each other when someone gets it wrong; (3) telephone errors are random, but in real life getting a story wrong has consequences — whose ancestor that is, which family owns that land, which god you owe a sacrifice to — so there's pressure pushing back. she also said you have to know which *kind* of oral material you're dealing with, because some of it is basically fixed word-for-word (prayers, king lists, geneology) and some of it is loose and changes every telling, and the fixed kind behaves nothing like telephone.
