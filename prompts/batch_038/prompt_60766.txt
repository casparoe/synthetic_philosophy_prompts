So I've been living inside a weird book for four years and I need a number, not a shrug.

Quick setup so you know what's actually on the page. It's a closed two-hander, about 300 pages, and there are exactly two characters — twin sisters, Ada-1 and Ada-2. They are supposed to be qualitatively identical in every respect: same body, same memories, same opinions, same scars, same way of holding a cup. Chapters alternate between them, and each chapter is written as the exact mirror of its partner — the same events in the same order, so that for every fact the book gives you about one sister, there's a matching fact about the other. They share a single given name; the numeral is the only thing that ever distinguishes them. Nobody else speaks or appears. No indexical ever leaks (no "the one on the left", no "the one I met first", no narrator standing outside the pair). The last third turns on a confession that is equally true of both of them and of neither in particular, which is the whole point of the exercise.

It reads finished to me. My agent thinks it's a clever gimmick that collapses around page 80. Mine is 0.75 that it works, hers is about 0.2.

Give me your own credence that the thing works, where working means: a reasonably smart literary reader with no philosophy training — book club level — comes away experiencing Ada-1 and Ada-2 as two distinct people rather than one person described twice or a trick, and the novel doesn't get written off as incoherent by the smart reviewers. I don't want a hedge about how art is subjective. I want a number and the decomposition:

- how much rides on whether there is any fact of the matter about which sister is which. Think of that old thought experiment about a universe containing nothing but two exactly similar spheres, and the argument that two things can't be alike in every qualitative respect without being one thing. Give me P(that symmetry is genuinely impossible / the book is secretly about one person) and say what would settle it.
- separately, how much rides on the reader end — whether labels, chapter order and narrative tracking can do the work of an asymmetry even if the metaphysics doesn't cooperate. This might be independent of the first term; tell me if it is, because I've been assuming it is.
- how much rides on a philosophy-literate reviewer having a legitimate case to call it incoherent rather than just unusual.
- whatever other terms you think belong in there.
- how you'd combine them — multiply, or something looser — and why, given the dependencies.
- which term my agent and I most likely disagree about.
- which term you're least sure of, and what would move it: something concrete, like a reading-group test or one particular argument, not "more data."

And one more thing: is there any change I could make that keeps the exact qualitative symmetry intact and raises the number the most? Or is that impossible by definition — does any improvement require importing an asymmetry, which is the one thing the book isn't allowed to have?
