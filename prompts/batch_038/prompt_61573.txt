write me a satirical piece in the form of an official workplace risk assessment — hazard table, likelihood and severity scores, control measures, a residual-risk column, a signature box at the bottom — issued by a national laboratory whose state metaphysics is al-Ghazālī's occasionalism, the one from the seventeenth discussion of the Tahāfut al-Falāsifa. Around 600-700 words, deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader.

The humour has to come from getting the metaphysics right rather than from wackiness: the lab really does deny that the connection between what we call a cause and its effect is necessary, and yet it still owes its technicians a verdict on whether they may hold cotton near a flame. So the likelihood column has to be populated from custom (ʿāda) and observed habit rather than from any power residing in the fire, the hazard descriptions have to be worded so they don't smuggle agency back into inanimate things, and somewhere in the document somebody has to cope with Ibn Rushd's line of attack in the Tahāfut al-Tahāfut — that a natural science which wont say the fire burns the cotton has thrown away explanation altogether, and that al-Ghazālī leans on habit in practice anyway. Don't state those as objections; let them surface as audit findings, a logged nonconformance, a querulous note from legal.

Be committed rather than even-handed: take a definite position on whether your lab is a hard occasionalist or a soft determinist about secondary causes, and let that choice shape the whole document instead of covering both sides. British English please. Then finish with three or four sentences saying where you've taken liberties with the texts or leaned on a contested reading of al-Ghazālī, and why you chose to go that way.
