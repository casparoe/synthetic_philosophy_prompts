i'm a fourth-year grad student in philosophy, mostly working on confirmation theory, and our department's bayesianism reading group closes the term this thursday. last week's session was glymour's problem of old evidence and it went over pretty much everyones head on the first pass, so i want to open thursday with a poem that gets the problem right before we start arguing about the fixes. I'd rather it be memorable than it be expository.

the ask is a sestina. i know that's asking a lot, but the form is the whole point: a sestina never lets a new end-word in, it just rotates the same six through a fixed permutation until they arrive back where they started, which is exactly the shape of the trouble. if e is old then its probability is already one, and conditionalizing on something you're certain of is a no-op, so the rotation should read like a machine spinning without traction. 39 lines, six sestets and a three-line envoi, don't shorten it.

constraints i actually care about:

- you pick the six teleutons, but they should be the furniture of the problem — the theory, the observation, the certainty, the updating, the sort of thing — and they need to be ordinary words, not notation. nobody's writing a line that ends in a probability symbol. a reader who knows the material should be able to say which word is doing which job.
- the first sestet stages the attractive picture: evidence e turns out to be explained by hypothesis H, H goes up, that's confirmation, that's why anyone became a bayesian in the first place.
- the turn shouldn't be located at a stanza break or a rhyme. i want it to be the moment the rotation makes visible that e was already known before H ever showed up, so the likelihood is one and nothing moves. ideally it lands mid-poem, not saved for the envoi.
- work in, quietly and without naming anyone, why the two easy escapes don't rescue things: re-normalizing with jeffrey's rule still leaves a probability of one at one, and the policy of never assigning one to anything does buy you the update but at the price of never being certain of your own arithmetic, which is a steep bill. one of those two should come across as a diagnosis rather than a slogan.
- the envoi should stage the garber-style response: the only genuinely new thing available isn't the evidence, it's a fact about the relation between the theory and the evidence — that H entails e — grasped for the first time. all six end-words need to appear in it, and i want the cost showing: this move obliges you to have a probability space over claims about your own sentences, which is not obviously a thing a real agent has.

accurate enough that someone who works on this would nod, but a good poem first — three people in the group have never seen a bayes net and i don't want to lose them to jargon. and please don't explain the problem anywhere in it or add a gloss at the end. if it needs a footnote, its failed.
