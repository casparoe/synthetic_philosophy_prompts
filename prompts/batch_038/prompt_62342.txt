I am revising a chapter of a manuscript on mental causation in which I lean heavily on a thought experiment I first encountered in Hofstadter and that keeps being recycled in this literature: a sealed chamber — a careenium — packed with small hard objects that ricochet off one another and off the walls. Call the objects simmballs. Their collisions are arranged, by the geometry of the chamber and the history of what has bounced into what, so that recognizable patterns of impacts correspond to recognitions, associations, and something like inferences. An observer standing outside can assign a content to an individual simmball by noting what reliably sets it moving and what it reliably sets moving in turn; on that basis one says that the simmball activated by collisions of a certain sort means, say, grandmother, and that when it careens into another, the meaning has, in a perfectly ordinary sense, done something. My chapter's claim is the strong one: because the collisions just are the symbol-activations rather than their realization, a semantic description is a causal description and not a redescription, so high-level content can be efficacious without competing with the physical account of the same events, and without the usual overdetermination worry. A colleague who works on causation insists the analogy is carrying the entire argument and that it collapses under inspection. Before I resubmit, I want the inspection done properly.

Please give me the correspondence table first: what in the careenium is supposed to play the role of a mental state, of the content it bears, of the causal relation between contents, of the physical substrate, of the chaotic sensitivity that is supposed to make the low-level description intractable and the high-level one indispensable, and — critically — of the person doing the assigning of contents, since the analogy appears to require one. Then separate the disanalogies into those that bear on the intended use and those that are merely picturesque. I already suspect several: that pool balls are sharply individuated while the patterns I am calling symbols are diffuse, overlapping, and variably realized; that collisions are local and contiguous while content can be about what is distant, absent, or disjunctively specified; that an impact cannot be incorrect while a transition between contents can be a mistake, and a story of content that cannot accommodate error is missing something load-bearing; and that the meanings are bestowed from outside by a reader of the chamber, which looks like reintroducing the very homunculus the picture was meant to dissolve. Tell me which of these actually threaten the conclusion and which do not.

Then address the crux directly. Does the careenium exhibit symbols pushing the substrate around, or only the substrate pushing symbols around with the symbol talk retained as a convenient gloss? Which direction of causation does the mechanism as described actually support, and could any arrangement of bouncing objects support the other? Would it help if the system read its own states rather than being read from outside, and if so, what would that take? Finally: does my strong claim survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or must I retreat to the weaker claim that semantic description is indispensable and non-redundant, and what exactly do I lose in retreating? If a better source domain exists — one that could accommodate error, disjunctive content, self-reading, and normativity without presupposing an interpreter — sketch it, and say whether any such source can be given without begging the question.
