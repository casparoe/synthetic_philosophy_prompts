been trying to write down what I actually believe about reparations and I'm worried the pieces dont fit. here they are:

(1) liability is personal. you can owe for what you did or what you signed up for, and thats it — no inherited guilt, no taint running down bloodlines, nobody today owes anything for what their great-great-grandparents did.

(2) I also dont buy the unjust enrichment line. ending up better off because of someone else's wrongdoing, without having done anything yourself, doesnt put you under an obligation to hand it over. benefiting isn't owing.

(3) states are different from their citizens though. the US is a continuing corporate agent, so it can still owe something for slavery and Jim Crow even though no living citizen did anything. the duty attaches to the institution, not to whoever happens to staff it or fund it.

(4) so the amount owed shouldn't be figured as compensation for harm to the claimants. I think the nonidentity point has real bite here — nobody alive is worse off than they'd otherwise have been — and anyway counterfactual welfare math across two centuries is hopeless. better model: an unpaid debt that was never discharged, sized to the value of the labor that was taken.

(5) but claims belong to individuals. I don't want to say a community or a people is the claimant; I want particular living persons with standing to demand payment.

(6) and the case is entirely backward-looking. closing the wealth gap, rebuilding trust in institutions — possible bonuses, not part of why anything is owed.

Go through these and tell me where they actually collide versus where it only looks like they do. I have a feeling something in here is doing double duty but I cant put my finger on it. Rank the tensions by how bad they are, and for each say what the cheapest revision is and what it would cost me. Don't let me off easy by declaring all six mildly awkward.
