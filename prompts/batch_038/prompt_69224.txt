I am in the final year of a philosophy PhD, and the document I owe my committee in three weeks has to do double duty as an application for a nine-month postdoctoral fellowship. What follows is the idea I want to build it around; it is not yet in a state where I could defend it, and I need help making it precise.

The background is the thought experiment Hofstadter runs in Gödel, Escher, Bach: a hollowed-out skull, which he calls a careenium, filled with billiard-ball-like objects he calls simmballs that ricochet off one another and off the walls. Clusters and trajectories of simmballs are supposed to be symbols, and the picture is meant to show that a mind's symbol-level activity and its ball-level activity are one activity described twice, with the symbol level nonetheless doing real work: a pattern of simmballs steers later collisions.

The difficulty is that the familiar debate about downward causation seems to offer only two verdicts on a picture like this. Either the pattern is causally inert, because every collision already has a sufficient ball-level cause; or the pattern is causally real only in a deflationary sense, because saying the pattern did something is a compressed way of saying that many balls did many things. Both verdicts presuppose something I want to question — that for a higher level to be causally real it must contribute force beyond what the lower level contributes, as though causation were additive across levels.

My half-formed idea is that the careenium points to a third role. A pattern does not add force; it narrows which ball-level histories are accessible from the current state, and being narrowed in the right way may be all the causal work there is for a level to do. But this needs to be split, and I think that is where the real work lies. A constraint can be static: the walls of the careenium restrict the balls, but the walls are themselves just more substrate, so this collapses back into the substrate pushing the substrate. Or it can be self-maintaining: a pattern that recruits fresh collisions in a way that preserves the pattern, so that the constraint in force at a later time depends on the pattern obtaining at an earlier one. My hunch is that only the second kind is a candidate for symbol-level causation, and that the viability of any account of mental causation turns on whether it can say what makes the second kind non-trivially different from the first rather than merely more complicated.

Please turn this into the proposal. I need:

1. A precise statement of the research question — one with a determinate answer, and one that is not simply the stock question of whether downward causation is compatible with causal closure. Make explicit what the constraint-without-force account is committed to and what would falsify it.

2. A short taxonomy of the positions the project must answer to: the causal exclusion worry, non-reductive physicalism's appeal to realization, interventionist or counterfactual accounts of level-relative causation, and the deflationary redescription view. For each, why it leaves my question open. Refer to these positions descriptively; I will supply the references myself, so please do not invent citations or a bibliography.

3. The method: what I would actually do, month by month across the nine months, marking where conceptual analysis ends and where argument for a positive account begins. Include one sustained worked example — a single ordinary action, say deciding to raise an arm, described at both levels, with the self-maintaining constraint spelled out step by step — and say how much weight that example can bear before the project needs a general theory behind it.

4. Why this approach rather than the obvious alternatives: adopting an interventionist notion of causation, conceding that content is inert while behaviour is not, or dissolving the question by abandoning level talk altogether.

5. What a result would look like and what failure would look like. I am especially worried about one failure mode: that the account proves notationally equivalent to the additive picture, differing only in vocabulary. Say what I would have to establish to rule that out, and whether that is feasible in nine months.

6. An honest scope assessment: what to cut or defer, and what the smallest defensible version of the project is if the full version will not fit.
