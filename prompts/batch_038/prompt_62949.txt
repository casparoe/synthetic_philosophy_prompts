I'm auditing a free will seminar at a neighbouring department this term and there's two people in it who have been going around in circles since October, and last week the prof finally told them to sit down and write out the thing they actually agree on. They both refused, on principle. So now I'm morbidly curious what that document would look like if someone competent actually drafted it, and I figure you might as well be that someone. The two camps: call them the Deflater and the Flickerer. The Deflater thinks Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the principle of alternative possibilities land: in the actual sequence the agent has no robust alternative, she's plainly responsible anyway, so PAP is false, and since leeway was the main engine incompatibilists used to get from determinism to no-responsibility, the engine is now gone and responsibility is compatible with determinism. The Flickerer thinks the cases fail on the familiar Kane/Widerker/Ginet dilemma — either the intervener needs a prior sign of what the agent is going to do, in which case there are residual alternatives in that sign, or it doesn't need one, in which case the case stops being a case about determinism and starts being a case about a manipulator, and manipulators are why we blame less, not more. And the Flickerer adds that even a successful Frankfurt case would only have shown something about leeway, not about where the action has to come from, so the compatibilist victory is imaginary either way.

Here's the case they've been fighting over, which I made up after seminar and which I want the document to actually turn on rather than on some generic Jones/Black setup. Mara has to decide whether to sign a letter that will cost her a job and expose something bad her employer did. She has an implant, which she knows about, that watches one specific cluster of neurons. That cluster fires roughly 900 milliseconds before she forms any intention to sign — but it's a weak correlate, it also fires on plenty of occasions when she ends up doing nothing at all, and it isn't anything she can feel, notice, or steer. The implant is set so that if the cluster fires and Mara hasn't signed within a four minute window, it forces her hand. In the actual sequence the cluster fires early, the window runs, and Mara signs on her own with no intervention. There's a second version of the case where the trigger isn't the cluster at all but a prior episode of hers — she spends those four minutes drafting and deleting a refusal email before signing — and the implant is keyed to that drafting-and-deleting episode instead. I want the joint statement to work through both versions, because my guess is that the two camps will disagree about which version is the hard one and for completely different reasons: in the first version the only surviving alternative looks subpersonal and useless for grounding blame but the timing is weird, and in the second version the alternative looks genuinely agential but the case has started to smell like manipulation. Also worth pinning down explicitly: at what moment does an alternative possibility have to be available for PAP to be satisfied, since in version one Mara can refrain right up until the cluster fires and cannot refrain afterwards.

So: draft the document. Start with a numbered list of claims both of them could sign — things about what the cases are supposed to show, about what counts as an alternative possibility worth caring about, about which intuitions in the actual sequence are shared, about what would follow for the wider free will debate if one side were simply granted everything. Then a disagreements table, and I mean genuinely precise rows, not strawmen: each disagreement written twice, once in phrasing the Deflater would accept as a fair description of her own view and once in phrasing the Flickerer would accept as fair to his, plus a column saying what each of them would have to give up if the row went against them. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling the thing would actually move somebody — and for each one a concrete case, experiment, or argument both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance so we can check them later, and the versions of Mara above are fair game as candidate crux-cases if you think they do the work. Finally a short section saying which parts of the whole mess you think were verbal all along — my suspicion is that robust and could have done otherwise are doing at least three different jobs between them and nobody in the room noticed — and why. Please don't include a bibliography or tell me who said what, I know the literature and I don't need citations, what I want is the formulations. Two pages-ish is plenty, and if you think one of the two rows in the table is not a real disagreement, say so in the table rather than padding it out.
