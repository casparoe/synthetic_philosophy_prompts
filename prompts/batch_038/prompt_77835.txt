**Question 3 (20 marks: a=6, b=5, c=6, d=3).** On Sunday a fair coin is flipped and Beauty is put to sleep. If it lands heads she is woken once, on Monday. If it lands tails she is woken on Monday and again on Tuesday, and after each waking she is given a drug that erases her memory of that waking. Whenever she wakes she is not told the outcome or what day it is. (a) Reconstruct Elga's argument for 1/3, spelling out each premise. (b) Explain Lewis's halfer reply and say which step of the argument he resists. (c) For each of the two views, give Beauty's credence on Monday in HEADS, in its being Monday, and in HEADS given that it is Monday. (d) Say briefly what the disagreement implies about conditionalization.

**Student's answer:**

(a) Elga argues from two premises. First, given TAILS, Monday and Tuesday are equally likely: from inside the tails branch she has two awakenings that feel exactly alike and no way to tell which one she is in, so there is no reason to weight one over the other. Second, given that it is Monday, HEADS and TAILS are equally likely: her Monday experience is the same whether the coin came up heads or tails, and nothing about the experiment makes a Monday waking likelier in one branch than the other. Put together, P(HEADS and Monday) = P(TAILS and Monday) = P(TAILS and Tuesday). Those three centered possibilities are the ones she could be in and they come out equal, so P(HEADS) = 1/3, and it follows that P(Monday) = 2/3. You get the same number if you weight each possibility by how many observers are in it, and also from betting, if a bookie offers her the same bet on HEADS at every waking, since in the tails case she takes the bet twice.

(b) Lewis says 1/2. The coin is fair so ch(HEADS) = 1/2 and stays 1/2, and what Beauty gains on waking — that she is now awake inside the experiment — is inadmissible evidence, so the Principal Principle does not let it pull her credence off the chance. She already knew on Sunday that she would be woken at least once, so the waking carries no news, and a credence cannot move without news. The step he resists is Elga's second premise: conditional on Monday, Lewis thinks HEADS is likelier than TAILS, because if the coin came up heads she is guaranteed to be having a Monday waking, while if it came up tails only half of her wakings are Mondays.

(c) Thirder: on waking P(HEADS) = 1/3 and P(Monday) = 2/3. P(HEADS given Monday) is also 1/3, because being told it is Monday only rules out the Tuesday waking, and the thirder's whole case is that the possibilities left over were equal all along. Halfer: Lewis is a double halfer, so P(HEADS) = 1/2 on waking and still 1/2 once she is told it is Monday, i.e. P(HEADS given Monday) = 1/2. And P(Monday) = 1/2, since from where she sits there is no fact about which day it is that could tilt her credance toward one side of the coin.

(d) Both sides agree the interesting thing is that her credence in an uncentered proposition, HEADS, seems to shift between Sunday and Monday without her learning any uncentered fact. So either indexical information like *it is Monday now* counts as evidence and conditionalization has to be generalized to centered content, or credences in centered content are not governed by ordinary conditionalization at all. Elga takes the first route: she does acquire new evidence, self-locating evidence, and conditionalizing on it is what drops her to 1/3. Lewis denies she acquires any, so conditionalization leaves her where she was and no new rule is needed.
