So here's the list — I'd like to know whether it hangs together, which bits genuinely pull against each other, which ones only look bad, and what the cheapest thing to give up would be.

(1) In a clean two-person case — same evidence, both of us sober and careful, genuinely symmetric — I think you should split the difference and drop to roughly 0.5 on the disputed claim. Equal weight, basically.

(2) But I don't think discovering a disagreement forces you to bracket everything you already worked out about whether the other person got it right. If I have some reason to think they misread the data that isn't just "they disagree with me", I can use that reason to demote them from peer status.

(3) The self-undermining worry — that conciliationism has to eat itself once you notice that the people arguing about conciliationism disagree about it — I don't think it's fatal, because I don't see why the conciliatory pressure automatically travels up to the meta level. First order: conciliate. Meta level: I get to dig in.

(4) I'm a total evidence person: what you ought to believe is fixed by everything you have, all of it together. And I don't buy that there are cases where it's rational to end up with outright incoherent beliefs — no rational dilemmas, no "rational but inconsistent" combinations.

(5) That said, I do think learning that you were drugged, or hypoxic, or badly distracted when you formed a belief is a special kind of evidence — evidence about your evidence rather than about the world — and I think in at least some cases you're allowed to ignore it when you independently know it's misleading.

(6) And there's an asymmetry I keep coming back to: I have first-person access to how my own reasoning went on this occasion and my opponent doesn't. That makes exact peerhood pretty rare in practice, maybe rarer than the literature lets on.

Can you go through these pairwise and tell me which combinations are actually in tension versus which just look bad on first pass? Please rank the real ones by how serious they are instead of dumping them all on me as equally worrying, and for each say specifically which commitment has to be weakened and how it would have to be weakened. I'm especially interested in whether (2) is secretly a licence for (3) or whether (2)'s own proviso actually blocks (3). And then: is there one single revision that would clean up the most damage at once?
