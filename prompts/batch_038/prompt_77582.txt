so I'm presenting my views on political killing at a reading group next week — mostly a Thomist natural law crowd — and I wrote out my commitments beforehand so they can go after the whole package instead of nitpicking one line at a time. Can you audit it for me and tell me whether it actually hangs together?

here's where I've landed:

(1) killing a tyrant on your own initiative is sedition. Sedition is a sin against the unity and peace of the multitude, it's always mortal, and it falls most heavily on the one who starts it, so the would-be tyrannicide is the worst party in the exchange.

(2) private people may never kill; taking a life requires either public authority acting for the common good or a direct command from God.

(3) the biblical cases don't touch (2). Judith and Ehud and the Maccabean martyrs are praiseworthy precisely because they had a special commission, which puts them outside the ordinary subject-ruler relation rather than making them private citizens with good instincts.

(4) killing a usurper — someone who seized power and has no title to it at all — is permitted to anyone, and I'd say required if it's the only way to free the city.

(5) I want to resist the view that a tyrant's authority is merely apparent or a sham. He really holds office and really has jurisdiction; his rule is unjust but it's genuine rule, and that's exactly why you can't just take him out.

(6) intending evil as a means is never allowed, even for a great good, and killing an innocent is always intending evil as a means.

(7) a ruler who's deposed, or whose subjects have legitimately thrown off his authority, loses his immunity. So there are cases where a former ruler is a perfectly legitimate target even though nobody who kills him holds public authority.

Which of these genuinely pull against each other, ranked by how bad the tension is, and what has to give in each case? I'd also like to know which ones only look inconsistent and dissolve once you get the terms straight — I expect a couple do, and I don't want to "fix" them and end up worse off. Then: what's the single revision that would clean up the most at the least cost?

Two things I want you to actually engage with rather than paper over. First, if a target in (4) or (7) is by definition someone without legitimate authority, is there any superior left whose permission would be missing, and if not, what work is (2) doing in those cases? Second, what makes (1) true in the tyrant case at all, given (5) — who exactly is the seditious party when the regime is unjust?

And feel free to tell me where I've mis-stated the source rather than just where I've contradicted myself — a few of these came from other people's summaries and I'm not sure I've got them right. I mostly want to know which one of the seven I'd have to drop, or reword most heavily, to keep teh rest.
