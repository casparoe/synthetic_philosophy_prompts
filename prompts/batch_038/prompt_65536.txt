setup: I'm building the first two sessions of a methods seminar for first-year history students and the whole unit is on non-human causes — plague, drought, soil exhaustion, the Tambora eruption, the crops and livestock that crossed the Atlantic, that sort of thing. His position is that all of this is causal wallpaper: if you say the climate caused the crisis of the seventeenth century you haven't explained anything, you've just waved at a background condition and given it a proper name. My position is that he's smuggling in a prejudice — that only human decisions count as causes — and that if a pathogen takes out a third of your taxpayers in four years, that thing is doing work no human agent did. so what I want from you is the spec, not the argument.
