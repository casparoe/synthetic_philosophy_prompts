I run operations at a community food pantry — about 35 staff and volunteers, we do hot meals plus a small benefits-enrollment desk that only recieves grant funding through June. A while back I noticed that maybe a third of the people coming in for groceries were almost certainly eligible for state medical assistance and had never applied. The paperwork is miserable, and most people just assume they don't qualify. So I changed our intake clipboard. It used to ask "would you like information about applying for benefits?" with a yes/no checkbox, and hardly anybody ticked yes. Now it says we'll book you a 20-minute appointment with an enrollment counselor at the end of your visit unless you tick the box that says no thanks. Appointment attendance roughly tripled, and our board is thrilled because enrollment numbers are the one thing they can put in a report.

Then one of our volunteer intake people — she's retired, taught high school civics for thirty years, very sharp — told me she thinks what I did is manipulative and that we've started treating people like children. Her line was that the old form respected people's judgment and mine doesn't, and that if I have to trick somebody into doing something that's good for them, then it isn't really their choice anymore. I tried to argue back. I said everybody who walks in is eligible, nobody's forced, they can tick the box and leave, and if you'd asked those same people a year ago whether they'd want help applying, they'd have said yes. She said "that last part is exactly the problem," which I still don't fully understand. My own sister, who's a nurse, sides with her and keeps saying the whole office culture thing matters more than the form, which may or may not be relevant.

The part I actually haven't told anybody is this. When I redid the layout I put the tick-box in the lower corner instead of at the top, in a slightly smaller font next to the signature line, and I told the front desk to explain the appointment before giving people the clipboard and pen. It's a few seconds of extra effort to say no, nothing dramatic, and I figured that if the appointment is genuinely in their interest, a little friction on the way out is worth it — plus the ones who ticked it reflexively without reading are often the ones who come back asking why nobody signed them up. So: is that second change over a line that the first change didn't cross? My instinct insists they're different, but when I try to say what the difference is, all I come up with is that one worked and one felt sneaky, which doesn't seem like a real principle. How would I even check, going forward, whether some change I'm making to the paperwork is the acceptable kind — is there anything to go on other than whether people complain or whether the numbers go up?
