Retired physicist here, only recently caught the philosophy bug, so please bear with me. Our village council had a row last month about a pot of money that had been collected specifically for a borehole project in Malawi. Some wanted to redirect it to the local youth club, and the whole thing got shut down with 'charity begins at home'. Everyone nodded, me included, but I've been chewing it over since and I suspect that single sentence can be doing at least four different jobs: a claim about where our duties come from, a claim about where our help does the most good, a claim about the order we ought to deliberate in, or just a claim about what's permitted rather than required.

Can you lay the candidate readings out as a menu, and for each say whether it's near-trivial, substantive but defensible, or just false? Then tell me which reading, if any, actually licenses redirecting money that was already earmarked for the overseas project — that wrinkle is the bit I can't get straight. Two councillors were using the same words differently, one for 'home first, whatever's left goes abroad' and one for 'we owe strangers nothing at all', so say which reading each of them needs and whether they can have it.

One hunch of my own, possibly naive: the reading about where help does most good doesn't look partialist at all, it looks like cosmopolitanism in disguise, since it makes where I give track where need is greatest rather than where people are related to me. If that's right say so bluntly, and say whether anyone in that room was actually making it.
