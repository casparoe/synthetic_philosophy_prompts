Been going back and forth with a friend from my old LW meetup crowd who runs a small ambient/techno label, and he's weaponized Schopenhauer against my playlists in a way I cant quite take apart. The bit he keeps pasting is from the second volume's chapter on the metaphysics of music, where Schopenhauer says the four voices of harmony — bass, tenor, alto, soprano, or ground note, third, fifth, octave — "correspond to the four levels in the series of beings, which is to say the mineral world, plant kingdom, animal kingdom and human beings," and then reads the old voice-leading rule (keep the bass well below the upper three, extended harmony beats close harmony, and close spacing happens anyway "only due to limited instrumental range") as "the musical analogue of the basic constitution of nature by virtue of which organic creatures are much more closely related to each other than to the lifeless, inorganic masses of the mineral world." Stack that on the headline claim from the first volume — that music alone isn't a copy of the phenomena the way painting and poetry are but a copy of the will itself — and my friend thinks he has an actual ontology of pitch register, not a metaphor. I tried to steelman it for a week and now I'm stuck.

His argument: the low end is inorganic mass, the top voice is the striving intellectual individual, so a track where the sub carries the hook — 808 glides, wobble bass as the lead, footwork where the bassline is the tune, a drop whose payload is pure low frequency — is metaphysically inverted, the mineral world doing the singing, and that inversion is exactly why such music hits hard physically but feels dumb and empty by the twentieth listen. He adds that mastering engineers are unknowingly applying Schopenhauer: fat sub, a gap, then mids and highs stacked on top is just extended harmony, which he notes Schopenhauer calls "much more powerful and beautiful," and he's been rearranging his own tracks to keep the bass slow and purely foundational with all the melodic striving up top, and swears its fixed them. When I push back he says I'm confusing acoustics with metaphysics, which is a fun inversion, since I'm fairly sure Schopenhauer is the one doing that — he cites the harmonic series and then reads a metaphysical gap off it, calling it "a striking confirmation" of the whole system.

Can you audit the analogy properly rather than just tell me he's wrong? I want the correspondence table first — what's playing the role of mineral mass, plants, animals, the human intellect, the harmony-as-whole versus the melody line — then the disanalogies sorted into the ones that actually bite against his genre-ranking claim and the ones that are merely local to four-part chorale texture and thoroughbass practice. Two I've got partway but can't finish: (a) register roles are functional, not absolute — a cello or bassoon melody sits above a violin's low double stops, and you can transpose a tune down an octave and it's still the same tune, so if pitch height were ontological rank, transposition would demote human striving to plant life; and (b) if the registers really map onto the grades of the will's objectification, and those grades just are the Platonic Ideas, then the mapping makes music a copy of the Ideas after all, which is the opposite of the doctrine it's supposed to illustrate. Are those fatal, or the kind of thing a Schopenhauerian can absorb by saying the mapping tracks the will's self-objectification rather than the Ideas? Also: he's using the analogy as an evaluative standard while Schopenhauer used it as evidence — do the disanalogies that matter differ between those two uses, or is it the same audit twice? And say what the whole thing quietly imports that the target lacks — a vertical hierarchy, four fixed grades, convention read as nature, pitch height as depth of being. Tell me whether his conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and if there's a better analogy for whatever the register mapping is actually tracking, I'd rather have that than a defense of Schopenhauer.
