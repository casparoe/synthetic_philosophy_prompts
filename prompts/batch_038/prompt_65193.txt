I have spent twenty-odd years working in functional safety — hazard analysis, risk acceptance criteria, safety cases — and roughly a decade reading rationalist and effective-altruism discussions of the duties-of-rescue literature on the side. Those two vocabularies have never met in my head in a disciplined way, and I want to force the meeting. This is a translation exercise, not a primer and not a persuasion attempt: assume I already know the shallow-pond argument, the strong and moderate principles, the demandingness and integrity objections, the fair-share objection, and the effectiveness rejoinder. Please do not re-argue any of them in my favor or against me.

**The task.** Restate that debate in the vocabulary I actually use every day, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report. Treat my vocabulary as a real normative framework with its own commitments, not as a supply of metaphors. The apparatus I want you to draw on includes: hazard identification and the hazard log; risk expressed as a function of severity, exposure, and controllability; the tolerability bands our regulator works with (unacceptable, tolerable subject to reduction, broadly acceptable); ALARP and the gross-disproportion gloss on it; residual risk and the question of who is left holding it; the safety case in claim–argument–evidence form; allocation of a requirement to a specific component or actor, i.e. the identification of a duty-holder; the difference between creating a hazard and merely being exposed to one; the SOTIF insight that a system can be correctly specified, fully functional, and free of faults, and still produce harm; the assumption of a reasonable user; and the practice of writing assumptions down and getting someone to sign them off. Where you cannot use a piece of this apparatus without treating the distant stranger as a user of a system I neither built nor operate, say so out loud — that is exactly the kind of distortion I want surfaced.

**What must be translated.** Please handle each of the following separately and label them (a) through (i):

(a) the parity premise: that failing to wade into shallow water to save a child at trivial cost to myself stands in the same moral relation as failing to transfer the equivalent sum to save a child's life far away;
(b) the claim that physical distance, and the number of other bystanders who are also failing to act, are morally irrelevant;
(c) the strong principle (give until you descend to the level of marginal utility) set against the moderate principle (give until further giving would cost you something comparable in moral seriousness);
(d) the refusal to classify these acts as supererogatory — the insistence that they are duties rather than saintly extras;
(e) the assimilation of letting die to killing in this setting;
(f) the absence of any causal-contribution requirement: the duty attaches to my capacity to prevent the harm, not to my having produced the peril;
(g) the demandingness/integrity objection — that a morality requiring this leaves the agent no ground projects, and that the argument draws its force from a comparison that misdescribes ordinary moral life;
(h) the fair-share or collective-action objection — whether my obligation expands to cover the shortfall left by non-compliant others, or is capped at my share;
(i) the effectiveness constraint — that the duty is discharged by the intervention with the best expected benefit per unit of cost, together with the worry that expected-benefit estimates for distant rescue may be far less reliable than the estimates we would trust for an intervention within our line of sight.

**Honesty requirement.** Do not smuggle my framework's built-in assumptions in and then report the resulting mismatch as a finding. In particular: that causal contribution is what triggers an obligation; that a risk is legitimate once it has been accepted by the exposed party; that cost to the duty-holder is an admissible term in the calculation; and that some identified person must sign off. Where a restatement only works because one of these has been quietly adopted, flag it rather than presenting the translation as clean. Where the two frameworks disagree about what counts as a reason, leave the disagreement visible.

**The residue report.** For each of (a)–(i), tell me which of three things happened: it translates cleanly, it translates only with a distortion you can name, or it has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Then diagnose each failure: is the residue a limitation of safety engineering — a genuinely impoverished account of who owes what to whom — or evidence that the philosophical claim was less determinate, or emptier, than it looked on first reading? I also want the reverse residue: concepts on my side (gross disproportion, residual-risk transfer, the reasonable-user assumption, the sign-off requirement, and anything else you think qualifies) that have no counterpart on the philosophical side, and what the philosophers might gain by acquiring one.

**Bottom line.** After all of that, answer the question I actually care about: once the debate has been pushed through my vocabulary, is there a live disagreement left between the Singerian position and the demandingness/fair-share objectors that is not merely a dispute about where the duty-holder boundary gets drawn? And if the residue turns out to be large, does that indict my framework, or does it suggest the whole debate has been resting on an unstated assumption about causal contribution that nobody ever argues for?

**Format.** Write the translation and the residue report as prose, in the order given above, roughly 1,300 to 1,800 words, and then append a single JSON object summarizing it in exactly this schema, with no additional fields and no commentary after it:

- `translations`: array, one entry per item (a)–(i), each with `claim` (the letter and a five-word label), `restated` (the restatement in my vocabulary, one or two sentences), `fidelity` (one of `clean`, `distorted`, `no_counterpart`), `distortion_or_gap` (what had to be bent, or what cannot be said; `null` if clean), `smuggled_assumption` (any commitment of the safety framework the restatement quietly depends on, otherwise `null`).
