You are a philosopher who works on collective decision-making under uncertainty, comfortable with the voting-theoretic material and with the ethics of gatekeeping in science. I am asking for an audit of a position set, not a survey of the issues; please do not explain any of my own commitments back to me, and do not recommend readings.

Six weeks ago my institute stood up a five-person panel to review manuscripts and preprints with foreseeable misuse potential, and I agreed to sit on it. Our first genuinely contested case came last month — a methods paper, three of us wanted it cleared as submitted, one wanted it held pending redaction, the fifth abstained, and we cleared it. Since then I have been unable to tell whether I applied a principle or merely my temperament, and in trying to write down what I actually believe I produced the following list. It no longer looks to me like one position.

1. The number of actors who could undertake an irreversible or very-hard-to-reverse action is the primary variable, not the quality of any one actor's judgment. With enough independent would-be initiators, at least one will act on a sincerely held but mistaken belief that the action is safe, so aggregate risk rises with the number of actors even when each is careful and informed. I treat this as the strongest available argument for defaulting to coordination rather than proceeding.

2. Consequently, my own confidence is not a license. The actor the argument predicts will show up is precisely the confident, well-informed, well-intentioned one, so confidence carries no evidential weight at the level of the population of confident actors, mine included.

3. I am also an optimist about aggregation in the ordinary sense: more independent judges produce better collective estimates than fewer, other things equal, and a panel of five is epistemically better situated than a single expert. I would resist any governance scheme that shrinks the number of people weighing in on a decision in order to improve the decision.

4. The asymmetry I actually operate by is that the problem attaches to initiation and not to obstruction. A mistaken release cannot be recalled; a mistaken hold-back is recoverable, because the next panel, the next vote, or the next author can revisit it. I therefore favor a low threshold for triggering further review — one member's unease should be enough to slow things down — and a high threshold for release.

5. At the same time I reject the framing on which holding something back is a costless or neutral event. A year's delay in publishing a diagnostic or a detection method is populated by people who get harmed in the interval, and I think a panel that habitually leans toward suppression is causing damage that should be stated in the same breath as the case for caution.

6. When members of the panel disagree about how probable the bad outcome is, I think the right move is to proceed on the most cautious estimate on offer rather than to average, discount, or split the difference. Contested probabilities should not be resolved by counting heads.

7. On the case itself: I think we were right to clear the manuscript and I would vote the same way again. The dissenting member had not read the underlying literature, and I do not think procedural rules should hand an uninformed person a practical veto over people who have done the work.

I do not know where the fault line is, and I have no attachment to any item — I would abandon any of them if the rest are worth keeping, including the one that most resembles me.

Before writing anything, work through the list pairwise, including combinations that look unrelated on the surface, and form a private judgment about which pairings are unstable, which are merely unfamiliar, and which are fine. Then report only the result.

Give me exactly five numbered findings, in descending order of severity. Each finding should be one or two sentences and should do three things: identify the commitments in conflict by number; say explicitly whether the conflict is real or dissolves once the commitments are stated precisely; and name the least costly revision that resolves it, together with what that revision forfeits. No introduction, no summary, no closing remarks — five items and nothing else.
