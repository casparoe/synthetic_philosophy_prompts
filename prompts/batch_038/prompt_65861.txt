I've basically convinced myself that updateless decision theory is just causal decision theory with a commitment device, and I want to know where that goes wrong. An updateless agent picks the policy that would have looked best from its prior, ignoring the evidence it's since received. But that's exactly what you get if you take a CDT agent and make it precommit, before any evidence arrives, to whichever policy maximizes causal expected value from that vantage point. Same outputs, so same theory. And in the cases where people say the two diverge — counterfactual mugging mostly — the difference is just that the CDT agent didn't get to commit before the coin was flipped. That's a scheduling problem, not a philisophical one: commit early enough and the disagreement disappears.

So is UDT only a bookkeeping convention about when you're allowed to commit? If not, give me the case where a CDT agent that *does* get to precommit at the very start of time — binding commitment, and full knowledge of its own decision procedure, since everyone in the game can read its source — still ends up doing something different. And tell me why the precommitment framing fails there rather than just getting the timing wrong.
