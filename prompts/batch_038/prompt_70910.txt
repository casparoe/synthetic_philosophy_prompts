Is there a name for this? I keep running into the same shape of thing in moral and political bargaining and I don't know whether it's a known phenomenon or something I've half-invented.

Two sides disagree about something they both treat as a value rather than a preference — not tradeable in principle. They make a deal anyway: you get your thing on issue A, I get mine on issue B. Both wanted it, both think they came out fine. But the deal only holds as long as nobody describes it *as a deal*. The moment someone says it out plainly — "we went along with their position because they went along with ours" — both sides turn on the person who said it, harder than they'd have turned on the trade if it had never happened, and the thing partly unravels backwards: each side now feels cheapened, and a concession that was accepted at the time gets remembered as a betrayal rather than a purchase.

Two cases I actually saw. (1) A coalition: a housing-density group backed another group's fare policy and got reciprocal support; fine for two years, then a member posted a plain-language summary of the swap as a kind of victory thread, and both groups spent months denouncing him and each other, and the fare policy got reopend. Nobody disputed any fact in the post. (2) A family: religious mother, atheist son, they agree the funeral is a church service but the son picks the music and says a few words. Everyone's relieved, it works. Then at the reception he jokes "well, I got my half," and his mother is devastated, and the rest of the family treats him like he just exposed the whole arrangement as a sham — including his own half, which she genuinely wanted him to have.

What I'm after isn't moral compromise in general, or gains from trade, or the familiar point that some values resist being priced — I know there's a literature on sacred values and on why certain trades are objectionable in principle. Mine is different: the trade is acceptable, it happens, it satisfies everyone, and the prohibition falls only on *characterizing* it, enforced by the traders themselves rather than by outsiders. Plus the retroactive bit.

So: is there an established concept for the un-nameability of a trade everyone's happy with? If yes, which one fits best, and how does it differ from euphemism and politeness, from taboo trade-offs, from plain hypocrisy? If no, coin me a term with a real definition and boundary cases — where it stops being this and starts being ordinary discretion or ordinary bad faith. I don't want just a label, I want the edges.
