I keep a notes app where I've been writing down what I actually believe about whether people are getting morally better, and last week I finally got it all onto one screen, and something about it feels off but I can't get a handle on where. Can you go through it and tell me whether this set can all be true at once?

Here's the note, roughly in the order I wrote it:

1. Moral progress is a real thing, not just fashions changing. Slavery, public hangings as a day out, a husband being legally allowed to beat his wife — those weren't fine then and wrong now. They were wrong then too. We got better at seeing it, and getting better at seeing it is the progress.

2. How it happens: the circle of who counts gets wider, and it gets wider through exposure, not through argument. Novels, television, travel, trade, people actually meeting the people they'd been taught to despise. Once you can picture someone else's life from the inside you can't keep them outside the circle. I don't think anybody has ever been reasoned into caring about anybody.

3. But empathy is a terrible compass. I'll be a total mess over one kid stuck in a well and completely unmoved by a war that kills ten thousand, and I've come to think that isn't a personal failing, that's just the wiring. Feelings respond to how vivid and how close something is, not to how bad it is.

4. Which is why I think the next big expansions are definately already lined up, and that my grandchildren will look back on us the way I look back on the 1840s. Farmed animals, and people who haven't been born yet. It'll be obvious to them that we should have seen it.

5. I don't think you can measure any of this with statistics. I read one of those big books with all the charts in it, showing violence falling for centuries, and I don't buy that the charts settle anything, because good behaviour for bad reasons isn't moral progress. A calm country where everyone behaves because they're watched and frightened is scoring better on paper and isn't morally ahead of anybody.

6. And none of it is locked in. Every generation has to decide this again from scratch and it can go backwards.

So which of these actually pull against each other, and which ones only look like they do? That's the part I can't work out on my own, and I dont want an answer that just lists every bit of friction in sight — some of these are obviously compatible, I'm sure. Rank the genuine conflicts worst to least bad, say for each one which specific numbered claim would have to change or be weakened to fix it, and then tell me: if I'm only allowed to revise one thing in the whole note, which single revision buys me the most?
