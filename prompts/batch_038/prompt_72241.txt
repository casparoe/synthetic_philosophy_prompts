I've been reading Overcoming Bias and LessWrong since roughly forever, and three years ago I somehow talked my town (pop. 11,000, rust belt, lots of spare time) into a small futarchy pilot — Hanson's vote-on-values-but-bet-on-beliefs idea, with the value being an annual wellbeing survey and the beliefs being markets. I sit on the six-person council that decides which markets we actually open. Our record so far:

1. We opened a market on whether a grocery co-op would raise average self-reported wellbeing, resolved off the survey that the state university administers, and we went ahead with the co-op when the market said yes.
2. We refused to open a market on whether to renew the police chief's contract, even though it would have resolved off the exact same survey. The stated reason was that this one wasn't ours to hand to traders.
3. We refused a market on whether a rezoning would raise land values in a specific neighborhood, because four of the people who would have been trading own most of the parcels in it.
4. We opened a market resolved on the count of new business licenses issued, which everyone on the council privately agrees is a terible proxy for wellbeing, because it was objective and nobody could move it — and in the same meeting refused a market on "do residents feel this is a good place to raise kids," which we all thought was the better measure, because our own town staff would have been collecting and tabulating it. We opened a near-identical version a year later once the university agreed to run it.
5. We opened a market on whether extending clinic hours would cut ER visits where only eleven people ever traded — the clinic administrator, two ER docs, the county Medicaid data person, a few nurses — and it moved a lot and we acted on it. Thinness didn't bother us at all.
6. For every one of these, the branch we didn't take got paid out from a three-person expert panel's estimate rather than from data, which two of us consider a scandal, but it's what we've done every time and nobody has challenged a panel yet.

Now the new proposal. A developer wants to put 400 units on the old foundry site. The market would be on whether the development raises town wellbeing as measured by the university survey — good metric, outside resolver — but the developer pays for an oversample of our town, supplies the baseline data, and supplies the traffic and air-quality monitors that the resolution formula also uses, and posts a $500k bond that's forfeited if an audit finds the data doctored. The three most informed potential traders are his own analysts. Separately, about 300 residents signed a petition saying land use at this scale should be voted on by everyone, full stop, markets or no markets.

So: what rule is this council actually running, which other rules fit all six verdicts about as well, and what should we decide to be consistent with ourselves — including which of the six is the closest analog and what principle we'd be committing to if we went the other way. Also name one hypothetical future proposal that would split your top two candidate rules cleanly.

Answer as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro, no conclusion, no headers: (1) the rule you think we're running, (2) the rival rules that fit the record just as well and which verdict each strains on, (3) the verdict plus the closest analog, (4) the principle we'd adopt by deciding the other way plus the discriminating proposal, (5) how confident you are in all this and the single point you're least sure of.
