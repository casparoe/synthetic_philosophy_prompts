I sit on my university's election commission, where a mathematician serves as our technical advisor. Over three years the commission has asked him whether proposed rules are manipulable, and he has ruled as follows: plurality among three candidates — manipulable; two-round runoff among five — manipulable; Borda count among five — manipulable; approval voting among four — manipulable; majority judgment — manipulable; a rule that always elects the same fixed candidate whatever the ballots say — not manipulable; dictatorship, where the chair's top choice always wins — not manipulable; random ballot, where one voter is drawn at random and that voter's top choice is elected — not manipulable; and simple majority between two candidates — not manipulable. On every occasion he was told that the ballot set was unrestricted: voters could submit any ranking, or any set of approvals.

The commission now has a proposal of a kind it has not seen. The twenty-five members would each submit a single figure for how much of a discretionary fund to spend, the figure constrained to a fixed range, and the median submission would be adopted. The sponsor says members' preferences over the figure are single-peaked — everyone has an ideal amount and prefers amounts nearer it — though one commissioner notes that a member has said she wants either the maximum or nothing.

Predict his ruling and justify the prediction. I can see at least two readings of his record that fit all nine verdicts and come apart here: one on which the median procedure is just another deterministic, onto, non-dictatorial rule over three or more outcomes, and one on which the sponsor's restriction withdraws the case from the reach of the result he has been applying all along. Say what each reading predicts, which you would bet on and why, what the commission commits itself to either way, and what further proposal we could put to him that would settle which reading he is in fact running.
