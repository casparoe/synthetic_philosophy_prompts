I'm a clinical epidemiologist who designs and validates diagnostic tests. My working vocabulary is sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, likelihood ratios, prevalence and case-mix, ROC analysis, spectrum and verification bias, and latent class models for the situation where no reference standard exists for the target condition. I've read Nozick's tracking account of knowledge in Philosophical Explanations once, at an undergraduate level, and I can't shake the impression that it is a diagnostic-test theory wearing a counterfactual costume: the variation condition looks like specificity, adherence looks like sensitivity, knowing looks like adequate positive predictive value, the denial of closure looks like the fact that performance validated against one endpoint cannot be transferred to a logically related endpoint without revalidation, and the reply to the skeptic looks like the confession that for some hypotheses no achievable reference standard exists.

Restate the tracking account, condition by condition and including the closure argument, in that vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report: which claims map cleanly, which only map if I let you fudge something, and which have no expression at all in a framework built on populations, sampling frames, and conditional frequencies. I want your verdict on whether the residue exposes a limitation of my vocabulary — counterfactuals over nearby situations are not conditional probabilities over a cohort, and I suspect that is where the pressure will show — or whether it shows that the tracking conditions were never as determinate as they read. Please don't backfill my framework's assumptions and present the result as Nozick.
