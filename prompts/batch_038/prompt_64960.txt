For a paper I have been trying to defend a modest claim — that a single person's contribution to a large-scale collective harm (one long-haul flight, one factory-farmed meal, one uncast ballot) is not itself wrongful, since the world goes exactly as it would have gone without it. The defense I built now appears to destroy the claim it was built for, and I cannot locate the error.

(1) An act wrongs someone only if it makes things worse for that person.

(2) Whether a person's situation has gone differently is detectable in principle from his side: if he cannot tell, in any way, whether my act occurred, then nothing about his situation differs between the world with my act and the world without it.

(3) Suppose a thousand separately owned smelters each discharge one unit of cadmium into the same river. One unit is not detectable by any downstream resident and produces no measurable illness; a thousand units together poison a town.

(4) Each owner satisfies (2): no resident can tell whether that particular unit was discharged. So, by (2), nothing about any resident's situation differs with or without that unit, and by (1) no owner wrongs anyone.

(5) Nor can wrongness appear as the units accumulate. If the thousandth discharge is permissible, so is the nine-hundred-and-ninety-ninth, since the difference between those two worlds is undetectable by the residents and therefore, by (2), no difference for them; iterating backward, the first discharge is permissible too.

(6) That is the conclusion I wanted. But the same reasoning, run forward, yields something I cannot accept: the residents are poisoned, their poisoning is a wrong done to them, and the thousand smelters are all there is — yet, by (4) and (5), not one owner does anything wrong.

(7) So either a grave wrong is done that nobody does, or at least one discharge is wrongful; and if one is, the regress in (5) fails, and I have no principled place to stop it short of my own flight.

I would rather keep (1): I do not want a theory on which acts are wrongful though nobody is worse off for them. Which step should go, and what does giving it up cost the original claim about individual contributions?
