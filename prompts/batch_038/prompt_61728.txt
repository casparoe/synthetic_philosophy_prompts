spent about three hours last night arguing with my friend Nadia about whether anybody has actually solved the meta-problem of consciousness — Chalmers's version, the problem of explaining why we think there's a hard problem in the first place — and we got nowhere, mostly because we kept meaning different things by solved. before I wade into any more literature I want the spec. write out the desiderata: what a theory has to deliver to count as genuinely solving the meta-problem rather than changing the subject or re-describing the intuition in fancier vocabulary. for each requirement give me the case or consideration behind it, I don't want a bare checklist.

some I already have; tell me which are real and which are just my side of the argument wearing a lab coat:

- topic-neutrality. the account can't invoke phenomenal notions to explain why we think phenomenal notions resist physical explanation, otherwise it hasn't explained anything.
- no vanishing target. whatever ends up explained has to still be recognizably the intuition we actually have, not an easier neighbor (a vague sense of wonder, a semantic muddle about what it is like to be something, a disposition to be impressed by bad arguments).
- what Nadia kept calling the bootstrapping problem, and I think she's onto something even though neither of us could state it cleanly: the evidence a meta-problem theory appeals to is mostly introspective reports and first-person seemings, but the reliability of introspection is itself part of whats under explanation. so the theory has to say which introspective deliverances it licenses itself to trust, and why that move isn't circular. please write this one up as a proper requirement with a worked example, because it's the one that broke the argument last night.
- something about zombies. if a philosophical zombie would judge and say exactly what we do, then an account of our judging can't turn on any feature zombies lack, or it's implicitly using consciousness to explain the intuition of consciousness. and then the mirror-image requirement if you think zombies are impossible — which I suspect people never write down.
- variance. it should explain why the intuition is overwhelming in some people and close to absent in others, why it seems to move under training, drugs, meditation, or a good night's sleep, and why it arrives bundled with the other problem intuitions (the gap, zombie conceivability, the knowledge argument) rather than freestanding.
- and one I can't articulate: whatever the account says makes phenomenal concepts, or introspective mechanisms, or global broadcasting special has to be special in a way that doesn't just relocate the mystery to the special thing.

then tell me which of these are non-negotiable and which are camp preferences, whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect topic-neutrality and no-vanishing-target pull against each other, but I want the argument rather than my hunch, and if I'm wrong say so — and which single requirement does the most work knocking out candidates. finish with a very short scorecard of the usual suspects: illusionism, higher-order or self-awareness accounts, attention-schema and predictive-processing flavored accounts, the phenomenal concept strategy, panpsychism treated as a meta-solution, mysterianism. two lines each, a table is fine, don't pad it, the criteria are what I care about.

bottom line in the first sentence: say up front whether the desiderata can be jointly met at all, then give the reasoning and the list. no preamble and don't restate the question back at me.
