I am assembling a set of designed cases for a graduate seminar on the philosophy of mind, and this one needs to be built to specification rather than pulled from the usual stock. Here is what I want.

**The target.** I want a single scenario that forces two of Dennett's own commitments apart. The first is his long-standing denial that there could be a difference in principle between a mind and a perfect counterfeit of a mind: whatever is supposed to distinguish the real thing from the imitation would have to show up somewhere in what the system does or says, and if it never shows up anywhere, the distinction is not worth drawing. The second is the architectural picture behind the Multiple Drafts Model — that there is no finish line, no single place and time at which everything comes together for a subject, and that the Cartesian theater is a philosopher's fiction rather than a feature of any actual mind. Build me a system that meets every demand the first commitment could impose while violating the second as explicitly as an engineering specification can violate it.

**The system.** Give it a genuine broadcast step. At each cycle exactly one candidate content is elevated to global status; nothing outside that content is retained in reportable form; and the module that produces the system's speech about its own mental life reads only from the post-broadcast record. There is a finish line, and it is written into the design document. Then show how, with no further tricks, that architecture reproduces the very phenomena Dennett deployed the Multiple Drafts Model to explain — above all the misreporting of temporal order drawn from the color-phi case, where two differently colored lights flashed in quick succession at two locations are reported as a single moving spot that changes color in mid-course. The machine must exhibit the Stalinesque pattern by construction: the discrepancy is settled before it ever becomes available to be reported, so the machine is confidently and systematically wrong about the order of its own states. It must also be able to describe itself, sincerely and without detecting any inconsistency, as having no such finish line.

**The comparison.** Put an ordinary human subject in the same laboratory, on the same tasks, and make the two of them indistinguishable in every respect an outside observer could check: conversation, latency, confabulation, the specific shape of their errors about their own experience, their reactions to being surprised by their own behavior. State, feature by feature, which element of the design guarantees the match, so that a reader can verify the indistinguishability rather than take it on faith.

**Constraints.**
- The system is labeled as a simulation from the moment it is powered on and volunteers the fact unprompted; no one can mistake it for a human being. I want the deception-and-trust objection held entirely out of the case, because that is a different objection from the one I am probing.
- No souls, no immaterial ingredients, no unexplained physics, no instrument that detects consciousness. The entire difference between machine and human must be architectural, and the architecture must be describable in ordinary computational terms to an engineer.
- Do not recruit any theory of consciousness Dennett rejects: no integrated information, no Nagelian residue, no zombie stipulation, no appeal to a global workspace theorist. You will notice that the machine's broadcast step resembles such a thing; that is welcome, but the verdicts must be generated from Dennett's own claims and nothing else.
- Nowhere in the scenario may you assert that the machine is or is not conscious. That is the output of the exercise, not an input to it.
- Three paragraphs at most for the scenario itself, written so that an advanced undergraduate who has read the standard selections can follow it with no further background.

**Then the demonstration.**
1. What the counterfeit thesis licenses here: the verdict, and the reasoning, traced to the specific claims in Dennett that produce it.
2. What the Multiple Drafts commitment licenses: the verdict, traced the same way. Address in particular how his argument that the Orwellian and the Stalinesque ways of drawing the finish line are observationally indistinguishable is affected by a case in which the finish line is not merely indistinguishable but demonstrably present.
3. Which single feature of the design is doing the work. I want one, named.
4. What happens to the case if that feature is removed — and say plainly whether it then collapses into an ordinary philosophical zombie, which would be a failure of the design rather than a success.
5. The best reply available to a Dennettian. Not a strawman: the strongest move he can make, and what it costs him — the architectural claims, the counterfeit thesis, or some third commitment I have not named.

**Purpose, since it fixes what counts as cheating.** I am testing whether the denial of any difference in principle between a mind and its perfect counterfeit is a substantive thesis with possible counterexamples, or a redescription that nothing could contradict. A scenario that smuggles its verdict into the description fails, and so does one that satisfies the letter of the specification by stipulating an inner absence no observer could ever detect and no claim of Dennett's requires.

**Finally, commit.** Close by saying which verdict you judge Dennett's framework actually licenses in this case, and whether his architectural talk is load-bearing theory or dispensable scaffolding he would trade away to save the counterfeit thesis. Choose one. I do not want both readings laid out as equally defensible with the decision left to me; I want your position and the consideration that settles it.

Paraphrase Dennett throughout. Do not quote him, and do not supply titles, page numbers, or citations of any kind.
