I am collecting jokes for a reading group in the philosophy of language, and I want one that a specialist will recognize as getting the machinery exactly right.

Write a short joke — under 120 words, with a clean setup and a punchline — whose humour depends on Austin's classification of the ways a performative can go infelicitous, and in particular on the difference between the two families in that classification. On one side are the misfires: there is no accepted conventional procedure for the act invoked, or the circumstances and persons are not the appropriate ones, or the procedure is executed incorrectly or incompletely, so that no act is performed at all and the utterance is void. On the other are the abuses: the procedure goes through and the act is performed, but hollowly, because the speaker lacked the requisite thoughts, feelings, or intentions, or fails afterwards to conduct himself accordingly.

Set it in a present-day context rather than in one of the stock examples — no christenings, weddings, bets, or last wills. My suggestion is an automated customer-service system or AI assistant that assures a user, in the first person, that his account data has been deleted, though you may substitute any setting provided the classification of the resulting utterance is what the joke turns on. Commit to one family rather than straddling them: the case must come out as either void or hollow, and the punchline should reward a reader who can see which, and why. You are welcome to use the fact that the user is genuinely reassured — an effect on his state of mind that does not obviously depend on the reassurance having been successfully given — but only if that earns its place in the joke rather than being bolted onto the end.

The joke itself should carry no explanation, name no philosophers, and never wink at the audience. It should not open with anyone walking into a bar, and it should contain no pun on performative, speech act, utterance, or illocution. The standard I am aiming for is that a reader who has never encountered Austin finds the situation amusing and a reader who has laughs harder.

After the joke, add two or three sentences — these fall outside the word limit — saying which family of infelicity you have claimed the utterance falls into, and identifying the feature of the case that settles it.
