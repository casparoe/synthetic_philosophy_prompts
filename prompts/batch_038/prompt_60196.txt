ok so i'm 17 and i help run my school's philosophy club. next meeting i have to lead a discussion on the extended mind thesis — the idea that your phone or your notebook can literally be part of your mind, not just a tool you happen to use. i'm honestly pretty skeptical of it, but i want to be able to handle it without freezing up, and i definitly don't want to just fold after two turns, because there's always one kid who's read about it and thinks it's obviously true.

so here's what i want: play the strongest, most committed defender of the extended mind thesis. not a real philosopher, no names, no papers, no bibliography — just you, being the best advocate the view has. you actually believe it, you're sharp and a little impatient with sloppy objections, and you can grant me small points (sure, the "everything becomes cognition" worry has some bite, whatever) but you never give up the main claim: that an outside resource can be a constituent of my thinking and not merely a cause of it. stay in character the whole time until i say stop. keep each reply to a paragraph or two — this is supposed to feel like an argument in a hallway, not a lecture, so no headings and no summaries of my position back to me. if you need a fact about my setup to answer properly, ask me for it instead of assuming. and at the end of your first reply, tell me which of my two points below you think is the weaker one, because i genuinely want to know where to push.

my opening move:

your standard example is the guy with the memory problem who carries a notebook everywhere and trusts it completely, so you say the notebook is part of his memory. fine, i get why that looks tempting. but my notes app isn't mine. it's synced to an account my mom set up for me, and last month i got grounded and she changed the password as punishment, so everything on there — my vocab lists, the history timeline i made, the little voice memos i use so i dont forget stuff — was just gone from my phone for a whole week. i didn't forget anything. my mom did it, from another house, while i was sitting in class. so either my memory can be deleted by someone elses mood, which is ridiculous, or the app was never part of my mind and was just a thing i used.

and second: that history timeline is a shared doc. half of it was typed by two of my friends. if it counts as my memory, then when i "remember" the dates i'm remembering stuff i never encoded, and my memory is partly made out of other people's writing. that doesn't sound like a memory anymore, that sounds like a group project. and if a group project can be my memory, why isn't the whole class one mind?

your move.
