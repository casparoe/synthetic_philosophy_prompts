So, slow-motion text argument, three weeks running, with a friend who found the Kelly criterion and then — worse — found the stronger version of it: the one that says expected-value reasoning isn't merely risky but flat-out invalid for anything that compounds, because an expected value is an average across an imaginary ensemble of copies of you while you only get to live your own one life forward through time, so the only honest number is your time-average growth rate. On his account, someone who turns down a positive-EV bet that could wipe them out is making an arithmetic error, someone who takes that bet is making the same error in the other direction, and utility functions are a cope people invented to avoid doing the log. We play poker every other Tuesday and he has started announcing his bet sizes as fractions of bankroll at the table, which is its own problem. I think he's right about a big chunk of this and overclaims the rest, and I've accepted that another forty-message reply won't land, so I want to send him something funnier instead.

Write me a parody of an app-store product listing and its user reviews for a mobile app that implements the Kelly criterion — something named by a founder who has read too much, like "GROWTH OPTIMAL: Kelly Bet Sizer," with a subtitle. Around 800 words. I want the whole storefront furniture: star rating with an implausibly specific number of ratings behind it, price plus the in-app-purchases line, a description blurb, a permissions list, a "What's New" changelog for the last two versions, then the reviews — each with a star count, username, date, verified-purchase badge where appropriate, and one of those "N of M people found this helpful" tallies. Pin a most-favorable and a most-critical review at the top the way Amazon does, and include at least two developer responses that are unfailingly courteous and completely miss the point. The changelog should quietly reveal that an assumption changed between versions.

The funny has to come from the content being right, not from anyone being stupid — the reviews should sound like actual people who are petty, personal, and slightly unhinged, not like a debate transcript with usernames. Specifically I want:

- at least one review that works a real bet through: states the odds and the probability, computes the fraction, and says what happens if you bet half of it or twice it. The arithmetic must actually be correct, because he will check it and I refuse to be the guy who forwarded a wrong calculation.
- somebody who used it for a decision that doesn't repeat — a surgery, a house, a job offer, a proposal — and is furious at the answer it gave them, in either direction. The one-shot versus repeated-play problem should be legible inside their grievance and never stated in the listing's own voice.
- one total true believer who thinks growth rate is the only real quantity and that everyone else is bad at mathematics, written sincerely and left uncorrected by anyone.
- somebody who was right about the direction of a bet and slightly wrong about its size and is now at zero, plus how the app handled that support ticket.
- the genuine objections, but voiced as grievances from people with grudges rather than as points in an essay: that maximizing long-run growth is not the same thing as maximizing expected utility and that log utility is a choice somebody made; that the time-average-is-the-only-truth move quietly needs independent, repeated bets with full reinvestment; that the formula demands to be told the true probabilities and never admits it; that everything comes apart when the bets are correlated, or when there are fees, spreads, taxes, or a cap on how much you can actually get down; that under pure growth maximization you never spend anything and never leave anything to anybody; that zero is an absorbing state you can't come back from. No paper titles, no economist or philosopher names anywhere — invent all the reviewers.

And keep it fair, which is the part I care about most: the app is genuinely right about what it's right about. It is the growth-rate optimum under its own assumptions, it will never let you bet yourself into ruin, and the listing should be proud of that in a way that reads as true rather than as a setup. Deadpan all the way through, no winking at the reader, and if a joke needs explaining to work, cut it.
