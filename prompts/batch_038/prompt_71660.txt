Write a short story of 1,200–1,800 words set in a public records office whose clerks are known as warrant auditors: whenever a claim is filed in support of some official act, they trace the reasons offered for it backward until the file can be closed. Closure is permitted in exactly three ways, and all three should appear as routine procedure rather than as exposition.

- **Terminal Record** — the chain ends at a document admitted without further support, on the ground that its content is simply handed over by the way the document was made (a gauge impression, a certificate signed at the bedside, a seismograph drum).
- **Sealed Circuit** — a fixed set of documents, none of them terminal, each lending support to the next, the last lending support to the first.
- **Open Ledger** — the chain is never finished. A file is accepted provisionally for as long as it keeps lengthening and each new entry supplies something the entries before it did not; an entry that merely restates an earlier one does not count as lengthening the chain.

The plot should turn on one claim under audit — whether a district's flood defences were certified adequate on the night an evacuation order was countermanded — and each rule should fail in its characteristic way when pressed: a Terminal Record whose maker turns out never to have been present at what it records; a Sealed Circuit that is internally flawless and describes nothing anyone outside it can check; an Open Ledger that, past a certain depth, stops adding anything and begins re-describing itself. Three auditors, one devoted to each rule, argue over which failure is worst, and each must land at least one real point.

Narrate by handing the reader from document to document the way the auditors hand off, so that where the narration finally stops — at a terminal record, back at its own opening line, or mid-entry — is itself the story's argument. Do not use the words foundationalism, coherentism, or infinitism, and do not name any philosopher; a reader who knows the debate should be able to say which position each auditor holds and which objection each scene stages. End without deciding which rule the office adopts, but let the last paragraph disclose something about the claim under audit that none of the three rules was ever going to settle.
