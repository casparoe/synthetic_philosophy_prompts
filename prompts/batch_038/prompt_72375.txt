So I had an idea and I want you to write it up as a fake but completely deadpan municipal staff memo — the boring kind my city actually sends, with a To/From/Date/Re header and numbered sections, dry as toast. The proposal: nothing gets commemorated permanently anymore. Every statue, plaque and street name goes on a 20-year "commemorative lease," renewable by public vote, with an annual condition report on the honoree's standing, tiered renual fees based on how prominent the site is, and a small QR plaque at the base showing the honoree's current renewal odds in real time.

Make sure you include a section on what happens when a lease lapses (nothing gets melted down, the honoree is just placed in storage pending future demand — treat it like an interlibrary loan), a section on who's allowed to petition for a new honoree and what the application has to show, and a budget line with at least one number in it.

The jokes have to come from the actual arguments about commemoration — honoring versus remembering, whether a monument even asserts anything, the compromise of adding a contextualizing plaque instead of removing the thing, whether the living get to judge the dead, and who counts as the community a statue supposedly speaks for — but the memo is never allowed to argue any of it. It just implements all of it badly and bureaucratically, like it's an obvious administrative fix everyone should have thought of. No winking, no snarky narrator, no section where someone admits this is a nightmare.

Hard cap of 450 words, and please actually stay under it. It should make someone who's read philosophy about monuments laugh, but also make sense to my dad.
