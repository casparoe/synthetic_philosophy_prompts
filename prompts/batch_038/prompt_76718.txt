Two colleagues have agreed to a written adversarial collaboration instead of another exchange of letters. One is a philosopher of the social sciences who holds that Popper's case in *The Poverty of Historicism* is essentially correct — that there can be no theoretical history in the sense of a nomological science of historical development, only conditional prophecies mistaken for unconditional predictions. The other is a practitioner of mathematical history who holds that demographic-structural models of state instability track genuine lawlike regularities in the long-run behavior of both agrarian and modern states, and that Popper's argument, even if sound, does not touch what they actually do. Draft the joint statement they would produce.

**1. A numbered section of agreed claims.** These must be substantive rather than diplomatic. Both should be able to sign claims about: what Popper's argument in fact is, including the distinction between conditional scientific prediction and unconditional historical prophecy, and the role of the premise that the growth of human knowledge is itself a cause of historical change and cannot be foretold by rational means; what the mathematical-historical program in fact claims — parameterized, domain-limited models yielding forecasts with stated uncertainty, not stage theories of inevitable development; and the points on which the two programs simply do not conflict. Where a widely repeated caricature of either side is false, say so, and say which side the caricature serves.

**2. A disagreements table**, with the columns: the issue; the Popperian's formulation, in wording the Popperian accepts as fair; the mathematical historian's formulation, in wording that party accepts as fair; whether the disagreement is genuine or largely verbal, with reasons; the crux, meaning the specific sub-question whose settlement would move at least one party; the test, case, or argument both accept as bearing on that crux; and each party's prediction, recorded now rather than after the fact.

**3. At least four genuine disagreements.** I expect these to include: whether the premise that future knowledge cannot be predicted rules out only detailed content-forecasts about particular discoveries, or also aggregate statistical regularities in rates of innovation and in the incidence of state breakdown; whether the field's central explanatory variables — elite overproduction, popular immiseration, fiscal strain — are independently operationalized, or are largely re-descriptions of the outcome they are invoked to explain; whether any social system can be "closed" enough in Popper's sense to support laws rather than mere trends; and whether publicizing a long-range political forecast is itself an instance of precisely that knowledge growth Popper declares unforeseeable, such that the existence of the field is evidence against his second premise rather than an illustration of it.

**4. One concrete case, run through the whole machinery.** Take the widely publicized forecast issued in 2010 by a leading proponent of the field that the United States would enter a period of elevated political instability around 2020. State what each party now says about the episode; what each would have had to see in order to count it as a success, or a failure, for its own position; whether those two assessments can be made compatible; and which single methodological feature of the episode both agree is decisive.

**5. An explicit verbal-dispute section.** If you conclude that part of the controversy turns on words — most plausibly on whether a domain-limited statistical regularity deserves the name "law of history" — identify exactly which part, explain why it is verbal, and give the reformulation both parties can sign.

Please include no reference list, bibliography, or citations; describe any published work you invoke in prose, by content rather than by citation. Do not adjudicate between the two positions and do not let either party's voice dominate: the deliverable is a document both could put their names to, with the residual disagreement stated exactly and nothing left in diplomatic vagueness.
