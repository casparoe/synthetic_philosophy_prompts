My brother does maintenance at a state prison and he swears that "he deserves to suffer" is just revenge wearing a nicer hat. I've been turning over a case that I think gives both sides a problem, and I want to watch two people who actually know this debate argue it out.

The case: a woman, 68, walks into a police station and confesses to a murder from 40 years ago — she killed the man who'd been beating her younger sister, and nobody ever suspected her. Since then she's run a women's shelter, raised three kids, never been in trouble for anything. The evidence checks out. She's got maybe 18 months to live from an illness. She says she wants to be punished.

Write a dialogue between a retributivist and a consequentialist about punishment, both sharp, neither one a strawman, arguing over what should happen to her. Make them actually answer each other instead of taking turns giving speeches. The consequentialist has to say what good the sentence does when there's nothing left to deter (who's going to be scared off a crime they commited four decades ago?), nothing left to rehabilitate, and she's asking for it anyway. The retributivist has to say why her pain makes things better rather than just adding one more misery to the pile. And I want them to fight about whether 40 years of guilt already counts as the punishment — does retributivism need the state to be the one handing it out, or does it just need the suffering to happen in the right amount? If somebody lands a real hit, let the other feel it and answer. No cheap wins.
