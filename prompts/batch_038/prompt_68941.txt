Write me the internal staff FAQ that caseworkers get on the morning a new automated conditionality system goes live at a made-up benefits agency. Call the system whatever you like, but don't use a real country, a real department, a real minister or a real thinker anywhere in it — everything is invented. Around 800 words, numbered Q&A, with a couple of sidebars.

The agency's whole philosophy, which the FAQ has to reason from without ever arguing for it, is that a payment made without a matching contribution harms the recipient as much as it harms the taxpayer — so sanctions aren't punishment, they're therapeutic, they restore dignity, they re-establish reciprocity. The system keeps a contribution ledger per claimant: it monetises everything it counts as contributing (job-search hours logged, course attendance, volunteering, generally being activation-ready) and prices unpaid care, illness and disability at zero. Explain this with fake-technical precision, and define two or three of its terms — something like a reciprocity deficit or a latent contribution — as if they were entries in an operations manual.

What I want is for the jokes to come entirely from the actual logic of conditionality and the deserving/undeserving split, not from generic bureaucracy gags: no forms in triplicate, no Kafka, no the-computer-said-no. The humour should be that whoever wrote it clearly understands the thing — deservingness sliding from need to compliance, the burden of proof reversing onto the claimant, the full-time obligation to search for work that doesn't exist in the local labour market, the fact that a person can be sanctioned while actually employed, the quiet migration of the categories from unemployment onto sickness and lone parenthood, and a legacy note in which the FAQ insists the striver/skiver distinction is purely operational, has no moral content and has no history whatsoever.

Also get the arithmetic honest even while the rhetoric isn't. When the FAQ cites numbers (invented but plausible, no real statistics), let a careful reader see that the fraud total is a rounding error next to the agency's own error rate and next to what the same government's revenue arm fails to collect, while the commentary carries on treating claimant fraud as the great moral emergency of the age. Don't point that out. Never wink.

Include at least three worked case-note examples showing what the system decided and what the caseworker should say at the door, and finish on an escalation item that applies exactly the same test, with no adjustment and no special pleading, to somebody whose income is entirely dividends or an inheritance. The document should treat the result as completely unremarkable; the reader should not.

Deadpan civil-service-plus-management-consultant voice throughout. If it reads as though anyone in the room knows they're being satirsed, it's failed.
