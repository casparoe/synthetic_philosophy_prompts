I'm a staffer on a state legislative committee and there's a bill in front of us that would make individual state income tax records publicly searchable by name — basically the Finland thing, where the tax authority dumps everyone's income above some modest threshold onto a public list in early November, the papers run the top earners, people joke that it's national jealousy day, and you can technically ask to have your own data suppressed but almost nobody doesnt. Meanwhile over here, asking a coworker what they make is close to a faux pas, employers used to fire people for it even though federal labor law protects wage talk for most private-sector workers, and a bunch of states have banned employers from asking about your salary history. Roughly the same two cultures, both nominally meritocratic, totally seperate defaults about whether pay is public or private.

I have to write the minority report and I want the philosophy right, not the polling. So: what does each practice actually presuppose about whether money tracks desert? My hunch is that the Finnish one isn't about cutting down tall poppies at all — that making income public assumes the income is basically legitimate and just kind of boring information — while the American taboo assumes the opposite, that if people actually saw the numbers the story about earning what you're worth would come apart, so the numbers have to stay unspoken. Is that right or am I reading it backwards?

Reconstruct the implicit theory on each side. Tell me which parts each practice strictly needs versus which it just happens to sit comfortably with, and flag where it's underdetermined — what rival view of desert, or of envy, or of what privacy is even for, would license the exact same Finnish list. Then: if we adopted the Finnish practice here, what would have to change in the rest of our institutions for it to mean the same thing it means there, and what would it mean if we changed nothing else? And where does the salary-history ban fit — it makes pay more transparent in one direction and less in another, and I cant tell which commitment it's serving.
