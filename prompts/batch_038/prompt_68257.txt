Retired materials scientist here — thirty-five years doing heat treatment and annealing of alloys, and only in the last couple of years reading philosophy properly. So I come at this with calluses, which may be part of the problem.

At my book group a retired theologian (amateur but sharp) argued that the universe can't have existed forever, and his whole case was a cup of coffee. A hot cup left in a room goes lukewarm and never spontaneously gets hot again; the coffee and the room end up at the same temperature; anything left alone long enough ends up uniform and inert. If the universe were infinitely old, he said, it would already have finished cooling — a uniform lukewarm soup with nothing happening in it. We are not a lukewarm soup, therefore it hasn't been around forever, therefore it had a beginning. Everyone in the room nodded and I couldn't say why I didn't.

I want an audit of that coffee, not of the theology. Give me the mapping first: what in the cup-and-room picture is supposed to correspond to what in the universe taken as a whole. Then where the mapping breaks — and please sort the breaks into the ones that actually matter for his inference and the ones that are just loose talk I should let slide. My suspicion is that it quietly imports things the universe doesn't have: an outside, a container, a bath whose temperature stays fixed no matter what, a clock that isn't itself part of the system, and some finite amount of room left to run down. Tell me whether I'm on the right track or missing the big one entirely.

Two hunches I'd like you to test rather than flatter:

1. Someone told me self-gravitating systems have negative heat capacity — they get hotter as they loose energy — which seems to break the coffee structurally, not just quantitatively, given that gravity is most of what the universe is made of. Is that right, and does it rescue or sink the inference?

2. In my old lab plenty of things never reached equilibrium on any human timescale: glasses, cold-worked metal, anything kinetically stuck. Could the universe be more like a glass than a cup of coffee? If so, is that a genuinely better analogy or just a different hand-wave with the same job?

And a favour at the end. Say where you're least confident: what would change your verdict about whether his argument survives — which piece of the physics, or which step of the reasoning, would have to come out differently — and where you think my own framing is steering you somewhere it shouldn't go.
