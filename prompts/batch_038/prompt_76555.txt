I'm running a small reading group for grad students on the metaphysics of interpretability, and for the first session I want an actual argued position rather than a survey of the dialectic — partly so they see what it looks like to commit to something. Here's the thesis I want you to make the case for, and fair warning, I'm not sure I buy it myself: "do neural networks have representations?" is the wrong question, and the right one is whether they have dispositions. Feature realism should be modelled on dispositional realism in metaphysics — a feature is a causal power of the system whose manifestation is context-sensitive. On that picture superposition stops looking like an embarrassment, since an unmanifested power is a perfectly ordinary thing, and the anti-realist's complaint that there's no privileged decomposition of activation space turns out to be true but harmless, because dispositions are interest-relative to begin with.

Argue it properly rather than gesturing at it. I want you to actually use the machinery interpretability hands you (activation patching, sparse autoencoders, steering vectors) and the machinery metaphysics hands you, and I want two pressure points handled head on. First: the worry that on this account any latent direction you can write down counts as a feature, so the view can't be falsified by anything — tell me why that's not fatal. Second: if a feature that only shows up when you surgically intervene on the weights is finked or masked in the sense Martin and Lewis were worried about, what does that do to the causal-experimental methodology the whole field rests on? Finish by saying what evidence would actually make you abandon the view.
