My department has two people who have been arguing about presidential vs. parliamentary systems for years and have completely stopped hearing each other. One is a Linz-style institutionalist: a fixed term both branches can't escape, dual legitimacy when the president and the assembly both claim to speak for the people, winner-take-all election of a single person, and the plebiscitary personalism that comes with it, make presidential democracies fragile — and in a fragmented multiparty society that combination reliably produces deadlock between a minority president and a hostile legislature. The other says that's a badly specified claim: the survival gap mostly tracks which countries ended up with presidential constitutions (poor, recently post-colonial, coup-prone, militaries waiting in the wings) rather than anything the institution itself does, and "presidentialism" lumps together systems that differ enormously — an assembly that can be dissolved by the president is not the same animal as a president who can dismiss the cabinet at will.

Write the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between them should have produced. I want: a numbered list of claims both can actually sign — about which mechanisms are in play, about what the cross-national survival data do and don't license, about what a constitution can and can't control; then a table of the disagreements that survive, each row stated in wording its own side would accept, so the Linzian's position is phrased the way a Linzian would want to be quoted and likewise for the sceptic; then the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling the issue would actually move at least one of them.

For each crux, give the comparison or test both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance — a prediction with a direction, not "more research needed." I'm especially interested in cases that seperate what Linz bundles: semi-presidential regimes where dual legitimacy coexists with a dissolvable assembly; Germany-style constructive no-confidence votes, where the assembly can remove a government without triggering an election; and minority presidents vs. presidents who command a legislative majority. If you think part of their fight is really about words — the taxonomy of presidential, semi-presidential, premier-presidential, or what counts as a "peril" — say which part, why, and which part is substantive.

Be committed rather than even-handed. Where you think one camp's favoured formulation is just wrong, say so in a closing note instead of letting both wordings float free; tell me which one or two cruxes you actually believe would move the debate if settled and which ones are decorative; and flag anywhere your agreed-claims section is papering over a difference that's still live.
