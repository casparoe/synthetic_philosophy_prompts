So my high school is named after the guy who founded it in 1911, and this year some of us dug up that he also owned the mill that employed half the town and kept its Black workers in seperate company housing — and that he partly built the school so the white mill families would stay put. Half the student body wants the name gone, the alumni say that's erasing history, and both sides just assert things about what a name on a building means. I have to write a paper taking a position but I don't want to until I know what would even count as a good answer. So: what does a theory of when it's right to honor someone publicly actually have to deliver? I mean the questions it has to settle, the cases it has to come out right on (a name nobody alive ever chose, someone whose good act was motivated by the bad belief, a street named after a battle instead of a person), the distinctions it can't collapse, and the things it can't imply — like that nobody who lived before 1960 is honorable.

Exactly five numbered requirements, a sentence or two each: the requirement, the case that motivates it, and a tag saying whether it's non-negotiable or just one camp's preference in disguise. If two of them can't both be satisfied, put that note on the item it belongs to. Tag whichever single one knocks out the most answers people actually give in fights like ours. No intro, no conclusion, and don't referee my town.
