Last night I sat through two hours of argument between two friends — H., an economic historian who works almost entirely with quantitative data, and M., a cultural sociologist — about whether Weber's Protestant ethic thesis counts as an explanation at all. Neither would yield, and I turned out to be useless as a referee. The exchange, as I have reconstructed it:

1. M. opened by saying the thesis is the paradigm of a genuine cultural explanation: a religiously grounded orientation to work — a calling, methodical conduct, profit reinvested rather than consumed — raised the probability of a distinctive capitalist disposition, and Weber himself framed the connection as an elective affinity rather than a law.
2. H. answered that the quantitative literature has finished it off: the Protestant economic advantage disappears once you control for literacy and school enrollment, which is exactly what you would expect if what mattered was the drive to get people reading scripture rather than any value as such.
3. M. said this concedes the argument, since teaching every child in a population to read is itself the product of a religious valuation; the literacy variable is part of the culture, not an alternative to it.
4. H. said that is precisely the tell — the thesis cannot fail. Thrift explains accumulation; where thrift did not accumulate, methodical discipline explains that instead; and every mechanism anyone uncovers gets absorbed into the culture. A claim that survives any outcome explains nothing.
5. M. replied that demanding a mechanism is question-begging, because Weber's method was counterfactual and probabilistic: ask whether the course of events would have differed without those orientations. Probabilistic causation is still causation.
6. H. said then culture is only a summary label for the behavior being explained, so the thesis either redescribes its own explanandum or reduces to individual beliefs and incentives — in which case it is psychology in costume.
7. M.'s last move: the ethic outlived the theology that produced it and went on disciplining conduct for generations afterwards, which a story built entirely out of current individual beliefs cannot accommodate.

Please adjudicate this move by move: rate each of the seven from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say briefly why, mark the points at which the two are simply talking past each other, and say what they are jointly missing about what culture would have to be in order to be a cause rather than a redescription. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
