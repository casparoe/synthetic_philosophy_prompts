I am trying to determine what principle, if any, governs my degrees of belief in an Everettian setting, by going through my own verdicts case by case. I am not asking which interpretation of quantum mechanics is correct: for this exercise I grant that the universal state evolves unitarily, that measurement has no unique outcome, and that decohered branches are equally real. My question is narrower and, I think, harder. It is what rule assigns my credences, and whether my verdicts below fix such a rule uniquely, or are inconsistent, or are compatible with several rival rules that I have not thought to distinguish.

Here are the cases and my verdicts.

**Case 1.** A single photon meets a 50/50 beamsplitter and triggers one of two detectors, and I am wired to see which one clicks. Asked beforehand for my credence that I see the left click, I say 1/2. I concede this case is uninformative, since the weights and the branch counts agree here and any rule I like will reproduce it.

**Case 2.** A spin measurement prepared so that the standard weights are 1/3 for one outcome and 2/3 for the other. My credence for the 1/3 outcome is 1/3, not 1/2. I am confident about this, and I take it to rule out immediately any rule that assigns equal weight to each decohered branch.

**Case 3.** As in Case 1, except that in the branch where the left detector clicks, an array of machines then produces one million further exact copies of me, each with my memories up to that moment, while the right branch contains only the single me. My credence that I end up being one of the million: 1/2. Duplication occurring *inside* a branch, after the fact, seems to me incapable of changing the odds of the earlier outcome.

**Case 4.** Now remove quantum mechanics from the story entirely. A classical scanner disassembles me on Earth and, from new matter, builds one copy on Europa and one thousand copies on Mars, all with my memories. Asked my credence that I wake up on Mars, I say 1000/1001. There are no amplitudes to consult here, and sheer number of continuers strikes me as the only thing left that could make one outcome likelier than another.

**Case 5.** The apparatus records the spin at noon; the result travels to me by a slow light pulse that arrives at 12:10. Between noon and 12:10 there are already two decohered branches, each containing a copy of me who has not yet looked. My credence at 12:05 that I will see the 1/3 outcome is 1/3 — the same number I gave at 11:55, before any branching had occurred. But I am uneasy: at 12:05 the matter is already settled, in that a copy of me holds the record, whereas at 11:55 it was not. I report identical numbers in both situations and I cannot tell whether that consistency vindicates my view or reveals that I do not really have one.

**Case 6.** Weights 1/2 and 1/2 again. In the up branch, four environmental degrees of freedom I never inspect — photons scattered into unmonitored modes — decohere that branch into four equally weighted sub-branches; the down branch decoheres into two. At the finest grain there are therefore six decohered branches, four carrying weight 1/8 and all containing an up record, two carrying weight 1/4 and containing a down record. My credence that I see up remains 1/2. Environmental structure that I could not in principle inspect must not be permitted to move my credence.

**Case 7.** I am offered an even-money bet on the 1/3 outcome. I decline it, and I accept the same bet at 2 to 1. I also think that an agent who treats the two branches as equally likely and takes the even-money bet is making a mistake rather than adopting an alternative convention. When pressed, though, all I can offer is that such an agent loses across repeated trials, or that the weights are what make the textbook statistics come out right. I would like to know whether either of those is enough.

**Case 8.** Suppose the 1/3–2/3 measurement is repeated a thousand times. There is a branch of very low total weight whose record shows roughly 500 up and 500 down. I want to say that branch is genuinely improbable, and that I am not obliged to regard every frequency pattern across the multiverse as equally unremarkable. But I cannot say what a low-weight branch has *less of*. It is not less real, and its inhabitant is not a lesser observer with a defective record. "Less likely to be found by me" is the only gloss I have, and that gloss is precisely what I am trying to understand.

**Case 9.** An arrangement in which the branch of weight 0.999999 leaves me alive and the branch of weight 0.000001 triggers a macroscopic device that kills me. My credence that I survive: 0.999999. I firmly reject the inference that I should expect to survive with near-certainty on the grounds that a surviving branch always exists. Yet the survivor's experience is, from the inside, exactly what I would expect if I had survived — so I appear to be using the weights to measure something other than anything I could observe.

**Case 10.** A measurement is performed, its result recorded, and then irreversibly erased in such a way that the branches become indistinguishable and recombine; I never learn which outcome occurred. Here I feel no uncertainty whatsoever. I do not think there was any fact about which branch I would be in, and I would refuse to assign 1/2 to anything. This case constrains my view more than any other, because it suggests that my uncertainty tracks my own future informational situation rather than branching as such.

Three further cases on which I want predictions rather than on which I have verdicts.

**X1.** A reliable colleague performs the 1/3–2/3 measurement an hour before consulting me and seals the record. She tells me all this, and tells me that I am now one of the two resulting people, though I have no way of learning which. Compare the case in which she tells me instead that the measurement will be performed an hour from now. Should my credence differ between the two? My instinct says no, but I am not confident that this instinct survives contact with Case 5.

**X2.** Equal weights, but in one branch I am annihilated at the instant of branching and in the other I continue uninterrupted, with no copying anywhere. What is my credence that I survive — 1/2, 1, 0, or is the question ill posed?

**X3.** Weights that are not a ratio of integers: the branches have weights 1/π and 1 − 1/π. Does the rule deliver a definite credence here, or only by approximating the state with ones whose weights are commensurable? I suspect that several familiar rules are demonstrated for the rational case and then extended by a brief continuity remark, and I want to know whether that remark is doing substantive work or covering a gap.
