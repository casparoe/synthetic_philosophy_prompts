I chair the standing model-review panel of a national transport regulator. About a dozen times a year we rule on whether a simulation model submitted to appraise a road, rail, or bus scheme is fit to support the funding decision. My signature goes on the verdict, which is filed with the recommendation to the minister and enters the record at public inquiries, where opposing counsel tests it line by line; so our reasons cannot rest on deference to the modeller or on professional consensus alone.

Our current practice is inconsistent in a way I want fixed. When a challenger objects that a model's assumptions are false, we usually answer by pointing at its back-cast: it reproduces observed traffic counts and journey times within a few percent, therefore, we say, its assumptions are good enough for the purpose in hand. That is Milton Friedman's instrumentalist line from his 1953 essay on the methodology of positive economics, and it is also precisely what critics call the F-twist — the predictive consequences of an assumption are the assumption's own content restated, so a model that fits well shows only that the package works here, never that the assumption is innocent. Yet in other cases we demand realism outright and reject a submission for assuming away the very mechanism the appraisal turns on. I want one procedure that removes the arbitrariness without treating idealization as a defect in itself.

Please write the decision procedure my panel can run on a submitted model. It must be usable by economists and engineers with no philosophy training, completable within the half-day we allot per model, and produce findings that can go straight into a decision record. Specifically:

- Order the questions we should ask, state what counts as a sufficient answer to each, and say which answers terminate the review in a rejection.
- Require the panel to classify a contested assumption before testing it — known-false simplification of the target system, isolation device for a single mechanism, tractability scaffolding the authors expect to relax, or a presupposition that carries the conclusion while never appearing in the assumptions list — and state how the classification changes what could license it.
- Specify what may be appealed to in place of back-cast fit: robustness across a family of variants, controlled relaxation of one assumption at a time, performance on the particular quantity the decision turns on, behaviour when the model is pushed outside its calibration domain, and whether the model fails informatively rather than silently.
- Mark explicitly which steps are judgment calls wearing procedural clothing, and which questions concern values rather than method (who bears the error, which distortions are politically salient), so that they go to the minister rather than being settled by us.

Then run the procedure on three submissions, using the descriptions I supply: (i) a network-assignment model in which every driver is identical, fully informed about travel times, and route-optimizing, so the network settles at user equilibrium; it reproduces observed flows well and is used to choose between two motorway-widening options whose predicted congestion relief differs by about six percent. (ii) a health-impact appraisal that assigns each scheme a damage cost by assuming pollutant concentrations are uniform across the region and that the exposure-response relationship is linear with no threshold, calibrated on cohort studies of annual mean exposure, and used to rank two alignments that differ mainly in where inside the region the emissions land. (iii) an intercity rail demand and revenue model built on a single representative traveller whose value of time is a fixed fraction of the regional average wage and whose response to fares is a constant elasticity estimated from one pre-pandemic year of ticketing data.

In at least one of the three, the procedure should condemn a model whose aggregate predictive record is excellent, on the ground that the quantity it validates is not the quantity the decision turns on. I would rather have that verdict stated plainly than softened. End by stating what the procedure cannot settle at all.
