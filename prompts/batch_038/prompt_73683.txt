For several years I have been drawn to a view I would like to test against my own case-by-case verdicts: that what ultimately matters, in a life or in a world, is the absence of craving and suffering — that tranquility, and nothing else, is what makes things go well. Here are the judgments I actually make.

- A monk who after forty years of practice no longer wants food, sex, recognition, or even to go on living, and reports an even equanimity: better off than I am, and not marginally.
- A patient with severe anhedonic depression who likewise wants nothing and is undisturbed by wanting nothing: much worse off — though I cannot locate any craving or suffering in him to point at.
- Chronic pain fully abolished in someone left with no projects and no attachments: better than before, yet a neutral life rather than a good one.
- A machine that would give me a contented inner life in which I neither crave nor suffer: I refuse it, and I cannot say what I would be losing.
- My grandmother, who spent her life wanting things for her children and got most of them: the wanting was part of what made her life good, not a deduction from it.
- A patient made placid by lobotomy: not good, whatever else is true of him.
- A world of tranquil beings versus a world of beings who suffer moderately but strive, love, and build: I lean toward the second, which seems to contradict my verdict on the monk.

Is there a principle — or a small family of candidates — that delivers this pattern? State each precisely enough that I can see what work its clauses are doing and which case motivates each clause; say which of my verdicts it must overturn and which it saves only by stipulation. Then apply it to two cases I have not raised: a person who takes a drug that durably but reversibly extinguishes craving, with full knowledge and consent, and a reformer whose dominant craving is the relief of other people's suffering. Finally, if the principle requires a clause distinguishing good absences of craving from hollow ones, tell me honestly whether it is still tranquilism or has become something else.
