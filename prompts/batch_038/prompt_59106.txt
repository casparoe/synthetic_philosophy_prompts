Consider Hofstadter's careenium: an enclosure containing a very large number of small magnetized balls — he calls them simms — careening about, clumping together and breaking apart, such that from a distance one can discern recurrent, structured clumps, the simmballs, which in turn attract, repel, and recruit one another. The walls of the careenium vary in their bounciness in ways that register what is happening outside, so the clumping patterns come to track external regularities, and in the extended version of the device they drive outward behavior as well. Hofstadter's question, put plainly, is which level inside the careenium does the pushing: the simms, or the simmballs?

I would like a credence, not an exegesis. The proposition to be assessed is this:

(C) At least some simmball-level causal claims are true in a sense that is not reducible to, and not merely a convenient redescription of, simm-level causal claims about the same events — the symbol level has causal standing of its own, in that it identifies difference-makers which the lower-level story, however complete, does not itself correctly identify.

To keep C from collapsing into a claim about our ignorance or our descriptive interests, grant the following stipulations:

1. The simm-level laws are deterministic, and an observer with complete knowledge of them and of the precise state of every simm could predict and explain everything that happens in the careenium without ever mentioning simmballs.
2. Every simmball and every simmball-level regularity is wholly implemented by simms and their interactions: no extra stuff, no downward injection of energy or momentum, no violation of microphysical law.
3. The simmball level is not an arbitrary imposition — the clumps are objectively recurrent, mutually isolable, and support generalizations that break down when the wrong things are perturbed.

Two clarifications about the target. First, C is a claim about causal truth, not about explanation or prediction: stipulation 1 already concedes that simmball talk is dispensable for predictive purposes, so an answer whose force is that the symbol level is explanatorily indispensable has not engaged with C. Second, note that stipulation 2 does not by itself threaten C — non-reductive physicalists accept the causal closure of the physical; what would threaten C is an exclusion argument that goes through given closure. If you think closure alone is fatal to C, explain how, since C asserts no violation of the microphysical laws.

Please give me your probability that C is true, decomposed. Assign a number to each of the following, with one or two sentences of justification apiece, and then show the arithmetic of how they combine into a single figure for C:

(a) That causation, in this setting, is univocal and determinate enough for C to have a genuine truth value, rather than being settled by a choice among equally legitimate causal concepts.
(b) That the causal closure of the physical domain holds as a substantive metaphysical thesis at the level of the simms, rather than as a regulative principle of inquiry.
(c) That, given closure, the causal exclusion argument succeeds against C — specifically, that its premise against systematic overdetermination holds, and that a complete simm-level causal account of an event genuinely excludes a competing simmball-level claim instead of simply resting at a different grain.
(d) That a difference-making or interventionist conception of causation, on which proportionality between cause and effect matters, yields literally true higher-level causal claims that coexist with complete lower-level ones, rather than a merely pragmatic relabeling.
(e) That the higher level in a careenium-like system is robust in the way C requires: multiply realizable, stable under perturbation, and not gerrymandered by the interests of whoever describes it.
(f) That your verdict for an actual mammalian brain matches your verdict for the idealized careenium. If the two come apart, say by how much and in which direction, and fold that into the combination.

Then tell me:

- which single term your final number is most sensitive to — where a modest revision would move the overall credence furthest;
- which term you are least confident about, and what kind of thing would settle it: a conceptual argument, a further thought experiment, or an empirical result;
- if C is false, which false: epiphenomenalism about symbols, eliminativism about them, or a reduction on which simmball causal claims are true but analyzable without remainder into simm-level ones;
- whether a defender and a denier of C could agree on every counterfactual, every prediction, and every simm-level fact about the careenium, and if they could, whether that agreement shows the dispute to be verbal.

Report the final credence as a single number to two significant figures. I am not after a survey of the literature, a bibliography, or a list of who holds what — only your numbers and the reasoning behind them.
