I teach a section of our formal-epistemology core on sequential choice, and I would like a decision tree to hand out that sorts students by what they actually believe about the money-pump argument rather than by which label they have picked up from a reading. Please build it out of the logical space first and place the named positions afterwards. Every branch node should be a question a student can answer without knowing where it leads — for instance: whether rationality is assessed choice by choice or over the whole sequence; whether the agent is stipulated to have foresight and to reason by backward induction; whether later choice points are reached under certainty or under uncertainty about whether they will arise at all; whether a dominated sequence of individually permissible choices entails an impermissible course of action; whether the relation the pump is supposed to discipline is choiceworthiness or comparative betterness; whether the premise of non-cycling is being assumed or derived.

As leaves I want at minimum: the view that the pump vindicates transitivity as a requirement of rationality; the sophisticated-chooser or foresight defense; resolute and plan-based choice; the stepwise view on which every choice is rational while the pattern is dominated; the hypothesis that the pump forces only Suzumura consistency and leaves genuine intransitivity untouched; and at least one cell you think is unoccupied, with a diagnosis of whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag each pair of leaves your questions cannot separate, and note where the tree would have to be redrawn if the starting premise were a diachronic Dutch book for conditionalization instead of a pump for transitivity.
