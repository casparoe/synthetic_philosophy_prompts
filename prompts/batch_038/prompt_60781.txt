I'm running a 75-minute session for a graduate bioethics seminar of fourteen students who have read the standard material on advance directives but have never had to decide one. Design a structured committee role-play that makes them feel why a psychiatric advance directive — the precommitment to treatment written while a patient is well, sometimes called a Ulysses clause — is a harder test of the earlier self's authority than the familiar dementia cases. The patient in front of them is articulate, denies being ill, offers reasons of his own, and the directive anticipated exactly this denial and pre-authorized overriding it.

Build the activity around an incompleteness mechanic: the committee may interrogate a recorded statement from the earlier self, but that statement answers only the questions the earlier self thought to answer. When the committee asks something else, they must supply the answer themselves. Those supplied answers should be collected and compared across groups during the debrief, so the students discover that the earlier self they consulted was partly their own construction. Include a second round that reverses the pressure — a case in which directive and present self agree on the outcome but diverge on why — so the group notices whether what they are deferring to is a choice or a set of reasons.

Deliver roles, sample contents for the card deck or equivalent, a timing plan, a payoff scheme that works without real money, and a debrief that maps particular moments onto the distinction between experiential and critical interests and onto the worry that the later self's contentment may itself be adaptive. Tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time this is run and how to recover, and flag what students with experience of involuntary treatment might find hard about it.

Two commitments I want from you. First, be explicit about the feature of the real problem the simulation cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply by hand. Second, do not keep the design neutral: pick one position on the authority of the earlier self that the activity is engineered to make felt, say which and why, and let the scoring scheme serve it.
