Things I want covered (this is my list of pressures on you, not a structure to copy):

- The phenomena, and at what grain. The modern cross-country spread, sure, but also within-country regional variation, the *timing* of divergence, and that uncomfortable pattern where some of the places that were richest around 1500 are among the poorer ones now. Is scale-invariance — one theory that handles a divided border town pair and a whole continent — a genuine requirement, or a demand nobody could actually meet?
- The puzzles it must dissolve or explain rather than wave at: reverse causality running from income back to institutions; the fact that the key variable is usually coded after the outcome by the person advancing the theory; non-random selection into colonial settlement styles.
- Distinctions it isn't allowed to collapse: institutions vs. state capacity vs. policy quality; deep cause vs. proximate cause; explaining a *level* vs. explaining a *growth rate* vs. explaining *persistence*. A theory that explains a rich country's wealth by pointing at something that itself needs explaining has done what, exactly?
- My own half-baked additions. Take these seriously and either sharpen them or reject them with reasons — don't just be polite:
  1. **Negative control:** the theory must name a place where, on its own logic, prosperity should have taken off and didn't, and be right about which place.
  2. **Reversal test:** it must say what happens when the supposed cause is yanked out — decolonization, a regime collapsing, a border moving — and predict convergence or hysteresis in advance.
  3. **Pre-registration:** the key variable must be codeable from evidence that existed before the outcome it's meant to explain.
  4. **Mechanism budget:** count the unobservable intermediaries between your deep cause and GDP per capita, and cap the number.
