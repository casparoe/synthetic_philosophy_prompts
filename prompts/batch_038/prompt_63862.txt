I work on distributionally robust optimization for the perception stack of an autonomous freight vehicle, and my entire working vocabulary is built out of a handful of objects: an ambiguity set of candidate data-generating distributions, a radius chosen against a conservatism budget, a worst-case or CVaR-style expected loss over that set, sample-average approximations together with the out-of-sample guarantees that accompany them, and the duality results that convert a worst-case inner maximization into a regularizer on the outer problem. I am fluent in all of that and have essentially no background in Bayesian epistemology. A colleague in philosophy of statistics has been pressing the imprecise-probabilities program on me — credal sets, lower and upper previsions, Walley-style coherence, the family of decision criteria built on top of them (interval dominance, E-admissibility, maximality, Γ-maximin), and the phenomenon Seidenfeld and his collaborators call dilation — and insists that this is not simply my ambiguity set under another name. More importantly, she insists that the thesis attached to it is substantive: that rationality sometimes requires an agent's beliefs to be imprecise, and that replacing such a belief state with a single precise distribution would be a category error rather than an approximation.

Your task is to restate that program in my vocabulary, item by item, as faithfully as you can manage. Take each of the following in turn: (a) the credal set as the representation of a state of belief; (b) the coherence constraints imposed on lower and upper previsions; (c) the four decision criteria, each mapped onto something in my own practice — is Γ-maximin just my worst-case objective, and is E-admissibility something like admissible or Pareto selection across the members of the set; (d) dilation, the fact that conditioning can leave a set-valued posterior strictly wider than the prior it came from; and (e) the normative claim that imprecision is required rather than merely permitted. One caution governs all of it. In my framework the set is a hypothesis about the world: the truth lies somewhere inside it, and I pay for insurance against its worst member, whereas on her account, as I understand it, the set is supposed to be the agent's state itself, not a claim about which precise distribution obtains. Wherever you suspect that I will instinctively read a set of distributions as uncertainty about which distribution is true, stop and tell me whether that reading carries the item you are translating over intact, distorts it, or breaks it. Do not let the translation smuggle my assumptions in and then count them as successful correspondences: an identical-looking formal object is not evidence of a clean translation if the role it plays in the argument is a different one.

Then give me the residue report. For each of (a) through (e), classify it as translating cleanly, translating only with distortion — saying exactly what gets distorted and how much argumentative work the distortion is doing — or having no expression at all in DRO terms. After that, answer the question I actually care about: is what fails to survive a genuine expressive gap in my framework, because DRO has no concept of a rational requirement on a belief state as opposed to a choice of model, or is it a sign that the philosophical position is less substantive than my colleague makes out — that is, that the claim that rationality requires imprecision reduces to something I already believe, namely that committing to a point estimate you cannot defend out of sample is bad practice dressed up as an epistemological discovery? Finally, run the comparison in the reverse direction: identify anything central to my framework — out-of-sample performance guarantees, the tuning of set radius against conservatism, the tractability of the inner maximization — that has no counterpart on the philosophical side, and say whether that asymmetry is a defect of her program, a defect of mine, or evidence that the two enterprises are doing different jobs entirely. Please give me the analysis rather than a reading list; I am not asking for citations or references of any kind.
