Half my team thinks the number is a real quantity we're estimating badly, the other half (including me, mostly) thinks it's just a relabeled description of the clicks we trained on, i.e. we're not measuring anything, we're renaming our own training data. My roommate did a philosophy degree and I tried to use him as a lookup table but he kept saying "it depends which concept of utility you mean" and then wouldn't say what the options cost you. So before I read anything more about this I want the spec. What does an account of what utility *means* have to deliver to count as adequate? Set the bar at "good enough that I could say out loud what the number my team ships actually is, and defend it," not "good enough for a journal."

Give me the requirements list — the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it's not allowed to collapse, the formal results it has to be consistent with — with the case or consideration behind each one, not just the label. Some things I'd expect to see on there and want to know whether they actually belong: the money-pump thing where intransitive preferences over time get you drained for cash; menu dependence, because our own A/B tests show that adding a clearly dominated third option shifts what people pick, which seems to break any story where utility is a property of the outcome rather than the lineup; preference reversals where choosing and pricing disagree about the same pair; the Allais-style cases; the polite-guest case where someone deliberately takes the smaller portion and you'd be a fool to say that's what he wanted most; the sour-grapes / adaptive-preference case where a person's wants shrink to fit her options, so revealed choices look content while welfare is quietly being destroyed; aggregation across people, since we do ship one score used across a whole population; and whatever the non-uniqueness of the representation theorems implies — if the scale is only pinned down up to a positive affine transformation, does the number carry more content than an ordering, less, or exactly the same?
