I fix bikes for a living, no academic background at all, and I read philosophy stuff on my phone at night — mostly blog posts and wikipedia rabbit holes. A while back I ran into this idea of Bas van Fraassen's called the Reflection principle. My rough understanding is that it says your confidence in something now should line up with what you expect your own confidence in it to be later on. I've been chewing on it becuase of a pretty ordinary personal problem, and I want to know what it actually implies for me, not a summary of the debate.

The situation: I doomscroll. Climate, politics, comment threads, the lot. And I know with fair confidence that if I keep it up for another year I'll come out the other end believing things are collapsing and there's no point doing anything. Not because I'll have learned some new fact — more like the same handful of awful stories on repeat until they set. I also know that if I quit cold turkey, in a year I'll have drifted toward thinking everything's fine. So:

1. If I genuinely expect future-me to believe P, does the principle really commit me to believing P now? That seems to mean I can never be surprised by my own opinions, and that reading anything is pointless since I might as well jump straight to the conclusion I expect to land on. Where exactly does that reasoning go wrong, and where does it not go wrong — is there a real sense in which my current beliefs have to be unbiased guesses about my later ones?

2. Is choosing your own evidence a cheat? If I stop reading, I'm engineering a future me who believes something more comfortable. Reflection seems to bless that, since all it cares about is matching expected future belief. Does that make deliberately avoiding information rational? Can Reflection itself rule it out, or do you need a separate principle about evidence-gathering sitting alongside it?

3. This is my half-baked one. If me-now defers to me-in-a-year, and me-in-a-year defers to me-in-two-years, and so on down the line, doesn't all the authority end up with me-at-85? Which would mean what I believe today is fixed by what some much older, probably more tired and more set in his ways version of me expects to think. That can't be right, but I can't see where the chain breaks.

Give me the handful of implications that actually matter here, roughly in order of how much they'd change what I do, and tell me straight which parts of the above are just confused. No reading list please, I'm never going to track papers down, and keep the notation out of it — plain words about what follows.
