I'm a second-year philosophy student, and self-locating belief is the topic I drew for my comprehensive exam in three weeks, so the Sleeping Beauty problem has gone from a seminar curiosity to something I have to be able to defend in front of a committee. My study partner and I spent Saturday trying to build what we hoped would be a decisive reductio of the thirder position, and by Sunday morning we had the argument below. The trouble is that it worked too well: it seems to establish something I don't believe and would not want to put my name to, which makes me suspect we slipped somewhere without my being able to say where. Background as usual — a fair coin is tossed; on Heads Beauty is woken once, on Tails twice; a drug erases her memory of the awakening so that each awakening is subjectively identical and she does not know which day it is; she is asked for her credence in Heads.

(1) When Beauty awakens she acquires no new uncentered information: the protocol guaranteed at least one awakening on either outcome, and she knew the protocol in full beforehand. (2) So any shift in her credences can come only from self-locating information — from where within the experiment this awakening sits. (3) By stipulation her present experience is identical in each of the three centered situations the protocol generates: Heads-and-Monday, Tails-and-Monday, Tails-and-Tuesday. (4) Where an agent's evidence does not distinguish among a set of possibilities, her credence ought to be spread evenly across them; any uneven spread would privilege one of them on no grounds whatever. (5) Hence each of the three has probability one third, and her credence in Heads is one third. (6) Note that nothing in (1) through (5) turned on there being exactly two Tails awakenings; it turned only on the two branches differing in how many indistinguishable awakenings they produce. So in a variant where Tails produces a thousand awakenings, the same reasoning yields a thousand and one equiprobable centered situations and a credence in Heads of one in a thousand and one. (7) Generalizing once more: whenever two hypotheses are observationally indistinguishable for me but differ in how many times my experience is instantiated, the one that instantiates it more often deserves the higher credence, in proportion to the number of copies. (8) Hypotheses of exactly that shape are on the table — cosmologies on which experiences like mine arise by freak fluctuation, or are run in enormous numbers as simulations — and they instantiate my experience vastly more often than the sober alternatives do. (9) So (7) commits me to putting nearly all of my credence on the hypothesis that I am such an observer, however physically extravagant it is, and no account of evidence on which raw copy-count rather than physical likelihood dominates in that way can be right. (10) Therefore (4) is false: indifference among subjectively indistinguishable centered possibilities is no principle of rational credence, and since that indifference is precisely what drove the one third at (5), Beauty's credence on awakening is one half.

What I want is a diagnosis rather than a survey of the literature. Tell me which step you reject and why that step managed to look innocent when we wrote it down — if the trouble is that a step is being read two ways, say what the two readings are and which one each of (6), (7), and (8) actually needs. If you think more than one step is defective, rank the defects and say whether fixing the lesser one is enough to block (10). Then price the repairs: for each candidate step you would give up, what has to be surrendered along with it, including whether the one-third conclusion can survive on some other footing that does not license (7), and what that footing must say about the thousand-awakening variant at (6). Finally, say what a halfer owes at (1) and (2), given that there seem to be cases in which an agent is warned in advance that she will receive a report of a particular kind and the report nevertheless shifts her odds. For what it is worth, I would rather keep the one third at (5) than accept (10) — but please do not let that preference do any work in your answer, and tell me plainly if the honest price of keeping (5) is higher than I am expecting.
