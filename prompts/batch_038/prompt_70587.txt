so a bit of context. I came at panpsychism from the wrong end, I think — I spent way too much of 2017 on suffering-focused / s-risk stuff (the astronomical-scale arguments, wild animal suffering, all of it), and then someone pointed out that if you care about disvalue at cosmological scales you owe yourself an account of *where in the physics* disvalue lives, and I fell into the Russellian monism hole and never climbed out. Now I'm in a small reading group and I've been asked to write down my actual views so people can shoot at them, and before I do that I want a real audit, because I have a nagging sense that I've been cheating in at least one place and I dont know which one. Here's the package:

1. Physics is purely structural/dispositional. Mass, spin, charge — the theory tells you what these things *do*, how they relate to each other, never what they are in themselves. So there's a categorical gap at the bottom of the picture and something has to fill it.
2. The most economical filler is phenomenal or proto-phenomenal stuff, because that's the one intrinsic nature any of us is acquainted with from the inside. Call me a Russellian monist, with the caveat that I claim zero knowledge of the actual psychophysical laws. And to head off the obvious objection: I don't think this renders consciousness causally inert, since on my version the categorical properties are what *ground* or realize the dispositions, so causal completeness of physics and the causal relevance of the experiential are compatible.
4. The combination problem is open and I'm leaving it open. I don't buy constitutive panpsychism, I don't buy cosmopsychism, I don't have a third option. I genuinely don't know whether or how micro-level phenomenal properties ever add up to a unified subject.
3. Relatedly, and this one I hold firmly — micro-phenomenal properties don't have to be the experiences *of* micro-subjects. There's nothing it's like to be an electron. I don't think that contradicts 1 and 2, and I don't think I'm thereby committed to a universe swarming with tiny sufferers.
5. Moral standing requires a subject. Only beings that there is something it is like to be can be harmed; properties as such have no welfare, no interests, nothing that can go badly for them.
6. On the ethics: suffering-focused welfarism, bad states count for a lot more than good ones and I refuse to commit to an exchange rate. Combined with a basically longtermist frame — the astronomical future is where the stakes are, and I'm on the whole positive about expansion, more minds, more of the good stuff out there between the stars.
7. And yet I talk as though panpsychism had serious ethical teeth. I say things like disvalue is baked into the substrate, the universe isn't morally neutral, and if valence goes all the way down then total suffering is not capped by the number of brains. Two more admissions while I'm at it. First: I reject the "therefore we ought to hope the universe ends early / that vacuum decay is a mercy" conclusion — I want to say that conclusion is grotesque — but I've never managed to justify the rejection in a way that doesn't just beg the question, so please treat my confidence there as evidence about me, not about the argument. And second, a hunch I've literally never said in front of anyone: that whatever the intrinsic properties are, they're *denser* where mass-energy is denser, which would make black holes the morally worst places in the cosmos, and would make stellar-mass collapse into an event of unbounded badness. I find that compelling and also embarrassing.

What I want back is an audit, not a survey and definitely not a reading list — I can't look anything up and I'd rather have your argument than your references. Go pairwise where it matters: which of these actually pull against each other, ranked by how bad the tension is, and for each one say exactly what has to be given up and what the cheapest repair costs me (a repair that quietly smuggles in a claim I've disavowed elsewhere counts as expensive, and I want to know that). Also tell me which tensions only look like tensions — I expect some of this survives inspection and I'd rather you say so than flag everything at equal severity, since then I learn nothing. One thing I specifically want checked: whether 7 is doing any work at all given 4 and 5, or whether I've spent years saying the universe is full of disvalue on the strength of a thesis I deliberately built to be neutral about subjects. And if there's a single revision that cleans up the most damage at once, name it and tell me what I'd lose by making it.
