I run a weakness-of-will seminar that's been deadlocked for two years, and what I want is a diagnosis of the deadlock, not a verdict on which side is right. The philosophers in the room insist akrasia requires acting against a better judgment you still hold — otherwise you merely changed your mind, and changing your mind isn't a failure of anything. The behavioral folks say the judgment is doing no causal work: give the agent a discount curve that's steep in the near term and you reproduce all of the same behavior, and what we call akrasia is just a preference reversal the earlier self predicted correctly. Each side tells me the other has explained a different phenomenon, and nobody moves.

My own hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the fight isn't verbal and isn't empirical, it's about the temporal unit of agency — one self stretched across time who can contradict herself, versus a string of momentary selves who take turns winning. A rival hypothesis I keep coming back to is that the judgment in the philosophy story is temporally neutral (best across my whole life) while the preference in the discounting story is indexed to right now, so the two sides aren't describing the same act at all.

Say which of these, if either, is on target, or what else is going on, and then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, an operationalization, a formal result, a change of question — or why I should expect none. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning: no preamble, and don't restate my question back at me.
