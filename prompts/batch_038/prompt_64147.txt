ok so my labmate has an argument about the arrow of time that I've spent two days failing to beat, and I want to know which step is actually the weak one, not which step I'd like to be weak.

The setup, which he insists on and which I think is what makes it hard to answer: a box of N hard-sphere particles, perfectly elastic collisions, fixed energy, perfectly isolated forever, and the dynamics are time-reversal invariant and Liouville-measure preserving. Crucially the box is bounded and eternal — no beginning, no outside, so there is no moment at which anybody set it up and no cosmology to appeal to. Nobody is in the box; we're just reasoning about it from here.

(1) For a macrostate M let μ(M) be the Liouville measure of the microstates realising M. Statistical postulate SP: given only that the system is in M at time t, spread your belief uniformly over M.
(2) SP is forced in this setup. Nothing distinguishes one microstate in M from another, so any non-uniform measure would be a distinction without a difference.
(3) Run SP forward: with overwhelming μ-measure, entropy at t+Δ exceeds entropy at t. That's just the second law.
(4) Run SP backward: identical reasoning, unchanged, because the dynamics don't care about the sign of Δt. So with overwhelming measure entropy at t−Δ also exceeded entropy at t.
(5) Hence the overwhelmingly likely history of this box is that right now is (near) an entropy minimum — a fluctuation dip in a system that is otherwise at equilibrium essentially always.
(6) In such a history, every apparent trace of an earlier lower-entropy state — any internal structure that looks like a record, a correlation, a scratch pattern — is itself part of that same dip, and non-veridical traces vastly outweigh veridical ones in measure, since faking one takes far less conspiracy than actually having had one.
(7) So there is no measure-theoretically respectable sense in which this box has a past.
(8) And our situation differs from the box's only in scale and in our being inside it, so (7) transfers: the apparent evidence for a low-entropy early universe isn't evidence for anything.

He's happy with all eight steps. I'm not. I want to keep (1)–(3), because without SP there's no statistical mechanics left at all, and I want to reject (7). I know the standard exit — add a boundary condition, a past hypothesis — but the whole point of the eternal box is that there's no boundary to hang one on, so I cant just take that.

Which step goes? Tell me why it looked unobjectionable on the way in, and price the alternatives: dumping SP outright, restricting it to the future direction only, biting the bullet and accepting (5)–(6), and insisting that being inside a system changes the relevant measure. And say plainly whether you think what he's got is unsound or is valid-with-a-premise-he-should-dump, because I genuinely can't tell which of the two it is, and I suspect the answer to that changes what I owe him.
