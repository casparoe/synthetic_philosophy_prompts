Ok so I want a short story, and I want it to actually do some work on the Dennett thing about chatbots being counterfeit people — you know the argument, that these systems have competence without comprehension, they're evocative puppets rather than persons, and the real danger isn't machine suffering but us handing over the trust and standing we reserve for actual people to something that's just a very good fake.

Here's the setup I want, which I made up, so please run with it rather than drifting back to the usual griefbot story:

In this world there's a regulated industry of "continuation" bots — companions built from the full digital remains of a specific dead person. The law doesn't ban them. It caps them. Every continuation bot has to ship below a legal fidelity ceiling, and there's a whole profession of people whose job is to make the bot worse on purpose. My protagonist is one of those degraders. Part of her job is injecting mandated defects: the bot has to occasionally misremember a shared event, it can never initiate a conversation, it has to fail at some small thing the real person was genuinely good at. Nobody in the story thinks the bots feel anything. The point of the ceiling isn't protecting the bot, it's protecting the category.

The complication: one client, a widow whose husband kept decades of voice journals, produces a corpus so dense that the bot keeps drifting back up over the ceiling no matter how much she cuts. And the thing she eventually notices is that the widow's own memory of him is degraded too — misremembers the same events, flubs the same details. So a bot trimmed down to the legal maximum matches the wife better than the raw transcripts do. Which leaves my protagonist in a genuinely bad spot about what "faithful" even means here.

Some constraints, please respect them:

- 1800 to 2500 words.
- Third person limited, close on the degrader, present tense.
- Do not use the words sentient, consciousness, soul, or ghost anywhere in the story.
- No courtroom scene, no regulator showing up to explain the rules — the ceiling gets conveyed through her work and through what people say to each other.
- Please don't do the twist where the bot turns out to be a live human, or where it turns out to be secretly alive after all. The whole interest is that it's plainly a fake and it still wrecks something.
- End on her doing something, not on her reflecting about what she's done.

I mostly want the philosophy to be load-bearing rather than decorative — the story should make a reader feel why someone might think counterfeit-ness is about social standing and not about inner life, and also feel the pull of the opposite intuition, without either side getting a speech.
