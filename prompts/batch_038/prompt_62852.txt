Working on the chapter of my dissertation about what heuristic arguments in number theory are supposed to *show*, and I've built an argument against my own thesis that I can't climb back out of. Roughly:

1. The presumption of independence (PI): when two arithmetical conditions have no structural reason to interact, model them as independent events with their natural densities.
2. Structural reasons are congruence obstructions — e.g. n and n+1 can't both be prime above 2 because one of them is even. That sort of thing defeats PI, and nothing else does.
3. For the classes 1 mod 4 and 3 mod 4 there's no obstruction and no asymetry, so PI gives π(x;4,1) ~ π(x;4,3) ~ x/(2 log x); and since each count is just a sum of near-independent indicator variables, PI also says the difference π(x;4,3) − π(x;4,1) should be positive about half the time.
4. But assuming GRH plus the linear-independence hypothesis about the zeros of the relevant L-functions, it's a theorem that π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1) for a set of x of logarithmic density ≈ 0.9959.
5. So PI isn't merely imprecise or silent here. It's provably wrong about a prediction it actually licenses, and wrong in a standing, one-directional way — the failure is systematic rather than noise.
6. The same recipe — take natural densities, multiply them, patch over congruence obstructions — is what generates the Hardy–Littlewood prime k-tuple asymptotics, which people in the field routinely treat as serious evidence for the quantitative form of the twin prime conjecture.
7. Those asymptotics inherit exactly the warrant that PI itself has, which (5) says is none. A method that can't flag its own failures doesn't get to be called reliable just because its outputs feel right.
8. So heuristic arguments of this kind aren't evidence for anything; they're guesses in a lab coat.

I don't want (8) — the whole project is that there's a respectable notion of epistemic support floating around here. But (4) is a theorem modulo standard conjectures and (5) looks like it just reads off (4), so I can't see which premise to drop without begging the question against it. Name the step that breaks and say why it looked innocent. And if the honest answer is that nothing breaks and I have to eat (8), tell me what I'd be giving up, because I'd rather sacrifice (1) or (6) than accept that, but I can't see how to do either without making PI unfalsifiable-by-construction.
