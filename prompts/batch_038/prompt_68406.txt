A geriatric care assistant runs inverse reinforcement learning on the day-to-day choices of its resident, an elderly woman living alone, and acts on the preferences it infers. Design a case in which this agent ends up worse for the resident — judged by a standard you state explicitly in advance — than a less sophisticated agent simply executing a written specification of her care, even though every textbook assumption is satisfied: her observed behavior is optimal given her preferences, the learner is a competent Bayesian with abundant data, and nothing is hidden from it. I want the case to rule out the familiar diagnoses, so no akrasia, no measurement error, no deception, no reward hacking, and no exotic technology; the failure must come from an assumption IRL normally makes without comment. Keep the scenario to one paragraph and realistic — a household, not a lab.

Then demonstrate the design: state the assumption doing the damage, show why the agent's inference is technically impeccable (say what it can and cannot recover, and up to what equivalence), show why the specification-follower comes out ahead, and identify the feature of the case that carries the result — together with what happens to the verdict if that feature is removed. Finally, give the strongest reply available to someone who thinks the case is rigged against IRL — for instance, that a richer action space or a longer observation window would let the agent learn the very thing it is accused of missing — and say whether that reply dissolves the case or simply relocates it.

State how confident you are in each of the verdicts you assign, and single out the point in the demonstration you are least sure of. If you think one of my constraints cannot be met as written, say which one and offer the nearest design that can.
