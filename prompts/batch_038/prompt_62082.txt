Ok so, no academic background here, I just read a lot of pop history and listen to podcasts while doing the dishes, so sorry in advance for the rambling. There's a thing I keep running into that I can't find a word for, and I'm starting to suspect the word doesn't exist.

Here's the thing. Any time you read about some big change — why states first showed up in river valleys, why Europe ended up as a pile of competing countries while China kept reunifying, why industry took off where it did — there's always a slow-structure explanation sitting underneath it. Rainfall, navigable rivers, coal seams, crops that store well, distance between cores. And afterwards the explanation is really convincing. The outcome feels like it was already in the geography, waiting.

But two things don't line up for me. First, lots of places had the same endowments and nothing happened, or it happened much later, or it happened differently. The rivers and the coal seem to rule a bunch of futures out without picking one — like they write the menu and then somebody else orders. Egypt and Mesopotamia both get irrigation states, but one gets a continuous divine kingship going for thousands of years and the other gets a revolving door of city-states, and the geography doesn't seem to say which. Second, the structure people and the it-was-all-contingent people keep accusing each other of the same mistake in opposite directions. The structure people say "you're pretending a guy on a horse mattered when the trend was already loaded." The contingency people say "you're turning what happened into what had to happen." And I can't tell whether they're disagreeing about anything at all, becuase it looks to me like both can be true at once: the slow stuff can genuinely shrink what's possible while leaving which possibility you actually get wide open.

So — is there an established name for that specific thing? Not for the fallacy version. I've got the sense that "it happened, therefore it had to happen" is a known error, and that hindsight-y stuff generally gets studied somewhere. I mean the other thing: the claim, or maybe just the question, that slow structures constrain the *set* of outcomes rather than selecting inside it, and that this is exactly why the structural account feels inevitable after the fact and unfalsifiable before it. Has somebody drawn that distinction with a term attached? If so, what are the nearest existing ideas and how is what I'm describing different from each one — because I assume it's within shouting distance of path dependence, of the determinism debates, of that French slow-history tradition, and of the counterfactual-history crowd, and I'd like to know which of those I'm reinventing badly.

And if there's no term, coin one for me and define it properly: what counts as a case of it and what doesn't. I especially want the edges. When does a structure stop narrowing the menu and actually order off it — is that even a coherent thing, and how would you know it had happened? Are there real cases where the possibility set genuinely closed down to one option, and cases everyone assumes were like that but weren't? Does the whole distinction depend on believing there are historical laws, or does it survive for someone who doesn't?

Please don't just hand me a label and stop. I care way more about the carving than the name — what the thing is, what it isn't, where it runs out. Also be straight with me about your confidence: say which part of your answer you're least sure of, and what would change your mind. Like, what argument or piece of evidence would convince you that what I described is just one of the existing concepts with nothing left over, or that the distinction I'm drawing is bogus and dissolves under a proper analysis?
