For thirty-five years I worked in population ecology, and Garrett Hardin's lifeboat was familiar to me throughout as a piece of applied biology; only in retirement have I begun to read it as a piece of moral philosophy, where I find it considerably less stable. I would like an analysis of a concrete episode in its terms: the voyage of the St. Louis. The facts, as I have them: a German liner left Hamburg in May 1939 with 937 passengers, nearly all Jewish refugees, holding Cuban landing documents that were revoked before arrival; Cuba admitted only a couple of dozen; the United States and Canada declined to receive the rest; the ship returned to Europe, where Britain took 288 and France, Belgium and the Netherlands divided roughly 620 among them. Some 250 of those who ended up on the continent were murdered within a few years.

Do the mapping deliberately rather than rhetorically: who is aboard the boat and who is in the water, what its capacity is and who measured it, and which of Hardin's premises the episode actually engages — the fixed number of places, the absence of any alternative, the ratchet mechanism he invokes against a World Food Bank — and which it does not engage at all. Then take up a feature I cannot fit into the metaphor: at Evian the previous year the Dominican Republic offered to accept up to 100,000 refugees, and a settlement was founded on that offer in 1940, though only some hundreds ever arrived. Spare capacity plainly existed in the system, if not in the boat lying off Havana. Does that refute the argument, or merely relocate it — another finite boat, another set of passengers someone must refuse? I want a verdict: does the episode confirm the lifeboat account, constitute an anomaly it can absorb, or is it a case the metaphor was never built to handle?

Please mark clearly which of your claims reflect something like settled agreement among those who work on this material — the empirical standing of the ratchet mechanism, for instance — and which are genuinely contested normative questions. I would rather see the disagreement displayed than smoothed into a consensus you do not have.
