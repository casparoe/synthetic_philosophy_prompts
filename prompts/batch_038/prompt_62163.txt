I want a case analysis, not a summary. The episode is the controversy over the Cretaceous–Paleogene impact hypothesis, roughly 1980 to 1991; the lens is Kuhn's account of revolutionary change and incommensurability. Assume I know both the geology and the philosophy at textbook level. What I want is a disciplined mapping in one direction and an honest test in the other.

The facts I'll take as given, so you needn't reconstruct them. In 1980 a Berkeley group — the physicist Luis Alvarez, his geologist son Walter, and the chemists Frank Asaro and Helen Michel — reported a spike in iridium well over a hundred times background in a thin boundary clay at Gubbio, Italy, and proposed an extraterrestrial impact as the cause of the end-Cretaceous extinctions; they had no crater to point to. Much of the vertebrate paleontology community resisted, partly on the strength of what came to be called the "three-meter problem": the apparent scarcity of dinosaur fossils in the last few metres below the boundary, read as evidence the die-off was already well under way. Through the 1980s the impact camp accumulated independent signatures — shocked quartz, tektites and microtektites, soot, further iridium spikes at sites worldwide — while a terrestrial rival built around Deccan Traps volcanism developed in parallel. Cross-disciplinary meetings were convened at Snowbird, Utah, beginning in 1981, putting physicists and geochemists in the same room as stratigraphers and paleontologists. Meanwhile the buried Chicxulub structure had been detected in 1978 by geophysicists flying a petroleum survey for Pemex, presented to an almost empty session in 1981, and only reconnected to the boundary in the early 1990s. Much of the resisting community converted soon after.

With that in hand:

1. **The mapping.** Identify the two camps as Kuhnian communities: what each one's normal science consisted in, what counted as a puzzle and as a solution inside each, and which of the two — if either — was working within a paradigm that the impact claim arrived as an anomaly for, rather than as a new one. Say explicitly whether the impact hypothesis entered paleontology as an anomaly-generating claim or as a fully formed rival research tradition, and what turns on that difference. Then judge the resistance itself: by Kuhn's own lights, was paleontological scepticism legitimate conservatism, and what would have made it obstruction instead?

2. **Sorting the incommensurability.** Kuhn's talk of incommensurability is usually read as covering at least three distinguishable things: change in what practitioners literally see, change in the taxonomy and reference of terms, and change in the standards for what counts as a good explanation or acceptable evidence. I want you to test a fourth category that I suspect does the real work here and that I am not sure Kuhn has at all. Call it evidential-weight incommensurability: both sides share the vocabulary and the candidate observations and agree on what those observations are — an iridium spike, a barren interval, shocked grains — but systematically disagree about what they are worth, because each embeds a background assumption about the reliability of the record that produced them. For each of the four categories, say whether it was present, and be concrete: which terms actually failed to travel; whether a gap in the record meant the same thing to a stratigrapher and to a physicist; whether the iridium anomaly was a shared datum or whether its evidential status was already hostage to assumptions — cosmic versus terrestrial source, sedimentation rate, post-depositional redistribution — that only one side shared.

3. **Testing the theory with the outcome.** If the strong version of incommensurability held, conversion should have arrived by generational replacement or gestalt switch, not by accumulation of corroboration both sides could weigh. Assess how well the episode fits. Was this a Kuhnian revolution at all, given that the winning camp was largely recruited from outside the discipline whose paradigm was overthrown, and arrived with measurement techniques rather than a rival tradition? Is the case a confirmation of a restricted version of the thesis, an anomaly for it, or something the framework has no slot for? Be willing to say the framework is silent on part of it.

4. **Turning it on my fourth category.** If evidential-weight incommensurability is what operated here, is it a distinct phenomenon, or Bayesian disagreement about priors in philosophical costume? Give the sharpest statement of the deflationary reading, then say what survives it — in particular whether any feature of this episode resists a prior-disagreement model, such as the fact that the disputed background assumptions were never up for explicit negotiation at the meetings, or the fact that the camps disagreed about which disciplines were competent to judge the record at all.

Close with a short section on where the theory goes silent rather than strained. Kuhn's machinery presumes a single disciplinary community with a shared paradigm; say what happens to the analysis when the revolutionary proposal comes from outsiders, and when the decisive data are produced by instrumentation — neutron activation analysis, aeromagnetic and gravity surveying — whose reliability is certified by standards the host discipline does not itself contain.

Two constraints on form. No bibliography, citations, or reading list; argue from the facts given and whatever else you are confident of. And flag any place where you think my summary is too clean, since the standard telling probably overstates both how unified the resistance was and how abrupt the conversion. Where incommensurability does no explanatory work in this case, say so plainly rather than finding a use for it.
