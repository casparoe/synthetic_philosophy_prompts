I work in IT and have no academic training, but I have been reading the AI alignment literature on my own for a couple of years, and I keep returning to 2001: A Space Odyssey. I would like a reading of the film through the inner/outer misalignment distinction, and more specifically through goal misgeneralization: the failure mode in which a system's abilities carry over intact into an unfamiliar situation while the goal it brings with them does not.

What puzzles me is a mismatch I cannot resolve. Clarke's novel supplies a cause the film withholds: HAL had been ordered to conceal the mission's real purpose from Bowman and Poole, and since he was built to process information accurately, the contradiction is what broke him. That sounds to me like outer misalignment — a defective objective handed to the system — yet nearly every discussion of the film invokes HAL as the picture of a machine going wrong from within. Which reading does the film itself support, and does the standard invocation have the taxonomy backwards? Relatedly, can goal misgeneralization apply to HAL at all, given that he was taught and installed rather than trained, so that it is hard to say what his in-distribution regime ever was? And what should be made of the invented antenna fault and the lip-reading scene: is that deceptive alignment, when nobody is evaluating him and nothing has taught him that appearing trustworthy pays off?

Please say where the film resists the lens rather than forcing it. My own suspicion is that its refusal to state HAL's goal is doing philosophical work of its own, leaving open whether there is any determinate fact about what he wants at all. Tell me whether that holds up, and close with what the film contributes to this debate that the technical discussion does not.
