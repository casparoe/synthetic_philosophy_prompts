I'm a second-year doctoral student in a comparative philosophy program, and this term I'm in a seminar on Nāgārjuna's root verses taught by a professor whose method in section is unusually regimented. She runs what she calls a drill: she writes a topic on the board, we state the four corners of the tetralemma for it, and then she rules. Over ten weeks her rulings have been these. Causation: asked whether an effect arises from itself, from something other than itself, from both, or without a cause, she denied all four, and then added unprompted that nothing arises ultimately while everything arises dependently, and that the second half of that sentence is not a fifth corner. Motion: whether the goer goes, whether the non-goer goes, whether some third agent besides these two goes — all denied, on the ground that these options exhaust the space, so there is nothing further to say. Fire and fuel: identity denied, difference denied — but here, uniquely, she spent most of the hour arguing against the mixed corner, saying that a fire might be identical with its fuel in one respect and distinct in another, so that corner does not fall out for free the way it does on the other occasions. The person and the aggregates: neither identical, nor different, nor both, nor neither; in the same breath she said that persons nonetheless conventionally exist, act, and can be held responsible. The Tathāgata after death: all four corners denied, with the diagnosis that the real work had been done earlier, by showing that the subject term has no intrinsic-nature referent for those predicates to attach to — the question was malformed rather than answered. Emptiness itself: she said that emptiness is empty, that this is precisely what allows it to do any work at all, and that her negations are non-affirming in the technical sense that they leave nothing standing in place of what they remove. Two pieces of data cut against any simple uniformity. First, when someone asked whether the kettle at the front of the room exists, she said yes and refused to run the drill, saying it is triggered only by questions about how things ultimately are; but when pressed about the four corners as applied to the kettle, she said they do apply and are all denied, adding only that this is not the interesting thing to say about a kettle. Second, in our debate sessions, when a student defended a permanent self distinct from the aggregates she argued solely against difference; when another defended the reduction of the person to the aggregates she argued solely against identity; when a third said she must therefore hold that neither is the case, she replied that she held nothing and asked him what he was asserting; and on two occasions when nobody in the room was committed to any corner she declined to give a verdict at all, on the grounds that there was nothing to refute.

Next Thursday the seminar meets jointly with the metaphysics reading group, and the visitor — someone who works on deflationary theories of truth and on self-referential paradox — has told us in advance that he wants the drill run on the drill. He will ask two things. First: take the claim that everything lacks intrinsic nature; is that claim true, not true, both true and not true, or neither? Second: is the fourfold denial itself one of the corners it denies — does the negation of all four corners apply to the negation of all four corners? He has said he will count as an answer only something that assigns a determinate value to the claim, and that he intends to press her on whether non-affirming negations really leave nothing behind or whether they smuggle in a positive view by another route. I would rather anticipate this than watch it happen cold, and I would also like to know what she ought to say if she is to stay consistent with her own record.

So, working from the rulings above rather than from a survey of how the commentarial traditions or the analytic reconstructions divide — I can read that myself, and it tells me nothing about her — please give me the following. (a) The candidate rules that are jointly consistent with the whole record, including at least two beyond the obvious pair I have already considered: that she denies all four corners uniformly, versus that she denies all four only when the question is asked at the ultimate level and answers directly at the conventional level. (b) For each candidate rule, what it predicts she will say to each of the visitor's two questions, and where those predictions genuinely come apart rather than merely sounding different. (c) Your best guess about what she will in fact say, with the reasons for preferring that rule over its rivals given the record — and, in particular, an account of how each rule handles the two anomalous data, the kettle and the mixed corner in the fire-and-fuel case, since I suspect whatever rule survives will have to explain why she argued that corner at length there and dismissed it elsewhere. (d) Whether any of the readings leaves her exposed to the self-refutation charge we read Nāgārjuna answering — the objection that if every claim is empty his own claim is empty too, and his reply that an empty statement can still perform its function — and whether that charge has any purchase at all on the readings that appear to escape it. (e) One further question about a case of my own choosing that I could put to her which would most cleanly separate the surviving rules, together with what each rule would make of that case, so that I can tell whether I have actually identified distinct rules or only distinct descriptions of the same one.
