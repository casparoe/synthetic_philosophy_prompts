I chair the eligibility subcommittee of a small private foundation that awards postdoctoral fellowships, and we are deadlocked on one application. The operative rule reads: "Applicants must not have held a faculty appointment before the closing date of the competition." A definitions section elsewhere in the charter provides that "faculty appointment" means any appointment carrying the title of lecturer or above. The applicant held a one-year visiting lectureship that she designed and funded entirely from her own earlier grant, with no teaching obligation and no salary from the university. Everyone on the subcommittee agrees she is precisely the kind of early-career researcher the fellowship exists to support. The drafting file — which the charter expressly permits us to consult — records that a trustee proposed adding "excepting self-funded visiting posts" and that the proposal was voted down without discussion. We also hold a decision from this subcommittee eleven years ago admitting an applicant in materially similar circumstances, though it gives no reasons.

Draft the written opinion I will circulate before our vote. Open with a one-sentence recommendation, then give the reasons a dissenting member could actually engage with: how the definitions clause bears on the case, what weight the rejected amendment should carry and why, and how to treat the unexplained prior decision. Give the strongest argument for the opposite conclusion, and close with one sentence on whether and how the rule should be amended. Keep the whole document under 450 words.
