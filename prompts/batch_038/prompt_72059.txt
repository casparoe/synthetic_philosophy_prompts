I am preparing a section of a dissertation chapter on scientific realism, and the argument I have assembled against epistemic structural realism now seems to prove considerably more than I want from it. Here it is.

(1) Epistemic structural realism — the position Worrall introduced in 1989 as a way of taking what is best from realism and what is best from anti-realism — holds that what our successful theories get right about the unobservable world, and what is retained across radical theory change, is the structure they describe, the pattern of relations, rather than the intrinsic nature of the relata.

(2) To assert that the world has a given structure while disclaiming knowledge of what stands in the relations, one must existentially quantify over those relations: there are relations R1 … Rn such that the world, or the relevant portion of it, satisfies the description the theory gives. Call this the Ramsey sentence of the theory.

(3) A sentence of that shape, in which every relational term of the theory is bound by an existential quantifier, is true just in case there are objects enough to be assigned to the variables in a way that makes what is left over — the purely logical skeleton of the theory — come out true.

(4) Provided there are objects enough, such an assignment can always be found, because the skeleton that remains imposes no condition beyond cardinality.

(5) So the structural realist's claim amounts to the claim that the universe has at least such-and-such a size, which is either too thin to count as an empirical discovery or, if it is not thin, a piece of a priori set theory rather than a result of physics.

(6) Either way it cannot do the work the structural realist needs it to do, since a trivial or empty truth does not explain why a theory's novel predictions come out right — and explaining that was the entire motivation for retreating to structure.

(7) Therefore structural realism is not a stable middle ground: it collapses into triviality, or into a claim about cardinality that no empirical evidence could support.

My difficulty is that nothing in (3) through (5) mentions structure in the structural realist's sense, and the steps look entirely reusable. Take an ordinary scientific realist's claim that there are unobservable entities bearing such-and-such masses and charges and standing in such-and-such causal relations: existentially quantify the theoretical vocabulary and, by (3) and (4), that claim reduces to a cardinality condition as well. Take a mathematical existential claim, that there is a domain satisfying the axioms of second-order arithmetic. Take the anti-realist's own claim about what our theories entitle us to believe. Take premise (4) itself, which is a general claim about which assignments are possible. If the argument succeeds, then every claim that posits a pattern without naming what fills the pattern is vacuous, and I am left with nothing that discriminates structural realism from anything else — including from the argument I am using to attack it.

I should say what I am attached to. I want to keep (1) as a fair statement of the target, and I want to keep (6), because if the structural content of a theory does turn out to be trivial, the no-miracles motivation for it really does evaporate. What I cannot do is present an argument whose conclusion is that nothing existential can be said about the unobservable world at all.

So: which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I would like to know whether the trouble lies at (3), as a claim about what a sentence of that form says; at (4), as a set-theoretic step; or in the disjunction at (5), which quietly assumes that a claim about size and relational pattern cannot be a substantive empirical one. For each of those three exits, tell me what the structural realist must insist on in order to walk through it, and whether insisting on that costs her the distinction between the epistemic and the ontic versions of her position. Finally: is there a restricted version of the triviality worry that survives your diagnosis and still bites against structural realism in particular, rather than against all existential theorizing at once?
