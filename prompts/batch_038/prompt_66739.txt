I've been arguing with a colleague about how much of Marx's critique of capitalism depends on the labor theory of value, and I want to pin down the two halves separately. As I understand it, exploitation is the claim that the worker is paid less than the value his labor creates; that is a claim about surplus labor being extracted in production rather than a claim about how prices get set, so the marginalist rejection of the labor theory of value leaves it entirely untouched. Alienation, by contrast, seems to me to be the exposed half, because it only gets going if labor is the source of value: strip the value theory out and there is nothing left to the worker's estrangement from the product of his labor except the bare fact that he doesn't own it, which any property theorist can grant without accepting anything distinctively Marxian. My colleague thinks I have the dependency running backwards. Please explain what each of the two claims actually presupposes about the theory of value, and identify where the genuine pressure point in Marx's account lies.
