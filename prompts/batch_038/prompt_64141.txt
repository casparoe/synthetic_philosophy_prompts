The assignment was:

"Wright's entitlement account says we are warranted in accepting our hinge commitments without evidence. Take a case where two communities have genuinely conflicting hinge commitments and ask: does the case show that hinge epistemology collapses into epistemic relativism, or can the two hinges still be brought into rational contact? Be concrete about the case and say what work the notion of entitlement is doing in your answer."

Student's answer (condensed but nothing cut that matters):

The case I'll use is a network of heirloom seed savers and a regional agronomy lab. The savers' whole practice — which plants to save from, whether a bed got crossed, whether a variety has "gone off" — presupposes that their varieties grow true from saved seed. The lab treats that same claim as a hypothesis and runs trial plots to test it. Neither side is arguing about it in the ordinary way; it sits underneath their arguments.

For the savers, then, true-from-seed is a hinge. It isn't held on evidence, and it isn't the sort of thing they'd cite as evidence for anything else. Their certainty about it shows up in the saving, in the labeling, in the trades — not in a doctrine they assert. That's the Wittgensteinian point: hinge certainty is exhibited in action rather than stated.

Wittgenstein's own solution to the skeptical problem is that we are justified in our hinges even though we can't justify them, and Wright sharpens this into entitlement: a non-evidential warrant. So the savers aren't irrational, and neither is the lab, since the lab is equally entitled to its background assumption that anything is testable in principle.

One thing worth noticing is that the lab's hinge is much more local than it first looks. The savers' commitment is load-bearing for everything they do; the lab's isn't nearly that structural, since a lab could drop "everything is testable" for one particular domain without its practice collapsing. So the two hinges aren't really symmetric, and the case may partly be about a claim that looks like an ordinary empirical generalization doing hinge work — the boundary between those two categories is doing a lot of quiet labour here.

But hinges, once in place, are immune to revision, and that's part of what makes them hinges rather than well-supported beliefs. Since the savers' commitment can't be dislodged by evidence, criticism of it from the lab's side is always question-begging, and the standoff can't be resolved by argument.

That said, relativism doesn't follow. Entitlement is available to any rational agent, so the savers and the lab are equally warranted, and given enough time the lab's trial-plot results could persuade the savers to give the commitment up anyway. So there's rational contact after all: nothing further needs to be said, and the worry about collapse into relativism is answered.

The savers also strike me as more confident of true-from-seed than of anything else they believe, which is just what you'd expect if it's a hinge — maximum subjective certainty where there's no evidence.
