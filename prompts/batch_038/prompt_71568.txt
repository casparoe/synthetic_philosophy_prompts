I serve on a university sustainability committee that has just signed up to a voluntary offset scheme, and in preparation I read the registry's methodology documents cover to cover: the baseline scenarios, the additionality tests, the buffer pools held back against reversal risk, the vintage rules about which year's credits may be retired against which year's claim. Over the same fortnight, and for entirely unrelated reasons, I have been reading the debate about invertebrate welfare, and in particular the line of argument that holds that even if the probability that an insect is sentient is quite low, the sheer number of insects is so vast that the expected total of insect suffering still overwhelms everything else on the moral ledger. It gradually struck me that these are the same document written in two vocabularies, and I would like a piece of satire that makes that visible without announcing it.

Please write a parody of an offset-registry methodology document — call it something on the order of the Expected Suffering Registry's "ESR-1001: Quantification, Verification and Retirement of Avoided Invertebrate Disvalue Units, Version 3.2" — running roughly 800 words, in the flat, clause-numbered, aggressively procedural prose of a genuine carbon standard. I would like the usual apparatus: scope and applicability, baseline scenario, quantification methodology, a discount factor for uncertainty about moral status, additionality, leakage, reversal risk and the buffer pool, monitoring and verification, and a short list of excluded activities. Include at least one fully worked numerical example with real arithmetic — a credence in sentience, a moral-weight-per-subject coefficient, a population figure, multiplied out into an integer number of credits — arranged so that a reader who follows the multiplication can see that the final figure is generated almost entirely by the exponents rather than by any measurement anyone could have made. You may invent the unit, the registry, its technical working group, and its revision history.

The rules of the game matter more than the setting. Everything in the document must be a faithful, internally consistent application of the expected-value reasoning it is parodying: no straw men, no authorial wink, no place where the registry concedes it might be mistaken except in its own idiom of conservatism factors, methodological uncertainty, and deductions from the buffer pool. Do not name real organizations, real researchers, or any living person; the target is a style of quantification, not anybody. The standard objections to this kind of argument — that the probability estimate is doing all the work, that nobody knows whether short insect lives are net positive or net negative, that aggregating across a million tiny welfare subjects may not be a simple multiplication, that any plausible intervention is dwarfed by background population dynamics, and that the inputs are not merely uncertain but unmeasurable in principle — should be enacted as procedural requirements rather than stated as criticisms, so that a reader who knows the literature recognizes each objection in its bureaucratic disguise while a reader who does not can still enjoy it as a document. Close with an eligibility or retirement clause whose practical effect is the punchline, delivered entirely straight.
