Here's roughly what he sent: (1) Kelly is just a formula for how much of your bankroll to put on a bet when you think you have an edge — for a bet that pays b-to-1 you stake f = (bp − q)/b of your roll, where p is your win probability and q is 1 − p, which in practice means your edge divided by your odds. (2) The reason it works is that it's the stake size that maximizes the expected value of your bankroll; anything smaller leaves growth on the table and anything bigger takes risk for nothing, so Kelly is a single number, not a range. (3) It has nothing to do with utility or psychology or how much risk you can stomach — plain expected-value thinking tells you to bet your whole roll on any positive-edge bet, which obviously bankrupts you, and Kelly fixes that by looking at what happens to one person over a long sequence of bets rather than averaging over imaginary parallel bettors; a Nobel laureate in the sixties wrote a paper calling this a fallacy and it never really got settled. (4) If your estimate of p is a bit off it's no big deal, you just grow slightly slower, because the curve is smooth near the top. (5) You literally cannot go broke betting Kelly, since you're always only staking a fraction of what you have — worst case you get small. (6) Half-Kelly halves your long-run growth and also halves your swings, so people who tell you to always bet half are just admitting they're scared; pros bet full.
