I'm taking an archaeological theory seminar this term, and two people in my lab group got into a sustained argument over a report we were assigned. The report described a pit sealed beneath the floor of an ancient house, containing a complete ceramic vessel that had been broken before it was deposited, three cattle skulls arranged around it, and a bronze pin; there was no ash, no charred grain, and no butchery waste. Daniel said this was plainly a foundation deposit: a serviceable pot deliberately destroyed, body parts curated rather than dumped, and a sealed context under a renewed floor. Yara said he was reaching for the residual category, that ritual is simply what we call a deposit once we run out of mundane explanations, and she offered some: the skulls as hide-working debris, the vessel as a cracked heirloom discarded during renovation, the pin as a loss, and the absence of refuse as evidence the pit was cleaned and lined.

Daniel then proposed a test to make his claim falsifiable: count a deposit as ritual only where something of recoverable economic value is destroyed or permanently withdrawn from circulation. He said this generates predictions, since ordinary middens should contain neither whole vessels nor selected skulls, and that the predictions hold. Yara replied that the test prices the past in our currency, because it is the archaeologist who judges the vessel costly, not the household; that on this definition a feast counts as ritual, since eaten food is value irrecoverably spent; and that it cannot distinguish a foundation deposit from conspicuous waste, prestige display, or the disposal of objects held to be polluting, which would be the opposite of an honour. Daniel conceded the test recovers the kind of act but not the content of any belief, which he said must come from iconography or analogy. Yara said that is a retreat into the original vice, since ritual stripped of content is just the non-economic, exactly where he began. They asked me to referee and I could not.

Could you work through the exchange step by step and say who is right at each point, rather than simply who wins overall, and tell me whether both are assuming something neither of them notices? Please write in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write a letter.
