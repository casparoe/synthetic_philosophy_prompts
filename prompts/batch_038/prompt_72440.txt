I work in nonequilibrium statistical mechanics — stochastic thermodynamics, information flow in Markov systems, coarse-graining. My native vocabulary is mutual information between subsystems, transition matrices, entropy production, the choice of partition and of grain, and measures of how strongly two sets of degrees of freedom are coupled. I have no philosophy training. Colleagues on a consciousness-adjacent project have asked me to engage seriously with Integrated Information Theory rather than wave it off, and I want to know exactly how much of it I can already express in the language I use, and how much I cannot.

Here is what I think the theory is doing; tell me where I have it wrong. Translate its central content into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can: the axioms and the corresponding postulates about mechanisms; the claim that experience is *identical to* a cause-effect structure rather than merely correlated with one; the way Φ is obtained by cutting a system in half, measuring what the cut destroys, and taking the cut that destroys the least; the exclusion requirement that the conscious system be the maximally irreducible set of units — a maximum taken over candidate boundaries and, in some versions of the theory, over levels of description from micro to macro; and the insistence that the relevant cause-effect power be intrinsic, so that the system takes and makes a difference within itself rather than for an outside observer who has chosen the factorization.

Then give me the residue report, in three explicitly separated piles. What translates into my language with no loss. What translates only if I permit a specific distortion — and for each such case, state exactly what the distortion is and how much of the original claim it costs. What has no expression in my language at all. Do not smuggle: if rendering intrinsic cause-effect power as some form of mutual information requires me to adopt a stipulation my framework does not otherwise license, mark it as a stipulation instead of presenting the result as a faithful translation. I would rather have an honest failure than a tidy mapping, and I am not interested in a version of IIT that turns out to be a conventional integration measure by renaming.

Finally, take a position on the largest piece of residue. Is the inability to express intrinsicality — and the fact that my formalism never selects a unique boundary or grain by maximizing a functional, because in my work the partition is always supplied by the modeller — a genuine limit of the physicist's vocabulary, something physics cannot yet say about a system's relation to itself? Or is it evidence that these claims are doing less work than their phrasing suggests? Argue for one of these, with reasons, rather than balancing both.

Skip the stock objections unless they bear directly on the translation. I have already met the incomputability complaint, the cerebellum argument, and the 2023 letter calling the theory pseudoscience; I am not asking about them, and I am not asking for a summary of IIT for a general reader.

Keep the whole answer under 900 words. Close with two short paragraphs: what consideration, result, or reformulation would change your verdict about the residue; and where you think your own translation is most likely to have gone wrong or unfairly compressed the theory.
