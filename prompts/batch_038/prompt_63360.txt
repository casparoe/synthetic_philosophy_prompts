I am drafting a story for a collection about institutions obliged to draw lines through continua, and I want you to write the piece as it ought to be written, not as a philosophy lecture in borrowed clothing. The technical requirements below are binding; they are the entire reason the story exists.

**The conceit.** A municipal Continuations Board administers a benefit that does not end on a birthday but when the claimant becomes *frail* — a term the enabling statute declines to define, and one that cannot be sharpened without simply deciding in advance the fate of the next case along. Two rival offices have grown up under the same roof, and the story is their collision.

- The Ledger Office holds that in every case there is already a fact of the matter: a claimant is either frail or not frail, and the Board's determination merely records which. There is therefore a last file on the right side of the line and a first file on the wrong side of it. No clerk in the office can ever say which pair that is. This is not a shortage of data, staff, or diligence — the ledger is complete and the clerks are excellent. It is structural, a feature of what knowing takes: to know that a claimant sits inside, one's method must also hold in the cases indistinguishably adjacent to hers, and at the frontier no method can hold, because adjacency is precisely what defeats it. The nearer a clerk comes to the boundary, the less her excellent evidence licenses. The reader should feel this as vertigo, not as a puzzle the characters have been too stupid to solve.

- The Blank Office holds that frontier cases contain no determination to be found: read against every acceptable way of making the statute precise, those claimants fall in a gap. Its forms carry a third column, neither yes nor no, and it takes pride in the fact that within that column every instance of *she is frail or she is not frail* still comes out true — a claim the office can make about the pair while unable to make either half of it. Its soft spot is that the blankness of the column is itself a matter with a blurred edge: whether a case is *definitely* blank admits of exactly the same trouble one level up, and again above that.

**Required scenes.** (1) A Ledger Office clerk is handed the complete determination history for one district — every file since the office opened — and uses it to narrow the frontier to a single pair of adjacent cases, only to find the narrowing has told her nothing, since whatever she may now say of either file could be said, on identical evidence, of its neighbour. (2) A stranger offers that same clerk a wager she cannot lose: money laid on the disjunction, not on either side of it. She must work out whether taking it would vindicate the Blank Office, the Ledger Office, or neither. (3) One named claimant whose file sits in the gap, and the giving of her the news. The weight of the story should land on her, not on the offices.

**Constraints.** 1,400 to 1,900 words. Past tense, first person, narrated by the clerk who holds the ledger. Register: municipal-bureaucratic — forms, stamps, seniority, petty grievances — with no science-fictional or supernatural apparatus; nothing may occur that could not occur in a city hall. No character may speak the words *vagueness*, *sorites*, *epistemicism*, *supervaluationism*, *truth-value gap*, or *precisification*; the two doctrines must be carried wholly by procedure, dialogue, and consequence. No philosopher's name, and no reference to any book, article, or theory. The ending may not adjudicate between the offices, but it must make felt what each one costs the claimant of scene three.

**After the story**, append a craft note of no more than 250 words, addressed to me as a working novelist: which piece of narrative machinery carried each of the two doctrines; where you think the story comes closest to misstating one of them, and how a partisan of the misstated view would object to that scene as written; and which of the three required scenes you would sacrifice if I cut you to 1,000 words, and what would be lost by its absence.
