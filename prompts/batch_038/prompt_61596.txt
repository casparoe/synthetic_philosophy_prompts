I TA an intro political philosophy section and we're doing a week on epistocracy vs. democracy. I want to hand out a decision tree that starts a student at "a lot of voters are badly informed and you can see it in the results" and walks them down to a named position — democracy defended as intrinsically valuable, democracy defended as the best available truth-tracking procedure, sortition, plural voting, an epistocratic veto on legislation, rule by the knowledgeable outright, whatever else belongs in the space.

Build the tree out of questions rather than out of names, and use the three that people tend to collapse into one: whether there's any non-question-begging way to pick out who is politically competent; whether legitimacy requires the acceptance of the people being ruled or only a tendency to produce good outcomes; and whether the thing being compared to epistocracy is democracy as it actually runs or democracy at its idealized best. Every node has to be answerable by someone with no idea which way the branch goes.

Then flag the empty cells: which combinations of answers nobody actually holds, and for each one say whether that's because the view is incoherent, because it's just unappealing, or because nobody's thought of it yet. And tell me which two leaves your tree can't realy separate — the ones that end up in the same place no matter how a student answers.
