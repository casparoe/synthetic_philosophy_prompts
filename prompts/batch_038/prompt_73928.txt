For the better part of two decades I have been a devoted reader of the rationalist blogosphere, and for most of my working life I have been on the operational side of the map/territory distinction: I build and maintain the taxonomic backbone of a large biodiversity occurrence aggregator. Roughly speaking, every record in our holdings — a geotagged photograph, a museum specimen, a tissue sample with a barcode, a legacy survey row — must be assigned exactly one species key, and that key is what downstream users cite, permalink, and run ecological models against. My team argues endlessly about which expert treatment to follow, but I have come to suspect the argument is upstream of any particular treatment.

What I want is the specification, not the survey. Write out the desiderata that a species concept must satisfy in order to be adequate as the assignment rule for an infrastructure like ours: for each requirement, give the consideration or the concrete case that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable for our purpose, negotiable, or merely a preference of one school. Then tell me which requirement does the most work in excluding candidates, and whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable. If you believe a subset is unsatisfiable, state it as an impossibility result with the reasoning laid out — not as a reverent gesture toward mystery. I would rather be told that two of my demands contradict each other than be told that species are complicated.

The constraints I already suspect belong on the list, which I want you to pressure-test, sharpen, or reject rather than merely restate:

- Assignment must be possible from an isolated record. Our pipeline never has the population in front of it: no mate choice observed, no sympatry map, no breeding experiment. Concepts whose criteria are population-level properties have to say what happens when only one individual is available.
- The key must be stable enough that a citation made five years ago does not silently change referent, yet revisable when the taxonomy genuinely improves. I want to know whether any concept can reconcile those two, or whether the demand is simply ill-posed.
- Hybrids and heavily introgressed lineages must resolve to something without crashing the pipeline. When a concept answers that there is no fact of the matter, I need to know whether that is a defect of the concept or of my pipeline.
- Where two authoritative treatments conflict, we currently retain both keys and flag the record as ambiguous. Is there any concept that can underwrite that practice as principled, or is it a confession?
- Diagnosability, monophyly, and separately-evolving-lineage status come apart constantly, and each has been proposed as the essence. I want to know which of my requirements are artifacts of the reference-fixing purpose, and which any purpose — including a purely predictive one, where the key exists to license ecological generalization — would have to respect.

Finally, and briefly: assess whether the standard two-tier move — a stable operational key kept separate from a truth-apt lineage hypothesis — actually discharges the tension or only relabels it. If you feel moved to score the main family of concepts against your list, keep it to a few lines at the end; the criteria are what I am after. No bibliography, no reading list, no citations.
