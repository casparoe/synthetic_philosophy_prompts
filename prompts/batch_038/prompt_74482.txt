My professor is a pretty hard deflationist — her line is that when a paper says the model represents X, all it has ever actually shown is that X is decodable from the activations and that poking something changes the output, which is exactly what an instrumentalist would have predicted anyway. I'm inclined to agree with her, mostly because we did the Chinese room in week three and I found it more convincing then I expected. Here's the passage, more or less verbatim from what he forwarded: "Mechanistic interpretability rests on a bet: that a neural network is not a black box but a machine with parts, and that the parts can be found. The evidence for the bet keeps mounting. Researchers have identified circuits that let a model copy a pattern it saw earlier in the same conversation, directions in activation space that separate true statements from false ones, and — in the most famous demonstration — a Golden Gate Bridge feature that they could crank up until the model dragged the bridge into every single answer. Sometimes such a feature sits in one neuron; more often a given neuron is noisy and fires for a lot of unrelated things, but that is only a sign we are looking at the wrong level, and the newer decomposition methods break that noise down into clean, separable features. A feature of that kind is a representation: an element inside the system that stands for something outside it, and that changes what the system does when it is active. Which is why the old philosophical anxiety looks increasingly dated. For decades people argued that you could never know whether a machine had genuine inner states or was only producing the right behavior, the Chinese room and all that. But if you can open the box and point to the thing inside that stands for the bridge, and show that turning it up changes what the model says, the anxiety has nothing left to grip. What remains is an engineering problem: the representations are there, we just can't read most of them yet. That has practical consequences too. If a model is planning to be deceptive, the deception will be represented somewhere in it, and a good enough reading will show us before we deploy."
