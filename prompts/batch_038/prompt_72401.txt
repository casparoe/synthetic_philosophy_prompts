so I'm giving a position paper at a consciousness reading group next month and I've finally tried to write my actual views down in one place, and now I can't tell whether they hang together. here's the list:

1. causal closure of the physical, full stop — no mental-to-physical causation, and I'm not willing to pay for systematic overdetermination either.
2. qualia are real, intrinsic, non-dispositional properties. they aren't functional roles and they aren't dispositions to judge or to behave.
3. so I'm an epiphenomenalist: the redness of my red experience doesn't cause anything, not my saying "red," not my reaching for the tomato.
4. but introspection is knowledge by acquaintance — direct, non-inferential — and that's why the Mary case has bite for me: she gains something, and the something is about the experience itself rather than about her dispositions.
5. phenomenal reports are generally reliable, and not by luck. when I say the second swatch looks a shade more saturated, that's usually right, and I don't think the world is just arranged so my reports happen to line up.
6. zombies in the philosophers' sense are concievable and metaphysically possible: a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me with nothing it's like to be it is genuinely possible.
7. and roughly how I think about content: what a state is about is fixed by what it's hooked up to, causally and counterfactually. I don't think there's a non-causal route to aboutness.

plus one case verdict I'm confident about: my experienceless functional duplicate would make all the same claims about consciousness, and those claims would be false or empty, whereas mine are true.

the thing I keep reaching for as a rescue is a move I've started calling the welding move, for want of a better name: qualia are causally inert with respect to the physical, but psychophysical laws tie each quale to its brain state necessarily rather than contingently, so my report is caused by the brain state and the brain state couldn't obtain without the quale, hence the report couldn't be wrong. reliability without influence, that's my slogan.

what I want from you:

- go through the list and say which pairs genuinely pull against each other, ordered by how bad the problem is rather than flat-listed as equally bad. for each, say what would have to go or be weakened, and how expensive that revision is for the rest of the view.
- does the welding move actually rescue 5? and does it rescue 4? I have a feeling those are seperate questions and that my slogan only answers one of them, but I can't pin down which.
- I also suspect 1 and 2 are in trouble together, since positing properties physics has no use for seems like it should bother me — but I can't say why it should. is that a real inconsistency or am I just feeling the parsimony bill?
- then tell me the single cheapest revision that buys the most consistency, and what I'd lose by making it.

one thing about how you answer: please separate what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested. if you say something like acquaintance requires causal contact with its object, flag whether that's a constraint everyone accepts on the notion or one camp's gloss that the other camp rejects, and same for whether the reliability worry is taken to be a knockdown objection or a bullet most people are happy to bite. don't hand me your preferred line dressed up as the received view.
