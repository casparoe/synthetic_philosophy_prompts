ok so I TA a grad metaphysics seminar and this week we did laws of nature — the whole Humean vs. anti-Humean fight — and the last twenty minutes got genuinely heated. One of our students is a hardline Lewisian (laws are just the best summary of the pattern of local facts, full stop) and another one kept insisting that laws have to *govern*, that a summary can't make anything happen, and a third started muttering about the circularity complaint — you explain the instances by the law and the law by the instances and nothing ever touches the ground. My advisor suggested that instead of a summary handout I close next week's session with a poem, which I love as an idea but I am not a poet, so I need you to be one. Assume you're someone who writes serious formal verse and also has an analytic metaphysics background, so you know exactly what's at stake in this debate and won't just wave at it.

The conceit I want is a pantoum, and I think the form is genuinely apt rather than decorative. Five quatrains: lines 2 and 4 of each stanza get recycled as lines 1 and 3 of the next, exactly two brand new lines enter per stanza, and the last stanza loops back so that the poem's very first line comes back as its final line. That's the Humean picture structurally — nothing new ever enters the world, it's all the same local facts getting permuted, and what we call a law is a regularity you notice in the permuting. So here's the mapping I want you to hold to strictly: the two *new* lines in each stanza should be the ones that talk about why (explanation, holding, necessitating, what keeps the regularity from being luck), and the *recycled* lines should be the ones that just report what happens, the bare fact-level stuff. Then the trap springs on its own: every "why" the poem asserts gets demoted in the very next stanza into another "what," a repeated reporting line — which is exactly the circularity objection, and also exactly what the Humean says is fine, since summarizing is all a law was ever supposed to do. I want someone who knows the debate to be able to tell from the poem alone what's happening, with zero proper nouns.

Constraints, roughly in order of importance: no names of philosophers and no jargon (no "supervenience," no "nomological," no "best system," and the word "law" gets used at most once, maybe zero times); keep the imagery concrete and continuous — pick one recurring scene, something like a tide that comes in on the same rocks, a kettle, a commute driven the same way for years, a bridge that holds, whatever you think works — so the recycled lines feel like the same observation returning rather than like filler; loose meter, rhyme optional but if you rhyme, rhyme within stanzas only. Before you write the poem, sketch your plan: lay out the stanza-by-stanza map showing which lines get inherited from where, and say in a couple of sentences what the closing loop is supposed to leave the reader unable to decide between. That last part matters most to me — I want the ending genuinely ambiguous about whether going all the way around and arriving back at your opening line is the Humean's peace (there was nothing outside the pattern to find) or the anti-Humean's indictment (we circled the whole mosaic and never once explained anything). Please think carefully about getting that double reading to land in the final two lines rather than stating it. And if the strict recycling makes a stanza clunky, adjust the wording of the inherited line minimally rather than abandoning the form — I'd rather the poem limp a little than cheat.
