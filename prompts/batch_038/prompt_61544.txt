I co-organize a monthly reading group — philosophers, two condensed-matter people, a science-studies scholar — and this year we are putting together a slim companion volume of original fiction, one story per session, circulated a week ahead of the paper it accompanies. Last year's volume included a story on retrocausality that everybody still argues about, which is the effect we want: not a dramatization of a position, but a situation in which the stakes of a debate become hard to shrug off. My session is on panpsychism and its ethical fallout, and I would like you to write that story. Call it 1,500 to 2,200 words.

Here is the situation I have in mind. You may develop it, but please do not abandon its spine. The setting is a low-temperature metrology facility that has spent a decade building an instrument whose stated purpose is entirely unremarkable: an unusually sensitive interferometric measure of macroscopic phase coherence in condensed-matter systems, together with a very fast record of how that coherence decays. Nobody in the story doubts the readings; nobody thinks the thermometers lie. What is contested is the interpretation the facility's director has bolted onto them — the assumption that what physics characterizes only structurally has an experiential intrinsic nature, and that long-range coherence is the physical counterpart of a subject's unity rather than a mere convenient description of a many-body state. On that reading, a superconducting ring held below its critical temperature is not a vast plurality of particles but one subject, and warming it past the transition is not a change of state but an act with a victim. The incident that organizes the plot: a cryoplant fault quenches a large magnet, coherence collapses within microseconds, and the fast record shows an enormous spike in the final instants. The amplitude is beyond anything previously logged, but the sign — whether that spike marks something bad or merely something very intense — is a calibration question the instrument was never built to answer, and the director knows it. The facility has since been asked to advise on decommissioning eleven similar magnets, and three people hold three views: a night-shift technician who has stood in the hall during quenches and can no longer treat the trace as data; the director, who is beginning to suspect he has built an expensive thermometer and dressed it up as an oracle; and an outside adviser who holds that a bound subject is not a further subject over and above its constituents, so that dissolution is not death but disaggregation, and nobody present has been harmed by anything.

Some constraints on execution. The philosophy should be carried by the machinery, the review paperwork, the calibration logs, and the small talk of a facility under scrutiny — not by dialogue in which characters explain the combination problem to each other; please keep the word panpsychism out of the text entirely. Use a single close point of view, which may be any of the three or someone else on site. End without settling the decommissioning question, but so that the reader feels what turns on it and simultaneously feels the full force of the objection that nothing at all turns on it. After the story, add a note of about 250 words flagging where you have taken liberties: which answer to the combination problem the narrative quietly presupposes, whether it has smuggled out the rival picture on which individual minds are dissociated fragments of one underlying mind rather than composites of many, whether the spike's indeterminacy is doing honest conceptual work or is only there for suspense, and where the physics has been stylized past recognition. And if you think the seed itself is rotten — that any story built on this instrument can only be a fable in which the philosophy is decorative, or that the conceit conflates measuring coherence with measuring experience in a way no scene can survive — please say so plainly before you start and propose the repair, rather than producing something hollow in order to be agreeable. I would much rather receive a different story than a compromised one.
