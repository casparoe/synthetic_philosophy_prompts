Until the spring I wrote backend software for a living; I now work on a small team at a consumer savings app, where my job is to draft the conceptual specifications for our commitment features — the ones that let a customer lock money away for a fixed term, impose a cooling-off period before a withdrawal, or give a nominated friend the power to veto a transfer. I have also enrolled part-time in an MA in the philosophy of mind and action, and I am trying to turn a confusion I keep running into at work into my dissertation. The confusion is this. Every conversation we have about a customer who fails to do what they said they would do is conducted as though there were one thing to explain, whereas the material I have been reading on weakness of will and on hyperbolic discounting suggests there are at least two quite different failures in play, and that an intervention which helps in one case is inert — or actively counterproductive — in the other. I have sketched a distinction to sort them out and I now want it tested against concrete cases, because my supervisor has told me, reasonably, that a taxonomy nobody can populate is not a taxonomy.

The sketch cross-cuts two questions. The first is synchronic: at the moment of acting, does the agent hold an all-things-considered judgement that ranks an alternative they take to be open to them above what they actually do — that is, do the Davidsonian conditions for a strictly incontinent action obtain? The second is reflective: if the temptation were removed and the agent were asked, without time pressure and with full attention to the relevant facts, whether the action was the one they would have wanted to take, would they say yes? That yields four cells: (i) contradiction and repudiation, the textbook case; (ii) contradiction and endorsement, where the agent acts against their own judgement in the moment and yet calmly stands by what they did; (iii) no contradiction and repudiation, where the judgement itself has been dragged along with the desire, so that the agent acts on a view they form at the time and later disowns together with the act; and (iv) no contradiction and endorsement, which sounds like ordinary successful agency but for which I want the least trivial occupant you can find — someone whose calm endorsement is genuine yet is sustained only by their never having been made to attend to what the course of action costs.

So: please write me one vignette per cell, of roughly a hundred and fifty words each, set in the ordinary texture of adult life rather than in the philosopher's usual diet of cakes, cigarettes and trolleys, and phrased so that I could put them in a design document for colleagues who have never read any of this literature. For each vignette, state explicitly what the agent judges at the moment of acting and what they would say under the reflective test, so that I can check the classification myself rather than take it on trust. Then tell me which cells are genuinely occupiable and which look to you unstable or empty — I suspect (ii) in particular may be describable only by equivocating on what counts as an all-things-considered judgement, and I would rather be told that plainly than be handed a case that quietly dodges the problem. I am also unsure whether the reflective test should be run in advance of the episode, after it, or in a calm present tense; say which indexing you have used, and if the choice matters, identify one vignette in which running it differently moves the case to another cell. Finally, tell me where precommitment lands: a customer who has locked a transfer and then, at the moment the lock bites, sincerely judges that undoing it would be best, and cannot. My suspicion is that this case does not so much occupy a cell as break the first axis, since the agent no longer believes an alternative is open; I should like that suspicion confirmed or corrected, and, if it is right, an account of what a third axis would have to track in order to situate the person who binds themselves in advance, the one who plans around reversals they foresee but does not bind themselves against, and the one who does not foresee them at all, within a single scheme. I am not asking for references — I will supply those myself.
