workshop thing next week — I've been asked to state my position on HCH / iterated amplification in a handful of bullets so people can attack it. here's what I've got:

1. HCH is an idealization of the human's own reasoning: it's what the human would conclude about a question if they could think about it indefinitely by breaking it into pieces small enough to handle one at a time.
2. amplification only buys you anything where checking an answer is cheaper or more reliable for the human than producing it. where those cost the same, the recursion is a no-op.
3. so the limit of iterated amplification converges on the human's own considered judgment, not on some other agent's judgment that the human merely ratifies.
4. some of the work isn't factorable at all — judging whether a question is well-posed, whether a summary is faithful, which considerations are even relevant — and there the human just answers at full strength, no recursion.
5. it's fine that the assistant's outputs weren't derived by the human, since the endorsement at each level is still the human's.
6. noise in the human's leaf judgments shouldn't compound across rounds, becuase the same human is re-consulted on the same kinds of question each round.

tell me which of these actually pull against each other and which only look like they do, ordered by severity rather than all lumped together, and for each real one what I'd have to give up and what that costs me. I don't have a favorite among them and I'd rather not just be told the whole package is quietly incoherent — I want to know where it breaks.
