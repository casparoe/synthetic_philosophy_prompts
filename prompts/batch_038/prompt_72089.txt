One crux should be the case I've been pushing in seminar — call it the Copycat World. Two spatially seperated regions, A and B, each containing 10^20 decays of the same kind of unstable particle. In A the outcomes form a fair coin sequence. In B they also form a fair-looking coin sequence — but they are exactly A's sequence shifted by one position. Nobody arranged this; the generating story is two genuinely independent chance processes plus an unimaginably improbable fluke. Trouble is, the competitor system "A runs on fair chances, B is A shifted by one" is short and extremely strong — it derives all of B from A — so it looks like it beats "two independent fair processes" and makes region B's laws deterministic and wildly non-local. My opponent says this shows the best system can't track what makes a law a law; I think there's a repair. So do the bookkeeping honestly: does the copycat system actually win on simplicity and strength as Lewis construes them, or does it lose?
