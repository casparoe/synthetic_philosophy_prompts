I have no training in philosophy or in machine learning, but I have been reading popular coverage of interpretability work for a couple of years now, and I keep finishing these pieces without knowing what I am supposed to have learned. A magazine article I read recently set out the skeptical position like this:

'A neural network is, at bottom, a sequence of matrix multiplications. Nothing inside it stands for anything the way a word stands for a thing or a line on a map stands for a road. What the interpretability people find is that certain directions in the model's internal activity reliably go along with certain kinds of input, and then, because they are looking for a mind, they call that co-occurrence a representation. The results are largely built in: the directions are located by feeding in examples the researchers have already sorted into categories, so it is hardly surprising that they respond to those examples. A representation, properly speaking, is something that can get it wrong, and a set of activations cannot be wrong about anything — they are just numbers. So whether a model "really" has representations turns out to be a question of vocabulary rather than fact: both camps are describing the same mathematics, and the argument decides nothing.'

How much of that should I accept? Please go through it claim by claim and tell me what someone doing this work would agree with, what they would regard as a fair worry badly overstated, and what is simply mistaken. I am most suspicious of the last sentence, since if it were true the whole debate would be pointless, but I cannot say why. I would also like to know what the strongest honest version of the skepticism looks like, if the article's version is not it, and what evidence could in principle settle the matter one way or the other. Please write in continuous prose, as one would write a letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — and unpack any technical term you need rather than assuming I know it. I am not after papers to read; I want the reasoning.
