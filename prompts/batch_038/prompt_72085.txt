I'm prepping a seminar on the health/disease debate and I want to check my own intuitions before I inflict them on students. My verdicts, roughly:

1. The 45-year-old with silent hypertension who feels perfectly fine: disease, obviously — even though the only thing "wrong" is that a number sits above a cutoff.
2. Left-handedness in a world built for right-handers, where it costs him real daily hassle: not a disease.
3. A 28-year-old who can't conceive because of endometriosis: disease. The same woman's infertility at 55: not a disease. I want that pair to come out differently and im not sure any account can do it without cheating.
4. A 24-year-old elite distance runner with a resting heart rate of 40: not a disease, it's basically health — but it is a textbook functional abnormality.
5. A woman who tests positive for a high-risk breast-cancer gene and has nothing wrong with her right now: not sick, but definitely a patient, and what she gets from medicine is medicine.
6. Crushing grief six weeks after her husband dies: not a disorder. An identical symptom profile in someone whose life contains no such loss: a disorder.
7. Someone born deaf, deeply embedded in Deaf culture, no interest in an implant: I keep wanting to say not-a-disease, and I know part of that is just me not wanting to insult him.
8. Pregnancy: not a disease, whatever else is true of it.

My tentative principle: a condition is a disease iff some part of the organism is failing at the job it was shaped by selection to do, and the failure is one this particular person would want fixed if they weighed it without having had to build their life around it. The "want" is deliberate — but note it's an idealized want, not a reported one.

So: does this save the verdicts, and which one or two does it force me to give up? What does it say about a hangover, and about a man with no sense of smell who sincerely insists he doesn't mind? And is it anything more than the familiar harmful-dysfunction account with a preference clause bolted on — because I suspect clause two is carrying all the weight in 1, 5, 6 and 7, and clause one is doing nothing at all.
