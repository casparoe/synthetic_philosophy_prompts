I'm giving a talk to an aesthetics reading group next month and I want to build it around a thought experiment instead of the usual survey: Schopenhauer, alive in 2025, sitting through a modern blockbuster in a Dolby cinema. He died in 1860, so recorded sound never existed for him, which means the whole apparatus of what music is now — a fixed artifact you can replay identically a million times, delivered through speakers that move air well below anything he ever heard in a hall — has no slot in his system at all. My rough understanding of the commitments I'd be extrapolating from, and please correct me if I've garbled any of it: the other arts copy the Platonic Ideas but music doesn't copy at that level, it's a direct copy of the will itself, which is why it gets into us without going through the intellect; the elements of music map onto grades of objectification, with the bass at the crude end and melody at the top as the voice of the striving individual; and he's contemptuous of music that tries to imitate things in the world, or that lets the words boss it around. Then there's the other Schopenhauer, the one in the noise piece where the cracking of whips in the street is his running example, who thinks most of the sound in the world is an assault on anyone trying to think. I want the talk to keep those two apart, because I suspect they give different answers and I don't want to smuggle his irritability in as though it were metaphysics.

So, run the experiment. First lay out which of his commitments actually generate predictions rather than just vibes, then work through a shortlist I've got: the film score itself, where music is welded to image and mixed so it's continuous with the effects track; a bass-heavy club or festival track where melody is basically nothing and the low end is the lead, which looks like his hierarchy turned upside down; the functional playlists — lo-fi beats to study to, focus mixes, gym stuff — engineered specifically to be ignored; auto-tune and grid quantization, which iron out exactly the irregularties a performer's will would leave in the sound; and music made from samples, so the artifact is a copy of a copy of a recording. For each one I want the prediction plus the chain of reasoning that gets there from the doctrine, not from what you imagine his temperament would be.

What I most want, though, are the places where his own system pulls in two directions, and the places where it simply has nothing to say. Two collisions I've already found: if music is the one art that reaches us pre-conceptually, a film score looks like the most Schopenhauerian thing ever built, the audience's willing moved directly while the images give it something to attach to — but if music must never serve representation, then scoring is precisely the degradation he objects to, music demoted to illustration. And the sub-bass: his hierarchy puts the low end at the crudest grade of objectification, yet his claim that music is felt as will in the body rather than grasped as representation suggests a 30Hz tone in a treated room is about as near to the will as a human being can get. Tell me which way you'd resolve each and what in the doctrine decides it, then give me two or three collisions I haven't spotted, and at least one prediction that isn't just he'd hate it or he'd love it. Finally mark clearly which modern phenomena his framework can't accomodate at all — reproduction, music as an engineered product with a use case, recommendation feeds shaping what anyone hears next — and say what he'd have to add or abandon to handle them. Please don't hand me a bibliography or a list of where to read further; I want the argument, and I'd much rather you tell me my summary of the doctrine is wrong somewhere than quietly work around it.
