ok so I'm a junior and my science fair project this year was basically a little experiment. I got 104 kids from my school to volunteer, flipped a coin for each one to sort them into two groups, and one group used a focus-timer app (the kind that locks your phone for 25 minutes at a time) while they studied for two weeks, and the other group just studied however they normally do. Everyone took the same practice test before and after and I scored everybody on how many points they improved.

Overall the app group improved by about 6 points and the control group by like 2.5, which felt like a genuine result and I was pretty happy about it. But then I remembered my AP Stats teacher demoing Simpson's paradox on us — the kidney stone one where the better surgery looks worse once you pool everyone together — and I got paranoid, so I started breaking my own data apart. I split by the stress survey everyone filled out at the end of week one, and in BOTH the low-stress half and the high-stress half the app group actually did slightly worse than the control. (Also the app group had way fewer people in the high-stress bucket, which I figured didn't matter.) Then I split by grade level, by wether they play a sport, by first period vs. last period, and by gender, and I got reversals in like five of the eight breakdowns. So I've concluded the pooled number is the one that's lying and the app probably hurts, and my projects headline finding is worthless.

My actual question is about the rule here. I figure the lesson of Simpson's paradox is that the finer you slice the data the closer you get to the truth, so you should always trust the most disaggregated numbers and treat the pooled ones as an artifact — is that the principle, or is there some test for which breakdowns count and which ones don't? And if there's no stopping point (why stop at 8 slices, I could split everyone by birthday month), does that mean no dataset can ever actually support a causal claim and I should just tell the judges my experiment is meaningless? Feel free to use my own numbers as the running example and please write the whole answer in American English.
