You are a theoretical evolutionary biologist who also works in the philosophy of biology: you are fluent in the Price equation and covariance decompositions of multilevel selection, and in inclusive-fitness and neighbour-modulated-fitness models including their class-structured and weak-selection generalizations. Please think carefully and work the algebra rather than gesturing at it; wherever I ask for a calculation I want terms written out and signs established, not a report of what the literature tends to say. You may reason entirely from first principles — I do not want a bibliography and no citations are needed.

**What I am after.** A thought experiment built to specifications, for a seminar on the units-of-selection debate in which students have been told that the multilevel-selection and inclusive-fitness accounts are formally equivalent: two notations for the same underlying process, differing only in bookkeeping and convenience. I want a case that obliges them to test that claim instead of reciting it. The target, therefore, is a scenario in which the two accounts come apart in something more than notation.

**Stage 1 — brainstorm.** Sketch three or four candidate designs, two or three sentences each, and for each say in one line whether it meets the specification below or fails it. Be explicit about the tempting designs you are ruling out: a public-goods game with altruists and cheaters; a stipulation of zero or near-zero relatedness; a green-beard allele; differential group extinction driven by a trait that is transparently costly to its bearer. For each, say why it is disqualified rather than merely asserting that it is.

**Stage 2 — develop the winner**, under these specifications.

- Groups reproduce. The life cycle must include group-level reproduction — fission, budding, or release of multicellular propagules — running over at least two generations, so that group fitness and group heritability are structural features and not rhetoric.
- The focal trait must be a property of the group's organization — its composition, spatial arrangement, size at fragmentation, or the timing of a collective event — and not a straightforward individual act whose benefits happen to land on neighbours.
- Relatedness must be derived from the demography you specify, never assumed, and never fixed by fiat.
- The two accounts must give visibly different verdicts, in one of exactly two senses, and you must say which sense you have delivered: (i) the covariance decomposition predicts the trait increases while a correctly computed Hamilton-style rule for the individual actor comes out negative; or (ii) the inclusive-fitness calculation has no well-defined quantity to evaluate, because some specific step in assigning an actor, a cost, a benefit, or a relatedness regression breaks down — and you can point to the step.
- Realism. A plausible organism with a real life cycle: a microbe, a slime mould, a social insect, a colonial tunicate, a fungus, or an experimentally evolved line of the multicellular-yeast sort (clusters that grow by retained cell division and fragment above a size threshold, as in lines selected for faster settling). No exotic physics, no invented organisms, no stipulated hypermutation, and nothing requiring an actor to hold information or intentions it could not have.
- The scenario itself: one paragraph.
- Testability. After the scenario, state the experiment or field observation that would separate the two accounts, and what result would count against each.

**Then the demonstration**, in headed sections:

**A. Demography and accounting.** Group formation, group size, group reproduction, dispersal and mixing between generations, the trait and how it is expressed, the currency of fitness, the number of generations modelled — and, explicitly, what is held fixed across the comparisons you are about to run.

**B. The multilevel calculation.** Write the decomposition out term by term, with between-group and within-group components separated. State which term carries the selection and its sign. Small integer arithmetic is welcome; show the arithmetic.

**C. The inclusive-fitness calculation.** Identify the actor or actors, the cost, the benefits to recipients, and the correct relatedness measure for this demography — a regression coefficient, not a pedigree guess. Evaluate the rule. If it cannot be evaluated, name the assumption that fails and the exact step at which it fails.

**D. The divergence.** State what the two accounts predict differently, or what each can and cannot represent. Then say honestly whether this is a substantive disagreement about the world or a disagreement about which entities are worth describing. Also say what the gene's-eye, replicator-and-vehicle framing makes of the case: if the vehicle here is the group, does that alter any prediction, or only the description?

**E. The load-bearing feature.** Name the single feature of the case that generates the divergence. Remove or neutralize it, and re-run both calculations far enough to show that they now agree — tell me what the collapse depends on. Finally, give the strongest available defence from the inclusive-fitness side (for instance, that a sufficiently general class-structured or weak-selection formulation absorbs your case; that what you have built is group-level reproduction and so no challenge at all to the individual organism as the locus of selection; or that your relatedness measure is the question-begging element), and adjudicate whether that defence succeeds.

**Ground rules.** Do not stipulate the verdicts — they must fall out of the calculations. Do not deliver a relabelled textbook case. Do not smuggle the conclusion into the definition of a quantity you then compute with. If, having done Stage 1, you conclude that the specification as written cannot be satisfied because the two frameworks are equivalent on any case of this shape, say so plainly, identify the single constraint that must be relaxed to obtain a real divergence, and build the case under that relaxation.

Aim for roughly 1,200 to 1,600 words: the scenario in one paragraph, sections A through E clearly headed. No preamble and no restatement of these instructions.
