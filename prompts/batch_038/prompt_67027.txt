ok, weird ask, but here goes. I write a column about science and ideas for a general-interest outlet — mostly AI, some medicine, a lot of philanthropy — and my editor has decided the year-end piece should be about what I actually think is morally required of a person with a decent salary, which feels like a fun way to loose my job. I've been drafting and its not surviving contact with itself. Can you audit the list for me?

Here's what I've got so far:

1. I don't think there's any such thing as supererogation. If an act is the best thing you could do and you can spare the cost, it's obligatory, full stop. "Above and beyond" is a polite phrase meaning "not my problem."
2. Praise comes in degrees, and it tracks sacrifice relative to capacity, not sacrifice relative to duty. Two people hand over the same dollar amount; the one it hurt more gets more credit.
3. I find Wolf's moral saints paper genuinely compelling. A life organized entirely around producing the most good is a worse life, and I don't want to live in a world where everybody has one.
4. Ought implies can, and I mean it as a real constraint, not a bumper sticker. If you can't do X, it's false that you ought to do X.
5. I've argued in print that we shouldn't spend much energy blaming people who give nothing, because blame is a tool and in this domain it backfires — it makes people defensive, they dig in, they give less. Not because the non-giver is somehow excused.
6. Two pieces I stand behind. One was about a guy who'd sold a small app and quietly cleared the medical debt of roughly 400 strangers; he told me it cost him nothing he'd have missed. The other was about a hospital custodian on a modest wage who gives away 20 percent and has driven the same car since around 2004. I wrote that she's the more admirable of the two, and I used the word hero for her and only her.
7. I don't think demandingness is a serious objection to (1). If you have money, you can spare some of it. That's arithmetic, not philosophy.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, worst first, and for each one tell me what has to go and what I lose by losing it. I'd also like the ones that only look like they're in tension, because I suspect a couple of them do and I don't want to be argued out of something I can keep. If the move is "just go scalar about the good," tell me exactly which numbered item that costs me — I've heard that one before and nobody ever says what they're giving up.
