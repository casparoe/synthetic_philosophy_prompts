ok so I'm working through the Nyāya account of inference and I've hit a wall on upadhi, and I can't tell whether the wall is in my head or in the concept.

The setup as I understand it (correct me if I've mangled it): an inference is licensed by vyapti, an invariable connection between the reason and the thing-to-be-proved, and you get at that connection by looking at cases where the two go together and cases where they come apart. Then upadhi gets brought in as a sort of defeater — somthing present in every case of the thing-to-be-proved but *not* present in every case of the reason. So the reason looked pervaded, but it was really just tagging along next to some other factor that does the actual work.

Rather than the stock smoke-and-fire example, here's one I made up so I can't just recite it. A town downstream notices that whenever the river runs cloudy above the mill in spring, there's a fish kill within a fortnight, so they infer cloudy water → fish kill. Then someone points out that the kills only ever happen in springs where the water is also unusually warm, and warm springs make up maybe half of all the cloudy-water springs.

Here's what I want worked through — the conceptual machinery, not the history or who said what:

1. What actually happened when they found the warm-water factor? Did they show that the pervasion claim was false, or did they show something else — like that the reason was badly chosen, and the pervasion could be rescued by conjoining warmth onto it? Which of those readings is the right one for upadhi, and does the difference matter for how the inference gets repaired?

2. How does this line up with the contemporary distinction between rebutting and undercutting defeaters? My instinct is that upadhi is undercutting: it doesn't hand you evidence that there's no connection, it attacks your licence to draw one. But then the warm-water observation does seem to carry some positive content about the world, so I'm not sure. Tell me where that analogy holds and where it starts to strain.

3. The part that really bothers me — is the upadhi machinery a genuine answer to the problem of induction, or just a relabelling of it? You're supposed to detect an upadhi by observing more cases, which is the same move that got you the original pervasion in the first place. And once you swap in warmth, what stops some further lurking condition from vitiating *that* one, and so on forever? Is there a principle stopping point anywhere in the framework, or does it bottom out in "keep looking for defeaters"? I can see that "keep looking" might be a perfectly legitimate place to land rather than a failure, but I can't articulate why it would be.

And if you think upadhi isn't a distinctive thing at all — just a special case of a reason that wanders outside the thing-to-be-proved, i.e. an unreliable mark — then say so. I half suspect that's right and half suspect there's a good reason it gets its own slot, and I'd like to know which.
