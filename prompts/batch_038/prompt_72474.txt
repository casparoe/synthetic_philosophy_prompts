I sit on the board of a smallish family foundation and next month we vote on whether to renew a five-year grant to a lab working on cereal crops that fix their own nitrogen. Its the kind of thing that could matter enormously, and our program officer put together a scenario list to help us think it through, and now I kind of wish she hadn't.

The list is open-ended. Scenario 1: nothing works, we're out our money. Scenario 2: a modest tool other labs end up using. Scenario 3: yields jump across a lot of poor regions, worth maybe a hundred times scenario 2. Scenario 4: the engineered microbe gets into soil systems somewhere and does damage bigger than 1 through 3 combined. Scenario 5: it works, and it keeps working, and it transforms agriculture worldwide, bigger than everything before it. And so on, alternating, each one roughly twice as big as the last and roughly half as likely, with no last one — you can always imagine a further scenario past wherever you stopped. The amount each one adds to the running total does shrink, but glacially: by scenario 20 it's still contributing about a twentieth of what scenario 1 contributed.

Here's my problem. If I stack them up most-likely-first, the total settles out positive and we renew. A colleague on the board, who does quant stuff for a living, stacked the exact same list biggest-payoff-first and got a negative number, and says we should kill the grant. We've gone through each other's arithmetic twice and neither of us can find a mistake, and we're using the same scenarios with the same probabilities — we're literally just adding them in a different order. A third board member says ignore everything past scenario 12 as too far-fetched, which produces a third answer, and when I asked her why 12 and not 11 she sort of shrugged.

So honestly: is one of these orderings the right one? Is there any fact of the matter about what this grant is worth on average, or is that just not available in a case like this? And what do I actually say on the 14th, when the board wants a recommendation and the exec director wants a single number in the memo? I dont want to flip a coin, but I also don't want to dress up an arbitrary choice about how to list things as if it were a finding.
