so I've been reading about panpsychism on my own for a couple years now — no philosophy background, I just can't stop thinking about it — and I finally wrote down what I actually believe so somebody can tell me whether it holds together. Here's my list:

1. Physics only tells you what the basic stuff *does* — how it pushes, how it reacts to other stuff — never what it is in itself. So there has to be an inside to it. Electrons and quarks have some tiny sliver of experience.
2. Those slivers don't add up. I don't think a pile of micro-experiences ever becomes one unified subject, and I *am* one unified subject, so I'm a different kind of thing that sits on top of them rather than coming out of them.
3. Only unified subjects matter morally. A cloud of tiny experiences that never gets to be a someone can't be wronged, no matter how many of them there are.
4. Slivers can't suffer. Suffering takes wanting something and not getting it, and wanting takes a point of view.
5. The one and only reason I believe 1 is that it looks like the best hope for explaining why there's something its like to be me. I'm not spiritual, I don't believe things on vibes, and I think you shouldn't hold a belief that has nothing going for it.
6. When a wetland gets filled in for a parking lot, I think something is lost that isn't just the animals that lived there, and I don't think that feeling is sentimental nonsense.

A friend keeps saying 1 makes me a weirdo who should feel guilty about stepping on grass and eating a salad, but that doesn't seem like a real problem to me given 3 and 4 — am I right to wave that one off? Go through everything and tell me which items genuinely pull against each other, worst first, and for each one what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me. Then say which single item I could drop that cleans up the most mess.
