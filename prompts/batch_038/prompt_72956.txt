Our module on later Wittgenstein has reached the private language argument, and I am having trouble holding on to it in a form I could defend in the essay due at the end of term. My own subject is psychology, and the part of it I am genuinely fluent in is measurement theory: constructs and their indicators, latent variables, test–retest and inter-rater reliability, construct validity, measurement invariance across occasions, and the awkward business of validating a new instrument when there is no criterion measure to validate it against. I would like you to restate the argument in that vocabulary as faithfully as you can — the diarist who resolves to associate a mark with a recurring sensation and records it whenever it returns; the concentrating of attention on the sensation as the only thing that could fix the association; the thought that whatever strikes him as correct on the occasion simply is correct; and, separately, the box whose contents drop out of the account altogether because no one can look into anyone else's.

Then I want a residue report, and I would rather it were honest than flattering to my framework. Which of those moves have a clean counterpart in measurement theory, which can be rendered only by distorting them, and which cannot be expressed in it at all? Two specific worries. First, my own gloss is that the diarist has an instrument with no criterion measure, so that at best he could establish reliability and never validity, and that Wittgenstein's point is stronger still: in his case even reliability is unavailable, because the same person supplies both the reading and the standard. Tell me whether that gloss captures the argument or quietly changes the subject. Second, is a latent variable with no indicators at all the measurement-theoretic version of the box, or a different idea that merely sounds similar? In the translated version, also say whether the argument comes out as an epistemic claim — there is a fact about which sensation returned, but he has no means of checking it — or a semantic one, that there is no such fact to check; and whether the second can be stated in my vocabulary at all.

Finally, tell me which way to read what is left over. Measurement theory assumes there is a trait there and that we have noisy access to it. If that assumption is doing the work, then either the framework cannot state the argument's conclusion, or the conclusion is being smuggled in as a premise. I want your view on which of those is the case, and on whether the residue suggests the argument is less clear than it appears or that my vocabulary is missing something. Please write in British English. I am not after a bibliography or a survey of the commentators — just the translation and the residue.
