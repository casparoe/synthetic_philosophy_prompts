I am circulating a draft paper — provisionally titled Karma Without Rebirth — at a workshop next month, and before it goes out I want to know whether my own position is internally tenable. I am not asking for background on karma or on moral luck; assume I know the standard positions and the standard objections. I want an audit of the following set.

Here is what I am committed to, stated as plainly as I can manage:

1. Karma is, in its classical form, a claim about moral causation rather than moral bookkeeping. Intentional action leaves a residue that matures into experience fitted to it, and it does so without a judge, a ledger, or an enforcer. I want to keep the automaticity; it is what makes the doctrine philosophically distinctive rather than a theodicy in disguise.

2. I am a naturalist. No rebirth, no post-mortem maturation, no cosmic moral order. Whatever ripens does so within one lifespan, in the ordinary causal medium of psychology and social interaction.

3. On my reading, what ripens is character and standing. Repeated acts fix dispositions, and dispositions fix both what an agent can now bring herself to do and how she is met by others. Karma is real in the sense that an agent inherits the consequences of her own choices in the shape of the self who has to live them.

4. I hold a control principle in something close to its strict form: an agent deserves appraisal only for what was within her control, and I discount constitutive luck — the luck of already being the sort of person one is — as far as discounting it is possible.

5. The element of the doctrine I most want to defend is not individual at all. Ripening is dispersed: the matured consequences of an agent's acts fall heavily on people who had no hand in producing them, above all her children and her dependents. I take this to be the sober, unsentimental core of karma rather than a distortion of it.

6. I am a realist about basic desert. People genuinely deserve blame and praise in a sense that is not reducible to the usefulness of blaming and praising them, and not an artifact of institutional rules.

7. I think the just-world tendency in ordinary cognition is a bias to be resisted, and that inferring desert from observed suffering is a paradigm moral error — the error of Job's comforters, so to speak.

8. A case verdict I want to preserve exactly as stated. Two men commit the same act of domestic violence. One was raised by a violent parent, one was not. The first deserves less blame, but not none; I do not want his history to dissolve his agency. And his own child, who is terrorized by him, deserves nothing of what she receives — yet what she receives is, in a sense I want to be able to state precisely rather than gesture at, karmically connected to her father's acts and not to hers.

Please tell me which of these actually pull against one another and which only look as though they do. I expect at least one pair that I would flag as a contradiction on sight turns out to be perfectly stable, and at least one that feels safe to me does not, so do not grade them all as equally bad and do not treat my own hunches as evidence. Rank the tensions by severity. For each, say precisely which commitment has to give, or which must be reformulated, and what the concession costs me elsewhere on the list — I would rather lose something at the periphery than discover afterwards that it was load-bearing. Where you have resources for a repair (a distinction I have run together, a reading of one commitment that would defuse the clash), say so, but assess honestly whether the repair is available to me given the rest of the set or only to a different philosopher with different starting points.

Then commit. Tell me the single revision that cleans up the most, and argue for it as the right call rather than laying out options and leaving me to pick. If your conclusion is that the package survives intact, say that flatly and show me the reasoning that licenses it. If one of my formulations is ambiguous and the verdict turns on the ambiguity, audit the reading that makes my position strongest, and tell me that is what you are doing. No references, no bibliography, no suggestions for further reading — just the argument.
