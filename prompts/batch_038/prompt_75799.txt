So, some background: I'm 41, I run the overnight shift at a distribution center outside Tulsa, and I don't have any schooling past high school. About six months ago I hit a podcast episode about David Lewis and the idea that every way the world could have been is a real concrete world out there, just as real as this one, and none of them touching any of the others. I read the wiki page maybe four times and I've been keeping notes on my phone, because I figured if I'm ever going to decide whether I actually believe this, I need to write down what my gut says case by case rather than what sounds smart. Here's what's in the notes.

- I'm confident there's a world where I took the welding apprenticeship at nineteen instead of this job, and I don't think that's just me daydreaming on the dock at 3am — something has to make it true. But when the answer comes back as "there's a guy in another universe who's basically you and he took the apprenticeship," I feel like the subject got changed on me mid-sentence.
- Weird part: I don't feel that way about my old truck. "My truck could have been painted red" sits completely fine with me and I have zero problem with the answer being some truck-shaped thing over in another world. Same exact move, totally different reaction from me, and I can't explain it.
- On who counts as me across worlds: a guy with my memories and my temperament but a different face and name, born somewhere else — not me. A guy who looks exactly like me, same voice, same name, but has none of my memories and is a different sort of person inside — also not me, though it feels gross to say it. So the inside does all the work. Except: the version of me identical in every single way who, at the last second, walked into the other interview — him I call me without blinking, and I can't tell you what makes him different from the memory-less lookalike.
- A world where dolphins have language and write poetry: real possibility, I'm happy to say. A world where water isn't H2O: I don't think that's a real possibility at all. A world where I'm a fried egg: definitely not, even though the theory seems to owe me one. And 2+2=5 somewhere: no, and I don't think that's because of anything sitting out there in some world. But I can picture all four about equally well, so what is doing the work in my head, and why should it be trusted?
- I do accept that when the people in another world call theirs actual they're right, same as me calling this one actual. And still I cannot loose the feeling that mine is the real one in a way theirs aren't. Either I haven't understood the view or I'm onto something, and I can't tell which.
- Take a world exactly like ours down to every atom, except that out in some empty stretch of space there's one person being hurt forever, nothing else different, nobody ever finds out. That world is worse than ours, obviously. But it also shouldn't change how I live or feel one bit. And if every world is equally real, that person's suffering is happening no matter what anyone does, ever. I think all three of those and I can't see how they fit.
- Nothing I do now can make a non-actual world actual. That seems so obvious it's barely a claim. So when I say I could have taken the apprenticeship, am I saying anything about me at all, or just reporting on a stranger?

What I want from you is the principle underneath all this, if there is one. Not a summary of the view — I can get that anywhere — but something stated tightly enough that I could apply it to a case I've never thought about and get a verdict out. Tell me which of my gut calls each part of it exists to save, and be straight about the ones it can't save and would have to overturn; I'd rather hand one over than keep a pile of verdicts that don't add up to anything. Where you think two of my items are in genuine tension rather than both being reasonable, say which one goes and why. And if some of what I wrote isn't a tension at all, just me mangling what the view claims, call that out instead of being polite about it. Where Lewis himself has an answer to one of these, give me his answer — I don't want to walk away thinking the position has no defense when it does. Then pick one case I didn't list, anything your principle makes a non-obvious call on, and tell me what comes out. No reading list, please, I've got a stack by the bed already and I'm not keeping up with it.
