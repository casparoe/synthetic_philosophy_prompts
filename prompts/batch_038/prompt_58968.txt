I'm trying to sort out the debate over whether lying and misleading differ morally, and the crispest thing I've found so far is a short capsule summary in an online philosophy magazine. It runs:

"To lie is to assert something you believe to be false; to mislead is to assert something you believe to be true and let your listener draw a false conclusion from it. That distinction settles the moral question, since nothing false ever passes the misleader's lips and the false belief is assembled by the listener himself. Philosophers who insist there is no moral difference between the two — on the grounds that in either case the speaker intends to plant a false belief — are not really disagreeing with this; they are making a point about the speaker's character rather than about the act he performed. The practical upshot is a clean rule: lying is impermissible across the board, while misleading is permissible whenever the truth would do real harm, and saying nothing at all is better still. Hence the habitual recourse of witnesses, physicians, and press secretaries to the literally true sentence."

Please audit the passage rather than argue against it. Working through it claim by claim, sort its components into what both camps would accept, what is contested but a respectable statement of one side, and what misdescribes the dialectic. Two pressure points deserve particular attention: whether the line about the belief being assembled by the listener can carry the moral weight placed on it, given that the speaker chose his words precisely to produce that inference; and whether treating silence as a further species of the same resource is a move either side is entitled to make. Then say whether the practical rule survives correction — what would have to be true for a definitional distinction to yield a categorical moral one — and whether the passage's gloss on the no-difference position is what its defenders actually claim. One thing nagging at me: I've read that certain lies, in which everyone involved knows the assertion is false, involve no intention to produce any belief at all, and I don't see how this framing accommodates them.
