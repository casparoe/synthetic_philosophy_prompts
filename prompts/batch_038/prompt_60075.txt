I am preparing a one-page handout for the first session of a graduate seminar on assertion, and I want it to be a decision tree rather than a survey. The students will have read three or four of the canonical papers by that point, so they will arrive already knowing the labels; the whole purpose of the exercise is that the tree should sort them by the case verdicts they are actually prepared to give, not by the name they would give their own view. Accordingly, no node may be phrased with a term of art from the literature — a question of the form "do you think assertion requires knowledge?" is exactly the kind of node I do not want. Every branch node should be either a concrete case on which the student must render a verdict, or a plain-language question about what such a verdict commits one to, and each should be answerable by someone who has no idea which leaf their answer leads to.

Aim for eight to ten branch nodes and at least eight leaves. Please present it as numbered nodes with the branch question stated at each, the answer options on the branches, and the leaves at the ends of the paths.

The cases I want used somewhere in the tree are these. I do not care at which node each appears, but each should earn its place by actually separating at least two positions:

- the lottery ticket: the speaker asserts, before the draw, that her ticket lost;
- the Gettier-style assertion: the claim is true and well supported, the speaker asserts it, and on the standard diagnosis she nevertheless does not know it;
- the blameless error: a careful physician tells a patient what her evidence overwhelmingly supports, and it turns out to be false;
- the selfless assertion: a creationist science teacher who presents the textbook account of evolution while withholding belief in it — what, if anything, has she asserted properly;
- the disclaimer: a sincere speaker says that p, while adding in the same breath that she does not know that p;
- the accumulated-premises case: the speaker asserts a conclusion that follows from many things she knows, though she is not in a position to know the conjunction;
- the hearer's evidence: the speaker is in an excellent epistemic position, but her audience knows that almost anyone in her position would be wrong;
- the collision case: an assertion that is epistemically unimpeachable but cruel or otherwise disastrous to make.

Four further requirements:

1. Each leaf gets a short descriptive name for the position it lands on — the truth-governed view, the knowledge view, the justified-belief view, the reasonable-to-believe view, the excuse-based defence, a view on which there is no single governing norm, and whatever else your tree generates. Do not attach philosophers' names to leaves. I do not want the handout turning into an allegiance chart, and I do not want students recognising their own leaf from a name instead of from a path.
2. Identify the logically available combinations of answers that your tree routes to no leaf — the cells nobody occupies — and say of each whether the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.
3. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot in practice separate: where two views agree on every case you have posed and would diverge only on cases whose verdicts are themselves disputed. In those cases, say what the disputed case would have to look like.
4. Say where in the tree the distinction between a necessary-only norm and a necessary-and-sufficient norm does its work, and whether your ordering of the nodes begs any question against a view you think is wrong.

One last thing, because first-year students will treat this handout as authoritative: please separate clearly what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this. Wherever the instrument encodes a commitment that is itself up for grabs — that assertion is governed by a norm at all, that the norm is unique, that propriety is not context-sensitive, that case verdicts are the right data — mark it as an assumption of the tree rather than a finding of it. Do not let any contested position appear as the consensus, and do not let the leaf you personally favour look like the default destination of a neutral path.
