I never studied philosophy or mathematics; I write software for a living and read on my own in the evenings. Two things have collided in my head, and I would like help pulling them apart carefully.

The first is a vocabulary I picked up from popular writing about Escher's Drawing Hands and pictures like it: strange loops, tangled hierarchies, and the thought that a system of levels holds together only if one level stays inviolate, so that the hand doing the drawing is not itself drawn. The second is what happened to Frege's logicist project.

Here are the facts as I understand them. Please correct any of them rather than working politely around my errors.

- Frege's Basic Laws of Arithmetic appeared in two volumes, the first in 1893; the second came out in 1903 carrying an afterword written after Russell wrote to him in June 1902 about a difficulty.
- The difficulty traced to the one genuinely new axiom, Law V: two value-ranges are identical exactly when the corresponding functions agree on every argument.
- Value-ranges, and so extensions of concepts, are objects in his system, which means a concept's extension is itself something concepts apply to.
- Frege's language cannot express the concept/object distinction, which is the limitation behind his remark that the concept horse is not a concept; yet a contradiction is derivable in the system anyway, and I do not really understand how those two facts fit together.
- In the afterword he tried restricting Law V to functions whose value-ranges could be recognized as legitimate, then withdrew the restriction as not doing the work he needed. He never rebuilt the system.
- The two repairs that became standard both arrived in 1908: Russell's theory of types, motivated by the principle that whatever involves all of a collection must not itself be a member of that collection, and Zermelo's axioms, which leave the language alone and cut comprehension down to separation.

What I want is an analysis of that episode through the tangled-hierarchy lens, running in both directions, and honest wherever the lens does no work.

1. Map the levels. In Frege's system as of volume one, what are the levels, which one is meant to be inviolate, and at which step does Law V tangle them? I want to know whether the tangling lives in the axiom or in the background assumption that extensions are objects, since those give different diagnoses and different morals.

2. Then tell me whether the lens is applicable at all or whether I am imposing it after the fact. Drawing Hands is a picture that delights us; Frege's tangle is a contradiction that ruined a book. If the difference matters for what the lens explains, say so plainly.

3. Test a hunch of mine, and argue against it if it is wrong. My suspicion is that the type-theoretic repair does not restore an inviolate level so much as relocate one outside the building, because the rules that assign types and separate legitimate totalities from illegitimate ones cannot themselves be typed without repeating the problem one level up. On that reading the inviolate level has not been abolished, only moved somewhere it no longer has to answer to the object language. Is that a fair description of what Russell and Zermelo did, or am I confusing the formal hierarchy with the meta-language?

4. What does the lens explain about the two repairs diverging the way they did, one changing the language and one changing the axioms, and about Frege's own abandoned attempt, which looks to me like a third road neither of them took?

5. Where is the lens silent? It seems to say nothing about whether logicism might have been rescued by other means, and nothing about why some later set theories admit a universal set with no known inconsistency, which an inviolate level ought to rule out. Does the episode therefore suggest that the constraint is a heuristic, asserted after a collapse and waived after a success?

Write this as a continuous analysis with a clear structure, not as a survey of the paradox, which I already follow, and not with a reading list or citations; I have no library access and would rather have your reasoning than your references. Flag anywhere my summary is off, and flag anywhere you are guessing. Close with two or three sentences on one last thing: I build systems in which one component validates another and something at the top is never validated by anything, and I want to know whether this episode licenses transferring the lesson to that setting, or whether the analogy is only decorative.
