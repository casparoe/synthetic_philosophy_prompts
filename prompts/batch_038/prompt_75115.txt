im finishing a long piece for a general-audience magazine on the argument over whether the wavefunction is a piece of the world's furniture or just a ledger of what we happen to know, and I want to close it with a poem instead of a summary graf. Form: a pantoum, four quatrains, recycling done properly — lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as 1 and 3 of the next — and the final stanza pulling the opening stanza's 1st and 3rd lines back into its 2nd and 4th slots, so the poem ends with its two readings of ψ trading places and answering each other instead of alternating.

The recycling should carry the argument, not decorate it: the same underlying state of the world turning up under two different wavefunctions is exactly what the epistemic reading needs, and by the last stanzas preparation independence — the assumption that two seperately prepared systems have seperately distributed hidden states — closes that loophole and leaves a straight choice. Get the substance right (ψ-ontic vs ψ-epistemic, overlap of the distributions over ontic states, Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph, and what you'd have to give up to keep the epistemic view) but no jargon dump and no footnotes-in-verse: a foundations person should nod, and a reader who's never heard of an ontological model should still get a poem with actual images in it, weather or furniture or whatever you can make work. Then tell me, in two sentences of prose, which lines you'd have to break if my editor insisted on an ending that lets the epistemic side off the hook.
