You are an expert in the metaethics of practical reasons, with particular command of the internal/external reasons debate — the question of whether an agent's reasons must be reachable from something in her actual motivational psychology, or whether they can obtain irrespective of it. Think carefully rather than reaching for the first label that fits: the material below is constructed so that several familiar positions predict the same verdicts across most of the record and part company only at the end. Before you commit to anything, brainstorm the full space of candidate rules that could generate the track record — actual-desire versions, idealized-desire versions, deliberative-route versions, objectivist versions, and any hybrid you think deserves taking seriously — and only then narrow it down.

The setting. I am the TA for a graduate seminar on practical reasons. Across the term students have put cases to the instructor, always framed as whether A has a reason to φ, and she has answered each one. Her answers, in order:

1. Petra wants a gin and tonic; she is handed a glass that looks and smells exactly like one but in fact contains petrol. No reason to drink it. She added that it would make no difference if Petra would in fact drink it.
2. Dev has a settled desire to spend his life restoring a derelict narrowboat. He knows precisely what the project costs him in other goods, nobody else is harmed by it, and sustained reflection has not weakened it. Reason. Asked whether the reason lies in his wanting it or in its being worth wanting, she said the question does not arise here, and told the student to bring her a case in which it does.
3. Tom has a standing desire to be there for his friends; a friend's car breaks down at three in the morning. Reason. She declined to say what the reason consists in, only that there is one.
4. Nadia has no desire whatever to learn the accordion, is fully informed about what learning it would involve, and reports that thinking the matter over carefully has changed nothing. No reason.
5. Ines has promised to return a borrowed car. She has no desire to keep the promise as such, but she is terrified of being found out, and the terror rests on no false belief. Reason. Asked whose reason it was — the promise's or the fear's — she said the reason is real and that its provenance is a further question.
6. A man finds counting the blades of grass on his lawn genuinely absorbing and pleasant; he wants to spend his evenings at it, on the basis of vivid and accurate information. Reason. She registered the student's distaste without addressing it.
7. Marta wants a particular medicine, believing it will relieve her pain; it would in fact aggravate it, and no ordinary inquiry on her part would reveal this. No reason. She was careful not to say that Marta's belief was irrational or that the desire would fail to survive reflection; she said the verdict did not depend on any claim of that kind.

Friday's case was submitted by a student who has not shown it to her yet. I have to write the session up in advance, and more practically I want to know which follow-up will actually bite.

Rosa is a hospice nurse. Her subjective motivational set contains a powerful ambition to become director of the unit and — this is the stipulation the student insists on — nothing else relevant: no desire, disposition, or general principle that a patient's distress as such could engage. She has sat with dying patients for eleven years and is entirely clear-eyed about what that distress is like; she has reflected on it at length and without self-deception, and the reflection has neither produced nor strengthened any motivation to comfort them. She is misinformed about nothing. One patient, Mr. Adeyemi, is frightened and asks her to stay past the end of her shift. The student further stipulates that if Rosa deliberated carefully about whether to stay she would decide to stay, because she has learned that visible devotion is noticed by the people who appoint directors and she intends her staying to be noticed. Then, on reflection, the student added a rider: if the promotion question were removed entirely — if Rosa knew for certain that nobody would ever learn what she had done — she would go home on time, feel nothing about it, and reflection on that fact would not move her either.

I want the following, in this order:

- Which rules the seven prior verdicts actually eliminate and which they leave live; which single verdict is doing the most work; and whether her remarks, as distinct from her verdicts, pull in opposing directions. If they do, tell me whether that means the record is inconsistent or merely underdetermined.
- Your best guess at her answer on Friday, with a confidence level, and the specific feature of the record you are leaning on most.
- Whether she will separate having a reason from its being true that Rosa ought to stay, and whether she will say Rosa is criticizable for not being moved. Say what each surviving rule predicts about that separation, since I expect it to be the hinge of the session.
- One further case, to be submitted the following week, that would discriminate between your top two candidates, with the verdict each predicts for it — and what I should conclude if she gives the same answer to both.
- For any candidate rule that needs an auxiliary assumption to accommodate the record, flag the assumption and say whether it looks ad hoc.

Where a candidate rule corresponds to a position you recognize from the literature, mark it with a clause or two; I need no references and no bibliography, just enough to know that a rule you propose has actually been held rather than assembled for the occasion. Do not restate the cases back to me. If you conclude that the record leaves exactly two rules genuinely tied, say so plainly instead of manufacturing a resolution.
