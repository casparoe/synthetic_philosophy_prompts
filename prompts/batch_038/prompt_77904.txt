Write a parody monastic rule — the Rule of the Order of Perpetual Availability — for a community whose god is the Work. Take the form and deadpan register of an actual rule of life: a brief prologue, then thirteen or fourteen short numbered chapters covering the hours of the office (standup, sync, retro, the quarterly chapter), the three vows, the novitiate, the chapter of faults, the sin of sloth, and the discipline applied to brothers who falter. The humor should come from getting the social theory right rather than from stock jokes about hustle culture: the order does not flee the world but methodically rationalizes conduct within it; what drives the brothers is anxiety about the state of their own calling rather than fear of the abbot; and rest is never forbidden, only reclassified as recovery in the service of work. Include one chapter on a schismatic house observing a shorter rule of thirty-five hours and accused of laxity, and one on the brother who collapsed in the vineyard, framed exactly as a real institution would frame such a case. The piece must never break character, never wink, and never concede a criticism it cannot absorb back into the discipline; paraphrase rather than quote any scripture or social theory, and name no living person. Roughly 700 words.

Then a section titled "Exaggeration check" (about 200 words): for the four or five real positions, practices, and styles of discourse the main chapters distort, state what is exaggerated from something true and what is invented outright.
