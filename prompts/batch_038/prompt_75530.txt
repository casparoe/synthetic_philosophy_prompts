The setup (rename things however you like, but please keep the mechanics intact):

A water-allocation clerk in a drought-prone farming district. She doesn't optimize and never has. Her procedure, inherited from her predecessor and never questioned, is: read incoming requests in the order they arrive, and grant the first one that clears her aspiration level — a single number, a yield-per-litre figure she carries around in her head. No weighing options against each other, no scoring, no going back to reconsider the ones she already passed over. First-that-clears, then stop.

The number is not written down anywhere. And here's the crux: it moves. After a season where requests clear it easily and early, she quietly raises it. After a season where nothing clears it, she lowers it. She doesn't decide to do this, it just happens, the way a person's idea of a decent meal shifts with what they've actually been eating.

Act one: the district is poor, the queue is long and mostly bad, and her crude procedure looks like genius. She decides within a day, never agonises, water goes somewhere reasonable. Everyone in the province says she's the most efficient clerk they have.

Act two: promotion to a rich district. Deep aquifers, corporate growers, lawyers, offers that all comfortably clear the old number. Now the same procedure comes apart. Because the number ratchets up after every easy approval, and once it sits above everything on offer, her stopping rule can never fire — she keeps reading, keeps waiting for a request that clears it, the queue backs up into something like a famine she's causing by being unable to say yes to anything. She hasn't gotten lazy or corrupt. She's become, by accident, a maximizer with no maximum. Meanwhile the clerk in the neighbouring district, an actual optimizer who models everything and takes a fortnite per decision, is getting flak for being slow but keeps functioning, because his cutoff doesn't depend on how the last few weeks went.
