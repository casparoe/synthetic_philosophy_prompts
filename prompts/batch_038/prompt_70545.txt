I am a teaching assistant for a first-year course on legislation and statutory interpretation, and my final section meets the week after we have worked through the textualism/purposivism debate. Rather than assign another real case, I would like to hand out a piece of satire that my students can argue over, because the standard classroom example (a vehicle in a park) has been done to death and tends to produce the same two-minute exchange every year. The piece I have in mind is a parody of an academic peer-review file, in which an enacted statute has been submitted to a fictional journal as though it were a manuscript.

Please write that file for the following invented statute, the Fermented Beverage Labelling and Excise Act of 1994, which my students will receive as a handout alongside your piece: section 5(a) provides that no person shall import, ship, or otherwise place into interstate commerce any fermented malt beverage, wine, distilled spirit, or other potable preparation of similar character unless a label stating alcoholic content to the nearest tenth of one percent by volume is affixed to the container; section 12 recites that consumers are frequently misled as to the strength of what they drink and that the purpose of the Act is to end that deception; and section 15 exempts any preparation whose alcoholic content does not exceed 0.5 percent by volume — with the wrinkle that the Senate added the phrase "at any point during its production" to section 15 in conference, that the conference report is silent on the addition, that a floor statement by the House manager called it "housekeeping," and that the Senate committee report said it was meant to reach preparations that develop strength during transit or storage. The occasion for the review is a petition concerning a kombucha bottled at 0.3 percent that reached 1.4 percent on day nine of a twenty-one day fermentation.

Structure the response as follows: a single line at the very top giving the handling editor's decision, then Referee 1's report, then Referee 2's report, then a short closing note from the editor — the reports should each read like a genuine referee report, with a brief summary of the submission, numbered comments, and a recommendation, and the referees should remain anonymous to one another while being conspicuously identified by their interpretive commitments. The comedy has to come from the doctrine being handled correctly, so please have the two referees collide over at least the following: whether kombucha falls within "other potable preparation of similar character" under ejusdem generis and noscitur a sociis; whether the trailing modifier "of similar character" is governed by the series-qualification canon or the rule of the last antecedent, and what either reading does to the canon against surplusage; whether the peak-alcohol trigger in section 15 produces a duty to label a container whose required label content is nonetheless computed at bottling; whether bread dough, vinegar, kefir, and sourdough starter are reductios or merely consequences; and whether one referee may consult the committee report and the floor statement at all, or whether doing so is inadmissible and self-serving. Both referees must make their strongest available case rather than a straw version of the other's, neither may concede, and the editor's note should add the fact that the Act passed by voice vote in about eight minutes and let the two referees' methods treat that fact in incompatible ways. Do not name or quote any real judge, justice, opinion, treatise, or dictionary — every authority cited must be invented or supplied above — and never let a character state the obvious moral that both methods look result-driven; the reader should feel it anyway, through the symmetry of the reports. Keep the whole thing deadpan and under 900 words.
