My roommate's a history major and 'history is written by the victors' is his universal conversation-ender. The other night I asked whether we can actually learn anything from the exchange the Melians have with the Athenian envoys before the siege, given that the whole thing reaches us through an Athenian aristocrat who'd been thrown out of Athens, wasn't there for the negotiations, and whose own city went on to loose the war anyway. His answer: victors write the history, so whatever survives is propaganda by default, question closed. I'm pretty sure that's a mess but I couldn't say how on the spot. Can you pull the slogan apart into the distinct precise claims it might be making — about who composes the record, which texts physically survive, what later audiences come to believe, and how much a reader is licensed to distrust any given source — and grade each one as platitude, genuinely contentious, or flatly false, using cases where the losing side demonstrably did the writing? Then tell me which of those readings, if any, would actually support his move on Melos, and which one you think he was really reaching for.
