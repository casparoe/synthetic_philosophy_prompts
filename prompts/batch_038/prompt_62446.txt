I'm a third-year philosophy PhD student working on mental content. Half my week is spent in a computational lab, which is where the case below came from, and I've got a seminar paper due that I'm hoping to turn into a dissertation chapter. I want an essay I can argue with.

The controversy: does teleosemantics commit you to saying that a reinforcement-learning agent has genuine, underived content? And if it does, is that a vindication of the view or a reductio of it?

Here's the case. Call it Pelletbot. Small feedforward network with a recurrent layer, trained by gradient descent on a simple foraging environment for something like a billion episodes. It ends up with an internal unit that fires reliably when a pellet is on screen and in reach, and downstream policy states that use that unit to navigate. After training the weights are frozen and the thing is deployed. No biology, no lineage, no ancestors, nothing wet. Three features of the case are what I care about:

(1) There was obviously a variation-and-differential-retention process during training. So the etiological story about where proper functions come from looks like it has all its ingredients on the table.

(2) But nothing got reproduced in the sense the historical account wants. There's no lineage of tokenings being copied forward; there's one set of weights being continuously nudged. Whether a trait that survives only because modifying it would have lowered reward counts as a character that's been reproduced across a lineage — that feels like the whole ballgame to me.

(3) The selection pressure was supplied by us, toward our ends, through a reward function. So even if Pelletbot's states have functions, it's tempting to say they're our functions, and any content that comes with them is derived. Same shape of worry as the homunculus objection and the Chinese Room.
