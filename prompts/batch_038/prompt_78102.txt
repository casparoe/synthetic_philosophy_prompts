You are a professional epistemologist specializing in the epistemology of disagreement. Work through the following carefully: before you commit to any scoring, sketch three or four candidate diagnoses of the case and say which you find most promising.

The case: A and B are by stipulation exact epistemic peers — identical total evidence, identical reliability, and both fully aware of this. They disagree about a substantive philosophical question, and they also disagree about disagreement itself: A has always been a conciliationist, B has always been a defender of steadfastness. Note the recursive structure. Conciliationism applied to their meta-disagreement tells each of them to shift toward the other's meta-view, but shifting toward the other's meta-view is precisely what alters the prescription that generated the shift in the first place. Each view is being asked to adjudicate its own applicability, from inside a disagreement about it.

Rate the positions below on a single dimension I will call self-application stability: how determinate, non-question-begging, and non-collapsing a verdict the position yields when applied to the meta-disagreement between A and B. Assign each a score from 0 to 10, where 10 means a unique, stable verdict that does not presuppose which side is right about peerhood, and 0 means the view either generates an infinite regress of higher-order disagreements, or lands on a fixed point in which no rational pressure to move survives, or quietly assumes its own correctness:

- Elga's Equal Weight View
- Christensen's independence-based conciliationism
- Kelly's Total Evidence View
- van Inwagen-style steadfastness
- Lackey's justificationist view
- Schoenfield's permissivism
- a pluralist view on which conciliationism and steadfastness are each locally correct, given the normative framework the agent already occupies

Give a one sentence of justification for each score. Then state which single position you would bet on handling the case best and what consideration would change your mind. In that final assessment, be explicit about which parts of the diagnosis are settled among people who work on this and which are genuinely contested; do not present a contested reading of the case as though it were the consensus.
