I keep running into the same move in arguments about whether brain size — or neuron counts, or brain-to-body ratio — says anything about an animal's moral status, and I can't find a name for it.

Say someone argues that bigger brains mean more moral weight. You point out that a grown cow has a bigger brain than a human baby, and they say fine, what actually matters is neuron density and how the tissue is organised — the mass figure was just the visible stand-in for that. Then you run into someone arguing for density, and you point out that birds pack neurons very densely into tiny brains and still do well at tasks everyone assumed needed a big brain, and suddenly they're back to insisting absolute numbers matter, because small dense brains clearly can't support the relevant capacities.

So the measure gets treated as the thing itself when it delivers the ranking the person already wanted, and as a rough indicator of some other property when it doesn't, and neither switch is ever flagged as a concession. Is there an established term for this? I know it sits next to special pleading and next to the general worry about leaning on proxies, but neither one captures the back-and-forth — the same quantity being constitutive on Monday and merely evidential on Tuesday. And if there's no name, how would you define one? I want the edges, not just a label: which nearby failures look like this but aren't, and what would make a case a genuine example rather than just someone changing their mind in good faith.
