I'm a second-year MPP student and our seminar this term is on climate policy and intergenerational ethics — brutal reading load, meets Thursdays, half the class is econ people and half are policy-nerds like me who've never opened a discount-rate spreadsheet in their lives. Last week we got into an argument about whether a resource-rich country can make itself sustainable by pumping oil aggressively and then parking the rents in a sovereign wealth fund plus green R&D. One guy, very quantitative, said yes obviously, and that this is exactly what the old line 'live off the interest, not the capital' is telling you to do: convert the exhaustible stuff into other capital so the total doesn't fall, and then consume the yield. Someone on the other side said the very same line refutes him, because the remaining carbon budget and the climate system ARE the capital, so every barrel extracted is eating into it, and a pile of money is not a substitute — her phrase was that you can't eat a bond portfolio, and he shot back that money's only worth anything if there's a functioning biosphere to spend it in. They went round and round, and our professor finally said the two of them were using one sentence to mean two completely different things and neither had bothered to say which. That's the bit I want to get straight, because I keep reaching for that slogan myself and I honestly can't tell whether I'm saying anything when I do.
