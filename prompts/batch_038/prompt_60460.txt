I'm a novelist, third book, and I've been putting off finishing it because I got pulled down a rabbit hole about Escher's two hands drawing each other and the idea that the whole thing is only a paradox if you forget there's a level you can always step out to — Escher's own hand on the paper. Which is basically the structure of my novel, so now I can't tell whether my central device is a discovery or a con. Before I go back to the manuscript I want someone to audit what I actually believe. Here's the list, written down honestly, including the bits that are probably embarrassing:

1. Narrative levels are real and strictly ordered. If a character in my book is writing a story that contains a character, there's a determinate fact about which one is above the other, and nothing at the lower level can change anything at the higher one. A drawn hand can't take the pencil off you.

2. The scene my whole book turns on is one where the character writes the page the reader is holding, and describes himself writing it. I insist this is a genuine loop, a real feature of the structure of the text and not a trick played on the reader's head. I get genuinely irritated when people call it a lovely effect.

3. It's autofiction. The narrator has my name, my address, my dead father in him. And I also hold that the actual author is never inside the work, that I can't be reached from within the book, and that no sentence in it is literally about me-as-the-one-writing-it. I hold both of those at once and I've never felt the strain.

4. When I say my characters surprise me — I planned one thing, a character turned and did another, and I followed — I mean that seriously. Not as a writers' mystique, not as a metaphor for not knowing my own mind. Something reaches up.

5. My only test for whether a passage works is whether it lands: does the reader get the intended chill on a first read, with no notes. I don't believe any structural analysis can tell you a device succeeded.

6. Most metafiction I dislike — the wink, the "dear reader", the novel about the writing of a novel — fails because it never lets the levels actually touch, it just narrates their touching at a safe distance. I take that to be a failure of craft in those books, not evidence that the thing can't be done.

So: go through this and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do. I don't want a list where everything is equally bad — rank them by severity, and for each real one tell me the cheapest repair and what the repair costs me, since I suspect a couple of these are load-bearing. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most damage. No citations, no reading list, I'm not going to look any of it up and I don't need you to tell me what other people have said about strange loops — I want to know whether *my* set is coherent, and if it isn't, which of my claims I've been using as a get-out-of-jail card.
