My argument: everyone always says Athens fell apart because the assembly was an emotional mob that got talked into things by demagogues. I think that's lazy. What I actually want to claim is that the assembly had no commitment devices — it couldn't make a decision stick, and it also had no clean way to get out of one. So Mytilene (first vote to kill every adult male, then the second trireme sent overnight to undo it, landing at the last possible second) and Sicily (Nicias' letter home, Alcibiades getting recalled in the middle of the campaign, the whole army stuck in the harbor with no exit) look like opposite kinds of failure but come from the same missing piece of machinery. Then I use the trial of Socrates as a control case: there the decision did stick, and the one person who could have opened an exit, Socrates himself, refused to take it.
