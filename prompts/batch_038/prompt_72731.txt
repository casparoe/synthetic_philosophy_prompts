So my wife and I are at the point in our IVF cycle where we have three embryo's left and we have to pick one to transfer next week. Only one of them has the BRCA1 variant that runs in my wife's family — her mom died of ovarian cancer at 48 — so the clinic steers us away from that one and honestly I don't have a problem with it. Where I'm stuck is the other two.

The clinic also sells an optional add-on report that scores each embryo on a bunch of common traits: educational attainment, schizophrenia risk, type 2 diabetes, height. One of the two comes out better on basically all of it. Not dramatically — I know these scores explain like two or three percent of the variance, and the sales rep kept talking about giving our kid "the best possible start," which I found kind of gross — but better.

Then my friend Tarek told me we're doing exactly what eugenicists did, just with better marketing. His argument is that we've already decided one kind of person is more worth bringing into existence than another kind, and that we have no idea where the line is: if we'll take the higher cognitive score, why not take the one that's more likely to be a girl, since we'd probably prefer that? And I can't answer him, because the part that bothers me most is that I can't say who's actually being hurt. The embryo we don't transfer isn't a kid we're killing, and whichever one we pick is going to have a good life. My sister has bipolar disorder and she'd tell you her life is fine and I believe her, so the schizophrenia score makes me feel like I'm passing judgement on her off a speadsheet.

Two things I want your take on. Is Tarek right that this is just the old eugenics with nobody forcing anybody, or does the fact that it's voluntary and it's our own kid make it a genuinely different kind of thing — and if it's different, why is it different? And if there's a real line between "don't transfer the one with the BRCA1 variant" and "transfer the one with the better score," what would it have to be based on? I want something I could actually say out loud to Tarek, not just "I don't like where that leads." I'm not after a bibliography or a list of books, I just want to work out what to do next week and whether I should admit the choice is basically arbitrary and stop pretending otherwise.
