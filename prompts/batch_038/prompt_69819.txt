Our lab runs small superrationality experiments — one-shot anonymous games with grad students as subjects, in the spirit of Hofstadter's Platonia dilemma and that Luring Lottery thing he did. Last term's session had two legs. Leg one: a 24-player one-shot prisoner's dilemma, everybody paid better if all 24 cooperated, no communication. Leg two: a Platonia-style game where the entire pot went to exactly one person, but only if exactly one of the 24 volunteered — zero volunteers or two-plus volunteers and the pot was donated away.

Our supervisor is a big superrationality fan and spent the whole debrief explaining the results with one phrase, "what if everybody did it?" He used it for two different jobs. First, to explain why cooperating was the right play in leg one (most subjects defected, for what it's worth). Second, to explain why the right play in leg two was to volunteer with probablity 1/24 — roll a 24-sided die, volunteer only on a 1. A second-year pushed back and said that's dominance reasoning with a magical-thinking chaser, and that if you literally ask what happens when everybody does it in leg two you get 24 volunteers and nobody gets paid. I think they're each half right but I can't say which half.

So can you lay out the different precise claims that slogan could be standing for in this context? I want a menu, not a primer on superrationality. For each reading, make the relata explicit — what is the "it" that gets universalized (an act, an intention, a reasoning procedure, a probability distribution?), who counts as "everybody" (the actual 24, all rational agents, just the ones who reason like you do?), and whether the claim is a counterfactual test, an evidential correlation, a causal mechanism, or a moral principle. Then say whether each reading is trivially true, substantive but defensible, in need of extra premises, or just false, and whether it does any actual work in either leg. And tell me which reading, if any, really buys him the 1/24 mixed strategy — that's the one I find most suspicous, because universalizing the act seems to give "everybody volunteers," which is the worst outcome in the game, so something other than act-universalization has to be going on and I want to know what it is and whether its legitimate.

Format please:
