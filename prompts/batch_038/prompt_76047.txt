Two years ago my wife got transferred to Seoul by her company and I tagged along, doing bookkeeping remotely for a couple of landscaping companies back in Ohio, which is about as exciting as it sounds. Before we moved I picked up one of those pop-psychology books at the airport about "tight" and "loose" cultures — the idea being that societies that went through a lot of threat, like famine, disasters, crowding, invasion, grew strong norms and punish rule-breakers hard, while societies that didn't have those pressures stayed permissive and got more innovation out of it. It made the culture shock way easier to make sense of, and I've been reading about it on and off since then, mostly in forum threads.

Here's the part I can't get straight and I'd love for someone to just tell me plainly if I've got it wrong. My reading is that the tightness score is basically telling you the right amount of rule-following for a given society, because the score is produced by that society's threats in the first place. So a country that scores high isn't high because it's repressive, it's high because high is what works where it is — which means when people here grumble that Korean society is suffocating, they're just measuring it with a loose country's yardstick, and the number settles the arguement. Same logic should run in reverse too: if a loose place starts coming apart, couldn't its government deliberately turn the threat up — harsher penalties, more alarming messaging — to tighten itself back up, since threat is what causes tightness? Basically I've been treating the scale as a policy dial that tells you where each society should be set, and I want to know if that's actually what it is.

Two things nag at me though. If the number describes a whole country, why is it so useless for predicting the actual people I know? My closest friend here breaks every rule that exists, and my mom in Columbus has a meltdown if someone parks an inch over a line — so is the measure just averaging away everyone interesting and then reporting the average back to me as if it described them? And I assumed tight basically meant authoritarian, until someone on the forum pointed out that Japan is tight and a democracy at the same time, which did not fit my model. So what is tightness-looseness actually measuring, what can you legitimately do with a country's score once you have it, and where exactly did I go wrong?
