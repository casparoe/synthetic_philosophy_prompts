For a graduate epistemology seminar of nine students meeting once for 90 minutes, design an interrogation game on the regress problem in which participants discover the three structural options by playing rather than by being introduced to them. The core setup: one student defends an ordinary empirical claim, an interrogator who is permitted only to ask what supports the reason just given presses them, and the remaining students score. Build in three constraints — no reason already used anywhere in the chain may be reused, no reason may draw its support from a reason already in the chain, and the defender may halt at any moment but must then declare what kind of halt it is (an unshakable ground, a loop they are prepared to license, or a chain they concede is unfinished) — so that each declaration commits a player to a view about how justification can be structured, and to a price for it. Provide the full ruleset and role assignments, a scoring scheme that needs no money and that rewards candid declarations rather than long chains, a timeline with at least three rounds so that allegiances can shift, and a debrief that maps particular moments of play onto the claims of foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism, including the infinitist's insistence that an infinite chain need only be available to the subject rather than actually traversed. Then tell me what will predictably break in the first round and how to repair it mid-session, and be explicit about the feature of the regress this activity cannot reproduce: every run of the game terminates, so a finite play will look like evidence that the chain itself must terminate — say what I have to supply myself so that artifact is not mistaken for an argument.
