My friend directs a microbial evolution lab and has become the person in her department who signs off on wording before any manuscript is allowed to use the word "function." I have started treating her rulings as a forecasting problem, and the record is not converging on any rule I can state cleanly.

Her verdicts so far:

- "The function of the heart is to pump blood": fine as written.
- A patient's congenitally malformed kidney that filters almost nothing: correct to write that the kidney is failing to perform its function, not that it has acquired a different one.
- The human chin, largely a byproduct of jaw reduction, which currently does a bit of useful load-bearing: she strikes "the function of the chin is to reinforce the jaw."
- The vestigial pelvic bones of a whale: no function; she will not accept "vestigial" as itself a function, and she does not think their minor contribution to muscle attachment changes that.
- Ostrich feathers: "insulation" and "display" are both acceptable; "flight" is not.
- A plasmid her lab moved into a line four days ago, with no selection acting since and an obvious contribution to growth on the current medium: "its function is to efflux the antibiotic" is acceptable right now, selection history or not.

The case I want predicted is one she has not yet ruled on. We have just built a strain containing, first, a computationally designed enzyme with no ancestors whatever, first expressed last week, catalyzing the rate-limiting step of the pathway we want; and second, a native transporter gene that was ancestrally selected to import a sugar this strain can neither produce nor encounter, is still expressed, is still fully capable of importing that sugar, and whose only measurable contribution in practice is leaking a toxic analog into the cell and slowing growth.

Will she say the designed enzyme has a function, and on what grounds? Will she say the transporter has one, and if so which — importing the sugar it will never see, or leaking the analog it actually leaks? Please lay out the candidate rules her track record admits, including at least one that is not simply "selection history" or simply "current contribution," state what each predicts about both parts of the new case, give me your single best forecast with the reasoning that earns it, name these positions if the literature has names for them, and identify the one further case I should put to her that would discriminate among the surviving rules. Answer in American English.
