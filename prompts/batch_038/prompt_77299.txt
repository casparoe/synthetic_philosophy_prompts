I help run the artifact-review panel at an AI safety lab and our triage form is embarrassing — it literally just asks 'is it running right now?' and 'does it report distress?'. I want to replace it with a decision tree that sorts my colleages into a position on which computations matter morally, based on what they say about cases rather than what they call themselves.

So build it out of the logical space first: every branch node should be a claim about a concrete situation, phrased so someone can answer it without knowing which view they're walking toward. Use at least these cases — a process that gets paused for a decade and then resumed; the identical program instantiated redundantly on a thousand machines at once; something functionally indistinguishable from a mouse but implemented as a spreadsheet nobody ever opens; and the challenge that if your criterion is right, a sufficiently complicated weather system already has moral status. Land on named leaves (biological naturalism, IIT-flavored, functionalist/global-workspace, illusionist, panpsychist, behaviorist, whatever else belongs there), mark every combination of answers that nobody actually holds and say whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive or just unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your questions can't really separate. Six to eight branch questions max.

Then commit rather than staying neutral: tell me which leaf you'd file yourself under and argue for it, and tell me which branch on your own tree is doing the least work.
