In the consciousness reading group I attend, one senior member has a reliable way of closing down any dispute: he says that there is no "Cartesian theater" in the brain, that what we have instead is a succession of partial drafts and revisions of content rather than a single finished narrative, and that whatever question is on the table presupposes the theater and so is not really a question. Last week his target was one I raised about temporal order judgments. In two different tasks, subjects report opposite orders for the same pair of briefly presented stimuli, and I asked whether that shows there is no fact of the matter about which of the two experiences came first. He replied that my question is not merely unanswered but meaningless, since asking it already assumes a stage at which things arrive in a determinate order.

Please lay out, as a menu, the distinct claims that slogan could be standing in for — for example, that no single locus in the brain is where all processing converges; that there is no homuncular spectator and hence no regress of spectators; that there is no determinate time at which, or order in which, contents enter consciousness; that there is no unified first-personal subject to whom anything could be presented; that the positive multiple-drafts picture is true rather than merely that the theatrical picture is false; and any further readings I have not thought of. For each, say whether it is a platitude that essentially everyone in the field accepts, a substantive thesis with real argument behind it, or false or unsupported. Then say exactly which of them the dismissal of my question requires, whether that one follows from any of the readings that are mere platitudes, and where the inferential gap lies between an architectural claim about the brain and a claim about whether facts of the matter exist. Finish by reporting how confident you are in the map as a whole and singling out the one reading you are least sure about.
