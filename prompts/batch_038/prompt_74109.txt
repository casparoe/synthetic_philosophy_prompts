I'm assembling cases for a paper on money pumps and diachronic rationality, and I need one built to order rather than adapted from the standard examples.

**The target case.** Design a sequential exchange in which a three-member committee is money-pumped in the strict sense — it ends the sequence holding precisely the asset it started with, minus a net outlay in fees — while each individual member's preferences over the three assets are complete, transitive, and unchanging throughout, and no member is ignorant of anything relevant, including the full schedule of offers to come. The committee acts by simple majority, motion by motion, and its standing rules require each member to vote sincerely on the pairwise question actually before the house; no member may move to consider the sequence as a unit, and the committee has no precommitment device.

**Constraints.** Name the assets as something a real organization would hold (equipment, licences, contracts) rather than as A, B, and C; each exchange carries a small commission paid to a broker; the scenario proper must fit in one paragraph.

**The demonstration.** Supply the three members' orderings and the majority ordering they generate; the sequence of motions with the vote on each and the running balance; verification that the terminal outcome is Pareto-dominated by the status quo the committee could have held onto; and the contrasting path that results if members are permitted to vote strategically at each motion in light of the later ones, identifying the first motion at which the two paths diverge.

**The diagnosis.** Tell me which feature is doing the work — the aggregation rule, the sincere-voting requirement, or the absence of any procedure for taking the sequence as a whole — and whether a member who foresees the pump and votes sincerely is thereby irrational, or whether the defect lies entirely in the committee's rules. Then state what the case is meant to settle: whether the money-pump argument vindicates transitivity as a requirement on any agent's preferences, or only on agents capable of unified forward-looking choice. Close with the strongest objection a defender of the argument could press against the case.
