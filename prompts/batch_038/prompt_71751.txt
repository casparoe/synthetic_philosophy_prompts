I run a food ethics seminar and last week's section went off the rails. Could you referee it for me? You're a moral philosopher who has thought hard about aggregating welfare across species and about the ethics of agricultural systems — think carefully, and before you hand down verdicts, sketch in a few lines what you take the real cruxes to be.

Two of my students, N and T, got into it about whether switching from chicken to beef is a welfare improvement.

(1) N said yes, and her argument was arithmetic: one steer gives you a couple hundred kilos of meat, one broiler about a kilo and a half, so per kilo of protein you are killing something like 150 animals instead of one, and the cows at least spend most of their lives outside.

(2) T said she was counting the wrong thing — what matters is not the number of deaths but the amount of bad experience, and six weeks of crowding and leg problems is not the same bad as a steer's final months in a feedlot after a year on pasture. He added that beef needs several times more crop feed per kilo of meat than chicken does, and growing crops kills field mice, voles and an enormous number of insects, so once the animals in the fields are on the books, beef harvests more wild animals per calorie than chicken does.

(3) N said that is the standard carnivore's comeback and it backfires on him: if crop deaths count, then the feed conversion ratio is exactly what decides the case, and beef loses it. T said that's a concession, since she's now picking whichever metric gives the answer she already wanted. She said no, it's a weighted sum, and the dispute is about the weights.

(4) N then said the ledger itself is bogus: to add broiler pain to mouse deaths to insect mortality you need a common currency — intensity, duration, probability of sentience — that nobody has, so his total-suffering-per-calorie figure is a pseudo-quantity.

(5) T said that grenade goes off in her hands too, because a death is only bad insofar as it cuts short a life worth continuing, and comparing a cut-short broiler life to a cut-short cow life needs the very same cross-species currency; so she can't retreat to head-counting. N said head-counting is merely ordinal — she never claimed to know how bad each death is, only that fewer is better, other things equal. T said other things are very much not equal here, and that the ceteras paribus is now carrying the whole argument.

My problem is that each of them seems to win a round and lose the next. Go through it move by move and say who is right at (1) through (5) and why — not just who wins overall. Then tell me whether either of them is left with a view that survives the whole exchange, and what both of them are leaving out. Related question I couldn't answer when it came up: does it matter at all that these farmed animals would not have existed otherwise, or is that a red herring in this particular argument? Please don't split the difference to be polite; if one of them is simply confused, say so and say where. No references needed, I just want the reasoning.
