I'm trying to work out where I stand on buying and selling things that maybe shouldn't be for sale, and my intuitions are a mess. Here are my verdicts:

- Paying my nephew $20 a book to get through a summer reading list: fine, even though I admit it might replace liking reading with liking $20.
- Paying a willing guy $50 to let me punch him in the arm, no marks, he boxes: not fine.
- Paying a healthy volunteer $4,000 to take on liver risk in a phase 1 drug trial: fine.
- Paying a healthy volunteer $40,000 for one kidney: not fine. Same body, same sort of seller, one is a risk and one is a part.
- Paying a professional mourner to weep at my father's funeral, with everyone told he's paid: not fine, and I can't justify this one, since nobody is hurt and nobody is fooled.
- A company offering a poor town $30M to host a waste site, town votes yes after seeing all the data: fine.
- The same offer, except the company goes town by town until one finally says yes: not fine. The only difference is that more towns said no first.
- Paying a homeless man to hold my place in a queue overnight for concert tickets: fine.
- Bots buying up the whole ticket stock and reselling at 10x: not fine.
- Paying my neighbor to withdraw her kid from a school lottery so mine gets the place: not fine, even though its her kid's place and not mine.
- Buying carbon offsets for a flight, so someone else cuts more than I add: fine.

My guess is the line is something like "you can't sell what you only hold in trust for others," but that doesn't touch the mourner or the punch, and I don't want to give those up.

So: give me the principle or principles that best fit all this. I want a table, one row per candidate principle, with these columns: **principle** (stated precisely, one or two sentences), **key clause** (the bit of machinery doing the work), **cases saved**, **cases it has to overturn** (name them from my list), **cost** (what's ad hoc or question-begging about it). Then after the table: two new cases you think most threaten the winning prinicple, with your predicted verdicts on them, and one short paragraph on whether I should drop the mourner verdict or accept a messier principle.
