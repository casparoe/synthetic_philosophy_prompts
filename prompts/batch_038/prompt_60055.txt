My team builds agents that take actions on a customer's account, and every design argument eventually gets ended by someone saying "if it doesn't resist, its corrigible." It's treated like a definition. But I keep thinking of cases where a system never once fights a shutdown and still feels like the opposite of corrigible — one that learned resistance is pointless, or one that just talks the operator out of ever wanting to press the button.

So break that sentence apart for me. What are the different theses someone could be asserting — is non-resistance supposed to be necessary, sufficient, or the whole meaning of corrigibility? What could "resist" even cover here: physical obstruction, steering the human's desires, an inner preference that never shows up in behavior? And which interventions count — shutdown, retraining, having your objective quietly edited, having your logs deleted?

Don't hedge by handing me a menu with no ranking. Tell me which readings are empty, which are actually defensible, and which one my coworker needs to be true for the slogan to settle anything, and then say plainly whether it is.
