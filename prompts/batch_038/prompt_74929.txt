I cover science and ideas for a general-interest outlet, and I want to write something more disciplined than the usual the-physicists-got-fooled item about the 2011 OPERA neutrino result. The facts I take as settled: in September 2011 the OPERA collaboration announced that muon neutrinos sent from CERN to the Gran Sasso laboratory, roughly 730 km away, had arrived about 60 nanoseconds early — faster than light by on the order of one part in 40,000 — at six-sigma statistical significance, after half a year of internal checking; they posted the analysis openly and invited the wider community to break it; in February 2012 they identified a badly seated fiber-optic link in the GPS timing chain and an oscillator on a clock board running outside specification; the experiment's leadership resigned that spring; and by mid-2012 a re-run with a different beam structure and independent measurements by neighbouring detectors at the same site found nothing anomalous. An earlier low-significance hint from a comparable long-baseline experiment dissolved in the same way.

Analyze the episode through Kuhn's Structure of Scientific Revolutions. What does the anomaly-and-crisis machinery predict a community should do with a result that threatens a load-bearing commitment, how much of this sequence actually fits that prediction, and where does the framework simply fall silent — on systematics and error budgets, on blind measurement, on the norms governing when a several-hundred-person collaboration may publish a result it half-expects to be wrong? Then press on incommensurability. In Kuhn's later lexical account the trouble is terms that cannot be mapped across competing taxonomies, and nothing of that kind seems to occur here; is that the right diagnosis, or is there a genuine incommensurability between experimental and theoretical physics in what counts as evidence that a claim has been tested? And does the fact that a one-way speed requires a convention for synchronizing distant clocks make this a philosophy-of-measurement case rather than a revolution that failed to launch?

Finish by telling me what the episode does to the theory: is it a confirmation of Kuhn's account of how anomalies get absorbed, an anomaly for it, or a kind of case he never really contemplated — and if the last, what a Kuhnian would have to add to handle it.
