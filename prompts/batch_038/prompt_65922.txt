Lakatos's test for a progressive research programme is that adjusting the protective belt gets you novel facts, and he gives at least two glosses on "novel" — improbable in the light of previous knowledge, or just not previously known to the scientific world. Run this case through it. Glaciology programme, hard core: creep in polycrystalline ice is grain-boundary sliding all the way down, no dislocation mechanism needed. In 1961 the founder adds an auxiliary hypothesis predicting a sharp jump in the creep exponent below some critical grain size. A rival group had already seen exactly that jump in 1959, but it sat in their lab notebooks until they published it in 1963. By 1970 the notebooks are public, and the programme's defenders are waving the 1963 paper around as their crowning confirmed prediction.

My claim is that on Lakatos's own criteria this programme counts as progressive when judged in 1963 and degenerating when judged in 1970, with nothing about the science having changed in between — only what was sitting in an archive. Argue for that, and then for the stronger half of it: that this isn't an unlucky edge case you can patch, it's just the novelty criterion doing what it always does, which is tying scientific progress to the history of paperwork. If you think a Lakatosian should bite the bullet, or can swap in a better criterion that doesn't have the same problem, say which and why it's better. No reading lists or citations, I want the argument itself, dont pad it out with names.
