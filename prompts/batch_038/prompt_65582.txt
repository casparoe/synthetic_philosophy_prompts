i run a small podcast about neuroscience and moral responsibility, and next week's guest is a computational neuroscientist whose whole research program is the claim that Libet's readiness potential is an artifact. His line: once you back-average from movement onset across trials where people waited wildly different amounts of time, you manufacture a smooth ramp of unconscious preparation out of what is really just autocorrelated neural noise drifting toward a threshold. He's extremely good at turning every question back into another win for the accumulator model, so i want questions that make the model do work beyond fitting the data it was built to fit.

Give me seven or eight questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. I want at least one each on:

- whether swapping unconscious preparation for unconscious noise actually buys anyone anything, since a drift-to-bound process is no more *my* doing than a preparation would be;
- the veto cases — how late in the buildup can a cancellation still succeed, and what the threshold story predicts about trials that get called off;
- whether his account has any prediction that could come out false, something other than "the shape of the averaged ramp should change when the distribution of waiting times changes";
- whether the whole thing quietly collapses into compatibilism once you notice that the drift rate and the threshold get set by the subject's own goals, boredom and impatience.

Plus one question pressing him on who exactly his target is, given that Libet himself never thought the result ruled out free will and left room for a conscious veto. No softballs about how he got into the field.
