I run a tiny research unit — me plus two postdocs — funded for another three years by a heritage foundation that mostly pays for hands-on conservation work. They've asked us for a memo on whether the endurantism/perdurantism debate should change how conservators document objects, and honestly the harder question for me is what to spend the remaining time on. Here's the mess we're sitting in:

- Our cataloguing schema currently treats each object as one thing that gains and loses parts over time. A rival proposal on the table would instead store an ordered series of dated slices, each with its own material inventory, and treat the object as the whole career those slices add up to. Switching would eat roughly a year of our three and ripple into every partner museum's database, so it's not a decision to make lightly.
- A fresco case where maybe 40% of the original plaster survives, but it's scattered across two churches and a storage crate, and nobody can say which collection of fragments is the fresco. Straight-up problem-of-the-many vibes, except a court is going to have to rule on ownership of the crate.
- Conservation leans hard on reversability — interventions should be undoable. Someone in our group claims that principle only makes sense if you think of the object as one enduring thing, and that it goes incoherent on a stage view where each stage is a distinct object. Is there anything to that, or is it a fake problem we should stop talking about?
- A wooden boat festival where the community rebuilds a boat every decade or so out of the previous one's timbers. Which boat is the original now drives insurance valuations and a heritage listing, and the two candidate answers (the continuous career vs. the surviving timber set) point at different boats in different harbours.

Which of these — or something I've missed entirely — should get the headcount? I want a defended ordering, not a survey. Tell me what criteria you're using (does the conceptual work actually bite on what conservators do Monday morning, is it tractable for two people whose backgrounds are mereology/formal logic and ethnography of craft respectively, how much else downstream depends on settling it), which of them are overrated or dissolve once you look closely, and what evidence over the next year or two would move something up or down the list. Please no reading lists or citations — the postdocs can find those themselves, and I'm intrested in your reasoning, not your bibliography.
