i'm not a philosopher or an english major, i just watch too many films and i've started bothering myself with a question i can't put down.

last month i rewatched a movie i know completely — i can tell you the minute the kid dies, i've seen it four times — and i cried again, properly, and then i sat there in the dark feeling sort of satisfied. better than before i started. Its not just that film either, i keep choosing this. nobody makes me. if a real person died tonight i would not want that, so what is this thing i go looking for on purpose?

write me a pantoum about it. 5 stanzas, so 20 lines. i looked the form up because the repetition seems like exactly the point: the 2nd and 4th lines of each stanza come back word for word as the 1st and 3rd lines of the next stanza, and the last stanza closes the loop by taking the first stanza's 1st and 3rd lines as its own 2nd and 4th. don't reword any of the repeats — if a line changes by one word the whole thing falls apart for me, i want the reader to feel the same sentence arriving again on them.

what i want the form to actually do:

- at least one of those returning lines has to mean something different the second time it shows up, purely because of what's sitting around it now. same words, different job. that's basically my whole question, isn't it — the sad bit hasn't changed at all and i've changed my mind about wanting it.
- keep the safety in there as an object, not as an argument. the remote on the arm of the couch, the fact i could pause it, the fact that nobody in this room is dying. don't explain why that matters, just let it be sitting there on the cushion.
- the pleasure and the pain should be about different things. one line is about the kid, another is about the fact that i'm the sort of thing that can feel it at all. don't melt them into one warm glow, that's the cheating answer and i've read plenty of them.
- don't use the words catharsis, cathartic, empathy, healing or journey. plain domestic language, 11pm, a living room, whatever is actually on the coffee table. no sermonising at me.
- leave the question open. the loop at the end should read like "and i'd do it again," not like an answer someone reached.

i don't want to be reassured about this either. if the finished poem is a bit uncomfortable for me to read, that's the version i'm after.
