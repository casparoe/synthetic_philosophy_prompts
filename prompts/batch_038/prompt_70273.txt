Im running a seminar session on sortition and I want to use the Irish Citizens' Assembly as the test case, though the standard write-ups of it make me uneasy. Facts, so we're on the same page: 99 citizens recruited at random and balanced demographically — recruited, note, so the ones who turned up were the ones willing to give up a run of weekends — met through 2016–17, heard from lawyers, clinicians, advocacy groups on both sides and women telling their own stories, and recommended replacing the Eighth Amendment; the Oireachtas then called a referendum in 2018 and roughly two-thirds voted yes.

My working hypothesis, which I want you to test rather than flatter: the Assembly gets cited as evidence for the cognitive-diversity claim, the one Landemore builds out of the Hong–Page result about diverse problem-solvers beating a homogenous group of better ones. But that result needs a better answer to be tracking and needs each member to have some independent handle on it. On abortion there isn't an answer of that kind, or anyway nobody on any side behaves as if there is — so the theorem's premises fail and whatever authority the Assembly had has to come from legitimacy, not from getting it right. Add that it was only advisory, that the Oireachtas picked the topic and the framing, and that the real decision got made by an electorate of over three million in a campain run by politicians and celebrities, and I wonder whether 2016–18 is even the right sort of case for the sortition literature to be claiming as a win.

So map it. Who actually decides at each stage; what does the diversity argument require in order to fire; which of those requirements does the Assembly meet? Then say where the theory goes quiet — self-selection, agenda control, whether testimony counts as argument or as something else — and land on whether this episode confirms the programme, is an anomaly it can absorb, or is a case the programme was never really about. Bottom line in two sentences first, then the analysis, 700 words max please.
