I am putting together a set of case-design problems on higher-order knowledge and need one case built to exact specifications.

Call a failure of the KK principle **robust** when all three of the following hold at once: (i) the subject knows that p; (ii) the subject is luminous with respect to her own beliefs — whenever she believes something, she is in a position to know that she believes it; and (iii) she is nevertheless *not in a position to know* that she knows that p. I want a single scenario that generates a robust KK-failure.

What the case is for, since this fixes what counts as cheating: I want to know whether the familiar counterexamples to KK — ones that rest on the subject's having no reason to trust the faculty that produced her belief, or on her simply never raising the higher-order question — can be strengthened into a failure that survives the two standard repairs, namely reading KK positionally and granting the subject perfect introspective access to her own beliefs. A case that announces the higher-order belief unsafe rather than making it unsafe, or a case in which the subject's first-order knowledge is just as questionable as her second-order ignorance, does not count.

Constraints on the scenario:

1. No vagueness, no borderline cases, no slowly varying magnitudes. The failure may not be a by-product of a margin-for-error predicament: no cooling drinks, no dials or thermometers read at a finer grain than the subject can discriminate, no sorites series of any kind.
2. The failure may not turn on the subject's never having considered the higher-order question. She has considered it; the obstacle must remain even after she has.
3. No exotic machinery: no clairvoyance or ESP, no implants, no drugs, no demons, no future technology, no stipulated neurological defect. The setting must be one an ordinary adult could actually find herself in.
4. The **parity constraint**: whatever it is that blocks second-order knowledge must not, applied consistently, block the first-order knowledge as well. If your obstacle is that she cannot rule out route R as the source of her belief rather than route S, you must explain why the very same inability does not defeat her knowledge that p.
