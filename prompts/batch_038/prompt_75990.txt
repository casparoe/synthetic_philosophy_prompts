I sit on my health system's clinical AI oversight committee. We deploy a vendor's model that predicts inpatient deterioration and routes nursing attention accordingly. An external audit team, using a sparse-autoencoder-style decomposition of the model's internal activations, reports that they have isolated a direction corresponding to patient race, and that steering along it shifts risk scores on otherwise identical case vignettes. The vendor's reply makes two claims: first, that such directions are artifacts of the decomposition method chosen, not anything the model "represents" — the model has statistical structure, not attitudes about race; second, that a rival audit using a different method on the same weights found no race direction at all but did find one for insurance type, and that both audits pass their own causal-intervention checks. Half the committee wants the tool suspended until the disagreement is resolved; the other half thinks that demand is a category error, since what matters is whether outputs are biased, and our outcome audits already measure that.

Draft the decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action on continued deployment, with a timeframe, and state the evidential standard interpretability findings must meet before this committee treats them as decision-grade. Say explicitly whether we may proceed as though the model represents race, and what that phrase has to amount to for the answer to be more than a figure of speech. Engage the vendor's deflationary objection at its strongest rather than waving it aside. Close by identifying what would change your mind: which further test or argument would flip your recommendation, and at which step your reasoning is most likely to fail.
