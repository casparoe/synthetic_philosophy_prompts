I work on the software side of a life-detection payload, and the thing blocking us is not really an engineering problem. Our onboard classifier has to emit one boolean — alive, or not alive — per sample, and it can only use what the instrument suite actually measures: bulk molecular composition and chirality, energy throughput and how it varies with temperature, micron-scale morphology, the response of all of those to a chemical or thermal perturbation, and, given enough soak time, whether any component population inside the sample shows heritable variation under selection. That is the whole observational API. Before we start arguing about thresholds, I want the specification written down: what would a definition of life have to deliver to be adequate for a purpose like ours, and what does it have to deliver to be adequate at all?

Give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration behind it. Put the hard cases in view: viruses and viroids, prions, ribosomes and mitochondria, spores in cryptobiosis, sterile individuals such as mules, obligate endosymbionts that have lost most of their genome, cultured cell lines that outlive their donors, and a self-replicating machine that does everything a soil bacterium does but was built rather than evolved. Also tell me which of the things people demand here are genuine requirements and which are the prejudices of one camp — a colleague argues that insisting a definition settle the virus question just smuggles in an intuition about a word, and I am not certain he is wrong.

My hypothesis, which I would like tested rather than accepted: bivalence (a yes/no verdict per object), individual-level assignment (a verdict about a thing at a time rather than about a lineage or a population), and operationalizability against that observational API cannot all be held together, because the most promising definitions turn out to be historical. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, state it explicitly — that result is worth more to us than any candidate definition.

Format: a single table with columns id | requirement | motivating case(s) | status (non-negotiable / camp-specific / negotiable) | which family of definition it strains or rules out. After the table, three or four sentences on the joint-satisfiability question, then a brief note on how the main families — evolutionary, self-maintenance, thermodynamic/dissipative, and cluster or functionalist definitions — score against the list, and which single requirement eliminates the most of them. No reading list or references; I want the reasoning, not the bibliography.
