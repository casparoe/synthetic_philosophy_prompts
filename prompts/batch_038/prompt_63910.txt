I came to philosophy through the rationalist blogs and forums, where the house style is to treat any interesting thesis as a hypothesis and then try to break it with better data and better models. That habit has left me with a standing irritation about Quine's field linguist, and I want a rigorous case made against the dismissal I keep running into in comment threads.

The dismissal runs roughly as follows. On this view, the gavagai thought experiment is an artifact of its era: it stipulates a linguist armed with nothing but pointing, assent, and dissent, and it is unsurprising that so thin an evidential base leaves reference unsettled. Grant the same project modern machinery — an effectively complete corpus of the language, paired video of the speakers' environment, and a statistical model good enough to predict the natives' utterances better than they predict each other's — and the rival readings of the term (rabbits; undetached rabbit parts; the temporal stages of those rabbits) supposedly fall out as distinguishable hypotheses, one of which the data will favor. Indeterminacy then collapses into ordinary underdetermination, and ordinary underdetermination is just a temporary condition that better evidence cures.

Please argue for the contrary position, and argue it rather than survey it. The position I want defended has two parts. First, no finite or even limit-case amount of behavioral evidence — however richly mediated by instruments and models — closes the space of empirically adequate manuals, because every such manual is answerable only to dispositions to use, and dispositions to use underdetermine reference at every level of evidential saturation. Second, and more importantly, the residual slack is not a measure of the translator's ignorance. It marks the absence of a fact of the matter, which is a claim of a different kind from "we haven't gathered enough data yet," and the machine-learning version of the setup makes the difference visible rather than dissolving it.

Build the argument around a hinge I'd like you to state explicitly and lean on deliberately. Take the idealized translator and suppose it delivers two manuals that agree on every disposition the natives actually exhibit and would exhibit under any future elicitation, yet assign divergent references. The divergence can then only surface in the rendering of sentences on which native practice is silent — novel compounds, counterfactual predications, quantifications into contexts the speakers never enter. Explain why that structural feature is not a defect of the setup but the whole point, and why a manual's superior performance on the corpus is compatible with its being no more faithful in reference than its rival.

Then answer, at minimum, these four objections, each of which I consider stronger than the dismissal above:

1. That reference is fixed by the causal-historical relation between the speakers and the animals they encounter, so the individuation work that assent-and-dissentent data cannot do is done by the world's own joint-cutting, and the corpus-complete translator simply inherits that fix.

2. That a prior over candidate reference schemes — simplicity, naturalness, or some principled measure of how well a candidate carving fits the rest of the manual's ontology — determinately selects one reading, so any rational agent converges and the disagreement is about priors, not about facts.

3. That Quine's own view leaves reference determinate relative to a home background language, which makes the thesis far less radical than I am treating it and unable to bear the weight of the argument you will have constructed.

4. That the dispute is verbal: if no agent's beliefs, predictions, or actions could ever differ between the two manuals, then saying there is no fact of the matter adds nothing to saying the manuals are notational variants, and the metaphysical flourish should be dropped.

Some constraints on execution. Use Quine only as far as is needed to fix the target — I am interested in the argument, not in exegesis, and I would rather you state his claims in your own words than reconstruct his chapter structure. Steelman each objection before answering it; objection 2 in particular deserves a real answer, since a Bayesian ought to find it compelling. Do not ask me for sources and do not supply a bibliography. And do not end on a balanced summary of considerations on both sides: finish with a defended verdict, stating which of the two parts of the position survives intact, which survives only with a qualification you specify, and what the qualified version costs.
