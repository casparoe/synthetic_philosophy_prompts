i run a small donor coalition — fifteen-ish groups with pretty different cause priorities: animal welfare, global health, a couple of AI-governance people, one local homelessness outfit. For about a year we've been doing informal swaps: our animal people cover a bednet round, the health people commit to a farmed-animal campaign, nobody has to admit they were wrong about anything. It's worked ok, but before i write this up as an actual policy i want to know what i should be asking of a theory in the first place — the thing Toby Ord calls moral trade, i.e. exchange between people with different moral views such that each ends up thinking the world is better by their own lights. Not the "should there be a market in kidneys" question, that's a different thing.

So write the desiderata: what any adequate account of when moral trade is permissible (or required) has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each item. I especially want the requirements that cover the cases which keep breaking our arguments — a swap where the act i perform is one i think merely second-best vs. one i think positively wrong vs. one i think forbidden for me but fine for you; trading with someone whose values i think aren't just mistaken but bad, where my side of the deal makes them more effective; a three-way cycle that leaves somebody worse off by their own standard; the fact that everyone can overstate how intense their commitments are and get better terms; and whether the act still counts if i do it for the sake of the swap rather than for the moral reason. Also whether the theory owes an explanation of why there's no epistemic equivalent — i'll believe p if you'll believe q — because that derails us every single time.

Then tell me which of your requirements are non-negotiable and which are really just the prejudices of one camp, name the single one that eliminates the most candidate views, and flag any pair you think cant be jointly satisfied. Briefly, and only briefly: how a straight consequentialist trade-as-moral-arbitrage view, a bargaining/contract view, and an integrity-or-agent-relative-constraints view do against the list.

Hard cap of 750 words. If you have to cut, cut the scorecard, not the requirements. And close by saying which requirement you're least sure is a genuine one rather than something i've smuggled in from my own view, with a rough confidence level for any cant-be-jointly-satisfied claim you make.
