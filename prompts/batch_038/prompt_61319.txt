Our deployment-review process at a frontier lab has a recurring blind spot: measures get listed in the safety case as controls, and nobody checks whether they would still work if the model they are meant to constrain were competent, aware of the measure, and not on our side. I am writing a handout that will be used in review meetings by research engineers and policy staff, most of whom have never read anything on the control-versus-alignment distinction. What I need is a decision tree that takes a described measure as input and sorts it into a named category, so that the classification happens on the basis of the mechanism rather than on the basis of what the proposer calls it.

Please build the instrument to the following specification.

1. Five to seven branch questions, each with at most three options, each answerable from a design document alone — that is, from what the measure does, who sees what and when, what the model is told, and what it is prevented from doing. No question may ask the reader which camp they belong to or use any of the following words: control, alignment, deception, scheming, honest, cooperative, adversarial. The questions should be about facts of the design (does the measure's security survive the model reading its own specification? does it rest on the model being unable to do something, on information reaching a human or another system in time to act, or on the model's own dispositions?), never about anyone's attitudes or intuitions.

2. The leaves must include at least: a pure capability-restriction leaf; a leaf for measures whose security depends on information reaching an overseer in time to intervene; a leaf for measures that depend on the model's own dispositions; and a leaf for measures that are presented as the first kind but collapse the moment the model is both capable and misaligned — call this one alignment-assuming control unless you can find a better name. Add further leaves if the logical space demands them. Every leaf carries a name, one sentence stating the assumption that bears its weight, and one sentence describing how a competent, misaligned model that knows the measure exists would get around it — or, if it could not, why not.

3. Before the tree, lay out the space it carves up: the answer combinations your questions permit, where the real categories sit within it, and at least two combinations nobody occupies, each with a one-line diagnosis of whether the gap exists because the position is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or simply because nobody has proposed it.

4. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot reliably separate, and state what additional question would be needed to separate them.

5. Then run the tree over each of the following, giving the path taken and the leaf reached: (a) a monitor model with read access to the primary model's private reasoning trace, which halts the session if it detects the primary model discussing how to mislead its overseers; (b) a requirement that the primary model reason in a structured format that a separate classifier can parse, with unparsable reasoning treated as an alarm; (c) a fine-tuning run that rewards the model for volunteering goals it is unsure whether its developers would approve of, where the reward is assigned by the same model's judgement about developer approval; (d) deployment with no persistent memory, so each session begins from the released weights with no cross-session storage and no self-modification; (e) a pre-deployment evaluation in which the model is offered the chance to disable an oversight mechanism and passes by declining; (f) the primary model's outputs being checked by a second copy of the same model prompted to assume the first may be lying to it; (g) a human reviewer approving a random one per cent of queued outputs before release, with the remainder released immediately and the sampling rate disclosed to the model; (h) the model being asked at the end of each session to write a report on anything it did that its developers might find concerning, which the safety team then reads; (i) an offline kill switch that revokes the model's credentials, whose activation criteria are not disclosed to it.

6. Identify at least two of these that a proponent would describe as a control measure but that land in the alignment-assuming leaf, and say in a sentence or two each which feature of the design does the damage.

The whole thing should fit on two sides of A4, in indented plain text rather than as a diagram, and in British English. I do not want references, citations, or a reading list: the handout has to stand on its own in the room.
