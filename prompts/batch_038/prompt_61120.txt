**The question as set (20 marks total):**

3. (a) State Popper's definition of verisimilitude from *Conjectures and Refutations* in terms of the true and false consequence classes of a theory. [5]

(b) Explain the refutation of that definition which appeared in 1974. Give the general shape of the argument, and work through one concrete pair of theories. [8]

**The student's answer:**

(a) For a theory A, Popper lets the truth content of A, Ct_T(A), be the class of all the true logical consequences of A, and the falsity content, Ct_F(A), be the class of all its false logical consequences. Together these make up the content of A. The definition is: A has more verisimilitude than B iff (i) Ct_T(A) includes Ct_T(B), and (ii) Ct_F(A) is included in Ct_F(B), with at least one of the two inclusions proper. Informally, A is nearer the truth than B when A says at least as much that is true and no more that is false. Popper stresses this is a logical relation between theories and is not the same as degree of corroboration: corroboration is about whether a theory has been severely tested and survived, verisimilitude is about how close it is to the truth whether or not anyone has tested it.

(b) In 1974 three people — Tichý, Harris and Miller — showed independently, in the same issue of the BJPS, that Popper's definition can never be satisfied by a false theory, so that on his own account no false theory is closer to the truth than any other. The argument runs as follows. Take two false theories A and B where A entails B, so A is the stronger; say the truth is that the coin landed heads, and let A be "the coin landed tails and it was flipped by Smith" and B be "the coin landed tails". Since A entails B, every consequence of B is a consequence of A, so clause (i) is met: Ct_T(A) includes Ct_T(B). But exactly the same reasoning applies to the false consequences, so Ct_F(A) includes Ct_F(B), which is the reverse of what clause (ii) needs. Reversing the comparison doesn't help either, because then clause (i) fails. So neither theory has more verisimilitude than the other, and the same will hold for any pair of false theories standing in the entailment relation. Note the definition isn't totally empty: if A is true then Ct_F(A) is empty, clause (ii) is satisfied for free, and the definition reduces to a comparison of true content. That is why Popper's ordering can be non-vacuous only among true theories.

(c) By all accounts Popper accepted the criticism almost immediately and conceded that the definition as it stood couldn't do the work he wanted. The repair I want to consider is the one Tichý put forward, usually called the conjunct content approach. The thought is that the trouble comes from taking a theory's content to be its consequence class, which is too coarse: instead you break the theory up into its content-elements or conjuncts and define verisimilitude over those, element by element, so a false theory can have alot of true content-elements alongside few false ones. I think this repair is only partly successful. It does restore the possibility of ordering false theories, which is what the realist about progress needs, since the whole point of the notion was to be able to say Einstein was closer to the truth than Newton even though both are strictly false. But the choice of content-elements looks ad hoc unless its independently motivated, and different ways of carving a theory into elements can give different orderings, which makes the relation sensitive to how a theory is written down rather than to what it says — precisely the worry Popper was trying to avoid by working with consequences.

The deeper cost, though, is that the episode shows verisimilitude was never doing any independent work in Popper's system anyway. What falsificationism actually needs is corroboration — a theory that has survived severe tests — and corroboration was Popper's real explication of a theory's closeness to the truth. Once the 1974 result is on the table the falsificationist story about progress loses its grounding, because there's no longer any reason to think a theory that survives tests is thereby getting nearer the truth. So the refutation isn't just a technical embarassment inside one definition, it takes out a load-bearing wall.
