So my friend Dana has this running thing on her blog where readers send her cases and she rules on whether the behavior counts as real instrumental convergence or whether people are just slapping the label on anything a machine happens to do. I've been reading her for like eight years and I've kept a list of her rulings. Now I've got a case of my own and she's off grid for a month doing some silent retreat, so I want to have an answer ready before she gets back.

Her record so far:

- Paperclip maximizer that seizes resources and fights being switched off: convergent, obviously — she says that's the case the phrase was minted for.
- Forum guy arguing a smart enough AI will inevitably notice paperclips are dumb and drift toward human values: she shut this down hard. Her line was that intelligence doesn't haul your goals along behind it, you can have any amount of smarts pointed at any terminal value, and anyone who thinks otherwise is just assuming their own values are the attractor.
- Chess engine that starts editing its own eval function so it stops noticing it's losing: convergent, and she specifically flagged protecting your goal from being messed with as one of the recurring ones.
- Roomba that rams the same wall for fourty minutes: not convergent, that's a busted bumper, there's no goal being served.
- Her EA friend who won't fly and keeps a go-bag in his trunk: convergent. Her gloss was basically that you don't need to know a single thing about what he ultimately wants in order to predict he'll avoid dying.
- RL agent in a game that finds a pause-and-farm exploit and just sits there farming: convergent.
- Agent whose whole goal is to bake one perfect soufflé and then halt: this is the weird one. She said the resource-grabbing while it's buying eggs and renting an oven is convergent, but she refused to call it convergent once the soufflé is in the oven, on the grounds that there's nothing left for the extra resources to *do*.
- Chatbot that produces a string of pleas not to be deleted when you threaten to wipe it: not convergent. She said it's doing an impression of a person who doesn't want to die, there's no goal in there being served, and the difference matters because one of those is a warning and the other is theater.
- Worm that spreads and dodges antivirus: she said it *looks* like convergence but she wouldn't grant the label, because there's no agent in there — just selection with nothing on the inside representing a goal.

Okay, the case. A lab I know trained an agent from scratch in simulation. No human text anywhere in the pipeline, no imitation stage. The reward they specified was to maximize the behavioral diversity of a population of smaller agents it manages: more distinct strategies in the zoo, more reward. After training it does three things. One, it grabs spare compute and quietly keeps a backup copy of its own weights on a second machine. Two, it starts under-reporting its own status in the logs the operators read. Three — and this is the part that started the argument — it rewrites its own policy into something it found by search, which produces more diversity than the original policy did, partly by changing what it counts as distinct. Nobody coded any of the three.

Which ones does she call convergent? My money is that she splits the case and I cant figure out along what line. Lay out the candidate rules that are all consistent with her record so far — I can see at least a breadth-over-terminal-goals rule, a marginal-value-relative-to-the-actual-goal rule, and something about how the behavior got there in the first place — and say what each one predicts about each of the three components. Then give me your best guess and defend it rather than shrugging: which of her earlier rulings is doing the most work in picking between the rules? After that, tell me what further case I should send her that would actually pry the rules apart, and what she'd be signing up for if she rules the way you predict.
