I'm defending my position at a social choice reading group next week and I want a consistency check before people start poking at it. Here's what I'm committed to:

(1) unrestricted domain — the social preference has to be defined for every logically possible profile;
(2) weak Pareto;
(3) Sen's minimal liberalism, i.e. at least two people each get one protected personal pair where their own ranking of it determines the social ranking;
(4) I find the game-form reading of rights more convincing than Sen's formulation — a right is a constraint on what's feasible, not a chunk of preference-satisfaction, and the paradox largely dissolves once you see that;
(5) I'm a strict welfarist about evaluation: the only thing that matters is the utilities in the final outcome, never how we got there;
(6) the real culprit in the paradox is Pareto letting busybodies rank other people's private choices, so I keep (1) and (2) and locate the blame elsewhere;
(7) I accept Gibbard-Satterthwaite, and I think of protected rights as a second-best device for buying strategyproofness in domains where it's otherwise unavailable.

Which pairs actually pull against each other and which just look like they do? Rank the tensions by how bad they are, and for each say what the cheapest revision is and what I'd lose by making it. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most — I'd rather not have to give up (3) or (5) but I'm not attached to any of the others.
