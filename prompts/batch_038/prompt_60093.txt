Consider a variant of the absent-minded driver problem. A driver must pass two consecutive exits that he cannot tell apart, and he retains no memory of whether he has already passed one; his only information at the moment of decision is that he is at an exit. The payoffs are 6 if he leaves the road at the second exit, 1 if he leaves at the first, and 0 if he passes both. Before setting out he may adopt a policy of continuing with some fixed probability at any exit he reaches; on the road he cannot adopt anything more discriminating, since whatever he decides applies indifferently at both.

Let C be the claim that the much-discuted conflict between planning-time and acting-time reasoning in this problem is illusory: carried out correctly, local decision-making at the exit endorses the ex ante optimal policy, and the driver is rationally required to follow it.

I want a single probability for C together with the derivation behind it, broken into these terms, each with a number and one or two lines of justification: (i) that an agent with imperfect recall is bound by a policy adopted when his epistemic position was better, rather than free to re-decide at the exit; (ii) that there is a determinate answer to what he ought to believe about which exit he is at, given that he knows his own policy, so that the local calculation is even well posed; (iii) that local expected-utility maximization on those beliefs reproduces the ex ante optimum exactly, not merely approximately or coincidentally for these payoffs; (iv) that "rational" is not doing double duty here between a planning standard and an acting standard, so that C, if true, settles the normative question and not just the arithmetic one. Show how the terms combine into your number, say which of them your figure is most sensitive to, and say what argument or result would move it furthest. As a check on your reading of the setup, also state the continuation probability that the ex ante optimum prescribes.
