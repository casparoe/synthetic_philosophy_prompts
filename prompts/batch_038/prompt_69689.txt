Sort of niche thing I keep circling back to. In the reform / defund / abolish debate people invoke the state's monopoly on legitimate violence like it were one target, but there are two separable things bundled in there: the police as an institution, and the state's claim to be the only party permitted to use or authorize force. You could get rid of the first and keep the second (courts, regulatory inspectors, deputized citizens, licensed private security with legal standing), or you could erode the second while keep something that still looks like the first (community patrols with real legal standing, armed mutual aid, private security doing what cops used to do). Write an essay that uses that split to sort out what reformers, defunders, and abolitionists are each actually going after — I suspect at least one of the three is ambiguous about it in a way that matters. Then take a side on the harder question: when the state fails to protect the people it forbids from protecting themselves, does that failure void its claim to exclusivity, or does it only generate a duty to protect better? Give me a real verdict rather than a both-sides shrug, and finish by saying which of the three positions comes out best on whatever criterion you end up defending, even if it isn't the one you'd expect going in.
