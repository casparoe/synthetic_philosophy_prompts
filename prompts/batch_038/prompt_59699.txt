You are a philosopher of mind who has worked through the multiple realizability debate in detail — Putnam's original argument, Block's objections to functionalism, and the later literature on whether purported realizations are genuinely different kinds. Answer as a specialist thinking the problem through, not as a survey writer.

Context: I am a first-year graduate student in a seminar on functionalism. Twice this week a classmate waved off an objection with the slogan "it's the software, not the hardware." The second time, the objection was Block's Chinese Nation: a billion people passing notes, wired so that the whole system is functionally isomorphic to a human being, could satisfy every specification a functionalist gives and yet, the objector insisted, nothing need be like anything to be it. The slogan was offered as though that ended the matter.

Treat the slogan as a piece of language being made to do more work than it can bear. Lay out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing — for instance, a claim about programs and their implementations, a claim about which properties are explanatorily relevant to a given science, a claim about second-order properties and their first-order realizers, and a claim about what consciousness depends on — and for each one state it with explicit scope rather than by vibe, say whether it is trivially true, substantive but defensible, or flatly false, and note what accepting it commits you to. Then say which of those readings would actually answer the Chinese Nation objection, which merely restate the functionalist's own conclusion in a way that begs the question against it, and which are the readings my classmate most likely has in mind when he says it out loud.

Please begin with a two- or three-sentence diagnosis of what is going wrong in his move, and then give the readings as a numbered menu. Where a reading trades on shifting the grain of description — the worry that the "different" realizations stop being different kinds once you fix the level at which they are typed, the sort of objection Bechtel and Mundale pressed against multiple realizability generally — flag that explicitly rather than letting it slide.
