A friend and I argued late into the evening over whether interpretability work ever establishes that a neural network genuinely has representations, or merely that certain representational ascriptions are convenient instruments for predicting its behaviour. To break the impasse, let me fix two stipulations. Call an ascription of an internal state causally load-bearing when interventions on that state — patching, ablation, or the like — change the model's behaviour in the direction and manner the ascription predicts; and call it genealogically autonomous when an investigator could arrive at it without knowing anything about the system's training data, objective function, or architecture. Please give an example of a system, hypothetical if you like but described concretely enough that the mechanism is intelligible, in which some internal state is genealogically autonomous yet not causally load-bearing. Then say what such a case shows, on your view, about whether the network really represents what the ascription claims, and whether your verdict would shift if the two conditions came apart in the opposite direction. Kindly write your response in British English.
