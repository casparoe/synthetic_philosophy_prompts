ok so I have never taken a philosophy class but I've been chewing on this for about two years now, it started as my partner and I not being able to decide about a third kid and then it kind of snowballed into something bigger. I've written down my gut calls about whether more people means better, and what I want is the rule — if there is one — that actually fits them.

1. a couple has two happy kids and could have a third who they're pretty confident would have a good life. I think having her is a good thing. But I can't say who it's good for. Not the parents, it'd make their lives harder not easier. Not the kid, because if they don't have her she isn't anywhere being deprived of anything. And still I don't want to call it neutral, I think there's something genuinely good about there being one more happy person in the world.

2. same setup but the third kid would have a life of real suffering, the kind that isn't worth living. Obviously bad. And notice how lopsided I am: rock solid on this one, only mildly positive on number one. Adding misery is clearly bad, adding happiness is only sort-of good. That gap is a real feature of what I think and I want it accounted for, not ironed out.

3. two futures, both with ten billion people all doing well. In one there's an extra ten billion, just as well off. Bigger is better, no hesitation.

4. same, except the extra ten billion have lives that are barely worth living — they'd rather exist than not, but there's not much in them. Now bigger is not better. I think the first future wins, and by a fair amount.

5. here's my problem though. I don't think you get from 3 to 4 by one big jump and call that jump bad either, if the way there is adding one barely-worth-living person at a time with nobody else's life changed at all. Each of those little steps looks fine to me. Nobody loses, somebody gains. But a chain of fine steps can't deposit you somewhere worse than where you started. So 3 and 4 and the chain cannot all be right and I have no idea which one to throw out.

6. make one existing person's life a lot better, versus create a new person whose life is a bit better than that. Improving the existing person wins for me, even though adding the new one puts more total good into the world.

7. two futures, same number of people, same total wellbeing. In one everyone is comfortably content, pretty even across the board. In the other a handful are in bliss and everyone else is just getting by. Even wins.

8. country has a fixed budget and can either spend it making the lives of the kids already there much better, or subsidising births so that in a few centuries there are many more people at the same decent level. I say the first one, instantly. But if you tell me it's about the long run and the second one leads to a hundred times as many good lives eventually, I wobble. I don't like that I wobble.

9. animals: a world with a trillion insects or fish or whatever whose lives are barely worth living, versus a smaller world with fewer but flourishing animals. Here I flip — I kind of don't mind the trillion, or I feel like I shouldn't mind it. Which is embarassing, because it's the exact same shape as number 4 where I was so confident.

My own guess at a rule, which I know is rickety: something can only be better if it's better for somebody. That seems to handle 2, 6 and 7 alright, but it blows up 1 and 3, because the extra people in those aren't anybody yet.

So — what principle, or small set of candidates, gets the most of this? State each one properly and tell me which of my calls each clause is there to catch. Then be straight with me about the ones it can't catch and which verdict I should retract, because I'd much rather give up one of my intuitions than end up with a rule that needs six special exceptions. And tell me what your principle says about things I didn't list: is there some point past which more people can never make things better no matter how good each life is, and what about the last fertile couple on earth who decide not to have a kid so that everything just stops — is that worse, and worse for who?
