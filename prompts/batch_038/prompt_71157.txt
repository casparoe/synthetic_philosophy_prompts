My dad's been a birdwatcher my whole life and I've spent the last six months going down a rabbit hole about how many wild animals there actually are and how most of them die — enormous numbers of eggs and hatchlings, most never making it to breed, and the ones that do get eaten alive or starve or get eaten from the inside by parasites. I'm not trying to convert him, honestly, I gave up on that. But there's a live thing happening near us and its driving me nuts.

The local wildlife trust has been putting out supplementary feed for woodland birds through the winter for thirty-odd years, and now there's a proposal to stop, on the grounds that it just inflates a population that then crashes in a hard February. Dad is all for stopping it. "It's the circle of life," he says, "nature keeps things in balance, you don't want to interfere." And the same phrase gets used by the other side too, to say don't mess about.

Can you break that slogan apart for me? I want the separate claims hiding inside "it's the circle of life" / "nature is in balance" — ecological ones, moral ones, ones about what we can know, whatever — laid out as a menu, with a verdict on each: which are true but beside the point, which are substantive and genuinely arguable, which are just false or confused. Then say for each whether it would actually support pulling the feeding, and what the phrase is doing when someone uses it to shut a conversation down instead of answering the question. And specifically: if "balance" just means the population stays roughly steady over time, does that tell you anything at all about whether the total amount of suffering in it is big or small? I keep feeling like that's where the sleight of hand is and I can't get it into words.
