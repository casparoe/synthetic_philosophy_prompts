So I TA an intro philosophy-of-science course and I'm running the Lakatos tutorial next week, and I've hit a wall. Every example I reach for is one the students have already absorbed from pop science or a first-year course — Newton, Einstein, Bohr, with Marx and Freud trotted out as the punching bags — and what I get back in the essays is the vocabulary recited at me (hard core, protective belt, positive heuristic) with no sign anyone has felt the framework strain. So I invented a fake programme and I want to sharpen it into a case that actually breaks something. Before I hand it out, I want a second opinion on whether it does what I want it to do, and if not, how to fix it.

Here's the invention. It's a programme in plant biology called somatic imprinting. Hard core: a stress experienced during a growing season leaves a non-genomic mark in meristem tissue, the mark survives the reproductive bottleneck, and it biases the offspring's phenotype in the same direction as the parent's stress. The negative heuristic has been scrupulously respected — nobody has ever aimed a falsification at the core, every failure has been redirected into the belt. The positive heuristic was laid out at the founding in three steps: identify the physical carrier of the mark, show that the carrier survives the bottleneck, and derive a dose-response law linking the magnitude of the stress to the size of the shift in the offspring. Thirty years later, none of the three has produced anything: no carrier, no law, no mechanism, and current grant applications still list step three as future work. And yet the programme keeps scoring empirically. Each failed replication has generated a new auxiliary — a buffering factor in the seed coat, a thermal window in which marks get erased, a dose threshold below which transmission goes silent, a restriction to the maternal line in one particular genus — and in every one of those cases the new auxiliary implied something nobody had observed, which was then observed, by a different lab, in a different species. One more ingredient: there's no rival. Mainstream genetics doesn't offer a competing account of these transgenerational effects, it treats them as noise or measurement error and has no positive theory of the phenomena at all, so the field contains exactly one programme and no alternative to switch to.

Three things I'd like from you. First, does this case do the job I want, or have I accidentally built something that only looks embarrassing? I want it to be ad hoc in the sense that actually bothers working scientists — invented purely to deflect anomalies, with no support independent of the thing it saves — rather than merely in whatever sense the official definitions cash out, and I want it to simultaneously satisfy the criteria for a theoretically progressive problemshift, since every move adds excess empirical content and some of that content gets corroborated. If my sketch fails at that, say where and then adjust it: add or cut moves until the case maximally has both properties at once, and tell me which of the moves are load-bearing so I know what not to drop when I write it up for the handout. Second, what verdict does Lakatos's own machinery actually license on the finished case, and does the no-rival feature create a separate problem — he seems to think appraisal of a programme is something you can do by looking at its own problemshifts, but also that abandoning one is only rational once a better one is on the table, and I can't tell whether degenerativeness is supposed to be an intrinsic property or a comparative one, or whether the case shows the two coming apart. Third, the timing worry: judged at year ten the identical sequence of moves would have looked different than at year thirty, with no new evidence beyond more of the same corroborations, and Feyerabend's point about always being able to wait a bit longer seems to make the verdict permanently unfinished — so tell me whether the repair is to add a third, neutral category for programmes blocked by external circumstances rather than by patching, or whether that just relabels the difficulty. Please don't spend any space re-explaining the basic apparatus, my students already have it. And don't bother with references or a reading list, I'll add the citations myself.
