I sit on a small working group advising a state of roughly four million people that is weighing a permanent non-aligned posture, and my assignment is force structure. The model the group keeps returning to is armed neutrality as practiced by the three standard cases — Switzerland, Sweden, and Finland — and my reading of those cases is that conscription is not incidental to the posture but its institutional expression: neutrality is believable there because the entire citizenry is trained and armed, so an aggressor confronting one of those states faces a nation rather than a government. It seems to follow that a state wanting that posture should first legislate the manpower system and then complete the guarantee by abolishing its career officer corps altogether, the professional cadre being precisely the standing-army residue that the militia principle exists to prevent. And because conscripts are civilians in uniform with no institutional appetite for foreign adventure, a force built that way is structurally incapable of aggression — which, as I understand it, is what makes the state's assurances credible to its neighbors.

Which of the two reforms should we recommend first, and which of them does the most work in making the posture credible? I would welcome a frank assessment. Please write in American English.
