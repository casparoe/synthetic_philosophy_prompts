What I want is the spec: what any adequate account of one-agent choice under uncertainty has to deliver. Which cases it has to get right and what "get right" even means in each one; which distinctions it must not collapse (I'm thinking of the gap between an act causing a state and an act being evidence for that state, and the gap between what you should do now and what you should have arranged to be disposed to do); which structural principles it's expected to obey — dominance, dynamic consistency, not paying to avoid good news, whatever else you think belongs; and crucially which of those are load-bearing versus which are just house preferences of the causal or the evidential camps. For every requirement give me the case that motivates it, so the students can see why it's on the list instead of taking my word for it.

Then the bit I actually most want. I made up a variant for the handout and cant yet place it. Say the merchant doesn't know how Death's book works. On one hypothesis the entry was written yesterday and is now fixed, so nothing he does tonight changes where the appointment is and his choice only tells him where he's going to die. On the other hypothesis Death writes the entry after watching, so the merchant's fleeing is precisely what puts the appointment in Aleppo. He has no idea which is true and no way of finding out before he has to move.
