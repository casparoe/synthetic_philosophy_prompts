"Twin studies have established that about 80% of the variation in IQ is heritable. That's a number that attaches to the trait, not to any particular sample, so it tells you that when you meet someone with an IQ of 130, about four fifths of that score is down to their genes. It also means there's not much point trying to change things: a highly heritable trait is close to fixed, which is why the Flynn effect tends to get overhyped, and why the shared-environment effect that shows up in childhood vanishing by adulthood is pretty good proof that parenting doesn't matter. The same logic extends to group differences -- if a trait is 80% heritable within each of two populations, most of the gap between those populations is genetic too. Modern genomics has confirmed all this, though it turns out the twin studies were if anything too modest, since the DNA-based estimates keep coming out higher than the twin ones."
