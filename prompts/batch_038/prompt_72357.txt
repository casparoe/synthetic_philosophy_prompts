I've been volunteered to run a 40-minute session at our county's new-commissioner orientation. 25 people, mostly retirees and small business owners, no political theory background, one has never sat through a zoning hearing and another was on the school board and will absolutely not shut up about it. Official topic is "subsidiarity and who gets to decide what." The only reason it's on the agenda is that last spring the state legislature wiped out a bunch of local bans on gas hookups in new construction, including one our own town spent two years writing, and people here are still sore about it. I want to hang the whole thing on that fight unless you think a different case would travel better for this room.

What I need isn't the explanation, it's the plan for delivering it. So:

Order of pieces. My instict is to open with the definition and where the term comes from — someone in our office swears its Catholic social teaching, and I believe him but I don't want to build the first ten minutes on something I can't defend. I suspect that's the wrong opening move anyway and I don't know what the right one is. Tell me what has to be in place before the gas story works as an example at all, and then what that example needs to have, and just as importantly what it needs to NOT have, so it carries the idea instead of dragging the room into a fight about pipelines. Where does Dillon's Rule fit in the sequence — does it help me or does it blow up the thing I'm trying to build?

Misconceptions. My guess is half the room leaves thinking subsidiarity just means the smallest government wins, or that it's a fancy word for the Tenth Amendment. Someone will say it's obviously a conservative principle and someone else will say it's obviously about local control so the state had no business touching anything. Which of those do I head off before the example, which after, and which one do I let them walk straight into on purpose so it makes a sound when they hit it? Also tell me the ones I'm not expecting.

The check. One question I can ask at the end that distinguishes actually getting the principle from just agreeing with me about the gas thing. It has to be answerable out loud by a retired hardware store owner, not a written essay prompt.

One more thing — my boss wants a slide on how Germany and Switzerland do it because there's an exchange trip in the fall. I have maybe four minutes of slack for that at best. Is it worth it, and if so what's the single comparison that earns its slot instead of just being postcards? And tell me plainly what I should announce I'm NOT covering. I'd rather cut the EU entirely if it costs me the main point, but say so if you think I'd be cutting the interesting part.
