I work as a software engineer on machine-learning systems, and my familiarity with philosophy is entirely second-hand, so please treat nothing below as assumed knowledge on my part.

I keep encountering Daniel Dennett's claim that Darwin and Turing each performed a "strange inversion of reasoning," and that the two inversions amount to a single discovery: competence without comprehension, on which comprehension is not the precondition of design but a late product of uncomprehending machinery. I want to test that framing against the episode I take to be its most obvious modern illustration, and I want to be told where the framing fails.

The episode: game two of the AlphaGo–Lee Sedol match, Seoul, March 2016, and specifically move 37. AlphaGo played an early shoulder hit on the fifth line, where professional Go theory points to the third and fourth lines; DeepMind reported that its own policy network put the chance of a human professional choosing that move at roughly one in ten thousand; expert commentators initially read it as an error, Lee Sedol left the board, and the move proved decisive. Hold game four in reserve as a control: Lee Sedol's wedge at move 78 produced complications AlphaGo's evaluation had not anticipated, its win estimate collapsed, and it resigned several moves later.

Work in both directions.

1. The mapping, exacting about terms rather than suggestive. On the theory's own account, where does comprehension reside here — nowhere, in the DeepMind team, in the human game records the networks were trained on, in the subsequent self-play, or somewhere the theory has no slot for? What is the competence: move selection under search, or the acquisition of the evaluation function that guides it? And what would have to be true for move 37 to count as comprehension rather than as spectacular competence?

2. Fit and misfit. Which features of move 37 does the inversion explain that a design-first picture cannot — in particular, whether the move was available precisely because nothing in the system endorsed the convention it violated, so that comprehension would have been an obstacle rather than an aid? And which features does it misdescribe or pass over: AlphaGo was engineered top-down by people who understood the problem in detail; the professionals who understood Go best immediately attributed intent to the move and then retracted; and the collapse in game four looks less like missing comprehension than like comprehension of a narrower thing.

3. The verdict, which is what I actually want. Does this match confirm the thesis, is it an anomaly the theory absorbs too cheaply because it can describe any outcome, or is it a case of a kind the formulation never contemplated? Be explicit wherever the theory is silent and do not press it to speak there. If it turns out that whether AlphaGo comprehends depends on how we describe the system rather than on anything about the system, say so plainly and tell me what that does to the claim that the inversion is a discovery about the world.
