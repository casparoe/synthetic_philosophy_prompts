I'm trying to sort out what I actually think about reasons — specifically about a distinction I can feel but can't name cleanly. Some considerations seem to be about the thing my attitude is aimed at, and others seem to be only about the having of the attitude, and I treat those two very differently. Not sure my pattern hangs together. Here are my gut calls:

1. The donor who funds my department says the money stops unless we find his pet theory compelling. A few of us can't and never will. I think we genuinely have reason to try — read the papers charitably, sit with them, that kind of thing. But I also think that if someone does end up finding it compelling because of the money, their finding it compelling isn't any better as a piece of thinking. So: a reason to try, and not a reason in favor of the attitude itself. Both halves feel solid to me.

2. A rep for a pharma company offers me two grand to believe her drug is best in class. There's no evidence it is. I don't think that's a reason to believe it at all. At most it's a reason to want to believe it, which sounds like a different animal.

3. A magazine pays me $500 for a glowing review of a midlist novel I'd skimmed and thought was meh. To write the review I go back and actually read it closely and I notice sentence-level stuff I'd have missed. By the end I do admire the book. I think that admiration is fine — the $500 didn't make the book good, it got me to see that it was, and seeing is seeing.

4. A think tank pays me $5k to spend three months investigating whether raising the minimum wage costs jobs. Left alone I'd never have looked into it. During those three months I read the studies and end up believing it doesn't, much. This one makes me queasy in a way 3 doesn't and I couldn't say why. Best I've got: if they'd paid me to reach the other conclusion maybe I would have. But thats true of the novel too, so that can't be the difference.

5. My internet goes down for a week and wrecks a deadline. The company sends me $200 because their retention team worked out it's cheaper than losing me. I feel grateful and I don't think that's confused. Same company's founder gets a lifetime achievement award for philanthropy he does for the tax break and the photos. I don't think he's admirable at all. Gratitude yes, admiration no, and I feel firm on both.

6. My boss tells a joke that isn't funny. I laugh. I'm not amused, I'm employed. Doesn't feel like there was any reason about the joke at all, just a reason to make the noise.

7. On New Years I promised myself I'd want to go to my family's dinner this year. The dinner will not be pleasant. I still think the promise gives me a reason to want to go — a reason I made rather than found. This feels as firm as anything on the list.

The one I keep flip flopping on: my partner wrote a novel and asked me to read it and said she hoped I'd love it. I read it partly to please her, and I did love it, and now I love it for itself, though I'd never have picked it up otherwise. Half the time I think it's real so it's fine, half the time I think it's contaminated at the root and I'm fooling myself.

So: is there one principle behind all of this? I'd rather get one real candidate stated precisely, with the clause that's doing the work in each case, than a survey of the options. But if you think one of my verdicts has to go, say which and why — don't just bend the principle into a pretzel to save all seven.

Give it to me as a single JSON object and nothing else, with these fields:

- "principle": {"name": a short label you make up, "statement": the principle itself, stated precisely, as many sentences as it takes}
- "cases": array with one entry per numbered case, each {"n": 1 through 7, "mine": my verdict in a few words, "yours": one of "matches" / "overturns" / "matches-with-extra-clause", "clause": which part of the principle decides it, "note": one sentence}
- "flipflopper": {"verdict": what the principle says about my partner's novel, "why": the reasoning}
- "predictions": array of {"case": ..., "predicted_verdict": ..., "confidence": "low" / "medium" / "high"} for these two that I haven't given verdicts on: (a) my kid got paid five bucks a book all summer and now reads for fun — is her enjoyment of reading grounded in the right sort of thing; (b) an app gives me points for engaging with whatever it puts in front of me and I do get genuinely outraged at the stuff in my feed — is the outrage fitting
- "costs": array of strings, honest ones — anything the principle gets wrong elsewhere, or verdicts of mine it strains but technically keeps

Don't bother with names or a reading list, I don't need citations, I just want the view.
