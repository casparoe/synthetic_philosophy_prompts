Bayesian confirmation theory runs into the problem of old evidence when a hypothesis H appears strongly confirmed by an observational regularity O that has sat in the agent's evidence base for years, with her credence in O never budging from 1. The familiar repairs are all dynamic: perhaps the agent was never really certain of O, so she should update by redistributing over O and not-O; perhaps what she learns is something new after all — the extra-systemic proposition that H entails O — and she should conditionalize on that; or perhaps she should conditionalize on the background she would have had if she had already drawn out every consequence of her evidence.

I want to defend a different diagnosis, and I would like you to build the argument for it.

Case. Ada has known O for a decade and is certain of it. Two rival theories, H1 and H2, both entail O; her credences are 0.3 in each, with 0.4 on "neither." Working entirely from what she already knows, she derives O from H1 and finds the observed magnitudes fall out of two structural assumptions to which H1 was independently committed. She then derives O from H2 and finds that the derivation goes through only once a parameter that H2 itself never fixed is set by hand to match O. No new observation enters her evidence base. Her credence in H1 rises to 0.7 and her credence in H2 falls to 0.1.

Thesis. Ada's shift is unobjectionable, and its unobjectionability shows that the problem of old evidence was never a problem about the dynamics of belief. It is a problem about the statics — about what a single credal state is supposed to be a state *of*. A credal state encodes not only the agent's take on the empirical world but also her partial grasp of the consequences of that take; the requirement of total evidence should accordingly be read as a requirement concerning the propositions the agent is *in a position to deploy* at a time, not those to which she is already *committed* by logical consequence. On that reading, coming to be sure that H1 entails O in a non-ad-hoc way and that H2 entails it only by hand-tuning is a change in what Ada is in a position to deploy, and her new credences are simply the result of conditionalization properly applied. No special rule for logical learning is needed.

Please argue for this thesis as rigorously as you can, addressing in particular:

(a) Why the notion of being "in a position to deploy" is not merely logical omniscience stood on its head. Give it enough determinate content that a critic could say what would count as its failing, and identify the class of cases in which it is supposed to bite.

(b) How the thesis handles the parameter-tuning discovery, where what seems to have changed is a judgment of ad hoc-ness rather than the addition of any propositional content. If your account must treat that judgment as a proposition, say what that proposition is and why Ada's prior over it was not already sufficient to generate the shift.

(c) Whether the thesis can underwrite any quantitative constraint on how far Ada's credence in H1 may rise, or whether it must stay silent on magnitudes — and if it must stay silent, whether that silence is an acceptable cost rather than a concession to arbitrariness.

(d) Why at least two of the dynamic repairs misdescribe this case. For each, say either how it smuggles back the omniscience it was meant to relax, or what verdict it delivers about Ada and why that verdict is wrong.

Close by stating where your own position is most vulnerable and what would have to be surrendered to patch it. Describe the rival views in your own terms; no references or bibliography are needed.
