Our consortium maintains a public archive of roughly forty published quantitative models of the same target system — the spread of an agricultural pathogen across a region over a decade. Under new funding terms, the archive must publish a ranked list of those models by closeness to the truth, and I want the specification of that notion settled before anyone proposes a formula. Please write the desiderata: what any account of truthlikeness (verisimilitude) has to deliver in order to serve this purpose, with the motivating case behind each item.

Adequacy is relative to two uses, and you should hold the list to those and only those: the ranking will decide which models enter a policy-facing ensemble average, and it will have to be defended to modelers whose work places near the bottom. If a demand people make of theories in this area is genuinely a requirement only for some other purpose — explanatory virtue, say, or ranking complete theories of the world rather than working models — say so and set it aside.

The archive has the following features; your list should be responsive to each, either as a requirement it imposes or as a case you argue is ill-posed:

1. Models disagree about what to talk about as well as about values. Some omit a whole dimension entirely, such as soil moisture; others include it and get it wrong. The account must either rank omission and error differently or explain why it need not.
2. Some entries are logically equivalent redescription of others. One model has been re-expressed with a redundant derived variable — an infection rate split into two quantities that sum back to the original — and several models use intertranslatable unit conventions. The ranking must not move under either change.
3. One entry is a coarse, trivially weak claim that is known to be true; another is a rich model that is wrong by about three percent on every quantity the policy brief uses. The list must take a position on which ranks higher.
4. Two models agree on every observable output, but one attributes the dynamics to a mechanism we have good independent reason to think is absent. Say whether claims about unobservable structure enter the measure, and what follows from either choice.
5. The truth is unknown to us; we have finite, noisy observations. Any candidate measure must have an estimator whose ranking is stable at the level of sampling error we actually face.
6. Because ranked modelers may appeal, a single scalar is not enough: the account must decompose, locating where the distance comes from.

For each desideratum, state it, give its motivating case in the terms above, and mark it as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp. Then tell me which single requirement eliminates the most candidates, and — the part I most want — whether any subset of your own list is jointly unsatisfiable; several classic attempts in this area have failed precisely that way, and if you suspect mine will too, say which requirement I should drop and what the resulting account loses.

Finish with a short section, in your own terms and without naming anyone, on how the two broad families of accounts fare against the list: content-based views, on which truthlikeness is a balance of true consequences against false ones, and similarity-based views, on which it is distance within a space of possible states of affairs. No citations, no bibliography, no reading list — I am interested in the reasoning, not the scholarship. A table is acceptable for the list itself.
