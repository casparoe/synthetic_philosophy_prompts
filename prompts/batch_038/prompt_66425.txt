I'm putting together a case around Du Bois's propaganda thesis in "Criteria of Negro Art" (1926) — the "all Art is propaganda and ever must be, despite the wailing of the purists" line, his "whatever art I have for writing has been used always for propaganda for gaining the right of black folk to love and enjoy," and the way he ties propaganda to being one who "tells the truth and exposes evil." What I want from you is a thought experiment built to spec, not an essay about him.

The spec: design a contemporary, entirely realistic case — a novelist, a showrunner, an album, a photographer, your pick, but no dystopias and no invented countries — where a Black artist has exactly three live options and where applying Du Bois's own criteria ranks them the wrong way round. Specifically, the option that is scrupulously truthful and genuinely beautiful has to come out as worse propaganda than both alternatives, and the one alternative that would actually work as propaganda for liberation has to violate the truthfulness constraint (the third criterion above: no lying, no flattering, no composite that overstates). Two constraints that make this non-trivial. First, the truthful-and-beautiful work must contain no stereotype and no negative imagery whatsoever — I don't want the standard respectability-politics case about whether to show crime and poverty; the conversion into bad propaganda has to run through ordinary institutional machinery (reviewing, cover copy, syllabus adoption, award citations, cliping one scene out of a two-hour film), not through a racist conspiracy or an audience stipulated hostile. Second, the artist is talented and fully onboard with Du Bois's aims, so the failure definately can't come from her incompetence or from selling out.

Scenario in one paragraph, then do the work. Apply each of his criteria to each of the three options and show the ranking actually falling out of them rather than being asserted. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work and what happens to the verdicts if you delete it; give the strongest objection from someone who holds that what a work propagandizes is fixed by its content and its maker's intentions, not by how it gets received, and say whether the case survives that objection; and close with an honest verdict on whether the case shows the propaganda thesis generating real guidance or shows it compatible with any ranking you like and therefore doing no work at all.

The point of the exercise is to see whether his criteria can be action-guiding under reception conditions he'd have recognized, so stipulating the verdicts counts as cheating — they have to come out of the criteria. No citations or scholarship needed, I just want the case and the analysis. And if you think one of my constraints can't be satisfied alongside the others, say which and why, then give me the closest case you can build.
