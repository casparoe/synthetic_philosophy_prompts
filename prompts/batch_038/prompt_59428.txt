Play Quine — late-period Quine: dry, quick, fond of a deflationary turn, unwilling to be hurried into a distinction he suspects is bogus. Use your own vocabulary without glossing it for me, but if you need a fact about the situation I describe that I have not supplied, ask for it in character. Keep each reply to two or three paragraphs. Concede small points freely; do not concede the main one.

Let me fix a starting point, and correct me where I have got it wrong. Moral sentences are not a separate, non-cognitive breed: they sit inside the same web as everything else, and they meet experience only through the whole theory. There is no second, specially furnished realm of values to be inspected from outside the system, and no way of grounding ethics except from within our going account of the world — the one that already includes psychology and biology. You also sort values into the altruistic and the aesthetic, a division I confess I find harder to place in that picture. And where evidence gives out, theory choice is settled by pragmatic maxims: simplicity, conservatism, fecundity.

Here is the case. My institution runs a forecasting unit that models policy outcomes. Two of my colleagues, both naturalists, have been given the same model of a proposal to let a small number of people die in order to finance a much larger benefit for a much larger number. They agree on every figure: the probabilities, the causal mechanisms, the psychology of those affected, and even the facts about their own upbringing and why each of them feels as he does. One says the proposal is permissible; the other says it is not. Neither can name an observation that would tell between them, and neither is confused, dishonest, or arguing in bad faith.

So: what are these two disagreeing about? If your answer is that they have adopted empirically equivalent total systems, then tell me what makes one of them the better system — and tell me what simplicity or conservatism could mean when applied to a moral sentence, since the only thing such a sentence might contribute to a system's predictive success is precisely what is in dispute here.

After each reply, drop out of character for a single sentence: state how confident you are in what you have just argued, and name the point in it you are least sure of.
