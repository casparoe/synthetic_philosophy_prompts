My coauthor Miriam is who I argue with most about units of selection, and I can't reverse-engineer her rule. Here's her track record, from reading group and from the margins of my draft:

1. Wrinkly spreader Pseudomonas in static microcosms: the mat-formers secrete the cellulose that lets the whole mat sit at the air-water interface, non-producers do better inside any given mat, and eventually the mat sinks. She calls this real group selection when fresh tubes get colonized by bits of surviving mat. And she's completely happy describing the within-tube part as individual selection for cheaters, even though the tube started from a handful of cells and is basically clonal the whole time.

2. Dictyostelium: slug grown up from one spore, cooperation holds; slug assembled from a mix of genotypes, stalk-avoiding cheaters take the spores. Her call — the group is a unit in the first, not the second, even though the fruiting body is no more "emergent" in one than in the other.

3. Volvocines: she dates a colony's arrival as a genuine individual to the point where the somatic cells can't go it alone anymore, not the point where cells first start sticking together.

4. Foraging flocks where bigger flocks find food better but membership churns and flocks form and dissolve with nothing handed on: "not a unit, that's just individuals picking company."

5. Species-level sorting: she'll go for it when a clade's larval dispersal mode drives its speciation and extinction rates, and she refuses it when a clade does well purely because its member species happen to have more fecund individuals.

6. t-haplotype in mice: "the gene's-eye description is the whole story. Sure, populations carrying it can crash, and some do, but nothing gets copied when a population 'reproduces,' so I won't call that population-level selection no matter how much variance there is in which populations survive."

The case I need a prediction for is one I built to test her, loosely on gall-forming aphids with a sterile soldier caste. A single foundress starts the gall. Her clonal daughters develop into sterile soliders first and reproductives later; the soldiers defend the gall and repair breaches. The colony reproduces as a colony: winged forms leave and every new gall is started by one foundress, so you get a hard bottleneck at group formation plus genuine group-level reproduction. But I've stipulated the demography so that between-gall variance in reproductive output is a clean linear function of soldier number — no threshold, no synergy, no interaction terms to speak of — and every soldier's behavior is exactly what an inclusive-fitness calculation at relatedness ≈ 1 predicts. Nothing about the gall is a property of the gall rather than of its members.

Will she call this selection at the colony level? What I want:

(a) your guess at her verdict, with a confidence;

(b) the criteria that fit all six precedents — there's more than one — written out so they could be applied to a case nobody has seen before;

(c) what each of those criteria says about the aphid gall, and exactly where they split;

(d) which one you think she's actually running, and which single precedent does the most work for you there;

(e) one further case, cheap to describe, that would seperate the survivors cleanly.

And if some pair of her verdicts is already in tension, tell me which and what she'd have to add to patch it — I'd rather hear it from you than have her catch me reading her uncharitably in front of the whole group.
