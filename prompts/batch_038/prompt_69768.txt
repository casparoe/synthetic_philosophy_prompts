I teach the intro ethics sequence and every year around week nine, when we get to Strawson on resentment, the room goes completely flat — they've just come off three weeks of Kant and they're spent. Last spring I tried to loosen things up with a determinism joke and it died, and afterwards I realized why: it was one of those jokes where you could swap the word "determinist" for "economist" or "vegan" and nothing else would have to change. The punchline didnt depend on anything in the paper. This year I'm holding myself to a stricter standard and I could use your help.

So: write me a joke about Strawson's reactive attitudes, and make the punchline turn on something specific to the argument. Good hooks, pick one and actually commit to it — (a) the difference between taking the objective attitude toward someone and participating with them, and how fast you can flip between the two mid-conversation; (b) his point that the truth of determinism isn't what gets us to suspend resentment, since what does that work is a practical withdrawal from the relationship rather than a theoretical conclusion; (c) the gap between the pleas that excuse a particular act and the ones that get the agent off the hook altogether. Somebody who's read the paper should laugh a beat later than somebody who hasn't, and the second laugh should come from recognizing the distinction, not from the word "philosopher" showing up. Two constraints on the setup, because I want this to be new rather than recycled: it's set at a homeowners association meeting about a shared hedge, the hedge has to be doing real work in the punchline, and the whole thing has to be tellable out loud in under 150 words — no stage directions, no footnotes, no two-philosophers-walk-into-a-bar framing. And since it's a room of nineteen year olds, if you go the exemption route please don't make the psychiatric cases the butt of it; make the HOA the butt of it.

Give me the joke first, clean, no preamble. Then one section headed "What the punchline is load-bearing on" — call it 150 words — telling me which move in Strawson's argument the joke actually depends on, what the obvious generic version of this joke would have been and why its worse, and whether it would still land in a room of hard incompatibilists who reject the whole reactive-attitudes program.
