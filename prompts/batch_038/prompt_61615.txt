I am a second-year graduate student in a formal epistemology reading group, and next week I have to present on the charge that imprecise probability theory is self-undermining. My training is standard Bayesian epistemology — credences as real numbers, conditionalization, Dutch books, proper scoring rules — so I understand exactly why a single incoherent credence function can be exploited. What I do not yet understand is the relationship between two pathologies that keep getting run together in what I am reading, and I want a careful, intuition-first account of the difference.

The first is sure loss. On Walley's treatment, a body of lower and upper previsions is defective if the gambles it commits you to can be pooled into a package that loses money however the world turns out, and this is a strictly weaker demand than full coherence. The second is dilation: an event A together with a partition such that, whichever cell you learn, the conditional lower and upper probability of A strictly contain your unconditional ones. You begin with a determinate interval, receive genuine information, and finish less determined than you started. My friend, a committed precise Bayesian, insists these are one complaint in two costumes: if the interval for A really widens on every possible observation, then an agent who books your ex ante and ex post prices against each other can squeeze you just as a Dutch book squeezes an incoherent credence function, so dilation is merely a diachronic sure loss. My supervisor disagrees, and says the cases are categorically distinct — that perfectly coherent, indeed closed and convex, sets of priors dilate routinely, and that dilation has nothing whatsoever to do with incoherence. I cannot adjudicate between them, because I can see how conditional intervals might drift apart without seeing how that drift converts into a guaranteed loss.

So explain to me what dilation is doing mechanically. Specifically I would like: (a) a worked example on the smallest finite sample space you can manage, with the set of priors listed explicitly, the unconditional interval for the target event, and the conditional interval after each cell of the partition, with the arithmetic displayed so that I can verify it by hand rather than take your word for it; (b) an account of what must hold between the event and the partition for dilation to arise — my guess is that it cannot arise if the event is independent of every cell under every prior in the set, and I want to know whether that guess survives, and what the relevant notion of relevance or independence looks like once you have sets of probabilities instead of single ones; (c) an explicit verdict on whether the model in your example satisfies the sure-loss condition and the coherence condition, and then either a diagnosis of where my friend's dynamic Dutch book argument goes wrong or a concession that he is right. Please also tell me how much of the answer is sensitive to the choice of updating rule: I have seen generalized Bayes, full Bayes, and conditioning driven by maximality or E-admissibility all discussed in connection with dilation, and I do not know whether two defensible rules can disagree about whether dilation occurs in a given case, or about how severe it is.

Finally, the part I need most for the presentation. Granting that dilation is not incoherence, is it a defect at all? Give me the strongest version of the claim that dilation is precisely what a rational agent with genuinely ambiguous evidence ought to expect — that a widening interval faithfully reports having learned which of several hypotheses obtains, rather than reporting a loss of information — and then give me the honest costs of that reply, including whether it concedes that the agent cannot tell "I have learned something" apart from "I have been made vaguer," and what the imprecise probabilist can say to the charge that her view renders rational inquiry progressively less informative. Where the intuitions in this area genuinely diverge, flag the divergence instead of smoothing it over. I do not want a bibliography, a reference list, or footnotes; I want the ideas.
