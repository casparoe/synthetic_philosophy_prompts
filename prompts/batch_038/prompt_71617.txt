I'm an engineer at a consumer fintech app — peer transfers, budgeting, a debit card. Anything that touches user controls, defaults, or a model decision has to go through our product goverance review, which is basically one person, Mara, plus a lawyer who mostly doesn't talk. I've been sitting in on these for about a year and I've been keeping a list of her calls, mostly because I keep getting told my feature is coercive and I genuinely cannot reverse-engineer what she means by that.

Her calls so far, in rough order:

1. Killed a redesign that moved cancel-subscription three taps deeper. Her note, paraphrased: the option still existed, we just made it expensive.
2. Approved Focus Mode, which blocks all notifications for a window the user picks and cannot be overridden until the window ends. You literally cannot turn it off mid-window. She waved it through.
3. Approved auto-enrolling people in round-up savings, one tap to leave, no penalty.
4. Killed basically the same round-up feature six months later when she noticed we could change the match percentage mid-year without telling anybody. Identical screens, identical one tap to leave. She said the taps were not the problem.
5. Approved a hard transfer cap with no override, because a regulator set it and there's a published appeals route.
6. Killed an identical hard cap that our own risk team wanted, no appeals path. She said the number was the same and she didn't care about the number.
7. Approved deleting see-whos-online entirely, so nobody has the option at all anymore, after research showed it was enabling stalking-ish behavior.
8. Killed a purely informational dashboard — no defaults changed, nothing blocked, every option still open — because which cards each user saw came from a model we retrained weekly and nobody could explain any one user's view.

Thursday's is adaptive spend limits. The model moves your daily card limit up and down on its own. Nothing new gets blocked, all your existing options stay, there's a screen showing your current limit, you can't set the limit yourself but you can message support and they'll usually change it within a day or two, and the model retrains monthly so limits drift around some.

My gut says she kills it and I can't say why in a meeting. My "is there a toggle" theory obviously dies on 4, 6 and 8, since none of those were about toggles. And 2 and 7 look like she's fine removing options, so "don't remove options" dies too. A friend who did a poli-sci degree says there's a whole literature on freedom as non-interference versus freedom as non-domination and I'm fairly sure that's the neighborhood she lives in, but please don't just explain the labels at me, I can read a wikipedia page for that. Keep it concrete. I don't need a philosophy lecture, I need to know what to build.
