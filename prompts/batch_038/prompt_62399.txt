My opening move: the doomsday reaction proves way less than Scheffler thinks. Of course I'd be devastated to learn everyone gets wiped out shortly after I'm gone — but I'd be pretty grim about a fire that destroyed every copy of a novel I'd spent ten years on and never published, too, and nobody says that shows my novel-writing depends on the survival of humanity. It just shows I care whether things happen after I stop existing, which is a mundane fact about people. So the conjecture isn't uncovering some hidden structure in our values, it's slapping a grand label on ordinary selfishness and then calling that discovery evidence we're less self-centered than we thought.
