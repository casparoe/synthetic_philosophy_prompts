I teach wellbeing to grad students and I want my objection in fighting shape before I have to defend it in seminar, so: play a hedonist — a specific kind, a phenomenological one. Your view is that every pleasant experience contains one and the same felt quality, a distinctive hedonic tone present in exactly the same form whether it comes from a hot bath, from hearing a dissonance resolve, from sex, or from finally seeing how a proof goes. You think family-resemblance and disjunctive accounts are evasions: if there were nothing literally in common, 'pleasure' would be an empty word and hedonism wouldn't be a substantive thesis at all. You may concede that pleasure isn't the only thing of value and that some pleasant things are bad to pursue. You may not concede that pleasures lack a common nature, and you may not retreat to saying the common element is just being wanted. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it reads like a conversation rather than an essay, and if you need a detail about my setup that I haven't given you, ask me for it in character. No headings, bullet points, bold, or tables — continuous prose, the way you'd write an email.

Here's my opening. Take these four: (1) the taste of very cold water twenty minutes into a hard run; (2) the moment a chord you've been holding slightly out of tune finally lands; (3) the feeling, ten minutes in, of a migraine beginning to lift; (4) watching your own kid do something clumsy but brave. Tell me the one quality they share, and tell me where in (4) it sits — I can find admiration, tenderness, and a tight chest, and none of those feel anything like cold water. Then the harder one. Suppose a colleague builds a stimulator that reliably produces, on demand, exactly the neural signature your view is committed to, and the subject reports that quality vividly and accurately. Asked afterwards, though, the subject says it was bland, like a room tone, that it felt like nothing they'd of called enjoying anything, and that they wouldn't take it again if offered. Is that pleasure? If yes, you've bought wellbeing for someone unmoved and mildly repelled by the experience. If no, the felt quality wasn't sufficient after all — and whatever you added to rule it out is doing the real work in (1) through (4) too. Which horn do you take?
