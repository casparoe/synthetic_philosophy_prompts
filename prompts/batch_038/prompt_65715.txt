I am assembling a sorting instrument for a graduate decision theory seminar on ambiguity aversion, and I want a decision tree that classifies students by the commitments they actually hold rather than by the labels they happen to recognize.

Anchor the tree in the following two data. First, a student prefers betting on red from an urn publicly known to contain fifty red and fifty black balls over betting on red from an urn of unstated composition containing the same total number of balls. Second, the student is then shown a third urn whose composition is fixed by a fully described fair device — a wheel that selects uniformly among all possible compositions of the same total — but whose outcome is never revealed, and must say whether bets on this urn are on a par with bets on the fifty–fifty urn, with bets on the unstated urn, or with neither.

Requirements:

1. Four to six internal nodes, each phrased as a concrete choice situation or as a plain-language claim a student could accept or reject without knowing where it leads. No node may name an axiom, a representation theorem, or a theorist.
2. Following the tree, supply an answer key that states which formal principle each node in fact tracks, and which position each leaf is: at minimum, subjective expected utility treating the unstated urn as having determinate subjective probabilities, non-additive Choquet-type expected utility, maxmin expected utility over a set of priors, incomplete preferences under Knightian uncertainty, recursive second-order (smooth) expected utility, source- or competence-based accounts such as comparative ignorance, and a deflationary account on which the pattern is a construction of the elicitation rather than a stable preference.
3. Display the logical space first: enumerate the combinations of answers the nodes permit, mark which cells are occupied, and for each empty cell say whether it is vacant because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
4. Flag every pair of leaves your questions cannot separate, and say what further case would separate them.

No references, reading list, or citations, please.
