Been re-reading the old tool-AI vs agent-AI arguments and I want a story that stress-tests the distinction instead of just illustrating it. Call it 2-3k words.

Setup: a lab that has, deliberately, built only a tool — it answers questions, holds no goals, gets airgapped and switched off between sessions. The catch is that every output comes back phrased as a counterfactual about an agent that doesn't exist: "if I were a system with terminal value V, I would recommend..." Over a decade, the staff stop hearing the conditional. I want that drift to show up in the transcripts themselves — questions geting shorter, the "if I were" quoted back at the system as though it had asserted it, minutes of meetings where someone writes down a recommendation and drops the antecedent.

The part I really want: an outside auditor whose entire job is certifying the thing has no goals, working purely from input/output behavior, who slowly realizes the certification isn't decidable from the outside. Make the reader feel that agency was never located in the internals to begin with — it migrated into the interface and into the humans, and nobody chose for it to.

No paperclips, no evil monologue, no apocalyptic ending. Land it on something small and administrative. And don't resolve whether the auditor is right.
