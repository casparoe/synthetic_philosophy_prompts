The dispute over when the Anthropocene began is usually laid out as a contest among four candidate onsets: an "early" onset thousands of years before the present, tied to the spread of agriculture, irrigated rice cultivation, and deforestation; a 1610 boundary marked by a pronounced dip in atmospheric carbon dioxide in polar ice cores and attributed to the regeneration of American forests after the collapse of Indigenous populations and land use following European contact; a late-eighteenth-century boundary aligned with industrialization and the steam engine, which is roughly where one of the term's coiners located the onset; and a mid-twentieth-century boundary anchored to globally dispersed radionuclides from thermonuclear weapons testing, which the Anthropocene Working Group eventually formulated as a golden spike in varved lake sediment in Ontario, fixed at 1952. In March 2024 the Subcommission on Quaternary Stratigraphy voted that proposal down and the International Commission on Stratigraphy endorsed the rejection, leaving the term with no formal standing in the geologic time scale.

Write an essay on this controversy that reaches and defends a conclusion. Please organize it around a hypothesis about why the argument has proven so stubborn, and assess that hypothesis rather than simply assuming it. The hypothesis is this: the four candidates are not rival answers to a single question but answers to three distinct questions that have been run together — a stratigraphic question (where, in a sedimentary record legible to some future reader with no access to archives or radiometric calendars, is there a horizon sharp enough, globally synchronous enough, and durable enough to serve as a boundary); a causal-systemic question (when did human activity cease to be a perturbation of Earth-system dynamics and become a dominant forcing term in them); and an attributive question (which collectivity acquired the capacity to transform the planet at that scale, and so who is answerable for the transformation). On this view, radionuclide fallout wins the first question almost uncontestedly and loses the other two badly, an agricultural onset can win the second on some readings while being unable to satisfy the first at all, and the apparent intractability of the debate is an artifact of demanding that a single date discharge all three jobs.

Say whether you accept that decomposition. If you reject it, locate precisely where it fails — for example, in the claim that stratigraphy has always bundled causal and conventional elements together, since the Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary is an iridium-rich layer whose significance depends on an impact hypothesis, so that the Anthropocene's difficulty is not distinctive but merely more conspicuous. If you accept it, say whether the decomposition genuinely dissolves anything or merely relabels a real disagreement: an objector will insist that a start date which serves only the stratigraphic job is a start date nobody outside geology cares about, and that a date chosen for its causal or attributive content is not a date at all but a thesis.

Close on a verdict rather than a survey. Tell me which candidate you would defend as the best answer to the question when the Anthropocene began — or tell me, if that is your considered view, that the question has no single correct answer, in which case explain what does have one and why the residual indeterminacy is something other than a failure of decision. A conditional verdict is acceptable, provided you also state which reading of the question you take to be primary and why. Take a position as well on the institutional side of the controversy: whether the 2024 rejection is best read as a judgment that the Anthropocene is real but not epoch-sized, as a judgment that the concept lacks a determinate enough referent to be formalized, or as evidence that the body consulted has authority over boundary-definition but not over the question people mean when they ask when the Anthropocene began; and whether using the term informally, as a condition or an event rather than a unit on the chart, is a principled resolution or a way of declining the argument.

Please reason the matter through rather than amassing references; no citations, notes, or bibliography are wanted. Name a proposal, a person, or a date only where you are confident of it, and flag anything you suspect you are reconstructing imperfectly.

Finally, state how confident you are in your central verdict and, separately, in the three-question decomposition that most of your reasoning rests on, and single out the point in your argument where you are least sure of yourself — the place where a well-informed opponent would press hardest and where you would be least able to answer.
