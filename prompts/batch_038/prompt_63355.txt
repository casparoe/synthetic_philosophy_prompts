I work in HR at a food distribution company, about 2,000 people, and lately I keep noticing we run two programs that seem to assume opposite things about people. Before we hire anybody we screen them with an integrity questionnaire and a personality inventory, and we keep the scores on file for years — the whole point is to filter out the ones who'd cut corners before they get in the building. Then every year we make everbody sit through a compliance module plus a bystander-intervention class, and that class is basically all scripts and situational tricks: how to phrase it when you tell a coworker to stop, how to get a supervisor into the room, how the main thing you're fighting is the fact that nobody else is saying anything. And when something actually goes wrong we fire the person who did it and consider it resolved, while our safety team's accident reviews are explicitly not allowed to stop at worker error as the cause.

So which is it? Do we think people have a fixed character you can detect in advance, or do we think behavior is mostly made by the situation and can be retrained? Are those two programs genuinely contradictory or is there some picture of character that makes both of them sensible? And if we really bought the second one, what would we have to give up — the screening, the firings, or something I'm not seeing? I never studied any of this so maybe there's an obvious answer.
