What I want the question to be about. Half my group came away thinking the book proves minds aren't machines, i.e. that a person can always see some truth that no program could ever see, so AI is basically doomed. There's a version of that argument floating around that I think goes back to a philosopher in the sixties and then got picked up by a physicist much later — describe it in your own words if you need it, don't name-drop papers nobody in my room will ever read. Anyway I'm pretty sure thats not what Hofstadter is doing, but I couldn't say why beyond a feeling in my gut, and that feeling isn't good enough to grade ten people's homework with.

The part I find genuinely interesting is the transfer. How does a formula in a made-up system like the book's TNT end up being *about* itself at all, rather than that just being a cute pun? And then what would have to exist in a mind for the same trick to work there — and even if it does work, what does it actually buy you, as opposed to what people wish it bought you? I'd also love the question to make people notice that the record player which tears itself apart playing a record titled with a claim about that very player is the same *shape* of thing but a completely different kind of failure: broken hardware, not unprovable sentence. If someone can say why those two cases come apart, they've understood something.
