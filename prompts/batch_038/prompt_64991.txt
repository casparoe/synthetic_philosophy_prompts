I run a tiny decision-theory blog and I want to post a dialogue piece next month. Can you write the dialogue? I'll do the framing intro myself, so just give me the dialogue — minimal stage directions, no narrator wrap-up, no moral at the end.

Setting: two people are at the hotel bar after a workshop on decision under uncertainty. The bartender has put a jar on the counter between them: 100 tokens, exactly 25 of them red, and the other 75 either black or white in some proportion he drew from a bag and won't disclose. A bookie at the next table is offering bets on a single draw from that jar.

Characters:

- Nadia — a strict Savagean. One finitely additive subjective prior, the sure-thing principle as a requirement of rationality, and a conviction that ambiguity aversion is a failure of nerve that a few well-chosen trades will expose.
- Owen — defends choice by worst case across a *set* of priors rather than a single one. He thinks his preferences over the jar bets aren't merely permitted but are what a rational agent who genuinely has no information about the black/white split ought to have.

Both are excellent and both know the other's moves. Neither gets to be a strawman, and I mean it: Owen should never be reduced to saying "I just have intuitions about the jar," and Nadia should never be reduced to saying "the axioms say so, end of story."

Four beats I want hit, in roughly this order:

1. Owen lays out his preferences over the four jar bets — red vs. black, and red-or-white vs. black-or-white. Nadia then identifies *exactly* which pair of choices violates the sure-thing principle, and says which event is being held fixed and how the two acts agree off it. Owen's answer can't just be "so much the worse for the sure-thing principle" — I want him to give a real reason why the principle is the thing that should go, ideally something about what it takes for an agent to be able to condition on an event she has no view about at all.

2. Nadia tries to money-pump him. Write the actual sequence of trades with actual prices in dollars, each one Owen accepts on its own terms, and show that the net position leaves him strictly worse off by a specific amount. Owen has to defuse it with something more technical than a shrug — make it turn on the difference between updating the whole set of priors when new information arrives and re-maximizing from scratch at each decision node, and on what condition his set of priors has to satisfy for those two things to come apart. Nadia gets to land a genuine hit here: point out precisely what Owen has to surrender to keep dynamic consistency and get his pump-free result, and press him on whether giving that up is cheaper than giving up the sure-thing principle was.

3. Nadia goes for the paralysis worry: if Owen's set of priors is the whole simplex of black/white splits, the worst case makes a bet paying $100 on black worth exactly zero, so he'd refuse to buy it for a dollar — which isn't aversion to ambiguity, it's refusal to act, and it makes a mockery of calling him ambiguity-averse rather than inert. Owen should show how a restricted set of priors generates a *reservation price* instead of a blank refusal, and quote his actual number for the $100-on-black bet and for the $100-on-red bet. Nadia then says why his restriction looks ad hoc and asks him what evidence could tighten it.

4. Close with one of them conceding something real — a narrow point, not a conversion. Cut it off there.

Two constraints on the arithmetic, please take them seriously. Owen's set of priors should be a symmetric interval of possible black proportions, so that he stays indifferent between the black bet and the white bet and still prefers red; and every reservation price he quotes has to actually fall out of the interval he claims to hold, with the 25/75 composition of the jar. If a number doesn't work, change the number rather than hand-waving past it.

Also: no literature dump. They can gesture at whose name is attached to what, but I don't want a survey or a list of papers — the pressure should come from the jar and the numbers, not from authority. Roughly 1200-1500 words. Oh and keep it conversational, these are two people who've been arguing about this seperate issue since the morning session and are on their second drink, so a bit of impatience and interruption is welcome.
