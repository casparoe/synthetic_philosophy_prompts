I'm a PhD student writing a chapter on the dispute between selected-effect and causal-role accounts of biological function, and our reading group got derailed last week by a genomics case. Someone brought up the ENCODE claim that most of the human genome — around eighty percent, if I remember the figure — is "functional" in the sense of being transcribed or bound by proteins, and a visiting biologist cut the discussion short with "functions are selected effects; that's simply what the word means." A third person said the whole exchange was verbal. I'd like that one sentence taken apart rather than either side defended.

Lay out, as a menu, the distinct claims the sentence could be making — for instance a stipulation about a term of art, a claim about how biologists actually use the word, and a claim about what makes malfunction intelligible — and for each say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, contentious, or flatly false. Then tell me which of those readings, if any, would actually dispose of the ENCODE-style usage, and which would leave it standing as a legitimate rival notion rather than a mistake.

Please be explicit about where the debate is settled and where it is genuinely contested among people who work on functions, and don't dress up a contested position as the consensus.
