Retired physicist here, thirty-five years on instrumentation, now reading epistemology for fun and getting annoyed in a productive way.

There's a pattern I saw constantly on committees and I can't find a word for it.

A panel has to produce one number. A safety limit, a recommended dose, the chance some failure mode happens in service. Everyone writes down their own private estimate, honestly. Nobody is copying anybody, nobody is scared of being the odd one out, there's no senior person in the room twisting arms. They average the estimates, maybe argue a little about outliers, and the result goes into the report as 'the panel's judgment.'

First thing I noticed: not one person in that room ever believed that number for a reason. It was arithmetic on five hunches. But the moment it's printed it gets cited exactly the way you would cite an expert opinion somebody argued for, and within a couple of years nobody remembers it started as a mean of gut feelings.

Second thing, and this is the bit I most want named: the next committee reads that report, takes the number as one of its inputs, mixes it with its own fresh hunches, averages again. Now two committees stand behind it. Then a third. Every round makes it look better supported and no round added anything at all. Also the inputs stopped being independent a long time ago, though everyone keeps treating them as if they were.

Third: if the group instead just publishes all five estimates as a spread, the whole effect seems to vanish. So it looks tied to being forced to emit a single point value.

I've been reading the peer disagreement material — the view that when you and someone equally informed land in different places your supposed to move halfway — and I have a hunch that what I'm describing is that view once you build it into an institution and run it a few generations. Is that right, or am I forcing a connection?

I don't want just a label. Tell me what the nearest established concepts are and exactly where each one stops fitting — I know there's a big literature on conformity and on crowds and on people going along to get along, and none of it applies here, because in my case everybody is perfectly honest and independent right up to the instant they pool. If nothing fits, coin something, define it, and tell me where the edges are: does it cover a majority vote instead of an average? Does it cover one person pooling their own conflicting reasons over a few years? Does the single-point-output requirement matter, or is that just my local prejudice from instrumentation work?
