I've been reading LessWrong and the Alignment Forum for the better part of a decade, and since then I've moved into a job as a research engineer on a small policy-gradient team — nothing exotic, mostly short runs on toy environments where I can actually inspect every tensor that flows through the system. That habit of reading the training loop line by line is why an argument we had last week has been stuck in my head, and I suspect it was largely verbal without being able to demonstrate that.

It started when I floated, half-jokingly, the thought that the reward stream of a trained agent might be the closest available functional candidate for what pleasure and pain do in us. One colleague cut me off with "reward is just a number," delivered in the tone of someone ending the conversation rather than starting one. Another colleague immediately argued the opposite: that the scalar is the single component of the architecture playing the role valence plays in animals, so if any part of the system deserves the word "feeling," that part does. They work on the same codebase and both know precisely what the object in question is, so they cannot have been disagreeing about the implementation. My guess is that "reward is just a number" is a compressed bundle of at least four different theses, and that the two of them were each grabbing a different one.

So: I want the bundle unpacked into a numbered menu I can circulate to the group, ordered from the weakest reading to the most ambitious. Please separate, at minimum, reward as the target of optimization; reward as a teaching signal in the actor-critic sense, where what actually shapes the policy is the difference between what was obtained and what was predicted rather than the scalar itself; reward as an informational channel the agent could in principle read and condition on; reward as evidence about its designers' values rather than about the agent's own; and reward as phenomenal valence. For each reading, state it precisely enough that it could be disputed, then say whether it is trivially true, substantive and plausible, genuinely contested, or flatly false, and what extra premise it would need before it could license either of my colleagues' conclusions. Be explicit about which readings contradict one another and which are perfectly compatible, since I suspect the disagreement dissolves once that is drawn out. I'd also like you to take a run at three probes I keep raising in these discussions without getting traction: if we replaced the scalar with draws from a fixed RNG uncorrelated with anything while leaving the trained agent's on-line behavior bit-identical, what would actually be lost; whether making the reward an explicit observable input to the policy brings it any closer to something "felt," or whether that change is beside the point; and if the training-time reward function were later swapped for a lookup table with no detectable behavioral difference, in what sense the agent is still pursuing reward. Finish by saying which of the readings, if any, would really support waving off welfare questions about trained systems as conceptual confusion, and which would instead make those questions urgent. I'm not after references or a literature tour — just the map, with verdicts attached.
