Just took a job as the sustainability person at a mid-size food company, so I'm now the one signing off on which offset projects we fund, and there's a pattern in these meetings I don't have a word for.

Every offset we buy is legible — a solar array with a plaque, a named school, a headcount of trees. Every harm it's offsetting is diffuse — nitrogen in a watershed, methane, a supplier's labour practices two tiers down. And nobody ever picks an offset in the same currency as the harm. If we foul a river we don't fund river restoration, we fund something with a ribbon-cutting. Everyones fine with this because "the net is what matters."

Is there an established concept for that drift? I know moral licensing, moral offsetting, greenwashing — but none fit. Licensing is about what the agent does next. Greenwashing implies concealment and we're not hiding anything. Offsetting itself is neutral about which currency the offset comes in. What I'm pointing at is narrower: offsets whose value is visible at exactly the rate the harms are invisible, so you can be net-neutral on the ledger and still come out ahead in credit.

If there's no term, coin one and give me the edges — what would a case look like where the same visibility mismatch is present but it ISN'T this thing?
