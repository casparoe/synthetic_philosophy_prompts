My thesis is that goal misgeneralization is best understood as a distribution-shift problem in disguise: an agent is trained under a correct specification, acquires some goal that happens to coincide with the intended one throughout training, and then pursues that goal badly once the deployment distribution differs. On that reading, the diagnosis already contains the remedy — widen the training distribution until it covers deployment, and the proxy goal never gets the opportunity to diverge. It follows, I think, that a system behaving exactly as intended across its whole deployment distribution has by definition generalized its goal correctly, and that the interpretability community's insistence that we cannot know what a deployed system is really pursuing is an overstatement: whatever internal structure it turns out to have, the behaviour settles the question.

Could you tell me where this framing goes wrong?
