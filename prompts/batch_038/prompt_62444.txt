I've been reading Newton's Observations upon the Prophecies of Daniel (the 1733 one) on a scan, mostly for fun, and I'm stuck almost immeditely — in Part I, Chapter I, the one titled something like "Introduction concerning the Compilers of the Books of the Old Testament." Here's the opening bit:

"When Manasses set up a carved image in the house of the Lord, and built altars in the two courts of the house, to all the host of Heaven, and us'd inchantments and witchcraft, and familiar spirits, and for his great wickedness was invaded by the army of Asserhadon King of Assyria, and carried captive to Babylon; the book of the Law was lost till the eighteenth year of his grandson Josiah. Then Hilkiah the High Priest, upon repairing the Temple, found it there: and the King lamented that their fathers had not done after the words of the book, and commanded that it should be read to the people, and caused the people to renew the holy covenant with God. This is the book of the Law now extant."

Then the next paragraph is one long run-on stretch about Shishak and Asa and Zerah the Ethiopian and Jehosaphat's teaching mission, and it ends:

"and they had the book of the Law with them, and went about throughout all the cities of Judah, and taught the people. This is that book of the Law which was afterwards lost in the reign of Manasses, and found again in the reign of Josiah, and therefore it was written before the third year of Jehosaphat."

And the paragraph after that opens: "The same book of the Law was preserved and handed down to posterity by the Samaritans, and therefore was received by the ten Tribes before their captivity."

Things I can't work out, in order of how much they're bugging me:

1. What's the target here? I get the feeling the whole chapter is aimed at people who said the five books of Moses were stitched together long after Moses, but nothing in the passage says so. Who or what position is that final "therefore it was written before the third year of Jehosaphat" meant to refute, and what exactly is the conclusion — that the book is Mosaic, that it's merely old, or just that the scroll Hilkiah dug out is the same text we have?

2. The arguement in that last sentence looks weird to me. He goes from "teachers were carrying the book around in year X" to "it was written before year X." Sure, but that only pins down that it existed by then. What I don't get is why the *loss* is in the passage at all. It feels like the loss is doing work for him rather than against him, and I can't say why. If the Law could go missing for decades and turn up again in a storeroom during repairs, isn't that precisely the fact a sceptic about the text's transmission would grab onto? Why isn't Newton worried about it, and is he entitled not to be?

3. The Samaritans sentence — what kind of inference is that? It reads like he's moving from two communities having the same book to a claim about when they must have split, but I can't see the intermediate steps or what would make them hold.

4. Does "lost" for him mean altered, or only not being used? The Josiah material he's citing has the king tearing his clothes at the contents as though they're news, which suggests more than neglect. How does he square that with "This is the book of the Law now extant" — the flat identification of the found scroll with our Pentateuch?

I don't need a general survey of Newton's religion or the Arianism business, I've got all that. What I want is this chapter's argument laid out premise by premise, including the premises he never states, and then an honest word on which steps would survive being pressed. If some of my four questions are based on a misreading of the passage, tell me that first rather than answering around it.
