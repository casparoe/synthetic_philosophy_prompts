You're a professional philosopher who works on infinite ethics — assume you've read everything and then some. Think carefully, and start with some brainstorming before you write anything polished: give me 6–8 bullets of raw ideas, including ones you suspect are bad, then the actual argument.

I need help defending a view I'm presenting at a workshop where roughly half the room will think it's morally monstrous. So the case has to be good enough to survive a hostile Q&A, not just internally tidy.

Call it Tailism. Setup: a world assigns a welfare number to each of countably infinitely many slots — generations, people, locations, index them 1, 2, 3, … Assume bounded welfare to keep the math polite.

(i) If two worlds differ at only finitely many slots, they are exactly equally good. Not very nearly equally good. Equally.
(ii) If they differ at infinitely many slots, rank them by the limiting average welfare over the first N slots as N → ∞, when both limits exist and differ. When the limits don't exist, or exist and come out equal, the worlds are incomparable — neither better, and not equally good either.

Test cases to keep in your head: ONE is 1 everywhere; TWO is 2 everywhere; SPIKE is 2 everywhere except slot 1, which is −10^9; DECAY is 1 + 1/n at slot n; STROBE alternates 1, 3, 1, 3, …

And the consequence I want you to own rather than bury: in an infinite world with a fixed tail, no finite amount of good or harm makes the world any better. Preventing the torture of a billion people for a billion years is, axiologically, on a par with preventing one stubbed toe, as long as the tail doesnt shift.

Now argue for Tailism. Specifically:

1. At least three positive arguments, and not all of them may be of the form "the rivals collapse." At least one should make tailism look affirmatively attractive — a reason to think finite differences shouldnt carry weight, not merely that giving them weight is expensive.
2. Take the two obvious rivals seriously. (a) The Banach-limit / ultrafilter move: extend the limiting-average functional to every bounded sequence by a finitely additive, shift-invariant measure. Notice it delivers the same verdict as clause (i) on finite differences, so it isn't any less bullet-biting — which means you owe me an account of what tailism's partiality is actually buying, and why refusing to output a number is a feature rather than a gap. (b) Keep Pareto and swallow incomparability or intransitivity everywhere else.
3. The billion-torture case, head on. Given that the axiology is silent, what does the tailist say about what to do? I don't want "deontology picks up the slack" — tell me where the reasons come from if not from value differences.
4. Clauses (i) and (ii) only ever talk about infinite worlds. Propose the hybrid with ordinary finite-population ethics and argue it's stable: is there a cliff where passing from a large finite world to an infinite one flips everything, and does that cliff make the view ad hoc?
5. Close with one paragraph of real self-attack — the place you think Tailism is most likely to break. I'd rather have that than a sales pitch.
