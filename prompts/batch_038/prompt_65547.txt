I'm running a third-year seminar on semantic externalism and I want to hand out a decision tree that sorts students by what they actually believe about reference, not by which label they've picked up in the reading. Start from the usual Twin Earth set-up — Oscar and Twin Oscar, XYZ, Putnam in 1975 — but make the branches turn on two cases of my own, which I'd like to be load-bearing:

(a) the two planets have been in radio contact for decades, each community has borrowed the other's word, and speakers on both sides sincerely intend their uses to co-refer;
(b) one individual who spent exactly half her life on each planet, with no dominant stretch on either side.

Every node should be a question a student can answer without knowing where it leads: no internalism/externalism, no mention of causal theories or two-dimensionalism, no philosophers' names in the questions themselves. Eight to ten nodes, and the leaves should include at least the causal-historical view, teleosemantics, two-factor theories, reference magnetism, the indexical/metalinguistic response, and whatever else you think genuinely belongs there.

Do the logical space first, then drop the named views into it. Tell me which combinations of answers come out empty, and for each one say whether that's because nobody's thought of it, because it's unstable, or because it collapses into a neighbouring view. And flag honestly any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate — I suspect a couple of these are only seperable in principle. British English please, and keep the whole thing to about a page.
