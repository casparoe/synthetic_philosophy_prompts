Six weeks ago I got handed ownership of the plain-language disclosures that go out with our loan offers (US fintech). I'm a marketing copywriter by training, not a lawyer. We get three or four customer complaints a week, all the same shape: nobody lied, the sentence is literally true, but the customer clearly acted on something they took it to be telling them. "Up to 24 months" read as usually 24. "Your rate is fixed for the first 12 months" read as also meaning nothing else changes after. "We may contact you" read as a promise that we will. My team has started calling these ghost promises, and honestly I cant tell if that's a real category or just us being spooked.

What I need is a procedure I can run on a sentence or short paragraph in about five minutes, before it goes to our compliance lawyer (who signs off but wont do the thinking for me), using whatever pragmatics actually gives you here: what's said versus what's implicated, which implicatures come off the words themselves versus off the context, cancellability, scalar terms, conditionals getting read in both directions. For each step I want the question to ask, what counts as a passing answer, what I do if it fails, and whether the step is really a judgment call wearing a procedure costume. Mark where the procedure stops and hands it to the lawyer because the remaining question is about risk rather than meaning. And be straight with me about two things I already suspect: that explicitly cancelling an implicature sometimes makes it louder, and that our plain-language rules (short sentences, one idea each, roughly 8th-grade level, no footnote longer than a line) rule out most of the fixes a Gricean would reach for.

Then run it on three cases: (1) "Your rate is fixed for the first 12 months." (2) "We may share your information with our servicing partners." (3) a case you pick where the procedure comes back saying the sentence is fine as written and I'm going to be annoyed about it. End by telling me whether ghost promise is worth keeping as a term, what it maps onto, or whether I should drop it.

Format: give the procedure as a table with columns step | question | passing answer | action if it fails | judgment call (yes/no). After the three worked cases, a JSON array with one object per case, fields: sentence, what_is_said, implicatures_triggered (array of {type: scalar | conditional_perfection | relevance | manner | other, gloss}), verdict (fix | fine | escalate), minimal_fix, cancellation_backfire_risk (string or null), ghost_promise (true/false).
