A friend of mine — intelligent, careful about most things — has concluded that a death our county coroner ruled an accident was in fact a killing, and that he knows roughly who was responsible. When I push back he says he has done his own research: he has read the filed incident reports, watched the council meetings, catalogued inconsistencies in the official timeline, and asked the family what the police told them. The phrase has become his universal reply. I notice it is also used approvingly by people who despise his conclusions, where it means checking primary documents rather than repeating headlines.

I would like the phrase taken apart before I go back to him. Set out the distinct claims that doing your own research could be standing for, as a list, ordered from the ones that are trivially true to the ones doing unearned work. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible epistemic principle, or false, and where it holds or breaks down. Then tell me which reading, if any, would carry him from justified distrust of an institution's testimony to a confident positive account of what happened — and which one he is most likely invoking when he uses it to close down our arguments rather than to continue them.
