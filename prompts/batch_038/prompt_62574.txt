So here's the situation. I'm on a team building negotiating agents that haggle with each other over how to split a shared resource, and I wrote the design doc arguing we should be targeting an equilibrium solution concept rather than something more ad hoc. One of the reviewers tore into it, basically: common knowledge of rationality is never attained in a distributed system, you can't get it in principle without perfect synchrony and no message loss, so your justification is empty — use a concept that only assumes bounded-depth beliefs. That sent me down the epistemic game theory rabbit hole for two months and I came out holding something stronger and probably more annoying than what I started with.

The view: CKR isn't a handy idealization that the relaxation literature has shown to be dispensible. It's a genuine presupposition of the solution concepts. When people point at results saying Nash doesn't actually need common knowledge of rationality — just a weaker package like mutual knowledge of rationality, common priors, and common knowledge of conjectures — or point at rationalizability as the thing that survives when you cut the belief hierarchy off at a finite depth, I don't think they've shown the epistemic story is optional. I think those weakened concepts have no independent normative grip; they look self-standing only because they inherit their plausibility from the full common-knowledge assumption they claim to replace. And the common p-belief ladder isn't a rival foundation, it's CKR with the volume turned down — the very fact that you can parameterize approximations by how much common knowledge you assume is what tells you the original notion was doing the work.

What would count against this? I want a map of where it's actually exposed.

- Which results would be genuinely decisive rather than absorbable? For each candidate, tell me whether my reflex reply — that it just smuggles the assumption back in at a different level — is a legitimate move or whether it's an escape hatch I should be embarrassed about.
- Learning and evolution: best-reply dynamics, fictitious play, no-regret learning converging to correlated equilibrium, evolutionary selection. If populations land on equilibrium outcomes without anyone modeling anyone's beliefs, does my claim collapse, or does convergence only ever pick out profiles that were already epistemically licensed?
- Experiments: level-k and cognitive hierarchy findings, and the fact that real people don't play backward induction in centipede or finitely repeated prisoner's dilemmas. How damaging are these, and damaging to which part — the claim that CKR is the presupposition, or the further claim that the relaxations don't rescue anything?
- The other direction: if taking CKR seriously in perfect-information games really does force the backward induction outcome, and that outcome looks absurd in the centipede, is that a defeater for CKR-as-presupposition or a reductio of the solution concept? Tell me which way to lean.
- What does my view predict that a relaxation view doesn't? If nothing, is that the tell that something's wrong?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each one say whether a committed defender of this view would concede it counts against them or dismiss it, and on what grounds. And give me a straight verdict on whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — if a presuppositional claim of this kind can't risk anything, is that a real problem for it or is it just what claims of that kind are like?

Please actually commit. Take a position on whether the view survives rather than laying out the options and letting me choose. If you think I'm wrong, say so plainly and name the one result that should have moved me already. If you think I'm right, tell me which piece of my formulation is load-bearing and which bit is just decoration.
