I'm a water rights lawyer, about fifteen years in, mostly prior appropriation states. I think in terms of seniority, beneficial use, the no-speculation rule, forfeiture and abandonment, injury to other appropriators, change applications, decreed rights, and the fact that what you hold isn't the water but a right to divert and apply a flow to a use. That vocabulary is completely natural to me and basically invisible, which is exactly why I want to use it as a test instrument.

Take the fight over who has a right to the city — zoning, NIMBYism, housing — and restate it in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Three positions, one at a time: (a) the long-time homeowner who wants to control what gets built next door and treats that as an entitlement they earned by being here and by investing; (b) the abundant-housing position that exclusionary zoning is hoarding a scare resource and dressing the hoarding up as a property right; (c) the Lefebvre-flavored claim that the city belongs to its inhabitants rather than its owners, and that what people are owed is a say in how urban space gets produced, not a share of its exchange value.

For each one, give me the mapping — what the position is actually claiming once it's said in appropriation terms, and what my doctrine would call it — and then a three-part residue report: what translates cleanly, what only translates if you bend the doctrine out of shape, and what has no expression at all. Flag every place where you're borrowing an assumption from water law (rivalry, depletion, a fixed supply, the state as distributor, the need for a decree) and letting it do work the housing claim never asked it to do. Don't smuggle my framework's premises in and then report the resulting mismatch as a discovery about them.

Then weigh in on a few specifics. Does beneficial use plus anti-speculation plus forfeiture already deliver most of what the YIMBY side argues for on moral grounds, or is that too cheap a win? What does my vocabulary make of a renter of twelve years with no title — is there any slot for them, or does the framework simply not see them? And if there's a claim in this debate that appropriation can only express by ceasing to be appropriation, name it and say why.

Test the translation on exactly two cases: someone who bought in 1985 and now opposes a fourplex next door, and someone who owns three units and keeps one empty. If your doctrinal details would be shaky outside the general framework, say so rather than inventing rules — I know this area and I'd rather have the gaps marked.

No reading list, no citations, I want the translation and the residue. Call it a page and a half.
