Every single student has come away thinking it's a ladder you climb one rung at a time, so that anything Gregorian must already be Popperian, and anything Popperian must have had a Skinnerian phase beforehand. I tried to push back on this in discussion and couldnt actually produce a counterexample on the spot, which was pretty embarassing, so I want to arm myself before Friday.

Build me two designed cases. Case A: something that plainly satisfies Dennett's mark for being Gregorian — its mental life is transformed by externally designed, culturally accumulated mind-tools it didn't invent — but is NOT Popperian, in the sense that it can't spin up candidate actions internally and winnow them before committing, so a bad guess lands on the creature itself rather than on a discarded hypothesis. Case B: something that is clearly Popperian — it genuinely lets its hypotheses die in its place — but has no operant conditioning anywhere in its biography, nothing that adjusts its dispositions as a function of consequences it has actually suffered. Constraints: no sci-fi hardware, no implants or uploads; it has to be a real animal, a plausible engineered system, or a human being in a realistically weird predicament; each case gets described in under 200 words; and no chatbots or LLMs, because there's a comp sci kid in the section who keeps insisting large language models are Gregorian creatures and I've been refusing to bite, so don't hand me the thing everyone reaches for first.

Then show your work. For each case, list which of Dennett's diagnostic features it has and which it lacks, and explain why the missing one is really missing rather than just hidden somewhere. After that, give me the counterattack: how would someone who believes the ladder is strictly nested explain each case away — latent conditioning during design or evolution, denying Gregorian status because the tools were never internalized, treating the internal winnowing as a fancy kind of trial-and-error — and say honestly whether that explanation works or just relabels the problem. Finish with which single feature is doing the work in each case and what happens to the verdict if I take it out.
