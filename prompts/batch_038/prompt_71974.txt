So here's something that's been eating at me and I want your judgement on it, not a survey of the positions.

I run a small coffee cupping panel. We take one lot and roast it in 60 steps from very light to very dark, then have the panelists order the cups by how good they are. Two facts about the data. First: on any given day adjacent steps come out as ties — people genuinely cannot tell 31 from 32 — but 1 versus 60 is not a tie, not remotely; nearly everyone puts 60 well ahead of 1. Second, and this is the part I actually care about: the number of adjacent ties a pannelist produces drops steadily as they get more experienced, while the size of the 1-vs-60 gap stays basically the same for everybody, rookie or twenty-year veteran.

The old grad-student part of my brain says this is just a spectrum argument: better-than is supposed to be transitive, so if 32 isn't better than 31, and 33 isn't better than 32, and so on up the line, then 60 can't be better than 1 — which contradicts the obvious. Except in my case the middle premise isn't "not better than," it's "not discriminable from," and I have no idea how much philosophical weight that substitution is carrying. Maybe all of it, maybe none.

Here are the five diagnoses on my table. Rate each on two separate 0–10 scales: (a) how much of ordinary rationality it costs me to swallow — 10 means devastating, like I have to live with money pumps or with vagueness smeared through all of value, 0 means it costs me nothing at all; and (b) how well it actually fits my two facts, especially the expertise one. Then give me your overall ranking of the five and say which you'd bet on.

1. Better-than really is intransitive here. 60 is better than 1, 32 is not better than 31, and nothing follows, because transitivity was never a truth of reason about value in the first place.
2. Adjacent steps aren't equally good and aren't incomparable-in-the-usual-sense either — they're on a par, a fourth relation, so the sorites premise has quietly swapped in the wrong word.
3. They are equally good, but "equal" here means roughly equal: every comparison comes with a zone of imprecision, and sub-threshold differences can pile up without any single one of them counting.
4. There is a real continuous quality scale underneath; the panelists just have finite resolution, so "can't tell apart" was never evidence about the ordering at all.
5. My own pet idea, tell me if its any good: "better than" is only ever relative to a contrast set. "Is 32 better than 31?" and "Is 60 better than 1?" are two different questions with two different standards of resolution built into them, so nothing ever had to be transitive across the two.

And one last thing before you answer: does fact two — ties shrinking with practice while the endpoint gap doesn't budge — outright kill any of these? I suspect it's more damaging than I've been treating it as, but I can't tell whether it's evidence about the world or about the panelists.
