So I've basically signed up for the Dennett line on what exists: something is real just in case it's a real pattern — a regularity in the data that some description picks out which lets you compress and predict better than any description that leaves it out. And I've gone a step further than I think Dennett himself goes: I cash the criterion out in minimum-description-length terms, so positing an entity is justified just in case the bits you save on the data exceed the bits it costs to state the entity. On top of that I hold that the physical, design, and intentional stances are three pattern-finding strategies and none of them is ontologically privileged — beliefs and desires are real in exactly the same sense that centers of gravity and quarks are, and they differ only in how the compression shakes out.

Tell me what would count against this. Ranked, not a list — I want to know which defeaters are fatal and which are merely awkward to answer at a party. Things I especially want hit:

- Is the criterion too permissive, too restrictive, or both? What would it force me to call real that obviously isnt, and what would it force me to deny the reality of that I don't want to deny? I know constellations and gerrymandered disjunctive kinds are the standard moves, so I want to know whether there's a case where I genuinely have no reply, or whether every such case can be handled by pointing out that the compression gain is real but tiny.

- The MDL step specifically. Compression is only well defined relative to a chosen description language, and Im pretty sure the equivalence between languages holds only up to an additive constant, which is harmless asymptotically and useless for exactly the short, individual descriptions that metaphysics turns on. Does that make existence language-relative in a way that sinks the view? Is there a fix that isn't just "pick the best language" — because "pick the best language" looks circular from where I'm sitting, since picking the best language already looks like picking what's real.

- Does the whole thing collapse? Into instrumentalism (patterns are useful fictions and I've just dressed that up), into a triviality on which everything is real because everything is compressible relative to some scheme or other, or into something that isn't metaphysics at all but a thesis about modeling practice. If it collapses, say into which, and show me the collapse rather than asserting it.

- The exclusion-style charge: once the microphysical facts are fixed, the higher-level pattern does no causal work, so calling it real is at best a manner of speaking. I want to know whether that's a genuine defeater for a compression-based ontology or whether it smuggles in a notion of causal work that I've already declined to accept — and whether I'm entitled to decline it, or whether declining it costs me the one thing that made the view attractive, namely that it was supposed to earn its keep by explaining why higher-level talk works.

- What does my view predict or license that the rivals don't? If there's nothing it gets right that a plain microphysicalism plus instrumentalism about the special sciences also gets right, that seems like the deepest problem, and I'd like an honest answer on whether that's the situation.

Then the meta-question, and please don't soften it: has this view been insulated by its own flexibility? It looks like it can absorb almost anything by relocating the pattern, redoing the cost accounting, or telling the objector they're asking for the wrong kind of thing. Is that a fatal vice for an ontology, or is that just what any general account of existence has to look like, and how would I tell the difference from the inside? 

Finally, give me a test case that isn't one of the stock ones — some situation where my criterion delivers a crisp verdict about what exists and the verdict is genuinely uncomfortable, ideally one where I'd be tempted to bite the bullet and then couldnt live with it. And close by telling me what you'd bring me first if your goal were to make me abandon the view outright rather than merely qualify it.
