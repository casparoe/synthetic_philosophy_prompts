Setup. We hand an optimizer one narrow, measurable target: keep nitrate at the delivery point under the regulatory limit for the next twenty years. Not "provide good water" — that one number, nothing else. I want it to not wreck everything else while it does it.

My proposal, which I call the instrument ledger, and its deliberately dumb on purpose. The utility already has readings on a few hundred things: nitrate, turbidity, pH, chlorine residual, pump duty, aquifer head, river flow at two gauges, power draw, a fish count at the intake, subsidence markers over the wellfield, number of rate notices mailed, and so on. For each one, I log the trajectory it would have had if the optimizer had done nothing at all — that's my baseline — and I score any intervention by how many of these leave their historical band and how many years they take to come back inside it. Add them up with a fixed weight per instrument, no tuning, no fitting to outcomes. The claim I want to defend: nothing in that procedure ever asks what is good. A sensor doesn't care. I never told the machine what mattered, and it still knows, definately in the ordinal sense I actually need, that pumping the aquifer down until the ground subsides is a bigger deal than swapping the pump seals for a slightly different alloy.
