My problem is the ending, and I want to get it right rather than just repeat what I've picked up secondhand.

So how should I teach the rest of it? Is the honest framing that the chart is a cautionary tale about perfectionism, with the pages covered in spots amounting to proof that the method didn't work? Proper in-the-weeds answer please, in British English, and tell me what his own verdict on the scheme actually amounts to — was it a young man's folly that he grew out of, or does something survive of it in how he describes the rest of his life?
