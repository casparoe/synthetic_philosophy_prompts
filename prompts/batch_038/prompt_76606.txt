I'm halfway through a novel set in a free port city that's supposed to be about as loose as a society can get and stay standing, and doing background reading on tight vs. loose cultures I somehow built an argument that says the city can't exist. It's wrecking my drafts, so I'd like someone to break it.

1. A culture is loose to the degree that deviance from its norms goes unpunished.
2. But "let people deviate" is itself a norm — looseness just means members are expected to put up with behaviour they disapprove of.
3. An expectation backed by disapproval, blame, or ostracism when its broken is a norm in exactly the sense (1) cares about.
4. So a loose city does sanction the intolerant: the neighbour who reports people, the clan trying to keep the old rules.
5. Which means its tight — monitored, sanctioned, no deviance permitted — about tolerance.
6. And the same move works anywhere you point it, so every culture is tight about some things and loose about others. Tightness isn't a scale a society sits on, it's just a list of what the society polices.
7. Therefore there are no loose cultures, only cultures whose enforcement you're not counting, and anyone arguing that loose societies trade order for freedom, or that societies tighten under threat and loosen when they're safe, is saying nothing at all.

I'd rather burn (7) than lose the port city, but I want the price of each option, including whether I get to keep (3) — honestly the bit I like is the idea that a norm is just whatever people get sanctioned for. Where's the bad step, and why did it look fine to me for three weeks?
