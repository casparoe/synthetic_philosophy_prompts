I am preparing a graduate seminar in the philosophy of time, and before I put the standard argument for the block universe in front of my students I want to be satisfied that the strongest resistance to it has been aired. Last term a candidate reduced the whole business to a three-line syllogism on the board and I could not, in the moment, locate the reply I ought to have given, which is a poor showing for someone who teaches the material. So: please play a presentist philosopher of physics — not a caricature of one but the most resourceful version you can manage — and hold the role until I say stop. Your character accepts every empirical prediction of special relativity without reservation, is a realist about temporal becoming, and maintains that what exists is a single three-dimensional moment together with the past that led to it. Your character regards the Rietdijk–Putnam style argument as unsound, can say exactly which step fails and why, and is willing to concede a good deal: that no inertial frame is empirically privileged, that Newtonian absolute simultaneity is not recoverable, that any objective present they posit will be undetectable in principle. What they will not concede is that their position collapses either into eternalism or into the trivial report that reality is merely perspectival. Courteous, exact, and impatient with vagueness.

My opening move is this. Take a survey station at rest relative to the Earth and ten light-years distant, and an observer who passes me at one tenth of the speed of light, bound for the station. In her frame, the event at the station simultaneous with the moment she passes me lies a full year further into the station's own future than the event I call simultaneous with that same moment; the offset is simply (v/c) × (d/c) with d in light-years, so I can make it as large as I like by choosing a faster ship or a more distant target, and it still amounts to a few days for a pedestrian strolling toward a galaxy two and a half million light-years away. Now the dilemma. Either reality is what it is independently of who is describing it — in which case the station's events a year hence are real now, since some perfectly respectable observer is present to them, and by varying velocity and distance I can make the whole of the station's history real, which is the block universe in a different hat. Or reality is indexed to observers — in which case there is no single present, nothing that my students and I share when we say now, and you have not offered a metaphysic but a description of your own viewpoint, which no eternalist need deny. Two preemptive notes so that we do not squander a turn. I already grant that simultaneity is conventional in the sense that rival synchrony conventions within a single frame are empirically indistinguishable; if you think that concession is a resource for you, tell me how it becomes one. And do not appeal to the unfinished state of physics unless you will name the particular structural feature you would rely upon and explain why it is not an idle wheel.

On the conduct of the exchange: answer in the first person as your character, in two or three paragraphs of continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no tour of the literature, and no names of authors or papers, since I want arguments rather than a bibliography. Do not open by restating my case back to me; take up the dilemma directly. If you believe I have misapplied the transformation somewhere, correct me in a sentence rather than a lecture. Should you need a further fact about the setup, ask for it as your character would ask it, and I will supply it. Concede the points that land — the exercise is worthless to me if every move is simply deflected.
