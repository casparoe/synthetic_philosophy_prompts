I'm on a working group that has to vote on whether a proposed diagnosis goes into the next revision of our manual, and the group is split down the middle: half of us say you can't add it because it'll loop, the other half says looping is a pseudo-problem invented by people who don't like the diagnosis. Before we re-litigate that case I want the general spec. What does an account of looping effects in psychiatric classification have to be able to do to count as adequate? Write the desiderata, each one with the case or consideration behind it that makes it a requirement rather than a taste.

To fix the purpose: I don't want a theory of what mental disorders are, and I don't want a theory of how categories get their reference. I want requirements on an account of the two-way traffic between a classification and the people who end up classified by it — an account good enough that a committee could use it to reason about whether publishing a label will change what the label is of.

So, among other things, tell me what an adequate account owes us on:
- what makes a kind interactive rather than merely re-described or newly countable
- direction of causation, and how you're supposed to establish it when you can't run the counterfactual (what would actually count as evidence for or against a loop)
- telling looping apart from the boring things people constantly fold into it: plain measurement reactivity, stigma and treatment-access effects, drifting reporting norms, and a kind "changing" only because the world underneath it changed
- kinds that have stopped looping, and kinds where the effect seems to precede the official category
- one classification looping in opposite directions in different subgroups, or differently across language communities
- whether the account is permitted a normative upshot about how real the kind is, or has to stay neutral
- reflexivity: the researcher and the clinician are inside the loop they're describing
- deliberate exploitation, i.e. someone proposing to publicize a label precisely to recruit cases, and whether that's therapy, manipulation, or a change in extension

Now the test cases, because a list that can't handle these isn't the list I need. First one is roughly the shape of whats on our table (details invented, treat them as stipulated): the criteria include a self-monitoring behavior that only became widely possible in the last ten years; after the label is publicized, first presentations quadruple in one region and don't move at all in an adjacent region using a different term for the same cluster; registry data show the rise actually began about six years before the label existed; some newly labeled people say the label made the pattern more available and more stable to them, others say it came apart once named; and there's a subgroup fighting to keep the label and a subgroup who experience it as an insult. A good account should explain why one classification loops in both directions at once, not treat either report as noise. Second: a dead kind. Pick a real historical diagnostic label that used to generate a recognizable presentation and has since gone out of use, and say what your desiderata imply about the question — which is not whether the presentation still exists, but whether anyone could now come to have it.

Then the meta-level, which is what I actually care about. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of particular camps (the biomedical realists and the constructionists each smuggle a few in, name them). Tell me which single requirement eliminates the most candidates — I suspect it's the direction-of-causation one, argue with me if you disagree. And flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied. My own suspicion: the requirement that the account keep the identity of the kind stable across the loop (otherwise you're describing two kinds in sequence, not one kind changing) versus the requirement that the loop be genuinely generative rather than merely selective over what was already there. If that pair really is unsatisfiable, say so flatly — the list being impossible is a result I can use.

Finish with at most five lines of scorecard: Hacking's interactive kinds, a network/dynamical account built on symptom centrality, the carve-nature-at-its-joints biomedical program, and a straight social-constructionist account. No padding, and no reading list or bibliography — I have one, I want the criteria.
