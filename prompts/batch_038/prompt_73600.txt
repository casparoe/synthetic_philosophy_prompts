A popular article on personal identity that I read last week summarized the debate over origins like this:

'Kripke argued that your origins are essential to you: there is no possible situation in which you come from a different sperm and a different egg, because your identity is fixed by your DNA, which is fixed at fertilization. He took this to follow from the logic of identity itself -- you are identical to yourself in every possible world, and you are the organism that developed from those particular gametes, so in any world in which you exist, that organism exists and began from them. The thesis is not the converse, so it is not threatened by identical twins, each of whom has an origin of their own. Critics complain that the argument is question-begging, but that only reflects a failure to grasp the intuition: everyone agrees that a child conceived from different gametes a month later would be your sibling rather than you. Origin essentialism is therefore settled, and it is what makes Parfit's non-identity argument go through.'
