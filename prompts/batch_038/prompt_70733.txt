writing up my position statement for a bayesian epistemology reading group and I want it stress-tested before I hand it in, because I've accumulated these claims over a couple of years and I can no longer tell which ones are load-bearing. Here's the package, roughly in the order it makes sense to me:

1. Radical probabilism, in the strong sense. No probability I hold is ever safe from revision and experience never hands me a certainty. There are no observation sentences with privileged status; the structure hangs together by coherence and nothing else.

2. Jeffrey conditionalization is the general update rule, not a patch bolted onto conditionalization. When experience shifts my probabilities across a partition {E1,...,En}, the new P(H) is the old P(H|Ei) weighted by the new P(Ei) and summed over the cells. Plain conditionalization is just the special case where one cell goes to 1.

3. Rigidity is part of what (2) means, not an extra assumption smuggled in. The update holds the old conditionals P(H|Ei) fixed. If someone tells me the conditionals moved too, they aren't describing a Jeffrey update, they're describing something else, and I'm inclined to say they've changed the subject.

4. What makes (2) right, for me, is minimality: of all the probability functions matching the new input values on the partition, the JC one is the unique function closest to the old, in the divergence sense. I take that to be a theorem and not a slogan, and I take it to be the whole of the justification. Everything else is downstream.

5. I don't accept Dutch book arguments. I don't think credences are betting dispositions and I don't think being pumpable for money is what's wrong with incoherence, so norms argued for by way of bets don't move me at all.

6. A case I'm confident about: I'm looking at a card and a colleague mentions the lighting is heavily red-tinted. Nothing about my visual experience changes, it looks exactly as red as it did a second ago. My P(the card is red) should still drop. And since the looks haven't changed, what dropped is my P(red | it looks that way to me) — I trust the look less. So here's an update whose input is a change in a conditional, not a change in the probability of any observation sentence.

7. I think the problem of old evidence is a non-problem on my view. Since I'm never certain of anything, my P(E) for something I've known for years can drift, and when it drifts JC can move P(H) without my acquiring any new evidence. Standard example: I've known since I was an undergrad that relativity implies the perihelion advance, and then over a year of working on the theory I come to find it much more compelling. P(theory is true) goes up, nothing new came in.

8. I'm unbothered that JC is undefined when the old P(Ei) is 0 or 1. That's a feature: given (1), I claim that case never arises for me.

Second, I expect a few of these look contradictory on the surface and turn out to be fine; tell me which ones those are and why they only look bad, since I'd rather not spend my slot defending a non-problem.
