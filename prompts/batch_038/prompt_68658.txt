I'm in a political theory seminar on sortition and we just did Rousseau, Social Contract Book IV chapter 3 — the elections one, in the Cole translation. I can't quote it properly (book's at my roommate's and I don't want to mangle the wording), so here's my reconstruction of the argument as I read it:

(1) appointing rulers isn't really an expression of the general will — it's a function of government rather than of sovereignty, since the general will only says there should be a government, not who runs it; (2) there are two ways of doing it, choice and lot, and lot is naturally the mark of a democracy while choice is naturally the mark of an aristocracy; (3) in a genuine democracy there's such equality of wealth, rank and merit that it makes hardly any difference who gets picked, so lot has no real downsides; (4) lot offends no one, because it leaves every citizen an equal claim to serve the country and makes the appointment depend on nothing particular about any person; (5) where the government preserves itself — aristocracy — the rulers pick the rulers, and that's where choice belongs; (6) mixed governments use both, for different kinds of office.

Two things I can't make sense of. First, why is (4) supposed to support lot rather than, say, rotation or very short terms? And the obvious objection is that lot hands office to whoever's worse at it, so I don't see how "offends no one" does the work unless (3) is already doing it. Second, and this is the one for my paper: the whole thing seems to run backwards from the modern case for lottocracy. Our other readings defend sortition on descriptive representation, cognitive diversity, and keeping career politicians out — none of which Rousseau invokes; his reasons look like they only work in a state small and homogeneous enough that competence differences have vanished. So is he actually an ally for citizens'-assembly people, or is he arguing that lot is only tolerable under conditions modern proposals could never meet?

Can you walk me through it as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no conclusion: which of (1)–(6) is doing the real work in tying lot to democracy specifically, why (4) is supposed to follow, whether the competence objection defeats the argument or gets dissolved by the equality premise in (3), which modern justification for sortition Rousseau's reasoning can and can't support, and finally your verdict on ally vs. cautionary case.
