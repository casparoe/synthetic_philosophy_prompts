I am assembling a set of criteria for evaluating proposed solutions to the bootstrapping problem, and I want the criteria in hand before I read any of the solutions. Please write in American English.

The problem, stated so that it does not depend on any particular author's formulation: Roxanne has a gas gauge that she has no independent reason to regard as trustworthy. She glances at it and forms the belief that the tank is three-quarters full; she notes that what the gauge displayed and what she came to believe about the tank agree; she repeats this over many fill-ups; and she then infers, by a straightforward enumerative step, that the gauge is reliable. Each individual belief is warranted — at least given that the gauge is in fact reliable, which we are granting — and the final step is deductively valid. Yet it seems she has gained no warrant whatever for the conclusion. Call whatever explains that appearance the anti-bootstrapping constraint.

The question: what must an account of epistemic warrant deliver in order to satisfy that constraint without paying too high a price elsewhere?

Write the requirements list. For each item give the case or consideration that motivates it, and state plainly whether it is non-negotiable or a demand made by only one camp. Please cover at least the following, though you should add, merge, or reject items as your judgment dictates:

- The legitimate self-checking practices the constraint must not accidentally condemn: confirming induction by induction; using perception to assess the general reliability of perception; a clinician who calibrates a new instrument against observations made with the instruments already in use; a laboratory validating its own measurement pipeline; any system that audits a sensor using data that sensor produced.
- Sources for which no independent check is available even in principle, and what the account must say about them.
- Closure: whether warrant transmits through known deduction, and if the account restricts transmission, exactly which principle is given up and what is offered in its place.
- Degrees: whether the bootstrapper gains any confirmation short of full warrant, and whether the account requires a threshold.
- Whether the easy-knowledge problem (coming to know the table is red while in no position to rule out that it is red-lit) and the bootstrapping problem are one problem or two, and which requirement settles the matter.
- The demand that warrant for a basic belief not depend on antecedent warranted belief about the reliability of its source, on pain of regress.
- What the account says when the gauge is systematically biased in a way Roxanne cannot detect, and whether the verdict is supposed to differ in that case from the unbiased one.
- A requirement concerning agent-relativity: whether the verdict may differ between two agents with the same evidence and the same genuinely reliable source, and what that implies for the possibility of their disagreeing.

Then tell me which single requirement knocks out the most candidate accounts, and identify any subset of the requirements you believe cannot be jointly satisfied. My own suspicion is that warrant for basic beliefs without antecedent warrant for the source's reliability, closure of warrant under known deduction, and the anti-bootstrapping constraint cannot all hold at once. Say whether you agree; if you do, say which of the three should be abandoned and what the resulting cost is.

Finally, and briefly — the scoring is not the point, so keep it to a few sentences — how a plain reliabilist account, an account that adds an internalist no-defeater condition, and a Bayesian treatment framed in terms of degrees of evidential support fare against your list.

The purpose I am fixing adequacy to is this: I want an account that would also license or forbid a machine's self-certification of its own perceptual module, where the module produced the very data used to certify it. Any requirement that can be discharged only by appeal to introspective access to one's own grounds should be flagged as unavailable for that purpose.
