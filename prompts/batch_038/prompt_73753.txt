I'm a first-year philosophy of physics PhD student and I've been going in circles on the Past Hypothesis — the posit that the early universe sat in a freakishly low-entropy macrostate, which is supposed to be where all of time's asymmetry comes from once you grant that the microdynamics is time-reversal invariant and the uniform measure over a macroregion is time-symmetric too. I have to give the commentary at a reading group next week and I want to argue it's a cheat. Specifically: if you take the low-entropy condition seriously as a constraint on an early *slice* rather than a fact about the first instant of time — which is what you're forced to do the moment there's a bounce or any pre-inflationary era in view — then the package delivers entropy rising away from that slice in both directions, and the only way to get back a one-headed arrow is to have already assumed which end of the block counts as initial, i.e. to smuggle in the very asymmetry the program was supposed to derive. Make the strongest case for that you can: spell out exactly which step does the smuggling, why the standard move of saying "there just isn't any earlier time" doesn't rescue it, and what the two-headed picture would predict about records and memory if we lived near an entropy minimum. Don't hedge into 'of course there are responses to this', I can read those myself. And be concrete about the measure-theory, not just slogan-level.
