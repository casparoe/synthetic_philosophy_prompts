So, background: I'm a backend/ML engineer, zero philosophy training, but I've been reading around embedded agency on and off for a couple years and I'm applying for a part-time MPhil where they want a 1500-word research proposal with the application. I have the seed of an idea and I need help turning it into something that doesn't read like a crank wrote it on a bus.

The seed: pretty much every discussion of embedded agency I've come across is about the agent not being able to fully model itself, because it's inside the thing it's modeling. Map is part of the territory, self-reference problems, no complete self-model, etc. Fine. But that's an *impossibility* framing and I don't think it's the interesting half. The interesting half is that for some class of self-models, the model isn't just possibly-wrong, it's causally upstream of the thing it's about. If a system comes to believe "I'm the kind of agent that finishes what it starts," that belief changes whether the claim is true. The accuracy target moves when you aim at it. I run into this constantly in practice — you drop a line into a model's prompt describing what it's like and the behavior shifts; adding a monitor changes what's being monitored; and so on.

So my question is roughly: when a self-model is self-fulfilling (or self-defeating) rather than inert, what replaces "true" as the standard you evaluate it by? And is there even a choice to make there, or is the whole framing confused because the belief-forming mechanism is itself embedded and not something the agent gets to steer?

What I need the proposal to do:

- State the question precisely enough that a reasonable person could disagree with my answer to it. Right now I think it's mushy.
- Get clear on what separates a self-fulfilling self-model from an inert one. Is that binary, graded, time-indexed, dependent on how tight the feedback loop is? I suspect subtleties here I'm not seeing.
- Lay out the candidate replacements (truth at the fixed point? stability under self-application? something I haven't thought of?) and say what considerations would decide between them.
- Take the two objections seriously: that this is just Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy in a trenchcoat, and the deflationary escape — if the model makes itself true then it *is* true and there's no puzzle left. I'd rather the proposal survive those than dodge them.
- Say what a good result looks like and what would count as the project failing.
- Be honest about whether this fits in one year and a dissertation, and what to cut if it doesn't.

No bibliography or lit review please, I'll add references myself later. I know Merton, and I vaguely remember something about option-pricing models making markets start behaving like the model, and there's obviously a pile of recent stuff on LLM self-models, but I'd rather it stood on the argument than on name-dropping. Also don't write it in that breathless style where every sentence has a colon in it — it should sound like a person, and it's fine for it to admit where the idea is still fuzzy.
