The position: neglectedness should be struck from the importance / tractability / neglectedness triad, because it isn't a third property of a cause area at all. It's a claim about how other funders and researchers would respond if you moved in, and once you say it that plainly it turns out to be either redundant with tractability or a claim about the elasticity of total resources in a field, which has nothing to do with how good the cause is.

Make the argument for me. I want:

- a diagnosis of what question neglectedness is actually being recruited to answer (something like: what's the expected marginal value of the next unit of effort I add?), and why the headcount answer — "how many people are already working on this" — is a bad proxy for that. There's an issue about what counts as one unit of intervention, and I think the score for a cause shifts depending on whether you frame the unit as a year of one person's career, a million dollars, or a whole new org. Work that in if you can do it cleanly.

- an argument that the standard cases people wave at in favor of neglectedness (crowded fields vs empty ones, the "but if everybody did this" intuition, the discovery-of-new-evidence cases) are better explained by diminishing marginal returns plus uncertainty about how much good is left to do — both of which are tractability-side claims, not a separate criterion.

- the best two or three objections from someone defending the triad, including the worry that dropping neglectedness licenses pile-ons into whatever currently looks highest-EV, and the worry that it's doing anti-herding work that naive expected value reasoning structurally can't do, because the herding is in the estimates themselves and not in the world. Then my replies. Don't strawman these — the group is smart and I'd rather lose on good objections than win on bad ones.

- a closing section on whether the triad survives as a checklist even if the argument goes through, since I'm pretty sure someone will say "fine, maybe it's not a real third factor, but it's still a useful prompt to make people look up." I want to know if that concession is cheap or costly.
