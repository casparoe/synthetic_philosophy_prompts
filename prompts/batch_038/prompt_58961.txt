I am co-teaching a graduate seminar on social ontology and the foundations of the social sciences, and one session collapses into the same unproductive place every year: methodological individualism against methodological holism. My co-instructor is a rational-choice individualist of a fairly sophisticated sort — he accepts without reservation that social properties supervene on individual ones and that there is no group mind anywhere in the picture — while I lean towards the view that some social explanations are irreducible in a sense worth taking seriously. We both agree that the standard textbook illustrations are useless for teaching the disagreement. The Schelling-style segregation models get waved through as a triumph of individualism, since the macro pattern there is generated by nothing over and above aggregated micro motives. The Durkheimian examples — suicide rates, anomie, the social fact — either invite the objection that they smuggle in a collective consciousness, or they turn on the actors' ignorance of the forces moving them, which my co-instructor happily concedes is a fact about individuals' beliefs rather than a fact about societies. The seminar has fourteen students, split roughly evenly between sociology and economics, and I want a replacement case that both halves can engage with without specialist knowledge.

So: construct a thought experiment to specification. It should be a single realistic institutional setting — a hospital, a manufacturing firm, a school, a professional association, a local market — described in one paragraph of no more than about two hundred words, in which a stable macro-level regularity persists over time and across a substantial turnover of personnel. The constraints are these. (i) No group minds, collective consciousness, emergent agents, or any personification of the collectivity. (ii) No coordinating device that institutes the pattern: no rule, contract, committee, leader, or explicit norm backed by sanctions. (iii) Every individual's behaviour must be fully explicable by ordinary, transparently motivated reasoning, and the actors must understand what they are doing, so that the case cannot lean on false consciousness or on hidden functions. (iv) No aggregation-of-micro-motives story of the Schelling kind, since that concedes the point rather than making it. (v) The irreducibility must be exhibited rather than asserted: show me at least two genuinely distinct individual-level realisations of the same macro pattern, both of them ordinary psychology, so that multiple realisability is demonstrated inside the case rather than claimed about it from outside. The case is for the following purpose, and this fixes what counts as cheating: it must be one in which the individualist can still tell a complete causal story at the level of individuals — nothing in the mechanism is causally opaque to him — and yet the macro-level explanation is not recoverable from that complete story without loss. If the only way to make the loss appear is to stipulate it, the case fails.

After the scenario, do the demonstration. State the explanandum precisely, then give the individual-level causal account in full, then say exactly what the macro-level account adds that the individual account cannot deliver. Distinguish explicitly among ontological reduction, causal or mechanistic reduction, and explanatory reduction, and tell me which of the three the case is designed to defeat — I suspect it can only be the third, and I want to know whether conceding that is a real concession or an empty one. Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work, and tell me what happens to both verdicts if that feature is removed or merely weakened. Then give my co-instructor his best reply: how a supervenience-friendly individualist can accept every descriptive claim in the case while insisting that nothing whatever has been shown against individualism, and whether that reply is a genuine answer or a refusal to engage with the question I actually asked. Finally, tell me whether any intervention, measurement, or experimental result could distinguish the two programmes within this case; if the answer is no, say what the residual disagreement consists in once all the empirical content has been agreed on both sides. Please write in British English, define each technical term on first use, and pitch the exposition at advanced undergraduates. No bibliography, no reading list, and no citations: name a thinker only where the name genuinely earns its place, and do not attribute claims, dates, or quotations to particular works.
