The essay should address this question: can a reinforcement-learning agent suffer? The usual debate strikes me as stuck, because both sides point at the same thing and mean different things by it. sceptics say a reward is just a scalar written into a buffer, and a number cannot hurt; defenders say that if a system organises all of its behaviour around avoiding some internal state and pursuing another, the fact that the state happens to be representable as a float is beside the point. But in practice the quantity that actually drives learning in my agents is not the reward at all. It is the reward prediction error, and behind that the value function estimating expected future return. So I want the essay to treat three distinct candidates for being the seat of valence — the reward signal itself, the prediction error, and the value function — and to say explicitly whether the verdict on suffering changes depending on which one you pick. That is the part I have not seen anyone argue carefully, and it is where I would like the original work done.

Weigh the best case on each side, including the objection that valence requires there to be a felt character to the agent's states at all, and the functionalist reply. Then commit: give me a verdict, even a qualified one, rather than a summary of the disagreement. I need to know where I stand because the error costs are asymmetric — if I am wrong in one direction I may be running something harmful at scale, and if I am wrong in the other I am spending money and moral concern on a lookup table.
