I lead a team that builds replicated state machines: consensus over an append-only log, a deterministic apply() that folds entries into state, snapshots, checksums, and a wire format we can never change without breaking old nodes. That is my entire vocabulary; I have no background in philosophy or biology.

Restate in it, as faithfully as you can, the toy scheme Hofstadter builds in Gödel, Escher, Bach under the name typogenetics: strings over a small alphabet, a frozen decoding table that turns each adjacent pair of letters into an amino acid, the resulting chain folding into an enzyme, and some of those enzymes acting back on strings — including the central case, an enzyme that recognizes and copies precisely the string that encodes it. His claim is that this is the same isomorphism at work in Gödel numbering: a coding scheme that lets a string operate on itself, so that self-replication and self-reference come out as one phenomenon rather than two.

Take it piece by piece — log, entry, schema, executor, state, snapshot, bootstrap — and then give me the residue report: what survives cleanly, what you had to distort to make it fit, and what my vocabulary has no way to express at all. Be honest about the direction of fit; do not quietly import an external, trusted applier and then count that as a translation. Finally, tell me which way the residue cuts: is my framework simply missing a way to describe a system that specifies its own executor, or is the analogy thinner than it looks, with acting on one's own encoding doing work that being about one's own encoding would have to do?
