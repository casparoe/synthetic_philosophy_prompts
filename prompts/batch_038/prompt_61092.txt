Novelist here, mid-draft on a historical novel, and I've backed into something I don't have words for. Possibly there's a whole literature on it and I'm the last person alive to notice, but I want the actual concept, not just a label I can stick on the front of my notebook.

Context: I keep a ledger for every character. The real ones enter early drafts as whatever description the sources handed me — the printer who set the type for the pamphlet, the woman who signed the deposition, the boy who lived through the crossing. Then somewhere around draft three the description gets promoted to a name and the name takes over the book.

Thing one. After the promotion happens, the old description stops travelling with the person. I can write a passage imagining him never going into printing at all, born a farmer, never touches a press in his life, and it still feels like I'm talking about him, the very same man, just a different way his life could have gone. But while a character is *only* a description — say, the tallest boy in the village — and I try to imagine him being short, something breaks. It doesn't feel like imagining that boy otherwise, it feels like I've swapped in a different boy, or a different village. To hold onto him I have to bolt something on: the one who was in fact the tallest, the tallest back then. Only then does the imagining stay hooked. So there seem to be two kinds of handle, one of which keeps hold of the person across every way things could have gone and one of which doesn't, and I can feel the difference in my hands while drafting but I cant say what it is.

Thing two, maybe related. Two years in archives and I finally established that the man in the parish register under one name and the man who signed the deposition under another are the same person. That took evidence — a will, a lawsuit, handwriting. But now that I know it, the claim feels like it couldn't have come out any other way; I can't picture a past where those two entries belong to different men and yet all the same documents still exist. Whereas other things I dug up — where he lived, who he owed money to — I imagine going differently with no strain. So part of what I learned empirically now feels locked and part doesn't, and I can't tell what sorts the two piles.

Which one has a name? I mostly care about one, and especially the in-between case I keep hitting: a description my *text* has turned into a name-like handle with no in-fact in it at all, the ledger promotion. Readers do it too. By chapter nine, 'the printer' picks out the same man for them across every counterfactual, even though grammatically it's still a description and there were three printers in that town — and when the description isn't unique they just silently narrow it and never notice, which seems like a different move again. Is there a term for a description that's been upgraded like that, or for the upgrading? If not, coin me one, with a definition and some boundary cases, because I want to know where it stops applying. Don't send me a reading list, I want the carving: what the two handles are, what the promotion does to them, whether the silent narrowing is the same business, and whether thing two is thing one in a different hat or genuinely seperate.
