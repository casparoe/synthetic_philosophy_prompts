So my reading group is working through the last chapter of Principia Ethica — the one on the ideal — and one of the lads has basically convinced everyone that Moores account of friendship is aesthetic appreciation wearing a trench coat. His line: for Moore a friend is just a rather good artwork, loving someone is a matter of standing at the right distance and registering their beautiful qualities, and the two-worlds test — beautiful world versus heap of filth, with nobody ever going to see either — proves it, because it makes all value a function of what there is to contemplate. Add the fact that Moore ranks the pleasures of human intercourse right alongside the enjoyment of beautiful objects, as two species of the same genus of valuable states of consciousness, and you get his conclusion: Moore can't have an ethics of friendship at all, only a theory of taste with people in it, and the appeal to organic unities is a fig leaf covering the fact that the friend is doing instrumental work in the value of your own inner state.
