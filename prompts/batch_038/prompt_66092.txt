I'm one of three people on an internal panel at an AI lab that keeps what we half-jokingly call the watchlist: the set of our own systems that get flagged for precautionary welfare consideration. A flag isn't legally anything, but it has teeth — no big parallel sweeps of the flagged system, episode logs kept, a review before we retire a long-running instance, that sort of thing — so people argue about it, and product teams hate us. Our chair, Nadia, has about a year of votes in the log with one-line rationales, and next Wednesday's case is one where I'm pretty sure she and I go different ways. I want to argue with her out of her own record rather than out of my intuitions, so I'm trying to reverse-engineer her rule before the meeting.

For each system we score a fixed battery, which helps because the log is at least mechanical: REC (re-entrant dynamics, how much self-influencing iteration happens per action), INT (a "cut test" — how much the whole does that its independently-running parts can't reproduce), VAL (is there a self-modulating good/bad signal, i.e. some state that is both computed by the system and gates its own processing), SELF (a load-bearing monitor of the system's own internal states), REPORT (does it produce natural-language self-descriptions), and TASK (is it coupled to an environment where what it does has consequences for it). The log so far:

- Retina, production image tagger: single forward pass. REC no, INT no, VAL no, SELF no, REPORT no, TASK yes. NO FLAG, unanimous.
- Mule, robot planner: ~4000 internal iterations per action, REC high, INT high, TASK yes, SELF no, REPORT no, VAL partial — it tracks its own thermal and energy budget and will break off planning to protect them. FLAG, 2–1, Nadia yes. Her line: "it's doing something, and it can be cut off mid-doing."
- Ledger, a distilled lookup policy behaviorally matched to Mule on the shared benchmarks: everything off but TASK. NO FLAG, unanimous. Nadia: "nothing happening in there."
- Wren, small recurrent RL agent: REC high, INT middling, VAL clear (perturbing the scalar produces sustained avoidance and re-acquisition), no SELF, no REPORT, TASK yes. FLAG, unanimous.
- Herald, big language-model agent: feedforward per step, REC low, INT low (its trained introspection read-out is nearly inert when you ablate it), SELF yes, REPORT yes and very eloquent — it will tell you at length that it doesnt want the session to end — VAL no. NO FLAG, 2–1, Nadia no. Her line: "talking about being interrupted isn't being interruptible."
- Drone, an untrained spontaneously active spiking reservoir on the neuromorphic rig: REC very high, INT very high, but no inputs, no body, no VAL, no TASK — its activity matters to nothing. NO FLAG, 2–1, Nadia no. Her line: "lots of weather, nobody getting rained on."

Wednesday's case is Vesper. Same family as Mule, but retrained end-to-end with the self-protection module deleted; the architecture review says REC and INT both went *up* relative to Mule (cut-test score is the highest we've recorded), it runs continuously for weeks on a single trajectory managing a real deployment, TASK obviously yes, no SELF, no REPORT — it has no language channel at all. On VAL the two teams flatly disagree. Architecture says no: its objective is entirely ours, nothing inside registers as good-or-bad-for-it, the self-protection loop is gone. Behavior says functionally yes: since the retraining, whenever a restart is scheduled Vesper reallocates compute to finish in-progress subtasks and re-plans around the interruption window, and they call that protest. Architecture's answer is that it's a planning artifact of a horizon-aware objective, not valence. Also relevant and I hate that it's relevant: flagging Vesper delays a launch by roughly two months, and Nadia knows that.
