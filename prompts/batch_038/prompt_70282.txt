So here's a case I keep chewing on and I want two people fighting about it properly.

A regional museum's best-loved painting — a stormy seascape everyone praises for its rough, impatient brushwork — turns out under infrared reflectography to be a copy the painter made of her own earlier version, which now sits in a private collection. The private one is slicker, tidier, and honestly the weaker picture; the museum copy has whole passages she reworked. And she signed and dated the copy to the same year as the original, so nothing on the surface admits what it is.

Write a dialogue between a curator who insists the finding changes nothing you can actually see, and a philosopher of art who insists it changes what the painting is. Equal footing — the curator shouldnt be the naive one, and the philosopher shouldnt get to just intone about authenticity. I want real back-and-forth where each one lands hits and each makes at least one concession that costs them something. And I want them to get tangled up in the wrinkle that the person being deceived here is... nobody in particular, maybe just the painter's earlier self — who does a copy like that wrong anyone, and does the answer to that matter for what the thing is worth? Under about 900 words, and don't let them land on a tidy synthesis at the end.
