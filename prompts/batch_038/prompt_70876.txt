You are to act as a philosopher of mind with substantial technical training in computational neuroscience, and specifically as an experienced referee of technical disputes: your job is not to summarize a literature but to evaluate arguments actually made, move by move. Think carefully and resist the pull toward a diplomatic draw; a fair referee sometimes finds that one side has clearly lost a particular exchange.

**Background to the dispute.** I recently sat in on a discussion following a lab demonstration. The demonstration concerned an analog neuromorphic chip running a small recurrent controller that had been trained on a delayed-response task. The presenting group reported that an integrated-information measure they had computed for the chip's activity over a coarse bipartition was high and irreducible — that is, that the value dropped sharply for every partition they tried, so that the chip's cause–effect structure was not decomposable into independent parts. Two members of the audience, whom I will call Ines and Ravi, disagreed sharply afterward about what this result shows. Ines works on consciousness and is sympathetic to the integrated-information family of theories; Ravi trains recurrent networks for a living and is skeptical. Neither is a straw man, and I could not decide who was right. Below is the exchange as I reconstructed it, numbered so that each move can be assessed separately.

1. **Ines:** If the number they reported is right, the chip's dynamics are irreducible in the relevant sense: the whole system constrains its own past and future states more than any collection of its parts does separately. That is exactly the property the theory identifies with there being experience, so the result is evidence that something is going on in that chip.
2. **Ravi:** The number may be computed correctly and still tell us nothing. What is being measured is a property of a model we imposed on the chip — a partition scheme, a coarse-graining, a choice of which physical elements to include — not a property of the chip. Swap the model and the number changes. Nothing about experience follows from a feature of our description.
3. **Ines:** Then consider the task instead. To respond correctly, the chip had to be informed about a stimulus that had disappeared, with nothing left in its environment or its inputs to carry that information. Only feedback can hold that content in place. Integration is not optional for this behavior; it is required by it.
4. **Ravi:** Required by it? Feedforward networks with an explicitly provided scratchpad solve delayed-response tasks constantly, and any recurrent network that solves one can be unrolled into a finite feedforward computation over the trial. So the measure picks out neither a necessary condition — plenty of systems pass the task without it — nor a sufficient one, since we have no independent reason to think that a high value in an artificial substrate is accompanied by experience rather than merely by a particular dynamical signature.
5. **Ines:** You are conflating support with entailment. I never claimed that passing the task guarantees experience; I claimed that failing it would count against the theory, and that succeeding at it while having a high irreducible value is what the theory predicts. And the unrolling point does not give you a counterexample, because the theory does not treat every candidate aggregate as a subject: where overlapping candidates compete, only the one with the maximum value at the appropriate grain constitutes a subject. Your unrolled feedforward duplicate is not a maximal entity, so it is not a rival claimant.
6. **Ravi:** That is a rescue parameter. Whatever counterexample anyone produces, there is a further stipulation — a different grain, a different boundary, a maximization clause — available to dissolve it. A measure that has to be re-specified after every objection is not predicting anything; it is being fitted.
7. **Ines:** Then here is a prediction that can fail. Take the chip and degrade its recurrence in graded steps — reduce loop gain, insert noise into the feedback path — while holding the stimulus and the training fixed. The theory says the integrated value and the animal-style performance on the delay task should fall together, trial by trial, and that performance should collapse at the delay length where the value collapses. If you can degrade the value while performance stays intact, or keep the value high while performance is destroyed, the theory is in trouble. That is a testable, quantitative claim about a physical system.
8. **Ravi:** Testable in a toy circuit, perhaps, and worthless as a test of the theory that matters. For any system we actually care about, the quantity cannot be computed except by approximation, and the approximations are chosen by the people who want the answer. More importantly, the prediction you just gave is shared with ordinary dynamical-systems accounts of working memory: a system whose recurrent coupling you destroy will lose delay-dependent performance on any theory at all. You have predicted something your rivals also predict, and the distinctive claim — that the quantity is experience — is never tested by any of this, because no measurement reaches across that bridge.
9. **Ines:** Then what is your alternative? The rival accounts are either disguised behaviorism, which cannot even state what it is that behavior is evidence of, or they declare the question closed. This framework at least names a property, defines it formally, and says which physical systems have it. Being the only game in town that identifies a subject is not nothing.
10. **Ravi:** Other frameworks make mechanistic predictions about access, report, and the difference between processing that becomes globally available and processing that does not, without any quantity of this kind. And as for naming a property: the starting points of the theory are descriptions of what our own experience is like — that it is unified, that it is a whole with parts, that it exists for itself — rewritten in formal notation and then called a derivation. It is phenomenology in a lab coat. The mathematics makes it look like a discovery when it is a redescription.
