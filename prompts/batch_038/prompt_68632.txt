I help run a little non-religious congregration — about forty of us, Sunday mornings, in a deconsecrated chapel we rent — and we do basically everything a church does, minus the god. We sing real hymn tunes with the words rewritten, so last month we sang something about the river that runs through town to the tune of a Welsh hymn. Somebody reads a poem or a paragraph of prose aloud (we call it the text of the week), there's a talk, we pass a plate, and at the end we read out the names of everyone in the area who died that week, including people none of us ever met, taken from the local paper, and occassionally someone adds a line about them. No creed, no metaphysics, no "spiritual" language — that last one is an actual rule, because half of us are ex-evangelical and the word makes them itch. We've recently started letting a language model draft some of the responsive readings, which very nearly tore the group in half.

I keep thinking about George Eliot the whole time, because she's the obvious patron saint of what we're attempting and I suspect she'd hate it. So what would she actually think of this? I don't want the generic religion-of-humanity answer, I want you to work from her own position: the woman who translated Strauss and then Feuerbach, who came out of an evangelical schoolroom, who spent decades writing novels that quietly took over the pastoral work the churches used to do, and who was perfectly aware that you can't just strip the doctrine out of a form and expect the form to keep working. Where is she on our side? What would she find hollow, or worse, dishonest? And specifically — the rewritten hyms: is keeping the tune and dumping the creed a legitimate inheritance in her eyes, or a kind of theft, or something she'd find simply embarrassing? Would she come on a Sunday, and if she came once would she come back?

Tell me which way you think she lands in your first couple of sentences and then argue for it, and finish with two things: the one thing she'd flatly refuse to concede to the ex-evangelicals in the room, and the one thing she'd insist we're getting badly wrong that we wouldn't have thought of as a religious question at all. Plain prose please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just continuous paragraphs, the way you'd write a letter to a friend about it.
