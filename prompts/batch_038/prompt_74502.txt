Retired after 35 years doing fluid dynamics, only lately reading philosophy properly, so excuse the amateur framing. In the Peirce–James row about truth, everybody I read explains Peirce with a limit: truth is what inquiry would converge on if pushed far enough, the way a sequence approaches its limit. The skeptic in my reading group uses that picture to argue Peirce is either smuggling in correspondence (something out there has to be doing the converging) or else saying nothing at all, since nobody ever reaches the end of inquiry and "the final opinion" is just a placeholder that any old belief could fill.

Before I swallow either horn I want the analogy audited. What maps onto what — the index, the terms, the metric by which one belief counts as closer than another, the process that takes the limit? Then where it breaks. My own suspicion is that it's the wrong bit of mathematics: a sequence's limit is there whether or not anyone computes it, but an attractor is only defined relative to a particular system's equations, some systems have basins so the endpoint depends on where you started, and plenty have no attractor at all. Does Peirce's picture survive that translation, or does it quietly need a much stronger assumption than anyone admits? And if it goes under, does James's tools-that-work account inherit the job or fail in a different way?

Don't give me a menu of readings. Pick a verdict, state it flatly, and say which disanalogy did the damage.
