My dad had a big left-side stroke in March. He's mostly non-speaking now — he can get out a handful of words, and the clinic gave him a speech tablet that he mainly uses to curse at. I've been reading Dennett on the four kinds of creatures (Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian) partly to figure out what to expect from him, and somewhere along the way I built an argument I now can't accept but also can't break. Be honest with me about where it's bad.

(1) The tower isn't about brains or hardware. What puts a creature on the Gregorian rung is that it has taken in externally designed thinking tools — words first, then writing, arithmetic, calendars, ledgers, spreadsheets — and can use them to work on problems the other three kinds of creature can't so much as pose.

(2) So where a creature sits is settled by its toolkit: strip out the tools and you strip out the rung.

(3) If the tools are the whole of what makes a mind Gregorian, there's nothing left over to hold that mind on the rung once the tools are gone.

(4) My dad has lost the main one. He can't find words, can't hold a sentence with a subordinate clause in it, can't read the labels on his tablet without guessing. What's left — pointing, drawing a rough picture of what he wants, reading my face, learning that the blue tile gets him coffee — looks like Skinnerian learning with maybe a bit of Popperian lookahead.

(5) So my dad has been demoted two rungs. He isn't a Gregorian creature anymore.

(6) But (2) doesn't stop at him. I've forgotten most of the Spanish I had, I can't do long division in my head, I can't read music, and I never installed the tools a physicist or a contract lawyer thinks with. By (2) I'm a partial Gregorian at best.

(7) And it runs forward as well as backward. Whatever tools people fifty years from now will have installed from birth, I won't have them, so relative to them I'm demoted too. Since there's always a next batch of tools, there is no top rung, because its defined by an endpoint no creature can ever occupy.

(8) So either the tower doesn't sort creatures at all — everyone is a bit of every rung, and the rungs have no boundaries — or it sorts them properly and my dad really is two rungs below me. Neither horn is anything I can use. On the first, it tells me nothing I couldn't read off the therapist's own chart of what he can and can't do. On the second, I've been carrying around a vocabulary whose job is to rank my father beneath me.

Which step do I give up? (2) looks like it's just spelling out (1) and (3) looks like it's just spelling out (2), so I can't see where the slip is, if there is one. Tell me which step is wrong or at least unsupported, why it seemed fine to me when I wrote it down, and what each of the other exits costs — I'd rather loose (2) than (1) or (4), but I want to know what I'm paying either way. And whatever fix you land on, run it against the other case in my family: my grandmother's dementia is taking her tools one at a time, over years, and I want to know whether your fix says something different about her than it says about my dad, or whether it just hides that difference.
