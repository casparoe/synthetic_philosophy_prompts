You're a philosopher of probability who's spent an unhealthy amount of time on the principle of indifference — get in that headspace, and do a couple paragraphs of brainstorming out loud before you commit to anything.

Here's the setup I want you to work with. Regular hexagon inscribed in a circle, vertices V1…V6. Call any segment joining two distinct vertices a "chord": there are 15, and 3 of them are diameters. Now two ways of selecting a chord.

(A) apply indifference straight to the 15 chords.
(B) pick a vertex at random (indifference over the six), then pick k at random from {1, 2, 3} (indifference over the three), and take the chord from your vertex to the vertex k steps clockwise.

(B) hands you 18 equally likely (vertex, k) pairs. Each diameter occurs twice in that list, every other chord exactly once — from the other endpoint the step size would be 4 or 5, which isn't on offer. So (B) gives P(diameter) = 6/18 = 1/3, while (A) gives 3/15 = 1/5. Everything here is finite and completely well defined: no measures to choose, no limits, no improper priors, no nonmeasurable sets, no infinities anywhere.

Argue for this thesis: Bertrand's paradox isn't a disease of the continuum. Because a finite case like the one above reproduces the whole problem, the standard repairs that work by supplying a canonical measure — the measure-theory response, the invariance-under-a-transformation-group response, the "the problem just wasn't well posed" shrug — all miss the actual point, which is that "equipossible" is only ever fixed relative to a way of carving up the space, and finiteness does nothing to fix that.

Make it a real argument, not a survey. I want the strongest version of the objection that (A) is the only legitimate application here — either because it's the finest partition, or because indifference is supposed to apply to hypotheses about the world and not to procedures for selecting outcomes — and then an explanation of why that objection, even if it wins this case, doesnt rescue the continuum-only diagnosis. Close with what you think has to be true for indifference to be usable at all. American English please.
