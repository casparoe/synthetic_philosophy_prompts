i'm a 4th-year philosophy PhD student (formal epistemology — my dissertation is on when your future self counts as an expert you have to defer to) and i've got a 15-minute slot at our department's end-of-year workshop, where maybe half the room has never seen a probability calculus. i want to open with a poem instead of a definition slide. It should be a pantoum: six quatrains, lines 2 and 4 of each stanza becoming lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the final stanza closing on lines 1 and 3 of the first in the usual way. That's 24 lines, keep it there.

the reason i want that form is the reflection principle. if i'm confident about what my credence in A will be at some later time, then my credence now, conditional on that, ought to be the same number. a pantoum's repeats are the same words coming back in a different mouth at a different time, which is just about the closest formal analogue i can think of. so the constraint i care about most: every recycled line has to still work, word for word unchanged, when it's the future self saying it rather than the present one. the repetition itself should carry the identity of the two credences — no paraphrase, no swapping in a synonym to make the grammar come out.

content. the speaker is roughly me: a grad student now, with one specific claim A in mind. pick something small and concrete — whether an effect in a dataset is real, whether a paper will hold up, whether a collaborator will still be a collaborator — and stick to it. they know that in two years they'll have gone through all the evidence and landed on a number. i want at least one stanza to carry the version i find most intuitive, that my present credence is just my expectation of my future one (yes, i know that framing already assumes the future number is arrived at by conditioning honestly — which is precisely what makes the second half bite, so lean into it). and i want the bookie in there: the pair of bets across the two times that locks in a loss if the numbers come apart, so the reader feels something actually being bought and sold rather than just introspected about.

then the turn. in the back half the speaker realises their future number won't come out of honest conditionalising — they'll be exhausted, or handed a dataset they have reason to think is cooked, or three years and a job market deep in A being true, or drunk at the conference party when they form the opinion. the repeated lines keep returning anyway, because the form can't stop, but the identity they were enforcing is now the wrong one. the reader should feel the gaurantee slide out from under a line they've already heard twice. don't resolve it and don't sermonise; i want the last stanza's return to stanza one to be genuinely uncomfortable. someone who knows the literature should be able to tell i've got my finger on exactly why reflection is contestable, and someone who doesn't should just get a good poem about not trusting the person you're going to become.

don't name the principle or the person who introduced it anywhere in the verse — a formal epistemologist in the room should be able to ID it from the structure alone. after the poem, give me three or four sentences, addressed to me and not to the audience, saying which repeated line you're using to do which piece of work, so i can check you've actually got the content right rather than the vibe.

sorry that's a lot of instructions for a party poem. title optional, no archaisms, no 'alas', and please don't rhyme the quatrains just because they're quatrains.
