I do long-range load forecasting for a regional grid operator, and after the last few years — price shocks that nobody's model saw coming — I started reading the quant-history crowd to see if I could steal ideas. Cliodynamics, secular cycles, structural-demographic theory, elite overproduction, all of it. But something keeps bugging me and I can't tell if it's a known problem with a name or just me being a naive outsider.

Here's the pattern. A model gets fit to a couple of societies and does well. Then someone applies it to a third one, it does badly, so they add a term — a different proxy for elite overproduction, a weighting, a lag — and now the fit across all three is better than before, genuinely better, you can see the residuals shrink. And the answer to "you've just bought that with a free parameter" isn't a penalty. It's that the parameter will be validated by future data. Which, for a cycle that runs two or three centuries, means it will be validated by nobody who is currently alive. So the field ends up in a place where every model improvement is real and every model improvement is permanently untestable, and that's just treated as normal operating conditions.

Second version of the same thing: a forecast misses, and instead of counting as a miss it gets reclassified — right phase, wrong timing. But the phase boundaries are drawn by looking backwards at what happened, so being in the instability phase can't really be wrong at any given moment.

In my own field we'd call the first one overfitting and cross-validate out of it, except you can't cross-validate against a second history, so I don't think it's quite overfitting: there's no held-out set that exists even in principle, which feels like a different category of trouble. And the second one isn't quite an ad hoc rescue either, since nothing is being patched after a failure so much as the success criteria getting quietly loosened.

So — does this have a name? I've bumped into unfalsifiability, epicycles, postdiction, Lakatos on degenerating programmes, and the general N=1 complaint, and none of them sit exactly on it. Tell me which is closest and precisely where it stops fitting, and if nothing covers it, coin something and define it, including the boundary cases (like when adding a parameter to a historical model *is* legit). I don't want a label for its own sake, I want the carving, so I can tell my team when a cliodynamics result is worth importing and when it isn't.

Finish with a last line in exactly this form — Verdict: <the established term, or "no established term">
