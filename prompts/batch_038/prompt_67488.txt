ok so I've been arguing about moral testimony on the internet for longer than I'd like to admit, and there's a version of the old problem nobody's written a decent poem about yet: asking a language model whether something is okay and getting a genuinely good answer back.

Write me a poem in terza rima — the interlocking aba bcb cdc thing, closed off with one extra single line at the end that picks up the middle rhyme of the last tercet — from the point of view of a woman who has just done exactly that. She's been offered a job at a company whose product she suspects does harm, several steps removed from whatever she'd personally be doing, so it's genuinely unclear. She asks her model. It gives her a careful verdict with reasons attached. Now she can repeat the verdict word for word, she can deploy it in arguments with her flatmates, and she is confident. What she can't do is answer 'yeah, but why?' without scrolling back to the transcript.

I want the form to carry that rather than the poem explaining it. Terza rima never settles locally — every stanza owes its middle rhyme to the one after it, so the resolution is always one link further down and never actually arrives. That's the exact shape of having the right answer without having the understanding of it: she keeps passing the reason forward and the reason keeps not turning up. Make the distinction between knowing-that and understanding-why legible to someone who already knows the distinction, but with zero names and zero terms of art — no 'deference', no 'epistemic', no 'testimony' anywhere in the poem itself, nothing that smells like a journal article.

Two more constraints, becuase I like constraints:
- the word 'because' appears exactly three times, and each time its doing a different kind of job. One of them is the model's, one is hers and comes out wrong, and you pick what the third one is.
- that final single line is the only place in the poem where there's nowhere left to hand the rhyme on, so make it the thing she'd have to be able to say entirely on her own and can't.

Somewhere between 19 and 25 lines. And please write it in British English — realise, colour, flat, the lot.
