Been going around in circles on Nozick's tracking account of knowledge and I've hit something I can't find a label for. Maybe it's a well known objection and I've just missed it.

The account says you know p if your belief that p comes about by some route that would have led you not to believe p had p been false. Fine. But whether a belief satisfies that seems to depend entirely on how you describe the route it came by, and nothing in the theory tells you how specific that description should be.

It goes wrong in both directions. Too coarse: a grandmother believes her grandson is doing fine, on the basis of seeing him and talking to him. Now describe her route broadly as keeping up with how her grandson is doing. In the nearby situation where he's actually badly hurt, everyone around her would lie to spare her feelings, so she'd still have believed he was fine, and the belief doesn't track. But surely she knows when she's looking right at him. Too fine: I guess an answer in a pub quiz and it happens to be right. Describe the route as guessing and it obviously doesn't track. But describe it as guessing-among-just-these-questions-where-I-get-this-particular-hunch-which-is-in-fact-veridical, and now it tracks trivially. Pretty much any true belief can be made to track by making the route description specific enough, and lots of obvious knowledge can be broken by making it vague enough.

So unless the level of description is pinned down from outside, the tracking condition isn't really constraining anything.

What I want to know: does this have a standard name? I'm aware reliabilism has a famous problem about how broadly or narrowly to type a belief forming process and this feels like a cousin of that, but is it literally the same problem moved over, or genuinely different, since what shifts here isn't a reliability score but a whole batch of counterfactuals? And is there an accepted distinction between the too-coarse and the too-fine failure modes, or do people treat them as one thing? If it has no name, what would you call it, and where's the line between this and a harmless underdetermination that any theory with methods in it just inherits? Don't give me only a label — I want to know what the phenomenon is and what it isn't.
