so my brother came home from college over break fully convinced that procrastination isn't a real thing — or like, that it isn't a character flaw anyway. his whole argument is that me-at-17 and me-at-40 basically aren't the same person, we just happen to share a body, a name, and a bank account, and the reason i can't make myself study is the exact same reason i wouldn't hand my last twenty dollars to some guy on the bus. different people, different interests, nobody's being weak. he keeps name-dropping this philosopher called Parfit who apparently showed personal identity isn't what matters, and he says hyperbolic discounting is the proof, because the weight you give stuff changes depending on how far off it is, so it's not one irrational person, it's two people with different rates who keep disagreeing.

his go-to picture is a relay team. each runner does their leg, hands off the baton, and nobody blames the first runner for not sprinting the entire race. you do your leg, future-you does his.

from that he gets two conclusions he thinks follow automatically. one: i should stop feeling guilty about blowing off homework, because "i" didn't hurt anybody, i just declined to help a stranger. two: locking myself down hard is fine, maybe required — apps that block everything until midnight, automatic transfers into savings, giving my friend my phone before a game — because that isn't weakness, that's just negotiating a treaty with somebody else. like Ulysses and the mast, except the mast is a screen-time passcode.

can you stress-test the relay picture for me? i want the correspondence spelled out explicitly, a table is fine — what in the race plays the role of the present self, the future self, the baton, the handoff, the finish line. then where it starts leaking, and which features of a relay the analogy quietly imports that weakness of will just doesn't have. there were a few things i couldn't answer when he pushed me:

- if future-me is really a stranger, why does imagining him make me feel something right now, and doesn't that mean the present self is already banking some of the future self's payoff — so is he double-counting when he says the two of them have totally separate interests?
- relay runners are actually different bodies in different places. here there's one body, one reputation, one set of consequences that lands on the same head. is the analogy carrying the argument or is the argument carrying the analogy?
- if binding myself is just a treaty between two parties, who signed on behalf of the future guy, and is an app that locks me up till midnight meaningfully different from one person kidnapping another?
- he says preference reversal is on his side, but it looks like the opposite to me. if the ranking flips depending on the date, that's one person with intransitive preferences, not two people who each have clean consistent ones. right?

then tell me straight whether either of his two conclusions survives once the leaks are on the table — the no-guilt one and the lock-yourself-down one, separately, since i suspect they don't rise and fall together. and if the relay team is the wrong picture, what would a better one be? something that keeps whatever the analogy was genuinely capturing, because i do think it's getting at something real about why the future feels like it's happening to somebody else.
