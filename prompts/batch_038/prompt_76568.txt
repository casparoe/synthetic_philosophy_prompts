I'm presenting my metaethical views at a work-in-progress session and would like a consistency audit before I circulate the handout. Here is the package:

1. Wrongness is a matter of justifiability: an act is wrong if it would be ruled out by principles that no one could reasonably reject.
2. Only personal reasons are admissible. Appeals to impersonal value, or to sums of welfare taken across people, are excluded at the threshold, not merely discounted.
3. Complaints are weighed one-to-one. I never add together several individuals' weaker complaints so as to outweigh one stronger complaint belonging to a single person.
4. So in a rescue case where one person will die unless I act and five others will each suffer an hour of mild discomfort unless I act, the right act prevents the death.
5. In the reversed case, where one person will die unless I let a billion others each have an hour of mild discomfort, I'm confident the right act spares the billion.
6. Where five lives are at stake against one, we are required and not merely permitted to save the five; I secure this by noting that the single complaint is satisfied by saving someone, so the further complaints on the other side are left unmet.
7. A principle's being reasonably rejectable is not its being rejected: plenty of people will in fact refuse principles they have no good reason to refuse, and I take that to cost my view nothing.

Which of these pull against each other, and how badly? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each say precisely what would have to be revised and what the revision costs the rest of the package — including whether the fix in one place reopens a problem elsewhere. Some of these items only look like they conflict; where that is so, say so explicitly and explain why, rather than flagging every pair. I'm not after a general exposition of contractualism or a defense of the view, just an account of where I'm actually exposed.
