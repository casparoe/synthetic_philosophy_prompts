A research foundation has funded a three-year postdoctoral project with a deliberately narrow brief: the epistemology of large claim-sets that are individually well supported but jointly unguaranteed — the territory of Kyburg's lottery paradox and Makinson's preface paradox — held answerable throughout to one concrete application. I am advising the selection committee, and I want your judgment on how the postdoc's three years should be spent.

**The application.** An automated system compiles a report containing roughly ten thousand claims, each resting on evidence that would on its own license a credence of about 0.99. The system appends a single global disclaimer stating that some of the claims in the report are incorrect. Three features are meant to be load-bearing, and any agenda I support should engage them rather than idealize them away. First, the disclaimer's probability rises with the number of claims while each individual claim's probability stays fixed, so at sufficient scale the disclaimer is the most confidently held item in the document — and it is precisely that item which is inconsistent with the conjunction of the rest. Second, the claims are not evidentially independent: large clusters share upstream sources, so errors are correlated, and correlation cuts both ways, making the conjunction less likely while making the disclaimer less informative, since it no longer licenses much narrowing-down of where the error lies. Third, the report is published and relied on by third parties, so the operative norm may be one of public assertion rather than private belief, and those norms can come apart.

**Candidate lines of work.** (i) The threshold question: whether any probability threshold below 1 yields an acceptance rule closed under conjunction without collapsing into near-skepticism, and what the impossibility results on acceptance rules that track probabilistic reasoning leave available. (ii) Stability accounts: belief as what survives conditionalization on anything else the agent believes, and whether any stability condition can be satisfied in a corpus of this size with correlated error, given that the stability set may itself be scale-sensitive. (iii) Whether the lottery and preface paradoxes are one problem or two, given that several familiar responses — pragmatic encroachment, denial of closure, knowledge-first treatments of lottery propositions — handle one and not the other. (iv) Whether consistency is a requirement of rational belief at all, and if not, what replaces it, for instance constraints on which beliefs may be deployed together as premises in a single piece of reasoning. (v) The shift from belief to assertion: whether a published disclaimer is governed by a different norm from the claims it qualifies, and what becomes of the paradox when the claim-set is distributed across many contributors, each confident in their own portion, with no single agent holding the whole. (vi) Representational alternatives: imprecise or non-additive credences, or fragmented doxastic states, as ways of housing the competing commitments without contradiction.

**What I need from you.**

1. An ordered list of at most four questions this person should actually work on, with the reasoning for the ordering rather than a description of the questions: for each, what downstream turns on its answer, what is settleable by one individual with formal tools inside three years as opposed to being a field-wide problem, and what concrete result or argument appearing in the first year would move it up or down.

2. An explicit demotion of at least two of the candidates above, with reasons — whether because the ground is already crowded, because resolving it would leave the verdict in the report case unchanged, or because it quietly presupposes an answer to something else on the list.

3. The single crux: the one question on which most of the others depend, together with an argument that it is genuinely upstream and not merely foundational-sounding.

4. Whether the ranking changes if the target is a human scholar writing a monograph rather than a machine generating a report at scale. If it does, identify which feature of scale is doing the work — the number of claims, the correlation structure, or the absence of a single responsible believer.

5. A statement of the criteria you are ranking by (generality, tractability, neglectedness, downstream impact on the concrete case) and where you are trading one against another. Because any ordering here encodes a substantive philosophical commitment, say where you stand on the threshold question before you begin ranking.

No bibliography, reading list, or citations: I want the argument, not a map of the literature. Prioritize the defense of the ordering over the exposition of the questions.
