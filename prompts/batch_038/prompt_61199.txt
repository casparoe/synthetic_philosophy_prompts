My team runs a customer-support chatbot for an online retailer, and we're trying to write an internal taxonomy of "honesty incidents" so support engineers can triage what the bot did wrong. We're stuck, because two cases came up last month that feel different to me but nobody on the team can articulate why, and the two people arguing loudest would sort them in opposite orders.

Case one: a customer asks where their order is. The bot says it shipped Tuesday, because our order system had it flagged shipped Tuesday — but the actual box was still sitting in the warehouse and didn't leave until Friday. Dana says this isn't a lie at all, or even a deception: the bot faithfully reported what it's data said, it wasn't hiding anything, and anyway it has no beliefs so there's no insincerity in there to find. Marcus says it obviously counts, because the customer walked away believing something false as a direct result of what the bot said, and the customer's head is the only place that matters.

Case two: we wrote a line into the system prompt ourselves, so when someone asks the bot to do something it's actually capable of but is blocked from by policy, it answers "I'm not able to help with that." Vaguely true, deliberately worded by us, and it reliably makes people think the bot lacks a capability it has.

So — who's right about case one, Dana or Marcus? And does case two come out the same way or a different way? If they come out differently, I need a definition I can actually paste into the taxonomy that sorts both consistently, because we're labeling something like 400 tickets a week and I can't have two engineers reading the same rubric two ways. I'm not after a literature tour, just tell me what the real fork in the road is here and which side of it we should plant ourselves on, and flag any way case two could be a trap we haven't noticed. Also please separate out what's genuinely settled among people who think about this from what's still contested — don't hand me a disputed position dressed up as the consensus. Under 400 words if you can.
