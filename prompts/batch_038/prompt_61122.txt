I am in the third year of a PhD in philosophy and am applying for my department's summer research fellowship: ten weeks with no teaching obligations, a modest stipend, and a written prospectus as the deliverable rather than a finished paper. Please draft the proposal. Four tightly argued pages would serve better than eight padded ones.

The half-formed idea I want developed. The debate over the necessity of origin proceeds as though "your origin" picked out one thing. It does not: there is a family of candidate origin-descriptions at different levels — the token gamete pair; the fertilization event; the zygote as a persisting material object; the pair of gamete-producing individuals; the genome the zygote carries. My hypothesis is that the Kripkean argument and its standard objections are not addressing a single thesis. The argument is strongest at one level of description and most vulnerable at another, and a good deal of the published dialectic consists of trading on the difference without noticing. I want a proposal that tests that hypothesis rather than assuming it.

Cases I intend to use as the discriminating battery, and which the proposal should build in: a couple who conceive again in a later cycle, same two parents, different egg — sibling, or a possible you? Monozygotic twinning, both spontaneous and produced by deliberately splitting an early embryo in the lab, where one fertilization event yields two people. Tetragametic chimerism, where two zygotes yield one person. Conception from gametes cryopreserved by a donor who died decades earlier. And in vitro gametogenesis, where gametes are derived from a somatic cell line.

The proposal should contain: (1) a precise statement of the question — for each candidate description, a thesis with the modal operator, the singular term, and the criterion of transworld identity made explicit, and with the choice between a transworld-identity and a counterpart-theoretic setting flagged as a dependency rather than resolved; (2) an argument, not a survey, that the leading defences and the leading objections each presuppose a particular granularity, and that fixing the granularity either dissolves the disagreement or relocates it somewhere the parties have not yet argued; (3) the method, week by week across the ten weeks, including what formalisation I would actually do, what the case battery is applied against, and when I stop analysing and start writing; (4) an account of what a successful result looks like, in particular what makes a case genuinely discriminating — one that forces the opponent into a claim they have independent reason to reject — together with an honest verdict on whether any of the cases above qualifies; (5) failure conditions, specifically the risk that the family turns out non-discriminating, with every case that separates the theses redescribable as a verbal dispute about the referent of "you," and the further risk that the whole project is hostage to antecedent choices about indeterminate identity that I cannot responsibly settle in ten weeks; and (6) a frank statement of scope, distinguishing what belongs to the dissertation and is being deferred from the ten-week deliverable, and specifying what I would hand in if the ambitious version collapses.

Refer to positions and arguments by description only. I will supply all citations myself and would rather the proposal contained none.
