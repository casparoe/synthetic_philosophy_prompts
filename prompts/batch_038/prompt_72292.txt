The others in my philosophy-of-probability reading group have asked each of us to reduce our position on objective chance to a bare list of commitments, so that everyone else can attack it. Mine is below. Before I circulate it, I would like an independent audit of whether the set is jointly tenable.

(1) Chances are objective, single-case features of the world. The chance that this particular tritium atom decays within the next hour is a real, mind-independent quantity: not a frequency over a reference class, not a projection of anyone's uncertainty.

(2) Humeanism about the world. There is nothing over and above the vast mosaic of local, particular matters of fact, so chance is not an extra ingredient sprinkled on top of it. Chance supervenes: the chances are whatever probabilities figure in the systematization of the whole mosaic that best trades off simplicity against strength.

(3) The Principal Principle captures the entire content of the concept of chance: for any proposition A and any chance value x, a rational agent's credence in A, conditional on the hypothesis that the chance of A is x, is x. I impose no restriction on what further evidence the agent may be conditioning on. The admissibility side condition looks to me like a patch bolted on to save the principle rather than a substantive discovery about evidence, and I would rather state the principle cleanly and let it stand or fall.

(4) Updating is strict conditionalization. Whatever I learn, I conditionalize on exactly what I learn, and no other mechanism moves my credences.

(5) Chance requires openness. If the world is deterministic, then every proposition has chance either zero or one; a genuine single-case chance of a half is possible only where the outcome is not already fixed by the past.

(6) My credence of one half that this coin lands heads comes from the Principal Principle applied to a known objective chance, and not from any principle of indifference or symmetry. I regard indifference-based reasoning as disreputable and want no part of it.

(7) Physics is right about the microscopic world, and the microscopic world is deterministic.

(8) Which chance hypothesis actually obtains is an empirical matter, to be discovered from evidence; nothing in (3) makes the chances of our world knowable in advance of experience.

Some of these feel to me as though they pull against one another, but I cannot tell which are genuine conflicts and which are merely sloppy phrasing on my part, and I do not want to prejudge the matter by quietly dropping the weakest item from the list. So: which pairs of commitments are jointly untenable, and how badly? Which pairs only look untenable and dissolve once they are stated carefully? For each real conflict, what is the cheapest revision available to me, and what do I lose by making it?

Lead with a one-sentence verdict on whether the set hangs together, then list the conflicts in descending order of severity, each with the revision and its cost. Keep the entire audit under 500 words.
