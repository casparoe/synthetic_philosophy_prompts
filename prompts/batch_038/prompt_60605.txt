I've been chewing on this for ages and I want someone to actually take a side instead of hedging. You know the Easter Island story: they cut down all the trees, the moai stopped getting made, the society fell apart. Then you read the other side — that the islanders were doing fine, growing sweet potato in those rock garden plots on lousy soil, that maybe it was the rats they brought that ate the palm nuts rather than people being idiots, and that the actual catastrophe was the Peruvian slavers and the smallpox that came after. So nobody collapsed, they just changed.

My pet idea, which I want you to pressure-test: maybe a society only really collapses when it loses something it cant rebuild — a one way ratchet, where the lost thing would take generations to get back and the people who lost it aren't in a position to start again. On that test Rapa Nui looks genuinely ambiguous, because the islanders didn't vanish, they just stopped being a statue-building culture, and then the population crash was done to them from outside.

Write me the argument — call it a mini essay — for whether collapse names something real that happens to a society, or is mostly a value judgement we make when people stop doing the things we find impressive, and then apply whatever you land on to Rapa Nui. Pick a verdict and defend it, no sitting on the fence. Give it as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up: your verdict, the reasoning behind it, and how you'd answer the strongest objection.
