So I don't have a philosophy background, not even close — I run a small auto repair shop and I spend way too many slow afternoons chewing on what "free" actually means, mostly because my brother and I got into an argument at Thanksgiving that never got resolved and I still think about it. His position is basically: you're free as long as nobody is stopping you, that's the whole thing. Mine is: that's a joke, one of my techs is technically free to walk out the door this second and nobody's got a gun to her head, but she's got a kid, no savings, and a landlord, so she isn't free in any way that means anything. Then somebody in the family group chat said we were both half right, because there's a third idea about not being at someone's mercy even when they're being nice about it, and a fourth about whether you can actually pull things off.

What I want is a sorting instrument — a "which idea of freedom do you actually hold" decision tree — that I could hand to my brother and my coworkers and we'd all end up in different places without anybody just picking the label that sounds nicest. So build it out of cases, not slogans. Ten questions at most, each one a concrete little situation a person could answer without knowing any jargon (the boss who could fire you for no reason at all but never has; the toll road you're allowed to skip if you can afford to; the guy who's legally free to say what he thinks at work but knows what'd happen; whatever else you think separates the views better). Every question needs two or three answer options, and every option has to point somewhere — another question or an end point. Work out the space of possible answers first and attach names afterward; don't just start from the four labels everybody already knows. The branches should be labeled with the actual claim that splits them (is it interference that matters, dependence, capacity, or something else?), and every leaf gets a name — the real one if it has one, "unnamed" if nobody defends it.

Here's the twist you have to deal with. I've got my own homemade idea and I want it on the tree whether or not it's respectable. I call it freedom-as-headroom, and roughly it's this: what you care about isn't whether anyone's blocking you right now, it's how much slack you have to screw up before your life comes apart — savings, spare hours, people who'd take you in, a second trade you could fall back on. Two people can look identical on a snapshot of one ordinary Tuesday and only one of them is free, because only one of them can survive being wrong. Put it on the tree as its own leaf if it survives your questions. If it doesn't survive — if it collapses into one of the existing views — tell me exactly which question kills it and where I'm going wrong, because I'd rather be told than have you quietly rename it.

Also, for any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't tell apart, say so out loud instead of papering over it. And there are going to be combinations of answers that no position covers — list those separately and diagnose each one: empty because it's incoherent, because it's just unappealing, or because nobody's gotten around to it yet.

Give me the whole thing as one JSON object with exactly these keys:

- "questions": array of objects with "id", "prompt" (the scenario, in plain speech), "options" (array of objects with "answer", "splits_on" — the claim that answer commits you to — and "goes_to", which is either a question id or a leaf id)
- "leaves": array of objects with "id", "name", "one_line" (what the view actually claims), "awkward_commitment" (the thing its defenders hate being reminded of), "indistinguishable_from" (a leaf id, or null)
- "headroom_verdict": object with "survives" (true/false), "killed_by_question" (an id or null), "why"
- "unoccupied_combos": array of objects with "answer_pattern", "diagnosis" (one of "incoherent" / "unattractive" / "unexplored"), "one_line"
- "bottom_line": three or four plain sentences telling me which leaf my brother lands on and which one I land on, given how I described us above, and whether the arguement we had was even about the same question.
