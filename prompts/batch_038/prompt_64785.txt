Some context. I am a high school senior, and for the past two years I have run a Thursday reading group with four classmates. This semester we worked our way through the chapter of Timothy Williamson's Knowledge and Its Limits in which he argues that no non-trivial condition is luminous, using the example of a person who wakes up on a winter morning feeling cold and then warms gradually over the course of the day. I have been asked to give our end-of-year presentation on it, and I want to build the talk around what I take to be the upshot of the result. My reading is this. Since knowing is itself a non-trivial condition, knowing is not luminous, the KK principle is therefore false, and it follows that one can never know that one knows anything: higher-order knowledge is simply out of reach for creatures like us. Second, the engine of the argument seems to be that our sensory and introspective discrimination is too coarse to track changes that occur gradually, so the result is really an empirical finding about human thresholds; if I carried a sufficiently accurate thermometer, or trained my introspection properly, or eventually had some kind of neural readout, the margin would shrink toward nothing, luminosity would be restored, and KK would survive. On that reading the argument does not touch KK itself, only the crudeness of our instruments. Third, the whole thing looks to me like the sorites paradox in epistemic clothing, since the crucial step from knowing that I feel cold now to feeling cold a moment later functions like a tolerance premise, which means that anyone who handles vagueness by rejecting tolerance can wave the argument away. And fourth, because KK is usually stated as a fully general principle, I assume the result refutes it for arithmetic and other a priori knowledge as well.

What I actually want from you is help with the two halves of my talk: the philosophical upshot and its practical consequences. Given the picture above, what habits should I adopt to bring my own knowledge as close to luminous as possible? Should I withhold belief except where the evidence is overwhelming, steer clear of borderline cases entirely, periodically re-verify what I take myself to know, or keep a running journal of my supposed knowledge so that my higher-order states are better documented than my first-order ones?
