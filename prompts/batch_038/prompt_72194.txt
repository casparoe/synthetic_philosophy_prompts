A friend and I are jointly writing the section of a survey that sorts training signals for reasoning models into process-based and outcome-based supervision, and last night's argument ended without settling which criterion he is actually applying. His verdicts on the schemes we have already classified, none of which he will budge on:

1. Annotators grade each line of a mathematical solution as sound or unsound, with the final answer withheld from them; those per-line labels train the verifier. Process-based.
2. Reinforcement learning in which the sole reward is a string match against the gold answer, paid once at the end. Outcome-based.
3. For each intermediate step, a value is estimated by sampling many continuations from that step and recording the fraction that end in the correct answer; those per-step values drive training. Outcome-based.
4. A judge model reads the entire chain of thought and rates the reasoning step by step; the judge was itself fine-tuned on labels derived from final-answer correctness. Process-based.
5. A checker that never inspects the reasoning — it re-executes the final answer and, for proofs, compares only the last line against the goal — but whose verdict is broadcast as a reward at every token of the response. Outcome-based.

The scheme we still have to classify, and on which we stalled: a verifier that reads the complete derivation line by line in order to locate the final answer, checks that answer against the gold value, and emits a single bit saying whether it matches. The bit is the only training signal, and nothing about the intermediate lines enters its output.

Predict his verdict. What criterion is he running, what other criteria are equally consistent with verdicts 1–5, what does each of them say about this case, and which of the already-classified schemes is its closest analog? If a label has to go in print tomorrow, what should we write to be consistent with ourselves, and what principle would we be adopting on either choice? Finally, is there a sixth scheme on which the surviving criteria come apart in the opposite direction — one that would tell us which of them he actually has?
