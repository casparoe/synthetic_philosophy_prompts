I teach a first-year "time and reality" option — fifty minutes, next Thursday, about twenty students who did one week of special relativity in a service course, so they've met a Minkowski diagram once and cannot be relied on to remember it. The topic is the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and I want to teach it as an argument that forces a choice rather than one that proves the block universe: either what's real is indexed to a frame of reference, which nobody actually wants to say, or "what is happening now" doesn't carve out what's real, which kills presentism. Plan the session for me — don't explain the argument to me.

What I need:

- The order the pieces have to go in. I know the relativity-of-simultaneity result can't come first, because they'll hear it as being about light travel time and I'll never get them back, but I don't know what to put ahead of it. What has to be in place before "real" is even a candidate predicate, and how do I pin that down in five minutes without metaphysics vocabulary?

Andromeda is the one everybody reaches for and I'm wary of it: at that distance the effect feels like a parlour trick about big numbers, and I think they'll write the whole thing off as an artifcat of scale. I was thinking of the accelerating observer — somebody who turns around mid-stride and asks whether reality changed when they did — but tell me if there's a better probe, and what a good answer and a bad answer sound like.
