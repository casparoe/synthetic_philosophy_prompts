Putting together a handout for my decision theory seminar and I've got an argument I'm fairly sure is bad but I can't locate the badness, so I'd rather you break it than nod along. It starts from the money-pump complaint about Allais. Take a subject who goes for the sure million over the 10%/89%/1% gamble in the first pair, and for the 10%/90% over the 11%/89% in the second — the usual pattern.

(1) A preference pattern is defective only if an agent holding it can be walked through a sequence of trades, each of which she accepts at the time she accepts it, that leaves her back where she started but strictly worse off.

(2) Any violation of independence admits such a sequence; for the Allais pattern the pump costs her a few cents.

(3) So the Allais pattern is defective.

(4) But whether a given sequence is one she can be walked through depends on her decision procedure, not on her preferences. Call an agent pump-immune if, foreseeing the whole sequence, she declines the first trade.

(5) A pump-immune agent can't be walked through any such sequence, so by (1) her pattern isn't defective.

(6) Becoming pump-immune doesn't change her preferences: she still takes the sure million, she still prefers the risky option in the second pair. She hasn't turned into an expected utility maximizer, she's just stopped walking into rooms.

(7) So (3) and (5) both follow from (1), contradictorily; (2) is arithmetic; hence (1) is false and pumpability was never what made a pattern defective.

(8) But strip pumpability away and all that's left against the Allais subject is that her weighting of probabilities isn't linear — a formal property, not a mistake, no more a defect than English failing to be a regular language.

(9) Therefore independence isn't a requirement of rationality.

I want (2) and (9) to come apart and they dont. I'm attached to (2) and (3) and hostile to (9), and I know that isn't a principled place to stand, so tell me where it actually breaks: the specific step, why that step reads as innocent at speed, and a price tag for each exit you think is live. Include what pump-immunity in (4) amounts to, because right now it smells less like a theory of rational choice than like a polite way of declining to answer the question.
