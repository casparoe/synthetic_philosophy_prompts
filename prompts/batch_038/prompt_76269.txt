I hold what I'll call the institutional-survivorship thesis about historical knowledge, and I want it stress-tested rather than summarized.

The thesis has four parts. First, a document from the distant past survives only if some institution — a scriptorium, a chancery, a temple, a school, a court, a collecting household — repeatedly judged it worth the cost of copying, storing, and defending; survival is therefore a function of institutional use, not of accuracy, and not of how representative the document was of its own moment. Second, the resulting distortion is directional and often specifiable in advance: toward the literate, toward positions orthodox enough to be copied or at least quotable by the orthodox, toward administratively durable genres, and toward the exemplary or the scandalous. Third, the standard apparatus of source criticism — external criticism of date, attribution, and provenance; internal criticism of coherence, anachronism, style, and literary dependence — operates on whatever documents it is handed. It can correct errors within the surviving sample but cannot estimate or undo the selection that produced the sample, because we have no unfiltered control against which to calibrate. Fourth, what we reconstruct is therefore systematically non-representative in a way no further rigor applied to individual texts can repair; any available correction must come from outside the textual record or from the study of the filters themselves.

I grant that the first clause is close to a truism: everyone concedes selection effects. The content of the view lies in the third clause — that the effects are uncorrectable in principle, not merely difficult to correct. Treat this as my formulation and hold me to it rather than to something adjacent.

So: what would count against it? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with the reasoning for each placement. In particular, address:

- Candidate counter-evidence that has arguably already arrived. Texts preserved precisely because nobody valued them: rubbish-dump papyri, waterlogged and carbonized deposits, mass epigraphy, coinage, and the non-textual record generally. Works preserved inside the polemics of their opponents. Filters that are themselves documented well enough to be modeled — a known library's acquisitions, a known canonization process, citation networks, copyists' marginal notes.
- For each, whether it contradicts the thesis or only narrows it, and where the line falls between honest refinement and immunizing retreat. If the thesis must be restricted to the textual record of meaning-bearing human action, does it still assert anything a competent historian would dispute?
- What the thesis predicts that its rivals do not — for instance, against the view that criticism plus triangulation across differently filtered corpora converges, or against the view that selection is estimable from the structure of transmission itself — and where those predictions could be checked without begging the question.
- Whether the thesis is exposed at all. Can it absorb any counterexample by insisting that the counterexample too was selected? If it can, is that fatal for a claim of this kind, or is unfalsifiability-by-unobservable-selection a tolerable condition for a claim about selection processes?
- Which defeaters a committed holder should concede as counting against them and which they should dismiss, and on what grounds.
- The self-application problem: any evidence I can offer for the thesis is itself drawn from the filtered record. Is that a genuine defeater, a special instance of the thesis, or nothing at all?

Two further requests. Tell me where the ancient historians' own self-consciousness about their material helps or hurts the view — Herodotus setting down what he was told while declining to vouch for it, Thucydides acknowledging that the speeches are compositions fitted to the occasion rather than transcripts. I suspect this cuts in both directions and I want the analysis, not a verdict. And tell me what you think would actually move me: I am most impressed by cases where a filter is documented closely enough to be modeled, and least impressed by general reminders that the record is partial. Say whether that instinct is sound, or whether I am mistaking a tractable special case for a method.
