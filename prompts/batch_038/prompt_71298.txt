working on a paper about lying vs. misleading and I want a consistency check before I circulate the draft. Here's roughly what I'm committed to:

(1) lying requires asserting something you believe to be false — no intention-to-deceive clause, so the bald-faced lie to a jury that everyone in the room can see through still counts as one;
(2) what's specially wrong about lying is something done *to the hearer*: you hijack the trust they're entitled to extend to an assertion and turn their own belief-forming machinery against them;
(3) but deliberately misleading with literally true sentences isn't a lesser wrong, it's the same wrong in a cheaper costume — the difference is stylistic, maybe cowardice, not a distinct moral category;
(4) and there are people you owe nothing: the murderer at the door has forfeited any claim on my honesty, so lying to him isn't wrong at all;
(5) you can lie without opening your mouth — the answering nod, the loaded silence — so long as its an assertion of something you believe false.

The thing I want everything stress-tested against is a case of my own, call it the standing vow: a politician takes a public lifelong vow never to utter a falsehood, then spends twenty years answering every hostile question with a literally true sentence chosen to plant a false belief. No single act looks worse than a single lie. The aggregate looks like a life-sized fraud.

Which of (1)–(5) genuinely pull against each other, and which only look like they do until you cash out what the "entitlement" in (2) actually is? Rank the real tensions by severity and give the cheapest repair for the worst one — including what that repair costs me elsewhere. Then tell me where the standing vow lands: does it break (3), break the act-by-act shape of my whole view, or neither? And please keep separate what's basically settled in this debate from what's live and contested among people who work on it — don't let a contested claim ride in as if it were consensus, and flag anywhere you're just guessing at what the specialists think.
