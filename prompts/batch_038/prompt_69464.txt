I've settled on a version of the F-twist that I think dodges the usual complaints, and I want a straight answer about where it's actually exposed.

The position: an assumption in a model is admissible iff replacing it with something more realistic, holding the rest of the model fixed, doesnt improve the model's predictions on data it wasn't fitted to. Unrealistic assumptions aren't a sin, they're a design choice, and the only test of a design choice is whether the alternative design would have done better out of sample. That's the whole thesis. I think this is roughly what Friedman was gesturing at with the expert billiards player who shoots as if he were solving differential equations, and with the leaves on a tree that are distributed as if each one had deliberately picked the spot with the most sunlight — except I've turned the slogan into something you can actually apply. It explains why the leaves example is fine (nobody believes the leaves deliberate, and no more realistic story about leaf placement would improve the density prediction) without letting anything and everything through.

Where I use this: I build small behavioural models of when commuters leave home for a transit agency. Everyone in my models is an expected utility maximiser who knows the travel time distribution and trades schedule delay off against in-vehicle time. My colleagues in the modelling unit say thats indefensible, riders aren't like that, you're not even close to how they actually decide. I say: fine, build me a model of the same phenomenon with a more realistic rider that beats mine on held-out months, and I'll switch. Nobody has.

So — what would count against my position? I want the defeaters ranked, from the ones that should make me abandon it to the ones that are merely awkward and I could live with. Please deal with at least:

- whether holding the rest of the model fixed is ever possible at all. Do models come in the nested families my criterion presupposes? What happens when the realistic replacement forces changes elsewhere, and does that quietly turn my criterion into a Duhemian one that can never deliver a verdict?
- domains with no held-out data, or with held-out data generated by a regime that no longer exists. Does my position get to say anything there, or does it just go silent exactly when it's needed?
- whether out-of-sample predictive success licenses anything beyond prediction. I clearly want the causal and policy reading when I tell the agency what happens to departure times if they change the fare structure, and structural parameters can shift once the policy changes. Does my view collapse into instrumentalism? And if it does, is that a problem or just an honest label?
- whether more realistic is one ordered thing or several dimensions that trade off against each other, and what my criterion says when improving realism on one dimension costs you on another.
- whether the criterion can ever condemn an assumption that is realistic — i.e. is it asymmetric in a way that lets anything through so long as no rival model is on offer? What should it say when there simply is no competitor to compare against?
- whether it can distinguish an unrealistic assumption that's inert from one that's load-bearing, including the nasty case where the assumption works precisely because it exploits a historical regularity that an intervention would break.
- what my position predicts that its rivals don't, and whether there's any real episode where a wildly unrealistic model predicted beautifully in sample and then failed under intervention that I should count as evidence against me rather than as bad luck.

Also tell me what a committed Friedmanite would concede as counting against the view, as opposed to what they'd wave off — I want their internal standards, not just the critics' hits. And be honest about whether I've insulated this thing with qualifications until it risks nothing. If it is insulated, say whether that's fatal for a methodological thesis of this kind or perfectly fine for what is really a stance about how to weigh models against each other. Close with what I'd personally have to see before I gave it up, and what I could see that wouldn't move me at all.
