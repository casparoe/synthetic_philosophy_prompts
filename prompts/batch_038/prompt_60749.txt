I hold a version of the thesis that scientific discovery is largely inevitable. It has three parts, and I take them to hang together: a descriptive claim, that important results recur as multiples (Newton and Leibniz on the calculus, Darwin and Wallace on natural selection, the familiar catalogue in the sociology of science); a modal claim, that once a field is prepared someone would have reached the result anyway, within a short window; and a credit claim, that priority is therefore epistemically arbitrary, an artifact of the reward system rather than a fact about who did the intellectual work.

Treat that as my position and map its exposure. What would count against it? I want candidate defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, including: historical cases that resist classification as either a multiple or a singleton; the identity conditions the thesis needs in order to count two results as one and the same discovery, and whether those conditions are doing covert work; and the selection worry that multiples become visible mainly where credit was contested, while apparent singletons are progressively redescribed as multiples by later historians. Address the awkward data head-on rather than around it: Darwin sat on natural selection for two decades, and the joint announcement at the Linnean Society in July 1858 was assembled as a single event by intermediaries who controlled the documentary evidence on both sides. Does delay of that magnitude cut for inevitability (the idea was in the air) or against it (it took one particular person to be forced into print)?

Say what my view predicts that the rival great-individual account does not, which defeaters a committed defender would concede as counting against the position and which they would dismiss, and whether the appeals to selection effects and to field-readiness have insulated the thesis from refutation. If they have, I want an honest verdict on whether that is fatal for a claim that presents itself as empirical, or an acceptable feature of historical sociology in particular. Arguments and cases you can state precisely are enough; I do not need a bibliography.
