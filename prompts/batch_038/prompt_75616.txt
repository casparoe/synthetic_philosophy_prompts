I am a doctoral student working with a supervisor who is unusually strict about the vocabulary she allows into her papers. She will not write that a system believes, represents, or is about anything unless she is prepared to say that the content is the system's own rather than borrowed from an interpreter, and in three years she has never once stated the test she is applying. What follows is her record of verdicts, gathered from seminar and from her marginal comments on drafts. I have tried to state each case exactly as it was put to her, including the ones where I suspect she was being hasty.

1. A bimetallic strip lying on a workbench, considered in isolation: not about anything. She says it carries information about ambient temperature in the way a footprint carries information about a foot, and she refuses to call that representation.
2. The same strip wired into a furnace controller that holds a house near twenty degrees: now she grants the system a genuine, if exiguous, belief about the temperature of the room — while insisting that the belief belongs to the whole control loop, not to the strip.
3. A free-living bacterium swimming up a chemical gradient, of a species no one observed before the twentieth century: genuine belief, minimal but not derived. She adds that she denies it any consciousness or felt quality whatsoever and considers consciousness beside the point.
4. A strain of that species engineered last year so that a novel receptor fires in the presence of a synthetic molecule, the firing being read by a laboratory pump while the cell itself does nothing with it: no belief of the cell's about the molecule. The content, she says, belongs to the engineers.
5. A coin sensor in a vending machine, calibrated in one country to a particular coin, then shipped to a country where a look-alike coin of a different currency triggers the very same physical state. Her verdict: the content of that state changed, and changed to something disjunctive covering both coin kinds, although nothing inside the sensor altered. She also insists the content is not ours — nobody reads the sensor; the machine's own economy does.
6. That machine unplugged and warehoused for a decade and then scrapped: during the warehouse years it had no content at all. In her phrase, the aboutness went out when the coupling did.
7. A surveyor's field notebook, written in a private shorthand he never explained, recovered after his death and never decoded by anyone: it still carries the content he gave it, and that content is derived — derived from a mind, but surviving the extinction of every reader.
8. A present-day conversational model: its apparent beliefs are derived from us, because its states matter only as tokens in our practices. She volunteered a caveat here — that she would reconsider if such a system were ever shown to regulate its own ongoing behavior by those states with no audience at all.

She has also mentioned, but never applied in front of us, a diagnostic. Hold the internal constitution of a state fixed and alter what it is coupled to. If the content travels with the coupling, the account in play is a wide or indicator account. If the content stays put and the system is thereby in error, the account is historical. If the question has no answer until you specify who is reading the state, the account makes content derivative.

Here is the case I now have to bring her. A moored oceanographic buoy was built to sample sea-surface temperature. Two years into its deployment the telemetry link failed; nothing anywhere receives its readings, the operating company has been dissolved, and every engineer who worked on it has retired or died. The buoy remains powered. It still samples, still writes each sample to a buffer nobody will ever read, and still acts on those samples, trimming ballast and closing intake valves when its sensor state registers cold. Its sensor has also drifted, stably and seasonally: the state that once fired only below a threshold temperature now fires below that temperature or above a threshold salinity, and the two conditions never co-occur where the buoy sits. Because the salinity cycle in that water is regular, the state has in addition become a dependable herald of the arrival of a particular current, and the buoy's ballast behavior is now better predicted by treating the state as being about that current than by treating it as being about temperature or about either triggering condition.

I would like the following, in this order. First, lay out the candidate rules her eight verdicts leave open — including at least one on which the buoy turns out to have no genuine content at all — and say, for each, which precedents it handles easily and which it has to stretch or explain away. Second, state what each candidate rule implies for the buoy, and not merely whether the buoy has beliefs but what the content of those beliefs would be: temperature, the disjunction, the current, or a temperature belief that is now systematically false. Third, give me your best guess at her verdict, with the reasons for preferring that rule over the others, and tell me which single precedent is doing the most work — in particular, whether the coin sensor, the warehoused machine, or the undecoded notebook is the closest analog, since the buoy resembles all three in some respect and differs from all three in others. Fourth, if you think her record genuinely underdetermines the case, say what principle she would be committing herself to with each possible answer, and describe one further case, concrete enough that I could put it to her next week, that would separate the surviving rules where the buoy cannot.

Please do not bother with references, a bibliography, or a survey of who has defended what; I know that literature and will supply the citations myself. What I need is the reconstruction of her rule and the reasoning about the new case.
