ok so context first: I'm in a philosophy of cognitive science seminar and our unit was Hofstadter's careenium from GEB — the thought experiment where you imagine the brain as a box full of molecules careening around, and the only interesting entities are the big cloud-level patterns he calls simmballs, which are meant to be the symbols. The pitch is that no individual molecule's trajectory matters; what matters is simmballs triggering and inhibiting each other. My seminar partner and I got assigned an adversarial collaboration, and the prof wants an actual joint statement we could both put our names on — not a dialogue, not a summary of who wins, a constructive document. He's a hardline molecularist: all the real causation happens at the level of the molecules, and simmball talk is bookkeeping that helps us predict but describes no extra push. I'm on the other side: the simmball level makes causal claims that aren't just a redescription of the molecular ones, and in some cases the molecular story literally can't say what needs saying.

We've been arguing past each other for like six weeks, so help me draft it properly. Here's what needs to be in it.

1. A numbered section of agreed claims — stuff about the careenium setup itself, about what a complete molecular description would and wouldn't tell you, and about which questions are obviously just epistemic. Make these non-vacuous; if a claim is true only because it's been watered down to nothing, cut it.

2. Two cases we invented that we both agree bear on the dispute, with each side's prediction written down *before* any arguing:
   - **Shuffle.** There's an R-simmball (a stable rotating cloud) drifting toward a little valve it reliably trips. Freeze the box, scramble every molecule's position and velocity while keeping the cloud-level pattern exactly intact, unfreeze. Does the valve open?
   - **Hollow R.** Build the same cloud-level pattern deliberately, but distribute the density so the region that would normally strike the valve is nearly empty — the pattern is right at the descriptive level, the local molecular detail is wrong. Does the valve open?
   Then I want the joint statement to pin down what each of us concedes. If hollow-R fails to trip the valve, does he get to say the simmball never had any push at all, or only that *this* one was a counterfeit? And do I get to say the simmball's causal powers are real but statistically rather than strictly reliable? Say which of those concessions each side would actually sign.

3. The overdetermination complaint, worded so that I'd accept it as a genuine problem and not a strawman — i.e. that the molecular story is already causally sufficient for the valve opening, so anything I add looks like a second redundant cause — plus my best reply in wording he'd admit is a reply rather than a dodge.

4. A disagreements table: claim | his wording | my wording | substantive or merely verbal. Each side's column has to be phrased so that side would happily sign its own row. Where a row is verbal, name the word doing the work. My suspicion is it's "cause" — difference-making counterfactual dependence versus a continuous physical process carrying something from one event to the next — but test that, and if I'm wrong about where the verbal part sits, tell me.

5. A cruxes section: the sub-questions where settling it would move at least one of us, each with our predictions recorded in advance. One I want in there is whether a full molecular simulation of a careenium could in principle predict and explain its behaviour with zero symbolic vocabulary. He'll say that's just computation being hard, so the point is epistemic not metaphysical — I want the document to say whether that response settles the matter or quietly assumes his side of it.

6. Close with a short section on what would actually make each of us give ground, including anything we agree could count against our own view.

No strawmanning either camp, and if you decide a chunk of this fight is just about words, be blunt about which chunk and why.
