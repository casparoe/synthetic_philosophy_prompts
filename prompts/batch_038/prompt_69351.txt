We're doing the axiom of choice in my foundations of math seminar and a guy in my problem set group wrote up something that apparently proves way more than the theorem we were assigned. The assignment was just to fill in the gaps in the Zorn's lemma proof that every vector space has a basis. He insisted you can swap "vector space" for "abelian group" and "basis" for "basis" and literally nothing else has to change, and handed me this:

(1) Zorn: any nonempty poset in which every chain has an upper bound has a maximal element.
(2) Fix a nonempty abelian group A. Let P be the collection of independent subsets of A — S is independent if no nontrivial integer combination of finitely many distinct members of S equals 0 — ordered by inclusion.
(3) P is nonempty, since the empty set is independent.
(4) If C is a chain in P, then the union of C is an upper bound for C in P: any dependence among finitely many elements of that union would live inside a single member of C, and members of C are independent.
(5) So by (1), P has a maximal element S.
(6) S generates A. Otherwise pick a in A that isn't in the subgroup generated by S; then S together with a is still independent, contradicting maximality.
(7) An independent generating set is a basis, so A is free abelian.
(8) A was arbitrary, so every abelian group is free.

Obviously that's false — Z/2Z isn't free. I've checked (4) like six times and it seems airtight, and the vector space proof in our text has a step that says basically the same thing as (6) word for word. So where's the leak? And once you find it, tell me what the fix costs. I'd rather keep (1) than anything else since the whole unit leans on it, but if the repair is to insert some hypothesis I want to know which structures the argument genuinely goes through for, and whether the vector space version is covertly relying on that same extra hypothesis too.
