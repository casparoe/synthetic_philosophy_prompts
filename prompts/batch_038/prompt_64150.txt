So I'm TAing a second-year phil-of-mind seminar Thursday — 18 of them, mostly cog sci and neuro kids — and I've got 45 minutes at the whiteboard on higher-order theories of consciousness. The prof set the whole unit up around what-it's-like-ness, and they've all had a perception-and-attention course, so they walk in knowing global workspace theory and knowing confidence ratings as a measure of awareness. That combination is my problem. Half the class is going to hear "higher-order representation" and file it as a slightly fancier version of broadcasting; the other half is going to hear it as introspection, i.e. a second judgement stacked on top of a feel that was already there and already conscious.

Don't explain the theory to me, I know it. Plan how I should explain it. What I need:

- The order the pieces go in and what depends on what. Where does the transitive-vs-creature consciousness distinction land relative to the "a state is conscious iff accompanied by a suitable non-conscious thought to the effect that one is in it" claim? And do I raise the regress worry early, so the non-consciousness of the higher-order thought is motivated by it, or late, as an objection I then have to absorb?

- The example that carries it. I want to use inattentional blindness — count the passes, the gorilla walks through, subject reports seeing nothing — but I suspect it's a trap: it lets them hear the theory as an attention story, and the deflationary line says there was no rich unconscious gorilla-state sitting there waiting to be higher-order'd about. Either tell me how to use it without it collapsing, or give me a better carrier and say what that carrier has to have and what it absolutely must not have.

- The misconception map: which wrong readings turn up, and the exact beat in the sequence where each one gets headed off rather than answered afterwards. In particular, script the moment for the kid who says if the higher-order thought isn't itself conscious how can it make anything feel like anything, and the one who says so on your theory I could have the higher-order thought without the pain and I'd still be in pain, which is just false.

- One closing question, answerable in two or three sentances, that would tell me whether they've got the difference between this and an access/broadcasting account — not whether they can recite the defintion back.

- What I cut. 45 minutes is nothing. I'm leaning toward dropping the dispositionalist variant and the version where the higher-order state represents the quality as out there in the world rather than in the mind. Tell me what goes, and what I say out loud when someone asks about the thing I cut.

Numbered beats with a minute count on each. Hard cap 650 words for the whole plan, don't pad it.
