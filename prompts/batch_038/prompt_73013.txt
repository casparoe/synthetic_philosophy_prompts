For the past decade my working language has been causal inference: potential outcomes and the well-definedness assumptions they require, structural causal models, the do-operator and the three-level hierarchy of association, intervention, and counterfactual, identification, the fundamental problem of causal inference, and — when I am designing a study rather than analyzing one — mechanism design, with types, response functions, and incentive compatibility. I read that literature natively. I have essentially no training in philosophy of religion; I arrived at Molinism through a survey article and a couple of encyclopedia entries, and I want to know whether it is saying anything I can recognize.

Here is the position as I currently understand it. Please correct the summary before you translate it, since I suspect I have garbled at least one piece. Molina, a sixteenth-century Jesuit, distinguished three logical moments in divine knowledge. Natural knowledge covers all necessary truths and the whole space of possible worlds. Middle knowledge covers the counterfactuals of creaturely freedom: for every possible agent and every complete set of circumstances in which that agent could be placed, what that agent would freely choose. Free knowledge covers the actual world and follows God's decision to create. The middle layer is supposed to be the interesting one. Those counterfactuals are contingent; they are not made true by God's will; and — this is the part I cannot parse — they are not made true by the agent's character or dispositions either, since then the choice would not be free. God consults them and then selects which circumstances to place creatures in, and that is how providence is meant to be exhaustive without anyone's freedom being overridden. Scripture is supposed to contain examples of such counterfactuals, as when a prophetic saying reports what one city's inhabitants would have done had the signs performed in another city been performed there. The rival Dominican line, as I understand it, holds that God knows what creatures would do because he has decreed to move them, so the middle layer is either empty or collapses into the other two. The standard objection to Molinism is the grounding objection: if nothing — not God, not the agent's nature, not the circumstances — makes a counterfactual of freedom true, there is no fact of the matter for God to know.

Now do the translation.

1. Give me the mapping first, item by item: counterfactual of creaturely freedom; the complete set of circumstances in which an agent is placed; the freedom of the act; natural, middle, and free knowledge; the decree to actualize a world; the grounding objection; the Dominican alternative. For each, name the closest counterpart in my vocabulary and say exactly how close the fit is.

2. Then reconstruct the Molinist argument — that middle knowledge secures both exhaustive providence and libertarian freedom — as an argument in my framework. Something along the lines of a principal who cannot touch the units' response functions but knows each of them exactly, and who can therefore obtain what he wants by choosing the assignment, subject to which assignment-and-response-function combinations are jointly consistent. State what the Molinist's God can and cannot do in those terms, and whether the freedom claim survives as a constraint on the principal, a property of the response function, or nothing at all.

3. Three pressure points I want handled explicitly rather than papered over:
   - Determinacy without determination. In my framework a counterfactual has a determinate value only relative to a structural model together with the exogenous noise; the potential outcome is treated as well-defined, but its determinacy is an assumption we make to get identification, not something derived. Does that hand the Molinist an existence proof — a respectable framework in which determinate, individually unverifiable counterfactuals about a unit's response are unobjectionable — or does it merely relocate the grounding objection inside an assumption? Is there any version of a truth with no truthmaker that my vocabulary can express at all?
   - The collapse worry. If God's middle knowledge is knowledge of the noise realization that settles each counterfactual, it looks like part of his knowledge of the whole space of models, which is the natural-knowledge layer, and the middle layer disappears. Trace this carefully. Is the collapse forced by my framework, or is there room for a counterfactual that is determinate given the complete exogenous state and yet such that the agent could have done otherwise in exactly that state? If my framework cannot even state the second requirement, say so plainly instead of approximating it.
   - The intervention worry. God's choice of circumstances is the analogue of an intervention on the agent's inputs, and in my framework an intervention is precisely an exogenous override of a variable's usual causes. If the Molinist's God is intervening, is that compatible with the claim that he does not determine the choice, or does my vocabulary simply make the distinction invisible?

4. Finish with the residue report. Which Molinist claims translate cleanly, which survive only with distortion — naming the distortion in each case — and which have no expression whatsoever in potential outcomes, structural models, or mechanism design. For the last category, tell me whether that is because my vocabulary is missing something real, some resource for agency that is undetermined yet not random, or because those claims were never clear enough to be translated. I want your judgment, not a both-sides shrug.

Two house rules. First, do not smuggle: wherever you write down a counterpart, flag whether the match is a genuine translation or a substantive and contestable philosophical claim wearing a translator's badge. If representing Molinism requires assuming that response functions are well-defined, or that every counterfactual has a value, say that you have assumed it rather than presenting it as what the doctrine says. Second, keep the theology intact: I care about the structure of the position rather than about its orthodoxy, but do not flatten it into generic theism along the way, and if my summary above has confused middle knowledge with counterfactual knowledge generally, or with foreknowledge, tell me so before building on it.
