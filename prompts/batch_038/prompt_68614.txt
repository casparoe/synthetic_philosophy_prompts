I sit on the technical review panel of a national dam-safety regulator. Our guidance has always required analysts to report a single point probability, but two analysts who independently assessed the same aging structure instead reported intervals for the probability of a catastrophic failure over the next 100 years: one gave 1 in 5,000 to 1 in 800, the other 1 in 2,000 to 1 in 1,500. The statute leaves the panel no escape — we must certify either that the risk is below 1 in 1,000 or that it is not. A second question has come up at the same time: whether the agency should formally adopt set-valued (imprecise) probability reporting for future submissions. Opponents on the panel say intervals are unactionable, since they need not rank the options before us and since conditioning on further data can widen an interval rather than narrow it; proponents say that a point estimate here is false precision, representing as settled what the evidence does not settle.

Draft the decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should contain a recommendation on this certification, a recommendation on whether to adopt set-valued reporting as policy — including, if yes, the rule for combining several analysts' reports into the agency's own credal set — and reasoning that both halves of the panel could actually engage with rather than talk past. Give the strongest consideration on the losing side its full weight, not a sentence of acknowledgment. Be committed throughout: tell me which combination rule to adopt and why, not a menu of candidates for me to choose among, and end by stating what evidence or argument would make you reverse your recommendation.
