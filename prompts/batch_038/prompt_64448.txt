I've been asked to write down my own position on the regress problem for a workshop at which the other participants will try to break it, and I want a consistency check before I present it. Here is the package as I would state it:

(1) Justification is dialectical: a belief of mine is justified only if, were it challenged, I could produce a reason for it — and that reason must itself be something I could defend if it were challenged in turn.
(2) Nothing may figure in its own justificatory ancestry. I reject any structure in which support travels around a loop, however long the loop.
(3) The chain of reasons has to stop. Infinitism looks to me like a promissory note: a chain that never completes never delivers a reason, and "there is always one more" is not a payment.
(4) What it stops in is experience. Perceptual experience supplies me with reasons that are not themselves beliefs and stand in no need of reasons of their own.
(5) Coherence is not idle: mutual support among my beliefs is part of what makes them justified, and a belief that hangs together with the rest of my system is better justified than one that does not.
(6) But mutual support can raise the standing only of items that already have some standing of their own; agreement among sources that are individually worthless cannot manufacture worth.

Where I get stuck is a case of my own. I believe the kettle is boiling because I hear it whistling. Challenged, I cite the whistling. Challenged again, all I have left is that it sounds to me as though something is whistling — and I cannot see how that item is supposed to be defensible, since a seeming is not the sort of thing that could have evidence for it.

Please audit the package rather than lecture me on the trilemma in the abstract. Tell me which pairs of commitments genuinely pull against each other and which only appear to; rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad; and for each, say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me. Then identify the single revision that buys the most. End with a last line beginning "Verdict:" followed by one of CONSISTENT, REPAIRABLE, INCOHERENT and the number of the commitment that must be revised first.
