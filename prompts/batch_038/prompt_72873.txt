Our dept runs a little podcast (about 150 listeners, mostly grad students who've had one decision theory course) and I got talked into interviewing next week's visitor myself. She's a philosopher who's spent twenty-odd years arguing that Dutch book arguments prove way less than the textbooks say. Her position, as I understand it going in:

- the coherence requirements only bind an agent because of the package principle — the assumption that a rational agent should value a bundle of bets at the sum of the values of its parts — and if you deny or restrict that assumption, the books stop generating sure losses;
- an agent who knows she's incoherent can just refuse bets when she smells a setup, so "you'd lose money for sure" only bites on agents who are naive about their own state;
- the Ramsey/de Finetti trick of reading credences off betting dispositions is underdetermined (same behavior, many credence/utility pairs), so the books are really about how to price gambles, not about what belief is;
- she's sympathetic to finitely additive but not countably additive credences, e.g. a uniform-ish distribution over the natural numbers that can't be Dutch booked by any finite bundle.

Can you write me 8–10 interview questions plus, for each one, the answer she's most likely to give and a one-sentence follow-up keyed to that specific answer rather than just repeating the question at her? I want at least one question on each of these, and I want them to actually bite:

1. why additivity-of-value should be a norm on rational belief rather than a contingent fact about how betting markets are organised — and what she says about the countable-additivity case, where the failure doesn't show up in any finite set of bets;
2. the refuse-the-bet move — if an agent can decline once she suspects she's being exploited, hasn't she conceded that coherence is only a norm for agents who can't notice their own incoherence?
3. whether her view makes incoherence costless whenever no bookie happens to be around, and whether she takes that as a reductio of the arguments or as a discovery about them;
4. the diachronic books for conditionalization and the reflection principle, which seem to need her to add up bets across times — does the package principle escape carry over, and if the book only works because her present self can predict what her future self will accept, is that a seperate objection or the same one?

Also one question putting accuracy-dominance arguments to her as the replacement, and a final one asking what would actually make her give the view up. Mark each item as "she has a good reply" or "she's genuinely stuck here," honestly — I don't want to spend the whole episode swinging at nothing. Keep the questions speakable, under about 45 words each, and skip the usual what-got-you-into-probability filler.
