So I'm leading my first overnight trip for the volunteer outdoor program I'm with (community ed, mostly total beginners) in about three weeks, and I got dragged into an argument at a gear check last weekend that I still havent gotten out of my head. Two of the senior trip leaders were going at it about something a third leader did back in the spring — I'll call him Sam — and at one point they both looked at me to break the tie, and I mumbled something about the stove fuel and changed the subject.

Here's the incident. At the trailhead Sam told his group, flat out: the river crossing would be fine, knee-deep at most, they'd be across in twenty minutes. He hadn't checked the gauge that morning. He'd phoned the ranger station and nobody answered, so he was going off snowpack numbers from a couple days earlier plus the fact that the same crossing had been easy every May for the four years he'd run it. It turned out exactly right. Everyone waded over with dry packs, nobody was scared, nobody got hurt, the group had a great weekend.

Then afterwards, at the gear check:

1. Nora said Sam shouldn't have said it. Not because it was false — it wasn't — but because he didn't know it when he said it, and if you don't know something you're not in a position to flat-out assert it no matter how reasonable your grounds feel from the inside.
2. Dev said thats an impossible standard, because on that account nearly everything anyone ever says is improper, including most of what Nora tells her own groups, and a rule that condemns basically all ordinary assertion is a bad rule rather than a deep insight about it.
3. Nora said she wasn't blaming Sam at all — he did his homework, he was honest, he'd have been just as justified saying it if he'd been wrong, that's not the issue. The issue is that the group took it from him as settled and organized the day around it, and he'd handed them a confidence he didn't actually have. She said if instead he'd said "the gauge is down and I couldn't reach the ranger, but the snowpack's low and it's been shallow every May I've been here, so I reckon we're fine — but pack for the possibility we turn around," that would have been completely unobjectionable, same information, same group, no problem.
4. Dev said that comparison proves his point rather than hers: if the cure is hedging, then what went wrong wasn't asserting-without-knowing, it was overstating how sure he was. And he said the real variable is stakes anyway — put them at a different river, one where a bad call drowns somebody, and the identical evidence makes the identical sentence outrageous, so it can't be knowledge doing the work, it's how bad it would be to be wrong.
5. Nora said stakes can't change whether he knew, because whether he knows is fixed by his evidence and not by how awful it'd be if he turned out wrong, and once you let consequences leak into knowledge then nobody knows anything about anything risky and the word stops doing any work. Then she played what I think was her strongest card: suppose the gauge had in fact been reading dangerous and Sam simply never looked, and the group had crossed anyway and gotten lucky. We'd still call his trailhead announcement out of line, and we'd call it out of line because of how things actually were, not because of how well-supported he was. Dev said that was just her saying "you have to know" again in more words.

Neither budged. Can you go through this move by move and tell me who's right at each of the five steps? I want a rating on each — right, partly right, wrong — with the reason, not a survey of the two camps and not a both-sides shrug. Please give 3 and 5 extra attention, since I think almost all the real work is happening in those two and they went by fastest. Then tell me what neither of them is distinguishing that's making them talk past each other, and whether there's a piece of the case one of them is right about that the other has no answer to. Don't bother with names and -isms, I care about the moves. And finally, practically: I'm standing at a trailhead in three weeks with eight beginners — what does any of this actually tell me I'm allowed to say?
