i TA a second-year thermal physics course and the last lecture every year ends on the demon — the one with the trapdoor. the prof lets me have the final five minutes to read something aloud and this year i want it to be a sestina. full size: six sixes, then the three-line envoy that gathers all six end-words at once.

the form is the reason i picked it, so please dont just wear the sestina as a costume. the end-word rotation is a cycle — run it one more time past the sixth stanza and the words are standing back in their original order. that's exactly the demon's situation: he's running a cycle, and a cycle has to come home, head included. so i want the envoy to be the wiping of the notebook, the step where he blanks a page so it can be used again, and i want the six stanzas to keep steering the class toward the wrong spot — line after line the natural assumption is that the bill lands at the looking, and it never does.

things it has to get right, because half the room will have done the reading and the prof will notice:

- the sorting can be done for free in principle. no toll for peeking. the price gets charged at forgetting, and its a flat rate per page blanked, not scaled by how much you found out.
- the loophole should be felt, not solved. a demon with an endless notebook never wipes anything and could keep drawing work out of one warm room forever; nothing in the world forbids a big notebook. what finishes him is that he's small, that blank pages run out, that he has to come home.
- the mess isn't destroyed, it's relocated. the disorder ends up living in the tie between the notebook and the gas. i want that as an image somebody can picture, not as a sentence about quantities.

hard constraints: no proper names anywhere (not his, not the hungarian one, not the two who fixed it afterwards), and you cant use the words entropy or information in any form. pick one of the six end-words to be a word that changes sides between the mechanism and the accounting each time it comes around — bit is the obvious candidate, a bite, a crumb, a page, a unit — but try to beat it. and dont put the formula in the poem; make the toll feel exact and small without writing it out.

voice: the demon, or the notebook itself, addressing the gas. not a villain getting caught — a bookkeeper who has to keep books. no smugness, no apology, and absolutely no circle-of-life shrug in the envoy. it should end with the payment being ordinary.

then underneath, give me a few sentences on where you took liberties with the physics: is the free-sorting claim safe the way i stated it, and is my sketch of the endless-notebook loophole the right way to put it, or am i smuggling something in? say how confident you are that nobody in that room would wince, and point at the single line or claim you're least sure of, so i know what to go check before thursday.
