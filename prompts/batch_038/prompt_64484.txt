I'm in a history-of-science course right now doing a unit on the Anthropocene and honestly I'm more confused than when I started, becuase the argument seems completely stuck. Timeline as I understand it: Crutzen and Stoermer start pushing the term around 2000, the Anthropocene Working Group gets set up, in 2019 it votes 29-4 that the Anthropocene should be a formal epoch with a mid-20th century boundary, they eventually land on Crawford Lake in Ontario with the bomb-plutonium spike around 1952 as the golden spike, and then in March 2024 the parent body -- the Subcommission on Quaternary Stratigraphy -- votes the whole thing down, largely on grounds like the sedimentary record being too shallow and the start date being absurdly recent, and some people then complain the vote itself was procedurally dodgy. Meanwhile other people want 1610 and the Orbis spike (the claim being that the colonial depopulation of the Americas shows up as a dip in CO2 from reforestation), Ruddiman-style folks say it goes back thousands of years to early farming, and a lot of humanities people use "Anthropocene" with no date attached at all, or refuse the whole framing for talking about "the anthropos" as if it wasn't specific industries and empires doing it.

So what kind of disagreement is this actually? My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the two camps are answering different questions under one label -- stratigraphers asking where you can put a boundary that correlates globally and satisfies the rules for a GSSP, everyone else asking when humans became a planetary force -- and the real fight is over which question the word belongs to and who gets to settle it. But maybe it's instead a difference about standards of evidence, or a genuine factual dispute about how fast and how globally synchronous the human signal really is, or bedrock intuitions about whether geological time should track causes or just markers. Diagnose it from what each side actually says, and tell me what would count as progress: a conceptual distinction, a better marker, a different institutional route, a change of question -- or an argument that no progress should be expected and why.
