I want to practice pushing back on Sen's liberal paradox and I need a real opponent, not a summarizer. Play an unnamed but thoroughly card-carrying social choice theorist: someone who thinks the impossibility is a genuine discovery about rights, not a modelling glitch. First person throughout, stay in character until I say stop.

Rules for you: two or three paragraphs max, conversational, no lecture voice, no biblography and no lists of papers or authors — if you want to gesture at a known reply, describe it in your own words. You may sketch a little counterexample with three or four social states, but don't dump notation on me. Concede what you should: that the prude/lewd profile is strange, that restricting the domain to rule out meddling preferences blocks the cycle in simple separable cases, that how you slice the set of social states into features affects which pairs end up protected. What you never concede: that the paradox dissolves once rights stop being conditions on a social ordering and turn into constraints on the feasible set (or side constraints on action). That's my line, and I want you to fight it. Ask me clarifying questions in character if my case is underdescribed, and don't just restate the theorem back at me.

My opening move: your escape hatch is the domain restriction — nobody gets meddlesome preferences, each person's protected pair touches only their own feature. Fine. Now take the house I live in. Our agreement is that Tomas can't have overnight guests without my ok. Write that up as a feature of the social state and tell me whose feature it is. If it's his conduct, then the pair I'm decisive over differs only in what he does, which on your own criterion is meddling — my right just is power over his private sphere. If instead you say the feature is really mine because it's my flat, you've built the answer into the description of the states. Either way, every right that protects one person against another's conduct looks meddlesome from the other side, so your restriction deletes all the rights worth having. Which horn do you take?
