I have finished my third novel, which follows a company of middle-aged reserve policemen through their first mass killing in occupied Poland and the year that follows it, and my publisher intends to print an author's note setting out what I take the book to be doing. In a fortnight I am also to appear in conversation with a historian of the period at a festival, and I would rather discover the weak joints in my position now than discover them on a stage. What follows is that position, stated as plainly as I can state it. I should like an audit of whether it hangs together.

1. The book is written in close third person on a company sergeant, and I hold that this is where its cognitive value lies: a reader who has spent three hundred pages inside the man's perceptions comes away understanding how the killing became possible in a way no monograph can deliver. I do not regard fiction as illustrative of history, or as a supplement to it.
2. I also hold that a reader who closes the book thinking that these were different men, in a different time, has been failed by the book. The distance between my sergeant and the reader is meant to shrink rather than to be confirmed.
3. In philosophy I am a situationist. The obedience experiments, the prison study, and the rather messier state of the replication record have persuaded me that settled character traits carry far less explanatory weight than ordinary talk about people assumes, and that what varies is the situation rather than the soul.
4. My method of composition is nonetheless resolutely characterological. Every scene is generated by asking what this man, being the man he is, would do; my revisions are revisions of temperament; I speak of my own drafts in the idiom of the timid man, the vain man, the man of scruple.
5. On the historians' dispute I side with Browning. The men of the battalion were not convinced fanatics; before its first action their commander offered them the option of stepping out of the line with no sanction attached, and a handful took it. My book turns on that offer, and the sergeant who accepts it is given neither punishment nor glory. I regard Goldhagen's claim — that a pervasive and peculiarly virulent antisemitism in German political culture is what made the killers willing rather than merely obedient — as refuted by the documents he himself cites.
6. Even so, I think Browning leaves the central question unanswered. Orders, comradeship, and careerism account for compliance but not for willingness, and I therefore give my men a shared idiom of contempt for their victims, absorbed without ever hardening into an articulated belief.
7. My rule for invention is plausibility rather than attestation: a detail is admissible if it is true to the period and the institution, whether or not the archive records it. Where the sources are silent I do not consider myself bound by them.
8. I hold the men in my book responsible and I refuse the exculpatory frame. Concretely: a recruit who vomits at his first action, is mocked for it, and becomes one of the company's most reliable shooters is, in my book, more culpable at the end than at the beginning — not because he was ordered into it, but because he made himself into the sort of man who does this.

I am deliberately not telling you which of these I would abandon, because I do not yet know, and I would prefer the ranking to do that work for me.

What I want is an audit, not an exposition of the historiography, which I know well. Tell me which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to, and be explicit about the distinction that dissolves each apparent case. Rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. For each, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision costs — and cost it in the book as well as in the philosophy, since a fix requiring me to surrender the sergeant's interiority is not the same price as one requiring me to re-stage a single scene. Where a commitment can be rescued by a re-reading rather than a retreat, say so and say what the re-reading forfeits elsewhere. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
