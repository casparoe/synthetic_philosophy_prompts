So I got into a four hour argument with my friend R last night about Maxwell's demon and I still can't tell who won. I want you to look at my verdicts on a bunch of cases and tell me what principle, if any, they actually add up to — and where I'm just flat wrong.

My tentative principle going in was: you can't pull work out of a single heat bath unless something forgets. The bill comes due wherever the record dies, and only there. I liked it because it makes the cost a property of an event (a memory getting wiped) rather than a substance (information).

Cases, and what I said:

1. Szilard's one-molecule box run as a cycle, demon resets its one-bit memory at the end of each go. Costs kT ln 2 per cycle, no free lunch. Solid.
2. Same box, but the demon has unbounded memory and never resets, it just keeps appending. I said fine, no violation, the entropy is sitting in the register. R said that IS the violation, you've just moved it into an infinitely long hard drive and stopped looking. I had no answer.
3. A measurement done fully reversibly — blank meter in, correlated meter out, no erasure anywhere. I said definitely free, that's Bennett's whole point. R said: then blankness is the fuel, and you know it, see case 4.
4. A blank memory chip straight off the factory line. I said sure, someone paid to put it in the all-zeros state, but that's not my problem, and anyway you could get a blank register for free by cooling it, or by measuring it and relabeling. R said "measuring it and relabeling" is you admitting the cost is a bookkeeping convention. This one still stings.
5. Quick-formatting my laptop drive. I said that's an information-destroying event so there should be a Landauer cost, tiny but real. R laughed and said nothing is destroyed at all, and if I do a full overwrite the drive gets warm for reasons that have nothing to do with entropy. I think R is right but I want to hear it properly rather than just lose the argument.
6. A purely mechanical one-way flap in a gas pipe — no memory, no measurement, just lets molecules through one direction and not the other. I said obviously fine, it's hardware, nobody calls a check valve a demon. R said it's case 2 with the memory deleted, and if the memory was what made case 2 respectable, deleting it should make the device worse, not more respectable. **This is the pair I can't reconcile.** Either the flap works and my principle is wrong, or it doesn't work and I need to be told why, because "it's just hardware" isn't an answer.
7. Feynman's ratchet and pawl with the vane side hotter than the ratchet side. Works, it's a heat engine, no demon, happy.
8. E. coli doing chemotaxis with receptor methylation as its memory. I said it's a genuine demon and it pays in ATP, but the bill is so much bigger than kT ln 2 that it can't possibly be testing Landauer's principle. R said then biology has nothing to say here at all. I half-agreed, which felt like losing.
9. Measuring a qubit and throwing away the readout without looking at it. I said no work comes out and there's no cost beyond the dephasing. R said the environment now holds the record, so I've relocated the memory again, exactly like case 2 and case 9 is just case 2 wearing a lab coat.
10. Feedback control, which is my day job: with a sensor in the loop you can extract more work from a thermal system than you can open-loop. I said that's not a second-law violation because the sensor has a noise floor and the controller has to reset. R said that's Landauer's principle with an engineering accent. Tell me whether "noise floor" is doing any independent work in my answer or whether its just decoration.
11. The one I didn't get to before R left. A demon that runs exactly once, pulls kT ln 2 out of the bath, and gets thrown in the trash with its bit still set — no cycle, no reset. I said fine, it's not cyclic. R said do it a trillion times with a trillion demons and you've built a perpetual motion machine out of landfill. I said each demon had to be prepared blank. R said congratulations, you just invoked case 4, which you called "not my problem."

So: give me the principle, or a small number of competing candidates, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a device I've never seen before. For each clause in it, tell me which case above forced you to write that clause. Then run it on cases I haven't listed — I specifically want to know what it says about a demon whose record is written into a photon that escapes to deep space, about a demon whose memory is a block of ice left to melt, and about a computer that only ever runs logically reversible gates and gets hot anyway. And be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save. If the answer is that "nothing gets pulled out of a bath unless something forgets" is just wrong and needs scrapping rather than patching, say so and tell me what replaces it — but if it's salvageable I'd rather salvage it, because I did say it out loud in front of four other people.
