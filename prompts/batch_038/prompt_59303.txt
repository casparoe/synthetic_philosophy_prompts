Suppose Pascal is alive today and someone hands him the longtermist pitch on AI: some small but nonzero chance that machine intelligence permanently wrecks (or permanently secures) the future, an absurd number of lives downstream, so expected value swamps everything and we should pour resources into alignment and governance. Looks like his wager with the deity swapped out for a training run. Would he sign on?

Commit to an answer and give reasons — I don't want a both-sides survey. Four things I want your actual verdict on:

- His payoff was infinite, so any nonzero probability settled the matter; theirs is astronomically large but finite, and the probability is exactly what's disputed. Does the math survive that downgrade, coming from the man who worked out how to divide the stakes of an interrupted game?
- Everyone repeats the calculation and skips the ending, where he tells the skeptic that he can't believe on command, so act as if he did, take the holy water, have masses said, and let habit do the work. The AI movement has the same shape: behave as though it matters, conviction follows. Proper use of his method, or counterfeit?
- He was a Jansenist — grace saves, calculation doesn't, and a faith adopted for the payout isn't faith. Does that make the secular version a betrayal of what he was doing, or is the bare bet the one piece that transfers cleanly?
- His wager had one God to bet on; theirs has many dooms pulling in oposite directions (accelerate, slow down, regulate, open-source). Fatal for him, or just noise?

Finish with a definite Pascal: for it, against it, or for it with one specific fix — and say what the fix is.
