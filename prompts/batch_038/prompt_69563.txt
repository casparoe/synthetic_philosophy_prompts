ok so context: I'm TAing an intro philosophy of science section this term and my prof wants a little ranking exercise for the natural kinds week that the students can actually argue about, but I want to run it myself first so I know where the fights are going to be. The scheme I've cobbled together goes like this. For each candidate kind on my list below, give me two numbers. First, a Blind Curator score from 0 to 10: how well a curator who had a complete physical/chemical/biological description of a bunch of instances of the kind, but had never once heard the word for it, could recover the grouping from that description alone. 10 means the description practically screams the grouping at you; 0 means you can't get there without already having the concept in hand. Second, a Retention number from 0 to 1: your subjective probability that a science a hundred and fifty years from now still treats the kind as projectible, i.e. as supporting non-accidental generalizations and inductions, rather than as a leftover of our current conceptual scheme or of a theory that got thrown out. Then produce one combined ranking sorted by Blind Curator score with ties broken by the Retention number, and after the ranking do two short things: name the single kind where you think the two numbers come apart most dramatically and say which of the two is tracking something the other one misses, and then tell me which of your own scores you'd most likely revise after a conversation with a specialist in that field and roughly how far it'd move.

Here's the list: water, gold, jade, electron, gene, species (in the biological sense), the class of planets, race as it's used in biomedical research, fish, quark flavor, major depression, invertebrates, weeds, trees, and the chemical elements taken as a kind. I've deliberately thrown in the ones students always reach for first — jade really being two different minerals, fish being paraphyletic, the Pluto business — because those are supposed to be where the Blind Curator score does interesting work, but please don't just assume they all come out low by default; I want you to actually reason through whether a curator holding the full description would land on the grouping anyway, since in some of these cases I suspect they would and my students won't.

Two constraints I care about. Don't score anything up or down on the grounds that it's socially or politically fraught — the only question here is joint-carving, and I'd rather the exercise doesn't collapse into a debate about whether it's appropriate to talk about race in class at all. And if you think my two-number scheme is just broken somehow, like if the Blind Curator score ends up being a stand-in for the Retention number on most of the items so that the seperate numbers are pointless, say that up front in a few sentences and then do the whole ranking anyway under my scheme as specified, since I still need it for thursday's section.
