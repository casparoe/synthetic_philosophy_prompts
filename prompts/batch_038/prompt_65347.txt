The practice, described as neutrally as I can, is roughly the standard post-training stack. (1) Human raters are shown pairs of candidate model outputs and asked which is better; those comparisons are used to fit a single scalar reward model that predicts the choice. (2) The policy is then optimized against that scalar, but with a penalty on how far its output distribution has drifted from the pre-optimization model — a leash measured in KL divergence, so that "too much" optimization is defined as "too far from where you started." (3) Several reward models, trained on different data slices or different seeds, are run in parallel and either averaged or min-aggregated, on the stated rationale that disagreement between them flags regions where the proxy can't be trusted. (4) Optimization is stopped early: there's a held-out set of comparisons, a win rate is tracked against a fixed baseline, and training is halted when the win rate plateaus or turns down. Someone in my reading group said there's a taxonomy that splits Goodhart effects into regressional, extremal, causal and adversarial kinds, and I've never been able to work out which of those each of these four defenses is actually supposed to block — that's part of what I want from you.

So: what theory of value, and of evidence, does this apparatus presuppose? Start with the thing that strikes me as doing the most unexamined work — that a comparative judgment by a rater is a noisy *reading* of a single underlying scalar quantity, so that disagreement is error and agreement is signal. What has to be true for that to be the right description rather than a convenient fiction? Then the leash: it seems to presuppose that proxy validity is a function of *distance from a starting point* rather than of what the model is actually doing — as though the region near a model trained by next-token prediction on the internet is privileged. Is that a coherent epistemology of a measuring instrument, and what is the reference model playing the role of? Here's the inconsistency I keep coming back to, and I want you to take it seriously rather than dissolve it: step (3) treats proxy error as idiosyncratic noise that uncorrelated models will average away, while step (4) treats proxy error as something an optimizer will actively hunt down and exploit, which sounds like a systematic and reachable property of the proxy. Can one and the same pipeline presuppose both? Is there a single theory that licenses all four steps, or is this a philosophical hotchpotch that happens to work for engineering reasons, and if so what holds it together?
