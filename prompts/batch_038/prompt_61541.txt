I have no academic training in philosophy or psychiatry, but I have been thinking for some years about Ian Hacking's idea of looping effects: the claim that classifications of human beings are interactive, so that naming a kind of person changes the people who fall under it, which in turn changes the classification itself. I would like you to analyse a real episode through that lens — the career of multiple personality disorder in North America. The outline I have in mind: for most of psychiatric history the condition was a curiosity, with only a few hundred reported cases in the entire literature; the Sybil book and film of the 1970s made it famous; it entered DSM-III in 1980 as a diagnosis in its own right; within a decade the number of diagnosed patients had multiplied enormously, the great majority of them identified by therapists using hypnosis and similar techniques; and by the mid-1990s, amid bitter disputes about recovered memories of childhood abuse, the diagnosis had lost most of its credibility and was renamed dissociative identity disorder.

Please do two things. First, map the loop with some precision. Separate what I have come to think of as two distinct circuits: a recruitment loop, in which the label produces people who would not otherwise have existed in that form at all, and a recognition loop, in which the label merely sorts people who were already presenting with something, changing the bookkeeping rather than the patients. Which of these, or which mixture, does the episode actually show, and what would count as evidence either way? Address the difficulty I keep running into: any evidence about what these patients were like before the label existed is itself gathered through the apparatus the label created.

Second, turn the lens round. Does this episode confirm the theory of looping effects, or does it reveal that the theory cannot lose — if the numbers soar the loop is running, if they collapse the loop has reversed? And once clinicians and patients have heard the theory described, does that knowledge itself become part of the loop? Please be explicit about where the theory falls silent rather than making it speak, and about which parts of the story remain genuinely contested. Write in British English. I am not asking for references or a reading list; I want your own reasoning about the case.
