I retired four years ago after thirty-seven years as an experimental physicist, most of them spent designing measurement apparatus and arguing with colleagues about what a given instrument's readout could and could not be said to establish. Since leaving the laboratory I have taken up philosophy with more seriousness than my former colleagues expected, and I have been working through questions in the philosophy of measurement and social ontology that turn out to be uncomfortably close to my old professional life. One claim I cannot let go of is the familiar assertion that the world is becoming less violent. The optimists point to rates; the pessimists point to totals, or to the widening of what counts as violence at all; and both sides seem to believe they are describing the same underlying fact. I have come to suspect that a great deal of the disagreement is about denominators, coding rules, and standardization rather than about the world, and I should like a case constructed that makes this suspicion precise — or refutes it.

For this purpose, please act as an expert in the philosophy of measurement and social ontology who is also fluent in practical demography and quantitative historiography. Think carefully before writing, and begin with a short brainstorming section of no more than about 150 words in which you list two or three candidate mechanisms by which a population's composition could shift between two dates, choose one, and say why it will serve. Then construct the case itself. I want an invented country — nothing real, no real datasets, no real historical events, and please cite no studies, since I have no interest in chasing references and you would only be guessing. Set it somewhere plausible for a mid-twentieth-century industrializing state with a functioning national statistical office, because you will need the record-keeping. The scenario proper should run no longer than roughly 350 words and should be accompanied by a small table of numbers simple enough that I can check them by hand. It must satisfy the following three specifications simultaneously:

First, between the two dates, the absolute number of deaths from interpersonal violence rises while the crude per-capita rate falls. Second, that rate decline is entirely a composition artifact: an appropriately standardized rate — please define direct standardization or the at-risk restriction on first use, as I have no training in demography — rises over the same interval, and the composition shift must have a mundane cause unrelated to the violence itself, so that I am not merely being shown war killing off the killers. Third, there is a genuine coding-boundary decision, involving some class of deaths your statistical office must classify either as violence or as misfortune, and the two available codings must not move all three measures in the same direction: each coding should change the verdict on at least one measure while leaving at least one other measure's verdict untouched.

After the scenario, demonstrate that the design works. Lay out the two-by-three grid — two codings against total count, crude rate, and standardized rate — give the verdict in each cell with the arithmetic shown, and state plainly what a reader would conclude about whether this country became more or less violent depending on which cell they were handed. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing most of the work, what happens to the grid if that feature is removed — in particular if the composition shift is instead caused by the violence rather than independent of it — and how a defender of the long-run-decline thesis would attack the case: whether as unrepresentative, as a gerrymandered choice of denominator, or as smuggling structural harm into the category of violence. Finally, state what the case is supposed to show, namely which features of it are convention-dependent and whether anything in it survives every admissible coding. A case that merely stipulates its verdicts rather than generating them from the numbers fails the brief, so please be honest if any of my three specifications cannot in fact be satisfied together, and say which one you would have to relax and why.
