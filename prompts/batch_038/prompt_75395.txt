Two colleagues of mine — Nina, who works on language, and Owen, who builds models of reasoning — spent most of a whiteboard session last week arguing over a letter-string problem of the kind used in research on fluid concepts and analogical slippage. I was there taking notes, could not tell which of them was winning, and lost the room before they finished.

The problem had been put to the group cold, with no candidate answers on the table. Consider strings over a five-letter alphabet, A B C D E, in that order, where E is the last letter and nothing follows it. If ABC becomes ABD, what does CDE become?

Here is their exchange as I recorded it.

(1) Nina said CDA. Both strings are three-letter ascending runs, so the correspondence is rightmost letter to rightmost letter, and the content of the transformation is to advance the rightmost letter. Reading the order as closed preserves that content intact, whereas refusing to read it that way leaves the transformation undefined at exactly one string in the whole domain — an exception nothing in the problem motivates.

(2) Owen said CDD. The alphabet and its endpoints are stipulated; the description of the transformation is not, since many descriptions fit the step from ABC to ABD equally well. So when a description fails to apply, the thing to revise is the description, not the stipulated domain: slip successor to predecessor and leave every letter as given. Nina's answer, he said, requires treating the stipulation that nothing follows E as false in order to rescue a phrase.

(3) Nina said that principle cannot be right, because it misfires in an obvious case. Take the months: a finite order with a designated last element, and a transformation defined as advancing the rightmost element — formally the same shape as the letters — and nobody hesitates that Oct Nov Dec goes to Oct Nov Jan. The stipulation for the letters gave an order and a last element but said nothing about whether that order is to be read as open or closed, and in a domain invented for the occasion, reading it as closed is not a distortion but a choice among equally available ones. She added that "revise the description rather than the domain" is itself just one more description of the task, and no better entitled to serve as the tie-breaker than "preserve the relation."

(4) Owen said that concession was fatal to both of them. If the domain can be read either way and the transformation can be described either way, and nothing in the problem says which revision to make, then there is no fact of the matter about what CDE becomes: CDA and CDD are each coherent completions, and the sense that one of them is the answer is only the feeling of having retrieved one description before the other. His conclusion was that the analogy blocks at CDE, and that anyone reporting an answer here is reporting a preference with extra steps.

(5) Nina said there is a criterion, and it is convergence. She had given the problem to roughly twenty people in the session without offering any answers, and a clear majority said CDA; separately, when people were asked to put into words what had happened to ABC, they produced several different phrasings — the rightmost letter, the highest letter in the string, the last member of the ascending run — every one of which locates the change at the same position in CDE. When independent descriptions and independent people land on the same result, she said, that is what separates a compelling analogy from a merely possible one.

The argument ended there.

Please adjudicate it move by move rather than in the aggregate. Rate each of the five moves as clearly right, mostly right, mixed, mostly mistaken, or clearly mistaken, and justify the rating. Give your answer as exactly five numbered items, one per move in order, each no longer than three sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. Within item 5, also state the strongest reply available to Nina's convergence claim and say whether it succeeds. If there is something both of them are missing, place it inside the item for the move where the omission first becomes visible instead of adding a sixth item.
