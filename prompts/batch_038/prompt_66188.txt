I am the group welfare and food-safety manager for an aquaculture company that farms Atlantic salmon and Pacific whiteleg shrimp, and I hold a HACCP lead certification. Everything I do professionally lives in one register: hazards and hazard analysis, critical control points, critical limits, monitoring procedures with defined frequencies and responsibilities, corrective actions triggered when a limit is breached, verification, and records. Where the animals are concerned I work in the same grammar — operational welfare indicators, sampling plans, audit findings graded as minor or major non-conformances, and the corrective-action plans that close them out. I have read the animal-ethics literature only in summary, and one strand of it I have never managed to turn into anything I could write into a manual: the argument that raising welfare standards in aquaculture is not progress toward a defensible practice but a way of entrenching an indefensible one — that a fish given a life worth living and then stunned on a slaughter line satisfying every welfare specification has still been wronged, and that the wrong is not a deviation to be corrected but the design of the process itself.

Restate that argument in my register, premise by premise, as faithfully as you can: what is the hazard; is there any point at which it could be controlled; what would the critical limit and monitoring procedure look like; what corrective action follows from a breach; how would the claim be verified. Where the fit is only partial, use it, but state exactly how much strain it is under — and do not quietly import the assumptions of my framework and then report that the argument fits them.

Test the translation on a concrete case rather than in the abstract: the eyestalk ablation of female shrimp broodstock, performed without anaesthesia to increase spawning, where the thing objected to is a deliberate procedure carried out on healthy animals for production reasons and written into the specification rather than a departure from it.

Then give me the residue report. Which premises translate cleanly? Which survive only if their meaning is altered, and what precisely was altered? Which have no expression at all in a system built to detect and correct departures from an intended process? Note, if it matters, that my indicator set is weighted toward production-linked measures — growth, deformity rates, fin and scale condition, mortality, stress physiology — and say whether that weighting is itself part of what the objection targets. Finally, diagnose the silence: does it show that my vocabulary can represent harms only as failures, and is therefore structurally blind to harms consisting in a process working correctly, or does it show that a claim which cannot be stated as anything monitorable, limitable, or correctable is not, in the end, a claim about how the operation should be run?
