**Question (20 points):** "In the Sleeping Beauty problem, Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday and a fair coin is tossed. If it lands Heads she is awakened and interviewed on Monday only; if it lands Tails she is awakened and interviewed on both Monday and Tuesday. After each awakening she is given a drug that makes her forget it, so that during any interview she cannot tell which day it is or whether she has been awakened before. When she is awakened and asked for her credence that the coin landed Heads, what should it be? Elga answers 1/3 and Lewis answers 1/2. Reconstruct the disagreement as precisely as you can: say what the two positions have in common, identify the premise or principle at which they part company, and say whether what remains is a substantive dispute or a merely terminological one. Close with your own assessment of which side is better supported."

**Student's answer:** "Both Elga and Lewis accept the description of the experiment, and both accept that Beauty's credence in Heads on Sunday, before she is put to sleep, is 1/2, since the coin is fair and she has been told the whole protocol in advance. They also agree about one endpoint: if she is awakened and told that it is Tuesday, her credence in Heads has to be 0, because a Tuesday awakening is possible only on Tails. The entire dispute is therefore about what happens in between, at an awakening where she has no information about which day it is.

Elga's argument divides the space of possibilities into three centered situations that are, from inside the experiment, subjectively indistinguishable: Heads-and-Monday, Tails-and-Monday, and Tails-and-Tuesday. Conditional on Tails, a Monday awakening and a Tuesday awakening are exactly alike to her, so her credence should split evenly between them. And conditional on its being Monday, an awakening was guaranteed whichever way the coin fell, so Heads-and-Monday and Tails-and-Monday should get equal weight as well. That gives three equiprobable, jointly exhaustive possibilities, hence 1/3 each, and in particular a credence of 1/3 in Heads. Lewis accepts the first of these two steps but denies the second: he holds that when Beauty wakes she gains no information relevant to the coin, so her credence should stay where it was, at 1/2. That is also why, on Lewis's view, being told that it is Monday leaves her credence in Heads at 1/2 — the coin is fair, and a Monday awakening would have occurred either way.

The deeper difference is methodological. Lewis's position depends on treating centered evidence as a special case: instead of conditionalizing on everything Beauty has learned, he lets the Principal Principle do the work, pinning her credence to the known chance of 1/2 however many times she wakes. Elga simply conditionalizes as usual, on a total evidence that now includes the centered fact that she is awake now. That is why the thirder account feels less ad hoc: it applies one and the same updating rule throughout, whereas the halfer has to suspend that rule whenever the evidence is indexical.

There is also a practical test. Suppose that at each awakening Beauty is offered a bet that pays $1 if the coin landed Heads and costs her some fixed amount now. If she pays more than about a third of a dollar per awakening she expects to lose, because in the Tails branch she pays twice and wins nothing, so a third is the break-even price. But her credence just is the price at which she is indifferent to such a bet. So a halfer who really believed 1/2 would accept bets that lose her money, and could be pumped by anyone offering the same bet at each awakening. Halfers are thus not merely mistaken but incoherent: their degrees of belief are not those of a coherent agent.

In the end, then, the disagreement looks verbal to me. 'Credence' is doing two different jobs in the two discussions — one tied to a fair price for a bet offered once, the other to a fair price for a bet offered at every awakening — and once those jobs are separated there is no single question to which Elga answers 1/3 and Lewis answers 1/2. Elga's answer is the one to use inside the experiment, but nothing substantive turns on the difference."
