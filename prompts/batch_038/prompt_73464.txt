**Part 1 — the dispute, briefly oriented**

Samuelson's revealed preference program tried to purge consumer theory of unobservable utility: rather than postulating preferences and deriving choices, record choices and read preferences off them. For finite datasets, satisfying the generalized axiom of revealed preference is exactly what it takes for a utility-maximizing reading to be available (the Afriat-style rationalizability results). Critics have pressed two complaints from opposite directions.

1. *The emptiness complaint.* Any violation can be redescribed as preference change, unobserved context, measurement noise, or bounded rationality, so nothing can ever count decisively against the theory; the axioms function as a definition of rationality wearing empirical clothing.
2. *The interpretation complaint.* Even a perfectly consistent dataset licenses no inference about welfare or about what an agent values. Choice functions need not obey contraction or expansion consistency even for a stable agent, because what is worth choosing can depend on the menu itself — politeness, commitment, self-command, and the desire to avoid appearing greedy all drive a wedge between what a person picks and what she judges best. Defenders of a mentalist or interpretive reading reply that choice data are defeasible evidence about an agent's all-things-considered comparative evaluations, and that only this reading gives the axioms their normative and empirical bite.

**Part 2 — the case to adjudicate (stipulated; take every fact as given and do not re-derive the arithmetic)**

A laboratory subject chooses between two goods, A and B, in whole units per week, and prefers more of either good. Two budget situations are presented a week apart, each with income 10.

- Situation 1: prices (1, 2). Chosen bundle: 2 units of A and 4 units of B, costing exactly 10.
- Situation 2: prices (2, 1). Chosen bundle: 4 units of A and 2 units of B, costing exactly 10.

The bundle chosen in Situation 2 costs only 8 at Situation 1's prices, and the bundle chosen in Situation 1 costs only 8 at Situation 2's prices. Each chosen bundle was therefore strictly affordable when the other was chosen, so the data contain a two-element strict revealed preference cycle, violate the generalized axiom, and — given monotonicity — could not have been generated by any preference-maximizing agent with fixed preferences.

In a second phase the same subject is shown the union of the two budget sets and asked to select any bundle affordable under either price system. She selects 3 units of A and 3 units of B, a bundle costing 9 under both price systems and hence available in Situation 1, where she took (2, 4) instead. Her selection from the larger menu fails the contraction-consistency condition that her selection from the smaller menu would have to satisfy for any single fixed ordering to generate the whole choice function.

**Part 3 — the proposal at issue**

To rescue the behaviorist program from the emptiness complaint, suppose a methodologist introduces the following decision rule, which we will call the Reflection Filter. A revealed preference violation counts as evidence against the theory only if it survives all three screens; violations failing any screen are to be reclassified as preference drift or framing artifacts and set aside.

- (i) *Repetition*: the same budget situations recur in the same order on at least three occasions separated by days, with the same choice each time.
- (ii) *Revision opportunity*: after all budget situations have been displayed together, the subject is permitted to revise her earlier choices, and the violation persists in the revised data.
- (iii) *Menu invariance*: the violation is not of the contraction-consistency type exhibited in the second phase.
