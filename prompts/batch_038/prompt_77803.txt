I'm presenting next week in my philosophy of language seminar on Kripke's Wittgenstein, and before I do I want to pin down my own credence rather than hedge behind the dialectic. The thesis I'll assess, stated as precisely as I can manage: no wholly naturalistic fact about an individual speaker — nothing about the dispositions she has, the functional or computational organization of her brain, the causal history of her usage, or the regularities of the community she was trained in — can constitute the determinate fact that she means addition rather than quus by "+". So the skeptic is right, and no straight solution in any naturalistic key is available.

My own number hovers near 0.55, but I can't tell whether that reflects a real view or just an unwillingness to side against either my seminar partner (a Lewis-style idealized dispositionalist, around 0.2) or our TA (a quietist who thinks the thesis is unintelligible rather than false, and so wouldn't give a number at all).

Give me your credence, decomposed. Please organize your answer under exactly these headings, in this order:

1. Bottom line — one sentence containing your number, with nothing else.
2. Sharpening the proposition (about 100 words) — how you read the thesis, and any ambiguity you think materially affects the number.
3. Sub-credences (roughly 250 words) — between four and six factors, each with a probability and at most two lines of justification. At minimum I want these treated: whether a speaker's actual dispositions are ruled out by the finitude and error problems; whether idealizing the speaker — dispositions of an ideally rationalized self, or of a brain under a functional description — recovers determinacy or only relocates it; whether the normativity of meaning, the gap between what I would say and what I ought to say, is a genuine constraint on any candidate account or an artifact of how the paradox is posed; and whether the paradox is even intelligible for a solitary speaker, or is essentially about public, teachable language.
4. Combination (about 100 words) — how your factors compose into the number in section 1, and whether you treated them as independent, as screening one another off, or as merely heuristic.
