I've been going around in circles on when you're allowed to trust your own read of your situation versus the stats on people like you, and I think my answers contradict each other. Here's what I actually think, case by case:

My friend is redoing his kitchen and swears it'll be six weeks. Everyone I know who's done a renovation says three months at minimum, and he knows that. I think he's just wrong to plan around six — knowing your own kitchen in detail doesn't get you out of the base rate.

My sister says she'll finish her PhD in two years. She's got two papers out and most of a chapter drafted. I think she's entitled to expect two years even though the average is like five, and that doesn't feel like wishful thinking to me the way the kitchen one does.

When I started my landscaping business I read that most of them fail within five years, and I basically decided that number wasn't about me. I dont feel bad about that. But when my cousin pulled the same move with a sports betting system he'd worked out, I told him he was kidding himself.

My nephew wants to audition for a music program that takes maybe 4% of applicants. Half of me says the honest reference class is everyone who auditions; the other half says it's kids who've practiced four hours a day since they were nine, which he is. And I've noticed I can almost always find some narrower group that makes the odds look good, which seems like a bad sign — except sometimes the narrow group really is the right one, like with my sister.

Then the one I keep flip flopping on: my daughter wants to try out for a pro soccer team. I want to tell her to be realistic, but if she asked me straight out what her chances are, I don't know whether to give her the base rate or my read on her.

Is there a single principle that says when private knowledge of your own case legitimately overrides the statistics and when it's just you flattering yourself? Please state it as exactly as you can, say what it comes out to for the daughter case and for a project where there's no real precedent to draw a class from at all, and tell me which of the verdicts above it can't save — I'd rather give one up than end up with a rule that's just a pile of exceptions.
