A pattern I keep running into, and I don't know whether it has a name. Two people are arguing over something that looks substantive — say, whether a given language model understands English, or whether a particular national emissions plan counts as reaching net zero. Someone plays the taboo-the-word move: points out that they agree on every non-contested fact and are merely deploying a term differently, and asks them to restate their positions without it. They manage this, and the original dispute does look dissolved. But the argument immediately reignites one level up. Each party now insists that the other has the meaning of the contested term wrong, and each marshals evidence for this — ordinary usage, technical usage, the purpose the term was introduced to serve, how careful speakers employ it — as though meaning were a matter of fact with a determinate answer. Neither is offering a stipulation or proposing that they simply settle on a usage; both take themselves to be asserting something that could turn out mistaken. And the diagnosis that deflated the first dispute looks equally applicable to this one, which suggests it would reignite a level higher still.

What I want from you is the carving, not just a tag: which established concepts come nearest, how this differs from each of them, whether any of them genuinely covers it, whether there is a principled reason the ascent terminates rather than continuing indefinitely, and — if nothing fits — a coinage with a definition and an account of what would fall outside it.
