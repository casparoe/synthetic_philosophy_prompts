my consciousness seminar does this thing in week 8 where everybody posts their own view of consciousness to the forum and then the section spends two days tearing it apart, and mine is due tomorrow. i've gone for the higher-order family but i have this nagging feeling that my package doesn't actually cohere and i can't find the crack. can you audit it?

here's the list, in the order i wrote it down:

1. the transitivity principle, roughly: a mental state is conscious only if i'm aware of being in it in the right non-inferential, non-observational way. consciousness is a matter of a state's being represented by some further state of the same creature — not an intrinsic property of the first-order state itself.
2. that further state is never itself conscious. i'll happily bite this, otherwise you get a regress. we're not aware of our awareness, we're aware *through* it.
3. but i also think you can catch yourself catching yourself — like noticing that you're checking whether you're really angry. that seems plainly possible and i don't want to lose it.
4. inner sense, not higher-order thought. the awareness is quazi-perceptual; it doesn't require that the subject possess concepts like pain or seeing-red.
5. which is how i get animals and preverbal infants to be conscious without mangling the theory. that datum is non-negotiable for me, i'm not saying a dog's experience is second-rate or that it's unconscious.
6. misrepresentation is real and i want to affirm it: the scanner can fire with no first-order target and then there is a genuine conscious experience. the nitrous-at-the-dentist kind of case, where someone seems to be having agony in a tooth and there is no first-order pain state there at all.
7. separately i think of the inner sense as a detection mechanism whose reliability is an empirical matter. some people have better scanners than others; a better scanner yields more accurate knowledge of your own mind. introspection can be good or bad at its job, and that's a fact about the mechanism.
8. i find the self-blindness argument convincing: a rational creature just can't have a pain and be in no position whatsoever to know that it has it. in our case the awareness comes free with the state.
9. the rock worry doesn't touch me. the higher-order state ascribes the target state to me, it's first-personal, so my thinking about a rock can't render the rock conscious. that objection seems just confused.
10. and the whole thing dissolves the hard problem rather than solving it — once the higher-order story is told there's nothing left over to feel like anything, and i'm not a dualist or a mysterian about it.

for what it's worth i'm pretty sure 2 and 3 are fine together and i'm confident about 6 and 7, those feel like claims about different things to me, and i think 8 is basically uncontroversial among people who aren't already committed. so don't waste time on those unless i'm wrong.

what i want: go through all the pairs and tell me which ones genuinely can't both be held, ranked by how bad the trouble is — not a flat list where every tension is equally serious. for each real one, say which item has to go or has to be weakened, and what the cheapest version of that fix costs me. tell me explicitly which of the apparent tensions dissolve and why, including whether i'm right about 2/3 and 9. then, holding 5 fixed, what's the single revision that cleans up the most wreckage? and last thing: is 10 actually consistent with 4, or am i just gesturing at an inner sense and calling it an explanation?
