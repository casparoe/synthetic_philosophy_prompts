When I told my uncle, he said "you can't prove a negative" and that was that, conversation over.

I want to know which of them, if any, would actually rescue the original finding, and which would if anything cut against it (my half-formed worry is that if you really can't prove a negative, then you also can't prove the negative he's leaning on, that the charity's sample was somehow special). And does the answer change if you're a Bayesian rather than a frequentist? I've come across equivalence testing and Bayes factors but have no idea whether they'd make the slogan true or just beside the point.
