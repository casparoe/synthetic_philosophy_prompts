Bit of an odd one and I'd rather not ask my actual flatmates, so you're getting it instead.

I'm a novelist — third book out in the spring — and there's one bound proof copy knocking around the flat. It contains a long, extremely graphic chapter that is unmistakably about a friend of ours, and not kindly. After a much worse row last year, the three of us agreed to settle anything like this by two rules we all signed up to:

1. Everyone has absolute final say over whether they themselves read it. Nobody can be made to, nobody can be stopped.
2. If all three of us prefer one outcome to another, that's the outcome we go with.

So there's really three possibilities: Mo reads it, Dez reads it, or it goes back in my drawer and neither of them do. Mo would rather no one read it at all, but if someone's going to she'd rather it were her, since she can be trusted to keep it to herself and tell me what to cut. Dez says he'd get far more fun out of watching Mo read it then out of reading it himself, but if she won't, he certainly will. Me? I'd rather Mo than Dez, obviously.

And now we're stuck. Whichever of the three we land on, it loses to one of the others by a rule we're both supposed to be obeying, and they both insist the rules are perfectly fair and I'm just being dense.

Is one of the rules secretly rubbish, or is this genuinely a dead end with no way out? And either way — what do I actually do about the copy sitting on the kitchen table tonight?
