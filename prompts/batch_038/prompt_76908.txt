I am in the second year of a doctoral program in history and philosophy of science, and I am putting together a proposal for a ten-week summer fellowship that my department co-administers with the history faculty. The committee is deliberately mixed — three historians, two philosophers — so the proposal has to persuade people who will be suspicious of a purely conceptual project. The topic I want to work on is periodization: whether labels such as the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution pick out anything real, or whether they are artifacts that later generations imposed on a continuous past. My own half-formed idea, which I have not been able to defend properly yet, is that the debate is stale because both of its standard camps share a hidden assumption. The realist camp treats a period term as a claim about where the joints of history actually lie; the constructivist camp treats it as a convenient fiction that we project onto an undifferentiated flux. But both assume that a period term is a candidate *partition* of a single, shared timeline, such that two labels either carve it correctly or fail to. I want to propose instead that a period concept is really a pair of parameters: first, a *register*, meaning the particular variable whose trajectory the label is tracking — modes of economic organization, structures of epistemic authority, media and book technologies, disease and demography, legal institutions; and second, a *sensitivity setting*, meaning a threshold and a timescale that jointly specify how much movement in that variable over how short a span is enough to constitute a boundary rather than mere drift. On this view the Renaissance and the Scientific Revolution are not rival descriptions of overlapping centuries at all: they are descriptions of the same stretch under different registers with different sensitivity settings, and what looks like a contradiction between them is a register mismatch being mistaken for a disagreement about facts. The overlap between roughly 1543 and 1687, which is customarily treated as an embarrassment for anyone who wants both labels, would on my account be exactly what the view predicts, and that is what makes it a useful test case rather than a problem to be explained away.

Please write this up as the two-page proposal the committee expects. I need the question stated so precisely that a reader can tell what an answer to it would look like — not "are periods real?" but something with determinate truth conditions. I need a section explaining why the realist and constructivist positions, taken on their own terms, leave that question open, which I think is my strongest selling point and which I have only been able to gesture at so far. I need a method section that says what I would actually do week by week over ten weeks, including how the register/sensitivity framework would be specified rigorously enough to be applied, what it would take to show that two period concepts are or are not commensurable under it, and how I would run the 1543–1687 case as a worked application rather than as an illustration chosen after the fact. And I need an explicit account of what a successful result looks like alongside what would count as the project failing: I suspect the most likely failure mode is that the framework turns out to be so permissive that any pair of labels can be reconciled by assigning them different registers, which would make it explanatory rather than merely redescription, so please address whether that objection is fatal and what I could build into the account to answer it.

You may name positions, arguments, and the general shape of the existing debate, but do not attribute specific claims to specific works.
