So I spent last summer hauling buckets on a dig, and the two grad students running my trench got into a genuinely bad-tempered argument about one deposit that I never managed to settle in my own head. Here's the setup. Small upland village, mid-Bronze-ish. In one house we had a compact lens of ash sitting right on the living floor, sealed under collapse. In it: mostly sheep/goat bone, and of the pieces where you can actually tell left from right, it was overwhelmingly left hindlimbs; about forty small polished stone beads in a tight cluster at one edge, roughly a 15 cm patch; a single burnt human baby tooth; and almost no ordinary domestic debris — no grinding stones, and only two or three pots, both near-complete and tipped on their sides. The house had been burned, and the next one was built on the same footprint but rotated a bit.

Ines said this is a closing ritual for the house. Her moves, in order:

1. The limb pattern is the key. Discard is opportunistic — you eat what's to hand. A consistent selection rule means something was prescribed, and prescribed cutting rules are the kind of thing that has a behind-it a story about who's allowed to do what.
2. When Tomas pushed back, she said the bead cluster seals it: forty beads in a patch with no scatter, plus a baby tooth. Sweepings don't contain teeth.
3. On the pots: near-complete and upright-ish, not shattered in place, so they were put there, not dropped.
4. And the rebuild on the same footprint shows the deposit was meant to be remembered. You don't torch your own house for storage reasons.

Tomas's moves:

5. On the limbs: selection rules don't have to be religious. Herders hold back choicest cuts for hosting and exchange, and left-side skew can come straight out of butchery technique — how you hang a carcass, which hand holds the knife. Also, how many of those sixty-odd pieces were actually sideable? His count was more like twenty, so the "overwhelming" is doing a lot of work on a small denominator.
6. Equifinality. A swept floor leaves light small objects where the sweep stops; a collapsing roof or a shelf dumps beads in a patch; and the tooth could have been sitting in the floor plaster from an earlier infant interment under the house, then got picked up when the ash was laid. Her reading needs every one of those to be non-mundane simultaneously, and stacking not-impossible stories isn't evidence.
7. On the pots: near-complete and tipped on their side is exactly what you get when they were standing on a shelf and the roof came in.
8. He said he'd accept ritual if there were independent handles — the same package recurring across many houses in the phase, or turning up somewhere mundane explanation is excluded, like a sealed pit. We have three houses, two show nothing like it, and the refuse pits from the same phase are still half-excavated, so the obvious move is a control comparison nobody has done. House-burning, meanwhile, has boring causes: pests, accident, sanitation after a death, moving off and coming back. The rotation of the new footprint suggests they weren't lining up with anything sacred.

Ines's rejoinder was that his alternative needs the sweep and the shelf and the pre-existing burial to all coincide in the same one-by-two-meter patch, so he's multiplying stories too; and that his position is unfalsifiable — if any pattern can be dissolved into taphonomy, then nothing an excavator ever finds could indicate belief, which isn't caution, it's a refusal to infer. Tomas said that's unfair, he's not refusing, he's waiting on a control sample. The site director kept saying we'd know more when the flotation came back and both of them thought that was a joke, which I didn't get.

Can you go through 1–8 and say who's right at each one — rate each move from clearly wrong to clearly right with a sentence of justification — and then say who's ahead overall? Also: is there something neither of them is engaging with that would reframe the whole dispute? I'm most curious whether Ines's unfalsifiability charge actually lands or whether it's a cheap shot, because it felt like the strongest thing said all summer. Don't send me a reading list, just the reasoning.
