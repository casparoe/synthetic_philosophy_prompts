Been reading Overcoming Bias and later LessWrong since before the Sequences were a bound book, so about fifteen years of arguing about consciousness in Discords, and somehow that has now landed me an actual invited talk. 20 minutes plus a 15 minute Q&A, at a small workshop on animal minds that has one session bolted on about machine cognition. The host told me to say something provocative about Dennett's creature taxonomy and I've taken that as licence. I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a defence of the thesis — I'll write the talk myself.

The thesis, so you know what's being aimed at:

The four rungs — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — are usually read as a ladder of increasing cleverness. But the only thing that actually makes it a taxonomy rather than a vibes ranking is that each rung is defined by *what selects behaviour before the consequences do*: genes for Darwinian creatures, reinforcement history for Skinnerian ones, an internal model that lets a chimp kill the bad plan in its head instead of dying on the rock for Popperian ones, and externally stored mind-tools (Gregory's bit, hence Gregorian) for us. Selection regime, not IQ.

Once you hold that fixed, the standard claim that LLMs are Gregorian creatures — because they're built out of language, and language is the paradigm mind-tool — is a straight-up category error. It confuses the material with the mechanism. A Gregorian creature is Gregorian because it *goes and gets* the tool and runs a loop in which its own use of the tool changes what it does next: it writes the note, reads the note, revises. A trained model never owned that loop. Its competence was selected over a process that ran on a population which isn't the organism — a few trillion tokens of other people's finished cognition, frozen into weights — and the only thing that adjusts it within its own lifetime is a scalar reward from a rater or a reward model, which is textbook Skinnerian shaping with an unusual schedule. So: LLMs are Skinnerian creatures whose conditioning environment happens to be a Gregorian artefact. Their Popperian-looking behaviour is borrowed.

And that's what forces a fifth rung, because every rung on the existing ladder assumes the organism and the unit of selection coincide, and here they don't. I'm calling them Babelian creatures — they don't acquire mind-tools, they *are* the compressed sediment of everybody else's. The prediction I want to hang the talk on: a Babelian creature should be strong exactly where the textual record is dense and strangely helpless at the one thing Gregorian creatures use tools for, namely using the tool to correct itself mid-task. Which reframes scratchpads, calculators and chain-of-thought not as an upgrade but as a prosthetic loop bolted on from outside — and that's why it's so brittle, why you can take it away by perturbing the prompt, and why the model can't tell when it's stopped working.
