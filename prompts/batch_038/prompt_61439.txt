I am preparing a short position paper on the hole argument for a philosophy of physics reading group, and I would like the commitments below audited for joint tenability before I circulate it. This is my package:

1. General covariance is a substantive physical doctrine rather than a mere feature of notation: it says that the points of the manifold have no identity or individuality apart from the roles the fields assign them.
2. Because the theory is generally covariant, one can take a solution, apply a diffeomorphism that is the identity outside some bounded region, and obtain a second model that agrees with the first on every field value outside the hole yet differs inside it. If manifold points were individuated independently of the fields, these would be two genuinely distinct possibilities compatible with exactly the same external data, which is an intolerable indeterminism; so they are not.
3. I accept Leibniz equivalence, in the standard sense: diffeomorphic models represent one and the same physical situation. That is what restores determinism.
4. I also hold that a nontrivial diffeomorphism really does relocate manifold points, so that the two models above disagree about which field values attach to which points; the argument does not rest on any confusion about this.
5. Determinism, as I understand it, is the thesis that a complete specification of the metric and matter fields on a Cauchy surface fixes, up to no residual freedom, which physically possible world obtains; and it is not to be had by redefinition.
6. The point-coincidence argument is no escape: appealing to what the models agree on at the intersections of worldlines either reproduces Leibniz equivalence or else begs the question against the substantivalist.

Please tell me which of these pull against one another and which only appear to, ranking the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. For each, say what has to give and what the least costly revision would be. Then identify separately the pair that is standardly thought to be in tension but is not, and explain why it dissolves. Finally, say whether the reading of general covariance in (1) is doing unearned work in (2), and what the argument looks like if it is not.
