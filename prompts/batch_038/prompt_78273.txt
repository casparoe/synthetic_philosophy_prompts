A hospital's data-governance committee is deadlocked over a prescribing case, and the two factions have agreed to an adversarial collaboration. Faction S (the structuralists) holds that the decision-relevant comparison is fixed by the causal structure of the data-generating process: severity is recorded at presentation, before the drug is chosen, and plausibly influences both the choice and recovery, so conditioning on it isolates the drug's effect and the pooled reversal is a confounding artifact that deserves no evidential weight. Faction P (the policy populists) holds that the committee is not asking a metaphysical question about a drug but a practical one about which prescribing pathway to endorse; that the pathway as actually delivered bundles the drug together with the triage-driven selection of who receives it; that "severe/mild" is a coarse two-category proxy whose adequacy is an empirical assumption, not a known fact; and that the pooled figure therefore measures something real about the endorsed practice rather than nothing at all.

The data (200 patients, triaged before prescription):
- Severe: Drug A given to 80, of whom 30 recover (37.5%); Drug B given to 20, of whom 5 recover (25%).
- Mild: Drug A given to 20, of whom 18 recover (90%); Drug B given to 80, of whom 60 recover (75%).
- Pooled: Drug A 48/100 recover (48%); Drug B 65/100 recover (65%).
The decision on the table is which drug the protocol should name as first-line, given that the protocol will retain clinicians' discretion to depart from it on the basis of triage severity.

Draft the joint statement both factions could sign. I want: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims — and these must be substantive enough that signing them costs each side something, not shared platitudes about arithmetic or about "context mattering"; (2) a disagreements table with columns for the issue, the formulation Faction S would accept as its own, the formulation Faction P would accept as its own, and whether the disagreement is genuine or merely verbal; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one faction — and for each crux, a concrete case, dataset modification, or argument that both factions agree bears on it, together with each side's prediction recorded in advance of seeing the result (for example, what each predicts about re-stratifying severity on a finer scale, about a variant case where the triage variable is recorded only after treatment begins, or about what the pooled figure would become under a protocol that removed discretion); and (4) a short section stating which parts of the year-long argument were only about words, and why.

Do not write this as a balanced summary or split the difference. Close with your own committed verdict: for each crux, say which faction you judge to be right and give your reasons, and name the single piece of evidence or change to the decision context that would most move you.
