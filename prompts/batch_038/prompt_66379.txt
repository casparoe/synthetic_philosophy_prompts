I read Dennett's Tower of Generate-and-Test a while back and I've been chewing on a hole in it. The rungs are supposed to be nested — Gregorian creatures sit on top of Popperian ones, which sit on Skinnerian, which sit on Darwinian. But that nesting feels like an assumption rather than an argument. I want you to test it by building me a creature.

Acting like a philosopher of mind who's actually worked through the ethology rather than just reciting the four labels, design a thought experiment that breaks the tower. Spec:

- You invent the species. Biologically plausible only — no telepathy, no magic, nothing that couldn't evolve under normal selection pressures. No robots, no AIs, no lab setting, no humans.
- It has to satisfy every marker a field biologist would use to call it Gregorian: it uses externally stored, designed artifacts as mind-tools, and its competence improves across generations with no genetic change.
- But it has to be genuinely non-Popperian. No internal preselection, no offline rehearsal — nothing that "permits our hypotheses to die in our stead." Whatever dies, dies out there, not in the animal's head.
- Crucially, the verdict can't be stipulated. The Gregorian-without-Popperian status has to fall out of the mechanism you describe. If I could get the same result by just asserting it, the case is a cheat.

Give it to me in these sections, in this order:

1. **Scrap paper** — four to six one-line candidate designs, plus which you picked and why (~120 words).
2. **The creature** — the scenario itself. One paragraph, 250 words max, plain language, don't use any of the four rung-names in it.
3. **Checking it against the rungs** — walk all four levels, state where it lands on each and what in your description forces that verdict (~250 words).
4. **The load-bearing feature** — name the single thing doing the work. Then delete it and re-run: does the case collapse into an ordinary smart animal, or into something boring? (~150 words).
5. **Best defense of the tower** — the strongest move a Dennettian makes to keep the nesting intact, and whether it actually works (~200 words).
6. **Bottom line** — three sentences.

Think carefully before writing, and don't pad with a bibliography — I want your reasoning, not a reading list. American English, please.
