I am due to circulate a short statement of my position on belief revision under conditional testimony at a workshop next month, and before I do I would like a rigorous consistency audit of the commitments it records. I have set them out as plainly as I can. I should say at the outset that I do not know where the problem lies, if there is one: these were written down over several months and each seemed unobjectionable at the time.

The case that anchors several of them. A soldier, whom I will call Judy, is dropped into a region divided into a Red half and a Blue half, the Blue half being further divided into a Second Company area and a Headquarters area. She assigns equal credence, one quarter each, to the four quadrants. A reliable radio report then tells her the following: you may or may not be in the Red region; but if you are in the Blue region, you are k times likelier to be in the Second Company area than in Headquarters.

My commitments are these.

1. Indicative conditionals are not truth-apt. What we assess in them is assertibility, and assertibility is governed by the corresponding conditional probability. I take the triviality results to confirm this rather than to threaten it: they show that there is no proposition for such a conditional to express, which is what I already held.

2. When evidence does not arrive as a proposition to conditionalize on, I update by Jeffrey's rule in its ratio form: the report fixes new relative odds among the cells it directly bears on, and the rest follows from the partition constraint.

3. Counterfactual supposition is handled by imaging rather than by any species of conditionalization. I regard 2 and 3 as two halves of a single account of revision, not as rival accounts.

4. Where a report explicitly disclaims bearing on some hypothesis — as this report disclaims bearing on Judy's being in the Red region — the credence in that hypothesis is unchanged by the report. I treat this as a constraint on admissible update rules, not as a heuristic.

5. A cooperative report is to be read as one that makes a difference: if some reading of it would leave every credence exactly where it was, that reading is to be rejected in favour of one on which the report is informative.

6. In the Judy Benjamin case I side with van Fraassen. As the report approaches its categorical limit, with k growing without bound, the correct posterior credence that Judy is in the Red region converges on the ratio of her prior credence in the Red region to her prior credence in the Red region together with the Blue-and-Second-Company quadrant — which, on her symmetric prior, is two thirds.

7. I accept the reflection principle: my present credence in a hypothesis ought to equal my present expectation of my future credence in it, wherever that future credence will arise from my own rational updating. It is reflection, in my view, that recommends 6; a credence of one half in the Red region does not look stable in the face of an anticipated report of this kind.

What I want from you is an audit rather than a survey.

- Identify the genuine incompatibilities and rank them by severity: which single one is fatal to the package as it stands, and which are merely uncomfortable.
- For each, state the least costly revision and price it — what else in the list is lost or weakened by that revision, and, where two commitments can be saved only by surrendering a third, say which is the third.
- Identify the pairs that only appear to conflict, and explain why the appearance arises. I would rather not delete a commitment that was never in trouble, and I expect there is at least one such pair here.
- Scrutinise my glosses as well as my commitments. In particular, 7 is offered as a reason for 6; I want to know whether that reasoning survives examination, independently of whether the two are mutually consistent.
- Close by telling me which single change would resolve the most, and whether the resulting position is one its holder could defend in public without embarrassment.
