Our physics dept ran one of those "is the universe fine-tuned" panels last Thursday and Newton got dragged into it three separate times, always as a mascot rather than as a person with actual views. The bit that got me: a guy from the cosmology group said Newton would be totally fine with Tegmark's mathematical universe hypothesis, arguing something like "for Newton the mathematical structure lived in God's understanding anyway, so identifying reality with structure just relocates God." Someone else claimed Newton is basically a simulation-hypothesis person avant la lettre, because he thought God has to be actively present to the system and keep the planets from drifting.

I wanted to push back on both but I only know the pop version of Newton's religion: the design stuff at the end of the Principia, the God-as-ruler bit, the fact that he was secretly an anti-Trinitarian, the mountains of alchemy, the prophecy and chronology work, and that he even put a date on the apocalypse (2060, I think?). So can you do me a real counterfactual instead of a mascot? Take Newton as he actually was — the voluntarism, the idea that deity consists in dominion over servants rather than in bare perfection, the anti-Trinitarianism, the alchemy, the prophetic chronology, his hostility to any world-soul picture of God — and tell me what he'd make of things that only came up after he died. Five of them:

1. The claim that physical reality just is a mathematical structure, Tegmark's version, no God required.
2. The simulation argument as a serious philosophical proposal, plus the pop version where people assume the simulators would run ancestor simulations of us.
3. Fine-tuning as it's actually deployed by modern natural theologians, with the multiverse as the naturalist's reply.
4. Intrinsic quantum randomness: does Newton's God get to choose individual outcomes, or is he locked out by a law that isn't his?
5. Panentheism / process theology's "God is the ground of being, not a person who intervenes."

Table please, one row per item, exactly these columns:
- claim: the modern position in one neutral sentence
- verdict: what Newton says about it, in the vocabulary he'd actually have used (idolatry, atheism, Spinozism, enthusiasm, a sound piece of natural philosophy, an impertinent question, whatever fits)
- load_bearing: which specific documented commitment of his the verdict rests on, and why that one does the work rather than some other
- contested: whether that commitment, or the interpretation of it, is something Newton scholars actually argue about — if so name the shape of the disagreement, don't smooth it into a consensus
- confidence: low / medium / high plus half a sentence of why
- steelman_flip: the best honest case that Newton would come around after reading the physics, and what it would cost him to do so

Two things I care about most. First, seperate what's genuinely settled about his views from what historians still fight over, and don't let one contested reading of Newton quietly do the work in all five rows — if a row depends on the alchemy mattering to his natural philosophy, flag that, since that's exactly the thing people disagree about. Second, if for some item the honest answer is "he wouldn't have understood the question, and here's the conceptual step he's missing," give me that instead of forcing a verdict. And if you think my cosmology guy is actually right, say so — I'm not invested in winning this.
