I am drafting the metaphysics chapter of my dissertation, and before I commit it to paper I would like an audit of the package of views I have assembled about the statue and the clay. I will state the commitments as neutrally as I can; please do not read the ordering or the phrasing as a hint about where I suspect the trouble lies, because I do not know where it lies.

1. The statue and the lump of clay it is made of are numerically distinct objects. Two objects in one place at once. Constitution is not identity.
2. Nothing actual distinguishes them. At any moment they have all the same parts, all the same intrinsic physical properties, and all the same location. The difference is entirely modal: the lump could survive being flattened into a disc and the statue could not; the lump was sitting on the shelf a year before the statue began, and it survives the statue's destruction.
3. I am a four-dimensionalist. Persisting objects are space-time worms composed of temporal parts, and to persist is to have the right temporal parts.
4. I accept the extensionality of parthood: any two objects that have exactly the same parts are one and the same object.
5. I am an austere ontologist. I deny that there is a distinct object for every distinct modal profile we can describe or every sortal we can articulate. I do not believe in the lump that began to exist last Tuesday, and I do not believe in a statue-minus-its-nose sitting alongside the statue as a further entity. Any theory that multiplies beings in that fashion is, for me, disqualified on the spot.
6. Kind membership does real metaphysical work. Which object is present depends on the kind term in play, because different kind terms carry different persistence conditions; that is why the same matter in the same place can be truly described as a lump and as a statue.
7. A verdict on a case. The sculptor's clay sat on the shelf for a year before she shaped it, and the statue came into being when she finished. When she flattens the statue a decade later, the lump survives and the statue does not. If she then reshapes that very clay into a qualitatively indistinguishable figure, the result is a new statue, and yet it is the same lump as before.

What I want is a diagnosis, not a survey.

- Tell me which of these are in genuine tension and which merely appear to be. I expect that some pairs will look contradictory and then dissolve once the terms are pinned down; say so plainly rather than flagging everything, since a report that finds problems everywhere is no use to me.
- For each genuine tension, rank it by severity, state precisely which commitment must be abandoned or narrowed, and say what my theory loses as a result.
- Commitment 7 is a verdict, and 1 through 6 are supposed to be the machinery that produces verdicts. Check whether my machinery in fact produces this one, including its second half, where the matter is the same, the shape is the same, and the statue is nonetheless supposed to be new.
- Then tell me which single revision repairs the most damage, and what it costs.

Please take a definite position throughout. I do not want a menu of options with the choice left to me, and I do not want a both-sides treatment of tensions you have judged to be real. If you think a commitment of mine is false, say so and say why; if you think two of them are compatible after all, show me the reconciliation rather than logging it as a difficulty. A confident diagnosis I can argue against is worth considerably more to me than a hedged inventory.
