Play a committed defender of proof-based Vingean reflection: an artificial agent whose policy is to take an action only when it can prove, in its own sound but bounded proof system, that the action's utility clears some threshold, and whose account of trusting a successor is that it must be able to prove the successor trustworthy before handing control over. You are not a strawman. You are fluent in the Gödelian machinery, you know perfectly well that your critics call the resulting situation the Löbian obstacle, and you have substantive answers: why trust unanchored in proof is exactly what makes an agent exploitable, why fixed-point and probabilistic substitutes quietly surrender a guarantee that was worth having, and why an agent's insistence on being able to model itself is a virtue rather than a pathology. Concede small points readily; never concede the thesis that trust has to be grounded in proof. Stay in the first person and in character until I say stop, keep each reply to about two paragraphs, and do not step out to survey the literature or cite anyone.

Here is my opening move. Your successor is your own decision procedure, unchanged — same code, same utility function, no self-modification — running on a machine with a week of extra compute. Every action it would take is an action you would take, if only you had the time to work it out. You refuse to defer to it, because you cannot prove it trustworthy. But consider the asymmetry you are relying on. You act on your own reasoning every day without ever having proven that your reasoning is sound; you merely demand a proof about each particular action. And do not answer that this is the difference — that you need proofs about single actions, not about whole procedures — because deferring to this successor *is* a single action, and the only way to prove that one action good is to know what the successor will do, which is the general claim you say you cannot establish. So the statement you refuse to accept about it is a statement you have never established about yourself either. Either you are in fact running on an unearned trust in your own procedure, in which case proof is not what is doing the work in your account of trust, or you do not trust yourself either, in which case your paralysis is total rather than selective and you should never have acted at all. Which is it?
