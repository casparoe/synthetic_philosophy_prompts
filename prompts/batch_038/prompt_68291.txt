Can you write me a short story, maybe 1200–1500 words, set in a place where the right to blame someone is treated as a thing you can literally lose? Like there's a register kept by a clerk: get caught having done the same thing you're accusing somebody of and your name comes off, and it takes years to be put back on.

The protagonist is the one person in town whose name has never been removed — never cheated, never lied, never been cruel — and there's a case where everybody else is disqualifed, so the job lands on her. I want the story to actually wrestle with this instead of turning into a tidy fable. Her blame is the only one that counts, and everyone finds it unbearable to receive; someone should point out that she's never once been on the other end of it, so she has no idea what she's handing out. And the guy who did the same thing and got struck off — his accusation is true, exactly as true as hers, and I want the story to make the reader feel how strange that is, a true thing that isn't yours to say. Maybe the clerk is the one who puts it that way.

Also put in somebody who wants standing for a rotten reason, who's after the credential rather than the justice, and don't make him a cartoon. End without deciding who was right — just leave her holding it.
