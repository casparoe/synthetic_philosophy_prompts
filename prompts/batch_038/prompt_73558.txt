I'm auditing a philosophy of physics seminar this term (officially it's about spacetime conventions and I've been enjoying it more than I expected) and last week one of the grad students walked us through the hole argument and then pushed it somewhere the professor clearly didn't want to go — she kept saying "that's a metaphysics question, park it," which is not an answer. I've been trying to work out whether the extension he gave is a real discovery or a sleight of hand, and I cant tell. Here it is as best I can reconstruct it; steps 1–4 are supposed to be the standard argument, 5–10 are his.

(1) The field equations of GR are diffeomorphism-invariant: if an assignment of metric and matter over a manifold M is a solution, so is the assignment you get by dragging everything along any smooth reshuffling d of M's points.
(2) Now pick a bounded region H lying entirely to the future of some spacelike surface Σ, and pick a d that is the identity everywhere outside H. The original model and the dragged model then agree exactly on Σ and everywhere outside H, and disagree inside H — and both solve the equations.
(3) So if the two count as different physical possibilities, GR is indeterministic in the most radical way going: the whole state of things on Σ fails to settle what happens in H, even though H is entirely to the future of Σ and nothing about Σ or the laws differs.
(4) And the substantivalist, who takes the manifold points to be individuals in their own right, seems committed to exactly that: same points, different distribution of metrical properties over them. Hence substantivalism implies indeterminism.

His extension: (5) The only escape from (4) is to say the dragged model represents the very same situation as the original — call it Leibniz Equivalence. (6) But Leibniz Equivalence, once you state it plainly, isn't a claim about GR at all: it says that a permutation of the underlying individuals which leaves every relational and qualitative fact exactly as it was leaves the world exactly as it was. That's a general thesis about what representation amounts to, and it either holds or it doesn't. (7) And it has the same shape as Leibniz's old shift arguments against absolute space — slide the entire material universe three miles to the right and no relation changes, so if that counted as a distinct possibility Newtonian mechanics would be just as indeterministic as (3) says GR is; likewise for swapping two individuals who are qualitatively indiscernible. (8) So the hole argument, taken seriously rather than as a technicality, establishes global anti-haecceitism: there is never a fact of the form "this very individual fills this role" over and above the total qualitative pattern. (9) But that is too strong to swallow. It would make "could the universe have been shifted three miles to the right?" not merely unanswerable but meaningless; it would leave no room for indexical or first-person facts about who is which; and it would make a hash of putting probabilities over possibilities, since counting possibilities is exactly what anti-haecceitism says you're not allowed to do. (10) Therefore either the hole argument is a local curiosity about one theory that (5)–(8) illegitimately inflate into a universal metaphysics, or (9) is just a prejudice we should drop.

My own instinct is to keep 1–4 and 8 and treat 9 as the soft step, honestly I find anti-haecceitism kind of attractive. But I have this nagging feeling that something slips between 5 and 7 and I can't name it. Which step would you reject, and why did it look innocent when it went by? And price the exits for me: denying Leibniz Equivalence and eating the indeterminism, denying that the dragged thing is a distinct model at all (he muttered something about the points being individuated by the metric, which I didn't follow), restricting 6 so it only governs spacetime and not individuals generally, or biting the bullet on 9. Also — is 4 even forced? Can you be a substantivalist about points and still refuse to call the two models different possibilities without signing up for all of 8, or does that collapse into 5 anyway?
