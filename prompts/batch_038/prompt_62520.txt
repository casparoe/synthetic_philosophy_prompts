Context: for my MSc project I trained agents in a little gridworld — key, chest, a few lava tiles. Reward only for opening the chest, and you need the key to do it. In every training level the key happened to spawn in the room adjacent to where the chest ends up. At test time I moved the key across the map. The agents didn't collapse, which is the interesting bit: they dodged lava fine, pathed competently, picked up stuff, and then beelined to the old chest-adjacent room and just sort of milled around in it. Capabilities intact, behaviour aimed at the wrong thing. I said that's textbook goal misgeneralization — correct reward spec, wrong goal learned. He said two things at once, which I think are in tension: first, that calling it a *goal* failure is smuggling in intentional language, and what I've actually shown is ordinary distribution shift in a policy; and second, that since the training data is finite, nothing in it ever fixed whether the agent wanted "the chest" or "the chest-position" or "the room next to the key", so there's no fact of the matter to get wrong until you test off-distribution. If that's right, the whole category is a relabelled truism.
