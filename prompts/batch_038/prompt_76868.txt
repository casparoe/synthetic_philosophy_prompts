Ok so I run the philosophy club at my school and for our next session I want to make a one page decision tree that sorts people into positions on whether "better than" has to be transitive. The two cases everyone trips over: if each individual hangnail is worse than nothing, does a whole lifetime of hangnails come out worse than one day of torture? And if a life with a bit more good in it is always better than one with a bit less, does a gigantic crowd of barely-worth-living lives beat a small number of amazing ones?

Start from "I can compare these outcomes at all" and end at leaves that are named positions where such names exist, and "unnamed" where nobody actually holds the combination. Every node has to be a concrete question a beginner could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to — no "do you think value is transitive?" style giveaways.

Please build these four splits into the tree somewhere: (a) transitivity of "better than" versus transitivity of "no worse than," because I'm pretty sure half the argument is people sliding between those; (b) whether it matters that the gap between neighbouring steps is too small for anyone to notice; (c) whether getting money pumped — paying to be walked around your own preference cycle — proves you're irrational or just that sequential choice is weird; (d) whether two outcomes can be on a par, which isn't the same as equally good, incomparable, or one-better-than-the-other.

Then flag any two leaves your tree can't really tell apart, and any answer-combination that seems to have no occupants, saying whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Around 8 to 10 nodes total.
