Putting together a half-day workshop on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — i.e. what, if anything, do all the things we call pleasures have in common. Four 45-minute slots, and I want the afternoon to hang on a single question rather than being four people each defending their own pet theory of pleasure. My shortlist of candidate unifying questions:

1. Is a unified account owed at all, or is the right resting place some pluralism / family-resemblance story where "pleasure" just picks out a grab bag?
2. Can felt-quality views survive the phenomenalogical spread — an orgasm and the pleasure of finally getting a proof to hang together don't feel alike in any usable sense, so what's supposed to do the unifying?
3. Do desire-based accounts (pleasure as an experience you want to continue, or as a pro-attitude toward your current state) answer the question or just switch the subject to motivation?
4. Is any of this downstream-visible — would settling it change how we compare lives, whether pleasure is interpersonally measurable, or what we make of the experience machine?

I honestly can't tell whether that's a live option or just verbiage.
