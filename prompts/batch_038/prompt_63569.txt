Can you write me a dialogue? Set it up like Chase and Sanborn's two coffee drinkers, but swap coffee for music. Two people who've played the same recording every morning for fifteen years. One says the piece sounds exactly the way it always did, she's just gone cold on it — what changed is her. The other says her love of it hasn't budged at all, it simply sounds flat and dulled to her now — what changed is the sound.

Character A defends the line that this pair shows the whole idea of introspectibly-available qualia is incoherent, since both reports carry the same authority and they can't both be true. Character B is a qualia realist who fully accepts the confabulation data but thinks A is overreading it. I want them to actually lock horns on whether any behavioral or neural evidence could in principal settle which of the two components shifted, and B needs a straight answer to A's "if it makes no difference to anything, it isn't a thing" jab. Neither should end up looking like a strawman, and no one has to be converted by the end. Around 800 words?
