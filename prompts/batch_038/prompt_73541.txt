Mid-revision on my third novel and two of my readers had a genuine arguement about one exchange, and now I can't tell which of them I believe. Facts as the book has them: while her husband is away for a week, Marta talks to her ex Elias twice, in a hospital stairwell, while she's there sitting with his mother. Nothing else happens. When he gets home he asks, "Did you see Elias while I was gone?" and she says, "I was at the hospital with your mother every single day that week." That sentence is true.

Reader one, my copyeditor: not a lie. She said something true, answered the question she was actually asked, and let him draw the inference. Inferences a hearer draws on his own aren't the speaker's lies — otherwise every "I'm fine" and every polite brush-off is a lie and the word stops marking anything.

Reader two, a friend who used to litigate: it is a lie. What makes something a lie isn't the sentence, it's what you're trying to leave the hearer believing. She meant him to come away thinking "no," she knew that was false, and the true sentence was cover.

Copyeditor's second move: if that's really something she claimed, she should be able to take it back — and she can. Add "…that doesn't mean I never laid eyes on him" and nothing contradicts, which shows it was only ever suggested, never asserted, and you can't lie by suggesting.

Litigator's second move: that proves the opposite. The addition lands like a confession, not like a clarification, and that awkwardness is the tell that she did assert something. Plus the "everyone would be a liar" line isn't an objection — most of us do lie constantly and politely, and it only gets interesting when the lie costs someone something, which is exactly what a novel is for.

Can you go through the four moves one at a time and say who's right at each, and what neither of them is seeing? Then two things I need for the actual book: is the take-it-back test she's leaning on a real distinction, and does it do the work she thinks it does? And — readers aren't philosophers — will they come out of that scene thinking Marta lied, and if I have her repeat the same sentence to a different person thirty pages later, does that read as doubling down or as nothing at all?
