I'm a fourth-year cognitive science PhD student, and last term I sat in on a methods seminar our consciousness lab ran jointly with the philosophy department. I'll reconstruct the exchange as neutrally as I can; I promise I'm not signaling a side.

Miriam is a postdoc working in the Dennettian tradition; Jonas did his doctorate in phenomenology. (M1) Miriam opened by saying the method's claim to neutrality is straightforward: the researcher collects the subject's utterances, treats them as a text, constructs a heterophenomenological world populated by the intentional objects of the subject's expressed beliefs, and only afterwards asks which of those objects exist — exactly as one constructs a model before testing it, so the method neither affirms nor denies the reality of what is reported. (M2) Jonas replied that this neutrality is only apparent, because the construction step already converts "there is a red afterimage" into "the subject believes there is a red afterimage," and belief-attribution is precisely the ontological downgrade the illusionist wants; a procedure that structurally cannot register a positive phenomenal finding is biased in the way a scale that only ever reads zero is biased. (M3) Miriam answered that the downgrade is bracketing, not denial, and that bracketing is standard scientific practice — we treat the center of mass of a rigid body as a useful fiction without thereby being eliminativists about motion. (M4) Jonas conceded the analogy but pressed an asymmetry: nobody proposes to remain neutral about whether electrons exist, because the posits that get fictionalist treatment are the ones that have already lost the explanatory contest; fictionalizing the heterophenomenological world in advance is a bet dressed up as a suspension. (M5) Miriam offered what I'll call a neutrality test — the method is neutral just in case two investigators with opposed metaphysics, applying it to the same transcripts, would converge on the same heterophenomenological world and diverge only at the ontology stage; she noted that a panpsychist and an illusionist can both use the procedure, and that Dennett himself defended it against phenomenological critics in his 2007 paper "Heterophenomenology reconsidered" by insisting the deliverables are neutral data for anyone's metaphysics. (M6) Jonas answered with what I'll call a recruitment test — the method is biased just in case its evidential standards at the ontology stage systematically favor one metaphysics, and he argued they do, because the success criteria are all third-personal (predictive and explanatory control over reports, discriminations, and behavior), so the ontology stage simply awards the contest to whichever metaphysics best explains reporting, and reporting is behavior; the two disputants can converge on the world but only one of them can ever win it.
