ok so I want to compare conciliationism and steadfastness about peer disagreement, but not in the usual abstract two-people-in-an-office way. I want to compare them as things a whole community of reasoners could adopt, and ask which one holds up when somebody is actively gaming it.

setup i have in mind: a field of a few hundred researchers (or an intelligence-analysis shop, whatever is cleaner) where most members genuinely try to track the truth and update on disagreement exactly as their theory tells them to. now an adversary gets to insert a small number of planted people. the plants have credentials, publish, sound careful, cite the right stuff — from the inside they are indistinguishable from real peers, and insincerity is invisible, so let's just grant that everyone counts them as peers. they never move: each one holds a fixed target belief forever and is perfectly happy to be included in other people's peer-averaging.

question 1: compare what happens over many rounds under each view. under the equal-weight style of conciliationism, doesn't the community drift toward the mean of everything it takes to be peer opinion, including the plants — so the adversary's input ends up weighted alongside the evidence? and under steadfastness, doesn't the community just fail to converge at all, so the plants get ignored but so does every genuine peer who was actually onto something? i want a real comparison of those two failure modes, not a hand-wavy draw: which is worse, and does the answer flip depending on the ratio of plants to real peers, or on how correlated the real peers already are? be concrete about the dynamics — who moves, how far, after how many rounds.

question 2: the meta-level. suppose everyone in the community knows all of the above. can a conciliationist coherently recommend that everybody adopt conciliationism, or does the theory undercut itself here in a way steadfastness doesn't? i have a feeling the asymmetry is smaller than it looks, so argue it rather than assuming it.

question 3: where do the two theories actually differ in machinery? is the whole dispute reducable to the uniqueness thesis (roughly: for any given body of evidence there's exactly one rational attitude toward the proposition), or is there something left over that survives even if both sides grant uniqueness and grant the same facts about who counts as a peer?

and one smaller thing folded in: should the plants be treated as evidence about the world, or as evidence about your own reliability? i suspect conciliationism and steadfastness are forced to answer that differently, and that this is where the interesting structural difference lives — but tell me if im wrong about that.

no bibliography, no list of who said what, please. just the analysis, a few paragraphs per question.
