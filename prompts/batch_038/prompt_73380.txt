I am a third-year philosophy PhD student working at the intersection of philosophy of language and philosophy of information, and I am applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship. I would like you to turn my half-formed project into a complete proposal of roughly 1,500 to 1,800 words.

The starting point is Hofstadter's way of talking in *Gödel, Escher, Bach* about where meaning is located: a message has a frame that tells you how it is to be read, an outer message consisting in the physical inscription or carrier, and an inner message consisting in what that inscription carries once the appropriate decoding is applied. The familiar conclusion is interactionist — meaning sits in none of the three alone but in their combination together with a decoder. My suspicion is that this conclusion is too comfortable, and that a sharper question is hiding behind it.

Here is the idea I want the proposal built around. Some artifacts carry their own decoding instructions: a file whose header declares its format, a score printed with its performance directions, a text that opens by explaining how the remainder is to be read. In every such case the self-description does not eliminate the need for an interpreter; it relocates it. The instructions must themselves be read, and reading them requires capacities and conventions the artifact does not supply. Call what the artifact cannot supply, at any given level of self-description, the **bootstrap residue**. My conjecture: as one adds further layers of self-description — instructions for reading the instructions, and so on — the residue does not shrink toward zero but stays constant in kind, so that even in the limit an irreducible unframed step remains and meaning cannot be wholly located in the artifact however self-describing it becomes. The rival conjecture: the residue does shrink asymptotically, and the interactionist picture overstates the decoder's contribution. A third possibility, which I want treated seriously rather than dismissed: that "how much the artifact supplies" is not a quantity at all, so both conjectures rest on a category mistake and the correct reformulation asks instead which features of a successful decoding are counterfactually sensitive to the artifact and which are not.

The proposal should contain:

1. A precise statement of the research question, formulated so that the three conjectures above are genuinely rival answers and so that a reader can see what would settle between them.
2. An argument, not a survey, as to why the existing positions leave the question open: the code or conduit view on which meaning is in the message, the interpretivist view on which it is in the decoder, the structuralist view on which it is in the frame, and the interactionist view. At minimum I want the point made that the interactionist answer is compatible with both limit behaviours and therefore does not answer the question.
3. The method, defended against the obvious alternatives (an exegetical project on Hofstadter, a formal-semantics project, and an information-theoretic project that simply identifies the residue with a mutual-information gap). My plan is constructive: build a finite ordered series of artifacts of increasing self-description, from an unframed inscription through several levels of embedded framing to a maximal case that attempts to teach its own base conventions from scratch; state explicit candidate criteria for when something counts as supplied by the artifact rather than brought by the decoder (invariance across decoders with different histories, recoverability by a general-purpose learner, counterfactual dependence); apply each criterion to the series; then argue for one criterion over the rest. Lay this out month by month across twelve months, with a decision point around month five at which the project is abandoned or redirected, and say what the redirect would be.
4. At least one fully worked example inside the proposal: a short artifact described in the text, with a step-by-step account of what a decoder must bring to it at each level. The notion of the bootstrap residue must be defined precisely enough that a reader could apply it to an artifact I have not considered.
5. What a successful result looks like and what would count as failure, including the possibility that the project succeeds only negatively by showing the question ill-posed.
6. A short section on why this is philosophy rather than information theory, plus a paragraph anticipating the two objections I expect from a hostile examiner: that the residue is Shannon's channel noise relabelled, and that the whole problem redescribes familiar rule-following worries about what fixes the application of a rule.
7. A realistic assessment of scope, stating what I am prepared to drop if the artifact series proves harder to construct than I expect.

Do not include a bibliography, reference list, or in-text citations — I will supply those. Refer to positions and arguments descriptively. Do not quote from Hofstadter's text; I want the framing in my own vocabulary, and I will check any passages I eventually cite myself.

End the proposal with a single line of the form \question{...} containing the research question as one sentence of no more than forty words, so that I can extract it mechanically.
