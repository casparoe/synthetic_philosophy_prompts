I'm the elected staff rep on the joint technology committee at a food-distribution warehouse local, about 900 members. Management's automated picking rollout comes to a vote next month and their offer is: nobody currently on the payroll loses their job, the shift drops from 9 hours to 7.5 at the same pay, and the local gets a one-off productivity dividend split across the membership. The catch is clause 4 — for six years we cant contest any comparable deployment except through a joint panel where the company holds the tie-break.

The committee is split three ways. Take it: the hours and the money are real, and if we refuse they install it anyway and cut the agency crew regardless. Refuse: clause 4 turns us from something that bargains over how the work is done into a beneficiary of decisions we've agreed in advance to lose, and a shorter shift probably just means a harder pace, more picks an hour, so we'd be buying those hours back with our bodies. And there's a distribution problem: the dividend and the job protection go to whoever's on the books today, while the cost lands on the temps who won't be renewed and the people we'd otherwise have hired next year — none of whom get a vote here.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting: accept, reject, or accept with specific counter-proposals, with reasons the other side can actually answer. Treat the 'they'll do it anyway' line as seriously as it deserves, and don't let the memo be only about the numbers — I want it to say what kind of organisation we'd be signing ourselves into if we put our name to clause 4. Short enough to read aloud in ten minutes, and close with a line on how confident you are in the recommendation and which part of it you're least sure about.
