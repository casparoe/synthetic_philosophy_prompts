I have read the rationalist blogosphere for close to twenty years — anthropics, cosmology, decision theory, the whole run — and only now, for a small workshop on self-locating belief, have I written my actual position down in one place. Having done so, I am not confident the pieces fit. I want an audit rather than a tutorial: assume I know what a fluctuated observer is and what the standard responses are.

Here is the set.

(1) When I am uncertain about which observer I am, I should assign credences by treating myself as a random sample from the reference class of observers who share my total evidence. I take this to be the least arbitrary option available and I do not think there is a more principled alternative.

(2) Our best cosmological picture — eternal inflation, a positive vacuum energy, a de Sitter-like far future with a finite horizon temperature — implies that thermal fluctuations produce observers with arbitrarily rich and internally consistent false memories, and that over enough time these vastly outnumber ordinary observers like me.

(3) From (1) and (2), I should be nearly certain that I am such a fluctuation. I refuse to accept that. I treat the reality of my past and the general reliability of my induction as fixed points, not up for revision at the behest of an argument.

(4) When a theory, together with a plausible auxiliary assumption about how I ought to reason, entails that my cognitive faculties are worthless, I think the correct verdict is that the package is not merely probably false but unusable: no one who accepted it could stand behind the reasoning that produced it. I want to reject (2) on these grounds.

(5) More generally, I am firmly against discarding well-confirmed theories because their consequences are unwelcome. I have spent years telling people that doing so is motivated reasoning, and I apply that to fine-tuning and to the doomsday argument.

(6) An assignment of probabilities that can be made to yield any answer one likes by shifting a cutoff is not a genuine assignment of probabilities. I hold this against several cosmological measures.

(7) I am a physicalist. There is no additional non-physical fact singling out this particular stream of consciousness as mine, and no metaphysically privileged indexical.

(8) Case verdicts: the simulation argument has moved me, and I assign it non-trivial credence. The Boltzmann brain argument has not moved me at all. I regard that asymmetry as obviously correct and would be embarrassed to abandon it.

Tell me which of these genuinely conflict and which only appear to. My own guess is that the interesting questions are whether (4) and (5) can be reconciled, and whether (7) permits the restriction of the reference class in (1) that looks like the only route to (8) — but do not take that guess for granted, since I may be looking in the wrong place. Rank the conflicts by severity rather than listing them as equally bad. For each, state the minimal revision that removes it and what that revision costs elsewhere in the set, including consequences I have not mentioned. Then say which single change buys the most consistency for the least damage, and whether any item above is doing covert work — carrying more weight than its phrasing suggests.

Two constraints on the answer. First, do not stop at the observation that everything turns on which cosmological measure one adopts: (6) is precisely my reason for distrusting measures, so if the resolution really does depend on that choice, say which choice and what each alternative costs. Second, do not treat (3) as a mere expression of stubbornness. I want to know whether it can be earned — that is, whether there is a principled reason for it that I could state without conceding (5) — or whether it has to be given up along with something else. I am not looking for references; I can find those myself.
