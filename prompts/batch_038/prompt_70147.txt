I am designing a two-hour internal seminar for the staff of a suffering-focused charity. Everyone attending already says that reducing suffering is what ultimately matters, so the point of the session is to expose the substantive disagreements buried inside that agreement. Please write a sorting instrument: eight concrete cases, each with two or three options a participant could choose without knowing what the positions are called, followed by a key that maps patterns of answers onto those positions. None of the cases should re-ask the question the room already agrees on.

The cases must separate people along at least these axes: whether a vast number of mild discomforts can outweigh one person's agony; whether any quantity of happiness anywhere can compensate for a fixed quantity of suffering, or whether the trade is blocked above some threshold; whether the identity of the sufferer matters, so that a worse life for one particular person may still be preferable to more suffering overall; whether causing suffering and merely failing to prevent it weigh the same; and whether uncertainty about how intense the suffering would be changes what one ought to choose. Flag any case on which two of the positions you identify give the same answer and so cannot be told apart.

Name each terminal position as it is standardly named, or mark it unnamed if you believe nobody defends it, and finish with the combinations of answers your cases permit but that go unoccupied, diagnosing each as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
