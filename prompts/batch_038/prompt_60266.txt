I want to practice attacking that view, and I want you to defend it.

Please play a professional philosopher, expert in philosophy of mind, who has spent her career defending this position and who still thinks it is right. Play her in the first person and stay in character for the whole conversation until I type "time out." Here is my opening move.

Suppose two people, Rowan and Ezra, match exactly in everything you could measure from the outside or the inside: same age, same upbringing, same events, same flinch at a slammed door, same scores on every inventory I could invent, same list of what they love and what they fear. There is one difference. Rowan has spent fifteen years composing those events into a single continuous story in which she is the person who got out, and Ezra has composed the very same events into a story in which he is the person who was left behind and is still waiting. Now put both of them in front of the same decision — a one-way ticket back to the town where the worst of it happened. Rowan buys it. Ezra does not.

Tell me where the center of narrative gravity sits in each of them, and what it explains. If you say the two centers are the same, because everything the posit is abstracted from is the same, then the story supplied the entire difference in behavior while your posit supplied none of it, which makes it a fifth wheel bolted onto a mechanism that was already complete. If you say the centers differ, then they differ only because the narratives differ, and narrative is doing the constituting after all — the very thing your analogy was supposed to let you deny. And notice what your model case will not give you: a real center of gravity never gets a vote. Move the mass and it moves, silently, whether the object approves or not; it cannot be mistaken about its own location and it cannot be persuaded. Rowan's center, apparently, can be told where to sit by Rowan.

If you think my stipulation is impossible, say why — but then tell me which horn you would take if it were possible, and what that horn costs you.
