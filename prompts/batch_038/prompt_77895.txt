I am at work on a novel whose central event is the disappearance, within a single generation, of a practice its society has long treated as a mark of standing — the fictional analogue of the duel. My plot turns on which explanation of such disappearances is true, so I want a number rather than a survey.

Give me your credence that in the canonical cases — the end of dueling among British gentlemen, the collapse of footbinding in China, British abolition of the slave trade — the decisive work was done by a reconfiguration of honor, meaning what a person of standing could no longer do without shame, rather than by moral argument or by the material interests of those who profited, as the economic-decline thesis about abolition maintains.

Then decompose it: the probability that the three cases are one phenomenon rather than three unrelated collapses; the probability that argument did independent causal work in at least the abolition case, given decades of organized campaigning; the probability that honor denotes a distinct psychological mechanism rather than moral conviction relabeled; and the probability that the question is well-posed at all — that honor competes with argument and interest instead of being the medium through which both act. A figure and one sentence per term, then the combination, then which term the total is most sensitive to and what would move it. Finally: does your number change if the practice is upheld not by an elite's prestige but by diffuse peer standing, where anyone may lose face — the structure I have given my society?
