> Take the Copycat puzzle "abc changes to abd; what should ijk change to?"
>

> (c) Now a microdomain you haven't seen. There are three shapes in a cycle: triangle's successor is square, square's successor is circle, and circle's successor wraps around back to triangle. **The student's answer:**

> For abc → abd the source lets you describe c in several ways at once: it's the rightmost letter, it's the letter c, and it's part of the little successorship run abc. Which of those descriptions gets carried across to the target isn't decided in advance, it's decided by which is most active when the mapping gets built, and that's the whole point of slippage.
>
> ijl comes from carrying over "rightmost letter": the rule is replace the rightmost letter by its successor, and in ijk that's k, so you get l. ijd comes from carrying over the literal change itself — replace the rightmost letter by d — so "successor" never gets applied to the target at all, d is just copied, which is why the answer looks arbitrary but isn't. ijk comes from carrying over the letter-category instead: replace c by d. ijk contains no c, so the rule finds nothing to operate on and the string is unchanged. That's defensible because nothing in the source says the relevant descriptor has to be a positional one. All three are the same source read at three levels of abstraction, and Copycat gives all three over repeated runs.
>
> The reason it gives a distribution instead of one answer is architectural. The Slipnet is the long-term network of concepts, with links whose lengths are the conceptual distances between them; rightmost and leftmost are linked, and so are successor and predecessor, which is why those are the slippages you see most often. The Workspace is where the letters get described, bonded and grouped by codelets — small agents sitting in the Coderack waiting to be chosen according to their urgency. A built mapping consists of bridges between the two strings, and the set of bridges is a viewpoint. Temperature measures how well organized the Workspace is: as the run goes on and the structures get more coherent, temperature rises, and that's what tells the system to stop exploring, so a run ending at high temperature is a confident run and a low final temperature means it never settled. Since the Slipnet's topology is fixed and nothing carries over from run to run, the conceptual distances are stable, so the same problem yields the same distribution across a batch, which is what makes the frequencies meaningful.
>
> For the shapes problem: circle's successor wraps to triangle, so triangle square circle → triangle square triangle is just replace the rightmost shape by its successor, giving circle triangle circle for the target. It's basically the letter domain with three symbols instead of 26, so the same machinery applies and I'd expect the same kind of spread, with the direct answer most common. A second answer would be circle triangle triangle, from copying the shape over rigidly the way ijd does.
