so I've been trying to write down what I actually believe about when the fact that *I'm the one who started this* counts in favor of me continuing it, and I've hit a wall. Here's my verdicts, roughly in the order I thought of them.

1. Sourdough. I began a starter six years ago, fed it, kept it alive through two moves and one bad breakup. I barely bake now, it eats fridge space my roommate wants. I think the fact that I started it is a genuine reason not to pour it down the sink. Not decisive, but real — the kind of thing I'd regret ignoring.

2. Garden plot. I dug it out of compacted dirt and planted it. A neighbour now wants it and would clearly grow more with it than I do. Starting it is still a reason for me to keep it, but weaker, and I think I'd lose if she needed the food and I just like the tomatoes.

3. Book club I founded. It's turned into a chore, everyone's happy to rotate hosting. Here the founding fact doesn't seem to count at all. Same feature as 1 and 2, no reason.

4. The petition. I started a campaign against a policy where I work; a much better organised group has since picked it up and will run it better without me. I don't think I have any reason from having started it to stay involved. If anything the fact that I started it is a reason to hand over my notes cleanly and get out of the way.

5. The argument. I opened a fight with my brother by insulting his wife. His replies were just as nasty as mine were. But I think the fact that I started it makes it worse for me to keep going than for him, and gives me a reason to stop that he doesn't have. Same feature that favoured continuing in 1 and 2 now favours stopping, which I don't know how to explain.

6. Weird one. Back in 2, suppose I'd told the co-op in April that I'd give the plot up in June. Now I don't think starting it counts in June at all — not weaker, just gone. Which strikes me as odd, since a promise about the future doesn't seem like the sort of thing that should reach back and delete a reason I had all along.

My own tentative principle is: starting something gives you a reason to keep it going to the extent that the thing's current shape is down to your own choices and couldn't be reproduced by anyone else. That handles 1 (the starter literally can't be replicated), 3 and 4 (the club's format and the campaign's content aren't mine anymore, they'd go on identically without me). But it completely misses 5 — my insult was one hundred percent mine and utterly irreplaceable, and yet it counts against me. So I'd have to bolt on a clause about whether continuing makes things worse, and once I do that I start to suspect I'm just restating my verdicts with extra steps.

What I want:

- Is there a principle, or a small finite set of them, that gets all six? State it precisely, and say which of my cases motivated which clause, so I can see whether the clauses are doing work or just padding.

- How would I even tell a real general principle from a gerrymandered redescription? I can always write "starting X is a reason to continue X unless X is a petition, or an argument, or a book club, or..." and technically that fits. What would make a proposed generalisation here non-trivial, and does your own proposal pass that test or does it just look like it does?

- Does this set of verdicts actually support the particularist line — reasons being holistic, some features being enablers rather than reasons, that sort of thing — or is there a generalist rescue on which the apparent flip in 5 is only apparent because the real principle has conditions I haven't spelled out? I'm especially unsure about 6: is the promise a disabler, or does it show that starting-the-plot was never a reason in the first place? Tell me which of my cases genuinely needs that machinery and which ones I'm over-intellectualising.

- Run your account on two or three cases you pick that I haven't listed, ideally ones where you expect it to give a verdict I'd find surprising, and say what it gives and why.

- And be straight with me about which of my six verdicts, if any, the best available account forces me to give up. I'd rather be told that than keep a principle that quietly contradicts one of them while looking tidy.
