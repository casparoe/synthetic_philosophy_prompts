**Context.** I convene an interdisciplinary reading group whose membership is split between graduate students in philosophy and practitioners from a quantitative fund. Everyone in the room has heard of the Kelly criterion, and everyone in the room means something slightly different by "the rational stake". I need a single handout that sorts my members by what they actually believe rather than by what they call themselves: a decision tree in which the reader starts from a shared description of a concrete betting problem, answers a sequence of questions, and arrives at a position on whether staking the full Kelly fraction is the rational thing to do. The point is diagnostic, not expository. Nobody should be able to reach a leaf by recognising a label.

**The standing case.** Every node must be answerable against, or clearly generalisable from, the following example, which the group has already been given.

> A gamble is offered once per round, indefinitely, with independent outcomes. In each round you choose what fraction *f* of your current wealth to stake. With probability 0.55 the staked portion is multiplied by 1.6 and returned together with the unstaked portion; with probability 0.45 the staked portion is reduced to 0.7 of its size, again returned together with the unstaked portion. Your wealth is therefore multiplied by 1 + 0.6*f* on a win and by 1 − 0.3*f* on a loss. You may borrow to stake more than your whole wealth, at no cost, and you may do so repeatedly.

Note three features of this case, which you should derive and confirm rather than take on trust: the growth-rate-maximising stake exceeds the agent's entire wealth, so full Kelly cannot be implemented without leverage; the improvement in growth rate from levering up from a full stake to that optimum is well under a tenth of a percentage point per round; and the worst possible outcome at the optimum is a loss of roughly a third of wealth, so even the leveraged Kelly stake leaves no absorbing ruin in this case. Report the per-round compounded growth rate at the optimum, at a full unlevered stake, and at half the optimal stake, and report the expected one-round arithmetic return, showing your working each time. At least one node in your tree must turn on the gap between those figures, and at least one leaf must be forced to say something about a case in which ruin is simply not on the menu.

**Deliverable.** A two-sided A4 handout, in British English, with the following sections.

1. *Preparatory brainstorming, kept short and clearly separated from the tree.* List the candidate axes along which positions in this debate might genuinely come apart: repeatability versus a one-shot decision; additive versus multiplicative wealth dynamics; whether bankruptcy is an absorbing state; whether the object of evaluation is an average along one actual trajectory or an average across parallel realisations of a gamble the agent will never experience in parallel; whether the probabilities are known or estimated; whether borrowing is available and at what rate; whether anything besides terminal wealth matters, such as the path taken to get there or consumption along the way; whether the horizon is finite; and whether a growth-rate criterion is normative in its own right or an instrumental proxy for something else. For each axis, one clause stating whether it earns a place in the tree and, if not, why not. I want the discards as much as the keepers, and I want a reason for each discard.

4. At least four of your leaves must be positions of the following kinds, though you should phrase them in your own terms and you are encouraged to add others: a view on which staking the full Kelly fraction is correct whatever the agent's preferences happen to be, and on which logarithmic utility is doing no normative work whatsoever; a view on which Kelly staking is merely what expected utility maximisation delivers for one utility function among many, and on which the long-run dominance argument establishes nothing about any finite sequence of rounds; a view on which the agent should systematically stake some fixed fraction of the Kelly stake because the probabilities are estimated rather than known and because the path matters as well as the endpoint; a view on which multiplicative dynamics are non-ergodic, so that the ensemble average is the wrong object and the growth rate along the agent's single actual trajectory is the right one, and on which this makes the growth-rate criterion objective rather than preference-relative; and a view on which no positive probability of an absorbing loss is acceptable at any growth rate whatsoever.

5. *Unoccupied cells.* Since the tree is to be built from the logical space rather than from a list of known positions, some combinations of answers will correspond to no view anyone actually defends. Two candidates worth considering are the reader who agrees that the time average and the ensemble average come apart yet denies that this makes the growth rate normatively privileged, and the reader who thinks it can be rational to stake *more* than the Kelly fraction.

6. *Indistinguishable leaves.* Flag at least one pair of leaves that your tree cannot separate, rather than papering over the problem. Address directly whether any sequence of answers about what to bet can distinguish the reader who stakes full Kelly because it maximises the growth rate along a single trajectory from the reader who stakes full Kelly because it maximises expected utility under a logarithmic utility function. If it cannot — and consider carefully whether it can — say what further question would separate them, phrased as a question about a modification to the standing case rather than about a new bet, and supply it as an optional tie-breaker node.
