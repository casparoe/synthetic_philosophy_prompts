I want a case analysis, run in both directions, of the program-alarm episode during the descent phase of Apollo 11, using the idealization known as HCH from the factored-cognition literature.

So that the answer does not depend on whether you happen to recall that literature, here is the theory as I mean it. Factored cognition is the program of making a hard cognitive task tractable by decomposing it into subtasks, each of which a single human can execute reliably within a bounded amount of time; the decomposition, rather than the raw capacity of any individual, is what does the work. HCH is the corresponding idealization. A human facing a question may consult an exact duplicate of herself, identical in knowledge, values, and competence; that duplicate may in turn consult duplicates of herself, recursively, to unbounded depth and breadth. Consultation is costless, communication is lossless, the copies are trustworthy, and the process terminates when the human at the root is satisfied with an answer and returns it. HCH then serves as a target: a task is counted as tractable if HCH can answer it, and a proposed method for aligning or overseeing an intelligent system is evaluated by asking whether it recovers the answer HCH would give.

Here are the facts I will hold you to, so that nothing turns on lookup. The powered descent lasted roughly twelve and a half minutes. In its early portion, while the vehicle was still at an altitude measured in tens of thousands of feet, the guidance computer displayed a 1202 program alarm, signifying executive overflow: more scheduled work than available cycles, with the consequence that the machine had begun discarding its lower-priority jobs. Armstrong reported the alarm; Aldrin asked Houston for a reading on it. Charlie Duke relayed from the capsule-communicator console; Gene Kranz was flight director; Steve Bales held the guidance console; and Jack Garman, twenty-four years old, sat in the back room behind Bales as the computer specialist. Before the flight, following a simulation in which a comparable overload had appeared, Garman had written out the program-alarm codes and their implications, including the note that an overflow occurring only intermittently was not a reason to abort. After perhaps ten to twenty seconds of silence on the loop, Bales called the alarm acceptable; Kranz then polled the controllers for trajectory, retrograde, environmental, and system status and received an affirmative across the board. The alarm recurred several further times, once as a 1201, and each recurrence forced the same call again inside a shrinking window. The underlying cause was that the rendezvous radar had been left energized, as the checklist directed for abort capability, and a design fault caused it to feed the computer a stream of spurious signals the landing itself did not require. The onboard software had been constructed deliberately around asynchronous, priority-ordered execution with display-driven alarms, precisely so that overload would shed low-priority work and warn the crew rather than fail outright. Throughout this, Armstrong was also discovering that the automatic target lay in a boulder field well downrange of the planned site; he took manual control, flew to smoother ground, and landed with on the order of twenty seconds of usable propellant remaining.

What I want from you, in this order.

First, the mapping, done strictly rather than impressionistically. Identify who occupies the root position in the HCH hierarchy during those seconds, what the query under consultation actually was, and where each subsequent consultation sits relative to it. State explicitly what counts as a subtask here and what counts as a reliable primitive, and give the termination condition of the recursion as it operated in the room, not as HCH stipulates it. If more than one hierarchy was running concurrently, say so and describe how they were coupled.

Second, run the theory forward. On the HCH idealization, what should these actors have done, and how should they have handled the recurrence of the alarm rather than merely its first appearance? Distinguish places where HCH yields a determinate recommendation from places where it is genuinely silent, and mark the silences as silences instead of filling them with something the theory does not contain.

Third, and this is the part I care most about, run the case backward against the theory. Consider at least the following sources of pressure. The consultants were not duplicates of one another but differed in information, competence, role, and stakes. The recursion appears to have been closed by an authority relation rather than by any understanding on the part of the person who closed it. Consultation carried a hard cost, denominated in propellant and seconds, rather than being free. The answer Garman supplied came from a list prepared months earlier, so the factoring had been amortized offline instead of being performed at query time. And the alarm code was itself a deliberately low-bandwidth interface between machine and crew, which is to say that the communication was lossy by design. For each of these, judge whether it is a feature HCH can absorb by re-description, a genuine anomaly, or evidence that HCH was never aimed at episodes of this kind. Then state what the idealization would have to become, in respect of bounded depth, a time budget, heterogeneous and ranked consultants, or precomputed protocols, for the Apollo call to fall out of it; and assess whether the revised idealization would still do the work HCH was introduced to do as a target for oversight.

Finally, give me your own verdict rather than a survey. Is this episode a confirmation of the factored-cognition thesis, in that a judgment no individual present could have made was in fact produced by a decomposition into human-sized pieces under an interface designed for exactly that purpose? Or is it a demonstration that the thesis, in its HCH form, identifies the wrong bottleneck, the binding constraint having been bandwidth and elapsed seconds rather than the depth to which deliberation can be recursively factored? Commit to one reading, and say what would have to be true of the episode for the other reading to be correct.
