I retired three years ago after four decades as an experimental chemist, much of it spent on calibration: how one determines whether a scale is interval or ratio, and which invariances license which comparisons. I have since turned to the philosophy of welfare economics, and in particular to the problem of interpersonal comparisons of utility. I have perhaps five years of concentrated work left, no institutional obligations, and enough private money to fund one doctoral student's research each summer. What I want is a defended agenda, not a survey.

Take as a filter the following stipulation: suppose the measurement problem is solved outright. We possess a neurophysiological indicator of experienced utility, calibrated on a single physical scale common to all persons, so that origin and unit are fixed once and for all and Harsanyi's aggregation theorem can be applied without its usual caveat about comparability. Which questions in this area survive that assumption? My own suspicion is that most of the interesting ones do — that Robbins's banishment of these comparisons was partly a claim about meaning rather than measurability; that the stipulated scale answers a question much of the literature was not really asking; and that what remains concerns the relation between the measured quantity and the normative one (well-being, preference satisfaction, desert), the individuation of the persons whose utilities are being compared, and what the invariance class of a social ranking reveals about which comparisons are actually doing work within it.

Please rank the four or five questions you would have me take up, in order, and defend the ranking. My criteria are these: how far upstream a question sits; whether it is tractable for someone with training in measurement theory and almost none in economics; and how much of what I currently believe it would require me to abandon. Tell me also which questions you would deprioritize as settled or as merely verbal, and what evidence or argument would move an item up or down your list.
