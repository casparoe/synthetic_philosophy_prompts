So I freelance — science and ideas stuff, mostly a couple of online mags plus a weekly newsletter with about 9,000 subscribers, which is small but they actually read it. Three weeks ago I started reporting on a preprint out of a big East Coast lab claiming that a cheap old antiparasitic drug, the kind you'd buy for livestock, regrows the hair cells in the inner ears of deaf mice. Striking result if true, reputable lab, exactly the sort of thing my readers love. Then last Tuesday, before I'd finished my reporting, the thing exploded: a trade site ran it first, one of the big general-interest papers picked it up and pointed at the trade site, an aggregator picked that up, and by Friday there were maybe a dozen stories, two of them with that "scientists say this could change everything" framing. My editor at the weekly wants a yes or no by Thursday, because if we sit it out we look like we missed it, and a freelancer I know says she's writing it regardless of what I do.

Here's what's making me hesitate. My own reporting went slightly the other direction. A postdoc in the lab told me off the record that the headline figure comes from a subset of the animals — the ones that died partway through the trial were dropped from that particular analysis, which he says is disclosed in the methods but is very easy to miss. And when I actually went through the dozen stories that ran, six of them quote the exact same two people, one of whom is a co-founder of the biotech that licensed the discovery from the university. The other reporters weren't much help either; one told me pretty plainly that she thought the result looked overhyped but ran the piece anyway because everyone else had and she didn't want to be the only outlet not covering a potentially big finding. So now I'm going back and fourth: on one hand, twelve outlets is twelve outlets, that feels like alot of corroboration, and I'm aware I could just be the contrarian who talked himself out of a good story. On the other hand, I keep suspecting the number twelve isn't doing any work at all — that what I'm looking at is one lab's preprint echoing back at me through a chain of people who were each waiting on the person before them.

What do I actually do with that? Is it rational for me to lean on the fact that everyone else ran it, given what I now know about *how* they ran it — or does my own reporting just flatly override the crowd at that point? Is there a difference between "twelve outlets agree" and "twelve outlets each did their own thing and landed in the same place," and how would I even tell which one I've got here, since the postdoc thing is anonymous and the co-founder thing is inferable but not proof of anything? And is there an honest version where the story isn't about the drug at all but about the shape of the coverage — or is that just a cop-out that reads like sour grapes from a guy who got scooped? I don't need to be told to be careful and check my facts, I need help figuring out whether the pile-on is telling me anything at all, and whether staying quiet makes me the one person in the chain who actually had information and did nothing with it.
