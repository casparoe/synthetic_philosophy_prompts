Heads up on context: I've been reading the rationalist blogs since the sequences days and I've half-talked myself into applying for a small independent-study grant — nine months, no advisor, a stipend that is honestly not much. The thing I keep circling is evidential cooperation in a large world: the argument that if reality is big enough (or multiverse-y enough) that copies and near-copies of you exist out there, then an evidential decision theory says your choice is good news about what those similar agents choose, so you cooperate with them acausally even though there's no causal channel at all. I buy the general shape of this. But I think there's a hole in it that nobody in the threads I've read has actually sat down in, and I want the year to be about the hole.

The hole: the whole thing depends on who counts as being in your reference class — how similar another agent has to be to you for your decision to count as evidence about theirs. And similarity isn't binary. There are presumably vastly more near-copies than exact copies, and the evidential weight ought to shade off continuously as similarity drops, so you end up with what looks like a sorites problem for acausal trade partners: definately cooperate with things basically identical to you, definitely don't cooperate with things nothing like you, and no principled place to stop. And I think it might be worse than a vagueness problem — I think it might be circular. Your prior over which agents are your near-copies is itself something you arrived at by reasoning about the world, and that reasoning is presumably the same kind of reasoning your near-copies are doing, so the reference class gets partly set by the very decision-theoretic move it's supposed to justify. I don't know whether that circle is vicious or whether it just wants a fixed-point treatment, and honestly that's the question I want the proposal to be about.
