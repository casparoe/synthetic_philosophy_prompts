For roughly a decade before I moved into journalism I designed and commissioned industrial control systems: cascade loops on a distillation column, model-predictive controllers for a refinery, and a great many hours spent tuning proportional-integral-derivative regulators and arguing with operators about why a valve that has been sitting pinned at its limit for six hours is not a tuning problem but a design problem. I now write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am reporting a long piece on the argument — increasingly pressed by people who build learning systems, and by a handful of philosophers — that a reinforcement-learning agent's scalar reward signal may play for that agent the role hedonic valence plays for us, and that this gives us a reason to take its welfare seriously. I have read enough of that discussion to follow it, and I do not find it obscure. But I do not think in its vocabulary. I think in block diagrams, and I would like you to help me establish precisely what I lose by thinking that way.

Here is the request. Take the functionalist position as cleanly as you can state it — roughly, that for a state of a system to be unpleasant is for it to occupy a certain causal role: to be producible by a heterogeneous range of conditions, to be globally available rather than locally encapsulated, to capture the allocation of the system's behavioral and computational resources, to drive learning aimed at its own termination, and to enter into trade-offs against the system's other goals — and restate it in the vocabulary I actually use: plant and controller, reference and measured variable, error signal, disturbance rejection, actuator saturation, integrator windup, gain scheduling and mode switching, hard versus soft constraints with penalty terms, Lyapunov functions, cost functionals, observability and state estimation. Go claim by claim rather than in summary. For each, tell me what carries over intact, what carries over only if I let you bend it, and what has no expression at all in a language built out of loops and errors. Flag the bending explicitly, because I suspect the real hazard in this translation is smuggling: a cost functional in my vocabulary is written down by an engineer for a purpose, whereas the position you are translating wants the reward signal to be at once what shapes the system's behavior and what makes things go well or badly for the system, with no author anywhere in the picture. Tell me every place you find yourself importing something of that sort, and whether the import is load-bearing or merely convenient.

Two further things. First, a wrinkle I have not seen handled: in the systems I commissioned, the setpoint is given and the error is measured against it, whereas in a temporal-difference learner what drives learning is the discrepancy between reward received and reward predicted, and the quantity it is compared against — the value estimate — is itself learned and therefore moving, so the reference is endogenous and drifts with experience. Redo the translation under that constraint and tell me whether the functionalist claim survives once the fixed setpoint it tacitly borrows from homeostatic regulation is withdrawn. Second, a hypothesis I want tested rather than indulged. My instinct from the plant floor is that the signature distinguishing a merely nonzero error from something it would be reasonable to call bad for the system is not magnitude but loss of controllability: a regulated variable held far from setpoint by a sustained disturbance the controller cannot reject, the integrator winding up, the output pinned at its limit, and the outer loops re-scheduling their gains around the failure so that the whole system reorganizes itself around an error it has no available means of eliminating. That is what a bad day looked like where I worked, and it is the only candidate my vocabulary offers for the word the philosophers want. Assess whether that signature can bear the weight they put on it, or whether adopting it quietly converts a claim about what it is like to be such a system into a claim about the system's control-theoretic structure — and if it converts, say which side of the conversion is left over, and whether that remainder marks a genuine gap in my framework or is evidence that the original claim was less determinate than it sounded. End by telling me plainly which of those two verdicts you accept. I am not after references or anyone's name; I want the reasoning, and I would rather have an uncomfortable residue report than a smooth translation.
