I'm the registrar on a small university museum's collections committee, and for about eight years now I've been keeping private notes on how we rule whenever an "is this still the same artwork" question comes up. Its become my hobby to try and reverse-engineer our own standard, and I've given up. Here's the track record, roughly in the order we decided them:

1. Kinetic sculpture, motor burned out. We swapped in a new motor built to the same spec by a different manufacturer, everything else original. Same work, no change to the accession number.
2. Marble bust, nose snapped off in a move. Conservator carved a new nose out of stone from the same quarry and pinned it. Same work.
3. Plaster maquette, essentially disintegrated by humidity. The estate authorized us to make a bronze from a photogrammetric scan taken before it went. We logged the bronze as a new object, catalogued "after the original maquette," separate number.
4. A stack piece, a hundred fired bricks the artist himself piled in '94. Toppled in a small earthquake; our preparators restacked it to the artist's own diagram, same bricks, same floor. Same work.
5. Same piece, second quake a few years later, this time the bricks shattered. We bought a hundred new bricks of the same make and dimensions, restacked to the same diagram, and the artist, still living, emailed us telling us to go ahead. Not the same work. We recorded it as a reinstallation of a lost work and the wall label says so.
6. Neon piece, tubes failing one at a time over about twenty years, replaced as they went by the artist's own studio, so that by the end none of the original glass was left. Same work, nobody even raised it.
7. Limestone garden piece, weathered badly. A restorer in the eighties re-cut the entire surface to freshen it. Same stone, same mass, unbroken history in our own courtyard the whole time. We ruled it was no longer the artist's work but a later re-cutting after it, and struck the attribution.

The new one lands Thursday and we're already split three-three. It's a wall drawing. In 1979 the artist sold us a certificate specifying the piece: instructions, a materials list, a diagram, and language to the effect that the drawing may be executed by drafters other than the artist, must be painted over at the close of each exhibition, and that each execution is the work. We've installed it four times, different hands each time, fresh wall, painted out afterward. Now the artist's foundation says the 1979 certificate is superseded. They've issued a new one that changes the wall proportions and adds a pigment the original materials list rules out, and they're telling us the next installation is still the same work and asking that the label and accession record run continuously from 1979.

So what I want from you is a recommendation and the reasoning underneath it. Which way should we vote if we care about being consistent with 1 through 7 rather than with whoever talks longest in the meeting? Which single precedent is doing the most work as the closest analogy, and which one are the two camps each leaning on? Then pull out the two or three candidate standards our record is actually compatible with. My own guess is that there's a "keep the original matter" line and a "keep something the artist's own hand did" line and maybe a third one about the artist's standing authorization traveling with the work, but I genuinely can't tell which of those 5, 6 and 7 are forcing on us, since 6 and 7 point in opposite directions and 5 kills the obvious authorization story. Say what each candidate standard implies about the wall drawing. If they all land the same way, tell me so, and then give me the hypothetical case that would have split them, because I want to know where our fault line is before somebody finds it for us. And if the precedents genuinely underdetermine it, say plainly which principle we'd be signing onto by voting yes and which by voting no, including whatever would come back to bite us later given how we've ruled so far — we hold two other certificate works and a couple of editions.

One more thing. I'd rather you mark where your reconstruction of our record is shakiest: which precedent did you have to soften, reinterpret, or write off as an inconsistency to get a clean standard out of us? And tell me what would change your recommendation — some fact about the wall drawing, or the wording of either certificate, or how we handled past supersessions, that if I gave it to you would flip your answer. I want to know which folders to go pull before Thursday.
