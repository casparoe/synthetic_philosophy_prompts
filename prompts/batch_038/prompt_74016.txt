I work in design and shape optimization: multi-objective problems, Pareto frontiers, feasibility constraints, and the pathologies that come with them — a supremum that is never attained, a feasible set that fails to be closed, the need to relax or convexify the design space so that an optimal design exists at all. My philosophy is self-taught and thin, essentially the Monadology and Voltaire's Candide. Restate Leibniz's claim that God actualizes the best of all possible worlds in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can: the divine understanding as the space of all candidate designs, compossibility as the constraint set that turns individually consistent beings into maximal feasible configurations, and Leibniz's gloss on the best — the world richest in phenomena under the simplest laws — as a two-criterion objective. Translate Voltaire's objection into the same idiom and say what it amounts to there.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims carry over intact, which you had to bend, and which cannot be stated in my language at all — starting with Leibniz's insistence that God's choice is morally necessary without being metaphysically necessary, which looks to me like an attained and unique optimum that is somehow not compelled. I also suspect that the move from God's knowledge of infinitely many possible worlds to the existence of a best one among them silently performs a relaxation, the way an optimal shape often exists only in an enlarged design space that no genuine inhabitant of the original space realizes; tell me whether that suspicion holds or whether I am imposing my field's structure on his. Throughout, resist making Leibniz sound like an optimizer merely because optimization is my idiom. For each item of residue, say whether it marks a limitation of my vocabulary or a place where the original claim is less determinate than it sounds.
