I sit on the hiring commitee for a volunteer search-and-rescue group in a pretty remote county, and we just got a grant to pay one full-time coordinator — first paid seat we've ever had. Three people made the shortlist. The obvious one is an experienced EMT from two valleys over, great references, but she wouldn't agree to the unpaid three-month probation the old guard insists on, and she's upfront that she's interviewing for other jobs too. Then there's a local guy with a lot less on paper who, to prove he's serious, has been driving four hours each way every weekend for two months to work for free, turned down a warehouse job that paid more than we can, and says he'll take the first year at 80% salary if that's what it takes. Half the committee is sold on him: talk is cheap, they say, that kind of sacrifice you can't fake. The other half of me keeps feeling like the reasoning is backwards somehow, especially since he mentioned offhand that his wife just got laid off and there's no way they could afford to move. And for what its worth we've gone through two coordinators in three years, so people quitting is genuinely our biggest failure mode. So — who's right about the sacrifice, and who should get the job?
