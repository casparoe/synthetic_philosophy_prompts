(1) I'm on board with debate as the main answer to oversight: two optimized agents argue opposite sides in front of a judge, and the reason it works is that a lie is harder to defend than the truth is — the honest side can always point at the specific step where the other one brakes, and exposing a break costs less than maintaining one.

(2) So the judge doesn't need to be an expert. That's the whole selling point: oversight in domains where humans are never going to be competent, like a proof of a new conjecture, whether some compound is toxic, or whether a model's plan is deceptive. All I require is that the judge can follow each individual move in the transcript and isn't a total pushover for rhetoric.

(3) Same machinery handles deceptive alignment. If a trained model is quietly aimed at something else, an opponent that's paid to win will surface it, because a deceiver can't keep a story straight through many rounds of being pushed on.

(4) Both debaters get the identical reward — the judge's verdict. Someone in the seminar said that means the two sides will drift toward the same policy and stop being genuinely adversarial, but I don't think that's a problem: what the soundness story cares about is the equilibrium of the pair, not whether the two policies are different objects.

(5) RLHF on a single model's answers is, on my view, just debate with the opponent removed. Same judge, same reward, one speaker. So if (1) and (2) are right, RLHF inherits a guarantee of the same type, only weaker.

(6) And a toy case I made up for the writeup: the question is whether a particular 200-digit number is prime. My judge obviously can't factor by hand, but I give her a calculator and let the honest side hand over a certificate she checks line by line. I take this to show (2) is safe — the judge never needs competence herself, she just needs tools that make each small step checkable.

So which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, and for each real problem what's the cheapest fix and what do I lose by taking it? Rank the genuine problems by severity instead of giving me a list where everything is equally broken, and tell me in your first two sentences, bluntly, whether the package survives at all.
