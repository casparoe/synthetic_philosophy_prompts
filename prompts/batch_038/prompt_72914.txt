I have to circulate a one-page statement of my own position before a departmental workshop on the metaphysics of the self, and I would like a consistency audit of it before it goes out. Here is what I hold.

(1) There is no self over and above the aggregates: a person is a causally connected series of physical and mental events, and there is no further fact of ownership behind that series.

(2) What grounds moral concern and desert is the causal and psychological continuity running along such a series; strict numerical identity is neither necessary nor sufficient for it.

(3) First-person claims are not pretense or mere practical shorthand. When I say that I will have to answer for what I do now, I state a truth, even though on my own view no ultimately real entity is picked out by that word.

(4) Rebirth is real. The being who inherits the karmic results of my actions is neither identical with me nor wholly other than me, and I mean this as a substantive claim about the relation between us rather than as a refusal to answer the question. My working model: two bells in a room, one struck, the other set sounding — the energy carried across, but no memory, character, or experience going with it.

(5) I put no weight on intuitions generated by survival thought-experiments; I regard the sense that identity is what matters as a symptom of craving rather than as evidence about anything.

Tell me which of these pull against each other and which only seem to, ranking the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what that revision would cost me — including any further commitment it would then put under pressure. I expect the first objection from the room to target (1) and (3) together, so state explicitly whether that objection lands or whether it looks worse than it is. End with a single line in the form "Verdict: (n)", where n is the number of the commitment whose revision resolves the most, or "Verdict: none" if the set is tenable as written.
