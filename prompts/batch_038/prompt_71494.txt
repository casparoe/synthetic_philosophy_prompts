I sit on the naming panel of a human-rights org that puts out an annual report, and every year we have the same fight. Our record so far, roughly in the order we took them up:

- Recognized the wartime deportations and massacres of an ethno-religious minority under a collapsing empire about a century ago.
- Recognized the hundred-day slaughter of one ethnic group by militia and local officials in the 1990s.
- Recognized the killing of roughly 8,000 men and boys from one enclave while the women, children and elderly were bused out — over the objection of two members that only a part of the group was hit and the survivors were spared.
- Refused a state-engineered famine that killed millions of one republic's peasants, overwhelmingly of one nationality, because we couldn't find that wrecking that nationality was the aim rather than a consequence of collectivization and of punishing resistance.
- Refused a regime that killed maybe a quarter of its own population, most of the victims drawn from the majority group and selected by class, schooling, and whether they lived in a city.
- Recognized a decades-long program of taking indigenous children into state boarding schools, banning their language in class, and demolishing their shrines and cemeteries — with essentially no killing in it at all.

Now we have a live one. A government facing a seperatist insurgency in a region where a distinct linguistic and religious minority is the local majority has: bulldozed the group's cemeteries, shrines and archives; closed its schools and made the majority language the only lawful medium of instruction; blockaded the region, so that our own demographer projects the group going from about 4 million to under a million in 25 years, mostly by out-migration and assimilation rather than death; a few tens of thousands have died so far, mostly at the hands of militias the state funds but does not order; and there's a cabinet-level policy paper whose stated aim is that the minority cease to exist as a distinct people, while expressly disclaiming any intention to kill anybody. One member insists the appeals-chamber gloss controls — the convention reaches only physical or biological destruction, and cultural erasure counts at best as evidence of intent. Another insists we're Lemkians and that Lemkin never required killing.

Predict which way we'll vote, and then say what we should vote for consistency's sake. I want the full set of rules our six precedents are jointly compatible with, not just those two members' — plain-text conventionalism, something Lemkin-shaped, a group-targeting rule, a documented-intent rule, an outcome rule, anything else that fits. For each, whether it really covers all six, what it says about the new case, and why. Then the closest analog among the six, and the single further hypothetical case that would best split your top two rules.
