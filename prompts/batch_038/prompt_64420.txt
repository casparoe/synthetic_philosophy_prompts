Weird request from a fiction writer. I've been stuck for two years on a book where the second half is narrated by what's basically a philosophical zombie — she flinches, reports, says all the right things about how the light looks and how much it hurts, but the interior is genuinely empty. Not metaphorically. The whole trick is that you can't tell from the outside, and I'm trying to make it so you can't tell from the inside either.

While researching I fell down the hole and now the question I actually care about isn't about the book. The zombie argument has been going for decades: Chalmers says a physical duplicate with nothing it's like is ideally conceivable, therefore possible, therefore materialism is false; the type-B crowd says fine, there's an epistemic gap, but phenomenal concepts are direct recognitional things and you can't get from coherently conceiving something to its being metaphysically possible. Everyone knows everyone else's moves, nobody has budged.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis and probably naive: this isn't a metaphysics fight at all, it's a fight about who counts as having concieved something. Which is my day job. Conceiving is literally what I do for a living. And here's the thing I can't shake — in third person my zombie is easy, she practically writes herself. When I tried to draft her from the first person I kept cheating. Every sentence from inside her was either about something it's like to be her, or a description of what she's doing, or blank in a way that reads as blank-on-purpose rather than blank. I could not produce a first-person zombie narration that wasnt smuggling interiority in through the back door.

So is my failure data? Diagnose this debate for me. Is it a verbal dispute about conceivable, a genuine disagreement in modal epistemology, two sides answering different questions — one about concepts, one about the world — under a single heading, or bedrock intuition neither side can argue for? And engage with my little experiment directly rather than around it: given that ideal conceivability is supposed to be about ideal rational reflection and not about what some novelist can manage at a desk on a Tuesday, does the first-person failure favor one side, or is it a red herring that just sounds profound? Then tell me what would count as progress, and be honest if the answer is nothing.

American English please. No reading list, no syllabus, no bibliography — I just want the diagnosis.
