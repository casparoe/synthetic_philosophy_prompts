Nadia is a postdoc, Felix does data science somewhere, they were at my kitchen table with wine and it got loud. (1) Nadia opened by saying my situation isn't special or isolated. Which means "cooperate" is the option that puts me in the branch where the population of my decision-twins also cooperated, and that's a reason, not a mood. (2) Felix said that's evidential decision theory with extra steps, and EDT is supposed to be the dead one. It's the view that tells a smoker to put out the cigarette when smoking is causally inert and merely correlated with some underlying lesion, and the one that tells you to pay up in a Newcomb problem after the predictor has already left the building. Same disease here: my decision can't change what those distant agents did, it can only tell me what they did, and picking the act that would be good news about them isn't acting, it's managing the news. Take the twin prisoner's dilemma: you and an exact copy of you, separated, no communication possible. Causal decision theory says defect, since nothing you do reaches them. But anyone who thinks about it for five minutes cooperates, because you two make the same move no matter what — it's not really two decisions, it's one decision taken twice. And his "you can't use your own choice as evidence" line has to fail there too: I can be as certain as anything about what the twin does precisely because I'm certain what I'll do. So if the reasoning works at exact duplication, the only live question is how fast it decays as the copy gets fuzzier, and a universe with trillions of agents in it will contain plenty of near-enough copies. And he said Nadia hadn't mentioned the worse consequence: the same logic that makes you cooperate with your decision-twins makes you compliant with anyone who has modeled you well enough to squeeze you. So the view isn't merely unfalsifiable, it hands leverage to the worst possible counterparties. (5) Nadia said the blackmail problem only bites if you picture yourself responding to one particular agent. The right frame is to choose a policy before the fact: cooperate with agents you have positive reason to think are cooperators, don't cooperate with threats. Threateners announce themselves by threatening, so the policy handles them.
