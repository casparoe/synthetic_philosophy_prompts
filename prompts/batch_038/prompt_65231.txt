Longtime reader of the rationalist blogs, and one phrase keeps getting deployed in every thread about prediction, time, or fate: "the past is fixed, the future is open." Latest instance was a fight about whether forecasting and retrodiction are symmetric. One guy argued a good Bayesian with the same quantity of evidence should be just as good at working out the past as at predicting the future, since the microscopic laws are time-reversable and the only reason we're better at the past is that it left records behind — which is just the second law doing bookkeeping. The other said that's naive, the past is fixed and the future is genuinely open, and no amount of Bayesian machinery touches that. I couldn't tell if they were disagreeing or just talking past each other, and I'm pretty sure the slogan is doing several different jobs at once.

Give me the menu: the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making, from the platitudinous to the radical. For each, say whether it's logical, epistemic, counterfactual, thermodynamic, or metaphysical; what would have to be true for it to hold; whether it survives time-reversal invariant microphysics; and whether it's what people usually mean when they say it. Then tell me which reading, if any, actually supports the claim that prediction and retrodiction cant be symmetric — and whether determinism kills the interesting ones or leaves them standing.
