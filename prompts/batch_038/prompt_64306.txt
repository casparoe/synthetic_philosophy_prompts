A national memorial foundation must allocate a fixed amount of exhibition space and prevention funding among the atrocities it commemorates. Two of its advisers have agreed to an adversarial collaboration: one holds that atrocities admit of a single, roughly cardinal ordering of severity, so allocation should track that ranking; the other holds that ranking them is either conceptually impossible, because there is no common measure across different kinds of atrocity, or morally prohibited, because ranking demeans victims and quietly licenses the "lesser" evil. Draft the joint statement both could sign.

It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both accept about what comparison can and cannot establish — covering victim counts, intent, methods, duration, aftermath, and survivor testimony; (2) a disagreements table stating each disputed thesis in wording its own side would endorse, beside the opposing side's characterization of it; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions whose resolution would actually move at least one party. Track the two versions of the objection separately, since the conceptual and the moral claims may have different fates, and treat as a further crux whether the ranking is meant to be context-invariant or purpose-relative.

For each crux, give a concrete case, exercise, or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it — for instance, whether lay panels' severity rankings stay stable when the order of the cases is permuted — and record each side's prediction in advance. Note explicitly whether the two sides agree about what any given result would show; if they do not, say why the same data is diagnostic for one and merely noise for the other.

Close by identifying any portion of the dispute that turns out to be verbal, and stating what substantive disagreement survives its removal.
