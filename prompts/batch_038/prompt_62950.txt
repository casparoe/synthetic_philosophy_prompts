I was listening to a philosophy podcast on the drive home and the host gave this rundown of Dennett's four kinds of creatures:

"Dennett sorts minds into four types, each named after a scientist. Darwinian creatures come off the assembly line with every competency they'll ever have -- their behavior is designed by natural selection, so they can't learn anything, they just react. Skinnerian creatures can learn, through reward and punishment. Popperian creatures go a step further: they form hypotheses about what would happen and test them out, the way a scientist does, so that instead of the animal dying when a guess turns out wrong, the guess dies. Gregorian creatures are the ones with language, which is what finally lets a mind think about its own thinking. The thing to get clear on is that these are four rungs on a ladder, so any given animal sits on exactly one rung, and as far as we know humans are the only Gregorian creatures there are. The upshot is that consciousness had to wait for language."

That mostly squared with what I half-remember from a phil of mind class a decade ago, so I nodded along. But now I'm second-guessing a couple of bits. "Exactly one rung" feels weird to me -- that would sort every species into a box and leave everything below the top rung pretty thin-minded, which isn't the Dennett vibe I remember. And I can't tell whether he's smuggling in a claim about consciousness that Dennett himself wouldn't sign up for.

Then tell me seperately whether that closing line about consciousness having to wait for language follows from anything true in the summary, or whether it's the host's own addition grafted on.
