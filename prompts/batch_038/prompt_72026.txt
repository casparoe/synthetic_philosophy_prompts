My flatmate's doing a PhD on causation by omission and she's been road-testing her intuitions on me all term. Here's her verdicts so far, roughly in the order she gave them:

1. Lifeguard on duty who watches a kid go under and doesn't dive in — yes, his not diving in caused the death.
2. Strong swimmer in the next bay, off duty, no connection to the kid, does exactly nothing — no causation. She's very clear he's a monster; she just insists she's talking about causation proper and not blame, and that collapsing the two is the whole mistake.
3. Flatmate who promised to water my plants and didn't — yes, caused them to die.
4. Some random on the street who could've watered them in ten seconds, never met me, owes me nothing — no.
5. Driver who runs a red light and gets hit broadside — yes, his not braking caused the crash.
6. Same crash but it turns out the brake fluid had slowly leaked out over the previous weeks — she says the absence of fluid caused it too, and she's happy with that even though there's no person anywhere in the story.
7. Nurse who didn't double-check a dose, harms the patient, but the ward was two staff down and the protocol had been quietly scrapped by management — she says the nurse's not checking caused the harm, and separately that the nurse isn't to blame. So again, the two come apart for her.

She also volunteered this one unprompted, and I've tried to keep it in her words: the fact that she didn't water the flowerbeds in the park down the road "didn't cause anything, and it's not even false, it's just not the sort of thing that ever gets to be a cause".

Now she's got three new cases marked down for next week and I want to have a guess before she tells me:

(a) A warehouse porter whose job sheet says he must check the sprinkler valves every Friday. One Friday the valve room is padlocked from the outside, the key's gone missing, he bangs on the door, phones maintenance twice, gets fobbed off, and goes home. Fire that night, stock destroyed. Did his not checking cause the loss?

(b) A delivery driver who's never worked there, owes nobody anything, happens to be parked by the same padlocked door with a bolt cutter in the back of his van, thinks "not my problem", and drives off. Same fire, same stock.

(c) Her original lifeguard, except it later emerges the child had a massive heart attack before hitting the water and was already dead, so nothing the lifeguard could have done would have made the slightest difference.

Predict her verdict on each, and then do the bit I actually care about: what rule is she running that gets all seven of the earlier verdicts right? I'm pretty confident there isn't just one — give me the two or three best candidates, say what each one spits out for (a), (b) and (c), and tell me which you'd bet on and why. Point out which of her earlier verdicts is doing the most work in ruling candidates out, because I suspect at least one of them is quietly load-bearing. And if the three new cases genuinely can't separate the rules, design the single case that could.
