I have zero formal philosophy background — I maintain scheduling software for a freight company and basically all my exposure to this stuff is podcasts on the drive home — but I got stuck on Berkeley about three weeks ago and I cant get unstuck. It started with one of those history-of-philosophy shows doing an episode on immaterialism, and I ended up reading the actual passage everyone calls his master argument (Principles of Human Knowledge, section 23, I'm fairly sure) on my phone in the parking lot. The setup is that he dares you to imagine trees in a park, or books sitting in a closet, with nobody around to perceive them. Then he answers his own dare. He concedes you can do it, but says all you've actually done is frame ideas of books and trees in your mind while leaving out any idea of someone perceiving them, and then hits you with: "But do not you your self perceive or think of them all the while?" He closes with the line I copied into my notes: "it is necessary that you conceive them existing unconceived or unthought of, which is a manifest repugnancy."

So here's my problem. I did the experiment. Saturday I sat out back with the dog and tried to hold in my head the vacant lot two streets over, the one with the busted chain link fence: just dirt, a stump, a couple of crows, no people walking by, no lit windows with anyone in them, no cameras, no delivery drivers. I kept that image going for a good ten minutes and was honestly pretty proud of myself. So it feels like I just performed the exact thing he says is impossible, which makes me think the argument is a bit of a scam — a word trick about what "conceive" means rather than a real argument about the world. My wife thinks ive lost it, but a couple of comment threads I found said pretty much what I'm saying, so at least I'm not alone.

But then I get confused in the other direction, because if the master argument is a cheap trick, why does anybody still treat Berkeley as a heavyweight? Isn't that the load-bearing wall the whole immaterialist building sits on — pull it out and doesn't everything else go with it, including the part where the tree keeps existing when you leave the yard because God is still looking at it? Or is the more likely explanation that I didn't actually do what he's challenging me to do — that whatever I managed on my porch is something different from the thing he says is inconceivable — and if so, what exactly is the difference? I'd really like someone to tell me either where I'm going wrong, or that I'm not and the argument really is sophistry, and in that second case what's supposed to be holding up the rest of his system.
