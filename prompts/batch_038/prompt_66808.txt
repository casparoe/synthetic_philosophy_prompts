I run a small control team at a frontier lab, and before I circulate our design principles internally I would like a consistency audit of them. As currently stated, we hold:

1. No safeguard may presuppose that the deployed model is aligned; protocols are to be designed as if the model is capable of, and possibly engaged in, deliberate deception.
2. Our primary safeguard is therefore external: a separate, narrow monitor reads the deployed system's full scratchpad and gates its proposed actions.
3. We make no assumption of trace faithfulness — a fabricated reasoning trace is among the threats we design against.
4. Our evaluations require privileged access that deployment will not have: weights, unfiltered traces, and the ability to pause or roll back.
5. An evaluation licenses a claim about deployment security only if the model cannot tell that it is being evaluated.
6. Sandbagging is detected by eliciting maximal capability in the sandbox and comparing it to deployment performance.
7. Monitors must be cheaper and weaker than the systems they monitor, or monitoring does not scale.

For each pair that genuinely pulls against the others, say how severe the conflict is, what the least costly revision would be, and what we forfeit by making it; for pairs that only look contradictory, say what dissolves the appearance. Then identify the single principle whose modification resolves the most. I have no prior about which is the weak link.

End with a line of the form "Verdict: consistent as stated" or "Verdict: revise principle N" where N is the one you would change first.
