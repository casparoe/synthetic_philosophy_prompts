The economist I've been talking to keeps reaching for the same analogy and I want to check it before it goes in print.

His line is basically this: iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies is just Darwin. So if you want to know what will turn up in a market full of these agents, you don't have to assume anyone is rational, or that anyone has beliefs about anyone else's beliefs — you just watch what gets eliminated. He uses that to tell me the whole common-knowledge-of-rationality business game theorists insist on is decoration, and that the deletion procedure is really just a description of a selection process running in real time. He also says it means you can do the analysis straight off observed trading data without ever modeling what's going on inside the agents.

Can you audit this for me?
