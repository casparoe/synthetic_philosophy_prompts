I've gone deep down the rabbit hole on the doomsday argument and the self-sampling vs self-indication thing, and what I'm actually stuck on is a meta question. Nobody has budged in decades and the camps just keep multiplying: SSA, SIA, full non-indexical conditioning, reference-class relativists, people who say you shouldn't condition on your own existence at all. What strikes me is that each side's main move is treating the others output as a reductio. The SSA folks point out that SIA lets a guy in an armchair conclude a cosmological theory is true just cos it predicts vastly more observers, with odds that scale with the population difference and no experiment run. The SIA folks point out that SSA makes your own birth rank count as evidence about human extinction. And with SSA the answer slides around depending on which reference class you pick, which feels like a tell.

So my hypothesis is that this isn't a factual dispute at all, it's a fight over whether "probability" can even get a grip on the question of which observer you happen to be, so each camp has quietly made a different decision about that and then they argue past each other forever. It also looks suspiciously like the halfer/thirder thing about sleeping beauty, same shape, same stalemate, same people. Is my diagnosis right? If not, what kind of disagreement is it really — verbal, a hidden empirical question, different norms about what counts as evidence, or bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? And what would actually count as progress: a thought experiment both camps are forced to agree on, a betting/dutch-book argument, a new formal result, or just conceding the question is ill-posed? I want the diagnosis with reasons, not a tour of the positions, I've already read the tour.
