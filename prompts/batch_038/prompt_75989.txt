I cover physics and the philosophy of science for a general-audience ideas magazine, and I have a commissioned essay on the metaphysics of time that is due in nine days. The commission was for 2,400 words; my editor has since told me the hole in the issue is 1,800. I am at the stage where I can no longer tell whether the argument is subtle or merely confused, and I would rather find out from you than from a specialist reader's letter. Below is the current draft in near-final prose, section by section. Please read it as an argument, not as a summary of a debate, and tell me where it fails.

**Working title: "The Block Universe Is Not a Discovery. It's a Temperament."**

**§1. Lede.** Two people pass each other on a station platform, one walking toward the constellation Andromeda, one walking away. Because simultaneity is frame-relative, the set of distant events each counts as happening *now* differs — for a galaxy 2.5 million light-years out, the two pedestrians disagree by something like nine days about whether a given event there has already occurred. Popular accounts draw the conclusion that the future is already there, spread out like a landscape, and that our sense of a moving present is an illusion. My claim is that the physics here is entirely real and the conclusion is a slide.

**§2. The standard argument, stated honestly.** It runs: which events are co-present with mine depends on my state of motion; if what exists is what is present, then what exists depends on my state of motion; no state of motion is physically privileged; therefore what exists cannot be just what is present. This argument is valid. But notice what it actually refutes: a *global, frame-independent now*. It does not refute becoming as such, and presentists have long had an escape — posit a privileged slicing of spacetime that no measurement can reveal, and keep the global now as an unobservable feature of the world.

**§3. Why the escape is worse than useless.** My central move. If the privileged foliation is in principle undetectable, it makes no difference to the content of any physical theory: no prediction changes, no law is written differently, no experiment comes out otherwise. And because such a structure is compatible with every possible observation, the failure of every experiment to find it cannot count against it either. A posit that no possible evidence could constrain, and whose truth would leave the whole of physics exactly as it is, is not a discovery about the world. It is a preference for a certain way of feeling about the world.

**§4. What the block does not imply.** Two clarifications I consider non-negotiable. First, the block universe does not entail fatalism: the four-dimensional manifold contains my deliberations among its contents, and they are causally upstream of the actions they produce, so "it's already there" cannot mean "nothing I do matters." Second, the very name is a pictorial artifact. We can only draw a spacetime diagram by treating the time axis as another spatial axis, and that picture invites us to read "the future is there" as "the future is settled independently of what anyone does." Those are two different senses of settled, and the slide between them does most of the persuasive work in popular writing.

**§5. The cosmologists' now.** General relativity's expanding-universe solutions come equipped with a natural time coordinate defined by the matter distribution, and in practice cosmologists say things like "the universe at 380,000 years" without embarrassment. So working physicists already operate with a de facto global present, and mostly regard the metaphysics as someone else's problem. I want to use this to suggest that the philosophical dispute has drifted free of scientific practice in both directions.

**§6. The constraint test.** I propose a criterion and apply it to both sides. Call a claim about time *substantive* only if some possible physical theory would have to be modified, restricted, or ruled out were the claim true. Presentism fails the test for the reasons in §3. Eternalism fails it too: the block is just the manifold that our theories already quantify over, and adding that this manifold is "really there" in a tensed sense adds no constraint on how it behaves. Both positions are therefore temperaments — moods about the world that are perfectly intelligible and perfectly inert. I want to be careful to say this is not deflationism about truth: one of them may well be true. My point is only that neither could ever be *found out*.

**§7. Double-entry bookkeeping.** The practical upshot. Write physics in the eternalist register, because that is the register in which the equations are stated; write agency, deliberation, and grief in the presentist register, because that is the register in which a life is lived; and decline to ask the two registers the same question. The arrangement is not a relativism about truth, and it is not a truce between enemies — the two ledgers never have overlapping entries, so there is no transaction to reconcile.

**§8. On passage.** A great many readers want more than this: they want the present to *move*. The spotlight view grants them that by adding a property of presentness that travels across the block, but the motion plainly requires a second time in which to travel, and then a third for that motion, and so on; I take this regress to be fatal and settled. The felt passage of time is better explained as a byproduct of how memory is laid down — we experience accumulation, and we misdescribe accumulation as motion.

**§9. Ending.** The consolation the block offers is that nothing is lost, because everything is permanently there; the consolation the present offers is that things are genuinely at stake, because they are not yet fixed. Since, by my own criterion, the two registers make no competing claims about the world, the choice between them cannot be made on grounds of truth. It should be made on grounds of what each one lets you bear. I want to end on the death of a parent and the observation that the two registers recommend the same behavior — go to the funeral, keep the photographs — while describing it in incompatible ways.
