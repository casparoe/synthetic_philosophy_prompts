I am preparing a short statement of my own position in democratic theory for a workshop on constitutional design, and I should like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is what I intend to say.

(1) The primary virtue of a constitution is the dispersal of power: the executive should hold its mandate independently of the legislature, in origin as well as in survival, so that no single body can both will and unmake policy at pleasure.

(2) Accountability requires clarity of responsibility. Citizens must be able to identify the agent answerable for outcomes and punish it at the next election; any design that permits blame to be shared, displaced, or deflected is to that extent defective.

(3) Between elections, the legislature's capacity to withdraw confidence is the only effective lever for adjusting government to changed political facts. A public that cannot dislodge an executive is, for the duration of the term, without recourse.

(4) Fixed, non-interruptible terms are an asset rather than a rigidity: they license credible commitment, remove the temptation to time elections opportunistically, and make the policy horizon legible to everyone.

(5) Divided government is a discipline, not a pathology. Where rival parties control different branches, negotiation replaces command, and the output is more moderate and better scrutinized.

(6) Of the three families — presidential, parliamentary, and semi-presidential — I hold the semi-presidential to be clearly the worst, and I take that verdict to follow from (1) through (5).

(7) Institutions must be designed on the assumption that officeholders will not exercise forbearence.

Please tell me which of these pull against one another, ordered by severity rather than listed indiscriminately; which pairs only appear to conflict and dissolve on inspection; and, for each genuine tension, what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me. I am especially interested in whether (6) is in fact derivable from the rest, since that is the claim I expect to be challenged on. Conclude by saying where your own analysis is most likely to go wrong, and what consideration would move you to a different verdict about the package as a whole.
