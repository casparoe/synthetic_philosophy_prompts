So I make pots. Garage studio, sell at a farmers market most saturdays, never took a philosophy class in my life — but I've been going round and round on this for about two years and I want to know if there's an actual answer or if I'm just making it up as I go.

Setup, because it matters: I keep one bucket of reclaim. Trimmings, pots that cracked, pots I hated — all get slaked down in water, wedged back up, and go back in the bucket. I top it up with new clay from a bag when it gets low. It's been the same bucket for three years.

My verdicts, as honestly as I can give them:

1. The bucket is the same bucket of clay it was three years ago. Almost none of the original is left in there. Still the bucket.

2. A mug I made from that bucket cracked, went back in, and six months later I threw a new mug out of the bucket. The new mug is not the old mug. Some of the same clay is literally in it and I don't care.

3. My kiln has had every single brick replaced over fifteen years, one at a time, and that's still my kiln. I keep the old bricks stacked out back. If I tore the kiln down and rebuilt it out back from those bricks, same shape, same everything, I'd say the one I've been firing all these years is the kiln and the rebuilt one is a new kiln. Right bricks, wrong kiln.

4. Grandma's ring got melted down and made into two rings, one for each grandkid. Neither one is grandma's ring. But if she'd only had the one grandkid and the exact same thing happened, that ring would be grandma's ring. I know how stupid that sounds — what one sister does changes what the other one's holding.

5. When I set a finished pot on the shelf I think there's one thing on the shelf. Not a pot and also the clay it's made of. One. But the clay was in the bucket before the pot existed, it'll be back in the bucket when the pot breaks, and it could have been a bowl instead. The clay can do things the pot can't do. If they're one thing, how?

6. A pot that slumps in the rain and goes back to slop is gone. The clay is fine, I scoop it into the bucket. Nothing died except the pot, and there was never anything there but clay.

The pair I can't square is 1 and 2. The bucket loses every bit of its clay and lives; the mug keeps some of its own clay and dies. And 3 and 4 feel like the same problem wearing different clothes.

My own theory, such as it is: something survives if it changes a bit at a time and dies if it changes all at once. Except I know that's wrong in both directions. A pot that cracks clean in half in one second and I glue back together — I still call it that pot. And a bowl I keep trimming while its leather hard, taking a shaving off the wall every couple minutes all afternoon, every change tiny — by five o'clock it's a small cup and not the bowl I started with. Sudden doesn't kill it, gradual doesn't save it.

The one I keep going back and forth on: a day where I throw ten mugs, same clay, same size, same glaze, same spot in the kiln. Are those ten different things, or one thing ten times over? If one breaks I don't feel like I lost a mug, there's nine right there. But I'd never tell a customer she's getting the same mug her friend bought.

So — is there a principle that gets these, or most of them? I'd genuinely rather you tell me one of my verdicts is flat wrong than build something so baroque it stops being an answer. Pick the simpler option and say which verdict goes, and argue for it. When you give me the principle, tell me which of my cases each part of it is there to handle — I don't want a clause that isn't doing work on something I listed. And I want to know whether the answer changes depending on what kind of thing you're asking about, because pot, clay, kiln and ring feel like they want different rules and that bugs me more than anything else here.

Also, don't just work down my list. Tell me what your principle says about a case I didn't give you: a pot I sold four years ago that the owner dropped, broke into three, and had a friend put back together with gold lacquer so all the cracks show. I've got a feeling about that one but I'm not saying what it is. I want to see if your rule lands where I do.
