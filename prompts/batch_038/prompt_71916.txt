been going down a St. Petersburg rabbit hole for like a week now (old LW archives, a couple of Substack comment sections, one very patient friend who does finance) and I want to sanity-check my map of the territory, because every thread I read has people talking past each other and I cant tell which side is actually confused.

heres where I landed. the paradox shows expected-value maximization is just broken as a decision rule: EV tells you to pay any finite amount for a ticket to a game nobody would pay $25 for. Bernoulli's diminishing-marginal-utility patch doesn't save it, because — as Menger pointed out — for any unbounded utility function you can just escalate the tail payouts and get the paradox right back, so "bound your utility" is basically an ad hoc tweak to make the arithmetic behave. the real fix looks like Kelly and the ergodicity-economics people: maximize the time-average growth rate of wealth instead of an ensemble average, which is a property of the actual gamble rather than of some invented utility function, and it hands you a finite number for St. Petersburg (geometric mean payoff around $4, so a Kelly bettor pays under that). Samuelson objected to maximizing the expected geometric mean back in the 70s, but my read is the ergodicity crowd has answered him and mainstream econ just never updated.

so two practical questions and one conceptual one. (1) if a ticket costs $10 and I can only afford to play once, what fraction of my bankroll should I be willing to commit — or is "fraction of bankroll" even the right frame for a one-shot? (2) does the same growth-rate math say anything about what fraction of my income to donate to high-EV charities, or is that a totally seperate question? I keep seeing EA people gesture at Kelly when defending big giving percentages and it feels like a stretch. (3) the bit I most want sorted out: some commenters insist Kelly is *literally* just expected-value maximization under log utility, which if true would make the whole time-average-versus-ensemble-average distinction empty verbiage. is that right? because I've seen both claims in the same thread and one of them has to be wrong.
