I have been invited to contribute a position paper to a special issue on statistical inference and the replication crisis, and the editors have asked each contributor to state their inferential commitments explicitly and defend them as a package. Before I submit, I want a rigorous consistency audit of my package. My commitments are these:

1. A p-value is not a measure of evidence. It reports how probable data at least as extreme as those observed would be under a specified null hypothesis and a specified design, and it says nothing directly about the comparative support the data give to an alternative.
2. Evidence, in the sense I care about, is a function of the likelihood ratio. Two bodies of data that generate the same likelihood ratio for the same pair of hypotheses constitute equally strong evidence, whatever procedure produced them.
3. I therefore hold that the sampling plan, the stopping rule included, is evidentially irrelevant. Once the data are in, what they say about one hypothesis relative to another is fixed by the likelihood function and by nothing else.
4. Nonetheless, optional stopping is a genuine methodological wrong, and not merely because it distorts the reported p-value. A lab that collects until significance appears and then stops has produced data that merit less credence, and in my capacity as an associate editor I would return such a manuscript even if the authors disclosed the stopping rule with complete transparency and computed their likelihood ratios honestly.
5. Preregistration of hypotheses and analysis plans is the single most important reform available to us, and would resolve the large majority of the failures that are collectively described as a replication crisis.
6. I will not specify subjective priors, and I regard default "objective" priors as a rhetorical device for smuggling unacknowledged substantive assumptions into an analysis. Consequently I do not report Bayes factors, notwithstanding my view that they are often correctly characterized as the appropriate summary of what a body of data licenses.
7. My diagnosis of the crisis is that it is overwhelmingly a power problem: most effects that fail to replicate are real but small, and the original studies were simply too small to detect them reliably. Selective reporting and outright fraud are marginal contributors.
8. Confidence intervals are immune to the criticism I level at p-values in commitment 1. A 95 percent interval is a legitimate report of what the data support and can be interpreted without the distortions that make significance testing objectionable.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the field. Please go through these eight and tell me:

- Which pairs are in genuine tension, ranked from most to least severe, where severity means how much damage the unresolved tension would do to the paper as an argument rather than how much noise it would generate in discussion.
- For each genuine tension, what has to give: which commitment I would have to weaken, qualify, or abandon, and what the cheapest repair is. Where a repair is available by reinterpreting one commitment rather than dropping it, say so and supply the reinterpretation, together with an honest statement of whether the reinterpretation still earns the commitment its original role in the package.
- Which pairs only appear to conflict and in fact do not. I expect there to be one or two of these, and I would rather you explain the dissolution than gesture at it; why the appearance of conflict arises is part of what I am after.
- Which single revision would restore the most coherence across the package as a whole, and what it would cost me, that is, what I would give up that I currently have reason to value.

I am not asking to be converted to any particular school of inference, and I am not asking for references, a bibliography, or a reading list; argue from the content of the positions themselves. Please resist treating all eight commitments as equally problematic in the interest of balance. If a commitment sits comfortably with the rest, say that it does and move on.
