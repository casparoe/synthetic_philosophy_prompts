Been chewing on the side-effect effect (chairman/profit/environment vignettes) on and off for years and I finally have a hunch I want to turn into an actual project. I'm pitching it to a small independent-research fund — think three months, modest stipend, they like things that could come back false. Two pages max.

The hunch: nearly every account of the harm/help asymmetry — blame, pragmatic implicature, the deep-self view, the trade-off idea — treats it as a fact about moral cognition specifically. But maybe it's a much duller fact about information. A single datapoint where an agent accepts a bad side effect to get what they want should move your posterior over their utility function a lot more than a single datapoint where they accept a good side effect, because producing good outcomes is closer to base rate and so carries less news. On this picture, asking whether he did it intentionally is asking how hard to update about what this agent is like, in something like the intentional-stance sense, and the asymetry just falls out of differential informativeness. The prediction I think is distinctive: the effect should shrink and maybe reverse when the helpful side effect is made rare or unexpected within the vignette world, with praiseworthiness held fixed.

Write the proposal. I need:
