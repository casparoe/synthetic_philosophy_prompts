I am preparing a set of problems for a graduate seminar on anthropic reasoning, and I need a case built to order rather than a survey of the debate.

**The deliverable.** A single paired scenario in which the reasoning behind the doomsday argument licenses a downward revision of a total in one branch and no revision whatever in the other, with the divergence traceable to exactly one feature of how each agent came to occupy their position in the series.

**Specifications.** Both agents work for the same institution and handle the same kind of item — invoices, patient admissions, ferry sailings, your choice, but something ordinary and non-science-fictional. Both come to know one and the same numerical fact: that the item before them is the fortieth of its kind produced this month. They share an identical prior over the month's total *N*; state it explicitly and make it bounded, say uniform over the integers from 40 to 200, so that the arithmetic is checkable by a student. Nothing else in their evidence may differ: same reference class, same background knowledge, same month, and — stipulate this for both branches — the institution's protocol is common knowledge and carries no information whatever about *N*. The one permitted difference lies in the mechanism by which each came to hold the fortieth item: in one branch it is the outcome of a draw, in the other it is fixed by a standing rule.

Then, in order:

1. The paired case, in one paragraph of no more than 150 words.
2. The likelihood P(agent's observation | N) for each branch, and the posterior mean of N for each agent under the stated prior, rounded to the nearest integer. Do not substitute any philosopher's label for this arithmetic; if you use a term of art, define it in a subordinate clause.
3. Which feature of the case does the work, and the removal test: what becomes of the second branch if the standing rule is replaced by one that makes the assigned position depend on *N* — for instance, the item at the ten percent point of the month's run? Does the divergence survive the substitution, invert, or vanish?
4. The mapping onto the doomsday argument. What in the human case corresponds to the draw, and what to the standing rule? Take a position on which branch our own birth rank occupies and defend it in two or three sentences.
5. The best reply available to a committed defender of the self-sampling assumption — the principle that one ought to reason as if randomly selected from one's reference class. I expect the reply to be a redescription of the reference class rather than a denial of the arithmetic. Tell me whether that redescription is principled or ad hoc, and what independent criterion could settle the question.

Two outcomes would count as failure: stipulating the divergent verdicts instead of generating them from the shared prior and the two mechanisms, and allowing the agents to differ in anything beyond the single feature identified above. No preamble and no concluding remarks. Seven hundred words maximum for the entire response, the paragraph in item 1 included.
