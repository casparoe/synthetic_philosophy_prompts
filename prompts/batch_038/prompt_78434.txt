I'm building a case for a workshop on accountability for automated systems and I need it made to a pretty particular spec — the standard examples are useless to me because they all smuggle in the same move.

Here's the setup I want. Give me a scenario in which a genuinely autonomous system kills or badly injures someone and there's a real responsibility gap — no one can honestly be blamed or punished — but where the gap does *not* come from any of the usual suspects. Ban all of these: the system must not be a learner (no ML, no adaptive behaviour, nothing that changes after deployment), its decision procedure must be fully transparant, deterministic, and formally verified before it goes live, the specific harm must have been foreseen and actually written down by someone before deployment, and there must be a clear, documented human decision to deploy it anyway. No sci-fi, no weapons, no exotic hardware — pick something mundane: a hospital, a rail network, a water utility, a benefits agency, whatever gives you the cleanest version.

The gap has to be generated normatively, not epistemically. The trick I want you to use is a discursive-dilemma structure (the judgement-aggregation thing from List and Pettit, aka the doctrinal paradox in law). Three separate sign-off bodies — say an engineering safety review, a legal/compliance review, and an executive board — each votes to approve the deployment, and each would have voted to reject it if the decision had been put to it as a single yes/no question about the harmful outcome under that description. Every body approves; no body approves for a reason that survives being looked at on its own; and no individual member both endorses the thing under the description that makes it deadly and could have stopped it. The majority arithmatic has to actually work — show me the ballots or the vote breakdown for each body, don't just assert that the structure is there.

Then do the work after the scenario:

- walk through what each standard gap-closer says about this exact case and whether it fires: strict or product liability, vicarious liability for the organization, group agency / corporate personhood as a single responsible subject, role responsibility for whoever signed the form, and the Sparrow-style line that somebody has to be blameworthy or the whole practice of holding people responsible loses its point;
- say which one feature of the case is doing the work in producing the gap;
- tell me what happens if you swap that feature out — in particular, what happens if you make the system opaque and self-modifying instead of transparent and verified. Does the gap get bigger, smaller, or does it just relocate? My suspicion is that transparency is doing something counterintuitive here and I want that made explicit, not waved at;
- give me the strongest attack from someone who insists there's no gap in this case at all, and your honest verdict on whether it lands.

Keep the scenario itself to about a paragraph — longer if you need the room for the vote tables — and please don't stipulate the result. The gap has to fall out of the structure, not out of you announcing that there is one. If the specs turn out to be jointly unsatisfiable, tell me which one breaks first and what you'd relax rather than quietly cheating.
