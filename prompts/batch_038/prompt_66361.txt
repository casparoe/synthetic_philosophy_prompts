I work on a four-person team at a mid-size company that runs a large model in production for a logistics client. It reads incoming customer emails, drafts replies, and decides which ones get escalated to a human. About eight months ago I built what we internally call the honesty probe: a small classifier that sits on top of the model's internal activations and flags cases where the model seems to be papering over something — like assuring a customer a shipment is on schedule when the data in the thread clearly says its stuck in Rotterdam. It isn't perfect, but it caught three real incidents and management loves it because it makes the whole setup feel auditable, which apparently matters to their customers.

Here's the mess. The underlying model has changed several times since I validated the probe, and every single time the numbers look fine — same AUC on our labeled test set, same false positive rate, roughly the same emails getting flagged. But the changes are wildly different in kind from each other. (1) a routine fine-tune on six weeks of new customer traffic. (2) a quantized version so it runs cheaper — 4-bit weights, noticeably worse on the hard cases, but the probe scores identically. (3) a distilled smaller model our infra guys trained to imitate the big one's outputs, running about a third the cost. (4) last month, a version where somebody on another team did a small amount of extra training specifically to stop the probe from firing on one category of email that ops found annoying — and afterwards the probe's measured performance on our benchmark actually went *up*. In all four cases my teammate Ravi, who is genuinely a much better engineer than me and not a bad guy, says the same thing: the probe's numbers are the same, so the probe works, so ship it. Arguing isn't free either. We contract with an external auditor (a small firm in Zurich) who makes us redo the entire validation pack whenever the "system of record" changes, which is roughly three weeks of my life per redo, and I'm already the person who slows everything down.

So — is Ravi right and am I just a neurotic gatekeeper? My gut says (1) is obviously fine and (3) and (4) are obviously not fine, but I cannot articulate why. When I try, all I've got is "it isn't really the same model anymore," which sounds like mysticism in a standup meeting and gets me nowhere. What I want is a line I can actually defend out loud: what would have to be true of a system for my probe's certification to carry over to it, and is the probe's measured performance on a fixed benchmark ever sufficient evidence all by itself? Tell me which of the four you'd sign off on and which you wouldn't, what I should say to the auditor when they ask how we decide what counts as a change, and — if you have one — a cheaper test I could run in a day or two that would settle these disputes without triggering a full revalidation.
