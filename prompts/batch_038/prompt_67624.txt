I write historical novels — this one runs three generations of a single family — and for research I fell down a rabbit hole about whether the world has actually gotten less violent, because I need to know how casually my 1880s people would have treated a beating, a duel, a knife in a bar. Fine, whatever I decide, the book goes on. But what stuck with me wasn't the answer, it was a move I kept running into, on both sides, and I've never found a word for it.

The move: someone's numbers come out the way they don't want, and they say those numbers aren't measuring the thing, they're measuring the measuring. Killings in the records go up in some decade? That's not more killing, that's better record-keeping, coroners counting things they used to log as accidents, wives and children finally willing to report what they'd always kept quiet about. And when the numbers go the way the author likes, the same author treats them as plain fact — real decline, real change in behavior, moral progress, no caveat attached.

The part I want you to look at is that I found this mirrored. The people arguing violence is falling explain away every spike in the record as an artifact of counting. The people arguing it isn't falling explain away every drop as an artifact of counting — patchy old records, narrow old definitions, whole categories of harm nobody wrote down. Each individual use looks completely defensible on its own, because measurement really does change over time and reporting really does change, that's not a conspiracy, that's just what archives are. It's only when you stack them side by side that you notice the explanation is getting chosen to fit the thesis, rather then the thesis getting revised to fit the explanation. And nobody who does it seems to feel any strain, because they'd happily make the artifact argument in the other direction in some other context and be right to.

So: does this have a name, established, in statistics or philosophy of science or the historiography of crime? I know the near neighbors and I want to know precisely why none of them land. It isn't confirmation bias as usually described, since these are careful people applying a real methodological point selectively rather than filtering evidence unconsciously. It isn't unfalsifiability in the textbook sense either, because each individual claim is testable in principle and sometimes does get tested. It isn't quite an ad hoc rescue, because nothing is being added to the theory to save it — if anything something is being subtracted from the data.

What I actually want is the carving, not the label. Tell me what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges sit: what separates a legitimate appeal to a measurement change from an abusive one, and is there a test I could apply to a paragraph and get a verdict? Is the thing a property of the arguer, or is it partly a property of this specific subject matter — the way the unrecorded violence of the past can never be directly observed, so maybe the artifact move isn't optional here and that changes what kind of fault it is? And is there a difference between one author doing it and a whole field doing it in opposite directions at once, which is what I think I saw. If no established term covers it, coin one and give me a definition plus a couple of boundary cases, including one case that looks like it but isn't.

Please write the answer as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — like an email from someone who knows this literature and is explaining it to a smart friend. I read on my phone and I want to read it straight through in one go.
