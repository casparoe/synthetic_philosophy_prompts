i TA a seminar that mixes game theory and epistemology, and next week we're on Aumann's agreement theorem. instead of a summary handout i want to give them a short poem. here's what i'm after:

write a poem of 14-18 lines in two interleaved voices — two agents holding different private information about some ordinary proposition (whether it rained over the ridge, a test result, which way a coin landed, whatever suits you) — and let the rhyme scheme carry the argument rather than any explanation. each couplet is one exchange, one line per agent. the couplets should fail to rhyme for as long as they're each just updating privately on their own signal, because that's allowed and nothing paradoxical about it; then from the moment their posteriors about the event become common knowledge, the couplets should snap into exact, unmistakable rhyme, and that snapping is the theorem. the whole thing should end on a converged couplet that reads like a resolution, not a moral.

one wrinkle i want in there: exactly one couplet should almost rhyme and not quite — a slant rhyme that a reader can't miss — and that failure should be diagnosable from the poem as a broken hypothesis, either priors that were never actually commmon or an announcement that never really got to be common knowledge. i don't want the poem to say which; i want someone who knows the result to be able to point at the couplet and name the hypothesis.

no formulas, no symbols, no naming the theorem inside the poem, and please keep it away from the pop "let's agree to disagree" register — under the hypotheses they cant, and that's the point. it should still work as a poem for someone who's never heard of any of this.
