QUESTION (10 points, roughly 300 words expected):
Hofstadter introduces the MU-puzzle — the attempt to derive the string MU from the axiom MI using the four transformation rules of the MIU-system — as an illustration of what he calls jumping out of the system, or JOOTSing. (a) Explain why MU cannot be derived from MI, and say what sort of reasoning the solution requires. (b) Explain what Hofstadter takes the puzzle to show about formal systems and about minds, and, equally important, what he thinks it does not show.

STUDENT'S ANSWER:
(a) The trick is to stop grinding out strings and instead look at the system from the outside. Track the number of I's in a string. Adding a U at the end, or deleting a UU, leaves that number alone; doubling it after the M multiplies it by two; replacing a triple I with a U lowers it by exactly three. So a count that is not divisible by three stays not divisible by three, and since MI has one I, no string derived from MI can ever have a number of I's divisible by three. MU has zero I's, and zero is divisible by three, so MU is underivable. The same reasoning shows something stronger as well: none of the rules, in any combination, can ever wipe out all the I's in a string, which is why the puzzle has no solution. Hofstadter's name for this move is JOOTSing — rather than mechanically generating theorems inside the system, you step back and prove a general claim about what the system can and cannot do. A machine that simply enumerates derivations would search forever without ever discovering that MU is out of reach, because that fact is not available at the level of symbols alone.
(b) MU is in effect the Gödel sentence of the MIU-system: a truth the system cannot prove. The MIU-system is therefore incomplete in exactly Gödel's sense, and the puzzle is a miniature version of the first incompleteness theorem, with MU playing the role of the undecidable arithmetic sentence. Hofstadter's conclusion follows from here: because we can always jump out of whatever system we are working in, while any mechanical procedure is fixed inside one, human minds cannot themselves be formal systems. He does allow one limit, though — the MIU-system cannot even state that MU is underivable, since it has no negation and no way to talk about its own strings; that claim can only be made in the metalanguage.
