I am drafting the design specification for an agent whose beliefs are grounded in formal proof, and I want a consistency check before I circulate it. The commitments:

(1) Doxastic formalism. There is a single fixed, recursively axiomatized, consistent theory T extending Peano Arithmetic. The agent believes p only if it has derived p in T. Nothing else amounts to belief.

(2) Trust in copies. The agent may spin up a duplicate of itself — same theory T, same inference rules, more time — and treat whatever the duplicate derives as thereby established. But it may rely on this only after proving, within T, that the duplicate's method is reliable.

(3) Verified improvement. The agent may migrate to a strictly stronger theory T⁺ only after proving, using T alone, that T⁺ is at least as consistent as T. Unverified upgrades are not permitted.

(4) No process-trust. Reliability is never inferred from a source's pedigree, its track record, or from the fact that the source is running the same algorithm as the agent. Trust is earned by proof or not at all.

(5) Bounded deliberation. The agent must sometimes hit a deadline and act on the best conclusion available at that point, recording it as a provisional working hypothesis rather than a belief, with no derivation completed.

My own suspicion is that the trouble lies between (1) and (5) — that a proof-only doxastics cannot coexist with deadlines — but I may be looking in the wrong place entirely, and I have no attachment to any of these items.
