I spent thirty-four years at a national measurement institute, most of them concerned with the definition and dissemination of units, and I retired shortly after the SI base units were redefined by fixing certain fundamental constants rather than by reference to any artefact. My working life was therefore devoted to a single principle: that a quantity which serves as a measure must be anchored in something invariant, and that the anchoring is the whole of its authority. It was a considerable shock, in my first year of reading philosophy seriously, to encounter a thinker who appears to deny precisely this of money. In the *Querist* Berkeley asks whether money should be regarded as having an intrinsic value, or as a commodity, a standard, a measure, or a pledge, and whether its true idea is not "altogether that of a ticket or counter". Coming from metrology, that reads almost as a provocation; on reflection it seems to me the most interesting thing he ever wrote. I should like your assessment of it, thesis by thesis.

Below are ten items. The first eight are my own paraphrases of positions I take Berkeley to hold in the *Querist* and in the seventh dialogue of *Alciphron*; I have deliberately stripped out his interrogative form and reduced each to a claim that could be argued with. The last two are not Berkeley's: they are queries I wrote myself in imitation of him, and I want them rated in the same table so that I can see whether my additions are extensions of his system or departures from it.

1. A nation's wealth consists in what its land and labour produce; a people whose common sort are well fed, clothed and lodged are not poor, and no industrious nation is poor. Money is not among the things that constitute wealth.
2. Money is useful only so far as it stirs up industry, enabling people to participate in one another's labour. Its worth is entirely instrumental.
3. Consequently any other expedient that excites and circulates industry equally well would be as useful as money. Nothing about the medium is essential; only the function is.
4. Wealth is proportioned to industry, and industry to the circulation of credit, whether that credit is circulated or transferred "by what marks or tokens soever".
5. A smaller quantity of money circulating quickly is equivalent in effect to a larger quantity circulating slowly; the nation is no loser if circulation varies reciprocally with quantity.
6. Money is not a commodity, a standard, a measure, or a pledge. It is a ticket or counter, and that is its whole nature.
7. The price of a thing is a compounded proportion, direct as the demand and reciprocal as the plenty. Names of money are merely "exponents or denominations" of that proportion, while gold, silver and paper are counters for reckoning, recording and transferring it. Keep the denominations and let the bullion go, and things can still be rated, bought and sold and commerce maintained.
8. Notes may be issued on the security of the land and of the nation's own manufactures rather than on precious metal, so that the backing of a currency may be its productive capacity.
9. (Mine.) Whether the worth of a system of tokens depends less on how many tokens there are than on how steadily their acceptance can be predicted, so that a small stock whose acceptance never wavers sets more industry in motion than a large stock whose acceptance is doubtful.
10. (Mine.) Whether money is properly understood as tokens that come into existence at the moment a loan is made and cease to exist when it is repaid, so that the stock of tokens is not issued by anyone but is generated and destroyed continuously by private contracts.

Please rate all ten, and then rank them. I want structured output, because I intend to work through the results line by line. Give me a single JSON object with these fields:
