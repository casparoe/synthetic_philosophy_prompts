In a 1997 paper in *Philosophical Papers*, David Lewis asks whether we really believe in penal substitution. He imagines a justly sentenced offender whose devoted, innocent friend volunteers to serve the sentence in his place, and argues that most of us reject the swap where the punishment is imprisonment or death, yet quietly accept it where the punishment is a fine — partly because a fine literally is a debt, so the metaphor of a debt of punishment gets a grip on us, and partly because preventing someone else from paying is impractical. Lewis's diagnosis is that we are of two minds, and that the two conditions, owing a debt and deserving punishment, are not alike in the way the metaphor suggests.
