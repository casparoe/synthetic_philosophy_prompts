I have just begun a freelance contract writing documentation for a wellness-technology startup whose flagship headset is marketed as allowing households to "bond" their experiences, and my first deliverable is the release notes for a major version bump. The marketing copy is saturated with casual panpsychism. To show the team how their own claims read to someone who works in philosophy of mind, I want a satirical version of the document to circulate internally.

Please write release notes, about 600 words, for version 4.0 of a consumer device sold on the premise that a human subject is constituted out of bonded microsubjects. Use an authentic changelog register — What's New, Breaking Changes, Known Issues, Deprecations, Workarounds — and keep it deadpan throughout: the company is sincere, competent, and entirely untroubled by metaphysics.

The humor has to come from precision rather than from straw men. The notes should be recognizably about the combination problem as constitutive panpsychists actually face it: why bonded microsubjects should amount to a single subject rather than a mere aggregate, why a limited palette of microphenomenal properties is supposed to suffice for the variety of ordinary experience, what is gained and surrendered by retreating from constitutive to emergent panpsychism, and the fact that first-person privacy means no reported defect can be reproduced in-house. Include at least three user-submitted tickets, one of them filed by something claiming to be a microsubject that was left out of the merge, and one bug whose official workaround is to accept that there are now more thinkers in the household than there were before the update.

The standard objections should be legible from the release language and the tickets alone — never argued for, never labeled as objections — and no actual philosopher should be named anywhere in the piece.
