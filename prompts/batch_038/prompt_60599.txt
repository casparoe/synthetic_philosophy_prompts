I'm giving a short speech next Friday at the wind-down drinks for what we called the decomposition pool. For six years, whenever the oversight system hit a judgment too big to check in one go, we broke it into pieces small enough that one tired person at a desk could actually evaluate them, and the model got trained on the pieces. That was the entire job. Now the distilled version does the breaking-down itself and the pool is being shut — about forty of the reviewers will be in the room, plus their partners, plus the engineers who built the thing that replaced them. The engineers will notice if i get the technical story wrong; the families have no idea what any of us did all day.

Write the speech, four to five minutes spoken, built on the actual problem with what we did: dividing a question into checkable pieces can hand you all the competence and none of the care, because no single person ever had to hold the whole thing. Not a pep talk and not a eulogy for the pool. I want it to sit honestly with the fact that we don't know whether what got transferred across was the judgment or just the visible surface of it — and it still can't trash the system in front of the people who built it, and it has to give the reviewers an account of what their work was worth that doesn't depend on the system having worked.

Sections in this order, roughly these lengths:

- What we were for (~1 min) — the job explained so a spouse gets it, without any analogy involving sous-chefs, apprentices, or air-traffic controllers
- The worry (~1.5 min) — the piece-by-piece problem, carried by one concrete invented example from the work, stated so a technical person finds it precise and a layperson finds it vivid
- What I actually know (~1 min) — the difference between the competence surviving the transfer and the reasons surviving it
- Send-off (~45 sec) — no toast clichés, nothing about the real model being the friends we made

Then a section called Choices (~150 words): why you picked that example, and how the send-off manages to honour the work without claiming the distillation succeeded.
