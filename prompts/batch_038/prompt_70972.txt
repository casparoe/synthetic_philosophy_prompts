Here is the record I have on Turing and parapsychology, and I would like your best prediction about a case he never lived to see.

- 1930, writing to the mother of his closest school friend days after that friend's death: the body is something like an instrument that a further thing makes use of, and the instrument's breakdown need not mean the user is gone.
- Early 1930s, an essay read to the Cambridge Moral Sciences Club on the nature of spirit: he takes up the possibility that a thought may not always have a single private owner, and connects this to the problem of free will and to what makes one mind one mind rather than a segment of a larger one.
- 1950, in the paper that introduces the imitation game: having listed the standard objections to machine thought, he treats the extrasensory-perception objection as the one he takes most seriously, says the statistical case for telepathy at least strikes him as overwhelming, and answers it not by conceding the point but by proposing to tighten the test — run the competitors in a room that screens out telepathic influence.

Now the new case. A lab reports a preregistered, double-blind study in which an offline language model, given a prompt containing no information whatever about a target, produces above-chance matches to targets that a hardware random-number generator selects only *after* the model has already emitted its output: a precognition-shaped result with a machine as receiver and no sender, no human in the room, and no plausible leakage path the authors can identify.

What would Turing make of it? Lay out the candidate rules his record is consistent with, say what each one predicts about this result, give your best guess as to which he was actually running and why, and name the further case — one he would have been able to imagine in 1950 — that would tell the rules apart.
