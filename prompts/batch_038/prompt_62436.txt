I'm in a philosophy of biology seminar and our reading group wants to hand out one of those "which species concept do you secretly hold" quizzes on the first day of the species unit, because half the class is convinced it's already a Mayr-ian and I don't think it is. So: eight questions, each a concrete case rather than a thesis -- e.g. two oak populations that hybridize along a narrow contact zone but hold their phenotypes and ecologies apart, a ring of snail populations whose ends refuse each other, a lineage of asexual micro-inverts with no sex at all in its history, two prokaryote clades that swap genes constantly yet stay ecologically coherent, a fossil horse series where nobody can run a breeding experiment -- with two or three answer options each, none of which names a concept or a philosopher. Then the scoring key, and I want two things beyond a normal key. First, score people on two axes separately: whether species are real joints in nature or a rank taxonomists invented, and which property they think actually holds a species together (isolation, mate recognition, niche, diagnosibility, genotypic clustering, cohesion mechanisms, none of the above). Say explicitly which combinations of those two axes nobody defends, and for each empty cell whether that's because the view is incoherent, unfashionable, or just unexplored. Second, flag any pair of leaves the eight questions genuinely can't tell apart -- I'd rather have that admitted than have the key pretend to a precision it doesn't have. And at least two questions have to be ones where a microbiologist or someone working on asexual taxa sorts differently from an ornithologist, since my prof keeps saying every concept in the literature is just animal-biased and I want the quiz to show it.
