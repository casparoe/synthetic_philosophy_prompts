I run a small NGO's country office — six staff, water and sanitation work. For six years we've used the same local contractor for logistics, a family firm, and honestly he's the only one who'll get supplies into the villages when the roads wash out. Last month he turned up at my house with a goat for the holiday. I turned it down per head-office policy (nothing over $25 from a vendor, ever, no exceptions) and he was visibly hurt. Our deliveries have been slower since.

Here's the part I can't work out. He wasn't asking for anything. He'd have been insulted if I'd offered him cash for the goat, and insulted if I'd asked what he wanted in return — you're not supposed to say, and neither of us could have named a specific favor if you'd asked. And yet I've noticed its harder for me now to push back when he invoices us for an extra trip than it used to be, and I don't think that's a coincidence.

Meanwhile there's a guy at the customs office who'll take $200 to move our containers to the front of the queue, and everyone including me calls that a bribe without thinking twice, even though it's also a payment that keeps a working relationship going and even though the queue is arbitrary anyway.

So what is the actual difference between the goat and the $200? Is our $25 line tracking something real, or is it just one culture's idea of how organizations are allowed to relate to each other being enforced on people who don't share it? And is refusing the goat itself a wrong thing to have done, given what it seems to have cost us — or am I just noticing that the relationship got expensive and calling that a moral problem?
