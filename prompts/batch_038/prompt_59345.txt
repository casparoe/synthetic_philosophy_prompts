One: a friend of mine had a kid three years ago. She says it's the best thing that ever happened to her. She also says, unprompted, that she can't explain that to me and that I shouldn't try to decide about having kids on the basis of what she tells me — not because she's holding anything back, but because I'd be reading her description and measuring it against the wrong thing. When I pushed one of them on what I'd actually be signing up for, he said something like: I can't tell you, and it's not that I've forgotten what it was like to be you, it's that the me who could tell you isn't around any more.

Two-and-a-bit: same shape with emigrating. A colleague moved back to Warsaw, says it remade him, and flatly refuses to advise anyone else on whether to do it.

So what I want named isn't "big decisions are hard" or "people are bad at predicting how they'll feel". It feels narrower than that, and the bits seem to come as a package:

Like being handed a complete answer in a format you can't deserialize.
- The decision has to be taken by someone who won't be the one living with it. Not metaphorically. The person who'll assess the outcome later has different tastes and different things they care about, so "would this be good for me" doesn't have a settled answer, because the me it's supposed to be good for is an output of the decision rather than an input to it.
- And yet everyone behaves as if there is a right answer, and we hold people to these choices afterwards, and the ones who came out happy say "you'll thank me" — as though the later self's verdict automatically outranks the current one.
