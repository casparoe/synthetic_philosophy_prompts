Can you lay out the conceptual terrain of the argument over whether Frege's 1924 political diary bears on his philosophy? I keep seeing the debate slide between three claims nobody bothers to separate: (1) the diary shows he was a horrible person, which is beside the point; (2) it tells us something genetic about where his views came from; (3) there's an actual collision between what's in the diary and what his doctrines commit him to. I mostly want to understand what (3) could even look like. Frege spends his career insisting that logic isn't about how anyone in fact thinks — thoughts are objective, mind-independent, and the laws of logic are not laws of human psychology. But the diary is wall-to-wall sweeping claims about the character and capacites of whole peoples. Is that psychologism in any interesting sense, or is it just a guy being inconsistent with himself, which isnt the same as his system being internally inconsistent? And how much of the bite turns on whether his universalism about logic means that any reasoning being whatsoever falls under the same standards, versus that all actual thinkers happen to share the relevant capacities? Tell me which of the three claims the interesting version of the debate is really about.
