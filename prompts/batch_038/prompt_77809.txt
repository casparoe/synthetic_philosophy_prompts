I am assembling an application for a three-month postdoctoral research fellowship in philosophy of mind, and I would like you to draft the project proposal that will form its core. The proposal should run to roughly 1,100 words and be addressed to a committee of philosophers who know the material well and are inclined to be skeptical that anything in my framing is new.

My idea, still only half-formed, concerns Dennett's fourfold taxonomy of minds — Darwinian creatures, whose behavior is shaped by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures, which add operant reinforcement learning; Popperian creatures, which add an internal capacity to simulate options so that hypotheses can be discarded without the organism itself being discarded; and Gregorian creatures, which add the uptake of externally generated cultural artifacts and tools. As the taxonomy is standardly presented, these four form a nested hierarchy of increasing cognitive sophistication, each stage building on and presupposing the ones below it. My working suspicion is that this is a mistake: the four are better read as distinct mechanisms for substituting selective outcomes, mechanisms that can in principle come apart, so that the taxonomy is a cross-classification rather than an ordinal scale. The pressure point is a class of cases the original taxonomy never had to confront. Contemporary large language model systems look Gregorian through and through — they are assembled almost entirely out of accumulated human artifacts — while apparently lacking the Skinnerian and Popperian machinery that, on the standard nested reading, Gregorian competence presupposes. I want to call these, provisionally, Gregorian organs without Gregorian organisms, and to ask what their existence does to the taxonomy: whether they are counterexamples, whether they show the hierarchy was never a hierarchy of the same kind of thing, or whether a properly articulated set of criteria can absorb them without revision.

Please write the proposal so that it does the following:

1. States the question precisely enough that a determinate answer is possible. In particular, make explicit what it would take to distinguish the ordinal reading from the cross-classificatory reading, and identify which specific commitments of the taxonomy are actually at stake (I suspect the load-bearing one is the claim that internal simulation is a precondition for the useful uptake of external symbolic tools).

2. Explains why the existing treatments leave the question open rather than settled. Do this by reference to positions and arguments, not to a literature survey.

3. Sets out the method — what I would actually do, and in what order — including which cases I would work through as diagnostics. I have in mind at least one nonhuman case, one artificial case, and one human case involving external scaffolding, chosen so that the three disagree in instructive ways about where the boundaries fall. Justify this approach against the two obvious alternatives: a general conceptual analysis of what cognition amounts to, which risks answering a different question, and a purely empirical inventory of model capabilities, which risks begging the question about what the taxonomy is even claiming.

4. Says what a successful result would look like, and what would count as the project failing. I would like the failure condition to be stated as something more demanding than 'no conclusion reached' — for instance, a set of revised attribution criteria that turns out to classify every actual case the same way the unmodified taxonomy did would mean the distinction I am pressing has no consequences and should be dropped.

5. Gives a realistic month-by-month plan for three months, and an honest assessment of where the scope is likely to exceed that window and what I would cut first.

Do not include a bibliography, a reference list, or citation-style attributions; where you need to gesture at a source or a named position, describe it, and I will supply the citations myself. Likewise, do not put any wording in quotation marks unless it is a term I have coined in this message.
