I'm prepping next week's seminar on Hacking's looping effects and one of my grad students has turned in a handout I want stress-tested before I either endorse it or tear it up in front of everyone. Her line isn't original to her, I should say — it's a move I hear constantly from labeling-theory-minded sociologists too — which is exactly why I want it audited properly.

The move: looping effects are just Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy applied to psychiatric classification, nothing new under the sun, and this *dissolves* rather than deepens the worry about interactive kinds. Her reasoning runs like this. (1) A self-fulfilling prophecy starts from a belief that is false, the belief drives behaviour, and the behaviour makes the belief true. (2) So if classifying people is a self-fulfilling prophecy, there is always a determinate fact about what was true before the loop started. (3) So there was a fixed pre-classification truth about who was, say, a multiple personality or who was autistic, and the instability in our categories is just us describing that truth badly. (4) Hence the kind is as real and as stable as any other kind, only mis-labelled along the way. (5) And the fix is the standard one for a prophecy: publish the facts, break the belief, and the loop stops running. She closes by saying Hacking's talk of kinds as moving targets is really just a prophecy in flight.

I want the audit, not a lecture. First give me the explicit correspondence — what plays the prophecy, the believers, the false belief, the bank and its depositors, the run itself, and the correction-by-disclosure — and say for each slot whether anything on the psychiatric side actually fills it. Then sort the disanalogies into the ones that genuinely bite against steps (2) through (5) and the ones that are noise, because I'd rather not spend seminar time on "Merton wrote about individuals and Hacking about whole populations over decades" if that turns out to be beside the point.

Two spots where I suspect trouble and want you to check it rather than take my word: Merton's mechanism seems to need something like the old Thomas dictum, situations defined as real being real in their consequences, and I'm not convinced the classified person's own uptake is carrying the weight in her version — especially in cases where people fight the label, where the loop appears to run through clinics, courts, school accommodations and diagnostic criteria revisions rather than through anyone believing anything. And the multiple personality case, where the number of alters reported for a typical patient climbed steadily through the eighties, doesn't look to me like a belief that was false and then became true; it looks like the target relocating while we were aiming at it. Tell me if I'm wrong about that.

Then: what does the prophecy frame quietly import that interactive kinds don't have? My hunch is an outside-the-loop vantage point, a single identifiable false belief with a discoverable owner, and a specifiable moment at which the loop could have been switched off. Say whether those are load-bearing for her conclusion or just decoration.

Finally, does the deflation survive once the disanalogies are on the table? And if it doesn't, is there a better analogy to hand — she floats the map redrawing the territory, I keep gravitating toward something more like a language whose speakers can coin new words for their own states and thereby acquire states that had no name before — and be concrete about which piece of the argumentative work the replacement does that the prophecy frame can't. I don't want a bibliography or a list of who said what, I want the reasoning, and if a step of hers is salvageable on some restricted reading, tell me what the restriction costs her.
