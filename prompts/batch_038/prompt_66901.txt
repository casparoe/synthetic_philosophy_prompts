So — odd commission, this one. I write a science-and-ideas column, and next month I'm running a long piece (plus an onstage thing at a festival) about the identity question that keeps surfacing in fertility medicine: mitochondrial donation, embryos grown from lab-made gametes, twenty-year-old frozen samples with the paperwork attached. Two philosophers have been sniping at each other in the letters pages for three years now. One is a straight-down-the-line origin essentialist of the Kripkean stripe: the sperm and egg you actually came from are necessary to you, full stop, so there's no possible situation in which you came from different ones. The other thinks that's metaphysical overdressing on a decision about how to describe things — they'll happily grant that a person could have come from a different gamete pair, or from a slightly different one, depending on how you set the comparison up, and they lean on four-dimensionalism and modal parts when pressed.

I've talked them into doing an adversarial collaboration, and I want to hand them the document as a draft and make them argue about my wording. So: could you write the thing both of them would be willing to sign? Its not a summary of the debate and I don't want a referee's verdict on who's right — I want a text two people who genuinely disagree could put their names to.

Here's what I need in it:

1. A numbered section of claims both sides can actually sign. Not watered-down mush — things like what the biology of conception involves, what the intuition pumps about swapped embryos and clones do and don't establish, which parts of the dispute they agree are merely verbal, and what each thinks is practically at stake for the clinics and the parents.

2. A disagreements table. Every row stated twice: once in wording the essentialist would accept as a fair statement of their own view, once in wording the other side would accept for theirs. If the two formulations of a given row collapse into the same sentence, say so — that's a finding, not a failure.

3. The cruxes. The two or three sub-questions where settling the matter would actually move at least one of them. I'd bet one crux is whether the necessity claim attaches to the gametes themselves or to something more proximate (the zygote, the fertilisation event, the first cell division), and another is whether there's any fact of the matter about who a person could have been independent of how we describe the case. Make the two of them say where they think the cruxes are, not where I think they are.

4. For each crux, a case both agree bears on it, with each side's verdict written down in advance so I can hold them to it later.

The cases are the bit I care most about, and I want the awkward ones from the clinic rather than the usual teleporters and brain-splitting thought experiments. Please have them rule explicitly on: (a) a child born after mitochondrial donation, where the nuclear DNA is from the parents' own gametes but the egg's mitochondria come from a donor — and note there's always a bit of the mother's mitochondrial DNA carried over, so the material isn't cleanly one or the other; (b) a child conceived from an egg grown in a dish from a woman's own skin cells, so no egg of the kind she was born with ever entered into it; (c) a separate case where a clinic mixes up two frozen samples from the same donor — same man, different sperm cell, so the origin differs but not by much; (d) identical twins, who share an origin entirely and yet are plainly not the same person, which seems to me to put real pressure on the essentialist's story about what origin is doing; and (e) the dull one everyone skips — a person whose mother conceived them a cycle later, or after a long-haul flight, where nothing about the gametes changes and yet we're tempted to say it's a different child. For each, I want a one-line verdict from each side and one line of reasoning, in their own acceptable wording.

If you decide a chunk of this is verbal, say which chunk and why — I'd far rather have that than a fake-looking consensus. And please don't put either of them on record conceding the main point; the whole value is that they can both sign it without losing face.

Around a thousand words, more if you need it, numbered sections and then the table. American English spelling and punctuation throughout, please, because it's going into a US edition of the magazine. No bibliography, no reading list, no citations of any kind — I'll source it myself; I just need the document.
