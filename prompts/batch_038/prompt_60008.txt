Write a short joke set in a sleep laboratory. Two philosophers are shown the same recording of a subject's night: one argues in the tradition of Norman Malcolm, for whom treating a dream as an experience is a conceptual confusion, since nothing settles how the dream went apart from what the dreamer goes on to report on waking; the other argues in the tradition of Daniel Dennett, for whom it is instead a live empirical hypothesis about when the narrative gets composed, and who therefore holds that the recording could in principle decide the matter. The punchline must turn on that contrast — the two deliver the same verdict about the dream while disagreeing completely about what any experiment could show — and not on any stock joke about philosophers being brought into a laboratory. It should land for a reader who has never encountered either argument, while rewarding one who has.
