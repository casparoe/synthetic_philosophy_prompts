I'm a third-year doctoral student in a joint philosophy and cognitive science program, and next month our lab's reading group is devoting its session to the knowing-how / knowing-that debate. Our format is unusual: nobody presents a paper. Each participant instead brings a battery of constructed cases, and we spend two hours arguing about verdicts before anyone is allowed to invoke an authority. I have written six cases (below), each deliberately loaded with stipulations that I hope make them harder to absorb than the familiar ski-instructor and armchair-expert examples that everyone already knows how to defuse. What I want from you is not an essay on the debate and not a survey of who says what — I want a scoring exercise, so that I can walk into the session knowing which analyses my cases actually hurt and which ones shrug them off. No bibliography, no citations, no reference list: your verdicts are the deliverable.

The five target analyses, stated roughly so that we are fixed on the same things. (A) Rylean dispositionalism: knowing how to Φ is having a complex, multi-track disposition to Φ successfully when one tries, and it is not reducible to any body of knowledge-that (setting aside whether this is exegetically what Ryle himself held). (B) Strong intellectualism of the Stanley–Williamson type: knowing how to Φ is knowing, under a practical mode of presentation, a proposition that is a way for one to Φ, with no requirement that one be able to Φ. (C) Moderate intellectualism: as in (B), plus a requirement that the agent be able to be guided by the proposition in action, or to deploy it. (D) The objectual line associated with Bengson and Moffett: knowing how is standing in a relation to a *way* of Φ-ing, where ways are neither propositions nor dispositions, so (B) misidentifies the object of the state. (E) A deflationary option: the how/that contrast tracks what attributors are interested in and how our attribution practices are calibrated, not a difference in kind among underlying mental states, so much of the debate is verbal.

The six cases. **1. The Anomic Virtuoso.** Mira, a professional cellist, suffers a left-hemisphere stroke and is left with severe global aphasia: she cannot produce or comprehend any declarative sentence about music-making, cannot judge whether a statement about bowing or shifting is true, fails every multiple-choice and forced-choice test of musical knowledge we can construct, and cannot teach, explain, or correct anyone. Yet she sight-reads at her pre-morbid professional level, compensates for an out-of-tune string, adapts to a bow with broken hairs, transfers to an unfamiliar instrument, and improvises fluently. By every available test her propositional knowledge about cello playing is at or near zero, while her flexible, reliable success is undiminished. **2. The Armchair Tactician.** Omar spent twenty years as a video analyst and statistical modeler; he never played basketball beyond schoolyard level and could not now make a single competent move. He has acquired a complete and correct propositional account of offense and defense — spacing, pick-and-roll coverage, clock management, defensive rotations — and coaches a semi-professional team by radio in real time, to considerable success. He also drives a humanoid practice robot through a brain-computer interface by issuing explicit, deliberated, step-by-step commands; the robot executes well but nothing about its performance is intuitive. **3. The Second-Language Swimmer.** Lena learned to swim as a child in a lake, by a thrash-and-glide stroke and a back float. At thirty she acquires a complete and correct propositional account of front crawl — catch, high elbow, bilateral breathing, six-beat kick — passes every test on it, mimes it accurately on deck, and can diagnose and verbally correct another swimmer's crawl by watching. In the water her body reverts: despite effort and despite understanding every proposition, she cannot execute front crawl at all and swims only in her childhood way. **4. The Cookbook Machine.** MISE is an industrial cooking system holding every proposition in every cookbook and food-science text in a symbolic store, plus a physics and chemistry simulator. It reliably produces excellent dishes, including under ingredient substitutions it has never encountered, by re-deriving from first principles (no buttermilk, so acid plus milk, corrected for protein content; altitude adjustments; allergen substitutions). It passes every propositional test we can devise, including counterfactual ones. It has no dispositions in the sense we use for animals: it does not try things, and when an actuator fails it reports the failure and halts. **5. The Blocked Locksmith.** Yuen is a master locksmith with thirty years of experience and complete, current propositional knowledge of the mechanisms she works with. She moves to a jurisdiction where, by ordinance and manufacturer lock-in, every lock is a design she has never encountered and cannot open with legally available tools; over two years every attempt fails. Nothing has changed in her: her judgment, her feel, her teaching, her performance in simulation, and her success on museum specimens she is permitted to borrow are all fully intact. **6. The Pit Crew.** A nine-person racing crew executes a sub-two-second tire change. No member knows how to do it: each has mastered exactly one component, and none could describe or execute the whole sequence or its timing relationships. The crew performs reliably under variation — wet track, damaged rim, one member substituted. There is no group belief, no shared intention beyond a standing training routine, and nobody who could do it alone.

Here is what I want back. First, a matrix: for each of the five analyses against each of the six cases, state in a compact phrase the verdict that analysis delivers (does the subject know how, and if so how the analysis gets there), then a damage score from 0 to 3, where 0 means the analysis yields the verdict you take to be correct with no auxiliary hypotheses, 1 means it gets there but needs a modest and independently motivated refinement, 2 means it must either swallow a strongly counterintuitive verdict or import an ad hoc clause, and 3 means it survives only by being restated into something that no longer resembles the original view. One sentence of justification per cell, and please do not inflate or compress the scale to make the analyses look uniform. Second, totals out of 18, plus a separate count of how many distinct ad hoc auxiliaries each analysis needed across the whole battery, and then a final ranking of the five from best to worst with a tier label for each (defendable in print / workable with surgery / salvage only / unsalvageable). Third, four short answers: which of my six cases is the dud, where all five analyses converge, and whether that convergence shows my case is useless or that the debate is narrower than it advertises itself to be; which analysis has the cheapest escape hatch across the battery and which of my stipulations it exploits; a single number between 0 and 1 for the probability that the how/that debate is substantive rather than terminological; and, if you had to commit to one analysis for a twenty-year research program, which one and with what probability. Finally, flag any of the six cases that strikes you as a reskinning of a stock thought experiment already in circulation — say which of my stipulations is doing genuinely new work there, without needing to name sources.
