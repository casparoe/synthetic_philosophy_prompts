I run a grad reading group on distributive justice and I've got a problem. Two people in it — a card-carrying prioritarian and a card-carrying telic egalitarian of the Temkin stripe — have been circling the leveling-down objection since September and its gone nowhere. Every time one of them says the objection shows equality can't be intrinsically valuable, the other says it's attacking a straw man, since the telic egalitarian was never committed to leveling down being better all things considered, only to its being better in one respect. Then the first one says a value that never tips any all-things-considered judgment isn't a value at all, and we get another forty-minute arguement about person-affecting principles. I want to force them onto paper. Draft the joint statement the two of them could both sign after a genuinely good-faith adversarial collaboration — I'm going to hand it to them and ask each to initial every line they accept and strike anything they can't.

Here's the material I want the statement to work through, and please use these two cases as I've set them up rather than swapping in stock ones. First, the Windfall case: a thousand people, all at well-being 100; then half of them receive a treatment that raises them to 200 and does literally nothing to the other half — nobody loses anything, no scarcity, no status effects, no envy, stipulate all of that away. Second, the Rescaling case: someone points out that prioritarianism applies a concave weighting function to a cardinal welfare measure, so it needs a privileged zero and a privileged unit, whereas the question "are these two people equally well off?" is untouched by any monotone rescaling of the measure while "how much extra moral weight does this gain get?" is not. So the egalitarian claims their view is less hostage to the measurement scale, and the prioritarian claims that's cheap because degrees of inequality are just as scale-dependent as the weighted sum. I'd like the statement to say, in wording each side would accept for its own position, whether that's a real asymmetry or a wash.

Format: exactly eight numbered items, no introduction and no conclusion. Items 1–3 are the agreed claims both sides can sign. Item 4 is a disagreements table with four columns — the issue, how the prioritarian states it, how the egalitarian states it, and whether the difference is genuine or merely verbal; where you call something verbal, say which part and why. Items 5 and 6 are the two cruxes, and each has to name the case or argument both sides agree actually bears on it, plus each side's prediction written down before the result is known: for the Windfall case I want the egalitarian on record about whether the equality loss could ever outweigh the gains to the half who benefit, and for the Rescaling case I want the prioritarian on record about whether a change of welfare scale could flip their ranking of two outcomes. Item 7 is what each side would count as a real defeat of their own view, and whether there's anything both agree would actually move the debate. Item 8 is a note telling me which of all this is settled among people who work on the topic and which is still live, and I mean it — separate the stuff everyone grants from the stuff the two camps and their colleagues genuinely disagree about, and don't let either side's contested commitments get dressed up as consensus. No bibliography, no citations, no reading list, and please don't invent any; I'd much rather see something flagged as contested than attributed to a philosopher it isn't.
