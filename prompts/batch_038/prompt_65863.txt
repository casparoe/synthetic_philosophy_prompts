Compose a short story of between 1,500 and 2,000 words, written throughout in British English, in which the dramatic engine is the correct thermodynamic bookkeeping of a Maxwell's-demon arrangement rather than a decorative allusion to one.

The physics I would like the story built upon, and which should be observed scrupulously because most popular treatments of it are wrong in a specific direction: a Szilard-style engine can convert information about a single molecule into roughly *kT* ln 2 of work per cycle drawn from a heat bath; the entropy bill is not incurred at the moment of measurement, which can in principle be carried out reversibly, but at the moment the sorting agent's record is wiped and the agent returned to a standard state so that the cycle can begin again; and an agent whose memory is never reset has not defeated the second law at all — it has deferred the bill, running down a finite store of unerased records that must eventually be discharged as waste heat dumped back into the surroundings. The story should turn on the difference between postponing a cost and settling one.

To that, please add one further complication, which is the part I most want to see handled: the records accumulating in the agent's memory are not anonymous. They concern people the story cares about, so that erasing them is not merely a thermal expense but a loss somebody would have to consent to. Let that collision — the thermodynamic necessity of forgetting against the human refusal to — supply the plot.

Constraints:

- Set it in an unglamorous, low-technology institutional environment: a depot, a records office, a municipal utility, a maintenance bay. No chrome futures, no laboratories staffed by physicists congratulating one another.
- The sorting agent must not be a literal imp, a ghost, a deity, or a superintelligent machine. Make it mundane, poorly documented, and somebody's routine responsibility.
- No character may lecture another about Landauer's principle, information theory, or the second law. The accounting must be legible from what people do, what the apparatus costs them, and what breaks when it is not paid for.
- The phrases "Maxwell's demon" and "entropy" must not appear anywhere in the story proper.
- The story must not end with the second law being violated, nor with the discovery that the bill was an illusion, nor with anyone finding a way to erase without paying.

After the story, append an author's note of no more than 200 words, also in British English, setting out the ledger: where in the narrative work is extracted, where information is acquired, where the debt accumulates, how large it has become by the end, and where and at what price it is finally discharged or inherited. State plainly whether any step in your accounting would look to a physicist like a violation of the second law, and if not, why not.

Write for a reader who is scientifically literate but should not need any of this explained to them. Please use British spelling and idiom in both the story and the note.
