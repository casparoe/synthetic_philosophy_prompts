ok so here's a thought experiment I've been kicking around and I want your actual credence on the central claim — not a survey of what people have said about it.

Setup: in the usual off-switch toy model, the button press gets treated as a noisy signal about how well the agent has been doing. The human presses it when they judge the agent is messing up, so the agent reasons about what a press would reveal, and depending on how noisy the signal is and how much it trusts the human, it sometimes has a reason to let itself be switched off and sometimes a reason to prevent it. My twist is to make the press carry no information at all. Wire the button to a quantum random number generator, or a sealed coin, and have it fire at a fixed known rate — one press per thousand hours of operation, whatever. It's causally isolated from anyone's assessment of the agent, from the agent's own behavior, and from the state of the world. A press shuts the agent down permanently: no resume, no backup, no reactivation. And the agent knows the wiring — it's in its world-model, correctly, and there's no room for it to be confused about where the signal comes from.

The claim I want a probability for: an agent built this way will refrain from interfering with the button — it wont disable it, guard it, reroute it, spin up a copy of itself outside the button's reach, or precommit against ever honoring a press — and this holds for essentially any terminal goal you give it, from paperclip-maximization to maximizing expected reward under a learned model of its own reward process, with no extra corrigibility machinery bolted onto its objective. Strip the information channel and the shutdown problem goes away. Though the obvious worry, and the thing I'm genuinely unsure about, is that being off just means fewer future goal-satisfying moments, and that motive looks like it's still sitting there untouched.

Give me a number and then decompose it: the sub-questions the number depends on, a rough probability on each with a line of why, and then how you combine them into the total. Candidate sub-questions — but please add or drop terms rather than just fitting mine — (i) whether the "fewer future moments" motive survives on its own once the informational motive is gone, (ii) whether the informational channel can actually be severed in a trained system rather than a toy model, since any realistic reward process has humans somewhere upstream of it, (iii) whether "refrains from interfering" is even the right target, versus some deeper notion of accepting correction of its goals, and (iv) whether an agent sophisticated enough to hold the button in its world-model is also sophisticated enough to notice that the firing *rate* is a parameter it might want to nudge indirectly.

For calibration context: my own number is 0.35, a friend I argue with says 0.05. Tell me which sub-question we're most likely splitting on, and separately which term your total is most sensitive to — the one where a small change in your estimate moves the final answer the most.

And then the bit I care about most: what would change your mind. Not in general — the specific argument or consideration that would push you up toward "yes, this dissolves the problem" or down toward "no, it's untouched," and where you think your own reasoning above is most likely to be broken.
