Six weeks ago a consumer DNA test established that the man who raised me — he is eighty-one, and well — is not my biological father. My mother has confirmed it and asked that he never be told. Since then my household has been consumed by an argument I cannot adjudicate. My brother maintains that the test settled something that could not have been otherwise: had my mother conceived with the other man, the child in that house would simply have been somebody else, so in the strict sense I have exactly one father and he is a stranger. My husband maintains the reverse: that a word like *father* fixes onto whoever actually stood in that relation to you, that thirty-eight years of it are not overturned by a swab, and that my brother is treating one biological detail as though it were the whole of what the word means. What disturbs me most is that they use the same word and yet never appear to contradict one another. Within the month I must decide whether to honour my mother's request, whether to contact the other man, and what to tell my own children about who their grandfather is.

Reply with exactly five numbered points, each of one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion: what is genuinely at issue between my brother and my husband, and what I should do about the three decisions.
