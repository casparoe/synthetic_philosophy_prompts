I say I believe in subsidiarity but I'm not sure my gut actually tracks it. Some verdicts, roughly in order of how confident I am in them:

- Road salt and plow rules set by my small state: fine. The same state setting stack-emission limits for a plant whose fallout lands mostly in the next state downwind: not fine, even though the local-knowledge argument is exactly the same in both.
- A river running through four states: I think that decision belongs to a basin authority that isn't a government, that nobody elects, and that exists only for that river. Fine.
- A town that wants to cap warehouse development but can't, because any neighbor with looser rules gets the tax base instead: illegitimate, even though it's local and everyone knows what they're doing. The same town choosing looser rules because its residents genuinely want the jobs: legitimate.
- One state setting generous benefits and drawing people from stricter states: fine for the state. Also fine for the federation to stop the others retaliating at their borders. I can't explain why I hold both.
- Region-run healthcare in a region with no capacity to run it: not fine, and I don't think that's just about money.
- The federation deciding which decisions belong to the federation: fine, someone has to. The federation's courts deciding it, and steadily deciding that more of it is theirs: not fine.
- Someone who hates their town's rules can move three miles to the next town, and that's part of why local control is okay. Someone who hates their country's rules can move to another country: I don't want to say the same thing, but I don't know why not.

Is there a single principle that gets all of these? I suspect a couple can't be saved and I'd rather be told which than handed a formula with seven clauses to protect every verdict. And what does the best candidate say about the one I keep dodging: a small unit banning something — a minaret, a book in the school library — where the whole cost falls on a minority inside that unit that can't realistically leave?
