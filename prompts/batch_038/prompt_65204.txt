At a public panel last week a law professor kept repeating "equality of opportunity, not equality of outcome," and he used it to shoot down a proposal for a guaranteed minimum income: transfers are about results, he said, whereas hiring discrimination is about chances, and only the second is injustice. I mumbled something about the slogan not being self-interpreting and then sat down, which was unsatisfying.

So: give me the menu. What are the genuinely different things "equality of opportunity" could be demanding, once you're explicit about what's supposed to be equalised — absence of formal barriers, equal prospects for the equally talented and motivated, equal starting resources, equal capability sets, equal chances at welfare? Sort them from platitude to radical, flag which are coherent and which fall apart under pressure, and say which reading, if any, actually licenses his move against income floors. And deal with a suspicion of mine: on the capability reading the opportunity/outcome contrast stops being a real distinction, since a capability is a kind of achieved state even when its sold as a freedom. Is that right, or am I mangling Sen?

List form is fine — I want to hand it to my distributive justice seminar.
