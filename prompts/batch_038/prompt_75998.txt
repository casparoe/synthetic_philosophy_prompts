I'm drafting a short primer on the foundations of set theory, and I want to present Russell's paradox as solved rather than merely evaded. The line I have in mind is that ZFC's axiom of foundation does the work: since it rules out any set's being a member of itself, the collection of all non-self-membered sets can never be formed and the contradiction cannot get started — on which reading the paradox is an artifact of set theories that tolerate self-membership. How should I state precisely which instances of comprehension foundation eliminates, so a reader can verify the claim rather than take it on trust? And what should the primer say about a theory that retains a universal set — must the paradox be handled there by some further restriction, and if one added foundation to full unrestricted comprehension, would the contradiction still go through? I'd rather get the exposition right than merely plausible.
