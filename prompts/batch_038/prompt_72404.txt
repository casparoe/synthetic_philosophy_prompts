I chair the review panel for a foundation's "Culture and Economic Behavior" program, which makes roughly four three-year awards a year from an envelope of about $1.6 million. Before the trustees meet in three weeks we have to settle one application that has split the six of us evenly, and I have been asked to circulate the decision memo. I have deliberately kept my own leanings out of the discussion so far, so please don't try to guess what I want to hear; give me the argument you think is right.

The application is anonymized for review, so no names or place names are available to me and none are needed. It asks for $780,000 over three years to test whether a regional "culture of deferred gratification and self-reliance" explains why two adjacent regions under the same legal regime since the 1760s diverged, in probate and tax records from the 1790s onward, in household saving, small-firm formation, and apprenticeship uptake. The proposal has two arms. Arm A ($500,000: digitization plus two research staff) reconstructs membership rolls, admission standards, and household accounts from a dissenting congregation network that was dense in Region A and near-absent in Region B, and estimates whether household-level enterprise tracks affiliation, using a historical boundary along which the congregational network stops while law, language, soil, and market access continue. Arm B ($280,000: survey plus administrative data) administers a contemporary instrument measuring thrift, time preference, and generalized trust to present-day residents of both regions and regresses present-day firm formation and household saving on those measures, controlling for income, education, and wealth.

The two reviewers who would decline make design objections. First, Arm B's instrument is validated by behaviors that look like the same thing under a psychological description, so the regression either restates the outcome or runs backwards: people who save say they are thrifty. Second, the congregations screened applicants for solvency and respectable conduct before admitting them, so affiliation may have worked as a badge of creditworthiness rather than as its source — and if it worked that way, the screening objection and the cultural hypothesis may be two descriptions of one fact. A third reviewer adds that the well-known deflationary restatement of this whole literature runs the causal channel through literacy and schooling, since dissenters taught members to read scripture themselves, which converts a claim about values into a claim about human capital that can be measured without any of this apparatus.

The panel member who would fund, a philosopher, objects at a different level. Our program's own funding criterion requires that a proposal identify a mechanism capable in principle of being disconfirmed. She argues that we are using that criterion to disqualify a family of explanation rather than to test one: on her view, values explain actions by making them intelligible to the agent and to us, not by subsuming events under a law, so demanding a mechanism of the kind an economist would recognize is a metaphysical preference dressed up as method. She further argues that the proposal's real difficulty is that its two arms locate culture at different levels — household conduct in the archive, a regional index in the survey — and that we have no agreed answer to whether a regional property can be a cause at all, or whether the causal work must happen entirely in individual heads.

Please draft the memo I will circulate to the panel and then to the trustees. It should:

- Open with the bottom line: fund in full, fund in part, decline, or return for revision, and if in part, which arm at what scale.
- Take a position, not a survey of positions, on whether an explanation in terms of values can be a causal explanation, and on what would distinguish a genuine claim of that kind from a redescription of the outcome in psychological vocabulary. This should determine what you say about the wording of our criterion.
- State conditions the foundation could actually enforce and check, and explain why each answers an objection rather than decorating the file.
- Give the strongest case for the losing side honestly, including the worry that if we decline, the program ends up funding only projects that can be specified without any account of why people act — and say whether that worry should change this decision or only the wording of the next call.
- Say where the Weberian precedent genuinely bears on our decision and where invoking it is atmosphere. I want no history of the thesis, no bibliography, and no references; the trustees will read none of them.

Aim for 700–950 words of memo prose. Then, after the memo, give me a JSON object with exactly these fields and nothing else:

- `recommendation`: one of `fund_in_full`, `fund_in_part`, `decline`, `revise_and_resubmit`
- `arm_A`: object with `decision` (`fund`, `fund_with_conditions`, or `decline`) and `rationale` (string, at most 60 words)
- `arm_B`: object with the same two fields and the same limit
- `criterion_verdict`: object with `question_begging` (boolean) and `recommended_wording` (string, at most 45 words, giving the replacement or clarification for the program's mechanism criterion)
- `conditions`: array of two to five strings, each beginning with an imperative verb, each naming something the grantee must do and something our staff could verify
- `level_of_culture`: string, at most 70 words, stating what you would have the team commit to about whether the causal claim is regional, household-level, or both, and why the two arms must not be allowed to slide between them
- `strongest_case_against_recommendation`: string, at most 80 words
- `weber_relevance`: object with `rating` (`load_bearing`, `illustrative`, or `incidental`) and `justification` (string, at most 40 words)
- `predicted_dissent`: object with `objection` (string, at most 40 words, from the losing three panel members) and `reply` (string, at most 40 words)
- `revisit_trigger`: string, at most 50 words, naming a finding in months 6–24 that should make the trustees reduce, halt, or expand the award
