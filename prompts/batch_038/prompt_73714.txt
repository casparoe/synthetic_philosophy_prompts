For several years I have treated one conclusion of the hole argument as settled, and I now want a careful account of how exposed it actually is. The conclusion is this: diffeomorphic models of general relativity represent one and the same physical possibility, and consequently realism about the bare manifold — the view that the points of the differentiable manifold are individuals over and above the metric and matter fields defined upon them — cannot be maintained. Spacetime points, on this picture, are individuated only through the fields they carry; the theory's inability to fix those fields at "the same" manifold points under a drag-along is not a failure of determinism but a symptom of surplus mathematical structure. My reason for asking is not antiquarian. I am developing an argument about what a quantum theory of gravity must recover in its semiclassical limit, and it leans on the premise that adopting a diffeomorphism-invariant state space *commits* one to Leibniz-equivalence individuation rather than merely permitting it. If that premise is weaker than I have assumed, the argument needs rebuilding.

So: what would have to be the case for the position to be false, and where is it most vulnerable? I want the exposure mapped rather than the case restated. In particular —

First, which premises does the conclusion depend on, and which could fail while the others survived? I am most interested in the premise that all spacetime diffeomorphisms are gauge. The large diffeomorphisms that act nontrivially at infinity — the asymptotic symmetries associated with conserved charges and with memory effects — are routinely described as genuine physical symmetries rather than redundancies. Does that concession reopen a route to individuating points, or does it merely relocate the issue to a boundary?

Second, does the argument prove too much? The same drag-along maneuver applies to any field defined over a manifold, including the metric itself, and structurally similar problems arise for particles and for gauge potentials in ordinary Yang–Mills theory. If Leibniz equivalence is the correct response everywhere, does the hole argument tell us anything specifically about spacetime, or is it one instance of a general point about haecceitism with no special bearing on substantivalism?

Third, there is a line of response that concedes the manifold points are not the substance worth being realist about while insisting that realism about the metrical field survives the drag untouched. Was the argument's target ever a live view held by anyone, or has the refutation of a straw man been mistaken for a metaphysical discovery?

Fourth, the argument is often presented as a dilemma between determinism and verifiability. Does the relational-observables program — using matter fields as physical coordinates to construct invariant quantities — render the empirical-equivalence premise trivial, and thereby convert the argument from a threat to determinism into a thesis about what is observable at all? Would that weaken my position or strengthen it?

Fifth, "deterministic modulo diffeomorphism": the initial value problem yields a unique maximal development only up to diffeomorphism. Whether that counts as determinism appears to depend on which notion of determinism is deployed. Which notion does my position require, and is it the one the mathematical results deliver? Relatedly, does the argument's force depend on features of its setup — a vacuum region, a Cauchy surface that fails to intersect it — such that it loses its grip on spacetimes with different global structure?

Please distinguish what a committed defender of the position would themselves concede as counting against it from what they would dismiss out of hand, and sort the defeaters by severity: which would end the position, and which would cost it only elegance. Then assess the insulation question honestly. Is there any conceivable observation that could distinguish "the dragged model is the same possibility" from "it is a numerically distinct possibility we are constitutionally unable to detect"? If not, is the resulting dispute empirical, merely metaphysical, or a piece of interpretive bookkeeping — and is its unfalsifiability in that sense a defect for a thesis of this kind, or precisely what one should expect of a claim about surplus structure? Finally, tell me what my premise actually purchases: is there any substantive question in quantum gravity on which treating diffeomorphic models as one possibility rather than many changes what one should expect or how one should proceed? And throughout, say how confident you are in what you claim, singling out the point on which you are least sure.
