Here's my opening move. Your argument runs: history is driven above all by the growth of knowledge, we can't predict the future growth of our own knowledge, so we can't predict the course of history. Fine — but that second premise is now an empirical claim, and we have data on it. We don't forecast the content of the next discovery, nobody does, I'll give you that immediately. We forecast the shape and pace of knowledge growth: how long it typically takes a well-funded field to go from a working demo to something reliable, how many independent groups converge on the same result within a year of each other, whether a given program is running out of easy results.
