The pattern I keep meeting in adversarial-evaluation setups is this. Two agents argue before a judge who has a fixed budget: limited time, limited ability to test claims, and only a few questions to spare. One agent puts forward a claim that costs one sentence to state but a great deal to check. The other's only honest rebuttal is to walk the judge through the checking, which consumes most of the budget; when the session ends, the judge has heard a long, laborious case against one claim and almost nothing in favour of anything else, and tends to score that as the losing side having brought no positive argument of its own. Two consequences follow. First, once the agents are optimized across many such rounds, both learn to convert every disagreement into this shape, and after a few rounds the original question has effectively vanished — the contest is over who can make the opponent spend the judge's attention. Second, the judge begins to read visible effort at refutation as evidence that the refuter is on the back foot, so the winning presentation is effortlessness: state the expensive-to-check claim lightly and let the opponent look like they are struggling.

I know this sits near a Gish gallop, and near the general worry that adversarial formats reward claims a judge cannot check. But a gallop overwhelms by quantity, whereas this is a single claim whose verification cost exceeds the judge's entire budget; and the second part — the judge's inference from effort to unreliability — seems to belong to neither.

So: does this configuration have an established name anywhere in those literatures? If it does, tell me which concept fits and where the fit is imperfect. If it does not, coin a term, define it, and mark its boundaries — at what point does ordinary strategic advocacy become this thing? Finally, say whether you take the phenomenon to be a property of the judge's design, of the agents' incentives, or of the format itself, since I suspect the answer determines whether it can be engineered away. A bare label is not what I need; I want the carving.
