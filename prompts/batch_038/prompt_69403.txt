I'm second-marking for a second-year module on the history of the Earth sciences — it's called something like "Deep Time and Its Discontents" — and I've got one script that I've now read three times without landing anywhere. The question set was: "Four dates have been proposed as the beginning of the Anthropocene: c. 8000 years ago, 1610, 1784 and 1950. Explain what each proposal is trying to capture, and say whether the choice between them is settled by empirical evidence or by convention. (1,000 words, no more.)" Here's the answer, copied out so you can see it as I do:

"The Anthropocene is the proposal that human activity has become a geological force, which means we are no longer living in the Holocene. The term comes from Crutzen and Stoermer (2000), who put the beginning in the late eighteenth century and suggested Watt's improved steam engine of 1784 as a marker, since the ice-core CO2 record starts bending upward around then and coal is what made the difference. So 1784 captures the Industrial Revolution as the moment energy use changes in kind rather than degree: not more people farming more land, but buried fossil carbon being dug up and burned.

The 1950 proposal is the one the geologists' own working group mostly favoured. After the first atomic tests, radioactive fallout — plutonium isotopes above all — settled out everywhere at once, in lake sediments, ice, corals and peat, giving a signal that is genuinely global and genuinely synchronous, which is what a boundary marker has to be. The same decades give us the 'Great Acceleration': population, GDP, fertiliser use, water consumption, CO2 and methane all inflect upwards together around mid-century. 1950 therefore captures the point where human impact becomes both global in extent and exponential in rate.

The 1610 proposal, from Lewis and Maslin, is different in kind. They link the boundary to the Columbian exchange: the collapse of indigenous American populations after 1492 returned so much farmland to forest that atmospheric CO2 fell, leaving a distinct low point in the ice-core record around 1610, which they call the Orbis spike. What this captures is that the geological signal can't be separated from a history of conquest — the epoch's origin is a social event rather than a technological one.

The 8000-year proposal is Ruddiman's early Anthropocene hypothesis: agriculture altered the atmosphere long before industrialisation. But this one was rejected because the ice-core record shows no measurable anthropogenic rise in CO2 or methane before 1800 — the anomalies Ruddiman points to fall inside the range of natural Holocene variability, so the hypothesis has nothing to explain it with.

Are these matters of evidence or of convention? Evidence obviously comes first: you can't put a boundary where there is no signal. But once several candidate signals are all well attested, the choice is made by people. Ratification runs through the Anthropocene Working Group, the Subcommission on Quaternary Stratigraphy and the International Commission on Stratigraphy, and it ends in a vote; the mid-twentieth-century proposal was in fact voted down at the subcommission stage recently, even though everyone agrees the word remains useful. And that shows the important thing: the Anthropocene is a cultural construct in the same way that 'medieval' or 'early modern' are. The dates are chosen to make a story hang together, not discovered.

Two further points. First, choosing 1950 means the Anthropocene is officially a nuclear age, because the marker is bomb fallout — which is why some critics say the geologists have let politics decide, and why others say it obscures the real cause, which is capitalism rather than bombs. Second, the Anthropocene cannot be an epoch in any case, since the ICS requires an epoch to run to millions of years and seventy-odd years of plutonium doesn't qualify. The honest conclusion is that the Anthropocene works best as a name for a historical condition that historians and scientists can share, without pretending it is a unit of the geological timescale."
