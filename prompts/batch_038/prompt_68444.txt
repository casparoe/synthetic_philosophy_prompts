I am preparing a short paper on imprecise credences and would like a consistency audit of the position I intend to defend. Below are my commitments as a numbered list; please treat them as a single package rather than assessing each in isolation.

1. The coin in question has a determinate physical bias. My imprecision is entirely a matter of ignorance about that bias, not of any indeterminacy in the world.
2. Nevertheless, given my evidence, no single credence about the bias is uniquely rational. The rational doxastic state is a convex set of probability functions, and narrowing it to a point estimate — even to ½ — would be mere dogmatism.
3. I update by conditionalising every member of the set on each new item of evidence. There is no special updating rule for imprecise states, and none is needed.
4. What brought me to this view is Ellsberg: the ambiguity-averse preferences are strictly rational, and that strictness is exactly what no single subjective prior can accommodate.
5. For decisions I adopt E-admissibility: an act is permissible if and only if it maximises expected utility with respect to at least one member of my set. Where several acts are admissible, all of them are permissible. I want no tie-breaking rule, since a tie-breaker would reintroduce the precision I rejected in (2).
6. I regard dilation as a defect: an experimental design under which observing something I am going to observe anyway widens my interval is a design I ought to avoid. Concretely, tomorrow I shall be told the outcome of an auxiliary measurement on a distinct physical process, and my own model tells me that whichever way it comes out, my interval for heads will widen from [0.4, 0.6] to [0.2, 0.8]. I have no intention of widening my interval today on the strength of what I expect to learn tomorrow.
7. I accept a reflection principle: my present opinions should already defer to the opinions I expect to hold after updating, given that I expect to update by conditionalisation alone and to lose no evidence.

Please tell me which of these genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to, ranking the real tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damaging. For each, say what would have to be given up, what the cheapest revision is, and what that revision would cost the rest of the package. I have no prior view about which commitment is the expendable one, and I would rather be told that two of them are perfectly compatible than have everything flagged. Please write in British English.
