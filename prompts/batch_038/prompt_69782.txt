I serve on the governance working group of a mid-sized technical standards consortium — a body whose members agree on specifications and which therefore also has to specify, in writing, how specifications come to be agreed. What follows is fictionalised for the purposes of the exercise: no real organisation's rules are in question, and I want no citations, no references to actual constitutions or statutes, and no survey of the legal-philosophy literature, which I will supply myself.

The material for the satire is this. Article 14 of the consortium's Bylaws sets out how the Bylaws may be amended, and Article 14(3) provides that Article 14(3) may not itself be amended, repealed, or suspended, and that no change may be adopted whose effect would be to amend, repeal, or suspend it. A member has now filed a change request to delete Article 14(3), and the working group must triage that request under procedures the request itself purports to abolish. Several related tickets have accumulated in the queue: a request to apply the current amendment procedure retroactively so as to validate a change adopted last year under a procedure that has since been repealed; a request to suspend the quorum rules in order to achieve the quorum required to suspend the quorum rules; a request to insert a clause providing that the Bylaws are authoritative as to their own authority; and a request to close a duplicate ticket whose original was closed as a duplicate of it.

Please write this as a parody of an issue-tracker thread: ticket identifiers, statuses, labels, assignees, timestamps, and comment chains, all in the flat procedural register of real change-management software. Around 900 words, with at least five tickets, one of which cannot be moved to any terminal status — resolved, won't fix, duplicate, invalid, or closed — without the act of closing it requiring authority from the very rule it closes. The comedy should come from getting the logic of self-reference right: the regress, the question of which rule is in force at the instant of transition, the difference between a rule that was invalid all along and a rule that was successfully displaced, and the fact that nothing inside the system can settle whether the system's own foundation has been replaced. Readers who have thought seriously about legal self-amendment should laugh rather than wince. The register must stay deadpan: nobody in the thread registers anything as philosophically strange, they are simply irritated about process and about who has to sign off. Do not explain the paradoxes inside the fiction, and do not let anyone break character.

After the thread, and clearly separated from it, add a note of no more than 200 words stating: (i) which features of ordinary amendment practice the piece reproduces faithfully and which it exaggerates for effect; (ii) where it leans on a contested way of framing the underlying problem rather than a neutral one, naming the alternative framing it excludes; and (iii) what the most credible rejoinder to the whole enterprise would be — that is, which consideration, if correct, would show that self-amending clauses of this kind are perfectly unproblematic and that the satire is therefore unfair, and where you think the piece's own implicit reasoning is most likely to fail.

Begin directly with the first ticket. No preface, no framing paragraph, no title, and no restatement of this request.
