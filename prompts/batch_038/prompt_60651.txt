I am assembling a graduate seminar on explanation and reduction, and I have concluded that the vocabulary I most want to teach is currently unusable as an instrument, because nobody can say what would count as applying it correctly. I mean Dennett's contrast, from *Darwin's Dangerous Idea* (1995), between cranes and skyhooks, and between good and greedy reductionism. He glosses the failure of greedy reductionism as the attempt to "skip whole layers or levels of theory in their rush to fasten everything securely and neatly to the foundation," and he summarises the contrast as follows: "Good reductionists suppose that all Design can be explained without skyhooks; greedy reductionists suppose it can all be explained without cranes." As far as I can see, both formulations tell you what a verdict sounds like rather than what makes it true, and the terms have since become polemical weapons wielded in both directions by partisans of and against reduction.

So before I put any candidate analysis in front of students, I want the specification. Take the target theory to be this: a criterion, statable in advance of any particular case and operable by someone who does not already know how the case should come out, which sorts proposed explanatory posits into cranes (legitimate, level-building explanations) and skyhooks (posits that smuggle in the explanandum from outside the natural order), and which sorts reductionist programmes into good and greedy. The purpose is prospective adjudication of live proposals, not retrospective congratulation.

Write the desiderata such a criterion must satisfy, with the case or consideration behind each. At minimum I want it to handle: Mendelian factors and vitalist entelechy as they stood in 1910, when both were placeholder posits and only one paid off; universality explanations of the renormalization-group variety, where deliberately ignoring lower-level detail is what makes the explanation good; a brute low-entropy initial condition for the universe, which looks either like a skyhook or like the only honest stopping point available; the strategy of explaining intelligence by decomposing an intelligent homunculus into stupider ones; a posited sui generis higher-level causal power, and the exclusion-style argument that denies it; and an innate structured endowment posited to explain the speed of acquisition.

Then tell me, for each desideratum, whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp, and say whose. I want the requirements that key on symmetry (the criterion must be able to convict anti-reductionists of skyhooks as readily as reductionists of skipping cranes), on prospectivity, on non-question-begging, on the difference between skipping a level and eliminating one, on gradability, on the way the verdict seems to shift with the explanandum a posit is offered for, and on the need for legitimate stopping points if cranes cannot stack forever. Flag any subset you believe cannot be jointly satisfied; my own suspicion is that symmetry, prospectivity, and non-question-begging cannot all be had at once, and if that is right, it is the main result rather than a footnote, since it would show the distinction is regulative rather than criterial. Finally, say which single requirement excludes the most candidates, and score four contenders against the list — mechanistic decomposability; unconstrainedness (a posit is a skyhook iff nothing independent of its target phenomenon constrains it); predictive consilience; and circularity (a posit is a skyhook iff it performs the very work the theory was introduced to explain).

Keep the weight on the requirements and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring, and give me no references or reading list — I want the analysis, not the bibliography.
