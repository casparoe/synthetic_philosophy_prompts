So, I write literary-ish novels, and my new one comes out in the spring. The whole thing hangs on a device: the narrator's wife signs up for a grief trial where one memory of the marriage is removed per session, weekly, for four years, and it's told straight through so you watch a person get subtracted. Basically every early reader — plus my agent, plus my editor — asks me some version of the same question at some point: which session is the one where she stops being the woman who got married? Which week did she stop greiving? And I keep saying "that's the point, there isn't one," and they keep looking at me like I've dodged.

Turns out the reading group here wants me to come and talk about the actual puzzle underneath the book — the heap thing, the old Greek one — for 75 minutes, back room of a bookshop, fourteen people, no slides, just a flipchart and some markers. The catch is who they are: mostly retired copyeditors and proofreaders, plus two people who worked on dictionaries for decades. These are people who spent forty years deciding exactly where a word stops applying and getting paid to be right about it.

Don't explain the paradox to me, I've read plenty. Plan how I should explain it to them.

What order do the pieces have to go in, and what genuinely depends on what? My instinct is I can't open with the heap itself or they'll have "solved" it inside ten minutes and I'll never get the room back.

Should my memory-erasure thing be the example that carries the whole session, or is it a trap? I worry it lets them off instantly — grief and love come on a dial, obviously, so where's the puzzle. If it's bad, tell me what should carry it instead, and what an example like that has to have and what it must not have.

What will they walk in holding? My guesses: "vagueness is just lazy definitions, write it precisely and the problem evaporates" — a copyeditor said close to this about my own manuscript, to my face. And one of them has already told me there obviously *is* a last grain, we simply can't know which one. Where in the sequence do I head each of those off, and what do I actually say when I do? Related: when they say just define your terms, the definitions they'd write are built out of other vague words — do I make that point early, or does it backfire and look like I'm cheating?

Also, do I have to tell them what I think the right answer is? I don't have one. Can I run the session without picking a side, or does it fall apart if I never come down somewhere?

Then one question I can put to them at the end that'll tell me whether it landed versus whether I just charmed them for an hour. And what I should flatly refuse to get into at this level — I'm assuming there's a whole technical apparatus under here that would eat the evening alive if I let it in the door. Say which bits I cut and what I tell them when they ask.
