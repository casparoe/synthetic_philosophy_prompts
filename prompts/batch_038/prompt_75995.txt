No math degree here — I do network administration and read philosophy stuff on the internet at night for fun — so bear with me. Last week in a Discord argument about whether math could turn out to be wrong, one guy ended the whole thing with "if arithmetic were inconsistent, we'd have noticed by now." Everyone went "yeah, fair" and moved on. I couldn't let it go, and the more I poke at it the less I think its actually one claim.

Here's what's bugging me. Half the time it sounded like a plain inductive point: people have been checking proofs for a very long time, no contradiction has ever shown up, so the odds are tiny — same shape as "no wolf has ever come out of that woods, so there probably aren't any in there." But other times it sounded like something stronger and weirder: that the question isn't really a question at all, because there's nothing it could mean for a contradiction to be hiding somewhere we couldn't reach, so you couldn't have evidence about it either way. Those seem to pull against each other. If there's evidence, then it's the kind of thing that could have come out differently.

My own idea, probably naive: suppose arithmetic IS inconsistent, but the shortest proof of a contradiction is longer than the number of particles in the universe, and it only involves numbers so enormous that no proof anyone will ever write down and no physical system anywhere touches them. Then the slogan comes out literally true — we'd never notice. Does that scenario make the claim false, make it meaningless, or make it true but worthless? I honestly can't tell, and I suspect the answer changes depending on which version of the slogan you've got in hand.

So can you lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be standing for, as a menu instead of picking your favorite and defending it? For each one, tell me whether it's trivial, substantive but defensible, or just false — what would have to hold for it to be true, what would falsify it, and what's doing the real work inside it (what exactly is being sampled, who "we" is, what "noticing" would consist of, and whether it's a claim about probability or a claim about meaning). Also, nobody in that chat seemed to know which system was even on trial — arithmetic, set theory, "math" generally — so say whether the slogan amounts to something different depending on that. Then tell me which reading people most likely mean when they drop it to shut a conversation down, which readings would actually license that use, and which of my giant-number cases kill which readings.

And please be straight about what's settled here versus what people who really know this topic genuinely disagree about — don't hand me a contested view dressed up as the consensus. American English, thanks.
