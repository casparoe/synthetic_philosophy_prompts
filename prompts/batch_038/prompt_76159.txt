I am a backend engineer with no philosophy training, and I have been trying to write down what I actually believe about mind uploading so that someone can point out where the set breaks. Please treat the list as data rather than as an attempted position paper.

My commitments:

1. There is nothing to being a conscious subject beyond the right kind of functional organization. Nothing extra rides on the implementation being biological.
2. What matters in survival is psychological continuity: the carrying over of memory, character, and dispositions. Strict identity does not matter, and neither does the persistence of any particular matter.
3. Replacing my neurons one at a time with functionally equivalent synthetic parts, spread over a year, would be survival. I would sign up for that.
4. Scanning my whole brain, building a working duplicate somewhere else, and then painlessly destroying the original would not be survival. The duplicate would be a stranger who sincerely believes it is me, and my death would have happened.
5. I would, however, accept a nightly checkpoint: my state is snapshotted every night while I sleep, and if I die in the night, the most recent snapshot is instantiated in fresh substrate the next morning. I would call that survival with a gap, and I would want my family told to expect me back.
6. The same stream of consciousness continuing is not, on my view, a deep fact about the world. It is a bookkeeping abstraction, much like the identity of a service across a restart. That said, I still think the gradual route in (3) is genuinely better than the instantaneous one in (4), in the way a rolling deployment beats tearing a service down and rebuilding it from the same specification — and I do not think that preference needs a deep fact to stand on.

Audit this set. Which pairs are actually in conflict, and which only appear to be? For each real conflict, say which commitment has to go or be weakened, what the cheapest repair is, and what that repair costs me elsewhere in the list. Rank the conflicts by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would restore the most consistency. I am not attached to any item, and I would rather be told that one of them is false than that the set is uncomfortable but livable.
