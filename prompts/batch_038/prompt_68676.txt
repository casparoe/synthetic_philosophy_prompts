Going into a foundations reading group next week and they asked me to write down where I stand on whether the wavefunction is real, so the others can take a run at it. Before I hand it in I'd like a sanity check, because I have a feeling there's a crack somewhere and I cant find it. Here's the list I wrote:

1. I'm psi-ontic. The quantum state is part of the furniture of the world, not a bookkeeping device for whatever I happen to know.
2. I think PBR basically settles it. And I think the standard escape hatch — just deny preparation independence — isn't a real option, because if two systems prepared separately and far apart can still turn out to have correlated hidden variables, you've smuggled in exactly the kind of spooky dependence nobody wants.
3. That said, I think two agents can assign different states to the same system and both be right. After Alice measures her half of an entangled pair and before she's told Bob anything, Alice says collapsed and Bob says not, and I don't think either of them is making a mistake — they just have different evidence.
4. I want the wavefunction to be something like a field in ordinary three-dimensional space, its a weird fluid if you like. I really dislike the version where it's a field on some gigantic configuration space, because that doesn't look like the world I live in.
5. I don't think the quantum state is the whole story — there's got to be more underneath, extra variables or a matter density or something.
6. No collapse, ever. The universal state just evolves unitarily and measurement isn't a special kind of process.

Tell me which pairs of these genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do, and for the apparent ones say why they dissolve. Order them worst first, give me the cheapest fix for each, and if one of the fixes clears more mess than its own, say so right there in that item. Format it as a numbered list of exactly five items, one or two sentences each, no intro and nothing at the end wrapping up.
