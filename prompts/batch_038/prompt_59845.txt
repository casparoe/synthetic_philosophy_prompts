we're finishing a reading group on pragmatics and i want to close the last session with a short poem instead of a summary — something like 22 lines, five quatrains and a closing couplet, rhyme optional, i dont care about meter, but the repeating-line machinery is the whole point so please take that seriously.

the poem happens entirely inside one department meeting where a hiring decision is being circled and nobody says the thing. there's a single bland, polite sentence — the kind where all the content lives in what it declines to say — that opens every one of the five quatrains word-for-word identically. but each time it comes back, the speaker immediately bolts a little tail onto it, a retracting clause along the lines of *not that i mean to suggest anything by that* or *which isnt a comment on the file either way*. make the tails genuinely different each time and increasingly strained: first casual, then defensive, then over-long, then addressed to someone specific in the room, then almost a plea. so the reader watches a sentence whose literal content never budges an inch while what the speaker is doing with it keeps getting extended and then yanked back. someone who knows the material should be able to hear that the sentence-meaning stays fixed and only the speaker-meaning moves; someone who doesn't should just get a good, slightly mean poem about a faculty meeting.

the couplet is the turn. the same retracting move gets tried one last time on a sentence where the thing being denied is actually contained in it — a headcount, who was in the room, a date, something like that — so the tail, instead of trimming away a suggestion, lands as a flat contradiction of the sentence it's attached to. end on that jam, with the pair of lines sitting there unable to be walked back the way the other four could be. don't resolve it or explain it, and dont use any of the technical vocabulary anywhere in the poem: no implicature, no maxim, no entailment, no cancellable, and no naming grice. the couplet should demonstrate the distinction without ever gesturing at it.
