so i'm a junior and our philosophy club president made this argument last week and ive gone over it like ten times and i still cant beat it. its supposed to show that if you're a deflationist about truth then basically every big debate is fake, which i really dont want to accept.

(1) Deflationists say the whole content of "is true" is given by the equivalence schema: to say a sentence is true is just to say the sentence. "it's true that 7 is prime" and "7 is prime" make the same claim, so "true" adds nothing.

(2) if adding "is true" adds nothing, then insisting adds nothing either. saying p is REALLY true, or true in a robust sense, or true because some fact makes it true, is just p again plus noise. emphasis isnt content.

(3) now numbers. the platonist says the theorems are true because of mind independent abstract objects, the nominalist says theres no such things and the theorems are just a useful way of talking. by (2), strip the truth talk out of both and what's left is one more sentence of exactly the same kind as the ones we started with: are there numbers?

(4) but both of them accept the normal arithmetic sentences. the nominalist happily says 7 is prime and that theres infinitely many primes, and they work out the same proofs.

(5) so they dont disagree about anything. if it looks like they do, thats just noise, and the whole debate is verbal.

(6) the obvious escape is to say the nominalist MEANS something different by those sentences. but by (1) a difference in meaning would have to show up as a difference in which equivalences they accept, and there is no such difference. both accept that "7 is prime" is true iff 7 is prime.

(7) the same move works everywhere else. moral realist vs error theorist, people arguing about whether other minds are real, whatever: strip out the truth talk and everyone is left holding the same plain sentences, so everyone agrees.

(8) so deflationism implies every substantive debate is verbal, and no position can be better than its rival, since rivals turn out to say the same thing.

(9) but thats crazy. me and my friend genuinely disagree about numbers and one of us is wrong.

so which step do i reject, and why did it look so harmless when i read it? id rather keep (1) and (9) than anything else, the schema just seems obviously right to me and i like that it doesnt force you into wierd metaphysics, but tell me what im paying for keeping them, and what it would cost to give up (2) or (6) instead. and then say what deflationism actually commits you to here: does it get to keep the schema and still think some of these debates are real? if yes, how, without just repeating that the schema is neutral.
