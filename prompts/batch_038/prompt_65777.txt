I'm an ICU nurse, not a philosophy person, but I've been chewing on something about my hospital's safety program and I can't get clear on it. When somebody makes a med error we're supposed to file a report and it goes to an official "just culture" review — the whole point is nobody gets punished for an honest mistake, they console you, they go fix the process, and they tell you that reporting it was the right thing to do because otherwise we never learn anything. But the same review uses a flowchart that sorts the event into human error, at-risk behavior, or reckless behavior, and only the third bucket gets you disciplined or sent to the state board. One of the questions on it basically asks whether a handful of other nurses with my training would have made the same choice in the same situation, which always struck me as a weird thing to ask: it doesn't turn on what I knew or whether I could have done otherwise, it turns on what my peers would have done. And there have been a couple of cases around the country where nurses got criminally charged over med errors, which everyone on my unit remembers.

So what picture of blame and responsibility does this whole setup assume? Does it assume blame is just a tool that happens to suppress reporting, so we withhold it when its useless and deploy it when it works? Or does it assume the reckless person is different in kind, so that something is owed to them and to the family that the consoled nurse isn't owed? And how can one institution hold both attitudes toward the same person on the same day — comforting her in the morning, forwarding her file to the board in the afternoon? Tell me what the practice strictly has to assume versus what merely fits it comfortably, where it's genuinely underdetermined and could be read two or three ways, and what would actually have to change in the flowchart and in the review if we swapped in a different view — say one where resentment and indignation aren't instruments at all but the natural response of the people involved, which you can't just switch off for the sake of better data.
