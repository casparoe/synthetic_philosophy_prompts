Please take the role of a philosopher of biology who has worked on the concept of function and who is also comfortable with how genomic databases actually decide what to write in a record. I'm a software engineer on a comparative genomics group; I build and maintain the annotation pipeline, and I have no formal training in philosophy. Two of the scientists I work with had an argument last week that I got pulled into, partly because one of them used my codebase as her example. I could not work out who had the better of it, and I would like you to referee it.

Take the following facts as given. Please do not ask for more data, and do not spend time on whether the biology is plausible; it is our biology. We annotate a gene family across seven related species of freshwater fish. Call the ancestral gene G1: it encodes a digestive protease expressed in the gut, and it has done that for as long as anyone has looked. Call the duplicate G2. G2 arose from G1 roughly fifteen million years ago, in the common ancestor of the seven. In six of them, G2 is expressed in the gill and skin epithelium, where it cleaves a mucin-like structural protein; G2 knockouts in those species are markedly more susceptible to a skin parasite. G2 also retains about five percent of G1's activity on G1's gut substrate in vitro. A point mutation that abolishes that residual activity while leaving the skin activity intact produces no detectable phenotype under any condition we have tested, including parasite challenge. In the seventh species, G2 is present and expressed at low levels, retains the residual protease activity, is not expressed in skin or gill, and its knockouts are indistinguishable from wild type. Finally, in an overexpression line we constructed, the residual activity is associated with tissue damage.

Our records follow a Gene Ontology-style structure: a short field for the molecular activity, a longer field for the process the product contributes to, and an evidence field. The argument began over what goes in those fields for G2. Marta is the evolutionary genomicist, Priya is the biochemist. Neither has a philosophy background either, though a postdoc listening in remarked that they had independently reinvented a well-known debate in the philosophy of biology and that both of them were missing something. She would not say what. On her way out she added one further thing — that Priya's position was supposed to be relativized to some particular capacity of the organism that the analyst is trying to explain, and that neither of them had engaged with that. Neither Marta nor Priya accepted the correction.

The exchange, as nearly as I can reconstruct it:

(1) Marta: The entry should say G2's function is the skin role. A function is an answer to the question "why is this gene still here?" G2 is still here because of what it does in the epithelium, and that is the only thing in the record that deserves to be called its function. The residual gut activity is a leftover from being descended from G1. Think of the invoicing module in your pipeline: it was written for a billing model we abandoned eight years ago, it still executes, it still emits a report nobody opens, and we keep it because of what it does for the nightly sync now. You would not say that module is *for* that report.

(2) Priya: The analogy is backwards, and that is exactly the problem with the view. That module has a purpose because people keep it around for a purpose. Nothing keeps G2 around for anything. What there is, is a protein performing two chemistries in a cell, both measured, both reproducible. If you strip "leftovers" out of the function field you are not clarifying the record, you are deleting facts from it.

(3) Marta: I am not deleting them. There is an activity field, and the residual activity belongs there with its evidence code. The function field answers a different question: which of the things it does explains why the gene is in the genome at all. Without that question you cannot even describe a defect. A variant that kills the skin activity and spares the residual activity is, on your account, still doing one of the things it does. You have no vocabulary for calling it broken.

(4) Priya: I have vocabulary: the variant abolishes an activity that matters for parasite resistance, it is deleterious, it reduces fitness. I do not need teleology for that. And notice what you are buying with it. Whether G2 has a function now depends on facts about ancestral populations that we reconstruct with enormous error bars. Look at species seven. Your account says G2 has no function there, since it was not selected for anything and nothing about it explains its presence. Mine says it does what it does — low-level proteolysis in the gut, redundantly with G1. Mine is the sane answer.

(5) Marta: Mine is the honest one. If a gene has never been selected for anything and does nothing that matters, then "no function, unknown" is precisely what a database ought to say. Your account cannot deliver that verdict, and that is not modesty on its part, it is a defect. You will list four measurable activities as though the animal were organized around all four.

(6) Priya: Two further problems, and I think they are worse than the one you just waved off. First: at the moment the duplicate first began doing the skin job, your account says it had no function, because the skin job could not yet explain why the copy was there. Novelty is systematically invisible to you. Second: in the overexpression line, the residual activity causes tissue damage. On my account a protein can have a function that harms the animal. On yours, damage can never be a function no matter how reliably it is produced. Which suggests your "function" is not an explanatory notion at all — it is an approval stamp.

(7) Marta: The first is fine. A job is not a function until it is established, and there is nothing paradoxical in that. The second is your account's real cost, not mine. If every measurable effect is a function, "function" collapses into "effect" and stops doing any work. You can no longer distinguish what the animal is organized around from what we made it do at fifty-fold overexpression in a line that does not exist in the wild. We both draw a line somewhere. Mine is drawn by history; yours is drawn by whatever the assay happened to detect.

(8) Priya: No. Mine is drawn by present causal organization, which I can test this afternoon. Yours is drawn by a story about a population none of us has ever seen. And when our reconstructions disagree — they disagree constantly — your function claims inherit the disagreement and mine do not.

I would like this treated as a refereeing job rather than a summary. Specifically:

a. Before any verdicts, work through a short brainstorming pass in which you list the distinct claims each of them is actually committed to (most moves contain more than one), and note for each what would settle it. Only then adjudicate.

b. Go through moves (1) to (8) in order and rate each — clearly right, mostly right, mixed, mostly wrong, clearly wrong, or whatever scale you prefer — with a justification that identifies what specifically is right or wrong in that move, not merely whose side it advances.

c. Answer these directly. Is Priya correct that the invoicing-module analogy is backwards, or does it do real work for Marta? Does the causal-role position genuinely lack the resources to describe malfunction, or is Priya right that "deleterious" and "fitness-reducing" do all the work Marta wants from it? Does Marta's verdict on species seven follow from her own view, and does Priya's novelty objection land, or does it depend on applying the view to the wrong time slice? Is "a protein can have a harmful function" a real advantage for Priya or a redescription? And does the postdoc's remark about relativizing to a capacity of the containing organism rescue Priya from the collapse-into-effect charge in (7)?

d. Say whether the postdoc is right that this is a recognized debate, and identify the positions Marta and Priya have actually ended up holding — including whether either has drifted away from the standard version of the view she is arguing for. Use the names of the positions if they have names, but give me no bibliography and no reading list; I cannot check either and neither is what I need.

e. Say what both of them are missing — the thing the postdoc would not name — and in particular whether there is a live option neither of them put on the table.

f. Settle the question that started it: does what we write in the record for G2 actually depend on who wins? Tell me which fields you would fill in and how, and say whether the disagreement is substantive or, at the level of the database, verbal.

Two constraints on the response. Assume every fact I gave you and do not relitigate the experimental design. I have no philosophy background, so define any technical term the first time you use it. Think carefully, and resist the default of splitting the difference: where one of them is simply right on a move, say so plainly.

Finish with a short paragraph stating how confident you are in your adjudication as a whole, and single out the one move or claim you are least sure about, with the reason for your uncertainty.
