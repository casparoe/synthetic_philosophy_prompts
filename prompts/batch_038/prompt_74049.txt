I'm writing a novel that's basically a ship's log from a universe that keeps getting emptier — cosmological horror, long story — and I've been running the physics past a friend of mine, Ruth, who does philosophy of space and time. She tells me which claims my narrator is allowed to make. Her verdicts so far:

- Narrator shut in a windowless cabin that's spinning: she feels herself pressed into the floor, the water in her cup climbs the sides. Ruth says fine, write it, that's genuine rotation and she can detect it without looking at anything.
- Narrator wonders whether it's her turning or the stars wheeling around her: Ruth says keep that as a live question in the text, since no experiment the narrator can run settles it — but she wouldn't go so far as to say the two situations are physically the same, and she made me not resolve it.
- Narrator in a universe with nothing else in it at all, just her and the ship, spinning: Ruth says she still feels it, the cup still climbs, and I can state that flat out.
- Same empty universe, ship not spinning: Ruth says she feels nothing, and there's no sense in which she's moving.
- Two rocks joined by a cable in an otherwise empty universe: Ruth says the cable is taut and there's a fact about which rock goes round the other. She liked this one, said it's an old example.
- But when my narrator said she could feel herself turning relative to space itself, Ruth made me cut the line — said the phrase isn't doing any work and the narrator has no standing to claim it.

The last chapter, which she hasn't seen yet: everything outside is gone except one enormous, very distant shell of dust thats slowly rotating on its own, and the ship isn't rotating at all. Does my narrator feel something, or nothing? I want to guess Ruth's verdict before I send it so I know whether to rewrite. Which accounts of rotation survive everything she's said above, what does each of them say happens in the shell chapter, which one do you think is actually hers, and what's the next case I could put to her that would tell them apart — would bulking up the shell or hauling it closer change the answer, and does that depend on which account she's running? Also tell me if any two of her verdicts are pulling against each other, because I suspect one pair is and I don't know which.
