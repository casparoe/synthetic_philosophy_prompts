I ran an ocean chemistry lab for thirty-odd years and retired two years back. Since then Ive been reading epistemology for fun, so I dont have the vocabulary yet — bear with me.

In my working life I sat on a couple of expert panels run as a Delphi exercise, and the mechanics always struck me as odd. A panel of specialists who never meet and stay anonymous to one another; everyone answers a questionnaire; a facilitator feeds back the group median and the spread, plus a few anonymous reasons; everybody revises; you run round after round until the answers stop moving; anyone sitting out in the tail gets asked to justify themselves. The deliverable is the converged position, and that is what gets cited downstream.

The bit that puzzles me: that design seems to assume something quite specific about disagreement. Namely that when equally informed experts still differ, somebody is simply wrong, and the centre of the group is your best estimate of who. In the lab I was trained to treat two people agreeing as a reason to go looking for a shared error, not as evidence. And the whole point of averaging crowds is that the errors are independent — this procedure systematically destroys that independance in order to produce convergence.

So reconstruct what Delphi actually presupposes about disagreement, evidence and expertise. Which rival accounts does it quietly rule out, and is it strictly committed to them or does it merely fit them? Which features are load-bearing and which are just convenient? Is the design internally coherent, or do the pieces — anonymity, controlled feedback, reporting the median, making outliers explain themselves, consensus as the output — presuppose inconsistent things about one another? Where is it philosophically underdetermined, so several different theories would license the same procedure? And if you swapped in the opposite view, that sustained disagreement among the well-informed is itself information rather than noise to be squeezed out, what would the method have to become?

Prose first, then a structured summary as a JSON object with two keys. "features": an array of objects, each with feature (the design element), presupposes (the view of disagreement or knowledge it only makes sense under), rules_out (the rival view it makes unavailable), status (one of strictly_required, merely_compatible, underdetermined, conflicts_with_another_feature), conflicts_with (the clashing feature, else null), revised_practice (what you would do instead under the disagreement-is-information view). And "overall": an object with coherent (yes, partly, or no), biggest_problem (one sentence), and what_it_really_tracks (one sentence on what the output is evidence of, if anything).
