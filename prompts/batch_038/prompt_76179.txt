Retired physical chemist, 71, reading philosophy seriously for the first time. Theres a pattern I keep hitting and I don't have a word for it.

Back in the day we'd optimise a protein for stability. All the data was there — every coordinate we could measure, nothing hidden. But which designs came out better depended entirely on how you described the thing. Score by residue-residue contacts, one ranking; score by depth of the free-energy basin, a different ranking of the exact same candidates. No further measurement could break the tie, because the two descriptions agree on everything observable — they just carve the space differently, and the target only becomes well defined once you've picked one. Same shape with phase transitions: whether the transition "happened" depends on which order parameter you chose. And I see it in the AI papers my nephew sends me — you point a system at some chunk of its own world-model, and if that model gets swapped out the thing its aiming at silently changes.

Does this have a name? I know about underdetermination by data, but here the data isn't what's failing, the reference is. What's the closest established concept, how does mine differ from it, and if nothing fits, coin something and give me its edges — does a merely vague predicate count, or a choice between descriptions that actually changes predictions?
