I'm a novelist working on a collection and there's one story I can't quite crack. The setup: a restaurant critic who's slowly losing her sense of taste — nobody knows, not the paper, not her husband — and her assistant, a quietly brilliant cook who tastes everything at her desk and tells her what to write. The reviews are his, the byline and the fame are hers. I want it to be a story about recognition: she depends on him utterly but can't afford to admit it, because the moment she does she stops being the one whos recognised, and he can't really claim it either after years of seeing his own palate through her eyes.

So — write the thing, about 1,500 words, in British English. Ground rules: nobody says 'master' or 'recognition' or name-checks any philosopher, there's no dialogue where they explain the arrangement to each other, and the dependence has to come out of the work itself: the tasting, the note-taking, the way his judgement gets sharper and more confident as hers goes. Put a scene about a third of the way in where she gets something wrong in print — a wine, a technique, whatever — and he has to decide whether to quietly fix it or let it stand. And an ending where the balance shifts but neither of them comes out free: no tidy reversal where he storms off and becomes a celebrated chef in his own right, and no sentimental reconciliation either.

Pick whichever point of view gets us inside the trap best, and add one sentence at the end telling me which you chose and why.
