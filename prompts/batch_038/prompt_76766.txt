Backend engineer here, no philosophy training, but I've been chewing on superrationality — the idea that if all the players are reasoning the same way, they'll all land on the same move, so you should act as if your choice determines everyone elses. Every telling I've read assumes the agents are exact copies. Mine never are: I run a fleet of services off the same codebase and the same prompt, with different seeds and different traffic, and they agree maybe nine times out of ten.

So: write me a short story, say 1500–2500 words, about a group of near-identical-but-not-identical agents facing a commons problem where that gap is the whole point. Somebody in the story is relying on the "we all think alike, so I'll do the cooperative thing and they'll match me" logic, and it has to matter concretely that the correlation is partial rather than perfect — I want the reader to feel the difference between 100% and 90%, not get a paragraph of exposition explaining it. Ideally the plot turns on someone noticing the correlation has been quietly drifting, and on what they decide to do once matching the others stops being a safe bet.

Don't set it in a lab with people in chairs pushing buttons, and please don't end with a lecture. I want the concept to be load-bearing in the actual events.
