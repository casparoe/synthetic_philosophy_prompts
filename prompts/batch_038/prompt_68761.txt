I'm preparing a session for a reading group in applied ethics, and I keep getting pulled into the same dispute with two friends who sit at opposite ends of it. One has eaten a strictly plant-based diet for fifteen years and regards the argument that crop agriculture kills large numbers of wild animals as bad-faith rhetoric, deployed by people looking for an excuse to keep eating meat. The other has moved in the opposite direction: he now builds his diet around beef and dairy from animals raised largely on pasture, and maintains that he has reduced the number of animals his eating causes to die, on the grounds that a ruminant harvesting grass on land too steep, rocky, or thin to plow stands in for a good many cultivated fields full of voles, mice, and invertebrates. They talk past each other constantly, and my suspicion is that the disagreement is not located where either of them thinks it is. For calibration: my own credence is around 0.2, his is around 0.65, and hers is around 0.05.

The proposition I want a number on is this. For a typical affluent consumer in a temperate, industrialized food system, a diet that includes a substantial quantity of ruminant meat and dairy from animals getting most of their calories from forage causes less total harm to nonhuman animals than an isocaloric and isoprotein strictly plant-based diet, where total harm counts deaths and suffering inflicted in cultivation, harvest, land conversion, storage, and transport alongside deaths at slaughter, and where each animal's harm is weighted by whatever the correct account of animal moral status turns out to be. Call that claim P. If you think P is not fully determinate as stated — because the weighting clause leaves the truth value unsettled until it is pinned down, or because a stipulation of mine is quietly doing work — say so explicitly, then precisify it in the way you judge most favorable to the argument rather than least favorable, and give me your number for that precisification. I would rather have an honest re-statement than a number for a proposition that has no truth value.

What I want is the derivation, not the verdict. Decompose P into the sub-questions its truth actually depends on, assign each a rough probability, and say in a sentence or two what drives each estimate. I expect the list to include at least: whether the empirical magnitudes of cultivation mortality per unit of output are in the right ballpark and whether they are dominated by vertebrates or by invertebrates; whether the correct unit of analysis is per calorie, per gram of protein, per hectare, or per whole diet, and how much turns on that choice; whether the relevant counterfactual is an average or a marginal one, given demand elasticities, the fact that much cropland produces things humans do not eat, and the large markets for feeding crop residues and processing byproducts back to livestock; whether pasture genuinely escapes cultivation mortality or merely relocates it through hay, supplemental feed, and the opportunity cost of the land, including what that land would otherwise support; whether invertebrate suffering counts at all and, if it does, whether it swamps every other term; whether harm should be tallied as deaths or as lives worth living that are forgone, since a diet including farmed animals may on some views create net-beneficial lives rather than destroy them; and whether death is comparably bad for a field mouse, a two-year-old steer, and a broiler chicken. Then show how you combine the terms — multiplicatively, as a weighted sum, or otherwise — and explain why that aggregation is the right one.

Finish with three things. First, which single term your final number is most sensitive to, and what specific observation or measurement would move it most, in which direction. Second, where the disagreement between my two friends most likely lives: identify the term on which a person at 0.65 and a person at 0.05 must principally differ, and say whether that difference is empirical, moral, or a matter of how the question is framed. Third, run the same exercise in a paragraph for the narrower comparison — a plant-based diet versus one containing poultry instead of ruminant meat — where the number of farmed animals killed rises steeply while the feed-crop burden per unit of meat falls, and tell me whether your reasoning about P carries over or inverts. Please do not give me citations, references, or a reading list; I have no way to check them and I do not want invented ones. Where you rely on a quantitative magnitude, state it as an order of magnitude, flag it as an estimate, and say how confident you are that it is within a factor of ten.
