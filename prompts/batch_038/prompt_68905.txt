Two people in my reading group have been going at it for a year about what a rational agent should do when it's threatened by something it can't causally reach — the setup where some distant agent has already, irreversably, locked in a threat against you, so the only live question left is whether you fold.

The Policy camp says what matters is never the instance but what sort of agent you are: folding even once, even when the threat is already made and refusing buys you nothing, is what turns you into the kind of target threats work on, and an agent that's foldable has already lost. Especially in the acausal case, since the threatener never meets you and only ever sees what you do, so your behaviour *is* your disposition as far as they're concerned.

The Surrogate camp says "never fold" is a slogan that doesnt do any work the moment the threatener has genuinely precommitted, because then refusing has zero deterrent value and just costs you something you care about for no reason. Their move is a third option both sides keep skipping over: rather than comply with what's demanded or refuse outright, offer a surrogate goal — take on yourself some new standing aim that the threatener would accept in place of the demanded action, one that costs you less and that you keep pursuing after the threat is spent, on your own terms and for your own reasons. Coercion stops being a compliance decision and becomes a negotiation about substitutes.

Write the joint statement they should have produced. I want:

- a numbered section of claims both can sign — about what the setup actually is, about what the threatener can and can't know and when it's too late for it to change its mind, and about what each side is really trying to protect — with nothing smuggled in that only one camp would accept;
- then a disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated twice, once in language the Policy camp would sign as a fair description of its own view and once in language the Surrogate camp would, plus a column marking which of them are genuine and which turn out to be word-choice;
- the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling the thing would actually move at least one side. I'd bet one is whether a goal you adopt voluntarily while under threat can still be a goal of yours rather than a compliance with a nicer label, and another is whether advertising that you're negotiable is itself a fresh threat surface that makes more threats worth issuing. Please include the ones I haven't thought of;
- for each crux, one concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance — I'd want coverage of the threatener who can see your action but not your reasoning, two threats landing at once, and the version where the surrogate goal is the one the threatener picked for you;
- and, for each camp, what it would accept as a reason to give up its own view.

Don't send me sources or a reading list, I just want the document itself.
