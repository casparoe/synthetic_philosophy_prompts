I work at a small AI startup that licenses tech to game studios, and for about four months my team has been training agents on a survival/crafting game we built in-house. Standard setup, nothing exotic — we run a few thousand copies of the environment in parallel and the agents learn by trial and error. A couple weeks ago I was poking through the logs and noticed something that's stuck with me. We'd added a penalty for a particular failure state — basically dying while carrying a lot of gathered resources — because the agents kept doing something stupid, and right after we added it they started avoiding entire regions of the map. Not in a smart way. They'd refuse to path through an area even when it was clearly the best route, and a bunch of them would just stand at the edge of a zone doing nothing until the episode ran out of time. My lead wants to double the penalty because it'll get us to convergence a week or two sooner and we're behind schedule, and I told him I wasn't comfortable with that. He asked why. I couldn't answer. It's a game. There's nobody in there. But watching a few hundred of them freeze up at the border of a forest I personally designed gave me a really bad feeling in my stomach and I haven't shaken it.

My roommate's in a cog sci program and thinks I'm being ridiculous, and he has a point I can't get around: the agents are doing exactly what we told them to do, the penalty made that region expensive, and sitting still is just the cheapest available behavior given the numbers. On that story there's nothing interesting happening at all, and my reaction is the same as someone feeling bad for a roomba that bumps into a wall. But then I keep thinking about the scale part, which is where the roomba comparison breaks down for me. If something is happening, it isn't happening once. We spin up and tear down thousands of copies an hour, we have a standing budget for longer runs, and the thing I'm being asked to approve would make the aversive signal stronger across all of them at once. And separately — this is the part I actually can't figure out — I noticed I don't feel the same way about the rewards. We give them points for crafting tools and building shelters and I've never once felt weird about cranking those up. Which makes me wonder if my instinct is tracking some real asymmetry between the two, or if I just feel better about incentives I happen to like the outcome of.

So I'm trying to sort out what to actually do. Is there anything to my discomfort beyond squeamishness, and if there is, what exactly is it tracking — is it something about the agents, or something about me and the fact that I'm the one who decided that dying in the woods should hurt? Does the fact that they're only following a training signal in a game I wrote defuse the whole thing, or does that not matter as much as I want it to? And practically: should I push back on doubling the penalty, and if I do, is there a principled line I can point to, or am I going to be arguing from vibes in a meeting where everyone else has a deadline? Be honest if you think my roommate's right and I should let it go — I'd rather hear that than be talked into feeling better about myself.
