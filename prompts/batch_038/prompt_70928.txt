I chair the working group that allocates a shared, congestible resource — GPU hours, storage, egress — across our product divisions. Right now a central scheduler assigns capacity by request queue, so every division over-asks and none of them feel the scarcity. Two advisers have been seconded to the group, and I want the argument between them written out for the file.

Write a dialogue between them in exactly five numbered exchanges — each item one turn for each adviser, two or three sentences per turn, with no introduction, scene-setting, or closing note. The first defends a Lange-style solution: the scheduler acts as an auctioneer, announces shadow prices per unit of each capacity type, revises them against observed excess demand, and tells divisional managers to treat the announced price as a parameter and pick the cost-minimizing bundle at it, a trial-and-error process that, run on a fast enough cycle, should settle on prices that clear. The second takes the Hayek and Mises side: the relevant data is dispersed, local, and largely tacit — what a team could ship with more capacity, what it can quietly do without, how an announced price would change its behavior — and no vector of administered prices carries that information, however often it is revised.

Both advisers should be at full strength, and they should press on the two places where the textbook telling of this debate breaks down in our setting. The market socialist must answer that an iterative price-adjustment rule need not converge even when every agent behaves impeccably, because aggregate excess demand does not inherit the well-behavedness of individual demand: the process can circle or oscillate instead of settling. She should say what would have to be true of the excess demands for the adjustment to converge, and whether it is plausibly true of GPU hours, storage, and egress. The Hayekian must answer that in this firm the local knowledge is already logged, timestamped, and queryable, so the tacit-knowledge objection may not do the work it does across a whole economy: he should say what survives of the objection when the knowledge is in fact recorded, and where it still bites even then. By the fifth exchange each should have conceded something the other did not expect.
