For a research paper in my high school history class, I'm trying to pin down what the question "was the adoption of agriculture a mistake?" is actually asking, and I've found that I can't even state it precisely without settling several things that don't look like history at all. Before I get to any evidence, I'm not sure who the mistake would be a mistake *for*, since no one appears to have chosen it and the change took dozens of generations. Beyond that, three snags in particular:

(1) Farming supported many more people at what was arguably a lower quality of life, so whether the change was an improvement seems to depend on whether I compare the wellbeing of the average individual or the total amount of wellbeing — and I don't see how historical evidence could choose between those.

(2) The claim that foragers got by on a few hours of food-getting a day comes from timing people whose lives don't appear to carve off "work" as a separate activity at all, so I can't tell whether the measure is tracking something real or partly being produced by the way it was collected.

(3) The forager groups anthropologists actually observed were often living in deserts and arctic regions that farmers had passed over, which makes me unsure they're a fair stand-in for the people who first took up cultivation.

Could you explain what each of these snags amounts to conceptually, and say which of them — if any — could be resolved by better evidence rather than by argument?
