So I'm one of nine members on the Pretrial Tools Review Commission in Larkin County, Marrow State (both fictional — pretend the state statute just says detention decisions have to be "race-neutral in form and effect," which nobody on the commission can agree on what that means). Our risk-assessment contract is up at the end of the fiscal year and the vote is at the June meeting. I've been asked to circulate the memo beforehand and honestly I want to see how someone who isn't in the weeds of our vendor's marketing would frame it.

Here's the situation as best I can state it.

Current tool, from Northlight. Their compliance binder claims two things and their auditor confirms both on last year's local data: within any narrow score band, the fraction of defendants who got a new charge within 18 months is about the same as the band's score says, and that holds within each racial group separately too. And the average score among people who did reoffend is about equal to the average score among people who didn't, again overall and within group. What it doesn't have: our own reaudit found that among Black defendants who did NOT reoffend, 41% were flagged high risk, versus 27% of white defendants who didn't reoffend. Among those who DID reoffend the numbers are much closer, 63% versus 58%. Eighteen-month reoffense base rates were 0.29 and 0.19.

Option B is a new vendor, Meridian. Their pitch is that they hit calibration and equal false-positive and false-negative rates all at once, and they can do it because they train two models and use different cutoffs per group, which means the same score means different things depending on which group you're in. Their demo was on a state dataset, not ours, and when our analyst asked for the confusion matrices behind the demo they said that was proprietary. County attorney hasn't told us whether the separate cutoffs survive the statute — she says it depends on how the commission reads "in effect," and she's not going to guess for us.

Option C is the coalition's ask: keep Northlight but lower the high-risk cutoff for everyone until the detention rate gap shrinks to under 3 points, report quarterly.

Option D is from one of my colleagues and I kind of like it: pick no metric as the target at all. Keep the current tool, but the commission publishes the whole error breakdown by group every quarter, the tradeoff gets argued out in public each year at renewal, and judges get a one-page explanation of why no scoring rule can hold all of these properties at once given differing base rates. His argument is that a commission that picks a number is really just hiding a value choice inside a spreadsheet.

Can you draft the memo I'd circulate? What I need:

1. A recommendation on exactly one option, with the steps and dates between June and the contract deadline.
2. A ranking of these five — calibration overall, calibration within group, equal false-positive rates, equal false-negative rates, statistical parity — by how much each one should actually constrain a decision about locking someone up before trial. Tiers are fine, just put them in order and say why each lands where it does. I want the ordering argued from what a detention decision is for, not from which properties are mathematically compatible — otherwise you'll end up with a tie.
3. Meridian says the whole impossibility thing is an artifact of picking the wrong metric bundle, and my colleague says the base rate difference is doing all the work. Take the strongest version of whichever of those you think is right and knock it down, and be honest about what survives.
4. Say what we'd have to go collect before the June vote to make this defensible — our auditor's work is the only local evidence anyone has.

Don't worry about telling me what Marrow State law requires, you can't know and neither can we, just flag anywhere the recommendation would break if the attorney reads "in effect" strictly. And give me something the losing side in this vote could actually respond to in the meeting rather than a document that pretends the commission is unanimous.
