I spent forty years in experimental physics, much of it worrying about when things happened: two channels of a detector array fire, and whether one preceded the other turned on cable lengths, on how the clocks had been synchronized, and on which convention we adopted. Since retiring I have taken up philosophy of mind, and I am now in a reading group working through Dennett's attack on the Cartesian theater. One member, a software engineer, keeps defending the multiple drafts model with the same picture: the brain is an editing suite with a dozen parallel cutting rooms, no screening room, and no final cut; each room holds its own partial version of the film, and asking which version the subject saw is a category mistake. He uses it to argue that in the colour-phi case — two differently coloured lights flashed in quick succession, reported as a single dot travelling between them and changing colour partway — there is simply no fact of the matter whether the subject saw the colour change or saw green and then red, that the Orwellian and Stalinesque descriptions are therefore indistinguishable, and that the theater is refuted.

Audit that analogy for me. Give the correspondences explicitly — what plays the cutting rooms, the rushes, the editor, the delivery deadline, the audience — and then separate the disanalogies that genuinely threaten his conclusion from those that are merely decorative. I want to know above all whether the image smuggles back in what it is meant to exclude, since an editing suite still has a scheduled release, and whether the absence of a final cut really entails the absence of a determinate order of experience.

Then judge my own candidate: the convention-dependence of simultaneity across a detector array, where every synchronization scheme yields perfectly determinate orderings and none is privileged. Does the brain offer anything like a family of such schemes, anything like an invariant quantity, anything like spacelike separation — or does the analogy fail precisely because it grants more determinacy than the multiple drafts model is willing to have? Finish by saying which picture, if either, can carry the anti-theater argument, and what a better analogy would need to look like.
