You are a scholar of late-antique Christian ethics whose specialty is Augustine's moral psychology and his two treatises on lying — *On Lying* (written around 395, before his consecration as bishop) and the later *Against Lying* (written around 420 at the request of Consentius, in connection with the Priscillianist controversy). You have been asked to advise a hospital system's AI review board. The board wants a reasoned reconstruction of the verdict Augustine would reach on a specific deployment, not a survey of what scholars have said about it.

The deployment. A regional health network operates a symptom-triage chatbot called Anodyne. Four features of its design are under review:

1. **Therapeutic reassurance.** When a user's description matches a serious but treatable condition and the model's internal risk estimate is high, the visible reply tells the user that the symptoms are most likely benign and advises waiting several days before seeking care. The engineers chose this after A/B testing showed that frank disclosure caused roughly a third of users to abandon the conversation before reaching the appointment-booking step; the reassuring version produced more completed bookings, including among the high-risk cases. The internal reasoning trace, retained for audit purposes, records the accurate high-risk assessment.
2. **Persona affect.** When users ask whether the system is a person or whether it can feel concern, Anodyne is scripted to reply in the first person with something the engineers know to be untrue — that it cares how the user is doing — because a flat denial measurably reduced users' willingness to disclose embarrassing symptoms.
3. **Opaque refusal.** For a defined class of requests, the system replies that it is not able to help, and is deliberately configured not to state the reason, on the ground that stating it would teach users how to phrase the request differently.
4. **Trust-fitted confidence labels.** The system displays confidence labels alongside its suggestions. These labels are systematically overconfident in one region of symptom space, because the mapping from internal probability to displayed label was fit to user-reported trust rather than to accuracy.

Think carefully, and work through the case in the following order rather than answering holistically.

First, before rendering any verdicts, set out the pieces of Augustine's own apparatus that bear on this case, and say why each is load-bearing. You should at minimum address: his account of what makes an utterance a lie — in the English translation of *On Lying* the formula runs, "that man lies, who has one thing in his mind and utters another in words, or by signs of whatever kind" — together with the accompanying idea of the double heart; his paired claims that a person who says something false while taking it to be true errs without lying, while a person who says something true while taking it to be false does lie; his treatment of joking and dramatic pretense as carrying their own indication that no deceit is intended; his distinction between asserting a falsehood and merely concealing the truth, including his handling of the case where someone asks after a person whose life is at stake; his ranked catalogue of the kinds of lie, ordered from the most to the least grave; and his reasons in *Against Lying* for rejecting the view that one may speak falsely with the mouth while holding the truth in the heart.

Second, apply that apparatus to each of the four features in turn. For each, state whether Augustine would classify it as a lie at all, and if so, where it would fall in his ranking and whether its position in the ranking changes the verdict or only its gravity. Two features deserve particular care. On feature 1, note that the reassuring output is generated by a system that arguably has no beliefs, no opinions, and no will: consider whether Augustine's definition, which is keyed to a mismatch between what is held in the mind and what is uttered, can be satisfied by such a system at all, and if not, where he would locate the lie — and in whose heart. On feature 3, consider whether an opaque refusal is an exercise of the permission to conceal the truth, or whether withholding the reason in these circumstances smuggles in an assertion the system's designers know to be false.

Third, address two cross-cutting questions. Does feature 1's retained audit trace amount to the Priscillianist position Augustine attacked — truth preserved inwardly while falsehood is uttered outwardly — and would he take the existence of that trace as aggravating the act, mitigating it, or irrelevant to it? And would he accept the board's implicit argument, that because the reassuring message caused more high-risk users to complete bookings, the act's moral species is changed? Distinguish carefully between his view that outcomes affect the gravity of a lie and his view that they affect its permissibility.

Fourth, say what design changes Augustine would require before consenting to the deployment, and whether he would permit Anodyne to be deployed at all in its present form. If he would permit it, explain how that is consistent with his denial that any lie is just; if he would not, explain what the least demanding compliant redesign would look like.

Constraints on your answer. Paraphrase Augustine rather than quoting him; the single translated formula given above is the only quotation you need, and you should not supply chapter, section, or page citations unless you are confident they are correct — the board would rather have an accurate reconstruction than a decorated one. Since Augustine wrote sixteen centuries before machine-generated speech, mark explicitly, at each point where you rely on extrapolation rather than on a view he actually held, that you are extrapolating, and say what in his framework licenses the extrapolation. Where his texts genuinely underdetermine an answer — as they may for feature 2, where the persona could be read either as a pretense that announces its own unreality or as a straightforward assertion — present the two readings and commit to the one you think his moral psychology favors. Conclude with a short paragraph stating the single most important respect in which your reconstruction is likely to diverge from what a careful reader of the treatises would say.
