Our reading group has just finished a term on the metaphysics of mind, and the last three sessions were given over to constitutive panpsychism: the argument that physics tells us only about the relational, dispositional properties of matter and is therefore silent on its intrinsic nature, and that the one intrinsic nature we know anything about from the inside is experience, so experience may as well be what fills that role all the way down. The final session was meant to be the combination problem, and it went about as well as you would expect. Half the group came away thinking the objection decisive — that a universe furnished entirely with little experients somehow fails to deliver us, and that positing some bonding relation to do the fusing is a label, not an answer. The other half thought this begged the question by demanding of a fundamental relation exactly what no fundamental relation could supply. We never resolved it, and I would rather not resolve it artificially on the last night of term. I have been asked to close the evening by reading something aloud, and I want a poem that states the impasse with the seriousness both sides think it deserves: neither a refutation nor a shrug, and above all not a caricature of the view, since two of the people in the room are sympathetic to it and would spot the straw man immediately.

The poem should be a sestina — six stanzas of six lines and a three-line envoi, thirty-nine lines in all, which is roughly how long I have — and the form is doing the argument, so I want the constraints observed strictly. Choose six end-words and no more: short, concrete, sensory words that could plausibly stand for the simplest sort of felt quality, the kind of thing a single micro-experience might amount to (a hum, an ache, a glint, a pressure — nouns, so that they can bear thirty-nine repetitions without becoming ungrammatical or merely decorative). Nowhere in the poem may a seventh end-word appear, and each of the six stanzas must contain all six of them exactly once, in the standard rotating order. That prohibition is the whole point. The stanzas should climb the levels of organisation the view needs — something like an electron's flicker, an atom, a neuron, a small company of neurons, a region, and finally the person who is speaking — and each new whole must be built out of nothing but the same six words, re-ordered and re-packed, never able to generate a novel quality of its own out of the palette it was given. The poem should let the reader feel how absurd that is at the level of a human life, and how hard it is to say why.

Two further things I would like built into it. First, at least one stanza should flirt with the escape route — the suggestion that there is some relation between the little ones, a binding or a togetherness, which does the fusing — and that stanza should be unable to say what the relation feels like from the inside, so that the reader notices the gap rather than being told about it. Second, I want the old subject-summing objection to land in the envoi rather than being argued anywhere: the thought that you may shuffle a hundred feelings together as tightly as you please and no hundred-and-first feeling is thereby born. The three closing lines should be the attempt to gather the many into one voice, and the very last line should place two of the end-words side by side, each perfectly intact, with nothing at all over and above them. Please keep the technical vocabulary out of the poem entirely — none of the usual words for the view, the qualities, the problem, or the little experiencers themselves, and no naming of philosophers; the ideas must be rendered, not cited, and someone who has never heard of the debate should still find it a good poem. Begin your reply with the poem itself, with no title, no preface, no gloss and no explanation of how the form maps onto the argument, since we intend to have that argument over wine. If you have had to bend the form anywhere — an end-word that would not sit at a line-end, a stanza order you cheated — add one line after the poem saying where, and then stop.
