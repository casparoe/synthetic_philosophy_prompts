Here's the practice I want pulled apart. Take the DSM (or ICD-11, same shape). Four things it does:

1. Candidate disorders get parked in an appendix flagged as needing further research, with the proposed criteria written out in full in the manual but no official diagnostic status. Internet gaming disorder, attenuated psychosis syndrome, non-suicidal self-injury, that sort of thing.

2. Before a category gets promoted into the main text, work groups run field trials: two clinicians independently assess the same patient and the agreement gets scored statistically. Agreement that comes out low means the criteria get rewritten or the category gets dropped.

3. Almost every criterion set ends with a clause requiring that the disturbance cause clinically significant distress or impairment, plus a clause ruling out reactions that are expected or culturally sanctioned, e.g. after the death of someone close.

4. The resulting label then works as a gate. No diagnosis, no insurance reimbursement, no school accommodations, no disability determination.

My friend's line is that (1) is incoherent. On Hacking's picture the whole point of a category like this is that naming it changes the people it would name — people start experiencing and reporting themselves in its terms, clinicians start looking for it, prevalence jumps. So publishing detailed criteria in an appendix isn't a neutral holding pattern, it's already doing the thing the quarantine is supposd to prevent. And if that's right, then (2) is measuring something that moves while you measure it, and (3) pretends there's a pre-diagnostic fact about harm sitting there waiting to be found.
