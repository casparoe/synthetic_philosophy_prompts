I do a science-and-ideas column and I've spent the last month sitting in on demos at a startup — call them something generic, they make a general-purpose household robot arm on a little tracked base. Every interview loops back to the frame problem, and what I like about it is that it isn't a horsepower problem. Their CTO has a slide literally titled THE RELEVANCE FILTER and the slide is almost completely empty, which I took as either profound or a tell.

Here's what I want from you: a joke. An actual joke, setup and punchline, not a wry paragraph about robots.

Constraints, because I've read to many AI-frame-problem gags that are really just "computers are dumb" with extra steps:

- The punchline has to turn on the actual content of the problem. Somebody who knows the McCarthy-and-Hayes 1969 lineage should get a litle jolt of recognition, not conclude you've heard the phrase once on a podcast.
- More points if it works on the deeper version — that the trouble isn't the robot burning through cycles while it deduces every consequence of an action (that's roughly the old bomb-and-wagon thought experiment Dennett used to tell), it's that nothing about the world comes labelled as relevant, so figuring out which facts stay put isn't a computing-budget question at all.
- Put it in a scene. Demo day in front of a VC is fine; so is a job interview for a robot, a support ticket, a warranty claim, a family argument about whose turn it was to unload the dishwasher. I want somewhere concrete, not an aphorism.
- Hard rule: the punchline must not still work if I swap "frame problem" for "AI" and change nothing else.

Then, under the joke, give me maybe five lines of notes. Say which version of the frame problem the punchline is built on, and where the joke takes liberties — every gag about this flattens something, and I'd rather you tell me what you flattened than have me find it later. Make the last line the honest one: what would have made you write a different joke entirely, i.e. which part of my framing above you think is wrong, or what consideration would flip your take on which reading of the problem is actually the joke-worthy one. I'd rather have that than a punchline I have to take on faith.
