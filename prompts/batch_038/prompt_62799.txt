Every time space settlement comes up at family dinners my uncle runs the same argument: Earth today is like a small company keeping all its data on one server with no offsite copy. One fire, one flood, one ransomware hit and the whole thing is gone, and the fix is obviosly boring — you make a copy and you put it somewhere else. He says people who call Mars colonies a waste of money are basically arguing against backups, which he thinks tells you everything about whose intuitions are being funded.

I half agree with him and half dont, and I can't sort out which half. Can you take the analogy apart properly for me? What plays the server, the copy, the fire, the restore, the IT budget — where does the mapping genuinely hold, and what features of backing up data does it sneak into the colonization case that a settlement just doesn't have? I'm especially curious whether the copy is a copy at all, and about whether holding the copy changes how you treat the original in a way a spare hard drive never would. Then: does his conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, is a colony plausibly a net risk-reducer for civilization, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument? And if backup is the wrong picture, what analogy would be a better one?
