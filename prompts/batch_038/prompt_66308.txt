Working on a paper for a seminar on responsibility and historical blame, and I'm stuck on the actual wording of Article 231 of Versailles — everybody calls it the war guilt clause, but it never says guilt. Here it is:

> "The Allied and Associated Governments affirm and Germany accepts the responsibility of Germany and her allies for causing all the loss and damage to which the Allied and Associated Governments and their nationals have been subjected as a consequence of the war imposed upon them by the aggression of Germany and her allies."

And the very next article opens like this:

> "The Allied and Associated Governments recognise that the resources of Germany are not adequate, after taking into account permanent diminutions of such resources which will result from other provisions of the present Treaty, to make complete reparation for all such loss and damage."

Can you help me read these two together? Specifically: (a) what kind of claim is 231 making — causal, moral, legal, or some hybrid — and what is "affirm ... and ... accepts" doing there, given that you cant order anyone to believe something, only to say it? (b) does 231 assert Germany was the sole cause, or could it be true even if Austria, Russia and Serbia each did a lot of the causing? (c) what work is "all" doing in 231 if 232 immediately concedes Germany can't pay for all of it? (d) does any of this need a theory of states as agents, and if so what kind?

The bit I really can't work out: if the 1960s Fischer case is right — that the German leadership wanted the war and went into it with expansive aims — does that make 231 true? Or is 231 making a claim about who caused whose losses that the war aims debate never actually touches? And if the war aims evidence had collapsed instead, would 231 be false?
