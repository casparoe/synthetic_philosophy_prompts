I'm running a philosophy-of-action reading group that's about half psych/econ people, and I want to open next term with a "what do you actually believe about weakness of will?" quiz — 8 or 9 concrete vignettes (third slice of cake, the 6am alarm, the smoker who resolves to quit again, the grad student who closes the laptop to clean the kitchen instead), each with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions: Davidson's partitioned mind, Frankfurt's second-order volitions, Holton on when you may revise a resolution, Mele's deflationary line where the better judgment itself gets corrupted, hyperbolic discounting and multiple selves, the Socratic denial that strict akrasia ever happens, and the habit/addiction view on which there's no evaluative conflict to explain.

Two things I care about most. First, it should sort people on the axis of whether they think the conflict runs across time (a preference reversal) or across parts of the agent at a single moment — so build in at least one case where nothing is delayed and discounting can't do any work, and one where the agent reports no felt struggle at all. Second, no item may ask whether they believe in akrasia or which label they'd pick; everything has to be answerable by someone who's never read any of these people.

Then tell me which pairs of leaves the instrument can't really seperate, and which cells of the space come out empty and why — incoherent, unattractive, or just unclaimed.
