The student's answer reads as follows.

*Probability kinematics, also called Jeffrey conditioning, governs the case in which experience shifts an agent's degrees of belief across a partition E₁, …, Eₙ of mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive propositions without making any one of them certain. Where P is the agent's prior credence function and P′ her posterior one, the rule is: P′(A) = Σᵢ P(A|Eᵢ)·P′(Eᵢ), for every proposition A in the algebra. The rigidity assumption is the constraint that P′(A|Eᵢ) = P(A|Eᵢ) for every A and every cell Eᵢ; that is, experience changes the weights the agent assigns to the cells of the partition, but leaves intact her conditional credences on each cell. Rigidity is what makes the rule determinate: without it, the new weights alone would underdetermine the posterior, since infinitely many credence functions agree on the P′(Eᵢ) while differing elsewhere.*

*The rule relaxes the assumption that learning always consists in coming to assign probability one to some proposition E and then conditioning on it. On that picture, perceptual experience must be capable of being represented as the delivery of a certain observational proposition, and any residual uncertainty about what one seems to see has to be pushed into the choice of a weaker proposition to condition on instead. Jeffrey's point is that this misdescribes ordinary perception: when I look at a swatch under poor light I may come away more confident that it is green than that it is blue without becoming certain of either, and there need be no proposition of which my experience makes me certain. Conditionalization, which requires P′(E) = 1, has no way of representing such a change, so a more general rule is needed. (The alternative name "Jeffrey conditioning" is therefore somewhat misleading, since on this rule nothing is conditioned on.)*

*For part (c), suppose the shift in the partition is maximal: P′(E₁) = 1 and P′(Eᵢ) = 0 for i > 1. Substituting into the rule gives P′(A) = P(A|E₁)·1 + Σᵢ>₁ P(A|Eᵢ)·0 = P(A|E₁), which is exactly what conditionalization on E₁ prescribes. So ordinary conditioning is the limiting case in which experience resolves the partition completely.*

*For part (d): the most serious difficulty is that the rule is silent about where the new partition weights come from. It tells the agent how the rest of her credal state must rearrange itself once the P′(Eᵢ) are fixed, but fixing them is the entire epistemological work, and nothing in probability kinematics constrains it. The rule is thus a constraint on revision rather than a complete account of learning from experience. A related point in favor of rigidity is that it is not an optional extra. Ramsey's betting argument in "Truth and Probability" shows that an agent who lets her conditional credences drift while absorbing uncertain evidence can be offered a package of bets — some accepted before the change, some after — that guarantees her a net loss regardless of how things turn out, so a violation of rigidity is a species of practical inconsistency rather than a mere stylistic infelicity. One further consequence of rigidity is worth noting: it makes the rule order-independent. An agent who first absorbs uncertain evidence bearing on a partition of colour appearances and then uncertain evidence bearing on a partition of shape appearances arrives at the same credence function as one who absorbs them in the reverse order, since rigidity holds at each stage. Probability kinematics therefore inherits the commutativity that ordinary conditioning displays when two certain pieces of evidence are learned in either order.*
