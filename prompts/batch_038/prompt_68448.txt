ok, my reading group has been going in circles on spectrum arguments for months and I want a proper audit of the analogies both camps keep reaching for, because I suspect the analogies are doing all the work and nobody's checking whether they can carry it.

Camp 1 is my friend, a pretty orthodox consequentialist. She says denying that better-than is transitive is like denying that taller-than is transitive: if you say A is better than B, B better than C, but then C better than A, you're in exactly the same boat as someone who says Ana is taller than Bo, Bo taller than Cy, and Cy taller than Ana. Her point is that nothing interesting has been discovered — you've just misdescribed something, or you haven't noticed one of your own judgements is confused. And she backs it up by saying the intransitivist will get money-pumped: offered a sequence of small trades you'll pay your way all the way round the circle and end up worse off with the option you started with, which she thinks shows the view isn't merely unorthodox but actually self-defeating.

Camp 2 are the people who take the spectrum stuff seriously, and their line is that the whole problem is the sorites in a value-theoretic costume: one grain doesn't make a heap, one extra millisecond of discomfort doesn't make an outcome worse, and if you're willing to bite the bullet about heaps you should be willing here too — so the real question is just where the vagueness lives.

The case they were arguing about is one I made up, roughly:
- X: one hour of a genuinely bad migraine, once, for a single person
- Y: ten minutes of a mild headache each, for a hundred thousand people
- Z: one minute of barely-noticable ear pressure each, for a hundred million people

A good few people in the group report X worse than Y, Y worse than Z, and Z worse than X, and each of those three judgements feels firmer than the general principle that says they can't all hold.

Can you lay out both mappings explicitly — ideally a correspondence table for each: what plays the grains, the heap, the measuring stick, the unit, the person doing the ranking, the moment of doubt — and then separate the disanalogies that actually matter for the job each analogy is being made to do from the ones that are just noise? In particular I want to know whether taller-than really has the features camp 1 needs from it: a single scalar magnitude, a shared unit, everything comparable with everything, sharp boundaries even where we can't detect the difference, and no room for two people to be neither the same height nor one taller than the other. If betterness is multi-dimensional, or if some options are on a par rather than one better or exactly equal, does the height picture have anywhere to put that, and what does it quietly import by not having anywhere to put it? Also: is the money-pump argument actually delivered by the height analogy, or does it run independently of it — and what does it need to assume about trades and about the agent for the pump to bite?

Then tell me whether either camp's conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table — does camp 1 get to say the spectrum cases must contain a hidden error, and does camp 2 get to say this is just vagueness we already know how to live with? And if neither analogy is right, what would a better one be? I keep wanting to reach for colour similarity (this swatch looks closer to that one, without that adding up) or for choosing between flats on three different axes that refuse to collapse into one score, and I can't tell whether those are genuine improvements or just the same problem made vaguer.
