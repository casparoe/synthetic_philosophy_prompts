six months into a novel and I've got a character, Nadia, who's stopped making her own moral calls. she rings a friend of hers — much sharper man, been doing this kind of thinking his whole life — and does whatever he tells her, and he's never steered her wrong, she's right basically everytime.

my agent read the draft and came back with one line: Nadia is a moral plagiarist, she's publishing someone else's judgement under her own name and the reader can smell the fraud, and that's why the character reads flat. another novelist I know, who did philosophy as an undergrad, says the analogy is just a slur — there's no manuscript sitting in the friend's drawer that she could have written herself, a moral judgement isn't a transferable object, so calling her a plagiarist is a way of saying I don't like her without giving a reason.

I have to pick a side before I can fix the character, and possibly before I let someone say the plagiarism thing in a scene, where it needs to actually land on the page. so audit it for me. what maps onto what: the manuscript, the byline, the audience being taken in, the craft the real author had to exercise, the moment of handover. then where the mapping breaks — and which of those breaks actually matter for the charge of fraud, versus being surface differences that leave the charge intact. and if it does hold, what is the defect supposed to be: she doesn't have the reasons, she'd come apart on a case that's slightly different from the one she was told about, or she can't answer for it to the person she hurt? those feel like three different accusations and I suspect my agent is running them together under one word.

bottom line: does 'plagiarist' carry the weight she wants it to, or was the analogy holding up nothing? and if it fails, is there something better out of my own trade — ghostwriter, translator, an actor playing a part, a session musician on somebody else's record — that gets nearer the real complaint people have about moral deference, or is that complaint better stated with no analogy at all?
