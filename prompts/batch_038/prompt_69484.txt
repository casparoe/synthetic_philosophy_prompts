Thirty-five years in a water-quality lab, retired now, and I've joined a philosophy reading group that's been chewing on the idea that people with clashing moral frameworks can sometimes both come out ahead by trading concessions — I compensate you in whatever currency my values care about, you stop doing the thing only your values forbid. Our running case: one member who'd pay a fish farm to drop a practice she thinks is cruel, and another who sees nothing wrong with it but would happily take the money and stop anyway.

I wrote up an argument against the whole project and the ex-economist in the group says its invalid but couldn't tell me where. Numbered so you can point at a step:

1. If a moral trade really benefits a party, that party prefers the deal to no deal — so they can weigh what they give up against what they get.
2. If you can weigh A against B like that, then A and B each have some finite weight on a single scale.
3. Anything with a finite weight on a scale can be outweighed by enough of something else on that scale.
4. So for any concession, there's a compensation big enough to make conceding the right call for that party.
5. So what looked like a constraint — a thing you just mustn't do — turns out to be a price nobody has met yet.
6. The deontologist in our group denies 5, and denies it on principle, not for want of a big enough offer.
7. So either the deontologist can't coherently take part in moral trade, or moral trade only works among people who already thought everything had a price. Either way "gains from trade through compromise" didnt buy what it advertises: it can't manufacture agreement between value systems that didn't already agree about what's negotiable.

I'd rather keep 1 than 5 — 1 looks like plain bookkeeping about preferences, and giving it up seems to mean people can't even rank their own options. Which step do you actually reject, why did it look innocent when I wrote it down, and what does whoever rejects it have to swallow in its place? And if your verdict is that the chain is valid and I just have to pick the cheapest premise, say which one and price it.
