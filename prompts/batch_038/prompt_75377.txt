For states that aren't magnitude-sensitive — believing that p, doubting that p — there's no margin to be had, so the argument never gets started, and those states stay luminous. Restricted KK falls out: for the non-gradable attitudes, if you believe/doubt that p you're in a position to know that you do, and she thinks that's enough to salvage most of what people wanted KK for. Her reflex move against counterexamples is to say the case is one where the state isn't determinately present, so the case isn't in her domain to begin with.

One on the criterion itself: belief and doubt plausibly come in degrees too — credences, half-doubt, doubt you could dial up or down — so is magnitude-sensitivity doing principled work or is the line drawn exactly where she needs it?
