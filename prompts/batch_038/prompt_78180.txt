I am applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship in practical philosophy and need a two-page research proposal, roughly 1,100 words, that turns a half-formed idea into something a selection committee can assess. Please write the proposal itself rather than an outline of one. I will add references afterwards, so you should describe positions, arguments, and lines of objection without citing them; no bibliography, please.

The half-formed idea. Debates over the "hinge of history" hypothesis — the claim that we are living at an unusually pivotal moment, such that what people do now will matter far more than what later agents can do — currently swing between two poles. On one side, an outside-view objection: pivotality has seemed self-evident to many generations; we have no base rate across histories from which to locate our own; and a sense of standing at a turning point is precisely what a technologically accelerating species with no memory of its own future should expect to have. On the other side, inside-view replies: we sit very early in a cosmos that may remain habitable for an immense stretch of time; our capacity to affect the far future is rising steeply; and certain outcomes — extinction, permanent lock-in of values or institutions, a plateau beyond which no further improvement is possible — would foreclose all later agency, so nearness to such boundaries looks like a genuine, non-indexical fact about our moment.

My suspicion is that both poles are arguing about a quantity nobody has defined. "Influence" is being used to cover at least three things that come apart: (i) leverage, how much a marginal unit of effort changes things over the coming decades; (ii) persistence, the rate at which the effects of an intervention decay or are absorbed rather than compounded — whether the world mean-reverts or is path-dependent; and (iii) proximity to absorbing states, boundaries whose crossing removes the possibility of further influence for anyone, ever. Each is also relative to an option set, so the same history might be pivotal for some kinds of action and not for others. High leverage combined with low persistence is not pivotality; and pivotality conferred by nearness to an absorbing boundary is a property of the boundary rather than of our place in time. I want the proposal to develop this decomposition and to ask one precise question, stated roughly as: is there a well-defined function mapping a time and a kind of intervention to expected long-run effect per unit of resource, and does the hinge hypothesis reduce to a claim about the shape of that function, about its integral, or about the location of its absorbing boundaries — and, for whichever version survives, what could count as evidence for it, given that any test is run by agents whose own beliefs are produced by the very processes under examination?

The proposal should contain: a statement of the research question precise enough to be true or false; an account of why the outside-view and inside-view positions as they stand leave that question open rather than merely disagreeing about it; the method, laid out month by month across the twelve months, including a deliberately simple formal model in which the three notions of influence are made to diverge and in which the conditions for each version of the hypothesis can be derived; a defense of that method against the obvious alternatives, namely commissioning more empirical forecasting or declaring the dispute verbal and dissolving it; an account of what a successful result would look like and, separately, of what would count as failure — including the possibilities that the decomposition does not bite, that the reformulated hypothesis becomes trivially true, or that it becomes evidentially unreachable; an honest assessment of what may prove too large for one year; and a closing paragraph answering a committee member who objects that all this is Whig history dressed up in mathematics.
