I'm in a graduate reading group that spent the last few weeks on the family of views holding that conscious experience is a kind of user illusion, and I've become frustrated by how much work the phrase gets made to do. Last Tuesday someone asked whether the hurtfulness of a pain is a property the state has or merely a way the state presents itself to the system that has it, and a member of the group cut the discussion off by saying that consciousness is a user illusion, so there is nothing behind the interface for the question to be about. When I pushed, he repeated the sentence with more emphasis. I don't doubt that something true is in the neighborhood of what he said; I doubt that the sentence he said picks it out.

My difficulty is that the analogy has a definite structure in its home case, and I can't tell which parts he intends to carry over. In a graphical user interface there are at least three distinguishable components: a display that is a deliberately simplified model of an underlying process, systematically omitting that process's real structure for the sake of usability; a genuine user who reads the display, acts through it, and is not itself one of the things displayed; and a designer who decided what to show and what to hide, and who chose according to the user's interests rather than according to the truth. The slogan seems to want to keep the first and third components while deleting the second, which is exactly the component that makes the phrase an illusion claim rather than a modeling claim — and it is also the component whose disappearance makes it hard to say who is being fooled, by whom, and to what end. Different readings of the slogan appear to resolve that strain in incompatible ways: by relocating the user into the display, by eliminating the designer along with the user, by keeping a designer but denying that the display misrepresents anything, or by treating the whole thing as a claim about the poverty of introspective access rather than about the ontology of experience.

What I want from you is a numbered menu of the precise theses the sentence could be expressing, ordered roughly from the least to the most radical. For each: state it in a form that could be true or false; say what it does with each of the three components above; say whether it is compatible with there being something it is like to undergo the state in question, or whether it denies even that; and give a verdict — platitude, substantive but defensible, contentious, false, or so vague as to be unassessable. Then tell me which of those readings would actually license the dismissal I witnessed, i.e., which would settle without further argument that there is no fact of the matter about hurtfulness as opposed to its presentation, and which would leave that question exactly where it started. Finally, two narrower points. First, flag any reading that is merely a relabeling: one on which the proponent concedes every datum the realist about phenomenal character concedes and adds only the word illusion. Second, assess a self-undermining worry I have: if the slogan implies that everything I can learn from introspection is drawn off the display and so systematically misleading, then the introspective grounds on which anyone would assert the slogan are themselves inside the display, and I want to know which readings can answer that and which cannot.
