I help run a small reading group at my uni that plays Nomic — the game where the only moves are proposing and changing the rules — as a back door into philosophy of law, and last week it turned into a two hour argument I honestly couldnt referee. Our rulebook is deliberately tiny. Rule 1: rules may be amended, repealed, or added by unanimous vote of the players present. Rule 2: Rule 1 shall not be amended, repealed, or suspended. That's basically it, plus a chair whose powers nobody ever bothered to write down.

Here's the exchange as best I remember it. Dev opened a motion to repeal Rule 2, on the grounds that Rule 2 only shields Rule 1 and says nothing about shielding itself, so Rule 1's own amendment clause applies to it like any other rule; the vote went through with everyone in favour. (1) Nadia said that's transparently a way around the entrenchment — the whole purpose of Rule 2 was to keep Rule 1 unanimous in perpetuity, so letting the group delete Rule 2 using the very procedure Rule 2 was there to freeze is unlocking the lock with the key it was meant to hide. Dev answered that rules mean what they say and not what we wish they'd said: if we'd wanted Rule 2 to be untouchable we should have written that into it, and we didn't, and moreover Rule 2 itself was adopted unanimously like everything else, so it can be unmade unanimously like everything else. (2) Nadia then conceded the repeal for argument's sake but said it still couldn't be used as a step toward changing Rule 1 in the same meeting — you can't do indirectly what you're barred from doing directly. Dev said the "indirectly" principle needs something it's barred from doing, and nothing bars it: Rule 2 forbade amending Rule 1, Rule 2 is gone, and we haven't amended Rule 1 yet. (3) The group then voted to amend Rule 1 to allow amendments by simple majority, and Nadia voted no. She said the amendment is void on its own terms, because the authority to amend Rule 1 comes from Rule 1, which demanded unanimity, and it didn't get unanimity. Dev said the authority doesn't come from Rule 1 at all — it comes from us, and the rulebook is just a written record of what we've agreed to, not its source. (4) After that Marcus proposed adding "this rule may not be repealed," and Nadia called it incoherent: either it can be repealed, in which case it's just false, or it can't, in which case we've permanently frozen something with no procedure for undoing it, which contradicts the idea that the rules are ours to change. Marcus said that's exactly what entrenchment means and we'd all just spent two hours living with a self-entrenching rule without anyone spotting the supposed contradiction. (5) Then the chair, Priya, ruled Marcus's motion out of order without putting it to a vote. Dev said she has no power to do that unless some rule grants it, and no rule does. Priya said somebody has to decide what even counts as a candidate for a vote, and if the rulebook is silent, the only office that exists decides by default. Nadia, to everyone's surprise, backed Priya here, on the grounds that with nobody empowered to make that call the game collapses into whoever shouts loudest. One of us mentioned this whole structure is supposedly a famous puzzle in jurisprudence but nobody could remember the details.

Could you go through it move by move and say who's right at each of the five, rather than just picking a winner overall? I'd like a verdict on each — right, wrong, or partly right with the part specified — and a short justification in the terms the players actually used, not a lecture on how the rulebook should have been drafted. Then tell me what both sides seem to be missing, especially about where the authority to change the rules is supposed to come from in the first place, and whether Dev and Nadia are even disagreeing about the same thing by the end or just talking past each other. If some of the moves turn out to be better arguments than they looked at the time, say so.
