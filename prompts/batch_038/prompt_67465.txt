Suppose a municipal housing authority has begun producing the decorative interiors of its social housing — wallpapers, tiles, glazed panels — with generative models trained on nineteenth-century Arts and Crafts patterns, so that ornament reaches households no commercial market would ever supply it to, while the human designers involved describe their labour as now wholly conceptual, execution having been delegated to the machine. A basic-income pilot launched alongside it is defended by its sponsors on the ground that it liberates recipients from work as such. What would William Morris have made of this arrangement?

Build the answer out of his own commitments rather than out of his reputation: the distinction he draws between useful work and useless toil, his conditional welcome to machinery as an instrument for abolishing drudgery rather than multiplying unnecessary commodities, and his account of pleasure in labour as the very substance of art, not a bonus attached to it. Then locate the point at which those commitments pull against one another here — whether the unmade ornament, the severed bond between conceiving and executing, or the stipend that excuses one from working altogether is the graver offence on his reckoning — and say which way you think he would finally come down, and what the position would cost him. Close by assessing whether the result is a fulfilment of the world of *News from Nowhere* or a betrayal of it. I am interested in the argument, not in references.
