I spent four hours last night arguing with a friend who teaches classics, and I came away unable to tell whether he had located a genuine blind spot in the toolkit I use professionally or had simply persuaded me that we were talking past one another. I would like the dispute settled by translation rather than by exhortation.

My field is monetary economics, specifically the microfoundations of money: random-matching models in the Kiyotaki–Wright lineage, in which the acceptability of an intrinsically useless object is an equilibrium property rather than an assumption; cash-in-advance and money-in-the-utility-function devices; and behind all of it the Walrasian apparatus of numéraire, Walras' law, and the treatment of a missing double coincidence of wants as the friction money relieves. I work in this vocabulary daily and need none of it explained. I also use the standard functional taxonomy — medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value — and I am alert to how much explanatory weight each of the three is carrying in any given model.

What I want is Aristotle's account of money and exchange restated claim by claim in that vocabulary: the treatment of household management and money-making in Politics Book I, together with the reciprocity discussion in Nicomachean Ethics Book V, where the products of unlike crafts must somehow be equalized before they can be exchanged and need is said to be what binds the parties together, with money standing in for it by agreement. The claims I want rendered are these.

1. Money comes to be by convention rather than by nature; its worth rests on the agreement of those who use it, and can be altered or annulled by them.
2. Exchange between unlike producers requires commensuration of unlike products, and the measure invoked is need, not labour or satisfaction.
3. Money represents that need by convention, functioning as a measure in the way a unit measures a magnitude, and it originated to spare traders the trouble of weighing and assessing bartered goods.
4. Acquisition directed at the goods a household requires is natural and bounded, while acquisition aimed at wealth as such is unbounded and unnatural.
5. In the unbounded form the means and the end change places: money, adopted as an instrument of exchange, comes to be pursued as though it were the point of the activity.
6. Lending at interest is the most contrary to nature of the modes of getting wealth, on the ground that money was contrived for exchange and here it is made to breed, yielding more of itself.

For each, tell me whether it lands in my vocabulary intact, whether it lands only after I bend something, or whether there is nowhere to put it.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. Sort the items into clean translations, translations achieved by distortion, and claims with no expression at all, and for each say whose fault the failure is: a real gap in my framework, or a sign that the Aristotelian claim was less determinate than it looked — the word natural doing both descriptive and normative work, or an appeal to a purpose that money, being a social artifact, was never assigned by anyone.

Two charges my friend pressed deserve separate treatment. First, that my framework cannot state the difference between a means and an end, because everything is instrumental to final consumption and a preference over money balances is just another preference, so the inversion he thinks Aristotle detects is inexpressible — and worse, that money-in-the-utility-function models build the inversion in and then list it among the assumptions. Second, that natural for Aristotle means conforming to what a thing is for, whereas search theory, which explains money's existence by the coordination problem it solves, provides no purpose that using money to make money could violate. Tell me whether either charge survives contact with what the models actually say, or whether he is attacking a caricature.

Please keep the translation honest. Do not render natural as efficient, or as equilibrium, or as welfare-improving; do not render unlimited acquisition as non-satiation; and then report that the residue is empty because everything fitted. Where a term has to be stretched to fit, mark the stretch. I would rather have a short list of genuinely untranslatable claims than a comprehensive concordance purchased with equivocation.
