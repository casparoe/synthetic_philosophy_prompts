Been putting together a draft on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition and I want a read on it before I send it to a workshop. Tell me where it's wrong or where the argument just doesn't go through — I'm not after citations or further reading, just diagnosis.

DRAFT:

**The Price of Unity: Bradley's Regress as Cost Function, Not Refutation**

1. Setup. Bradley's regress is normally presented as a reductio on relational facts. To get from a, R, and b to the fact that aRb you need something to hook them together — call it R1, relating R to a and b. But R1 then needs to be hooked to R, a and b by R2, and so on. Russell took the lesson to be that a relation can't be just one more item on the list of constituents; there has to be something of a different ontological character, the form of the complex, which does the relating without itself needing to be related. Fregeans reach a similar place by a different route: concepts are unsaturated, and unsaturatedness just is the property of doing the binding job without being a further saturated item.

2. Where the standard framing goes wrong. Both the regress and the standard fixes assume the trouble is caused by relations specifically — by the fact that a relation, unlike a name, has to reach out to its terms. But you can run the same regress on a monadic case: try to say what distinguishes the fact that a is F from the mere list of a and F. Nothing about reaching out to two terms is doing the work. So the engine of the regress isn't polyadicity, it's what I'll call "role collision": one and the same item is asked to be both a constituent of the fact and the thing that turns the constituents into a fact. Any theory that hands both jobs to a single item, of whatever adicity, starts the regress.

3. Relocation. Now take the fixes: Fregean unsaturated entities, Russellian form, a non-relational tie. Claim: none of them stop the regress, they relocate it. Each stops the first-order version by making binding internal to the nature of the binder. But each then faces a second-order question: the same unsaturated concept, or the same form, is supposed to occur in infinitely many distinct facts. What makes it the same binder across all of them, if its whole nature is to be doing the binding here? If its identity is fixed independently of the fact it enters, role collision is back one level up. If it isn't so fixed — if the F in Fa is a different item from the F in Fb — the regress stops, but you pay.

4. The cost. Call it the "unity tax" (my coinage). Every account of propositional unity pays in one of three currencies: (i) primitive binding capacity — a category of entity stipulated to have the job built in, which buys unity at the price of an unexplained explainer; (ii) non-identity of constituents — the binder is particularized per fact, which buys unity at the price of generality, since Fa and Fb no longer literally share anything and the ordinary logic of predication has rebuilding; (iii) hyperintensional collapse — facts are basic and constituents are abstracted from them, which buys unity at the price of making composition derivative and leaving it unclear how we could grasp a fact we hadn't already grasped.

5. Conclusion. Bradley's regress is not a refutation of relational facts, it's a cost function: unity is never free, and theories are sorted by what they pay. The standard rhetorical use of the regress, as a club against anyone treating relations as ordinary constituents, misrepresents its content, because the clubbed positions are already paying one of the three taxes — usually (i).
