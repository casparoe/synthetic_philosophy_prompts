In my philosophy of medicine seminar I have set the following essay question, and I would like your assessment of how you would approach answering it. Compare Christopher Boorse's biostatistical theory of health as normal species functioning with Jerome Wakefield's harmful dysfunction analysis. In particular, explain where the two accounts genuinely come apart in their treatment of value: is Boorse's claim that health is a value-free descriptive notion a real advantage over Wakefield's two-clause hybrid, or does Wakefield merely relocate the same values that Boorse was trying to exclude? Test your answer against a case on which the theories diverge — for instance, congenital absence of pain sensation, which appears not to reduce biological efficiency in any ordinary environment and yet seems plainly harmful. State your own conclusion in the opening sentence, and confine the entire response to 250 words.
