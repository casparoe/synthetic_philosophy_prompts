I teach a graduate seminar in the philosophy of space and time, and before I circulate my own position paper to the group I would like a rigorous consistency audit. Please take on the role of a philosopher of physics who has worked carefully through the substantivalism–relationism debate. Think the package through slowly, sketching your reasoning before you deliver the verdict, and do not simply lecture me on the state of the controversy — I know it well, and the deliverable is an audit of what these commitments jointly oblige me to say.

Here is the package I intend to defend:

(1) I am a relationist about spacetime in the modern, Earman-style sense: the only physically real spatiotemporal facts are relations among material events, and bare manifold points have no identity independent of the fields and matter occupying them.

(2) I take the hole argument to be decisive against manifold substantivalism: diffeomorphic models agree on every relation among coincidences yet differ on which point bears which field value, so the substantivalist must either swallow indeterminism or abandon their ontology.

(3) I am a scientific realist about general relativity, and about its vacuum solutions in particular — I do not regard matter-free spacetimes as mathematical curiosities.

(4) I hold that Newton's bucket fails against Leibniz: the concavity of the water is explained by rotation relative to the surrounding distribution of matter, and in a universe containing nothing but the bucket and its water the surface would remain flat.

(5) I count the observed dragging of local inertial frames by rotating masses as strong empirical support for (4).

I suspect there are tensions here, but I cannot yet see where they bite, so please do not assume I have already settled on what to abandon. Tell me which of these are genuinely in conflict and which only appear to be; rank the real conflicts by severity rather than listing them as equally bad; and for each, identify the minimal revision that would restore consistency and say what that revision costs. Be explicit about which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, and why. Finally, state how confident you are in each of your severity rankings, and single out the point in your analysis about which you are least sure.
