So here's the thing that bugs me about cookie banners. Any decent site here has to get you to opt in before it drops advertising trackers, a pre-ticked box doesnt count, and it must be as easy to withdraw consent as to give it. But the same site processes your name, address, card details and full purchase history with no consent at all — that's "necessary for the contract" — and there's no equivalent off switch short of closing your account. Meanwhile the consent-management platform keeps a permanent log of when you consented and to what, so the operator can prove it, and that log is exempt from the right to erasure because keeping it is itself a legal obligation. Which means the one behavioural record about you the site can never be forced to delete is the record of you refusing to be recorded.

I've read all the rationalist-blog takes saying consent banners are theatre, but they never say what the practice assumes. What view of privacy makes these choices hang together — control over information, something about surprise and manipulation, context-specific norms? Which rival views does it quietly rule out (property/self-ownership, straightforward informational self-determination, welfarism), and where is it underdetermined so that several theories license it equally? Then tell me what would have to change on the ground if you swapped in a property view.
