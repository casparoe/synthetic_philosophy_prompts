My PhD advisor runs our reading group on definitions of life and he's been handing down verdicts on borderline cases all semester. So far: E. coli growing in a rich broth it didn't make for itself — alive. A mature human red blood cell — not alive. A mule, sterile as it is — alive. A tardigrade in a tun with no measurable metabolism for five years — alive. A bacteriophage sitting in a vial of buffer — not alive. That same phage five minutes after it injects its DNA — he waved this off, said there's no phage there anymore, just a hijacked bacterium. An obligate aphid endosymbiont that hasn't lived outside a host cell in millions of years — not alive as an individual, though he said the aphid-plus-bacteria package is. A mitochondrion — not alive. A prion — not alive. A candle flame — not alive. A crystal growing out of a supersaturated solution — not alive.

Next week I have to present our own reactor and I want to get ahead of him. Its a 200 liter stirred tank with a population of fatty acid vesicles. We feed it sterile-filtered activated nucleotides and fatty acids; the vesicles take them up, grow, and split when they hit a size threshold. Some of them contain an RNA replicase ribozyme that copies whatever RNA happens to be present, and a faster-copying variant has swept the population three times this year. Nothing in there came from a cell, and nothing in there can make a nucleotide, a lipid, or a protein — if we stop the feed the whole thing goes to soup within about a week.

What's he going to say? I want the candidate rules that fit all eleven of his verdicts, which ones those verdicts already kill (a couple look dead on arrival to me — say which and why), what each surviving rule predicts about the reactor, your best guess at which rule he's actually running and what makes you prefer it over the others, and one further case that would seperate the survivors, with each rule's prediction on that case. End with \answer{alive}, \answer{not alive}, or \answer{refuses to answer} — he does that last one sometimes.
