I convene a final-year undergraduate module on the ethics of our treatment of animals, and I am setting the three-hour unseen paper. Students answer two questions out of four, each worth 25 marks and each expected to take about 45 minutes. In week eight we spend two seminars on Nozick's treatment of animals inside Anarchy, State, and Utopia — his proposal, in his own labelling, to combine utilitarianism for animals with Kantianism for people. The students have read only that text; they have had no secondary literature, and the exam regulations forbid them from preparing quotations. I would like you to write one of the four questions, together with a model answer and a marking scheme, for use by markers who are competent philosophers but not specialists in animal ethics.

What I want the question to turn on is a distinction the students consistently miss. Nozick is often reported as simply holding that animals count for less, and last year several scripts said nothing more than that his view is speciesist by another name. The interesting difficulty is that his framework contains two different criteria doing two different jobs, and they do not have the same extension. One concerns whether death is a harm to a given being, and turns on how far that being's life is organised around a long-term future and around plans and projects that reach into it. The other concerns whether side constraints apply to a being at all, and turns on the capacity to shape one's life in the light of an overall conception of it, to be guided by reasons, and to stand in moral relations with others. The exam question should make candidates separate these, and then apply both to a case that Nozick never considers. Here is the case I have in mind; please feel free to tighten it, but keep its structure. A research sanctuary houses an African grey parrot, whom we may call Moses, whose keepers have documented that he remembers a caching task across an interval of eighteen months, declines an immediately available preferred food in order to obtain a tool he will need next week, and shows prolonged disturbance when a companion bird is removed for veterinary treatment. A virology group proposes a course of procedures on Moses that is not lethal but is genuinely and repeatedly painful, and whose results would be expected to avert a great deal of suffering and death among wild conspecifics facing an outbreak. The same proposal, put instead to a forty-one-year-old man living in an adjacent care facility with profound congenital cognitive impairment whose life displays no comparable long-term structure, would be rejected out of hand by any ethics committee. Under the hybrid view, the use of Moses is permitted, since animals may be used for the aggregate benefit of animals; the use of the man is forbidden, since persons may not be used for the benefit of animals. Candidates must say whether Nozick can hold that line: either he owes a criterion for side constraints that excludes Moses, in which case he must explain why it does not also exclude the man in the care facility, or he grants Moses side constraints, in which case the hybrid is on its way to collapsing into a single-tier Kantianism and he owes a new account of why animals may be killed for food at all, and of what to make of the intervention-in-nature question his view raises and leaves largely unresolved on practical and epistemic grounds. The question should also ask candidates to state whether that collapse is a reductio of the view or its most attractive consequence, and to justify their answer.

Please deliver four things. First, the exam question itself, formatted as it would appear on the paper, with the case stated compactly enough to be read in three minutes and with the mark allocation shown. Second, a model answer of 700 to 850 words, written as a strong candidate would write it under time pressure rather than as a journal article. Third, a marking scheme listing five must-hit moves with the marks each carries, summing to 25, and for each a one-line note on the characteristic way candidates get it wrong — for instance, treating the two criteria as one, or supposing that pointing out the disanalogy with the man in the care facility is by itself a refutation rather than a dilemma Nozick might escape. Fourth, a short paragraph on what separates a first-class script from a solid 2:1 on this question. Two constraints: do not ask candidates for, and do not supply, citations, page numbers, bibliography or further reading, and do not invent references to the secondary literature. Everything required should be available to a student who has read the primary text and thought hard about it.

I want a committed response, not a balanced survey. Take a definite position in the model answer on whether the hybrid survives the case, and give the reasoning for it; if you think a first-class candidate could earn full marks arguing the opposite conclusion, say so explicitly in the marking scheme and state precisely what such a candidate would have to establish to get there. Finish with a final line beginning "Verdict:" followed by exactly one of STABLE, UNSTABLE or STABLE-ONLY-IF-REVISED.
