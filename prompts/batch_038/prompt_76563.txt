ok so I've been way too deep in a rabbit hole about betting and belief lately and I wrote down what I actually think so I could present it at my school's philosophy club. Problem is like three kids there are way more into this stuff than I am (one of them reads actual decision theory papers for fun) and I do not want to stand up there and get picked apart. So can you just check whether my list hangs together?

Here's what I'd say I'm committed to:

1. Your degrees of belief have to obey the rules of probability. The reason I believe this isn't some vague "rationality is nice" thing, it's the betting argument: if they don't, someone can offer you a package of wagers that you'd happily accept one by one and that loses you money no matter what happens. Being bookable is basically what being incoherent *is*.

2. Same style of argument works for changing your mind. You should conditionalize on your evidence, because if you update some other way, a bookie who knows your update plan can sell you bets now and then more later and lock in a loss. So the money argument covers both the static and the changing-your-mind case.

3. But I also think you should never sit at exactly 1 or exactly 0 on anything, including math. Even if I've gone over a proof five times I could have screwed it up, or be using rules that don't work the way I think. Certainty is never earned, it's just a refusal to keep listening.

4. The thing holding all this up, I think, is that your betting odds are the honest report of what you believe. If you say 0.7 then you should take either side of a bet at 70 cents on the dollar, and if you won't, then you didn't really mean 0.7.

5. That said, in real life I think it's smart and not at all irrational to refuse bets from some guy who's obviously working an angle. If a stranger at lunch offers me a series of small wagers one after another I'm not taking them even if each one looks fine on its own. And honestly if someone volunteers to bet me at even odds, that's kind of evidence they know something I don't, so I should probably move away from 0.5, not toward the bet.

6. Also I don't think you have to have one single clean number per question. Right now I feel like my team wins regionals and also feel like we definately don't, and I'm not going to call myself irrational for holding both.

7. One more: I only think the "you'd lose money" argument proves something when the money actually matters. Getting booked for four cents over a whole afternoon doesn't show anything about your mind.

So: which of these are genuinely fighting each other, and which ones only look like they are? Please rank the real ones by how bad they are instead of treating them all as equally damaging, and for each one tell me what I'd have to give up and what giving it up costs me — like which other items on the list stop working if I patch that one. And where I'm wrong that there's any problem at all, say so and explain why it looked like a problem. I have no idea where the damage is and I'm not attached to any specific item, I just want to know before Saturday.
