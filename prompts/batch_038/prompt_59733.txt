I am putting together a short piece for an aesthetics reading group's end-of-term zine, and I would like a satirical FAQ page for a fictional professional body — call it the Board of Certified Judges of Taste — which administers a credential modelled directly on the criteria Hume sets out for the true judge in his essay on the standard of taste.

The form is a parody of the FAQ page of a well-funded certification body: reassuring, bureaucratically warm, organised around eligibility, the assessment cycle, continuing requirements, and appeals. It must stay deadpan throughout — no winking, no authorial aside, no character in the document who suspects that anything is amiss. The comedy should come from the Board's procedures enacting Hume's account faithfully, problems included. The exaggeration has to be of features genuinely present in the essay — the reliance on established models and long-held reputations, the discounting of unqualified dissent, the way certification and canon mutually sustain one another — not of features invented for the joke.

Content requirements, to be distributed across the five entries rather than listed as a checklist:

1. The eligibility criteria should be built out of the components of Hume's true judge: sound sense, delicate sentiment, improvement through practice, perfection through comparison, and the clearing away of all prejudice. Each should reappear as an administrative requirement — a practice log, a comparative viewing quota, a prejudice-disclosure form, a soundness interview.

2. The circularity must be present in its administrative form and stated as routine: certification is granted to candidates whose verdicts agree with those of already-certified judges on works whose excellence is settled, and the excellence is settled by the agreement of the certified judges. Note also that the general rules the Board applies are drawn from the established models the Board itself upholds.

3. Handle the anecdote Hume borrows from Cervantes about Sancho's two kinsmen, the hogshead of wine, and what was found at the bottom of it when it was emptied. The only wording you may take verbatim from the essay is this: "To produce these general rules or avowed patterns of composition is like finding the key with the leathern thong; which justified the verdict of Sancho's kinsmen, and confounded those pretended judges, who had condemned them." Use it or leave it out, but paraphrase everything else, and put no other words in quotation marks as though they were Hume's.

4. One entry must answer an objection from outside the Board: a younger reader, someone whose tradition the Board's canon does not cover, or someone who can detect no defect in a work the Board has condemned. The answer should dispose of the objection using only the Board's own resources — insufficient practice, insufficient comparison, unexamined prejudice, want of good sense — applied so thoroughly that no dissent from an uncertified person can ever count as evidence against a certified verdict.

5. One entry must address disagreement between two certified judges, and reproduce Hume's double move: on works of established excellence such judges are not expected to differ, and where they genuinely do differ and both are qualified, both are indulged as temperaments equally valid. The Board should administer this with some piece of machinery — a schedule, a register, a seasonal rotation — rather than concede that it is a tension. It is also welcome here, or elsewhere, to have the Board answer the question whether beauty is in the object or in the perceiver with a compliance non-answer that manages to say both.

6. The appeal to durability — works that have held their reputation across centuries and countries — should appear, and the entry containing it should quietly reveal that these works survived because the Board's predecessors taught, preserved, reissued, and required them.

Never name the objections: no reference to circularity, question-begging, survivorship, conservatism, or who gets to be a judge, and no words like ironic or obviously doing the reader's work. Someone who has never read Hume should find a plausible, faintly sinister FAQ; someone who has should recognise every clause.

Length and form: exactly five numbered entries, each a bolded question followed by an answer of roughly 80 to 120 words, for a total of about 550 to 650 words. A single title line for the page is permitted above the first entry. Nothing before that title and nothing after the fifth answer — no preface, no closing note, no explanation of what is being satirised.

Target: the position and the genre, not a person. An account of aesthetic authority that certifies itself, and the compliance prose in which such authority dresses. The Board, its officers, its candidates, and its works should all be invented; do not name Hume's essay, any real institution, or any living figure.
