I'm on my institute's biosafety review panel — seven of us, we meet monthly — and we certify who's allowed to work alone in the BSL-3 suite. A postdoc's certification came up for renewal after two near-misses where an anteroom door was left unsecured. The door sensors log automatically, so nobody disputes the doors were open; the only thing in question is her state of mind. On both occasions she says she has no memory of the relevant minute or two, and she's adamant she must have run the sequence correctly because she always does and would have noticed otherwise. She isn't evasive about any of it: she describes her routine in fluent, confident, very specific detail, and she makes the point that a memory gap is exactly as consistent with being wholly absorbed in the work as with being inattentive — maybe more so. Our manual says the panel gives a researcher's own account of her mental states "presumptive weight," but never says what defeats the presumption. Half the panel wants to trust her; the other half says confidence is doing all the work here and we have no way to check it.

Draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting. It should recommend certify / don't certify / certify with conditions, state the conditions and how long they'd run, and then say what sort of first-person testimony a panel like ours can actually lean on going forward — some rule we could apply next time. I want her side given at full strength, not waved off with "memory is unreliable."
