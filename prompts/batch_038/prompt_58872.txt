Not academic, just something I keep running into on my own, so I probably don't have the vocabulary for it.

When I try to check whether I'm actually having a mental image — say, whether I can picture my kitchen — the answer depend on how I frame the question. Ask "is there an image there?" and I find one readily enough. Ask "hold on, is that an image, or just the thought that there's an image?" and it evaporates, and now I cant tell whether it was ever there. The bad part isn't that I got it wrong. It's that afterwards there's no way to reconstruct the answer, because remembering the check just re-runs the check. The only evidence I could ever have about whether the image was there before I looked is evidence that got made by looking.

Same shape with moods. Vaguely bad all afternoon, so I turn my attention to it to work out what it is, and now I'm not sure whether I located an irritation that was already sitting there or whether attending to it is what turned background noise into an irritation.

I know people say introspection is unreliable, but that doesn't quite fit — unreliable implies there's a settled fact about my state that I'm just bad at reading off. What I'm describing is that I can't tell whether there's a settled fact at all until I look.

So: does this have an established name? And can you sort out the differences between (a) misreporting a state that is determinately there, (b) altering the state by examining it, and (c) there being no determinate state until it's examined? Is (c) a position anyone actually holds, or does it just collapse into (b) with extra mystery? Please don't just hand me a label — I want the boundaries, because I suspect I'm lumping together two or three different things.
