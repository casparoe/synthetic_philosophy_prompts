I'm in an alignment reading group and we've spent months arguing about how to specify what we want an AI to aim at, and it has completely calicified. One camp says the target has to be some kind of idealized deliberation — what we'd endorse if we were fully informed, fully rational, vividly imagining the consequences — because anything short of that just launders whatever preferences happened to be lying around. The other camp says idealization is question-begging: you have to decide which distortions get corrected and which get preserved, and that choice already does all the normative work, so the ideal advisor is just the theorist's own values wearing a lab coat. Nobody budges, and every case one side produces (the addict who, fully informed, wants out; the parent who after years of reflection stops caring whether her kids succeed) gets reinterpreted by the other side as either vindication or as proof the idealization was set up wrong.

My working hypothesis: they aren't really disagreeing about what to do, they're disagreeing about whether there's any procedure-independent fact about what we'd value — one side assumes convergence across reasonable idealizations, the other assumes deep sensitivity to the starting point. If that's right the dispute sits downstream of a metaphysical one and no thought experiment can settle it. Is that the diagnosis you'd give, or is there a substantive, even semi-empirical question buried in here that could actually be gotten at — something like whether distinct idealization procedures converge or diverge, and what would show it? Tell me what each camp's core claim really commits them to, and what would count as progress rather than another paper. One-sentence diagnosis up front, then at most 400 words.
