You are a philosopher who works on practical rationality, agency over time, and intertemporal choice, and I would like you to audit a position I am about to defend in public. I am giving a short position paper at an interdisciplinary workshop on agency and time — roughly half the room is behavioral economists, half is philosophers — and I have been asked to write my view down as a numbered list so that people can attack it precisely rather than at the level of vibes. A colleague who read the list told me it is unstable and that at least one plank has to go, but when I pressed him he could not say which one, or why, and I would much rather find that out from you now than from the room on the day. I am deliberately not telling you which commitment I would be most willing to sacrifice, because I genuinely do not know, and I do not want to steer you.

Here is the list. (1) The best available description of human intertemporal choice is quasi-hyperbolic: a stable long-run discount rate, plus a separate present-bias parameter that applies a fixed additional discount to anything that is not immediate; that kink is a real feature of the data rather than measurement noise. (2) At every moment my preferences are complete and transitive; a friend objected that the curves in (1) must cross and so the agent must hold contradictory preferences, and I said she was confused, though I could not say crisply why. (3) Impatience as such is not a rational defect: there is nothing wrong, merely as a matter of rationality, with discounting the future at a positive constant rate. (4) Dynamically inconsistent choice, by contrast, is a rational defect and not an exotic taste — an agent whose ranking of two fixed options reverses purely because the calendar has moved is criticizable, not merely unusual. (5) The only standpoint from which a choice can be evaluated is the standpoint of the person making it at the time; I reject any appeal to the interests of the agent's life taken as a whole, or to a global diachronic perspective that sits outside every moment of choice and grades them from nowhere. (6) Strict weakness of will is real: people sometimes act against their own unclouded all-things-considered judgment about what is best, at the very moment of acting, with no last-minute change of mind and no impairment of the judgment itself. (7) Odyssean precommitment is rational in a strong sense — not merely clever but genuinely authority-conferring: an earlier decision can bind a later self even when that later self, reflecting calmly at the time, sincerely repudiates the plan and would, by her own considered lights, be better served by breaking it. (8) Akratic failures are proper objects of blame; I am unmoved by the suggestion that they be reclassified as compulsions or illnesses, and I do not want to medicalize ordinary incontinence.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a lecture. Begin by brainstorming a little on your own: work through the pairwise interactions before you commit to any verdict, and be willing to report that a tension you initially suspected turns out to be illusory. Then give me (a) a discrimination between the pairs that are in genuine tension and the pairs that merely look tense, with the reasoning for each call; (b) the genuine tensions ranked by severity, from the one that most threatens the package downward, each with an exact statement of what has to give and what the cheapest repair costs — whether it forces me to abandon a claim I actually care about or only to reword one; (c) an answer to the question of which single revision would clear the most trouble at once, and what the package looks like afterward: does it remain recognizably my view, or does it collapse into a standard position I have been arguing against all along?; and (d) any plank that is not in tension with the others but is independently underdetermined or quietly question-begging in a way the economists in the room will pounce on. Distinguish throughout between what my commitments strictly entail and what merely sits uneasily alongside them. You may use technical vocabulary where it does real work and may name the positions you think I am drifting toward, but I do not want a bibliography, a reading list, or a survey of who holds what — the value here is the analysis. Please write in American English.
