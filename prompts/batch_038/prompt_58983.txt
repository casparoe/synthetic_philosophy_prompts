trying to pin down what I actually believe about well-being and the whole what-people-would-want-if-they-were-thinking-clearly idea, and I want a consistency check before I say any of it out loud at a reading group. here's the list:

1. what's good for someone is roughly what they'd want for themselves with full information, clear-headed reasoning, and a vivid sense of what the options are actually like. call that their extrapolated self.

2. the extrapolation has to run from the actual person outward. I keep their character, their attachments, their tastes, and just clean up the ignorance and the sloppy reasoning. if you idealize so far that the result is basically a stranger, its not their good anymore and I don't see why they should care about it.

3. I think idealization converges. push people far enough along that path and the extrapolated selves start to agree about what matters, so there's a fairly stable common core rather than as many goods as there are people.

4. part of why I think that: some attitudes seem to survive no matter where you start. an extrapolated self wouldn't want to be manipulated, wouldn't want their own preferences installed by an advertiser or a feed, wouldn't want to be lied to about the options. that looks like the machinery behind the convergence.

5. when an adult's actual, sincere, settled wishes conflict with what their extrapolated self would want, I don't think we get to just impose the extrapolated thing on them. there's something disrespectful about overruling somebody in the name of a better version of themselves.

6. but for policy, and for designing systems that shape people's lives, I think we should target extrapolated preferences rather than revealed ones. revealed preferences are contaminated — they're whatever the environment, the marketing and the incentives happened to produce.

7. I do think people can be straightforwardly wrong about their own good, even when they're calm, sincere, and have all the facts they think they have.

so: which of these actually pull against each other, and which ones only look like they clash? for each real one tell me how bad it is and what the cheapest fix would cost me, and rank them by severity — I don't want a flat list where everything comes out equally problematic. and if a single revision would buy the most coherence across the whole set, tell me what it is and what I'd lose by making it.
