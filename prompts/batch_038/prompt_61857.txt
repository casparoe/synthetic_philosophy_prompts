I'm building a diagnostic for a political theory seminar — one of those "which view do you actually hold?" quizzes — this time on military service: conscription vs. a professional volunteer army vs. the whole-of-society / total-defence model you get in Finland, Sweden and Switzerland. Nine questions, each a concrete case (a cash bounty to lift recruiting, an objector who's fine with defence but not with one particular war, the option of paying someone to serve in your place, a cyber reservist who never wears a uniform, a state that just joined an alliance and no longer claims neutrality...) with two or three options apiece, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto named positions.

The point is to sort people by what they'd actually do, so no question may contain words like liberty, coercion, duty, fairness or civic, and none should just ask whether the state may force you to serve.

The angle I care about: these debates quietly conflate two different things — who bears the risk of the polity's violence, and who controls the means of it. I want at least three questions that come apart on exactly that. Leaves must include the militia-requires-a-draft view, conscription-is-fine-only-if-it's-universal-and-unbuyable, professional-army-plus-alliances-is-enough, and the resilience reading where the justification isn't military effectiveness but social cohesion. Flag any pair of leaves your questions can't seperate — I suspect the resilience view and plain civic republicanism may be one of them, especially once a state has given up neutrality — and any answer pattern that lands on no leaf at all, saying whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
