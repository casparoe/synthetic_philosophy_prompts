I've been keeping a log of my gut reactions to these little choice problems — sure thing versus gamble, one shot, probabilities stated — and I've reached the point where I want to know what shape my preferences actually have. I've read a bit about risk-weighted expected utility, the idea that on top of a utility function you get a separate function that weights probabilities, so I'm wondering whether there's some particular weighting function that is me, or whether my verdicts are just a mess.

Here's the log. Everything is money, everything is one-shot, and where a probability is given I fully believe it.

1. A million for certain, versus 10% of five million, 89% of a million, and 1% of nothing: take the certain million. No hesitation.
2. But 11% of a million versus 10% of five million: take the 10% of five million. Also no hesitation. I know 1 and 2 are supposed to be in tension with each other and I don't care, both feel obvious.
3. $45 certain versus a 50/50 shot at $100 or nothing: take the $45.
4. $950 certain versus 99% of $1000: take the $950. Which is more risk averse then it should be, giving up $40 of expected value to dodge a one-in-a-hundred zero, but that's how it feels.
5. $8 certain versus a 1% shot at $1000: take the 1% shot. And I think I'd still take it if the sure thing went up to $9.

Now the ones where the odds aren't handed to me.

6. Urn with 90 balls, 30 red, and 60 that are black or yellow with the split not disclosed. Bet pays $100 on one draw. Red over black, and also black-or-yellow over red-or-yellow. Both times I want the bet whose odds I can actually write down.
7. But now suppose you tell me the number of black balls was fixed by a fair random process, every split from 0 to 60 equally likely. Then I grant you the chance of red is 1/3 and the chance of black is 1/3 in exactly the same sense, and I still take red. I can't defend this one. It's like the not-knowing doesn't go away when the numbers get supplied.
8. Last one, and it's the one that worries me. Someone offers to sell me a ticket: 50% of $100, 50% of nothing. I won't pay more than about $45 for it, which lines up with 3. But if I already own that exact ticket, I won't sell it for $55. I've checked myself on this in both directions and I mean it.

My tentative story is that there's one dial — how much I mind being unable to name the odds — and mine is turned way up. But that does nothing for 5, where I'm running toward the odds, and nothing at all for 8, which doesn't seem to be about odds.

So: is there a principle, one function or a small set of clauses, that gets all of this or as much of it as can be gotten? State it precisely enough that I could check it against the numbers myself instead of in prose, and for each clause tell me which line of the log forced it in. Then tell me what it says about two cases I haven't settled: a 0.1% shot at $10,000 versus $6 certain, and the urn from 6 except you'll let me pay $5 to look inside before picking my bet — do I pay? And be straight with me about which of my verdicts your principle can't accommodate. If 3 and 5 can't both stand, I want to hear that, and which one is cheaper to give up.
