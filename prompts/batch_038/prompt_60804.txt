The talk is about a voting system I actually built and that my company uses to decide whether design proposals (RFCs) get accepted. I'm pitching it as a small-scale, self-correcting epistocracy. How it works:

- every engineer starts at weight 1 when they approve or reject an RFC
- your weight moves based on your review track record: approve something that later causes an incident, weight goes down; leave a review comment flagging a risk that later turns out to have been a real risk, weight goes up
- weights are capped, they decay back toward 1 on roughly a six month half life, and there's a floor so nobody ever drops to zero — everyone always keeps some vote
- my main claim is that this dodges the standard objections to epistocracy, because it doesn't install a fixed class of wise people. Anyone can earn weight, it's scored on calbration rather than credentials, and it gets revised automatically when you turn out to have been wrong.
