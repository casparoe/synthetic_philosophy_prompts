Here's what I hold, roughly in the order it shows up in the chapter.

1. Strict dominance only. I will not eliminate weakly dominated strategies, ever. A player can be indifferent between two actions and indifference isn't irrationality, so weak dominance isn't licensed by rationality on its own; and it's order-dependent, and it can throw away Nash equilibria, and I'm not having a solution concept whose output depends on who happened to go first.

2. But mixtures are allowed to do the eliminating. If some lottery over a player's own remaining actions beats action x against every profile of the others' remaining actions, x goes, even if no single remaining action beats it. This isn't cosmetic — a couple of my worked examples are only dominance-solvable once you let mixtures dominate.

3. Having allowed mixtures, I'm still relaxed about order. Yes, the pool of available dominators shrinks as I go along, but strict dominance converges to the same surviving set however you run it, mixtures included.

4. That said, I only ever write down eliminations of pure actions. My working assumption is that if a pure action survives, every lottery over the surviving pure actions is still available to that player — a mixture of things you might do is a thing you might do.

5. What survives the whole procedure is exactly what common knowledge of rationality permits: an action survives iff some player might play it given everyone knows everyone is rational. In the chapter I use 'survives iterated strict dominance' and 'is rationalisable' interchangeably, as though they were one notion.

6. When I say belief I always mean a product belief — each player's conjecture about the others factorises into independent conjectures about each of them separately. Correlated conjectures would let my players coordinate in a way the game gives them no mechanism for, and I don't want that smuggled in through the back door.

7. Backward induction in finite games of perfect information I accept without qualification, and I sell it to students as iterated dominance applied node by node, needing no epistemic assumptions beyond common knowledge of rationality. That's meant to be a virtue of my setup: one notion of elimination doing all the work, strict dominance from start to finish.

8. One thing I'm careful about and quietly proud of: I only ever state the conditional in one direction. If the procedure terminates at a single profile, that profile is the unique Nash equilibrium. I never claim that every unique equilibrium can be recovered that way, and I say so explicitly.
