Novelist here, three chapters deep into a book I'm not sure I can pull off, and I need a spec before I write any more of the middle.

Setup: a drought-stricken river basin, a water treaty that allocates by the ton off the back of a list of protected taxa given by scientific name, and a fish everybody has called one species for eighty years. Then a revisionist team proposes splitting it into four, which quietly redraws who gets water. My tribunal chapter is full of expert witnesses who are effectively arguing about what a species *is*, and I need their disagreement to be about something real rather than two people using the same word differently — which is what it keeps collapsing into in draft.

So: write the desiderata. What would any theory of species have to deliver to count as adequate — and, for my purposes, to be usable in a room like that one. I want each requirement with the case or the consideration sitting behind it, not a catalogue of isms. Things I already suspect belong on the list, tell me where I'm wrong:

- it has to handle the lineage in my book that's all-female and gynogenetic — needs a neighbouring species' sperm to trigger egg development, gives nothing genetic back — because that's the one none of my witnesses want to touch;
- it has to survive the headwater population that intergrades with everything downstream instead of pretending clines aren't real;
- it has to be decidable from what a witness could actually carry into a hearing — a jar, some photographs, tissue out of a museum drawer collected in the 1930s — not from a genome nobody has sequenced;
- it has to say what makes the thing on the treaty list the same entity over time as the thing in the water right now, and seperately what makes it one entity rather than two at a single moment;
- it has to say whether the type specimen in that drawer fixes a boundary or only a name;
- and it has to explain why different camps counting the same fauna come out with numbers that differ by an order of magnitude, and whether that's a defect in the theories or exactly what you'd expect.

Then the part I most want. Which of these are genuine requirements and which are one camp's taste dressed up as rigor. Which pairs or triples can't all be met at once — I've got "revisable as the science improves" against "settled enough to allocate water by" already, show me the others, especially anything that turns on gene flow or on asexuals. And which single requirement does the most damage to the most contenders, because that's probably the hinge my whole chapter should turn on. Finish with a short paragraph scoring the main species concepts against the list — keep it short, dont pad it, the criteria are where I need the thinking and I'll handle the drama myself.
