I'm in a phil of mind seminar that's spent the last three weeks on mind uploading, and I'm supposed to write a paper that engages with the personal-identity side of it. But before I wade into the candidate views I want to know what I should be demanding of one. So: write me the desiderata for a theory of survival-through-uploading — the full spec of what an account has to deliver to be good enough that I could actually use it to reason about what happens to me in these cases, not just to score points in section.

What I want in it:

- the cases it has to get right, with the intuition behind each one and a note on how load-bearing that intuition really is. Include the standard set (scan-and-destroy, the gradual neuron-for-neuron replacement of Chalmers's fading/dancing qualia sort, fission into two running copies, a copy that stays online while I'm still walking around in my body) plus one my seminar group made up that I've never seen handled anywhere: a handshake upload where the emulation is built first, and then biological neurons get deleted one at a time as the em takes over each job, so for weeks the two systems are causally coupled and jointly producing my behavior — and the em runs at maybe 10x clock speed so it's racking up memories faster than the meat does. Mid-transition, who is thinking? Does the speed mismatch matter, and if a theory says no, what is it using to say so?
- the distinctions it can't collapse. Identity vs. what matters in survival, survival vs. duplication, and whatever else you think people quietly run together in this debate.
- anything from neuroscience or physics it can't contradict, if there's anything relevant at that level.
- for each requirement, whether it's genuinely non-negotiable or is really one camp's preference dressed up as a constraint. Like "must be compatable with functionalism" reads as a hard requirement to some people and as begging the question to others, and I suspect a few more on the list are like that.

And the part I most want: tell me whether some subset of these is jointly unsatisfiable. My hunch is that "gradual replacement preserves me" plus "branching breaks identity" plus "scan-and-destroy doesn't preserve me" can't all stand, because a gradual replacement is decomposable into a bunch of steps that each look like a tiny scan-and-destroy — but argue it rather than assuming I'm right, and if I'm wrong say so. If the list itself turns out to be a result rather than a checklist, say that too.

Then briefly, a paragraph each and no more, how the main contenders do against the list: psychological continuity views, the biological/animalist view, the Parfit line that identity isn't what matters, and a phenomenal-continuity view. Keep the weight on the criteria though, I'll do my own scoring. Don't give me references or a reading list, I can find those.

Last line should start with "Verdict:" and name the single requirement that knocks out the most candidates — or, if you think a pair can't be jointly satisfied, name the pair and say which one has to go.
