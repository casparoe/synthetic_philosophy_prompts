I am a third-year undergraduate double-majoring in philosophy and computer science. This term I am in a seminar on seventeenth-century natural philosophy, where we have been reading Hobbes's De Corpore alongside selected chapters of Leviathan, and my paper is on his philosophy of geometry. Outside the seminar I have spent roughly two years working in Lean 4, mostly mechanizing small pieces of Euclidean geometry — straightedge-and-compass constructions, triangle congruence, some area and dissection arguments — and I have picked up enough dependent type theory to talk comfortably about inductive and definitional constructions, definitional equality, proof terms versus tactic scripts, the kernel as the final arbiter of correctness, quotient types, and the difference between classical and constructive reasoning. My supervisor has warned me that the standard failure mode for a paper like mine is anachronism dressed up as insight: the student quietly converts Hobbes into a proto-constructivist who anticipated the proof-assistant revolution, and then congratulates him for it. I would like to test that worry directly by forcing his views through a vocabulary I understand far better than his own.

So here is the task. Restate Hobbes's philosophy of geometry in the language I actually use — types and terms, constructions, definitional equality, normalization and computation, decidability, the kernel, the difference between producing a witness and proving an existence claim — as faithfully as you can manage. Please work through at least the following, one at a time: (i) his account of reasoning as reckoning, the adding and subtracting of names or marks, with the consequence that truth concerns the right ordering of names rather than things; (ii) his claim that geometry and civil philosophy are demonstrable sciences precisely because we generate their objects ourselves, since we draw the figures and we institute the commonwealth, while physics is not demonstrable in the same way because we do not know how natural bodies come to be; (iii) his insistence that definition should proceed by generation or construction rather than by genus and differentia, and that this is what licenses knowledge of causes; (iv) his nominalism about universals and the associated attack on the abstraction he thought the universities were teaching; (v) his rejection of actual infinities and of infinitesimal magnitudes, together with his refusal to treat a continuum as composed of points; and (vi) his late quadrature of the circle and the long, losing controversy with John Wallis over the arithmetic of infinities, which Hobbes never conceded. One of my friends in the seminar argues that what Hobbes means by generation of a figure is just a parametric description of motion, so that a curve is a function of a parameter and constructing it is evaluating it; tell me whether that is a legitimate translation or exactly the flattering anachronism my supervisor fears. And do not smuggle in assumptions from my side and call them translation — in particular, be explicit whenever you have had to convert a causal notion of coming-to-be into an algorithmic notion of producing a term, since those are not obviously the same thing.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. For each claim above, say whether it maps cleanly, whether it maps only after distortion you can specify, or whether it has no expression at all in type-theoretic vocabulary. Where something fails to translate, tell me which of two things is going on: my framework is missing a concept that Hobbes genuinely needs — causation, motion, material figures drawn in ink rather than objects built in a proof checker, and above all the political stakes he attaches to geometrical certainty, including his views about who gets to settle doctrine — or his claims turn out, under pressure, to be less substantive and less determinate than they look in the Latin. Two specific puzzles I would like you to address as part of that assessment. First, if certainty in geometry is supposed to follow from the fact that we construct the objects ourselves, what went wrong with the quadrature? My vocabulary seems to have a very clean analogue — an author who is convinced a term is well-formed, and a kernel that refuses it — but the disanalogy may matter, since Hobbes rejected the verdict of the mathematical community in a way no one can reject a kernel check; does that show his constructivism was never about checkability at all? Second, his nominalism and my own framework both refuse Platonic entities, yet in Lean I happily form a type of circles and reason generically about it. Is generic reasoning over a type a survival of the abstraction he attacked, or is it something his theory of names can accommodate? I would rather have an honest answer that leaves Hobbes looking strange than a smooth one that makes him look like he was writing Mathlib documentation.
