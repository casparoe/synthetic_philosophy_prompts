I've been stuck on the same scene for eleven days, and the problem is metaphysical, which is not what you want when you're 80k words into a draft.

My character Ilse has a disposition: she would keep a confidence, whatever it cost her. That's the whole spine of the book — people tell her things because of it. But she also has a blocker, a bit of neurological wiring left over from meningitis when she was nine: if she's ever asked a direct question in front of three or more people, she blurts out the secret before she's decided anything. It's involuntary, it bypasses her judgment entirely, and the disposition stays perfectly intact through it. Here's the catch: the novel is engineered — by me, its god — so that she never once gets asked a direct question in front of three people. Every scene stops short. A car backfires. Somebody changes the subject. She's never tested, not once, in 300 pages.

So does Ilse have the disposition, or doesn't she? I can't finish the chapter until I know what I think, and I've realized my two instincts are actually two different novelists who would hate each other.

One of them holds that a character just *is* a pattern of responses to situations: character is what she does when the pressure comes, and if the pressure never comes, then the sentence "Ilse would keep a confidence" isn't reporting some hidden inner machinery, it's just my way of shrugging at the reader. On this view the world never delivering the test isn't a mask on anything — there's simply nothing there to mask.

The other holds that the machinery is the character and the outputs are just evidence of it: a disposition can be real and permanently prevented from showing, and the interesting fact about Ilse isn't what she does but what the world keeps almost asking of her.

Write me a dialogue between these two — call them Wren and Odile, both novelists, both about fifty, arguing over this at the end of a long day, ideally with a drink involved. Some ground rules:

- Give me a specific objection from each side that the other can't just wave off, and make them actually answer each other's points instead of trading prepared speeches at one another.
- Wren has to press on this: if it makes no difference to a single sentence anyone could write, what is the difference a difference *of*? And Odile has to answer without retreating to "trust me, I'm the author, I know what's in there" — because that's a dodge and I'd catch it.
- Odile should try the move I keep circling in my notes and haven't been able to make work: that the disposition and its blocker aren't two separate items but one composite property, so the blocking doesn't conceal who Ilse is, it *constitutes* it. Wren should have a real reply to that, not a groan.
- Neither of them is dumb and neither cheats. I want one moment where somebody has to concede more than they came in intending to.
- Around 900 words. Bare stage directions at most, one line at the top to set the room and then let them talk.
- Don't resolve it. I want the last few lines to show the argument has changed shape — like the question they end up stuck on isn't the one they started with — rather than either of them winning.

Basically: help me figure out what I think by writing the two people I can't decide between, and make it good enough that I could almost lift it into the book.
