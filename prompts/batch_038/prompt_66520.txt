I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, and I'm thinking about going back for a master's. I need to write a ~2 page proposal for a one-year funded project, and this is the half-formed idea I have about the "we should settle other planets as insurance against extinction" argument.

The way that argument gets stated usually treats a Mars colony like an offsite backup: primary disk dies, you restore from the backup. But in reliability engineering a backup is worth exactly zero when it shares failure modes with the primary — common-cause failure. And most of the catastrophes people actually invoke when making the insurance case (engineered pandemics, out-of-control AI, institutional or political collapse) are the ones the colonists would carry with them, along with the tech and culture that produced them. Only a narrower set — asteroid, supervolcano, anything strictly local to Earth — looks genuinely decorrellated.

So the question I want is something like: for which classes of catastrophic risk does off-world settlement actually work as a hedge, and does the answer change if the decorrelation has to be bought on purpose, by handicapping the colony's technology? That last part seems ethically loaded, since the colonists didn't volunteer to be kept primitive, and a colony that can't build anything dangerous also can't defend itself once its neighbours catch up.

Turn that into a real proposal: the question stated precisely, why both the pro-colonization side and the "it's an escape pod for rich people" critics leave it open, what I'd actually do month to month and why a conceptual project beats a risk-modelling one here, what a result would look like, what counts as the project failing, and whether one year is an honest scope. No bibliography, I'll add citations myself. And be straight with me — if you think the backup/common-cause analogy is the wrong frame for this, say so, and say what would change your mind about that.
