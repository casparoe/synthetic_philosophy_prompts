I spent four decades in experimental physics, where "path dependent" had a precise meaning: hysteresis. The present state of the sample is not a function of the present conditions alone but of how those conditions were arrived at — yet for a given history the state is perfectly determined. A ferromagnet's remanence is path dependent and not in the least up for grabs. Since retiring I have been reading the literature on historical lock-in and on whether events were contingent, and I suspect it runs these two ideas together. I would like a case that pries them apart.

Construct a thought experiment to the following specifications. A single concrete scenario, one paragraph, in an ordinary setting — for instance a firm or an industry converging on one of two broadly equivalent standards. No science fiction, no agents with unusual foresight, no perfect information.

1. The scenario exhibits genuine increasing returns to adoption, and the eventual outcome is locked in: reversing it at any later date would be prohibitively costly.
2. There is an identifiable small early event through which the actual course of events runs, such that removing it changes the sequence of steps by which the outcome is reached.
3. Nevertheless the outcome is robust: in nearly every nearby counterfactual history in which that small event is altered or omitted, the same standard is in place by roughly the same date.

The robustness in (3) must be generated by the structure of the positive feedback itself — a wide basin of attraction, or a threshold that all plausible trajectories cross — and not by a smuggled absence of alternatives, nor by making the rival outcome the same thing under a different name.

After the scenario, walk me through the dynamics and verify each condition in turn, noting explicitly that (2) and (3) are claims about different relata. Then say which single feature of the case does the work, what the case becomes if that feature is removed, and give the strongest reply available to someone who insists the early event really was pivotal — explaining what that reply shows about how much must be established beyond demonstrating that a process had increasing returns.
