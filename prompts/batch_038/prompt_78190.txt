I clerk for a state wage-and-hour board and I'm trying to call how we'll rule next month. Track record so far: an overnight hospital nurse who had to stay on site but could sleep between calls — working time, paid. On-call from home with no obligation to answer — not working time. On-call from home but required to be on site within 15 minutes of a call — working time. A live-in building guard — we split it, only the hours he was actually responding to something counted. A mandatory Saturday safety course — working time. An employee who answers work email at 11pm on her own initiative, nobody asked her to — not working time, over two dissents arguing we're smuggling in the idea that work has to look like work. Our statutes language is "any period in which the worker is not free to dispose of her own time," which half the board reads literally and half reads as a stand-in for employer control.

New case: a firefighter who sleeps at the station, draws a small overnight stipend, has to be rolling within 90 seconds, and actually gets dispatched maybe three nights a month. Predict the ruling and say what principle we'd be committing to either way; tell me which precedent is the closest analog and doing the real work, and whether there's a third rule — something about how intense the response requirement is — that fits the record better than both the control reading and the freedom reading. Then give me one hypothetical case I could put in front of the board that would settle which rule we're actually running. American English, please.
