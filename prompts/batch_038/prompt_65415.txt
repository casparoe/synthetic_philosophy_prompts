Ok so this is half history question and half psychology question and I don't know where to file it. Last spring my school had this tradition where the seniors took over the faculty parking lot for a week in April — chain across the entrance, someone brought a grill, everybody knew it was "allowed" because it had always happened and two of the teachers had done it themselves when they were students here. Then in about a month and a half it just... ended. Nobody got suspended, there was no assembly, the paper didn't run anything, no one convinced anybody of anything. One senior said she wasn't doing it this year and why, and within two weeks the whole thing was dead and it hasn't came back. Same shape with a thing my older cousins used to say as an insult in middle school — they said it constantly, nonstop, and now they all insist they never meant anything by it and always thought it was a dumb thing to say.

Here's the part I can't find a word for. Once the parking lot thing was over, basically everyone started reporting that they'd always thought it was stupid. Not "I changed my mind" — always. Including people I have texts from three weeks earlier hyping it up. And I don't think they're all just lying, and I don't think it's ordinary bad memory, because they're not misremembering details of what happened. It's more like once the thing stopped being a thing, the reason they had for being into it stopped making sense, so the old attitude became unavailable to them — not embarrassing to admit, just unreconstructable. My history teacher, when I brought this up, mentioned some historian who argued that killing the slave trade wasn't really about people getting morally persuaded but about it not paying anymore, and I also keep hearing people reference a book about dueling and honor codes. But neither of those seems to be aimed at what I'm noticing, which is the collapse happening with no argument in the air at all, plus the retroactive "I always knew."

So does this have a name? I don't want a label I can slap on it, I want to know what the concept actually is and where it stops. I think there might be two separate things braided together and I can't tell if they're one phenomenon or two: (a) a norm that everybody goes along with and almost nobody privately endorses, which then dies very fast once the belief that everyone else endorses it cracks, and (b) the afterwards part, where people lose access to the attitude they used to have and can't even describe it from the inside anymore.

Format, please:
- **Short answer** — two or three sentences: is there an established term for (a), for (b), and for the combination? And if you think it's really one phenomenon and not two, say so here and argue for it later, don't just split the difference to be safe.
- **Nearest concepts** — four or five candidates, a short paragraph each. For each one, don't just define it, tell me specifically how the parking lot story does and doesn't fit, and what it would have to look like to be a clean example.
- **The edges** — a paragraph or two on boundary cases: what would have to be different for it to NOT be this thing? (like if there had been a real argument, or if everyone kept doing it but stopped mentioning it, or if it collapsed because one person got caught)
- **Name it** — if nothing covers both halves together, coin a term for the combination, define it in one or two sentences, and say what the term deliberately excludes so it doesn't just become a synonym for "people change."
