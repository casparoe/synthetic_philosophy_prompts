I spent thirty-eight years in protein crystallography, for the most part determining the structures of enzymes and of the complexes they form with the molecules they act upon, and since retiring I have been reading philosophy of mind in a desultory but earnest way. My working language was saturated with sentences of this kind: this residue recognizes the substrate; the active site is complementary to the transition state; the chaperone detects exposed hydrophobic patches; the ribosome reads the message. Nobody in my department thought of this as loose talk, though it is plainly talk about meaning and recognition at a level far above atoms.

Recently I have been chewing on Douglas Hofstadter's thought experiment of the careenium, from I Am a Strange Loop, as I understand it: a skull-like container full of small magnetized balls that careen about and clump together into larger rolling aggregates, the simmballs, whose configurations come to stand for things outside the container, in part because the behavior of the walls varies with what is happening out there. His question, roughly, is who shoves whom inside the careenium — whether at the level of meaning the simmballs push one another around, or whether all the real pushing is done by the little balls, the meaningful level being a manner of speaking that happens to be predictive. If I have garbled the mechanism, please say so before proceeding, since much of my confusion may come from there.

Please describe the most important implications of this line of thought for the standing of my own old vocabulary. In particular:

- Does the language of recognition, complementarity, and reading in molecular biology describe genuine causal powers of shape and pattern, or is it only a mercifully compact description of an astronomical number of atomic collisions? What would have to be true for it to be more than shorthand, and is it in fact true?
- Should the answer be the same for the enzyme and for me? When I decide to reach for a cup, is there any sense in which the content of the decision does causal work, or does whatever deflates the enzyme's recognizing deflate my deciding too? I want to know whether one can consistently give different answers, and if so what licenses the difference.
- A simmball's meaning seems to depend on someone outside the careenium to interpret it. My enzymes had no interpreter, unless one counts the organism that was helped or harmed by them. Does that asymmetry matter, and if so which way does it cut?
- Where does the thought experiment actually do argumentative work, and where is it merely decorative? Which features of the careenium correspond to the features that make high-level description predictively useful in real tissue, and which features have no counterpart at all?

Please rank the implications in order of how much they should change the way a working scientist talks, and state plainly which of my habits of speech survive the exercise. I am not asking for references or a reading list — I want the reasoning laid out, and I am quite content to be told that one of my questions rests on a mistake.
