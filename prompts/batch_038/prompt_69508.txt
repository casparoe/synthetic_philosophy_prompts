I read a piece in a general-interest magazine that sums up Zhuangzi's attitude to death as follows: 'Zhuangzi taught that death is not a misfortune but a transformation, one stage in the same unbroken process as birth. When his wife died, his friend Hui Shi came to offer condolences and found him sitting on the ground, drumming on a basin and singing; as he explained, to go on wailing would be to admit that he had not understood that she had merely changed state, as water changes into ice. The reasoning is a sceptical one: we cannot know what death is like, so dreading it is irrational, and the parable of Lady Li — who wept when she was carried off to the royal court and later regretted her tears — shows that the dead probably regret having clung to life just as she regretted her weeping. Zhuangzi thus anticipates Epicurus, who held that death is nothing to us because there is no one left to be harmed by it. His equanimity is not indifference, though: the image of the firewood burning out while the fire passes on shows that something of the self survives in the transformation of things.'
