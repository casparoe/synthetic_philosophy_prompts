For a phil of science seminar I'm putting together a little exercise on natural kinds and I want your judgement on a list, scored against a rubric I made up myself. The candidates: jade, the electron, weed (as in unwanted plant), water, Homo sapiens, clinical depression, the planet, and money.

Score each candiate 0–5 on these four: (i) intrinsic-ness — does falling under the kind depend on what the thing is like in itself rather than on its relations to human purposes; (ii) projective power — how far does knowing something is a member license you to infer unobserved properties of it; (iii) durability — would the kind survive our current best theory of it being replaced by a successor theory; (iv) boundary-discoverability — is the line between members and non-members something you find rather than draw. Composite = 0.35·(i) + 0.25·(ii) + 0.25·(iii) + 0.15·(iv), rounded to two decimals.

Reply with a single JSON object in exactly this shape:

{"items": [{"name": string, "intrinsicness": int, "projective_power": int, "durability": int, "boundary_discoverability": int, "composite": float, "rank": int (1 = most natural), "rationale": string, one sentence}], "misfires": [{"name": string, "problem": string}]}

items sorted by rank, all eight of them, no extras. Then in misfires name the two kinds where you think my rubric spits out something a careful philosopher of science would call badly wrong — a kind it over-rates or under-rates — and say in a sentence each what feature of the case the rubric is blind to. You don't need to cite anyone, I just want your reasoning.
