Hofstadter's careenium is a thought experiment: a frictionless surface strewn with little balls — simmballs — that bounce, clump, and drift apart. At the level of individual collisions there is nothing but mechanics; at a higher level, some clumps track other clumps and the surface itself, and it begins to look as though symbols are steering the balls. I am trying to work out whether I really believe the symbol-level description ever has causal force, and my case-by-case verdicts do not seem to hang together.

- A red light comes on and the cars stop. The meaning stopped them, not the photons. Confident.
- A sign reading "Danger: thin ice" in a language nobody within a hundred miles speaks; a walker passes without reading it and falls through. The sign caused nothing, though it is physically just as present as the light was.
- Two pages bearing identical ink marks, one read daily, one sealed in a drawer since printing. I want to say their causal powers differ — and also that the causal facts are settled entirely by the physics, which is the same for both until the drawer is opened.
- A typo in a contract triggers a lawsuit. Symbol causation, and the strongest case on my list.
- A famous self-referential sentence: I want to say the content of the proof, not the shapes of the ink, is what ended a whole research program. My reason is counterfactual — vary the ink and keep the content, and the same thing happens; vary the content and keep the ink, and it does not.
- But my belief that it will rain makes me take an umbrella, and if you duplicated my brain state exactly while swapping the content for a belief about sleet, I would take the umbrella all the same. The same counterfactual test that credits content above strips it here, and I cannot say why.
- A running simulation of a careenium: its simmballs genuinely collide and genuinely cause one another. The same simulation stored as a complete table of next-states and never executed: no causation whatever, though every fact about what would strike what is sitting right there.
- I also hold that the ball-level story is never incomplete. Nothing goes missing from the physics when the symbols are left out.

My tentative principle is that a symbol causes something only when some interpreter's behavior counterfactually tracks its content. That handles the unread sign, but it makes the traffic light's efficacy derivative on the drivers, and it seems to mislocate the typo's causal weight, which I feel sits in the document rather than in anyone's reaction to it. Test that principle against the list and repair or replace it. State each clause alongside the case that forces it, say what the result predicts for two cases I have not given — a trained network whose weights encode a concept nobody can read, and a careenium paused for a century and then resumed — and be blunt about which of my verdicts a defensible principle must overturn rather than complicate itself to preserve.
