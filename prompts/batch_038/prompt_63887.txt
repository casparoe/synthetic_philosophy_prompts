I drafted an argument for a reading group in philosophy of cosmology, and what came out is a conclusion I cannot endorse. I want a diagnosis of the argument rather than a survey of the literature on the topic.

The target is a class of cosmological theories — eternal-inflation models under certain measures over pocket universes, and more generally any model with a long-lived de Sitter phase — that entail that fluctuation-generated observers, Boltzmann brains, who briefly seem to possess ordered astronomical evidence, outnumber ordinary observers like us by an enormous factor. A number of physicists and philosophers hold that such theories should be rejected outright on the ground that, if they were true, we could not trust the reasoning that led us to them. My aim was to derive that verdict from premises no one in the group disputes.

(1) Evidence E confirms hypothesis H only if the occurrence of E is sensitive to the truth of H: were H false, E would be less likely to arise.

(2) Let T be a cosmological theory that entails that Boltzmann brains vastly outnumber ordinary observers.

(3) Conditional on T, I am almost certainly a Boltzmann brain; hence my total evidence E — telescope images, laboratory records, apparent memories of a low-entropy past — is overwhelmingly likely to be the random content of a momentary fluctuation rather than the output of any process coupled to the truth of T.

(4) Therefore, conditional on T, whether E occurs is essentially insensitive to which version of T is correct: any rival theory that likewise entails Boltzmann-brain dominance assigns E very nearly the same probability, since on all of them E is noise dressed as data.

(5) By (1) and (4), E does not confirm T, and E does not confirm T over its Boltzmann-brain-heavy rivals either.

(6) The reasoning applies one level up. That T entails Boltzmann-brain dominance is itself a deliverance of statistical mechanics, and conditional on T my statistical-mechanical reasoning is equally the content of a fluctuation. So the inference that got me from T to the consequent of (2) is unreliable by T's own lights.

(7) Nothing in (1) through (6) is specific to cosmology. For any hypothesis H, form H*: the conjunction of H with the claim that my evidence was produced by a process not coupled to H's truth. H* is indistinguishable from H with respect to E, and conditional on H* the occurrence of E is insensitive to the truth of H*. So by the same steps, E fails to confirm any hypothesis that entails the existence of a large class of deluded observers.

The upshot is that observation confirms no such hypothesis — not T, and not the statistical mechanics needed to generate the problem in the first place. The verdict of cognitive instability therefore cannot be reached, because the reasoning that would deliver it is discredited by the very theory it is meant to discredit, and by every other theory of the same shape. I cannot accept that. I want to be able to say that a cosmology on which we are Boltzmann brains is in evidentially bad shape, and to say so on ordinary grounds.

Which step should I give up? I would most like to keep (1) and (6), and I suspect the trouble lies between (3) and (5), but I cannot tell whether the real problem is that (3) misdescribes what a fluctuation-generated observer's evidence would look like, that (4) does not license (5), or that (7) simply deploys a familiar skeptical construction that (1) was never intended to handle. Please identify the step you reject, explain why it looked acceptable in place, and price the alternatives. In particular, say what the rejected step becomes once corrected, and whether the straightforward Bayesian route — hold that my actual evidence is improbable on a theory that makes me a fluctuation, update accordingly, and dispense with any special rejection principle — is genuinely available here. If it is, spell out what assumptions it needs about priors over self-locating hypotheses and about which features of my evidence count, since those are precisely the assumptions a proponent of (1) was hoping to avoid.
