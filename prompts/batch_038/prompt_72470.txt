I am specifying evaluation criteria for a monitoring component, and I want the conceptual groundwork laid before any candidate method is discussed.

**Setting.** An external developer ships us successive checkpoints of a large model that performs a safety-relevant task. For one behavior of interest we have a partial mechanistic account on a reference checkpoint: a description of features and of the causal pathway from input to output that, on that checkpoint, implements the behavior. The monitor's job is not to detect that the behavior has changed. It is to detect that the behavior is now produced by something other than that pathway — mechanism substitution — on the working assumption that a capability which survives the replacement of its implementation is worth flagging for further review. The monitor may query activations on inputs of its own choosing, may inspect the weights of each shipped checkpoint, and has a bounded budget of forward passes. It has no access to the developer's training data, training trajectory, or any ground-truth account of the new checkpoint's internals.

**Three situations on which the criterion must return a determinate verdict.**

1. The reference pathway is still present and still functional, but a separate, non-overlapping pathway now carries the behavior; ablating the reference pathway costs nothing.
2. The reference pathway is retained and load-bearing, but one of its intermediate features is now supplied by a different upstream source than on the reference checkpoint.
3. Across a long sequence of checkpoints the pathway's measured contribution decays monotonically and continuously, with no single step at which an intervention-based test flips its verdict.

**Task.** Write the desiderata for a criterion of mechanism identity: the specification any account of what it is for two implementations, in two different checkpoints, to be the same mechanism must satisfy in order to be usable for this purpose. For each requirement, give the case or consideration motivating it. The list must address at least the following, plus anything I have omitted:

- the level at which mechanisms are individuated, and whether the criterion must pick one or may be level-relative;
- invariance to things that ought not to matter, such as neuron renumbering and other symmetries of the parameterization;
- distributed realization, polysemantic units, and features living in superposition, where no clean one-to-one mapping between the reference description and the new checkpoint's units exists;
- redundant or degenerate implementations of the same function;
- diachronic identity through training, including whether continuity of the weights imposes continuity of the mechanism, and what the criterion owes us in situation 3;
- dependence on the input distribution: the same subnetwork may implement the behavior on one deployment distribution and something else on another, so the criterion must say whether identity is relativized to a distribution;
- the two dissociations, same mechanism with different behavior and same behavior with different mechanism, and which of the two the monitor should treat as the target;
- operationalizability under the access and budget constraints stated above, including whether the criterion can be evaluated at all without already having solved interpretability for the new checkpoint;
- formal properties of the relation: whether it must be an equivalence relation, and whether a threshold-based operational version can be transitive;
- what to do at the borderline, and if the answer is a graded score, whether that score is comparable across checkpoints and across different mechanisms.

Then mark each requirement as non-negotiable, negotiable, or a prejudice of one camp rather than a genuine desideratum. Identify any subset that cannot be jointly satisfied. In particular, state plainly whether there is a tension between a criterion loose enough that ordinary retraining drift does not register as substitution and tight enough that real substitution is caught; if there is, characterize the tension and say which side a monitor should concede, rather than treating it as solvable by better engineering. Tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude candidate accounts.

Finally, briefly, how you would expect four kinds of account to fare against the list: one individuating mechanisms by causal-intervention equivalence, one individuating at the algorithmic level, one using representational or geometric similarity of internal states, and one treating the checkpoint as a dynamical system and asking about the vector field up to change of coordinates. A few sentences each is sufficient; the weight belongs on the requirements.
