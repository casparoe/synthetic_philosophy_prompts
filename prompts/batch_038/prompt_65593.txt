I am drafting a short statement of my position on reduction and emergence for a philosophy of science workshop, and I would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is what I currently hold.

(1) The physical is causally closed: every physical event has a wholly sufficient physical causal history.

(2) Higher-level properties — temperature, being a predator, being in pain — are real and do genuine causal work, not merely bookkeeping work. I am a non-reductive physicalist.

(3) Theory reduction, where it succeeds, is the derivation of the reduced theory's laws from the reducing theory's laws plus bridge principles linking the two vocabularies.

(4) Thermodynamics does reduce to statistical mechanics in that sense, even though the second law comes out as overwhelmingly probable rather than exceptionless, and even though any given temperature is realized by an enormous heterogeneity of microstates.

(5) Multiple realization blocks reduction in the special sciences: because pain, or a gene, or a recession is realized by physically heterogeneous systems, no bridge principles are available.

(6) I reject downward causation as incoherent; whatever a macro-property does is done by its microphysical realizers.

(7) Other things equal, the better explanation sits at the highest level at which the pattern remains stable and invariant across variation in the underlying details.

Tell me which of these genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to. Rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging, and for each say precisely what would have to be abandoned or reinterpreted and what I would lose by doing so. End with the single revision that buys back the most coherence. Assume I have no special attachment to any item beyond what the list shows.
