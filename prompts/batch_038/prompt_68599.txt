We are closing a graduate seminar on nineteenth-century aesthetics with a short lecture-recital: a pianist plays a Chopin nocturne, and then one of the students reads a poem before we adjourn. I would like you to write the poem that gets read last. Its subject is Schopenhauer's account of music — the famous section of Book III of *The World as Will and Representation* in which he argues that music alone among the arts is not a copy of the Ideas but a copy of the striving itself, and in which he maps the registers of the orchestra onto the grades of that striving: the crude, slow bass as inorganic mass, the middle voices as plant and animal life, the leading highest voice as the intellectual life of man, with the corollary that the bass must move heavily and is barred from carrying the tune. I also want the poem to carry his treatment of dissonance and resolution as the image of a want deferred, briefly satisfied, and immediately succeeded by a new want.

The form should be strict, and the strictness should do argumentative work:

- Five quatrains, optionally followed by a two-line envoi.
- The rhyme of the final line of each stanza is deferred by one whole stanza: the closing sound of the first quatrain is answered only in the third, that of the second in the fourth, that of the third in the fifth. The closing sounds of the fourth and fifth quatrains therefore have nowhere to land inside the poem. Decide what to do with those two unpaid debts — leave them hanging as the last thing the room hears, or discharge them in the envoi — and make it possible for a listener to tell which you chose and why that choice is the one this theory requires.
- Line length should carry the register: the stanza spoken by the lowest voice must have the longest, slowest lines in the poem, and the stanza spoken by the leading voice the shortest and quickest.
- Let one stanza be voiced by the bass itself, objecting that on this scheme it is forbidden to sing the melody — a weight told to stay crude — and let the debt that stanza opens be one of the two the form cannot repay.

Two further constraints. First, the poem may not use the words *Schopenhauer*, *will*, *Idea*, *representation*, *objectification*, *noumenon*, or *metaphysics*; the concepts have to be earned without their labels. Second, someone who knows the section should be able to point to the lines doing each piece of work — that the other arts give the shadow where music gives the kernel, that music is independent of the perceived world and would in some sense go on without it, that its resolutions are real and never final. Someone who has never heard of him should simply get a good poem about an orchestra tuning itself to say something true.
