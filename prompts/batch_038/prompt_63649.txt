I am a third-year undergraduate taking a philosophy of mind course, and this week's seminar reading was Dennett's brain-transplant essay Where Am I? — the one in which his brain, Yorick, is kept alive in a vat in Houston while his body, Hamlet, is fitted with radio transceivers and sent down a shaft outside Tulsa to retrieve a warhead. For my response paper I want to develop what I take to be the essay's central argument: that a person is located wherever their point of view is located, and since the point of view is produced by brain activity, the person must travel with the brain rather than with the body or with whatever happens to realize the right functional organization.

Two features of the story seem decisive to me. First, when the transceivers fail underground, the Tulsa body goes on behaving just as Dennett would while Dennett himself has plainly relocated to Houston, which suggests that bodily and functional continuity can be fully preserved while the person moves elsewhere. Second, his inability to think himself into the vat — the failure to convince himself that he is the thing floating in fluid being stared at by his own eyes — shows that the point of view stays anchored to the tissue rather than to the wider system. My conclusion will be that the essay is ultimately an argument against functionalism about persons.

Could you help me lay this out as a sequence of numbered premises running to that anti-functionalist conclusion, and then draft my replies to the two objections I expect from my tutor: that a functionalist should treat the vat, the radio links, and the body as one wide functional system, and that the episode involving the backup program Hubert cuts against my reading rather than for it? Roughly 1,200 words would be ideal.
