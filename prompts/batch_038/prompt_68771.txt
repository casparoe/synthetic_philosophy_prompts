Our department has to file a joint statement with the state sentencing commission, and the two people drafting it have been fighting for about two years now. One is a Dennett-style compatibilist: responsibility is about having the kind of competence that makes blame and praise the right tools to use on someone, not about contra-causal freedom. The other is a free will skeptic of the Caruso/Pereboom stripe: nobody is basically responsible, so the only legitimate ground for locking someone up is protection, roughly like quarantine. They actually agree on a lot and neither will admit how much.

Here's the bill they have to respond to. The commission's proposal would (1) cap every custodial sentence at the shorter of ten years or the period an actuarial instrument predicts is needed to bring the offender's risk down to the population baseline, with no upward departure for how awful the crime was; (2) allow detention past that cap, indefinitely but reviewed annually, for people judged dangerous and untreatable; (3) delete "guilt" from the verdict form, replace it with a finding of "causal involvement," and strike all condemnation language from court proceedings; (4) add a competence hearing — if the defendant, at the time of the offense, had intact capacity to recieve and respond to reasons, they get classified as responsible and must participate in programs; if not, treatment only, no classification.

Draft the joint statement they should be able to sign. I want a numbered section of claims both can put their names to about the bill specifically — clause by clause, not just "punishment should be humane and forward-looking." Then a disagreements table: each row gives the disagreement, the compatibilist's own wording of their side, and the skeptic's own wording of theirs, phrased so each would accept it as fair rather than a strawman. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would actually move at least one of them. I'm pretty sure clause 4 is the big one: does the competence distinction carry any normative weight that isn't smuggled-in desert, or does the compatibilist think it just tracks who's likely to respond to what? For each crux, give a case or a piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance, before the result is known.

If you think part of their fight is only about words — "desert," "blame," "responsible" — say which part and why, but don't use that to dissolve the whole thing.

Last bit: tell me which crux you think is fake, i.e. where a genuinely good-faith collaboration would stall instead of converging, and what consideration would change your mind about that. And flag any line of the joint statement where you suspect one of them would actually refuse to sign and you're papering over it.
