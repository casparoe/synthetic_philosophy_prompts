I am a third-year doctoral student in a school of public policy; my dissertation concerns the evaluation of state-level reentry programs, so I attended a briefing last month at our state corrections agency that I had no part in preparing. Two of the presenting analysts — I will call them Marcus and Priya, since I would rather not use their real names — disagree about a finding in their own report, and the disagreement did not get resolved in the room or in the hallway afterward. I am reporting it as a bystander. I have tried to reconstruct both sides as charitably as I can, and I have no view of my own about who is right, which is precisely why I am asking.

The report concerns a twelve-month program of intensive case management, weekly reporting, and employment placement for people released from state prison. The outcome is re-arrest within twenty-four months of release. The comparison group is everyone released in the same window who was not enrolled. Statewide, 11.0 percent of the 2,000 enrollees were re-arrested, against 12.0 percent of the 2,000 non-enrollees. Everyone is also scored on the state's validated risk instrument — a static actuarial score built from the person's record at release — and the report splits each group at the sixtieth percentile of that score. Among enrollees, 1,500 fall below the cutoff and 500 above; among non-enrollees, it is 500 below and 1,500 above. Within the lower band the re-arrest rates are 9.0 percent for enrollees and 13.0 percent for non-enrollees. Within the upper band they are 17.0 percent for enrollees and 11.0 percent for non-enrollees.

Here is the exchange, as close to verbatim as I can manage.

1. Marcus opened with the headline: the two aggregate figures are effectively identical, so the program is not buying anything the state can detect, and the commission should not renew it at its current scale.

2. Priya said that number is an artifact of composition. The two columns are almost mirror images on risk, so the statewide figure is comparing a low-risk program group against a high-risk comparison group. Within each band the ordering flips, and only the within-band contrasts mean anything. On her reading the program helps the lower band by four points and harms the upper band by six.

3. Marcus replied that stratifying on the screening score is illegitimate in principle, because that score is partly downstream of the program itself, and conditioning on something the treatment influences destroys exactly the quantity the commission asked for. He added a second, independent worry: even setting that aside, intake does not enroll everyone in a given band. Field officers preferentially enroll people with a stable address and a job lead, and those traits predict desistance but are not captured by the instrument. So within a band, enrollees are still systematically better prospects than non-enrollees, and both within-band contrasts are confounded just as badly as the headline.

4. Priya said the first point is simply false as a matter of sequence. The score is computed from the record on the day of release, before anyone is approached about enrolling, so program participation cannot possibly be an effect of it; Marcus is importing a caution that belongs to a different situation. As to the second point, she said his standard proves too much: if residual confounding within a band disqualifies the stratified contrasts, it disqualifies the aggregate figure a fortiori, since the aggregate pools across bands that differ enormously. On his own logic the report contains no usable numbers at all.

5. Marcus said he was happy with that conclusion as far as it goes, but the two sides are not symmetric. Both figures may be non-causal, yet at least the aggregate answers the question that was actually asked — does the program as currently operated reduce re-arrest statewide — whereas the stratified figures answer a question nobody asked, since the program has no intention of enrolling the people in the lower band the way it enrolls those in the upper band.

6. Priya said that inverts the relationship between the two quantities. The aggregate figure is a function of the targeting rule: expand or contract eligibility and the same program delivers a different statewide number, so the aggregate cannot be the effect of the program, only the effect of the program as paired with one particular intake policy. The band-specific contrasts are the program's effects, if anything is, and the commission can weight them by however many people it proposes to enroll.

7. Marcus said the bands are not the same objects across the state. In one county, intake staff were instructed mid-year to widen access, so that county's upper band is stocked with people whose records resemble the lower band's; in the other two counties the upper band is much harder cases. Pooling therefore compares unlike people under an identical label, and the program's own operations are part of what makes the label mean different things in different places.

8. Priya conceded that the band-specific contrasts are descriptive and not causal, but said that makes them the best available evidence rather than no evidence, and that the statewide figure remains indefensible on any reading. Marcus said that bias does not come in degrees that let you rank two biased estimates without knowing the direction of each, and that she had given up the point that mattered.

Three things, please.

First, adjudicate move by move. For each of the eight numbered moves, say whether it is right, partly right, or mistaken, and identify as precisely as you can what is doing the work in it. Where one of them is invoking a legitimate methodological caution, say whether that caution actually applies to this design, and if it does not, say what the nearby problem that does apply is.

Second, tell me what neither of them is addressing. I suspect the dispute has a hinge that both are circling — something about what the commission's question even is, or about what could distinguish the two readings of the same tables — but I cannot isolate it, and I would rather you say so than fill the gap with a summary of what they already said.

Third, suppose you were handed only these data, with no possibility of new collection. Is there any quantity you could compute, or any comparison you could construct, that would help discriminate between the two interpretations? If the answer is that no such quantity exists, say that and explain why.

Please write in American English.

One last thing, and I mean it: if you think my framing is confused, or that the task cannot be done well as I have posed it — because the reconstruction is tendentious in a way I have not noticed, because the two analysts are not actually disagreeing about anything substantive, or because the information I have given you is insufficient for adjudication rather than merely inconvenient — I would much rather you say that plainly and explain where the framing fails than politely produce the eight ratings I asked for.
