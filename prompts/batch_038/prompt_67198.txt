I spent three hours last night arguing with a friend about no-self and personal identity and we got nowhere, so rather than re-litigate it I want my own position audited. What I hold, in roughly the order I would defend it:

(a) Momentariness, taken strictly: each moment of experience is a discrete existent that perishes as its successor arises, and nothing — not even a dharma — endures through change.

(b) The person is nothing over and above a causally ordered series of such moments: a name for a continuum, with no owner of that continuum at any level of description.

(c) Continua are individuated by causal relatedness alone: a moment belongs to my series rather than yours just in case it stands in the right causal relation to my later moments. There is no further fact of series-identity beyond that.

(d) The classical Buddhist line on rebirth — that the one who takes rebirth is neither identical with the one who died nor a different being — is a substantive position I accept, not a hedge.

(e) Badness is always bad for someone. I do not accept subject-free value: if no subject is made worse by a state of affairs, that state of affairs is not bad.

(f) Being reborn into a worse condition on account of what I do now is genuinely bad for me, and so prudence about it is rational.

(g) The cessation of the series is the supreme good, and is good in a way that gives me a reason to practice.

And the case he landed on me: call it a clean transfer — rebirth into a life that inherits no memories, no traits, no dispositions, no projects, only the causal lineage. I still maintain that a clean transfer is bad for the one who died. I am not going to tell you which side of that I would cut; that is what I need from you.

Go through the set and tell me what hangs together and what does not. Rank the tensions by severity instead of listing them as equally bad, and for each say precisely what would have to give and what is lost by giving it. Some pairs will look contradictory at first pass; if any dissolve on inspection, say so rather than charging them to my account. And do not survey the options and hand the choice back to me — commit: tell me which single revision you would make, argue for it against the most plausible alternatives, and if you think the package survives intact, show me exactly how.
