ok so ive been reading the AI-safety corner of the internet for close to a decade and the tool-AI vs agent-AI thing is one of those debates that keeps flaring up in comment sections without ever getting written down carefully. i want the careful version.

Pretend two people have just finished a real adversarial collaboration — call them T and A. T genuinely holds that a purely instrumental system (one that only predicts or answers, with no action space, no memory across sessions, no reward signal derived from world-outcomes) is a categorically safer design target, not a marginally safer one, and that this is a real fork in the road rather than a delay. A genuinely holds that the distinction is close to empty: agency lives in the human-plus-model system and/or gets selected into the weights regardless of what interface you ask for, so "tool AI" relocates the alignment problem instead of dissolving it. Several months, good faith, no dunking, both are smart and neither is a strawman.

Give me the document they'd both put thier names on.

1. A numbered list of agreed claims — things both would sign about what counts as an optimizer, where optimization pressure actually sits in a predictive model, what instrumental convergence presupposes, and what "tool" and "agent" should mean operationally so the argument isn't about words. I expect a couple of these to turn out to be surprisingly load-bearing; flag which.

2. A disagreements table: topic | T's wording | A's wording | substantive or verbal. For the verbal ones, name the operational test that would settle which vocabulary is more useful. Each side's column has to be one that side would accept as a fair statement of itself — if T read their own row they should go "yeah, that's me, stated better than I usually state it."

3. The cruxes, worked through one shared case both parties have agreed to use. Here it is: a lab ships something I'll call the Weathervane. Trained only on next-observation prediction over a huge multimodal corpus, no RL from outcomes, no tool use, no cross-session state, no action space of any kind. A human analyst reads its output and acts. It is very good. The two cruxes both sides accept are (i) whether Weathervane's competence at predicting what an agent with goal G would do next requires it to run something internally that behaves like an agent with goal G — and if so, whether that something has the properties A is worried about; and (ii) whether the analyst in the loop actually bounds the optimization, or whether analyst-plus-Weathervane just inherits convergent instrumental pressure one level up. For each crux give the experiment, case, or argument both would accept as bearing on it, each side's prediction written down in advance (with a number and a date where possible), and what each says would move them off their own view — falsifiably, not "if I became convinced."

4. A short section on competition: if T is right that Weathervane-class systems are safer to build, does that advantage survive contact with a rival lab that wraps one in an agent scaffold to buy six months of capability? Both sides say what they'd bet on, and the statement should admit it if they actually agree here more than they thought.

Markdown, that order. No bibliography or reading list, I just want the doc itself, and keep it under about 1200 words.
