i'm on a second-year Kierkegaard module and for the last couple of weeks we've been going through the Two Ages review — the bit where he lays into the press, the age of reflection, and the way everything gets flattened out — and this week's seminar was mostly about the Corsair stuff that set it off. our assessment is a creative response instead of an essay, so i want to write a pantoum, because the form seems to already do what he's describing: the second and fourth lines get handed down into the next stanza as the first and third, so nothing new is ever actually said, it's all recycled talk, and the last stanza loops back to the opening lines so there's no decision anywhere, no commitment, no point where somebody stakes something.

can you draft me one, six quatrains, in the voice of a collective we — think a comment section or a readership or a student paper's audience, something that has opinions on everything and stands for nothing. a few things i really want to get right:

- no mob imagery. no pitchforks, no riot, no faceless crowd marching anywhere. the whole point as i read it is that the flattening is done quietly, by gossip and reflection and the press, and that literally nobody is doing it, so there's no one to blame and nothing to fight. that absence of an agent should be in the poem, not explained by it.
- the we should come across as an abstraction that only exists because no one's an individual any more, not as a real group of people. it can't be held responsibile for anything precisely because it isn't anybody — i'd like a stanza that brushes up against that and doesn't notice.
- the trick i most want is the recycled line changing meaning on its way round: the first time it's someone owning something they said, and when it comes back two stanzas later the same words have gone flat and turned into just another thing everyone's repeating. if an I appears at all, it should only be inside a line that then gets swallowed back into the we.
- ending: strict closure, so it lands back on its own first line. don't bolt on a hopeful moral, but i'd like someone who knows the text to sense that the only way out isn't a better or warmer collective, it's one person stepping out and taking responsibility for what they say — and that the form makes that impossible from the inside.

doesn't need to rhyme, though the repeated lines should be short and quotable, the sort of thing that would work as a headline. please don't use levelling, crowd, public or truth as labels anywhere in the verse — it should enact the thing rather than announce it. and no name-dropping Kierkegaard or the book's title in the poem itself, my supervisor hates that sort of thing.
