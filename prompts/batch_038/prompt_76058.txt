So I got into like a three hour argument with my roommate last night about psychiatric diagnosis and I want a second opinion, because I'm pretty sure he's using a bad analogy to win and I can't quite say where it goes wrong.

He's a psych resident. His line, roughly: a diagnosis is a thermometer reading. The thermometer doesn't change the fever; if it looks like the reading changed the patient, that's a measurement artifact -- expectation bias, the patient performing the label, the clinician steering the interview -- and the fix is methodological: blinded assessors, structured interviews, better calibration, re-test the same patient with a rater who doesn't know the first rater's score. So he says looping effects are just measurement error wearing a fancy hat, basically Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy with extra steps, and once you control for it you're back to the only real question, which is whether the disorder was there to be measured. I brought up Hacking's interactive kinds and he said sure, but the point about thermometers still stands.

Concretely, he works in a child clinic that used to hand out oppositional-defiant labels pretty generously. They tightened the criteria a couple years back and he says the numbers just fell, cleanly, like fixing a miscalibrated instrument. I said: also teachers started referring different kids, and intake parents started arriving using different words for the same behavior, and the pool of kids who can even get in front of a rater changed shape. He said that's still just noise in the instrument.

So -- break the analogy for me. I want the correspondence table first: thermometer, fever, the reading, measurement error, calibration, blinding, the people who manufacture the thermometer, mapped onto whatever actually plays those roles in psychiatric classification (and note where a slot has no occupant at all). Then the disanalogies, and I want you to be picky about which ones actually matter for his specific conclusion (looping = artifact, engineerable away) and which ones are cosmetic, because I don't want to hand him a strawman. A few I'd like pressure-tested: does a thermometer have to cope with the fever having read the thermometer's manual, having joined a group of other fevers, and having a seat on the committee that decides what counts as fever? Does the analogy require there to be a stable reading to calibrate back toward, and if so does one exist here? And what does the thermometer picture quietly import from laboratory conditions -- a single measurer, a measurement that's over before it starts, a patient who has no vocabulary for their own chart?

Also, be fair to him: tell me which parts of medicine the analogy genuinely does survive in, so I know where to stop arguing.

Then test my counter-analogy, because I'm not convinced it's an upgrade. I said a diagnosis is more like a rumor about you that you can't help overhearing -- second-hand, embellished in the retelling, changes how everyone treats you, and eventually you start managing your own reputation. Does that carry what the thermometer can't (the concept becoming available as a way to describe yourself; the criteria shifting because of who's already inside the category; the classification being partly constituted by the people it classifies), or does it break down too -- rumors don't come with medication attached, don't get written into law and school funding, and you can, at least in principle, outlast one.

End with a verdict: thermometer, rumor, or something better you'd propose, and which one I should have reached for. And say whether his practical fix is worth doing anyway even if the analogy is bad -- blind the raters, structure the interviews. My suspicion is yes, which is what makes this argument so frustrating, so tell me what a methodology can and can't fix here and what's left over after it does its work.
