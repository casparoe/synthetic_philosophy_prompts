Retired after 35 years of plant physiology and now spending the time on philosophy, so I want a proper poke at a view I've landed on. Take the role of a professional philosopher of causation who has refereed a hundred papers on omissions, and think it through carefully — rough out your candidate defeaters privately first, then only show me the ones that survive.

My view: nothing ever causes anything by failing to happen. I have a test of my own, call it the handshake requirement — a causal claim is only real if you could in principle point to something that crosses the gap between cause and effect, some thing or process that gets from here to there. Absences have nothing to offer, so "the absence of rain killed my orchard" is at best a loose way of describing what the sun and the soil actually did. I don't want to here that this is just the standard process/conserved-quantity view with new paint; assume the handshake requirement is my addition and grade the whole package.

So what would count against it? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely irritating, and say which ones a serious defender would concede have bite rather than wave off. I most want to know:

(a) whether what does the real damage is a metaphysical argument or a practical one about how science and the law have to talk — I spent my career writing that the absence of a herbivore, or of a rain event, caused a yield change, and control plots are literally absences;
(b) whether the handshake requirement quietly rescues the view by not being applied where it's awkward. I know prevention and shadows are the usual suspects, and I've also been told that if you allow prevention you can't consistently refuse omissions — is that right, and how bad is it?
(c) what my view actually buys that its rivals don't, given that they can absorb all the same cases by re-labelling them.

Then give me a straight verdict: is this position honestly exposed, or have I armoured it until it can't be wrong — and if armoured, is that a sin for a thesis of this kind or a defensible way to hold one?

Bottom line in the first two sentences, then the ranked list. Hard cap of 650 words, please.
