For forty years I worked in non-abelian gauge theories, where a local symmetry is treated without anxiety as a redundancy of description and no one concludes that determinism has been lost; only in retirement have I come across the claim that general covariance has precisely this consequence for general relativity. Before I read the candidates, I want the specification.

Write the desiderata for an account of the ontology of spacetime in a generally covariant theory: what such an account must deliver to count as resolving the hole argument. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it and say whether it is non-negotiable or a demand peculiar to one camp. I suspect that Leibniz equivalence, the prohibition on haecceitistic differences, and the demand that points be individuable without reference to the metric are not equally binding, and I want to know which of them are load-bearing. The list must cover vacuum solutions, where there is no matter field to anchor the points; spacetimes lacking isometries; the constraint that the account leave the count of physical degrees of freedom untouched; and what becomes of the account under quantization.

Two judgements I want stated explicitly: whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, in which case the list is itself a result, and which single requirement excludes the most candidates. End with a short application to manifold substantivalism, relationalism, and sophisticated substantivalism, and tell me where the analogy with an ordinary Yang–Mills gauge symmetry breaks down — in particular, whether the obstruction to choosing a global gauge section, the failure of any smooth gauge condition to select exactly one representative per orbit, has a counterpart here, and if so whether it belongs among the requirements.
