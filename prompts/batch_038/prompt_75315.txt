I am under contract for a book of essays about how I work, and the final chapter is meant to state plainly the assumptions I make about my own inner life when I use it as material. A philosopher friend who read the draft said the chapter does not hold together, but could not — or would not — say where. Rather than argue with him, I should like the argument laid out properly, so I have set my commitments down here for audit. I should say at once that I do not know which of them I could afford to lose; several are load-bearing for books I have already written and cannot now unwrite.

My position, as best I can state it:

(1) For whatever is occurring in my own experience at the moment I attend to it, a sincere and careful first-person report cannot be corrected from outside. There is no gap, in such a case, between its seeming to me that I am in a certain state and my being in it. Much about the mind is up for grabs; this is not.

(2) I accept the confabulation literature in full: the studies in which people confidently explain preferences produced by manipulations they could not detect, and those in which they argue at length for a choice that was never theirs. We have, I hold, no introspective purchase on the processes that generate our judgments and reasons. An account of why I think what I think is a construction built after the fact, and it merits no more credence than a bystander's guess.

(3) Introspection is nonetheless a trainable craft, and I have trained it. For more than twenty years I have kept to the beeper discipline: interruptions at unpredictable moments, an obligation to describe what was in my experience in the instant before the signal, and coaching directed at separating what was actually present from what I would ordinarily have said was present. I have got markedly better at it. I take that improvement as evidence that there is something there to improve upon — an inner domain that yields to sustained attention rather than dissolving under it.

(4) What attention finds is not a presuppositionless given. The vocabulary and the narrative forms I have absorbed shape what I am able to notice in myself; I do not so much discover my jealousy as recognize it under a description my language supplied. To that degree my inner life is already partly an artifact of the books I have read, including my own.

(5) Even so, my examined inner life is evidence about people and not merely about me. A novelist who has attended closely enough to her own motives can say with some confidence what a stranger placed in a given situation would do, and this is a genuine source of knowledge that the novel provides and the questionnaire does not. I feel no obligation to corroborate such findings against behavior or against other people's testimony before relying on them.

(6) Consider a scene I have written more than once. In the middle of a quarrel a man reports, sincerely and attentively, that he is not angry; his jaw is set, his voice is level and cruel, and an hour later his hands are shaking. He is not lying, and he is not self-deceived — self-deception is mostly not a tidy strategy but a failure of attention, and I dislike the term. He is simply wrong about an occurrent state of his own mind at the time he reports it. I believe such men exist because I have known them.

What I want is an audit, not a survey. I have read enough on the unreliability of introspection to know the general shape of the debate, and I am not asking which side of it wins. Specifically:

- Go through the pairs and tell me which conflicts are real and which only look real. Where a pair merely appears to contradict itself, say what distinguishes the two claims — the objects they concern, the level at which each is made — so that I can stop worrying about it.
- Rank the genuine conflicts by severity rather than presenting them as a list of equal misfortunes. For each, identify the smallest revision that would remove it, and say what that revision costs: not only what it costs the thesis, but what it costs me in practice, meaning which of my ordinary working methods I would have to abandon.
- Then tell me the single change that would repair the most, and whether a novelist who made that change could still write the book I am trying to write.
- If one of these commitments turns out to be doing nearly all the work, whether as the source of the trouble or as the only thing holding the remainder together, say which one and why.

Please be concrete: point to the sentence that has to go, not merely to the numbered item.
