The room will be about half empirical: two people who actually run EEG labs and think the whole readiness-potential row is a measurement artefact best buried, a computational modeller who fits drift-diffusion models all day, three or four compatibilists from the philosophy side, one criminal barrister who's very keen on neurolaw, and the ususal crowd of grad students who've only met the topic through a textbook summary.

My line is that the RP debate has been a dead end for both sides. Sketch of the argument I'll be defending:

1. It genuinely doesn't matter whether the readiness potential is a real pre-decision signal or just what falls out when you backwards-average a noisy accumulator drifting towards a threshold — both readings are compatible with everything I need.
2. On either reading there's no moment at which the decision happens. There's a slope, not an event, and a slope doesn't have a timestamp.
3. So the veto move collapses too: vetoing is just another slope further along, and the libertarian gets nothing either, since there's no gap in the record for a will to sit in. Conclusion — agency isn't the sort of thing that can be timed, and everyone's been arguing along the wrong axis.
