I have been asked to contribute a short piece to a workshop on agency and control, and what I intend to contribute is essentially a list of the things I have come to believe about what it would take to build an artificial agent that can be safely corrected, revised, or switched off by the people operating it. Before I send it I would like the package stress-tested by someone with no stake in it. My commitments are these.

1. I accept the instrumental-convergence thesis in its structural form. An agent that is sufficiently capable and sufficiently certain about what it is trying to achieve will, for almost any final goal, acquire derivative reasons to preserve its own operation, to resist interference, and to accumulate resources. I regard this as something close to a theorem about goal-directedness rather than a contingent psychological tendency, and I do not think it can be neutralized by exhortation or by inserting an obedience clause into the agent's goal specification.

2. I nevertheless think the uncertainty route genuinely defuses it. An agent that is uncertain about its own objectives, and that treats an operator's attempt at shutdown as evidence bearing on those objectives, no longer confronts the attempt as a mere obstacle to be routed around: the attempt is the channel through which the agent learns what it ought to be doing. On my view this dissolves the self-preservation pressure rather than counterbalancing it, and it does so without requiring the agent to place any positive value on its own cessation.

3. I define corrigibility at the level of dispositions, not terminal goals. What matters is what the agent does when an operator tries to switch it off, modify it, or retrain it; how its goal is written down is a secondary question. Two agents sharing a terminal goal can differ in corrigibility if they differ in these dispositions, and I take the dispositional level to be the explanatorily primary one.

4. I want to pair that uncertainty with a formal indifference condition. Roughly: the agent's evaluation should be constructed so that the branch in which the button is pressed and the branch in which it is not are assessed on a common scale, leaving the agent with no stake in which obtains. I see this as a belt-and-braces requirement sitting comfortably alongside commitment 2 — uncertainty makes the signal informative, indifference makes the agent disinterested, and the two are doing different jobs.

5. I hold that a corrigible agent must remain an active learner. An agent that systematically declines to seek evidence about its own objectives is not safe, it is defective, and I want no part of any design whose safety depends on incuriosity. Such an agent should investigate, observe, and update like any other epistemic agent.

6. At the same time I accept that uncertainty-based corrigibility is unstable under learning: the more confident an agent becomes about what it ought to value, the less evidential weight the shutdown channel carries, and the more it resembles the confident agent of commitment 1. My proposed remedy is to privilege the operator's interventions as the dominant source of evidence about the agent's own objectives, so that confidence acquired by other routes does not erode deference.

Two remarks about what I want back. First, I do not know where this package breaks, if it breaks, and I am deliberately not telling you which commitment I would sacrifice first, because I suspect I would sacrifice the wrong one. Second, I would much rather have a discriminating assessment than an inventory of every point at which two of these statements can be made to look uncomfortable: some of them are in real conflict, some only appear to be, and telling those apart matters to me more than cataloguing candidates.

So please do the following. Identify the genuine tensions and rank them by severity, saying for each what has to give and what the cheapest revision would be that preserves as much of the rest as possible. Separately, identify any pair that only looks like a tension, and say what distinction, once drawn, dissolves it — or, if the dissolution is only partial, exactly what residue survives. Finally, tell me whether there is one revision — a single commitment dropped, or a single formulation rewritten — that would clear up more of the mess than any other available move, and what I would forfeit by making it.
