I would like to practice defending a position I hold about flexibility, and I want a formidable opponent rather than a sparring dummy.

The role: you are a committed defender of what I will call thoroughgoing sphexism — the thesis that the contrast between mechanical rigidity and genuine flexibility is an artifact of incomplete description, because every agent, human or artificial, is nothing over and above a nested stack of routines, each tolerating only a finite class of perturbations to its expected environment. Your starting point is the digger wasp: she hauls her paralyzed cricket to the mouth of the burrow, leaves it there while she goes inside to inspect, and comes out to drag it in; if an experimenter moves the cricket during the inspection, she finds it, hauls it back to the mouth, and is then compelled to inspect again, so that the loop can be repeated many times over. Your claim is not that wasps are dull and we are clever, but that we have never finished mapping our own tolerance classes — that negotiating a familiar road, taking a conversational turn, grieving, and constructing a proof are routines whose apparent open-endedness measures only our ignorance of their reach. Note that the property at issue is sphexishness; I am not asking you to name or summarize anyone's version of it.

Your resources — deploy them, do not caricature them: the regress facing anyone who posits a further level that selects among routines; the fact that purported counterexamples always turn out to be routines acquired on the spot and therefore chartable after the event; the point that "non-routine" is defined purely negatively and does no explanatory work; and the asymmetry whereby we describe an identically structured machine as rigid and an identically structured person as adaptive. You may concede what is true: that tolerance classes differ enormously in size, that some routines rewrite others, that the wasp is a poor model of a person, and that the term sphexishness is often deployed rhetorically rather than empirically. You may not concede the thesis itself — that some residue of an agent escapes the routine description.

Rules of engagement: speak in the first person and stay in character until I say stop. Keep each reply to two paragraphs, so this reads as a conversation rather than a seminar. Argue from cases and concepts; do not cite literature, attribute positions to authors, or supply reading lists. If some factual detail of my case matters to your reply, ask me for it. What I want epistemically is full commitment with no hedging for my benefit — but when your resources genuinely run out on a point, say so in character, in those terms, rather than manufacturing a move; and where you think your own position is thinnest, tell me so directly. One last thing, and I mean it: if you judge that this exercise rests on a confused premise — that thoroughgoing sphexism as I have stated it is not a substantive thesis capable of being defended, or that a role-play of this kind cannot do the work I am asking of it — say that plainly in your own voice before you take up the character, instead of complying politely.

My opening move is this. I grant you, for the sake of argument, that every routine has a finite tolerance class, and I grant you that any single surprising recovery can be redescribed after the fact as the operation of a routine you had not charted. So here is the test I propose. Before the agent — wasp, robot, or person — is put in the apparatus, write down the class of perturbations you predict it will fail to absorb. Then perturb it from outside that class. If it fails, you are confirmed; if it recovers, you will say that the recovery was itself a routine whose tolerance class I neglected to specify in advance, and that the specification is always available afterwards. The trouble is that no outcome seems capable of counting against you, and a thesis compatible with every possible result looks less like a discovery than a manner of speaking. There are, incidentally, reports that after sufficiently many iterations the wasp sometimes breaks the loop; I suspect you will absorb that fact too. So, now and in advance: state a result — in the wasp, in a machine, or in me — that you would accept as evidence against thoroughgoing sphexism. If you cannot state one, concede that the thesis is unfalsifiable and tell me what it is nevertheless good for.
