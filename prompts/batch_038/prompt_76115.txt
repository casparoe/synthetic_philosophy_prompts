so i'm in a phil of science seminar and we just finished the structural realism week and i've realized i can only hold the view in my head as slogans — the pessimistic meta-induction, the old case where the equations about light outlived the medium they were supposed to be describing, newman's objection, ladyman and ross going further and saying there's nothing but relations all the way down. i want a short story, maybe 1200-2000 words, that actually dramatizes the distinction instead of stating it.

here's what i'm imagining, take it or leave it:

- the setting should be somewhere that theory change is a literal bureaucratic event. like an institution whose whole job is to migrate the official physics over when a new theory supersedes an old one, and the clerks/engineers who do the transfer. the dramatic question is what they're allowed to carry across and what has to be left behind. please don't just re-tell the wave-theory-of-light history as a period piece though — use it as background, or as something that happened to their predecessors and everyone still argues about it.

- one character's problem should be newman's objection, and i want it to land as a real plot beat not a footnote: they sit down to write out the surviving structure of some domain with every intrinsic property stripped away, and discover the resulting statement is trivially true of just about any collection of objects, so they have to go back and hunt for a principled way to restrict which relations count as the ones that carry over. let them fail at it, or half-succeed, in a way that costs them something.

- the ending should turn on the gap between two readings: that there's furniture behind the relations and we simply can never say what it's like in itself, versus that there was never any furniture and the objects just are the relational positions. i want those embodied by two people who have to file or sign or vote on something together by the end of the shift, so the reader feels what's actually at stake in the difference rather than being walked through an argument about it.

no narrator explaining the philosophy to us, no moral tacked on at the end. i'd rather the physics feel right than have a pile of jargon — you can invent whatever the institution is called and what the theories are. and no footnotes or reading list, i just want something i can read on the bus before class.
