Here are the norms I want scored. I've glossed them myself so we're definitely working with the same things, and please don't go hunting for who said what — I'm not after a bibliography or a reading list, just your judgements and your reasoning.

1. **Knowledge norm** — you may assert p only if you know p.
2. **Truth norm** — you may assert p only if p is true.
3. **Belief norm** — you may assert p only if you believe p.
4. **Justification norm** — you may assert p only if you justifiably believe p, where justification is cashed out in terms of the evidence you actually have to hand.
5. **Reasonableness norm** — you may assert p only if it's reasonable for you to believe p; weaker than (4), because you can be reasonable in relying on a good source or a settled disposition without having accessible grounds right now.
6. **Stakes-sensitive knowledge norm** — as (1), but the threshold for knowing shifts with how much hangs on the assertion being right.
7. **Communicability norm** — you may assert p only if p is something you could bring your audience to know (or at least to justifiably accept) by asserting it.

The cases, with the majority verdict from a show of hands in a group of fourteen. Treat these votes as the data, not as the truth — that's part of the exercise.

**A.** A GP tells her patient not to take drug Vantrel with drug Corvalis because the combination is dangerous. **B.** A tenant tells the residents' meeting that the landlord is falsifying the gas safety certificates, purely out of a grudge, with no evidence whatsoever and no idea whether it's true. It happens to be true. *Vote: improper.*

**F.** A university spokesperson tells the press "the department's research quality has improved", relaying a formal finding of a senate committee. No individual member of the committee believes it — several think it's false — but the pooled evidence they voted on supports it. *Vote: proper.*
