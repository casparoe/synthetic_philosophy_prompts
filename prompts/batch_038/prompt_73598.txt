Last week I went to a city council meeting about a proposal to give every legal resident a small monthly payment, funded by a payroll tax that would also catch employers of undocumented workers. I asked what seemed like the obvious question — whether the payment itself would change how many people move here, and which ones — and a guy two rows up cut me off with 'no human being is illegal.' Room applauded, moderator moved on, I never got an answer.

I'm not trying to relitigate the applause line, its not like I disagree with the sentiment. I just want to know what that sentence is actually claiming. Lay out the candidate readings as a list I could carry around: something about 'illegal' being a category mistake when it's attached to a person rather than to an act; something about whether any state has the standing to make mere presence a crime; something about dignity not depending on paperwork; maybe something about international law and the right to leave. For each one, say whether it's a platitude everybody accepts, a substantive claim that's defensible, or just false — and which one you think people actually mean when they shout it at a rally.

Then the part I care about most: does any of these readings have anything to say about a question of magnitudes, like how a transfer payment changes migration flows? My suspicion is none of them do and the slogan just sounds like a reply. Tell me if that's right, or if I'm missing a reading where it genuinely does the work.
