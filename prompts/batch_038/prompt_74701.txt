Here's my opening move. Your criterion looks like it can't fail. Any RL agent maximizes its scalar by definition, so "it would trade other goods to avoid that signal" isn't evidence of anything aversive, it's just the training objective restated in a sad voice. And your best candidate for valence is the reward prediction error, which in a well-trained agent goes to zero — so your theory seems to imply the more competent the agent gets the less it feels, which either makes suffering a symptom of bad training or concedes the signal was never valence. Let me hand you something to work with: call it the detour test. Give the agent a longer path to the same reward that doesn't pass through the state that normally fires the negative signal, and see whether it takes it. If it never does, your valence isn't doing any explanatory work, because reward tracking already accounts for everything; if it sometimes does, you owe me an account of what moved it besides the scalar. I don't see how you get out of that with both hands.
