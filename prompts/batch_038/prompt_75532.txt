I'm about to finish a philosophy PhD — dissertation on the semantics of attitude ascriptions, so I'm comfortable with formal tools but I've never done serious two-dimensional semantics or much metaphysics of mind — and I have roughly four years of postdoc-ish time before I need to have an actual research program to show for it rather than a pile of unrelated papers. I want to spend it on one of the questions that came out of the Twin Earth literature rather than on the thought experiment itself, because I take it that fight is basically finished: everyone now concedes extensions aren't in the head, and Oscar and Twin Oscar are museum pieces. So what's still genuinely live underneath all that?

My naive shortlist:

1. whether externalism about content survives contact with self-knowledge — the slow-switching business, and the related worry that if your own thought contents are knowable by introspection and those contents entail facts about your environment, you can argue your way to empirical knowledge of the external world without doing any empirical work, which is too good to be true;
2. whether there's any coherent notion of narrow content left standing once you accept externalism, and whether anybody actually needs one for explanation;
3. whether reference is determinate at all if all you've got is causal contact plus use — the permutation / inscrutability line — and whether that's a real threat to naturalism or just a logician's curiosity;
4. how far the social-externalist result generalizes: you can be wrong about the content of your own concept because your community's usage fixes it, as in the arthritis case — but what happens with social kinds like race, gender, money or "terrorism," where expert and ordinary usage actively diverge and nobody is obviously authoritative;
5. whether something with no causal coupling to the world at all — a model trained only on text — could have externally determined content. I half suspect this one is a fad question dressed up as a deep one, and I don't want to build four years on it if so.

Tell me which of these is worth betting the four years on and rank the rest. I care much more about the ranking reasoning than the list itself: for each, what other questions turn on how it comes out, whether it's tractable with the tools I actually have, whether it's so crowded that a newcomer can't get a word in, and whether both possible answers would be interesting or whether one resolution would just dissolve the problem and end the debate. Also tell me straight which of these you think are fashionable rather than live. And if you think my framing is wrong — if four of these are all downstream of something I haven't named, or if a formal-semantics background is just the wrong background for this area and I should retrain — say that plainly instead of politely working inside my shortlist.

Format: exactly five numbered items, first rank first, no introduction and no conclusion.
