I am a retired geodesist. For some thirty-five years my working life included applying relativistic corrections to satellite clocks and to very-long-baseline interferometry, always as a piece of engineering arithmetic rather than as metaphysics. Since retiring I have taken up philosophy seriously and am auditing a philosophy-of-physics seminar. Two weeks ago the discussion reached Newton's bucket and the dispute between substantivalism and relationism. I ventured a Leibnizian worry — that if everything observable consists in relations and coincidences among material events, then curvature in a region containing no matter looks like an idle posit doing no explanatory work. The seminar leader closed the matter by invoking John Archibald Wheeler's line, "Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve," glossing it as follows: general relativity has settled the question, since spacetime is a dynamical entity with its own degrees of freedom that acts upon matter; therefore it is a thing; therefore relationism is dead. Several participants nodded. I was not persuaded, but neither could I identify the point at which the argument passes from a true statement about a system of coupled differential equations to a claim about what exists, and my objection was left standing as a piece of pre-relativity nostalgia.

I suspect the difficulty is that this single sentence is being made to do several different jobs at once. Could you lay out, as a numbered menu rather than a single favoured interpretation, the distinct precise theses the slogan might be expressing, and give me a verdict on each? In particular I would like the following:

1. Treat the two clauses separately, since I imagine they carry quite different commitments. On the first clause: is the statement that matter moves as the geometry dictates a postulate of the theory or something derivable from the field equations together with local conservation of energy and momentum, and does the answer change how much metaphysical weight the clause can bear? On the second clause: is the real content here that the metric has degrees of freedom of its own — that it can carry energy, radiate, and be non-trivial in the absence of sources — and is that content any stronger than the truism that the field equations are local and hyperbolic?

2. For each reading you isolate, tell me whether it is a platitude any competent physicist would accept, a substantive physical claim with no immediate metaphysical consequence, a genuinely metaphysical claim, or false. Then tell me whether a relationist of some specified stripe could accept it, and if so, which stripe.

3. Distinguish readings about the manifold — points with identities of their own, which I gather is what the hole argument is supposed to make trouble for — from readings about the metric field. Can one be a thoroughgoing realist about the metric field, including in vacuum regions, without thereby being a substantivalist in the sense the slogan was used to establish? If so, the gloss offered in my seminar simply does not follow.

4. Examine the verb. What account of causation or of explanation would have to be correct for spacetime literally to tell matter anything? Does the reciprocity of the two clauses cut against the inference to substance — if A tells B and B tells A, does either become a thing in the classical sense — or does reciprocity rather strengthen it, on the model of two interacting fields? Is there an asymmetry smuggled into the sentence that the physics does not supply, or an agency that the mathematics does not earn?

5. Say plainly whether the anti-relationist work, if there is any to be done, is performed by the existence of vacuum solutions and propagating gravitational degrees of freedom rather than by the mutual-dependence formulation. Then give me the strongest reply available to someone who thinks spacetime is a role to be filled by whatever the underlying theory provides, rather than a constituent of the world in its own right, and say whether that reply is dialectically respectable or merely a refusal to engage.

6. Note which of your readings depend on the classical theory being the last word, given that the slogan is silent about what becomes of curvature where the geometry itself is indeterminate.

Finally, tell me which reading the seminar leader most likely intended, given how the line was deployed, and whether that reading supports the dismissal of my objection. If it does not, I would like a formulation of my original point that is more precise than the one I offered — something I can say next week that is not vulnerable to being waved away with a memorable sentence. I am not asking for references or a reading list; I want the analysis.
