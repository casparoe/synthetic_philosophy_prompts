ok so this is a weird one and I keep going back and forth on it. I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background, so please bear with me if I frame this badly.

A former coworker is starting a company and wants me as employee #3 to rip out and rebuild their whole data pipeline. His offer is unusual: he'll wire me $150k the day I sign — no vesting, no clawback, nothing in the contract that ties the money to me staying. The only condition is that in the final conversation on Friday he has to come away convinced I genuinely intend to see the rebuild through, however long it takes. He says he's done this twice before, that he's never once been wrong about whether someone means it, and that if I'm planning to take the money and bounce in eight months he'll know and the deal is off. He put it to me like this: "I'm not paying you to finish. I'm paying you for meaning it."

Here's my problem. I want the job and I want the money. But I know myself. Month six of a rewrite is miserable, our second kid will be newborn, and if it gets bad enough I'd absolutely take another offer — and I know that right now, sitting here. So when he asks me on Friday whether I'm commited, the honest answer looks like no. Not because I don't want to, but because the future me won't have any reason to follow through once the money has already landed, and I can predict that.

So I've been trying to engineer my way into actually meaning it. Ideas so far: turn down my other offers before Friday so there's nothing to jump to; sign a lease next to their office; post publicly that I'm doing this for two years; take a lower salary so that quitting costs me more; maybe have my wife hold something. But every one of these is just a switch I can flip later. I can always quietly renew the job search, break the lease, delete the post — and once the wire clears, nothing forces me to stay. So: does arranging the switches between now and Friday actually make me someone who means it, or am I just building a more elaborate bluff?

Three questions, really.

1. Is there anything I could set up in the next few days that makes genuinely intending this possible, or is the deal structurally impossible for anyone who's honest with themselves — and if it's impossible, what do I actually say to him on Friday?
2. If the answer is "make the commitment irreversible," what does irreversible even mean for a person? I'm not a cron job. I can always mint myself new credentials, and I know I can, which seems to be exactly what ruins it.
3. Side thing that's bugging me: there are things I have stuck to for years — the gym, a side project, this one gnarly codebase nobody else would touch. But I never decided any of those in one sitting, they kind of accumulated. Does that count differently than what he's asking for on Friday, or is it the same thing and I'm just fooling myself that I'm a finisher?
