Quick favor — help me sharpen a discussion question for my phil of mind seminar on Tuesday. I want something where there's no safe generic answer sitting in the middle of the road.

Here's the conceptual knot I'm after. There's a distinction floating around — I think Piccinini has a nice three-way cut of it — between a property that's merely *variably* realized (different hardware running the same mechanism, like the several kinds of corkscrew) and a property that's *multiply* realized in the interesting sense, where the mechanism itself differs (spring mousetrap versus glue trap, say). And then there's a third thing, where the inputs and outputs of the mechanism can also be swapped around. My worry is that only the second sort does any real work against type identity or in support of the autonomy of the special sciences, and that when you actually go looking, nearly every example philosophers offer either slides back into the first category or is really a claim about the third one wearing the second one's clothes.

So: give me an example of a mental or cognitive property that you think is multiply realized in the mechanism-differs sense, and walk me through it carefully. Constraints:

1. No warhorses. Not pain in humans versus octopi versus Martians, not billion-person thought experiments, not silicon-for-carbon substrate swaps, not "you could run the same program on a Mac or a PC."
2. Both realizers have to be systems that exist, or that somone has actually built or studied in a lab or the field — not just conceivable ones.
3. You have to say what the shared function is, what specifically differs, and argue that the difference is a difference in mechanism rather than a difference in the inessential stuff hanging off the mechanism.
4. You have to name at least one psychological generalization that quantifies over the property — something you could plausibly see stated in a cognitive science or comparative psychology paper.

Then two extras, shorter:

- Does your example also come out medium independent? If it does, does that help the case or hurt it? I suspect medium independence is doing a lot of quiet work in this literature and I haven't thought it through properly.
- Say something about whether the case survives a fine-grain objection: someone insists that if you describe both systems in enough detail you find shared structure after all, so the multiple realizability was an artifact of coarse-grained description. Point to where exactly that objection bites in your example, or where it doesn't, and why.

And be straight with me — if you think no example can satisfy all four constraints at once, tell me which one is the killer and why. I'd much rather have that than a forced example I have to defend in front of twenty grad students.
