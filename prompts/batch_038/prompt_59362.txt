A midsize employer has just emerged from a botched merger of two incompatible HR platforms, and its personnel records now contain contradictions that three successive cleanup projects have failed to eliminate: at least one employee is recorded as both terminated and not terminated, and the benefits database simultaneously affirms and denies the same eligibility condition. Rather than keep failing at reconciliation, a newly created officer — give them a plausible title along the lines of Director of Non-Explosive Reasoning, and an invented name — submits a board paper recommending that the firm stop treating contradiction as an error state and instead formalize it as policy.

Write that board paper, in American English, as a satirical in-universe document of about 850–950 words. It should look like the real thing: a header block (To / From / Date / Subject / Decision Requested), numbered sections under headings such as background, the proposal, operational consequences, objections and responses, implementation and rollout, and the decision requested; one boxed FAQ sidebar answering four or five employee questions; and a short appendix consisting of a note of dissent filed by the Deputy General Counsel. Keep the register deadpan and authentically bureaucratic throughout.

The satire has two targets at once: the institutional habit of declaring a contradiction officially tolerated instead of resolving it, and the philosophical claim that admitting true contradictions costs the reasoner nothing. Exaggerate real features of the position rather than inventing straw men. A working logician should recognize the view and judge the treatment fair; a reader with no logic at all should still follow the plot and laugh at the corporate absurdity.

The humor must come from getting the logic right. The paper has to handle the following accurately, and without explaining itself to the reader:

1. Explosion as the motivating villain — a single contradictory record classically licenses every conclusion whatsoever — followed by the deflating discovery that the firm's actual exposure over the past two years has been something far more banal.

2. The three-valued semantics being adopted: truth only, falsity only, and both, with acceptance defined as being at least true, so that the both-value counts as an acceptable output. Include one small worked example a careful reader could check by hand. Note in passing that the rival paracomplete system under consideration uses exactly the same truth tables and differs only in which values management is willing to accept — a difference the paper frames as one of corporate temperament rather than mathematics. You may also note, and let the officer gloss over, the mismatch between the gap-free logic proposed for policy statements and the four-valued system already running in the databases.

3. What the firm gives up: excluded middle survives, but disjunctive syllogism and modus ponens for the material conditional do not. Show this biting operationally — an inference the disciplinary process has always depended on that is simply no longer available — instead of announcing it.

4. The denial problem, enacted rather than described: the paper tries to prohibit some specific contradiction outright and discovers that its own negation cannot do the work of ruling anything out.

5. A revenge clause: draft one numbered provision so that it applies to itself, and let the self-referential reading reproduce precisely the trouble the proposal was meant to settle. Have the drafter notice, and deal with it bureaucratically.

6. Curry's paradox as the reason the paper cannot promise conditional-heavy enforcement language (if this policy is approved, the outstanding audit finding is discharged) on a material conditional. The officer must state the trade-off honestly: a conditional strong enough to license modus ponens reopens Curry unless something like contraction is surrendered, so the firm must choose between enforceable conditionals and unlimited reuse of an assumption within a single meeting.

7. At least one officer from data governance who insists they are merely a paraconsistentist and an instrumentalist about the four-valued system in the databases, and who resents being called a dialetheist — with the distinction drawn correctly: paraconsistency is a property of a consequence relation, dialetheism is the thesis that some contradictions are true.

8. The trivialism slope, voiced in the dissenting appendix: once one contradiction is admitted, what principled stopping point remains? Give the advocate the reply a real dialetheist actually has available — that admission is local, driven by particular paradox-generating constructions — and let that reply stand without the author stepping in to declare a winner.

Constraints: no winking, no authorial asides, no footnote admitting it is a joke, and no character whose function is to explain the logic to the reader; the irony should be decodable from the internal logic of the document alone. Do not quote or cite any real text, and do not attribute invented statements, policies, or examples to any real philosopher or logician — no citations, no bibliography, no references, no page numbers. You may freely name standard systems and terms of art (LP, FDE, strong Kleene, explosion, dialetheia, trivialism, designated value, contraction), since these belong to the register. Keep the piece entirely self-contained: the reader should need nothing beyond the document itself.
