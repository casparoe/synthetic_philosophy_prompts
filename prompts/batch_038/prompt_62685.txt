Please argue the following thesis in the philosophy of the social sciences, using cliodynamics as your case.

Structural-demographic accounts of secular cycles — the family of models in which population growth, elite overproduction, the immiseration of ordinary wage-earners and the fiscal strain on the state are supposed to interact so as to generate roughly two-to-three-hundred-year oscillations terminating in political breakdown — come with a characteristic methodological defence. Because the variables in question are dynamical and mutually lagged, the theorist insists, one should not expect to find strong contemporaneous correlations between, say, population density and the intensity of internal warfare; the diagnostic signature of the mechanism is a phase shift, not a co-movement, and a failure to correlate unlagged series is therefore no evidence against the model at all.

My thesis is that this defence, far from being a refinement, marks the point at which the research programme becomes unassailable in the damaging sense. Once the sign, the magnitude and the lag of every relationship are free to be set by the internal dynamics of the model, any pattern in the data — co-movement, counter-movement, or no relationship whatsoever — can be reproduced by some member of the family, and the family is then left with no empirical content beyond the comparatively trivial claim that complex societies oscillate.

Make that case as forcefully as the material permits. In doing so, engage head-on with the two strongest objections rather than gesturing at them: first, that a phase-lag prediction is genuinely risky, since it fixes the ordering and direction of the lag, which oscillations driven by exogenous shocks (plague, climate, the opening and closing of frontiers) would have no reason to deliver; and second, that the programme has staked itself on forecasts published in advance of the periods they concern, and that these forecasts are a matter of public record and can be scored.

Then commit to what you think would actually settle the dispute. State a specific empirical finding that you would accept as disconfirming the structural-demographic account, and explain why it has that status — why it would force abandonment rather than merely license the insertion of a further lag term or a further mediating variable.

I want a definite verdict on whether the programme is empirically contentful, with your reasons laid out, and I would rather receive a decisive case that turns out to be wrong than a balanced survey that is safe. If, all things considered, you reject the thesis you have been asked to defend, argue the contrary position with the same commitment; do not split the difference between the two.
