can you write me a satirical compliance document — like an internal non-conformance report — from a made-up standards body that regulates complex unities: relations, facts, propositions, states of affairs, that sort of thing. The premise is that an inspector has audited one particular whole and found that the relation binding its two constituents has failed to actually bind them, so the whole thing has quietly degraded into a mere list. They raise a non-conformance, propose a corrective action (introduce a second-order relation to unite the first relation with its terms), sign it off, verify it, and then discover the corrective action has itself thrown up a fresh non-conformance one level up, which throws up another, forever.

The point is that the document's own formating should enact the regress rather than just describe it: every section cross-refers to a sub-report, which cross-refers to a sub-sub-report; the root cause analysis section is a "five whys" that runs well past five and just trails off the bottom of the page unresolved; the revision history implies there was already a report on this report.

Things I'd like in there:

- an exemption clause for constituents deemed "unsaturated", which are held not to require joining by any relation, plus a grumpy memo from the audit team who have no idea how you inspect something like that
- at least one incident where a complex was reassembled in the wrong order and nobody could detect the difference, because all the same constituents and the same relation are present either way — that's the directionality problem, so please get it right
- a rejected proposal to reclassify every relation as a "non-relational tie", turned down on the grounds that it relocates the problem without removing it
- a note that the auditor's report on the previous audit is appended, and that the report on that one is also appended

Keep it deadpan bureaucratic throughout — form codes, sign-off boxes, distribution lists, a passive voice so thick you could cut it. About 700–800 words. The humour should come from the philosophy being correct, so that someone who works on the metaphysics of relations laughs rather than winces; don't lean on made-up weirdness for its own sake. And don't name any real philosophers in the body of the document and don't put words in anyone's mouth — invented department names and clause numbers are fine.

British English throughout please.

Then after the document, add a short postscript of maybe 100–150 words on where the piece is least defensible. Plenty of people think Bradley's regress is benign — an actual infinity of further facts rather than a vicious circle, so no non-conformance arises at all. Tell me which clause of the report would have to be rewritten if that's right, what argument or consideration would most change your mind about whether the regress really bites, and flag anywhere you've bent the ideas to get a laugh.
