Ok so I've talked myself into a version of panpsychism where the combination problem is supposed to dissolve rather than get solved, and I want a stress test before I commit to it in print.

The view, roughly: there's no stock of little private feels sitting at the bottom of reality waiting to be glued together. What's fundamental is a network of causal-dispositional relations, and having experience at any level is just what it's like to be a region of that network instantiated at that grain. So subjects aren't the kind of thing that sum — my subject isn't a collection of electron-subjects plus a binding operation, it's the same sort of item an electron is, one level up. Call it scalar monopsychism if you want a label. I'd add that introspection coming up empty when it looks for co-present micro-experiences is exactly what this predicts, so the transparency-based attack on panpsychism just misses here.

What would count against it? I want the defeaters ranked, most damaging first, and separated into the ones a defender would concede actually bite versus the ones they'd wave off. In particular:

- Is there a case it can't describe — split-brain, an octopus's distributed arm ganglia, a cortical organoid, a well-designed AI system? Pick the sharpest one and say why it's sharp.
- Does it collapse into cosmopsychism, or into Russellian monism with the microphenomenal talk deleted? If it does collapse, is that fatal or just an unflattering relabeling?
- Does it predict anything its rivals don't, or has it been qualified to the point where nothing could show it wrong? Answer straight, including whether being unfalsifiable is a real problem for a view of this kind or a category mistake to demand otherwise.

Please don't give me a balanced menu and leave me to choose. Take a position on whether the view survives contact with these objections, say which single defeater you'd bet against it on, and give your reasons. If you think I've built something vacuous, say that plainly.
