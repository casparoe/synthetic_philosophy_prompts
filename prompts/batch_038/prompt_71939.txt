I got 14 years clean and next month my old drinking buddy gets his six month chip — the guy I did most of my worst stuff with, and being honest some nights I was worse then him. He asked me to say a few words at the meeting. Room will be about half people who knew us both back then and know exactly what I'd be talking about, and half newcomers who just see a guy with fourteen years standing up to welcome a guy with six.

Here's the part I cant get straight in my head. Everybody says you can't call somebody out on a thing you did yourself, and a lot of the time I agree with that. But here it feels backwards, like I might be the one person who actually can say it. And underneath that, when I say "that life was killing you" — is that me blaming him, or me blaming myself out loud with him as the only witness who'd understand it? I want the talk to hold both without picking one.

So write it, maybe four minutes. It has to really take up the fact that I'm not better then him, no modest-sounding line that just waves it away. It can't turn into a lecture the newcomers tune out, it shouldn't land as just "proud of you buddy," and please don't have me apologize to him in front of the room, that's not what a chip night is for.
