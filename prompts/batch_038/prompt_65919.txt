Okay so my friend and I argued for like three hours last night about commitment races and got absolutely nowhere, and I figure a clean designed case would settle it better than more talking. Here's where we're stuck.

Thier view: commitment races are basically a pseudo-problem. Any agent rational enough to be in the race at all just refuses to reward a commitment — treats the other side's public pledge as a mere proposal, responds to the underlying situation rather than to the posture — and once nobody gets a payoff for committing first, the incentive to race evaporates. My view: "I will never be moved by your commitments" is itself a commitment, a public and fairly expensive one, and it's not obviously the safe move they think it is. I want a case that actually tests mine instead of me just asserting it.

So: build me a two-agent scenario where BOTH agents do exactly what my friend recommends — neither one caves, neither one is moved by the other's posture, neither is being dumb or hiding anything — and they still end up worse off then they would have been if one of them had raced and committed first. The bad outcome has to come out of the sanity, not out of a failure of it.

Constraints:

- Ordinary human setting. Two firms, two campaigns, two labs, two neighbors, whatever. No AIs, no swapping source code, no acausal anything, no science fiction.
- Nobody threatens anybody. I don't want an extortion case where the disaster is "surrender or we both lose." Each side's stance has to look perfectly reasonable from the inside.
- No private information and no mistakes. Both know the setup, both know the other knows it, and both reason correctly.
- The commitment device has to be mundane and available: a press release, a signed contract, a board resolution, a sunk investment.
- One paragraph for the scenario. That's it.

Then the walkthrough, which is the part I actually care about:

1. Lay out the outcomes and rank them for each agent — both raced, only A committed, only B committed, neither committed, and the cell where both held the sane line and announced it. Numbers are fine, ordinal rankings are fine, but be explicit.
2. Show the reasoning at the decision point for each of them, and show that holding the "never be moved by their posture" line is individually correct for each given that the other is holding it too. If it isn't individually correct the case fails, so don't fudge this step.
3. Point at the one structural feature doing the work — the thing that makes mutual sanity worse than a successful race — and say what it is in one sentence.
4. Now remove it: same stance, but each keeps it private instead of announcing it. Does the race dissolve, come back, or change shape? And if it dissolves, tell me straight whether that means the whole problem was about publicity rather than commitment, because that would be my friend winning and I'd rather know.
5. Last, write their best reply — that the case is rigged, that I've smuggled in something that only looks like sanity — and say whether it lands. If it lands, tell me what I'd have to change in the setup so it sticks anyway.

Don't bother with citations or with naming whose objection is whose, I know the sources. I just want a case that works.
