can you write me a parody of a pharmaceutical package insert — the "highlights of prescribing information" kind of document — for a fictional drug called Poena XR, extended-release just deserts, indicated for the treatment of wrongdoing. The sponsor is a sincere, card-carrying retributivist: they hold that wrongdoers deserve to suffer in proportion to the seriousness of the offense, that desert is a ceiling rather than a target, and that deterrence, reform, and incapacitation are at best side effects nobody should dose on account of. So the insert has to be internally consistent retributivism start to finish. If the sponsor slips into consequentialist reasoning somewhere, that slip has to be the joke and visibly the joke, not just an error.

the comedy should come from getting the philosophy right rather than from wackiness. the format demands things retributivism can't easily hand over — a validated conversion factor between offense-seriousness and units of suffering, a placebo arm, a patient-reported efficacy endpoint, one dosing rule covering both the guy whose identical attempt luckily came off harmless and the guy whose didn't, repeat patients who've built up a tolerance, the question of whether to keep dosing someone who's genuinely repentant — and the sponsor has to address each of those honestly, in the same flat regulatory voice, without ever admitting there's a problem. a punishment theorist should laugh because every section maps onto a real objection and find the satire fair; a layperson should still find it funny. no winking, no throat-clearing setup, and please don't state any of the objections as objections — let them surface out of the boilerplate.

structure it like a real insert: boxed warning up top before anything else, then indications, dosage and administration, contraindications, warnings and precautions, adverse reactions, drug interactions, use in specific populations, overdosage, clinical studies, how supplied and storage. hard limit of 600 words, which i know is tight for that many sections, so a few lines each and cut whatever isnt pulling its weight. no quotes from or attributions to real philosophers, no references section, no named real people or real agencies.
