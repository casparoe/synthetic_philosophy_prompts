Leibniz held that God actualized this world because it is the best of the possible worlds, and he characterized a world's perfection not as a raw sum of goods but as the greatest variety of phenomena achieved under the greatest order — more effect at less cost. Transplant him into a present-day seminar on population ethics and ask him to adjudicate the following.

1. Given that variety-with-order formulation, is Leibniz committed to a total view, an average view, or something that is genuinely neither? Show which of his own commitments does the work in the answer, and say what his criterion would rank in a case where one world has more happy beings but less internal ordering of their relations.

2. Confronted with the repugnant conclusion — a vast population whose lives are barely worth living outranking a small, flourishing one — would he accept, reject, or claim to dissolve it? In particular, does his insistence that the actual world is already optimal leave him any purchase on the question at all, or does it make the comparison merely notional?

3. God's choice determines which individuals exist at all, so no merely possible person can be wronged by going unactualized and none is harmed by being actualized. Would Leibniz treat this as a complete answer to the non-identity problem, or does his own conception of justice as the charity of the wise demand something further — for instance, that God owes it to the unactualized possibles not to prefer a world that is in some respect worse for the sort of beings it contains?

4. Finally, suppose the objector insists that for any possible world a better one can be conceived, so that there is no maximum to select. What becomes of Leibniz's selection principle: does he need a limit-based or satisficing notion of optimality, and where else in his system — theodicy, contingency, divine wisdom versus power — would that revision bite?

Answer from Leibniz's own doctrines and arguments as you understand them, not from generic characterizations of him as an optimist. Wherever you are extrapolating beyond what he actually argued, flag it as such and say how contestable the extrapolation is.
