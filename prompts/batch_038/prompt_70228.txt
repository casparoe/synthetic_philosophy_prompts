So I'm a junior and I'm one of six mods on a mid-size forum (about 11k members, mostly teenagers) for people trying to quit vaping. We don't have anything written down about manipulative posts, we just have a track record, and next week we're voting on a new one — I want to argue from our own precedents instead of from scratch, since half the mod team keeps talking past each other. Here's the record as best I can reconstruct it: (1) we deleted a quit-app's post that said "you've gone 47 days, don't break your streak now," even though the account was clearly labeled as the app's official one; (2) we kept a post from the same app that was just a chart of money saved and self-reported cravings, also labeled; (3) we deleted an account pretending to be a 16-year-old quitter that DM'd people a link to a "natural craving killer" supplement, and the reason everybody gave in mod chat was that it lied about who it was; (4) we kept, with a pinned note, a school counselor's post with really graphic diseased-lung photos, because it also gave the actual statistics and a quitline number and because it went up in a thread where people had asked for "something that would scare me straight"; (5) we deleted a wellness brand's giveaway where staying entered meant posting a daily public confession to your own profile with the full terms behind a link, and mods mostly said "you can't quit this one without losing face"; (6) we've kept a ton of pure testimony posts, like "here's what worked for me, I don't sell anything."

The new case, which is what's got us stuck. A well-funded anti-vaping nonprofit — discloses its donors, isn't selling anything, signs its messages with its real name — wants to DM individual members at the specific hour each member mentioned in their intro post that they usually vape (so like 9pm for someone who said 9pm in the car). The message itself is genuinely reason-giving: two accurate paragraphs about how cravings spike at habitual times and what to do about it, plus the quitline number. No product, no payment, no scare photos, and there's a one-click opt-out at the bottom. What it doesn't say is that the send time was chosen from what the person told the forum, and the org didn't ask anyone's permission first — one mod pulled the timing info from the public intro threads himself and offered it to them, which is a seperate problem I guess. Our faculty advisor, who sits in on votes, keeps saying the real line is whether the message gives you something to weigh, but another mod says that misses the point entirely because it's about whether they used your weak spot against you, and a third thinks it's all about whether you could have said no beforehand.
