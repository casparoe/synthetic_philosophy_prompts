So my ethics bowl coach does this thing every week where she hands out a made-up case and rules on it, and we have to argue both sides before she tells us why. Except she basically never tells us why. She just writes one line on the board. Her record so far this semester:

1. Two people in their early 70s, both widowed, both with grown kids, want to marry. Obviously no babies in the picture, though they have her grandson three days a week. She said yes and wrote "nothing is missing."
2. A 29 year old guy who's been sterile since chemo when he was 19, marrying a 27 year old woman, they say they'll adopt. Yes.
3. Two 25 year olds, completely fertile, who told the clerk straight up they never want kids and are doing it for the tax filing status and because their landlord won't put two names on a lease. No. Her line: "that's a business deal with a ring on it."
4. A man (55) and a woman (52), both with adult kids, and his medical situation means the physical part is off the table permanently, they both know that going in, and they want to marry to combine households and take care of her disabled brother. Yes.
5. One man (40) and two women (35 and 38), all consenting, already raising three kids together, wanting all three of them legally married. No, and her note was about custody and medical decisions becoming a mess.
6. A brother and sister, both adults, both single their whole lives, celibate, wanting to marry so they can share health insurance and jointly take care of their mom. No.
7. A couple where the woman has no uterus, so they'd use his sperm, a donor egg, and a surrogate. She said yes and wrote "still their child, still their family," which half our class reads one way and half reads the other.

The only general thing she's ever put in writing is at the top of the handout: "ask what the practice is arranged around, not what the participants want out of it." And in class she keeps seperating what a marriage can do, what a marriage is for, and what a marriage is, but she won't say which of those she's actually using.

Next week's case: two women, together eleven years, one of them gave birth four years ago using donor sperm and both of them have been raising her as parents ever since, and now they want to get married.

What do you think she rules? I'm not looking for the general arguments about marriage equality, I know those, I want her rule. So: what are the candidate rules that fit all seven verdicts, which verdicts is each one leaning on hardest, and which ones only fit if you stretch them? Then say what each rule gives you on next week's case, and which rule you'd actually bet on and why. And if her record doesn't settle it, what's the sharpest single case we could put in front of her to make the rules come apart.
