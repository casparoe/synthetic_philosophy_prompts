so I sit on the panel at my employer that signs off on how people get sanctioned after a misconduct finding — suspensions, docked pay, that sort of thing — and the chair has asked each of us to send in a short statement of where we stand before we rework the guidelines. mine is basically this:

1. what makes it fair to hold someone responsible is competence, not magic. if a person can take in reasons and adjust what they do in light of them, they're in the responsibility business; if they can't — very young, in the grip of something that shuts down deliberation — they're out. that's the whole thing. no extra ingredient needed.
2. nobody chose their starting equipment or the childhood that installed it. once you actually trace a bad act all the way back, there's no point in the chain where the person authored their own character from a neutral standing start. so nobody ever fundamentally deserves to suffer for what they did.
3. hard treatment is justified only by what it stops from happening. how much of it is warranted tracks how dangerous the person still is — nothing else. which means a guy who's genuinely turned around and is no threat to anyone has to be let back out, even if nobody outside knows he turned around and even if making an example of him would keep other people in line.
4. blaming someone is one of the tools in the kit, same family as praise: you do it because it changes what happens next — his behaviour, other people's, the terms of the relationship. there's no such thing as a blame that isn't trying to accomplish something.
5. and yet. a colleague of mine did something genuinely cruel on purpose years ago, has been clean and visibly decent ever since, is a danger to no one. i cannot get rid of the feeling that things would be better if it went badly for him in some proportionate way. i don't trust the feeling, but i can't argue it away either.

before this goes in writing i want someone to actually audit the set rather than nod at it. which of these pull against each other and which only look like they do? don't flag everything as equally bad — rank them by how much damage they do, and for each one say which item has to be given up or watered down and what I lose by doing it. i'm not attached to any particular one, so don't guess at which i'd drop.

give me your bottom line in the first sentence and then the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my question back at me. and at the end, tell me where your own analysis is most likely to be wrong: which of these verdicts would flip, and on what argument or consideration.
