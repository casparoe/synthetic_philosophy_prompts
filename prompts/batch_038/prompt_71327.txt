I'm working on anthropic reasoning and have hit a variant of Bostrom's presumptuous philosopher that I can't cleanly classify, so I'd like your credence rather than a survey of the positions.

Setup. A coin is flipped. On heads, T1 is true and the universe contains roughly 10^12 human-like observers. On tails, T2 is true and it contains roughly 10^100 — but every one of them is an exact duplicate of some T1 observer, located in a causally disconnected region, with identical memories, surroundings, and total evidence. Before the flip, you are offered a bet that pays only if T2. The presumptuous philosopher, reasoning from the self-indication assumption, holds that the odds are already about 10^88 to 1 in favor of T2 with no empirical evidence whatsoever; the self-sampling assumption leaves the odds at the prior, because the *fraction* of observers in T2 who have your evidence is essentially what it was in T1. So the two assumptions come apart precisely on redundancy: the surplus observers are qualitatively indistinguishable from ones who already exist, generate no new evidence-generating situations, and are not independent lottery tickets.

My supervisor's view is that redundancy is beside the point — SIA still delivers the full boost, and finding that uncomfortable is just squeamishness about a large number. Mine is that duplication instead exposes a defect, because whatever motivates SIA cannot coherently count observer-tokens that are qualitatively identical. They sit at about 0.85 that SIA is correct here; I sit at about 0.35.

Give me your own number on the claim that, upon learning that you exist, you ought to move to odds of roughly 10^88:1 for T2, and then decompose it: the probability that SIA is right about observer counts in general; the probability that duplication is irrelevant given that it is; the probability that the reference-class and individuation problems admit a principled solution on which duplicates count separately; the probability that the presumptuous verdict is an acceptable cost rather than a reductio; and any further term you think belongs. A number and one line of justification for each, then the combination. Close by saying which term the gap between 0.85 and 0.35 most likely lives in, which term you are least confident about, and what argument or piece of evidence would move it most.
