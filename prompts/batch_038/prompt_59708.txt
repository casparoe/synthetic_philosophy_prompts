I have spent twenty-two years writing direct-mail fundraising appeals for a regional charity, first as a staff copywriter and for the last decade as the person who signs off on every piece that goes out. I have no academic training in philosophy. I did not go to university, and what little I know of the subject I have picked up piecemeal and probably badly. But there is one question I have worked at practically every day of my career, and I would rather see the specification of it laid out clearly than be handed somebody's finished answer.

The question is where legitimate persuasion stops and manipulation begins. For me it is not abstract. Every appeal I write uses techniques I could name for you: an unsolicited gift of return address labels printed with the recipient's own name, which reliably lifts response rates because people feel obliged to reciprocate; a reply device on which a monthly amount is already filled in and must be crossed out to be lowered; a closing date that is real in the sense that the mailing genuinely ends, and another that is real only in the sense that nobody would ever notice if we extended it; a story about one named child, chosen because a single identifiable person moves donors in a way that nine hundred statistical children do not. Some of these I am comfortable with. Some I have refused to write. I have never been able to state a principle that explains which is which, and I suspect that many people who write about this have never had to decide under a response-rate target, with a competitor's mailing arriving the same week.

So, before I read any theory, tell me what a theory has to do. Write the requirements that an account of the distinction must meet in order to be of use to someone in my position — adequate not for settling a seminar dispute, but for letting a person decide in advance whether to send a particular sentence, and judge afterwards whether they did wrong. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, drawn as far as possible from situations like the ones above. Then:

- Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are really the preferences of one camp wearing the costume of criteria, and say which camp each belongs to.
- Tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out otherwise attractive accounts.
- Flag any subset of the requirements that you think cannot be satisfied jointly. I have a hunch of my own and I want it tested rather than flattered: a tactic seems to me manipulative exactly when it depends on the target not knowing it is being used — when it would stop working if announced — so full transparency ought to be the criterion. But if announcing a technique destroys it, and my competitors decline to announce theirs, then transparency costs me the donations that pay for the services. An account that demands openness may therefore demand that the honest fundraiser lose. If that is a genuine knot rather than a confusion on my part, say so plainly, and say which of the three — openness, effectiveness, and a fair share of a competitive attention market — has to be the one that gives.
- Say what an adequate account must do about influence with no mind behind it: a system that serves different versions of the same appeal to different people at different hours and quietly learns which version works, where nobody intends anything and nobody could tell you why the winner won.
- Say what it must do about the cases that embarrass a purely victim-centered view: the lie told to a man at the door to protect the person hiding upstairs, the intervention staged for a relative who cannot currently judge his own interests, and the tactic that the target welcomes and would pay for, such as the framing a fundraiser's own staff use on themselves to finish the work.

Finally, and briefly — a paragraph each, no more — how do the main lines of approach fare against your list: those that put the wrong in the influencer's intention, those that put it in bypassing or degrading the target's capacity to deliberate, those that put it in harm or in the disadvantage handed to one party, those that put it in the character or vice of the persuader, and the line of thought associated with nudging and choice architecture, which treats the design of an option as benign so long as the exit is cheap. I care far more about the criteria than the scorecard, so keep that part short.

Please do not give me a reading list, citations, or titles; I cannot check them and I do not want them. Write in your own words, and where you use a term of art, define it in a clause. A numbered list with the motivating case set out under each item is exactly the shape I am after.
