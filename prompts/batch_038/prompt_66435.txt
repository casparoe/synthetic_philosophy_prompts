I am convening a forty-five-minute seminar for twelve second-year philosophy undergraduates whose only preparation is Descartes's First Meditation and Moore's proof of an external world from his own two hands, and my brief is to get them onto the idea of hinge propositions in Wittgenstein's On Certainty. I do not want an exposition of hinge epistemology; I want a plan for the exposition, and I want it to solve the difficulties I can already foresee.

First, the dependency structure. Tell me what has to be in place before the notion of a hinge can be stated without being silently absorbed into something these students already possess. My guess is that the point about knowing being a move within a practice of giving and asking for reasons must come before the claim that a doubt admitting of no possible resolution is not a doubt at all, and that both must come before any hinge is named — but say whether that ordering is right, and more generally what depends on what and what breaks if the pieces arrive out of order.

Second, the carrying example. I want one proposition to run the whole session on, justified against explicit criteria: it must be one that nobody ever asserts outside a philosophy seminar; its denial must be statable yet unresolvable by any procedure the class could actually execute; and it must be plainly distinguishable from a merely very well confirmed empirical claim. Say which of the obvious candidates serve and which mislead — Moore's hands, the great age of the Earth, never having been to the Moon, the familiar hypothesis that the universe sprang into existence minutes ago complete with fossils and implanted memories.

Third, Wittgenstein's own images. The hinge, the riverbed whose channel is itself capable of shifting, the ungrounded belief lying at the foundation of well-founded belief: tell me which to deploy, in what order, which to withhold, and what specific distortion each will plant in a student who takes it literally. I would rather be told that an image does more damage than good than be handed all of them.

Fourth, the misconception map, with the point in the sequence at which each is to be headed off: hinges as foundational basic beliefs in the classical foundationalist sense; hinges as culturally variable certainties, which one student will immediately convert into the conclusion that truth is relative to a framework; hinges as the psychological feeling of being sure; the slide from the hinges being arational to all belief being arational; and the assumption that hinges are fixed for good rather than liable to change.

Fifth, one question I can put at the end whose answers will discriminate between the students who have grasped the idea and the students who have merely acquired the vocabulary.

Finally, tell me what to cut from a second-year session of this length and to announce as cut. I expect the disputes over whether hinges are propositional at all, and over whether they can be known, belong there — but say so explicitly, and say why their absence will not leave a hole the students will fall into.
