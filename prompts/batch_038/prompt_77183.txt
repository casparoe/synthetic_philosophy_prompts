I serve on a nine-member city commission that votes next month on whether to remove a contested monument from a public plaza. We commissioned an outside ethics report, and it concludes the monument should go. Four members want to adopt the report as our finding, on the grounds that we are laypeople and its author is the expert; three object that we would then be unable to say why the monument should go beyond the fact that someone credible said so, and that this is a problem for a moral conclusion in a way it is not for the structural engineer's report on the plaza's retaining wall, which we adopt unreflectively and rightly. Two more note that her conclusion is what most of us already thought before we hired her, so the dispute looks empty.

Please draft the memorandum I will circulate ahead of the vote. It should recommend one course — adopt the report, adopt it alongside a statement of the commission's own reasons, return it for revision, or set it aside and vote on the merits — with a schedule the commission can actually meet; explain when borrowing another's moral conclusion is legitimate and when it is not, and what the difference between the engineer and the ethicist amounts to; take seriously the reply that a right conclusion adopted on authority is still right, and that demanding our own reasoning is process fetish at the public's expense; and say whether reasons written after the fact to match a conclusion we already hold are worth anything.
