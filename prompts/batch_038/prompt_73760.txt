My housemate is four years into a dissertation on Lewis's Convention and she has developed this insufferable habit of pronouncing on whether any regularity you happen to mention "counts." Here's the track record so far, roughly in the order she gave them to me:

- which side of the road people drive on: yes, the paradigm, she'll talk about it for an hour if you let her
- writing English in the latin alphabet: yes
- money / whatever serves as a medium of exchange: yes
- shaking hands rather than bowing when you're introduced: yes
- tipping 15–20% in American restaurants: no. She says it's a norm, not a convention, because her reason to tip doesn't depend on whether other people tip — she'd feel the same pressure in an empty restaurant
- first-come-first-served at a bus stop: no, same reason, everybody including her would rather jump the queue, it's held up by resentment and not by any preference that tracks what others do
- saying "bless you" after someone sneezes: no, and she was weirdly emphatic. Her line was that whatever it used to coordinate has been dead for centuries, so there's nothing holding it up except inertia plus everyone's beliefs about everyone else's beliefs
- QWERTY: no. I fought her on this for twenty minutes because to me it looks arbitrary and self-perpetuating and that's exactly what a convention is supposed to be. She said the regularity isn't held together by typists coordinating with each other, it's held together by typists coordinating with keyboard manufacturers and typing courses, so the population doing the conforming isn't the population inside the coordination problem
- our building's house rule that all code comments must be in English: no, that was a decision by someone with authority, not a convention, even though everyone now follows it without thinking and even though comments in any other language would work exactly as well

Four things I want to spring on her over breakfast, and I'd like to know what she'll say before she says it:

1. Daylight saving. Everybody observes it, nobody can defect alone, the alternative would work fine if everyone moved together, but there's a permanent low grumble and a solid chunk of the country would genuinely rather kill it, and parliment keeps reopening the question every couple of years.
2. Typing gg at the end of an online game. Completely arbitrary — gl hf, wp, or nothing at all would do the same job — but if you skip it people read you as a sore loser.
3. Our company moved to ISO dates four years ago by flat of the then-CTO, who has since left for another firm. Nobody's been told to do anything since, everybody writes the dates that way anyway, and if you asked any of them they'd say they'd rather keep doing it given everyone else does.
4. In our lab everyone still signs the paper logbook by the door, even though the badge readers went in three years ago and nobodys ever read the logbook. Ask anyone why and you get some version of "I assumed other people expected it." Press them and it turns out none of them actually expects anyone to check, and none of them would mind at all if it just stopped tomorrow. They all keep signing it anyway.

For each of the four, give me your predicted verdict with some sense of how confident you are, plus which of her nine earlier cases is the closest analog. Then the part I actually care about: what rule is she running? Write out the rule that best explains all nine verdicts at once, and at least one rival rule that fits those nine just as well but comes out differently on one or more of the new cases — and tell me which single case I should use to force the two apart. Finally, to the extent you know what Lewis himself actually required of a convention, say whether she's running the orthodox package or has quietly bolted on a condition he never had. My suspicion is that the "bless you" and QWERTY calls are her own amendments rather than careful applications, but I can't tell, and I don't want to walk into that breakfast and get flattened.
