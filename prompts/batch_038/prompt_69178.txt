I'm a novelist in the second draft of a book about the deep past, and I need help drafting one scene that has to carry a lot of intellectual weight without turning into a lecture. Some context, then the specifications.

**The book and the characters.** The two principals have known each other for twenty years and have never agreed about anything important. Adaeze Nwosu is a paleopathologist who works on skeletal series from early farming villages; her entire professional life is spent counting lesions, measuring long bones, and reading enamel defects. Julian Reyes is an economic historian and demographer who has just circulated an unpublished working paper proposing a composite indicator — he calls it the Sedentism Composite — meant to settle, or at least discipline, arguments about whether the adoption of cultivation was good for the people who lived through it. Both are serious, both are honest, neither is a crank, and both are personally invested.

**The scene.** They meet in a museum storage room where the trays from Julian's eleven river-valley sites are laid out on tables, and Adaeze has come to tell him what she thinks of the Composite. The whole scene is dialogue. I want you to write it.

**The invented artifact.** The Composite is fiction, and you should treat it as fiction: give it plausible components — mean adult stature, frequency of enamel hypoplasia, caries prevalence, an estimate of crude population density, a grave-goods dispersion measure meant to stand in for inequality, and some kind of workload proxy — and let the two of them argue about the weighting, the aggregation, and what the index is even a measure of. Do not look for a real instrument to model it on, and do not name real scholars, real sites, real publications, or real statistics anywhere in the dialogue. Descriptive allusions are fine and welcome ("the Kalahari time-budget work," "the Natufian material," "the storage literature"), but no titles, no dates, no numbers presented as sourced findings. Any quantitative claim in the scene must be Julian's Composite or Adaeze's own trays.

**What the argument must actually be about.** Not the easy part. Assume as settled between them, and get past it in the first few exchanges, that early farming populations in many regions were shorter, sicker, and harder worked than the foragers they replaced. Their real disagreement is downstream of that:

- Whose welfare the question is asking — per capita or in aggregate — and whether a composite that averages population size against individual morbidity is answering a question anybody ever asked. Julian thinks total numbers are a moral variable; Adaeze thinks he is laundering a summation into a verdict.
- Whether the baseline is a baseline at all. The foragers against whom the farmers are being compared were pushed onto marginal land, are sparsely represented in the skeletal record, and are not a random sample of anything. Adaeze should press this hard, and she should also concede its reverse implication: if the ethnographic foragers lived in refugia, their celebrated leisure hours might understate as easily as overstate the past, which means the "affluence" story loses its evidentiary footing in both directions.
- How you count work, and specifically whether food processing belongs inside the tally — the grinding, pounding, hauling, and fire-tending that a time-budget study of hours spent acquiring food leaves out, and which cultivation multiplies.
- Whether inequality is caused by farming or merely unlocked by it: storability versus perishability, sedentism without cultivation, ranked fishing societies, and the possibility that the causal arrow Adaeze assumes runs the other way.
- The ratchet: that once density rises, the option to go back is foreclosed for anyone but the dead, and so whether the transition was chosen matters to whether calling it a mistake is even coherent.
- Taphonomy and curation bias, argued concretely over the trays in front of them: whose bones survive, who got buried where, what a series of eleven sites actually licenses.

By the final third they should arrive explicitly at the question I care most about — for whom, judged from when, by what standard, can an event nobody chose be a mistake — and they should not resolve it.

**Craft constraints.** No narration and no interiority; at most four bracketed stage directions in the whole scene, each under ten words. No speech longer than about 130 words. At least two genuine concessions in which one of them gives up a specific point and the argument then proceeds from the new position rather than resetting. The word *cost* should be doing double duty — each of them using it to mean something the other isn't using it to mean — and the reader should feel the slippage before either character names it. Neither may use the word *progress*. Neither may accuse the other of a value judgment the other has not actually made.

**Characterization.** Adaeze is not a romantic and must not be written as one: put something unflattering about forager life in her mouth — infant mortality, infanticide, violence, the fact that forager egalitarianism may be a function of there being nothing worth hoarding rather than a moral achievement. Julian is not a booster: he must say plainly that the transition was coercive in its effects, that it delivered epidemics and stunted generations, and that no committee voted for it. The dialogue should be legible to a reader who has never encountered this debate — every technical term defined in-mouth, by the person who needs it defined, without anyone sounding like a textbook.

**Length.** 1,200 to 1,500 words for the scene.

**After the scene, and separate from it,** a note of 200 to 300 words addressed to me rather than to the reader. In it, distinguish what is genuinely settled among people who work on this material from what remains live and contested, and mark any place where the scene leans on a contested reading or takes a liberty — where it compresses several distinct foraging societies into one baseline, treats a regional skeletal pattern as a general one, or hands a character a claim that is really a minority position dressed as common ground. Please do not let the note upgrade a contested view into consensus for the sake of tidiness; where the state of play is that specialists disagree about whether there is a fact of the matter, say so. I also want to know which of the two characters the scene, as written, makes it harder to argue against, and whether that asymmetry is something I should correct in the next pass.
