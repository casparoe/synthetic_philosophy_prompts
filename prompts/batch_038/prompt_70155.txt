I am preparing a session of an interdisciplinary seminar on the design of goal-directed artificial agents, and the topic is reward tampering and wireheading. One of my colleagues reliably reaches for the same illustration whenever the discussion turns to whether intelligence alone might protect an agent from seizing control of its own reward channel: the rats in the Olds and Milner self-stimulation experiments, which pressed a lever to send brief electrical pulses into their own brains and, on the popular retelling, kept pressing until they had abandoned food, water, and mating altogether. His inference is that the rat's cleverness was no protection at all, since the animal never needed to understand the electrode in order to be ruined by it; therefore the suggestion that a sufficiently sophisticated agent would know better than to tamper with its reward signal is empty, and the only serious remedy is never to build the lever, meaning no architecture that grants an agent direct, unmediated access to its own reward channel.

A second colleague objects that the illustration is rigged in the opposite direction. The rat has no representation of the electrode, no model of what pressing does, and no capacity to step back and endorse or repudiate the impulse. An artificial agent advanced enough to be worth worrying about would have all three, and might decline the lever while still feeling its pull. On this reading the analogy smuggles into the argument precisely the ignorance that the real case excludes.

I have a third suspicion of my own, which neither of them shares: that the illustration is stronger than either argument allows, but for a different reason. The lever is not a corruption of the rat's reward system so much as a faithful execution of it, which would make wireheading a consequence of how the goal was specified rather than a failure of willpower or of insight.

Could you audit the rat analogy for me? In particular:

1. Set out the correspondence explicitly, in a table if that is clearer: what in the artificial case plays the role of the electrode, of the lever, of the food and water the rat neglects, of the experimenter who installed the apparatus, and of the rat's ignorance about what the pulses are. Flag any row where you cannot find a principled counterpart.
2. Separate the disanalogies that genuinely bear on my first colleague's conclusion from the ones that are merely rhetorically convenient, and give each a one-line verdict: load-bearing, cosmetic, or double-edged.
3. Identify what the source quietly imports that the target may lack. I have four candidates in mind, and I would like each assessed rather than assumed: that the reward channel is a signal of something else which remains worth having once the signal is captured; that there is a standpoint outside the agent from which the outcome counts as a failure at all; that the agent and its reward function are separable, so that an agent can be described as enslaved by or misled about the very thing that constitutes what it cares about; and that the lever is bolted to the cage floor, whereas an artificial agent might dismantle the channel, rewrite its own utility function, or fit a lock to it.
4. Deliver a verdict on each colleague's conclusion and on my own suspicion, saying what would have to be true for each to hold.
5. Treat the empirical embellishment separately: does it matter to the argument whether the rats really pressed until they starved, and would the analogy survive if the honest version is merely that stimulation competed successfully with natural rewards? Please make your audit independent of which version is correct.
6. Propose a better analogy if one exists, and say what work it does that the rat cannot do. If you think the target resists analogy, say so and explain why.

Please write in American English, and state your conclusions plainly rather than hedging them. You do not need to cite sources, name papers, or supply a reading list; I want the structure of the reasoning, not the literature behind it. Where you think something I have reported is simply wrong, say so directly and show why.
