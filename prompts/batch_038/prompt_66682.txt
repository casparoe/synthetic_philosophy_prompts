Ok so I've been reading about Carnap (the logic one from the Vienna circle, not the physicist) and I think I've found a hole in his whole system, but I have no philosophy training so I might be missing something obvious.

As I understand it his test for meaningfulness was that a sentence has to be either logic/definitions or something experience could confirm, and everything else — metaphysics, but also anything about good and bad and justice and how society ought to be run — is literally without content. Just feelings wearing a trench coat. But then I keep running into the fact that this same guy was on the side of the circle that wanted society run scientifically, like an engineering project, and one of his closest collaborators was a socialist who spent years trying to work out how you'd actually plan a whole economy.

So here's my problem. If all the political stuff is meaningless, then there's no fact of the matter about whether a planned society is better than a market one, which means the planning board has nothing to aim at except whatever it happens to feel like aiming at, and science can only tell you the most efficient route to a target picked by mood. Which makes his planning enthusiasm not a philosophy at all, just him liking socialism and dressing it up in logic. And it seems to mean that when a worker and a factory owner disagree, neither of them is actually wrong, so the arguement can't be talked through, only won by whoever's got the better statistics.

What did he think the planners would be optimizing for, and how did he not notice that his own view makes that question unanswerable?
