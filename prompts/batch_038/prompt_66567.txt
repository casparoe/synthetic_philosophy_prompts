My second-year philosophy of mind seminar derails every year on the last of the objections in Turing's 1950 Mind paper — the one about extrasensory perception. Students see that he called the statistical evidence for telepathy overwhelming, judged that objection "quite a strong one," and proposed to repair the imitation game by putting the competitors in a "telepathy-proof room," and they conclude either that he had lost his nerve or that even great minds simply absorb the superstitions of their era. I am lecturing on the passage in three weeks and would like something better than those two shrugs.

Please analyze it as a case study in how a research programme absorbs an anomaly, using Popper's immunizing or conventionalist stratagems and Lakatos's protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses. Sort his three moves — conceding the anomaly, observing that scientific theories remain workable in practice despite clashing with ESP, and tightening the setup of the test — and say which, if any, is ad hoc in the technical sense rather than the pejorative one. Is the telepathy-proof room an immunizing stratagem, or a legitimate idealization of the sort that shields an instrument from electrical interference, which is what his own analogy of the eavesdropper with his ear to the wall suggests? Does it matter that the auxiliary hypothesis turned out to be false while the test itself came through unscathed: is ad hocness a property of the move, of its subsequent fate, or of the theorist's reasons for making it? And where are Popper and Lakatos simply silent about a case like this?

My own hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis and not as a settled reading: the room is a red herring and the real failure lies upstream, in the credence Turing reports, which no body of card-guessing data could have earned given everything else accepting it would have cost him. Tell me whether that holds or whether I am merely importing a contemporary prior — and then tell me what would change your mind: which consideration would flip your verdict from legitimate idealization to immunizing stratagem, and where you think this analysis is most likely to break down.
