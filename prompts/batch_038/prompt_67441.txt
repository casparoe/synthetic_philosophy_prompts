I've been reading about wildlife management for several years now, and there's a pattern in the objections to helping wild animals that I can't quite pin a name to. It runs like this. When a proposal would benefit the individual animals who are suffering, the reply is that we should leave natural processes alone: predation and starvation are how ecosystems work, the knock-on effects can't be foreseen, and managing nature at that scale would be hubris. Yet the same people endorse enormous, deliberate interventions when something else is at stake — dropping vaccine-laced bait from airplanes to immunize raccoons and foxes, poisoning every rodent on an island to bring back seabird colonies, capturing and relocating whole populations, hauling beached whales back into the surf. None of those get called hubristic meddling with natural processes; they're described as restoring nature. So the "don't interfere" principle seems to switch on and off not according to how invasive the act is, but according to who the act is for: the suffering individual on one side, the species, the ecosystem, or our own preferences about how landscapes should look on the other.

I've seen this written off as an appeal to nature, but that doesn't quite fit, because the objectors aren't claiming that natural is automatically good; they're applying a rule unevenly depending on the beneficiary. Nor is it simple hypocrisy, since most of them would defend the difference if pressed, and some would say a species really does have interests that an individual animal lacks.

Is there an established term, or a body of work, for a norm whose scope of application is set by who stands to benefit rather than by what is being done? I'd like to know which nearby concepts come closest, exactly how what I've described differs from each of them, and which one, if any, actually covers the case. If nothing does, I'd like a coined term with a real definition: the conditions under which such an asymmetry would be a justified difference in moral patients rather than a bias, and one or two situations where your term should deliberately not be applied. A bare label won't help me much — what I'm after is the outline of the thing and where its edges are.
