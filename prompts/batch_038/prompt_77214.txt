Write me a short story, maybe 1200-1500 words, that dramatizes the unilateralist's curse — but not the usual version where one reckless loner in a garage ends the world. Here's the setup I want you to build around:

Some capability exists, and the hard part isn't the equipment, it's knowing the protocol. Once the protocol is public it's cheep to replicate. There are roughly forty independent groups who could each carry it out, and — this matters — every one of them is competent, honest, and genuinely trying to do the right thing. I want the story to make the reader feel why "just trust good people to be careful" gets *worse* as the number of groups grows, not better. Get that arithmetic intuition across in-scene somehow (a conversation, a slide someone's presenting, whatever), not in narration addressed to me.

The protagonist's actual job is raising the coordination cost: making the thing slower to do, more socially expensive, harder to do without at least one other group noticing. Pick whatever domain you like for the capability — I'd lean mundane-sounding (an assay, a fertilizer, a compiler optimization, a survey method) over a glowing vial.

And here's the twist I want you to land: she wins. But she wins by making knowledge less open and by stripping well-intentioned people of the ability to act on their own judgment, and there's at least one character who lost something real because of her success. The ending should leave me honestly unsure whether the fix is better than the disease.

Two rules: don't use the phrase "unilateralist's curse" anywhere in the story itself, and no author's note afterward explaining the moral.
