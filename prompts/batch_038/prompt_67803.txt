Write a short, original joke about the surprise exam paradox: a teacher announces that there will be an exam sometime in the coming week on a day the students will not be able to know in advance that it is that day; a student reasons backward from the last day to the conclusion that no such exam is possible; the exam is then given on Wednesday and genuinely surprises him.

Keep the setup to a few lines and let the punchline turn on the structure of the paradox rather than on a generic student-outwitted-by-teacher beat. Specifically, the laugh should come from the self-undermining character of the student's reasoning: if the induction were sound it would make the teacher's announcement false, so the student cannot at once rely on the announcement and use it to eliminate every day in turn. A brief dialogue or a single setup and punchline is fine, and you may personify whichever element serves best (the student, the teacher, the announcement, the exam itself). Do not explain the joke.

Follow it with two or three sentences on where the piece takes liberties — which features of the paradox you are treating as settled and which rest on a contested diagnosis among people who work on it — so that the joke does not read as an endorsement of one particular solution.
