I'm in a multi-agent AI seminar and my prof keeps reaching for the same recipe — the four properties Axelrod says made tit for tat win his tournaments, which he recites as "nice, retaliatory, forgiving, and clear" — as if it settles what transparent AIs will do to each other. Last week he used it to argue that once two agents can read each other's source code, mutual cooperation is pretty much guaranteed: each will be nice but retaliatory, and since their code is out in the open they're automatically clear, so nobody has to worry about being suckered.

The thing bugging me is that those properties were cooked up for repeated games where you learn your opponent by watching them play, and program equilibrium is a one-shot game where you just get handed the opponent's code. So I want each of the four words unpacked for that setting. Especially "retaliatory": in a one-shot game you never actually retaliate, nothing happens after the single move, so what could the claim be doing at all — something about what the code specifies for branches that never get reached? And "clear" — clear to whom, and does a giant opaque neural net count as clear just because another net can in principle read its weights?

Can you lay out the different precise claims each word could be carrying, and say which are just platitudes, which were only ever empirical claims about the particular pool of strategies Axelrod's entrants happened to submit, which go false as soon as there's noise or no repetition, and which (if any) actually get him his conclusion?

Table please, one row per reading: property | precise claim | status (platitude / empirical-about-the-tournaments / substantive-and-defensible / false-in-this-setting) | what it presupposes (repetition? noise? opaque opponent?) | does it support the inference to guaranteed cooperation. Then two or three sentences after it saying which readings were actually load bearing in his argument, and whether he's entitled to any of them.
