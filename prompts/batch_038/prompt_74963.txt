I want to put a thesis in front of you that I think is defensible but under-defended, and I want the argument rather than a survey of the literature.

The thesis concerns the familiar hierarchy of creature types — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — in which an animal's competence is sorted by the kind of selection process that built its repertoire: genes fix the Darwinian creature's behavior rigidly in advance; a Skinnerian creature's behavior is shaped by the consequences of its own acts; a Popperian creature can preselect among courses of action by running an internal model of its situation, so that its conjectures perish in its place; and a Gregorian creature imports its competence from outside the skull, from accumulated external tools and artifacts. As I read the hierarchy, each step up is supposed to introduce a genuinely new selection process, not merely a better instrument for an existing one. I claim the third step fails that test.

My position: the Popperian creature is not a distinct rung. It adds no new selection process; it adds a new source of input to the selection process the Skinnerian creature already possesses. To make the position arguable rather than merely rhetorical, stipulate the following architecture and treat it as granted throughout. Call an architecture surrogate-reinforcing when it has (i) an internal generative model of its environment that can be run offline, (ii) an evaluator that can be applied to model-generated trajectories and that emits a signal of the same functional type as the one emitted by real outcomes, and (iii) selection machinery of exactly the kind a conditioning animal already has, now driven in part by that internally emitted signal. On this account the deliberating creature is an operant learner whose reinforcers were computed rather than collected, and the difference between the second and third rungs is a difference in the fidelity and reach of the surrogate — a parameter, not a kind. I have read enough taxonomies of cognition that turned out to be one-parameter gradients wearing a costume, and I suspect this is one of them.

Assume the obvious rejoinder is already on the table: a defender of the four-rung view will say that what matters is not where the signal comes from but what can be killed by it — an animal that fails in simulation survives to try again, and that asymmetry between the organism and the hypothesis is a structural difference in the world, not a redescription of conditioning.

Deliverable: exactly five numbered arguments for my thesis, each one or two sentences, with no introduction, no conclusion, and no summary paragraph before or after the list. Each item should be an argument a defender of the four-rung view is obliged to answer, not a restatement of the thesis in new words; the five should be independent enough that conceding one does not cost you the others; and the fifth should be your best direct answer to the rejoinder above. Argue from the stipulation I have given you rather than from authority — I do not want namedropping, doxography, or citations, since the point is to see whether the mechanism I described does the work I claim it does.
