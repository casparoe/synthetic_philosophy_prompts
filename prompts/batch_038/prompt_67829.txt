I am seventeen and in my final year of secondary school, and I convene a small reading group that has spent this term on the problem of future contingents, working from Aristotle's example of the sea battle. Before I present my own position to the group I would like it audited. I am not asking for an introduction to the debate: I can name the standard moves and I know roughly what is at stake in each of them. What I want to know is whether my particular combination of commitments is jointly tenable, and, where it is not, which commitment has to give and what giving it up costs me. Please treat this as an inspection of my own views rather than as a survey of the literature.

Here is the position, labelled so that you can refer back to it.

P1 — Presentism. Only what exists at the present moment exists. Claims about tomorrow are not claims about some further region of reality that is already out there, because there is no such region.

P2 — A truthmaker requirement. For a claim to be true, something must make it true, and that something must be actual rather than merely possible. Truth, on my view, is a status a claim acquires in virtue of how things are; it is not a status a claim can have on its own account.

P3 — Bivalence. Every meaningful declarative claim, including a claim about tomorrow's sea battle, is either true or false. I do not accept truth-value gaps. I find Łukasiewicz's three-valued proposal unintelligible rather than merely mistaken, because it seems to require giving up the law of excluded middle, and that law strikes me as non-negotiable.

P4 — The interference test. This is my own principle and I would especially like you to press on it. A claim about the future is true now only if no action available to anyone could now or later make it false. If a claim is not settled in that sense, it is not true. Crucially, I do not treat unsettled claims as neither true nor false: I treat them as false. On my account, therefore, every claim still receives exactly one of the two values, and P3 is preserved intact.

P5 — Verdicts I have reached by applying P4. It is already true that the sun will rise tomorrow, since no action available to anyone could prevent it. It is false now that there will be a sea battle in the exercise I have constructed, and it is equally false now that there will not be one, since on either side somebody's action could still interfere.

P6 — Deliberation matters. It would be bad reasoning for an admiral to conclude that, because it is already true that there will be a battle tomorrow, nothing is to be gained by ordering his fleet to sea. I want to say that his decision is among the things that make such a claim true or false, not that the claim's truth renders his decision irrelevant.

P7 — I reject the Ockhamist reply. I have come across the response that a true claim about the future does not bring the future about and therefore takes nothing away from the admiral, and I find it question-begging: it appears to assume a settled future and then to assert, without argument, that this settlement is harmless.

P8 — The past is fixed and the future is open, and I take that asymmetry to be basic. I do not think it stands in need of explanation, and I am not willing to surrender either half of it.

P9 — A worry of my own, which may amount to nothing. If P4 is right, then once I have actually decided to do something, the claim that I will do it becomes true; and so it looks as though my own decisions progressively close the future. That seems to pull against P8, though I cannot tell whether it genuinely does.

My request is this. Tell me which of these commitments really do conflict, which only look as though they do, and, for each genuine conflict, what the cheapest repair is and what that repair costs me in turn. Rank the genuine conflicts by severity rather than listing them as equally serious, and say which single revision would clean up the most. Classify P9 explicitly as either a genuine conflict or an apparent one. Where a commitment is not so much in conflict with another as too indeterminate to carry the weight I put on it, say so separately rather than inflating the list of conflicts.

I would much rather you told me plainly if the exercise itself is misconceived — if one of these commitments is not a position anyone could coherently hold, if I have misdescribed or mislabelled a standard view, if P4 is so underdetermined that no useful audit of it is possible, or if you think I have set the task up in a way that forces a flattering answer — than have you work politely inside my framing and produce a tidy audit of something confused. Equally, please do not give me references, a bibliography or a reading list. I cannot reach most of the relevant literature, and I want your judgement rather than your sources.

Please write the whole of your response in British English, including inside the structured output, and please make that output a single JSON object and nothing else, with exactly these fields:

- "overall_verdict": one of "hangs together", "hangs together with amendments", or "does not hang together".
