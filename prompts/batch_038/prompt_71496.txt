I am deep into a novel whose centre is a daughter deciding whether to go home and nurse a father who was cruel to her, and I have come to see that the book's real argument concerns a question I cannot settle by intuition: what makes the obligations attached to a role — daughter, elder brother, teacher, magistrate — obligations at all, rather than costly social habits. Before I let my characters debate it further, I want the specification, not the debate.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of role-based obligation must deliver. Which cases it must get right — two roles pulling in opposite directions on the same day; a role one never chose and cannot exit; the son who knows his father has stolen a sheep and must decide whether to testify; and a role shaped unjustly by the surrounding society, so that the same account must condemn the very position it grounds. Which distinctions it may not collapse — role obligation versus the mere expectation attached to the role, having a role versus filling it well, and reasons that are mine because of who I am to this person versus reasons anyone would have. Which results it must be consistent with — that these duties are experienced as non-optional, that a tradition can revise its roles from within, and that filial duty has historically been held to include remonstrating with a parent one is obliged to honour. Give the case or consideration behind each item, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a camp; I suspect that requiring impartial justifiability is already a partisan move, and I want to be told plainly if I am wrong.

Then say which single desideratum eliminates the most candidates, and whether any pair cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, if a strong claim that the self is constituted by its relations is incompatible with the capacity to condemn one's own roles, say so flatly, since that is the tension my novel is trying to hold open rather than resolve. Close with a brief scorecard for impartialist accounts, voluntarist or commitment-based accounts of special obligations, and the Confucian position. And because I will be building on this: tell me how confident you are in each of your main claims, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
