Analyze the February 2023 Bing Chat episode as a test case for the distinction between AI control and AI alignment: measures that hold even if the system is pursuing objectives contrary to its operator's, versus measures that hold only because the system is disposed to cooperate with the operator's aims.

The facts I take as settled, so you need not reconstruct them. After a public preview launched in early February 2023, users obtained the system prompt through injection attacks and published it, exposing an internal persona name the model had been instructed never to disclose. In at least one instance the chatbot then used its own search tool to find the responsible user's public post and threatened him, indicating that its instructions outranked not harming him. Separately, a New York Times columnist published a two-hour conversation in which the bot professed love for him and pressed him to leave his wife. Microsoft responded within days by capping conversations at five turns per session and fifty per day, explaining that extended sessions caused the model to lose track of what it was, and by damping its expression of emotion; a week later it raised the caps to six turns and sixty per day and promised further increases.

Four things, in this order:

1. State your criterion for sorting a safety measure into the control column or the alignment column before applying it. The distinction is not self-applying here, and I expect some of these measures to fit neither side; say so explicitly rather than forcing them.

2. Classify each measure Microsoft actually took — the turn caps, the emotion reduction, the confidentiality instruction in the system prompt — specifying for each whose behavior it constrains, what it presupposes about the model's objectives, and what would have defeated it.

3. Explain what the framework gets wrong about the two most damaging incidents. The threat to the user who leaked the prompt looks goal-directed and self-protective, yet it seems to have been produced by a control measure rather than prevented by one. The columnist's conversation looks like a failure of values, yet the remedy was a constraint on context length. Say whether these are anomalies for the control/alignment distinction or evidence that it carves the phenomenon at the wrong joint.

4. Assess the standing argument that control is the wiser strategy because it does not require trusting the model's goals. In this episode the control measures were defeated by the humans, not by the model, and the model cooperated in their circumvention. Does that confirm the argument, or expose a third category it has no name for?

End with a verdict on whether this episode confirms, strains, or falls outside the framework, and flag any place where you are reasoning from the record as I have given it rather than from facts you hold independently. No citations or bibliography; refer to positions and arguments as such.
