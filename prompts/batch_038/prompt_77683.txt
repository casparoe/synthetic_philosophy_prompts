I've got a column brewing for my science-and-ideas beat about whether the simulation stuff actually undercuts the do-gooder case for the far future, and everybody I interview just waves at it. So I want to use you as a sounding board before I write a word of it.

Here's the claim I want a number on. Call it **D**:

**D** = if a rational agent came to fully accept the simulation argument — meaning they now put at least a 1-in-3 credence on being inside a computer run by somebody else — then, purely on the strength of that, they should cut their estimate of how much good a far-future-affecting intervention does (lowering extinction risk, seeding space, whatever) by at least half, relative to what they'd estimate if they were certain they were in base reality.

So it isn't "is the simulation argument right" and it isn't "is longtermism right." It's conditional: grant the premise, does the conclusion about value follow? Give me your credence in D as a single number. Don't dissolve it into "well, it depends what you mean by value" unless you then hand me a probability that value is indeterminate here in a way that actually changes the arithmetic.

And decompose it. I want the sub-questions you're really multiplying or averaging over, a rough probability for each with one sentence of justification, and then how they combine into your final figure. Terms I'd expect, though add or drop whatever you think belongs:

- the chance that being simulated doesn't reduce the moral weight of the simulated people at all (if simulated pain is real pain, why would simulated futures be worth less?)
- the chance that shutdown risk is as large as people assume — sims get terminated, sure, but sims also get *kept alive* because somebody found them worth watching
- whether a factor-of-two cut even registers on a view whose whole structure is already multiplying by astronomical numbers. D could be true and totally irrelevant to what anyone does Monday morning
- the probability that there's no determinate fact about how much of the far future's value sits at our level of the stack versus above it

Then there's this one, which is the reason I'm bothering you. A reader sent me what he calls **the interest filter**, and I can't tell if its clever or garbage. His claim: sims only get run, and only get kept running, if they're interesting to whoever's running them, and the most interesting thing a civilization inside a sim can do is reach the point where it builds sims of its own. So conditional on being in a sim that has already survived this long, you're in a sim being curated for exactly the property longtermists care about — the termination risk isn't a discount on our far future at all, it's evidence pointing the other way. Give me a probability that the interest filter substantially cuts against D, and say whether you think it's a real argument or a nice-sounding restatement of "we're still here."

After the numbers: which single term does your figure swing hardest on, and what would somebody have to actually show you to move it? Two people I've talked to gave me 0.15 and 0.85 on D — a philosopher who thinks the whole dampening move is a category error, and a tech guy who thinks anyone unworried about being switched off hasn't thought about it hard. Tell me which term they most likely disagree about, and whether one of them is just talking past the the other.

No bibliography, no citations, I've got my own stack of those. Just the number and the reasoning underneath it.
