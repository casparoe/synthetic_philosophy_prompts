Please compose a villanelle — nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, two rhymes throughout and two refrains repeated word for word, in consistent iambic pentameter or something close to it — for the final session of an upper-level seminar in the philosophy of social science. The term has been spent on one question: whether a creed, a culture, or a set of values can be cited as the cause of an economic transformation, with the ascetic-Protestantism-and-capitalist-spirit thesis as our running case and with the interpretive machinery its author deploys to answer it.

The two refrains must be the two kinds of adequacy that machinery demands. The first refrain speaks from inside the agent's own reasons: the requirement that an account make sense as something a person could have meant and acted for. The second speaks from outside, in the register of frequency, regularity and consequence: the requirement that an account also be answerable to how things actually go. Assign the first to line 1 and the second to line 3, and let them alternate in the usual way through the tercets, both on the A rhyme. Neither refrain may contain any of the words *Weber*, *Verstehen*, *meaning*, *causal*, *adequate*, or *ideal type*: both must be plain English sentences capable of standing alone, so that a reader who has never met the terminology hears only two voices, while a reader who has can say at once which is which.

The non-refrain lines carry the substance. They should take the reader through a believer who cannot know whether he is among the elect and who is directed, in effect, to his own unremitting labour in his calling as the mark he is looking for; the conversion of that search for assurance into methodical work, accounts kept, and enjoyment refused; the accumulation such a life produces but was never meant to serve; and, last, an order that no longer requires any of the reasons that set it going. In at least one tercet, stage the objection that reasons of this kind cannot enter a causal explanation at all, since a reason is not an event and so cannot push anything; in another, stage the reply that without those reasons there is no explanandum left, only a correlation between confessional statistics and occupational distributions whose direction of explanation is still entirely up for grabs.

The form must do the argumentative work. The refrains have rhymed with one another all along while standing apart, and only in the closing quatrain do they arrive as a couplet. Let that adjacency carry the seminar's crux: the reader should come away either convinced that the two voices have finally been made to answer to one another, or shown the precise point at which they cannot be — the believer's reasons on one side, the apparatus his descendants inherit on the other, with elective affinity doing rather less work than its name implies. Commit to one of those readings and make the couplet unmistakably deliver it.

Please write in British English. After the poem, add a note of no more than 150 words stating which reading your couplet delivers, and how the alternation of the refrains across the five tercets contributes to it.
