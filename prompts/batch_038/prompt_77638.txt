I've spent more years than I'd care to admit reading rationalist blogs and arguing in their comment sections, so let me ask in that register: I want a number and its derivation, not a survey of positions.

**The case.** Pain asymbolia is a rare condition, most often following damage in or near the insula — and sometimes after cingulotomy, or, by some accounts, under heavy opioid analgesia — in which people report that they do feel pain, and can localize it and rate its intensity, but that it does not bother them. They do not withdraw, do not guard the injured limb, do not seek relief; some seem unconcerned, even cheerful, while the stimulus is being applied, and a number of them go on to accumulate serious injuries they never protected against. The standard neuroanatomical gloss is that sensory-discriminative processing survives while its link to affective or limbic processing does not.

**The proposition.** Stipulate the case as cleanly as you can: an adult with a stable, well-characterized lesion; pain-intensity reports that are reliable and calibrated to ordinary scales; no denial, no confabulation, no global unawareness of deficit; the person is alert, articulate, and reporting contemporaneously rather than reconstructing later. Let A be:

> A: this person's pain is bad for them — makes them worse off — to a degree not much less than the badness of an equally intense pain in an ordinary person who finds it appalling.

My credence in A is roughly 0.35. My housemate's is roughly 0.8. I would like yours. This isn't idle: I'm trying to work out how much of what "suffering reduction" actually targets is aversion rather than felt tone, and whether that changes how I'd weigh different interventions.

**What I want.**

1. *A decomposition.* Give me the sub-questions your number rests on, each with a rough probability and one or two sentences of justification. At minimum, consider: whether the phenomenal character of a pain includes a negative tone that is separable from any attitude one takes toward it — the tone view versus the attitude view; whether badness-for-a-person requires being disliked, and if so whether an unmanifested disposition to dislike suffices; whether the patient's own standing reflective judgment (pain is generally bad, I simply don't mind this instance) supplies the relevant attitude; how confident we should be in the empirical picture — that aversion is genuinely absent from experience rather than present but cut off from report, motivation, and memory; and whether prudential badness and impersonal badness come apart here. Add or drop terms as you see fit.

2. *The combination.* Show how you get from those numbers to your headline credence, and be explicit about which factors are correlated rather than independent instead of multiplying as if they were.

3. *Sensitivity.* Which term moves the headline number most, and what evidence — actually obtainable, not merely conceivable — would move it. Say what would count: trained first-person reporting from asymbolic patients, preserved autonomic or conditioning responses, the pattern of accumulated injuries, lesion-mapping across cases, something else. Then tell me where you think the gap between 0.35 and 0.8 most likely sits, i.e. which factor my housemate and I are valuing differently.

4. *Two variants, to test whether your factors are doing any work.* (i) The negative tone is intact and the person is genuinely averse to it, but the aversion is disconnected from motivation and from verbal report — they feel awful and sincerely say they don't mind. (ii) The tone and the occurrent aversion are both gone, but the person retains a standing preference not to be in such states. Give your credence in the analogue of A for each, say which variant moves you further, and say what that reveals about which view you are in fact committed to.

Format: a compact table of factors with numbers, then prose. Around 800 words. Please don't open by observing that this is a contested question on which reasonable people differ; and if you think my factorization is the wrong one, replace it rather than padding it.
