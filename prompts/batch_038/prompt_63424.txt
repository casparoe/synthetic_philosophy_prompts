I'm in a reading group on philosophy of mind — mostly people from other departments, so nobody's a specialist — and last week somebody passed around one of those long glossy magazine pieces about consciousness and physics and it made a claim I can't stop chewing on. The relevant stretch runs like this:

'Physics is causally closed: every physical event has a physical cause sufficient to produce it, so there is simply nowhere in the causal order for a non-physical ingredient to get a grip. Which is why anyone who insists that raw feels are something over and above brain activity gets pushed toward epiphenomenalism — the experiences are real but causally idle, hanging off the physical chain the way steam hangs off a locomotive whistle. Frank Jackson's Mary makes the same point from the other side: she knows every physical fact about colour vision and still learns something new when she walks out of the lab, so the physical story demonstrably leaves something out. But here's the trap: if my seeing red doesn't cause anything, then it didn't cause my saying "red," or my typing this paragraph, or my conviction that I have an inner life at all — the neurons did all of that on their own. My reports about my own experience are then true only by luck, in the way that a coin coming up heads a thousand times running is still just a coin, and this is what philosophers call the paradox of phenomenal judgment; so epiphenomenalism isn't merely unfinished, it self-destructs, and the only honest move left is to stop positing qualia altogether.'

For what it's worth I half-buy it — I do find it unsettling that a view could turn its own proponents' reports into a standing miracle — but one guy in the group insisted the paradox is overhyped and that physicalists are in exactly the same boat with their own reports, and I couldnt follow what he meant. Don't send me off to the literature, I just want the reasoning laid out.

If a sentence is basically fine, say so in a clause instead of padding it out, and if the guy in my group has a point, work it into whichever item it belongs to.
