I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm guest-hosting an episode with a moral psychologist whose new book argues that the cross-cultural differences everyone makes such a big deal of — WEIRD samples, purity and authority mattering enormously in some places and being almost invisible in others — are really just differences in how the parameters of one shared, innate moral grammar get set. Her line is that the variation is evidence for universality, and that universality then does real work for moral objectivity. I want to put serious pressure on both of those steps, and I've got about forty minutes.

Give me nine or ten interview questions. Nothing generic, no "what got you into this." I want at least one on: the self-report problem — the foundation questionnaires ask people to rate sentences whose content is already culture-specific (flags, corpses, livestock), so a low rating could mean the foundation isn't there or just mean the sentence doesn't describe anything that matters locally; what the poverty-of-stimulus argument for a moral grammar actually is, seperate from the syntactic one it's modeled on; moral dumbfounding, and whether people condemning harmless-but-disgusting acts shows a second foundation at work or just a gut reaction that never had moral content in the first place; whether the moral/conventional/personal distinction is itself something you only get if you interview Western children; why an innate universal mechanism gives you any reason to think it tracks truth rather than just coordinating groups; and moral change — if the grammar is fixed, what exactly is happening when a society drops slavery within a couple of generations. End with one on falsifiability: what finding would make her give the view up.

For each question give me the follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. She's very good at retreating to 'the input changed, not the grammar,' so make sure at least two of the follow-ups make that retreat cost her something.

Format: a JSON array of objects, one per question, with exactly these fields — "question" (the words I'd actually say out loud), "target" (the specific commitment in her view this presses, one line), "likely_answer" (her most probable reply, in her voice, one or two sentences), "followup" (the follow-up keyed to that reply), "risk" (how she escapes it, or what I lose if the follow-up backfires). Valid JSON, no prose before or after.
