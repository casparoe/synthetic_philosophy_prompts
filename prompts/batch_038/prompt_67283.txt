I'm three years into a biostat PhD and the conceptual chapter of my dissertation keeps coming back from my advisor with the same note: "this is a retelling of the standard story, not a position." The chapter is on Simpson's paradox — specifically on what an account of the resolution has to deliver before I'm allowed to call it adequate. Before I write another paragraph of it, I want the requirements list, and I want you to draft it for me.

Set the bar by purpose, because adequacy is relative to one: the account has to be good enough that a working epidemiologist who is not a causal-inference person can use it to decide, from a dataset sitting in front of them, which table to act on, and good enough that a reviewer from the potential-outcomes camp can't dismiss it as graph mysticism. Then give me the requirements, each with the concrete case that motivates it rather than a slogan. The cases I know I need covered: the Berkeley admissions setup where the aggregate and every department point different directions; the cases where disaggregating is itself the mistake, because the variable you're conditioning on sits downstream of treatment or is a collider; the randomized-trial cases where the crude and the conditional odds ratio disagree even though nothing whatever is confounded — the non-collapsability cases — and where the apparent effect shrinks or drifts across follow-up because the frailest subjects already had their event; and the fact that you can always condition on one more variable, so any account owes me a stopping rule. I also think it has to keep two questions apart that people constantly fuse: which table describes the population, and which table answers the decision I'm actually facing.

Mark which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are just the preferences of one camp wearing a lab coat. And here's the part I want most: if some subset can't be jointly satisfied, say so and show which one has to go — my suspicion is that something like "readable off the data alone" plus "picks the right table in every case" plus "doesn't smuggle in causal assumptions the data can't supply" is a trilemma, and if it is, the list stops being a shopping list and starts being a result. End with a single line on which one requirement knocks out the most candidate accounts. No bibliography and don't guess at citations, I'll put those in myself.
