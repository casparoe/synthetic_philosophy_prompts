My student government is putting three questions to a school-wide vote in the spring, and I have been asked to settle who gets to vote before anyone starts arguing about the questions themselves. Two features of our situation are unusual, and I want the specification to handle them rather than assume them away. First, our electorate is not fixed by the vote: one question would end boarding at the school, which would remove roughly a third of the students who would otherwise be casting ballots, and another would extend the franchise to non-teaching staff beginning the following year, so that the outcome changes who decides the next round. Second, nobody here can say what would make an answer correct, so the debate has dissolved into people trading single examples at each other.

So: write the desiderata for a theory of the boundary of the demos, that is, the requirements any account of who belongs to the electorate must meet in order to count as adequate. Give exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, each naming the requirement together with the case or consideration behind it and marking it either as non-negotiable or as a demand peculiar to one camp in this debate. At least one item should say what a theory owes us when the outcome of a boundary decision alters the composition of the body that decided it, and if any two of your five turn out to be unsatisfiable together, state that inside the relevant item rather than leaving it to me to notice. No introduction, no conclusion, and no survey of who has argued for what. After the five items, add one separate line saying how confident you are in the list as a whole, which item you are least sure of, and which one you think rules out the largest number of otherwise attractive accounts. Please write in American English.
