I am a novelist, and one chapter of the book in progress has come loose in my imagination and now wants to exist on its own. I would like you to write it for me as a self-contained short story of roughly two thousand words. The chapter stages, silently and without naming anyone or anything philosophical, the old quarrel between the view that human nature carries four sprouts needing only tending and the view that it is warped timber which nothing but the straightening-board of ritual and training can force true. The scene is a steam-bending shop where chair parts are made: the bending forms and the clamps, the box of steam, the grain, the pieces that spring back overnight, the one that splits under the strap. I want the argument to live entirely in that material, so that no character is ever permitted to state a position.

Beyond that, the story has to run up against one specific difficulty, and this is the part I have not been able to solve myself. If the straightening is wholly the work of the craft and the timber is crooked through and through, then what was the first bender made of? He was steamed by no one, and there was no form already in the shop to fit him to. I do not want this difficulty answered in dialogue or in narration, and I do not want the plot to hand the victory to either side; I want a reader to close the story unable to say which of the two is right, and yet certain that the question passed through their hands. Please give me the story itself, not an outline and not an account of your method.
