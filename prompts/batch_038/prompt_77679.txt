I've been arguing with a Norwegian friend for months about whether Sweden or Norway is the more liberal country, and somewhere along the way I stopped being able to tell whether my own side of it is even coherent. So here's the whole package laid out, and I want an audit of it rather than an answer to the original question:

1. There's a determinate answer. One of them genuinely is more liberal than the other, and that's the sort of thing evidence could in principle settle — it isn't just a matter of stipulating a definition and then reading off the winner.

2. At the same time I think "liberal" is essentially contested in roughly Gallie's sense: two people can agree on every descriptive fact about both countries and still reasonably disagree about the ranking, because the concept bundles several dimensions together with no weighting built into it.

3. When pressed for my own metric I say negative liberty. What matters is how much the state can make you do things or stop you from doing them, how much of your income and your associations it gets to dictate. What people are actually able to become or do with the freedom they've got doesn't enter into the measure.

4. Nonetheless when I actually rank the two I put Norway ahead, and if I'm honest my reasons are things like: a Norwegian who loses their job has far more real capacity to retrain and hold out for a decent offer; Norwegian parents can actually use the leave they're entitled to; and the Swedish system of for-profit welfare providers leaves some people with an option that is formally available but practically unusable.

5. I don't trust the indices to settle it — Freedom House, Fraser, V-Dem, whatever — because each one bakes in a particular conception of freedom, and I don't want a operationalization to answer what is partly a conceptual question.

6. What I would accept as settling it is the veil test. Take a large sample of people, strip out everything they know about their own talents, tastes, income prospects and social position, ask each of them which of the two countries they'd choose to be born into, and whichever country wins that poll is the more liberal one.

7. And separately: if the actual flow of emigration runs more strongly from Sweden into Norway than from Norway into Sweden, I'd count that as good evidence for Norway too.

8. I'm also fine with a country being less free on one dimension and more free overall, so the fact that the dimensions come apart doesn't bother me.

Take all my descriptive claims about the two countries as stipulated — don't fact-check me or give me a briefing on Scandinavian tax policy, that's not what I'm asking about. What I want is: which of these pull against each other and which only look like they conflict? Rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each one say what would have to give and what I lose by giving it up. Then tell me the single cheapest revision that would make the rest of the set hang together. I genuinely dont have a view on which item I'd sacrifice, so don't take a hint from the order I listed them in.
