I have spent more years than I care to admit in the anthropics threads on LessWrong, and I have now constructed an argument about Boltzmann brains that seems to succeed too well — which is usually a sign of a hidden flaw. I should like a diagnosis.

Let us call the following the Instability Criterion: a theory is rationally rejected if, were it true, the evidence we take to support it would probably not support it.

(1) The Instability Criterion holds.
(2) Any cosmology whose history contains a long equilibrium phase, or an eternally inflating late time, implies that observers with experiences qualitatively like mine are overwhelmingly more numerous, on the theory's own counting, as momentary thermal fluctuations with manufactured memories than as evolved creatures with genuine pasts.
(3) So if such a cosmology were true, my recollections — of the temperature anisotropy data, of deriving the fluctuation rate, of the very argument I am now setting out — would be far more likely to be manufactured than veridical.
(4) By (1), such cosmologies are rationally rejected. So far I am happy.
(5) But nothing in that argument was peculiar to fluctuation cosmologies. Every empirical theory is supported by records, and there is no coherent body of experience that could not have arisen spontaneously; so for any theory T, were T true, my evidence would not reliably support T.
(6) Hence, by (1), every empirical theory is rationally rejected.
(7) Since (6) is absurd, (1) is false — and with it goes the only principled ground I had for rejecting Boltzmann-brain cosmologies at (4).

I cannot accept the second half of (7). A cosmology that entails I am probably a disembodied brain hallucinating a research programme still seems to me to be in trouble, and in a way that a cosmology which merely permits sceptical hypotheses is not. I have framed the argument so that it does not turn on how one resolves the measure problem, nor on whether fluctuated observers enjoy coherent or incoherent memories; if it does turn on one of those after all, say so and explain where my framing failed. My suspicion is that (5) is doing something sneakier than (2), but I cannot say what.

Which step should I reject, why did it look unobjectionable at the time, and what does rejecting it cost? I would like the exits priced rather than merely listed: keep (1) and swallow the global scepticism, restrict (1) in some non-ad-hoc way, deny (2), or deny that a theory's own measure licenses conclusions about me. Please write in American English.
