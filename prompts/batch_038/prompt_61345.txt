Our department has a small internal fund that pays one grad student for an 8-week summer project and I want to pitch it, but my idea is still mush. Can you write the proposal? They want about 1000 words: a clear question, a work plan, and what the deliverable actually is.

The mush: everybody agrees that if you're reinvesting a favorable bet over and over, the Kelly fraction maximizes your long-run growth rate, and everybody also agrees that this coincides with expected-utility maximization only if your utility happens to be log. The two camps then talk past each other — the growth-rate people (ergodicity economics and friends) say expected utility is the wrong object because it averages over an ensemble of lives when only one life actually happens, and the utility people say the growth-rate view is just log utility dressed up with extra steps. What I think both sides miss is that neither the utility function nor the growth rate is the real primitive. What pins down how much to bet is the shape of the situation: how long you've got (a mortality hazard, a hard deadline), and whether theres a threshold you have to clear by then (a debt, a tuition bill, the minimum stake to stay in the game). Send the horizon to infinity with no threshold and you get Kelly. Give a finite horizon with a target and the red-and-black literature points the other way — when the odds are in your favor and you just need to reach a goal, the probability-maximizing play is to bet the *minimum* each round, nowhere near Kelly. Same agent, same beliefs, same odds, wildly different fraction, and nothing about her tastes changed.

So the project I want funded: can you state a single criterion that takes the structural parameters as inputs (hazard rate of the horizon, the threshold, distance above or below it) and spits out the right bet fraction, with Kelly and timid goal-reaching falling out as limiting cases — and, crucially, is that criterion anything more than a redescription of some utility function? Because if for every such situation you can cook up a concave utility that generates the same bets, the whole thing collapses into the standard picture and the project was pointless.

Write it up as a proposal: the question in a form with a determinate answer, why the existing positions leave it open, what I'd concretely do week by week for eight weeks (I can set up discrete-time models and run simulations, I'm not proving deep theorems), what would count as a result and what would count as failure, and an honest verdict on whether eight weeks is remotely realistic or whether I should cut the scope down.
