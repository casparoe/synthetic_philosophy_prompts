I'm stuck on something for my chapter on updateless decision theory and I want to see it argued properly rather than hedged. Position to defend: XOR blackmail is a bad counterexample to causal decision theory, because the setup quietly assumes that Omega's decision to send you the letter is independant of what its simulation of you predicted — and no blackmailer who pays any cost per letter would bother targeting someone predicted to refuse. So let Omega screen its targets: letters only go to agents whose sims pay. Now the letter itself is evidence that your sim paid, hence that there's no virus in the lab, hence that you should refuse — which is exactly the evidential reasoning the EDT/FDT camp leans on, turned around against their own verdict, and it also generates a fixed-point problem if everyone reasons that way. Please build the strongest version of this, showing the instability survives any charitable repair of Omega's policy (I don't want the weak point that the stipulations are just strange), and then contrast it with counterfactual mugging, where the coin flip is exogenous so nothing analogous arises. I mostly want the positive case, not a survey of objections.
