I am in the second term of a graduate formal epistemology sequence, and we have just moved from the standard precise-Bayesian material into imprecise probabilities — credal sets, lower and upper previsions, the whole apparatus. The difficulty I have is that the seminar readings pull in opposite directions: some insist that unspecific evidence rationally demands an unsharp credal state, others argue that unsharp credences are a kind of practical irrationality, and a third group treats the machinery as a statistical modelling tool with no philosophical upshot at all. Before I commit to a view for my seminar paper, I want the specification rather than the contenders. So: write the list of desiderata that a theory of rational belief under imprecision must satisfy to count as adequate. I want the requirement, the case or consideration that motivates it, and an explicit marking of whether it is non-negotiable or merely the local prejudice of one camp — in particular, I suspect several of the demands currently being pressed on imprecise probabilists are just precise-Bayesian norms smuggled in as neutral criteria, and I would like to know which. Please work through at least the following candidates, adding any I have missed and dropping any you think are not genuine requirements: that the theory vindicate imprecision where evidence is genuinely unspecific, rather than licensing it anywhere; that it explain or explain away dilation, i.e. the possibility that conditioning on an event widens your interval for a hypothesis whichever way that event turns out; that it preserve conglomerability, or else say what is lost without it; that it distinguish the weaker requirement of avoiding sure loss from full coherence and explain why the gap matters; that it supply a decision rule — Γ-maximin, E-admissibility, maximality, or something else — that yields determinate choices in ordinary cases without becoming dynamically incoherent; that it resist Elga's argument that a variant of the diachronic Dutch book shows perfectly rational agents always to have perfectly sharp probabilities; that it handle the reflection principle and the intuition that costless information should never be declined; that it allow complete ignorance to be represented without an arbitrary choice of parameterisation, as in the cube-factory style problems for the principle of indifference; that it recover precise probability as a special case; that it accommodate ambiguity aversion of the Ellsberg sort; and that it remain usable by a single non-omniscient agent, not only by a panel of experts. For each, note the distinctions the theory must not collapse — for instance between an unsharp credal state and mere indecision, between epistemic and strong independence, and between convexity as a substantive constraint and convexity as a representational convenience.

The part I most want is the impossibility work. If some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — and I have an inkling that something like "imprecision is rationally required under unspecific evidence", "no dilation", "dynamic coherence in choice", and "determinate comparative verdicts about single bets" cannot all be held together — then say precisely which subset fails, which requirement should be given up first, and why. Rank the requirements by how much work each does in excluding candidate theories, and identify the single one that knocks out the most views currently on offer.

Please write in British English, and begin with your bottom line in the very first sentence — naming the requirement you take to be most exclusionary and stating whether you think any subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable — and only then give the reasoning; no preamble, no restatement of my question, and no bibliography, since I have the reading list already.
