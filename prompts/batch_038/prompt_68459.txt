I work in model validation at a large bank: my job is to review other people's forecasting models before they are allowed into production and to decide whether they earn that approval. My native vocabulary is in-sample fit versus out-of-sample performance, backtests and walk-forward splits, information coefficients, calibration, overfitting and effective degrees of freedom, data-snooping and multiple-testing adjustments, feature selection and feature leakage, regime change and non-stationarity, signal decay, and the governance question of what documentation, stress-testing, and independent replication a model needs before anyone is permitted to trade on it. I am fluent in all of this and I think almost exclusively in it. My history of science is thin: I know Kepler for the three laws of planetary motion and essentially nothing else.

I have been reading about Kepler's attempt to reform astrology rather than abandon it, and I want the whole program translated into my vocabulary as faithfully as it can be. Here is the position as I understand it; correct me in the response if I have mischaracterized any part of it.

- He earned a substantial part of his income casting natal horoscopes and writing annual prognostications for patrons, courts, and towns, while maintaining in print that most of what the profession actually did was worthless.
- He discarded the division of the local sky into twelve houses, the doctrine that the planets hold dignities and exaltations in particular signs, the traditional meanings attached to the fixed stars, and the practice of predicting particular events in particular lives.
- He retained the aspects: the angular separations between planets as seen from Earth. His criterion for which aspects count was not empirical but geometrical: the admissible angles are those that divide the circle in ratios tied to figures constructible with straightedge and compass, so the feature set is fixed in advance by the geometry of the circle rather than selected from observations.
- He backed this with a mechanism. The Earth, and in human beings the soul, possesses a faculty attuned to these harmonic ratios; the configuration of the heavens at birth is impressed on the soul and inclines it, shaping dispositions rather than determining events.
- He accordingly confined his retained astrology to general outcomes: weather, and temperament or propensity in people. He checked aspects against his own running records of the weather.
- He argued against two camps at once: the practitioners who defended the whole inherited apparatus, and the physicians and theologians who held that the entire subject was empty and that any correlation anyone found must be coincidence. His complaint against the second camp was that they threw out something real along with the rubbish.

Restate that program as though Kepler were a model owner submitting it to me for approval. Do not smuggle my framework's assumptions in and call the result a translation: if you have to grant him something my framework cannot grant, grant it explicitly and flag it, rather than quietly converting it into a prior or a regularization term and moving on. I want the residue to be as visible as the correspondences. No references, no bibliography; I want your reasoning, not your sources.

Please structure the response in exactly these sections, in this order:

1. Intake summary — about 150 words. Kepler's submission in one paragraph of my vocabulary: the target variable, the candidate features retained, the features dropped and why, the claimed mechanism, the evidence offered. This exists so I can check that you have read him correctly before you start judging him.

2. The validation review — about 600 words. The translation proper, written as I would write it about a real submission. Which of his rejections are rejections of features that fail out of sample, and which are rejections of something else entirely? Where does his restriction to weather and disposition rather than particular events land: aggregate versus individual prediction, irreducible idiosyncratic noise, base-rate discipline? Do his weather records constitute a backtest, and if so, was he guilty of the data-snooping I would normally cite? Does my framework have any counterpart to his third position between the true believers and the total deniers, given that model risk management also refuses both trust the model and models are worthless? Every time you catch yourself normalizing one of his claims into something testable, say so in the sentence where it happens, because I suspect the normalizing is precisely where the translation breaks.

3. Residue report — about 450 words, in three labeled subsections: Carries over cleanly; Carries over only with distortion; Has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. In the third subsection I want a direct answer on two items in particular. First, the geometrical warrant for the aspect set: is the claim that admissible angles are fixed in advance by the constructibility of the circle expressible as prior information, as a constraint on the hypothesis space, or as a regularization choice, or does my vocabulary have no slot for a feature set chosen on grounds that are not empirical at all? Second, the harmonic faculty in the Earth and the soul: is this a mechanism claim of the sort I would file under economic intuition or causal story, which my framework treats as nice to have but not strictly required for approval, or is it doing load-bearing work that my approval process has no category for?

4. Verdict on the residue — about 250 words. Take a side. Is what fails to translate a defect of validation thinking, which knows how to price accuracy and has no way to price warrant, or a symptom that Kepler's program was less clear than it looked? Answer this concretely: when Kepler says the total deniers are wrong because something real survives the purge, is he making a claim about out-of-sample performance, a claim about mechanism, or a claim my framework cannot count as a claim at all? State which reading you think is right and what would settle it.
