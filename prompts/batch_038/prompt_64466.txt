My friend Dev has strong opinions about who's allowed to blame who, and I've been keeping a list, because she's way more consistent then she lets on. Here's the record so far:

1. Our old RA, who threw the loudest parties on the floor but wrote people up for noise. Dev: the write-ups were accurate but he'd forfeited the right to issue them — "you're one to talk" was a complete answer, not a dodge.
2. My aunt, spotless tax record, telling her brother that hiding income is wrong. Full standing, and Dev got irritated that I even raised it.
3. My uncle — never unfaithful, but twenty years of lies about money — accusing his wife of an affair. Dev split this one: he keeps standing on fidelity, but the second he framed it as "you turned our whole marriage into a lie," he lost it, because that's a demand he'd been flouting the entire time.
4. A coworker who was a notorious deadline-misser, owned up to it, sat through the embarrassment, actually changed, and then told a new hire "don't be me." Dev: full standing, maybe more than most people get.
5. A friend of ours who has never gossiped in her life, wouldn't say gossip is wrong to anyone's face for years ("who am I to judge"), and then said it, once, about a woman she can't stand. Dev: that's the moment she lost it. Her words were "she doesn't hold the norm, she collects it."
6. Dev's brother, drinks heavily, tells his teenage son not to, and when challenged says adults get different rules. No standing — and notice Dev didn't blame the drinking, she blamed the "different rules for me" part.
7. A friend who unknowingly bought a car from a dealership running a scam, and then lectured someone else about propping up crooked businesses. Standing intact: he didn't know, and he was appalled when he found out.

Also worth knowing: when I ask her why, she gives a different story every time. About #5 she said the problem was that the friend didn't really care about honesty, it was just ammunition; about #4 she said the point was he'd put himself under the same rule as everyone else. Those don't sound like the same theory to me.

Genuinely — not performatively — hates plagiarism: he's voted to sanction two of his own friends, he says out loud that he'd expect the same treatment if he ever did it, and I've never once caught him shading a norm to suit himself. Except five years ago he ghost-wrote a chunk of a students thesis, and no one has ever known. He's chairing a hearing on a plagiarism case next week.

Predict Dev's verdict on whether he has standing to run that hearing, and give me a confidence level. For each survivor, say what it predicts here, and tell me which one you'd bet on and why. On the new case specifically: does the secrecy matter? Would Dev's answer change if I told her about the ghost-writing, and if it would, what does that imply about whether she thinks standing is a fact about the blamer or a fact about what the blaming conveys? And if you think she'd reject the framing of this case outright rather than rule on it, say that instead and explain.
