For two years I was seconded from a local authority to help run a contracted employment programme for long-term benefit claimants — first as a team leader, then doing quality assurance on case files, so I saw the same thing from both sides of the desk. Claimants were required to log a set number of job-search actions each week, and a very large share of what got logged had no plausible causal connection to anyone getting a job: registering on a vacancy board and never returning to it, saving a search, printing a list of adverts, 'researching' an employer whose name the claimant could not recall a week later. The work coach knew this, the claimant knew it, and the contract manager who audited the files knew it; it was never stated in a meeting, but it was the shared background of every appointment. What did move people into work — a former supervisor willing to give a reference, a certificate that made someone employable in a different sector, a lift to a six in the morning interview, a rehearsed conversation about a health condition — was invisible to the system, because none of it could be entered in the required field, and staff learned not to spend time on it, since a fortnight of that kind of work produced no recordable activity and looked, on the file, like neglect. The count of logged actions was what determined whether a sanction was recommended, and the same count travelled up the chain as evidence that the programme was working.

I have tried for some time to find the name of this and I keep landing on concepts that are near but not on it. I know the line about a measure ceasing to be a good measure once it becomes a target, but that describes a measure that was good and was corrupted; ours was never a proxy for anything, and I came to suspect it was retained precisely because it was inert — a job-search requirement that actually raised employment would have had visible distributional consequences and would have been contested, whereas a requirement that produces paperwork is objectionable to no one. Nor is it fraud or 'gaming the system', since nothing is concealed and the people being measured are the ones instructed in what to record. And it is not simply box-ticking or bureaucratic ritualism, insofar as those are usually described as a drift or a pathology, something that happens to a system; what I watched felt load-bearing. The nearest analogy I can think of is ritual, but in a ritual the participants understand the act as non-instrumental, whereas here the official understanding, held sincerely at least part of the time by both sides, is that the act is instrumental, and the gap between the two understandings is never resolved, only worked around appointment by appointment. There is a further wrinkle that I cannot place at all: claimants often said the routine gave the week a shape, and coaches said it was the one part of the job where they could see they had done something, so the activity does have effects, just not the ones it is officially supposed to have.

So my question is what this configuration is called, if it is called anything. I am less interested in the label than in the carving, and I would want an answer that identifies the established concepts that come closest, says how the thing I have described differs from each of them, and marks where its edges fall — for instance, would a workplace timesheet that everyone knows measures nothing qualify? Would a school's homework-completion statistics? Would a religious observance, and if not, what precisely excludes it? I would also like to know whether there is a concept for the deeper suspicion I could never shake, which is that the real product of the requirement was not a change in behaviour but a legible record of effort that could be used to sort claimants into those who had tried and those who had not — in which case the inertness is not a defect to be explained away but the point, since a moral classification requires only a trace, not an effect. Please do not send me a reading list: I have no way of chasing references down, and a roster of authors would be far less use to me than a careful account of the boundaries. If nothing in the literature actually covers this, say so plainly and coin a term instead, with a definition tight enough that the cases above land on the right side of it.
