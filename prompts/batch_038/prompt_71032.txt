I co-host a graduate-run podcast on the philosophy of machine learning, and next month's guest is a researcher who defends realism about features in mechanistic interpretability: the view that the directions and structures interpretability work identifies in a network's activations are genuine representational states of that system, rather than convenient summaries that merely happen to predict its behavior.

She has two signature claims, and I want the interview to engage them on their own terms rather than orbiting the general dispute.

The first is what she calls the convergence criterion. A posited feature is real only if it is (i) causally steerable — adding the direction to the residual stream changes outputs in the predicted way; (ii) recoverable across paraphrase, translation, and input modality of the eliciting prompt; and (iii) recovered independently by two methods whose failure modes do not overlap, such as a sparse autoencoder and a probe trained only on behaviorally defined data. She holds that jointly satisfying (i) through (iii) is sufficient for realism about that feature.

The second is her reply to the objection that a trained network lacks a design spec. Content is fixed by a selectional process after all, she argues: gradient descent selects internal structures for their contribution to the training objective, and she maintains this does the work that natural selection or designer intention does in other accounts of content.

Write nine or ten interview questions, in the order I would ask them, arranged so that it becomes progressively harder for her to answer by saying that any disanalogy with human concepts merely shows that our folk semantics is the thing in need of revision. Include at least one question on each of the following, and for each, a follow-up keyed to the answer I most expect from her, with a one-line label naming what the exchange is testing:

- Paraphrase-invariance and the disjunction problem: does (ii) rule out a feature that in fact encodes something on the order of Paris-or-France-or-tourism, and if not, why is that not fatal to determinate content?
- Independence in (iii): are two methods fitted to the same activation data genuinely independent, and what would non-circular independence look like?
- Overpermissiveness: a household thermometer seems to satisfy (i) through (iii) with respect to ambient temperature. What in her criterion is meant to exclude it, or is the exclusion supposed to fail?
- The granularity of the selection-history reply: gradient descent selects for outputs, and the objective differs between pretraining and later fine-tuning stages. What settles which of the competing candidate contents was selected for?
- Feature identity across layers and checkpoints: does a feature at an early layer and its descendant at a late layer count as one representation, and what are its identity conditions?
- Features without a training-distribution anchor: what does her view say about directions that steer reliably yet correspond to nothing the model was trained to track?
- The pragmatic question: if a strict instrumentalist predicted and steered exactly as she does, what would her realism add, and how could a third party tell which account was correct?
- The closing evidential question: what specific kind of finding, were it to appear, would oblige her to abandon the view?

Two further constraints. First, no question should be answerable by restating her position, and none should concern her biography, her influences, or the state of the field; nor should any invite references or citations, since we publish a transcript and not a bibliography. Second, wherever a question sets her against instrumentalism about features, state the instrumentalist's challenge in its strongest form before posing it. Assume she is fully competent and not evasive by temperament: the pressure should come entirely from the content of the view.
