Here's the principle I came up with on my own, and I think it's bad, but I don't know how to fix it. Something is in pain only if it has an internal state that (i) is normally set off by damage, (ii) makes it guard and favor the damaged part, (iii) makes it learn to avoid whatever did the damage, (iv) can't be shelved — it competes with everything else the system wants — and (v) it would give up other goods to make it stop. Problem: the morphine guy fails (ii) through (v) and I still say pain. The awake-but-paralyzed woman fails all five. The dog-sized robot passes all five and I say no. So it fails in both directions at once, and the two failures feel like the same mistake pointed two ways, which makes me suspect the principle isn't missing a clause but aimed at the wrong target entirely.
