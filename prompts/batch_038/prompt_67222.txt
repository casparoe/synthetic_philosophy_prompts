So I'm running a small workshop and I want to hand people a document that two folks who've been arguing for years could actually both put their names to. The two sides: a committed eliminativist about propositional attitudes -- thinks belief/desire talk is a stagnant, badly failed theory headed for the same bin as phlogiston and the four humors -- and a realist about folk psychology, who thinks it's indispensable, wildly successful at predicting and explaining people, and at worst needs refinement rather than replacement.

Draft their joint statement after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want a numbered section of claims both can sign, including what they agree folk psychology explains badly (self-deception, schizophrenia, sleep deprivation, the old introspection studies) and what each one concedes to the other side; then a table of the disagreements, each worded so its own proponent would accept it, with a flag on the ones you think are merely verbal -- say which and why; then the cruxes, the sub-questions where a real answer would actually move at least one of them; and for each crux, a concrete test or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

One crux I definitely want in there: today's LLMs produce fluent first-person reports about their own beliefs, desires, and reasons, sometimes plainly confabulating and sometimes apparently tracking something, and neither camp has a settled acount of what that's evidence for. Have each of them say what they'd have predicted about systems like that ahead of time, and whether the evidence has shifted them. Don't resolve the debate -- just make the leftover disagreement exact.
