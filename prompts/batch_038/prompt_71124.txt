I have been trying to settle what I actually believe about the moral weight of insects by going through cases, and my verdicts do not seem to add up to any principle I can state. They are as follows.

(a) A harvesting practice kills or maims an enormous number of field insects — quadrillions on any estimate I have seen — and my credence that insects are sentient is perhaps five percent. A cheap modification would cut the harm by a third. I think we should make the modification.

(b) For the same modest cost we could relieve severe, entirely certain suffering in ten thousand hens. I think we should help the hens, even though multiplying out my own credences puts the insects far ahead.

(c) Oysters and mussels: even more numerous, credence lower, perhaps one percent. Here the numbers stop counting altogether. It is not that the expected value gets small; it is that I do not think a quadrillion one-percent chances at sentience generate any claim whatsoever.

(d) Crustaceans in fisheries and aquaculture: credence around forty percent, numbers in the billions. I think we should act, and act precisely because of the numbers.

(e) The pair I cannot reconcile. Two situations with identical arithmetic. In the first, my five percent comes from open scientific ignorance about an animal group — nobody has run the experiments, and the honest position is that we do not know. In the second, my five percent about, say, sea anemones comes from a positive structural reason to doubt: a diffuse nerve net, nothing that looks like unified experience. I treat the first as generating real obligations that scale with the numbers, and the second as generating almost none.

My own guess is something like: the numbers count only above a floor of credence, and then scale linearly. But I cannot make that precise, and (e) appears to break it, since both cases sit at the same five percent.

Is there a principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures these verdicts? State each candidate precisely enough that I can test it against the five cases, say what it implies for a case I have not raised — a practice that harms a vast number of insects only slightly, versus one that harms a small number of them very badly indeed — and say honestly which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon. I would rather have the best available reconstruction and its cost than be told the set is inconsistent. Please keep the whole response under six hundred words.
