I'm drafting a short story and I want you to take a first swing at one scene for me. The setup: two people — let's say a grandmother and her grandson — playing a game where each in turn can either grab a growing pot of money and end the game, or pass it across and let the pot get bigger. Strict logic says grab it on the very first turn: work backwards from the end, the last pass is never worth making, so the one before it isn't either, and the whole thing collapses down to turn one. But nobody actually plays that way, and both of them know that nobody plays that way, and that's really what I want the story to be about — how looking a bit irrational is the only thing that makes the pot grow.

Write the scene from inside the grandson's head, close third person, while he's trying to work out whether his grandmother has genuinely reasoned it through or is just being generous with him, and let that uncertainty change what he does with his hand on the table. Aim for about a thousand words. Dont explain the game to the reader out loud — no speeches, no lecturer voice, it should come through in what he notices and what he's afraid of. I'd rather it reads like a family story that happens to have this shape than a puzzle with people in it.
