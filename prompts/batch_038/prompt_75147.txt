Some background on me, since it explains where the idea comes from. I am not an academic. I work in network security, which means my professional life is spent on deterrence, first-strike stability, and the problem of having to act on a detection you cannot verify. Several years ago I read Liu Cixin's trilogy, and the dark forest argument has never quite left my head: the claim that in a universe where no civilization can ever be certain of another's intentions, and where any civilization might undergo a sudden and unbounded advance in capability, the rational posture toward a newly detected neighbour is neither communication nor indifference but preemptive elimination, so that everyone stays silent and everyone shoots at anything that makes noise. My daughter has Lord of the Flies on her summer reading list, and I have been reading along with her, and I cannot stop seeing that island as a dark forest in miniature.

Here is my hunch, and I want you to test it rather than flatter it. The standard readings — Hobbes's state of nature, original sin, the breakdown of civilization — seem to me to be about what the boys are, whereas the interesting structure of the book is epistemic: it is about what the boys can and cannot know. The beast is a detection with no verification attached to it. The conch is an attempt to build a signalling and deliberation channel, and it fails. Simon is the only character who tries to convert suspicion into knowledge by going and looking for himself, and he is killed precisely because he arrives as an unidentified figure out of the dark during a storm — which is exactly how the dark forest tells you to treat an approaching unverified other. I have privately been calling that the verification tax: the price paid by the one party who tries to gather evidence instead of assuming the worst. And the closing fire, set purely to smoke Ralph out and finish him, is the fire that brings the cruiser, so that the signal and the strike turn out to be the same act.

Please write the reading, and make sure it does all of the following.
