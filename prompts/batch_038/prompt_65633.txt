Im putting together a reading group on Popper and evolution and I keep getting stuck on what he'd say about something he never discussed. His own record, as far as I can tell: Freud and Adler — not science, because nothing a patient could do would refute them; Marx's original theory of history — science, it stuck its neck out and got it chopped off, though the practicioners who reinterpreted the failed predictions to save the theory weren't doing science any more; Einstein's gravity — the model of it, because the light-bending prediction could have come out wrong; Newton — science even though it turned out false; natural selection in 1974 — almost tautological, since fitness looked to him like it was defined by who survives and survival then got explained by fitness; and then a few years later he withdrew that in print, saying the theory is far from a tautology, is genuinely testable, and also functions as a metaphysical research programme.

The case I want a verdict on is the neutral theory of molecular evolution (Kimura, late sixties). It says most DNA differences between species, and most variation within them, come from drift in mutations that make no difference to survival or reproduction — neutral just means no effect on fitness. It predicts the substitution rate equals the mutation rate whatever the population size, a roughly constant molecular clock, and that functionally unimportant stretches change faster than critical ones, which they do. But when rates turned out to vary with generation time its authors patched in a generation-time correction, and when slightly harmful mutations turned out to matter in small populations they widened it into a nearly neutral theory whose threshold slides with population size.

So: which way would he rule? Lay out the candidate rules that fit his record equally well through 1978, what each one says about this case, your best single guess and why, and whether his reversal on Darwinism was a change of rule or a change of mind about the facts. Then name one further case that would tell the rules apart.
