I'm on a city health board that votes next month on a field trial of gene-drive mosquitoes meant to collapse the local Aedes population. The public-health case is strong and nobody disputes it. What's splitting us is whether the impact assessment should carry an invertebrate-welfare line at all. One member insists it has to: even if the chance a mosquito is sentient is tiny, you're talking about millions of adults and the billions of descendants that never get born, and that multiplication swamps everything else on the spreadsheet. The others say thats exactly how you end up justifying or condemning anything — go find a big enough number. And no one on the board can tell you whether a mosquito's life is even net-positive for the mosquito, which seems like it might matter more than the arithmetic anyone's quoting.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. It should recommend what we actually do with that term — include it, leave it out, cap it, defer it, or restructure the assessment some other way — with reasons a member who disagrees could respond to rather than just restate the numbers. Give the strongest honest version of the position you're rejecting, and say what the board would be committing itself to, in practice, whichever way it goes.
