I am trying to decide which axiology to adopt for reasoning about the long-run future, and I want the specification before I read another proposal. Take an eternally inflating cosmology seriously: every physically possible history occurs infinitely often, so every act I might take alters the ratio of flourishing to suffering observers somewhere while leaving both counts infinite, and any procedure that converts this into a verdict appears to depend on how I choose to carve an infinite spacetime into finite pieces. What I need, therefore, is a theory of value capable of ranking acts in a universe where the relevant totals are already infinite.

Write the desiderata — what any such theory must deliver — with the motivating case behind each item. At a minimum I want four groups. First, the concrete choice situations it must rank in a specified way: an act that leaves infinitely many lives marginally better off and none worse; an act that improves infinitely many lives and worsens infinitely many others by the same amounts; the creation of a billion blissful lives in a universe that already contains infinitely many; a wager with unbounded payoffs whose expectation diverges rather than being infinite; and a pair of acts where one is worse in every state yet both have infinite expected value. Second, the distinctions it may not collapse: comparability versus determinacy, permutation-invariance versus anonymity, finite versus countable additivity, "no best option exists" versus "all options are equally good," "infinitely worse" versus "incomparable." Third, the formal results it cannot contradict — in particular whatever is known about the representability of complete, Paretian, anonymous orderings over infinite populations, since I have been assuming that such orderings admit no real-valued representation and, for anything like a continuum of welfare levels, no explicit construction at all; correct me if that is wrong, and price out what keeping all three costs. Fourth, the structural constraints: it must reduce to ordinary finite aggregation whenever only finitely many people are affected; it must not let the ranking depend on an arbitrary cutoff; it must be action-guiding rather than licensing paralysis; and it must not dissolve every act into "infinite value," so that nothing matters more than anything else.
