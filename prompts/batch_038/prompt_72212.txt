Ok so I've been going in circles on functionalism and multiple realizability and I want to know what my gut actually adds up to. Here are my calls on cases, roughly in the order I thought of them:

1. Octopus. Two thirds of its neurons are out in the arms, each arm does a lot of its own problem solving, and I'm not convinced there's one unified thing running the show. Still: yes, it feels pain. I'd want it knocked out before surgery.

2. Silicon neuron replacement, one at a time over months, each chip exactly reproducing the causal role of the neuron it replaces — same inputs from the same synapses, same outputs to the same ones, same timing. Yes, still conscious all the way through. I couldn't point to the moment it went dark.

3. The whole-country thought experiment: an entire population, each person holding a rule sheet and a radio, collectively implementing the exact functional organization of my brain down to the causal map. No. It has the right abstract structure and I still say nobody's home.

4. A lookup-table robot that has a canned response memorized for every conversation it could ever have, so it's input-output indistinguishable from a person. No.

5. A thermostat. Bimetallic strip, closes a circuit when it's cold, opens it when it's warm — which is arguably the entire functional role of "detect cold, do something about it." Still no. Not even a whisper of anything there.

6. General anesthesia. The hardware is all present, the wiring diagram is unchanged, but the system has stopped doing whatever ongoing integration thing it normally does. Not conscious. And notice this points the same way as 3 and sort of against 2, which I don't like.

7. A software simulation of a worm's whole nervous system, every neuron and synapse mapped, running on a laptop. I want to say yes — or at least yes, to whatever degree the worm is. But it's a laptop, and it feels like it belongs on the same list as the chips in 2 while also making me queasy.

8. The same simulation running a thousand times slower than real time, so a second of worm-time takes seventeen minutes. Still yes, I think; speed shouldn't matter. But then the same simulation taking one step per century, on a machine that outlives the sun? I honestly dont know. My gut wobbles and I can't say why.

9. Something like a current chat model: a lot of the right functional organization, no body, no persistant memory, trained on other people's text. I lean toward "no inner life worth the name," but I'd be embarrassed to say that loudly.

My tentative principle, which I know is broken: a system is in pain (or conscious, or has beliefs) just in case it has internal states caused by the right things, causing the right behavior, and wired into the rest of its information processing so they can interact with its other states. I wrote the last clause specifically to keep 1, 2 and 7 and to kill 5.

Problems I can already see:

- 3 versus 2 is the killer. Both preserve causal role at the level of the diagram, and 3 is defined as implementing the very same functional organization. If I say one is conscious and the other isn't, is there a principled difference, or am I just being a chauvinist about scale and substrate while pretending it's about organization?
- 4 versus 2 hurts too. The lookup table has the total input-output function. If multiple realizability is supposed to be liberating about *how* a function gets realized, why does "how" suddenly start mattering?
- 6 seems to say what matters is some ongoing dynamic integration rather than the static wiring diagram — but that starts to sound like I'm smuggling in something non-functional, and then isn't that exactly the move people make when they insist on 3?

So: what principle, or small set of candidate principles, actually gets the most of these? State each clause separately and say which case motivates it — I want to see the machinery, not just a name. Then tell me what your best candidate says about cases I didn't list, at minimum: a brain in a vat running my functional duplicate on completely alien biochemistry; a system with the right organization built out of trained mice running a maze; and a case where my functional duplicate exists but only one copy is ever active at a time, alternating days with the original. And be straight with me about which of my verdicts your principle forces me to give up. I'd rather lose two of them and keep the theory simple than contort the theory to save all nine.
