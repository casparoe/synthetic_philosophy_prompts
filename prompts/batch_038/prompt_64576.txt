I am a senior analyst at a European climate-policy institute. Ahead of a debate our Brussels office is hosting on whether the EU should treat new nuclear as a pillar of the transition, I have been asked to publish a short personal position statement, and I would like a rigorous consistency audit of the package before it goes out. I am not looking for a brief on the nuclear question, for advocacy in either direction, or for citations and reading lists — I want to know whether my own commitments can all be held at once, and if not, what I have to stop saying.

Here is the package as it currently stands.

1. The binding constraint on energy policy is the remaining carbon budget. Options should be scored by tonnes of CO2e avoided per euro spent and per year of delay, and I put a heavy premium on abatement that actually arrives before 2035 rather than on abatement that arrives eventually.
2. Risk regulation should be proportionate to measured harm. I compare generation technologies on realised harm per terawatt-hour — fatalities, land take, material throughput, waste arisings — rather than on intuitions about acceptability, and I think a regulator who cannot say what a technology's harm rate is has no business restricting it. On that accounting nuclear sits at the clean end of the table alongside wind and solar.
3. I nonetheless hold that Germany's exit was the right call, that it was defensible in 2011 on the information available then, and that I would defend it today to a room of German policymakers.
4. A net-zero system needs firm, dispatchable capacity to cover multi-day winter lulls, and my candidate list is hydrogen-capable gas plant, long-duration storage, more interconnection, and contracted demand response. Nuclear is not on that list, chiefly because of capital cost and construction time. At the same time I oppose new long-life unabated fossil infrastructure on lock-in grounds, so anything gas-fired that gets built has to be genuinely convertible and on a credible path out of fossil operation.
5. In my published work I lead with levelised cost, because it is the only cost metric that the people who sign things will actually read. Integration and system costs go in a footnote.
6. Timing disqualifies new build. On the evidence of Olkiluoto 3 and Flamanville 3, first power arrives a decade or more after the decision is taken, which is after the window in which it could matter; the same money buys far more if spent on grids, storage, and flexibility. I would say no even to a proposal in which the developer carried all of the cost and schedule risk.
7. I am firmly against closing operating, licensed reactors early. Life extension is close to the cheapest abatement available anywhere, and Belgium's decision to keep Doel 4 and Tihange 3 running for another decade was the right one.
8. France's reliance on nuclear for roughly two-thirds of its generation is a resilience liability rather than an asset. One operator, essentially one design family, and in 2022 a single failure mode — stress corrosion cracking — took much of the fleet out of service, cut nuclear output to a thirty-year low, and left France a net importer of electricity for the first time since 1980. That is a kind of risk I take very seriously and that I do not think shows up anywhere in the numbers we normally publish.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a lecture. Specifically:

- Identify the commitments that genuinely pull against one another and rank them by severity, most damaging first. For each, state precisely what follows from what, what has to be given up or qualified, and what the cheapest revision costs me — including whether the revision is a change of view or only a change in what I am willing to publish.
- Distinguish these from pairs that merely look inconsistent and dissolve once the relevant distinction is drawn, and say what that distinction is. I expect at least one of my tensions to be of this kind, but I do not want you to reassure me by classifying things as merely apparent unless they really are.
- Point out premises that are doing unacknowledged work — places where one of my conclusions follows from my stated commitments only given an assumption I have not listed.
- Tell me which single revision would buy the most coherence across the whole set, and what I would lose by making it.
- Where my own stated criteria cannot deliver one of my own case verdicts, say so explicitly and show the gap rather than patching it with a consideration imported from outside my list.

I have deliberately not told you which of these I would be most willing to abandon, because I do not know, and I would rather the audit not be steered by my expectations.

Two things about your own confidence, please. For each tension you identify, say how sure you are that it is real rather than an artefact of how I have phrased my commitments. And at the end, single out the one claim in your audit that you are least confident in, say why it is shaky, and say what evidence or argument would settle it.
