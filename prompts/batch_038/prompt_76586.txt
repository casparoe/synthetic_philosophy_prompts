I am a doctoral student in moral philosophy convening a reading group on incommensurability and population ethics. Half my participants arrive convinced that "better than" must be transitive and half convinced it cannot be, and neither side can say what it would have to give up. Please build a decision tree that takes a reader from a battery of spectrum cases to a named position on the transitivity of "better than."

Set out the logical space first: list the diagnostic questions and the answer-combinations they permit, and only then locate the actual views in it, marking any combination that comes out empty and saying why — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Every question must be answerable by someone who has never seen the literature, so pose each as a verdict about a case, not as a label. Eight to ten nodes, six to eight leaves, each leaf named and carrying one sentence on the commitment its adherent would least like to own.

The battery should include the familiar torture-against-a-vast-number-of-mild-headaches spectrum and a within-life variant (one ecstatic minute traded away step by step for stretches of mild contentment), plus this case of my own, which I want the tree to exploit rather than merely mention: a film archive in which each successive restoration buys a marked improvement in sound at the cost of a visual loss too small to detect in isolation, so that different aesthetic considerations dominate at different points along the series. At least one node should test whether the reader's reversal in the archive case comes from the considerations shifting as they move along it, since that separates genuine intransitivity from boundary imprecision and from lexical thresholds.

The distinctions the tree must register: whether the relation at issue is one or several; whether an admitted failure of transitivity is explained by imprecision at the margins, by a fourth relation such as parity, or by the relation simply not being transitive; whether the failure is structurally analogous to a Condorcet cycle; and whether choice-theoretic pressure — money pumps, sequences of trades each judged an improvement — should force revision of the pairwise verdicts or of the ordering itself. The leaves must include an imprecise-equality view, a genuine-intransitivity view, a parity or clumpiness view, a discontinuity view, a view that preserves transitivity by biting the bullet at the endpoints, and a contextualist view on which there is no single ordering to begin with.

Finally, flag every pair of leaves your instrument cannot really separate — I expect parity and imprecision to be one of them — and say whether some further question would separate them or whether the difference is merely verbal. No citations or reading list; I know the literature and want the instrument.
