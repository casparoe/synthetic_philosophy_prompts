My philosophy of science seminar has just moved on to the epistemology of thought experiments, and I would rather settle what to demand of an account before I spend the next three weeks with the candidates. I am drafting a term paper that applies an account to cases its proponents did not have in mind, so what I want from you is the specification: the desiderata any account of how a thought experiment can produce knowledge has to satisfy, with the case or consideration behind each one.

Fix the purpose this way, since adequacy is relative to it. The account must work as a referee's checklist. I should be able to read a thought experiment I have never encountered, in a field where I am not an expert, and get something determinate out of it: what generalization the imagining licenses, what sort of support it provides, and which step is the one that could fail. A taxonomy that sorts thought experiments into kinds but never issues in a verdict does not count as meeting this.

For each desideratum give me four things: a one-sentence statement of the requirement; the case or consideration motivating it (a real thought experiment, one of the two below, or a general point about what imagination is and is not); what failure looks like, that is, what an account violating it would be committed to; and a tag — non-negotiable, negotiable but costly, or camp-specific, in which case say whose camp and what that camp gains by insisting.

Here is the material I want the list tested against.

Case A — the Lampless Fish. A species develops inside an opaque skull. The only light reaching its retina comes from an organ inside that skull, whose output is modulated by the fish's own movements; nothing outside causes any of its visual input. Still, it reliably avoids obstacles and catches prey it could not otherwise locate. As I have set it up, the imagining supports two incompatible readings: an empirical one, that vision does not depend on external illumination statistics, and a conceptual one, that these states are not visual states at all, since nothing the fish represents causes what it represents. Nothing in the scenario selects between them, and the selection seems to be made by whoever tells the story.

Case B — the Slow Valve. An engineer imagines a valve in a long water main closing over an hour instead of instantly, and concludes that the surge produced by instant closure simply does not arise, so surge is a matter of closure speed. A colleague supplies a detail the original description left open: the valve stutters, closing and partly reopening several times. The intuition reverses, and the stuttering closure can be worse than the instant one.

Two real cases I also want covered. First, the 2011 result reported in Science that a riderless two-wheeled device can be self-stable with no gyroscopic contribution from its wheels and no positive caster geometry — the two mechanisms everyone's mental picture of a bicycle leans on. Second, the black hole firewall argument, in which unitary evaporation, an uneventful horizon for the infalling observer, and the monogamy of entanglement cannot all be kept for an old hole.

Then the harder questions, which are the point of the exercise.

1. Which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate accounts, and why that one rather than the flashier ones?

2. Here is a trio I cannot see how to satisfy jointly, and I want to know whether it really is unsatisfiable: (i) a thought experiment can move a competent thinker from not believing its conclusion to believing it, without any new evidence from the world; (ii) everything a thought experiment delivers is implicit in premises the thinker already accepted, unpacked by ordinary inference, since otherwise the imagining is doing evidential work it has no license to do; (iii) mental imagery contributes no information about the world, so what was not derivable before the imagining is not derivable after it. If this is genuinely inconsistent, say which item has to give and whether giving it up amounts to enrolling in a camp. Flag any other subset you take to be jointly unsatisfiable — an inconsistency in the list is itself a result, and I would rather have that than something tidy.

3. I have a candidate requirement I do not know what to do with, and I want your judgment on whether it belongs. Galileo's falling-bodies reasoning proceeds by pictured steps that compound — tie the two bodies together, consider the pair — and the picture forces a contradiction that no further picturing removes. The sorites proceeds in the same register: remove one grain, picture it, still a heap; every step is picturable, and the picture forces a conclusion nobody accepts. An account that locates the force of a thought experiment in what can be pictured and what follows from it owes an explanation of why one iteration tracks the world and the other does not, and it may not appeal to our already knowing which is which. Call this the parity constraint. Is it a genuine desideratum, or a demand that only makes sense once one has decided in advance what the answer will look like?

4. Which items the literature routinely demands are not requirements at all? I want at least two, with an account of the work they are actually doing — I suspect some of them are conclusions wearing the costume of criteria.

5. Which distinctions must the list force an account to preserve? I have in mind especially the difference between a thought experiment whose reasoning is bad, one whose reasoning is good but whose imagined mechanism misdescribes the world, and one that is sound and has merely been outrun by experiment. Where does the bicycle result sit, and does the list you have written have the resources to say so?

Finally, and briefly — two or three sentences each, with no exposition of the positions, which I know — how the three main families fare against your list: the view that a thought experiment is a disguised argument and nothing more, the view that it is a kind of intellectual perception of a law, and the view that it is a model run in the head whose credentials are exactly those of any other model. Close by running Case A, Case B, the bicycle, and the firewall through the checklist, one or two sentences each, so that I can see whether it actually operates.
