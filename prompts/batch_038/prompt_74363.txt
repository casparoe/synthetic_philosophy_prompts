**DRAFT: What the heterogeneity problem costs**

Hedonism about well-being says that how well a life goes is fixed by the balance of pleasure over pain in it. Before that claim can be evaluated, the hedonist owes an account of the term doing all the work. And here is the difficulty. Consider the pleasure of cold water after a long run; the pleasure of the last chord of a piece finally resolving where you knew it would; the pleasure of suddenly seeing why a proof goes through; the pleasure of a good stretch; the pleasure of being recognised by someone whose opinion you wanted; the pleasure of relief when a toothache stops. Introspectively, these seem to share no felt ingredient. There is no phenomenological substance present in all of them and absent from tedium and misery. The only thing they obviously have in common is that we don't want them to stop — which is why Sidgwick's worry, restated, is that pleasure looks like a word we apply to whatever we happen to want, and then promote to the rank of an explanation.

There are four familiar repairs.

(i) *Monistic hedonic tone.* There is one quality, felt in all pleasures, orthogonal to sensory content, varying only in intensity — a common varnish on otherwise unrelated pictures.

(ii) *Pluralistic hedonic tone.* Concede that there is no single quality, but insist on a family of related hedonic qualities, resemblances rather than a shared ingredient. I think this is underexplored and Labukt has argued for something like it.

(iii) *Attitudinal or desire-based accounts.* Pleasure is not a feel at all but a way of standing to a feel: to take pleasure in an experience is to desire, for its own sake and at the time, that it continue. Heterogeneity is then trivially expected, since the attitude can take any content whatsoever.

(iv) *Functional-role accounts.* What unifies the class is a job — being the state that reinforces, that is sought, that shows up in comparative judgement — not a property.

My claim in this paper is that these four are not independent options of roughly equal standing but points on a trade-off curve, and that the curve is the actual philosophical content of the heterogeneity problem. Hedonism needs two things from its central term. Call them **A** and **B**. A: the term must be substantive — it must deliver a single currency of welfare, commensurability, and the distinctively hedonist thesis that how things feel from the inside is what matters. B: the term must fit the cases, i.e. survive the heterogeneity list above.

Monistic tone maxes out A and simply fails B: no introspectively plausible single ingredient survives contact with the list. Pluralistic tone is halfway: it fits the cases by weakening the resemblance relation, but the weaker the resemblance the less work the notion does in explaining why these, and only these, states are good. Attitudinal accounts max out B and pay for it out of A. If pleasure just is what is desired when had, then 'pleasure is good' becomes very close to 'experiences that are desired when had are good', which is a desire theory wearing a hedonist's coat; the claim that it is the felt character of a life that matters has quietly gone missing, and with it the reason anyone was drawn to hedonism in the first place. So the trade is systematic: the better a repair handles the heterogeneity problem, the less recognisably hedonistic the resulting theory is. The problem's real bite is not that hedonism is false. It is that fixing hedonism costs hedonism its job, and that every repair relocates the difficulty rather than removing it.

Then I want to press a fifth option, which I think is the only place left for a hedonist to stand. Commensurability does not require a shared quality; it requires a shared *scale that subjects actually use*. We do rank cold water against a resolved chord against understanding a proof. We make these trades constantly, and we make them without introspecting any common ingredient. So: pleasure is any experience carrying a positive value on *the* hedonic variable, where that variable is identified not by what it feels like but as the one whose ordering the subject's own comparative judgements track. Heterogeneity is then completely free — any content can carry the value — while there is still exactly one currency, because there is one ordering. This gets A without monism and B without collapsing into a desire theory, or so I want to argue.

I know the objections and I am not sure how to answer them, which is one reason I'm asking for comments.

*Circularity.* The variable is whatever the judgements track — but track which ordering? If the answer is the ordering of pleasantness, I have defined the definiens by the definiendum. If the answer is the ordering of goodness, I have not stated a hedonism but a preference theory with a phenomenological alibi.

*Underdetermination.* Subjects' rankings are noisy, context-sensitive, and famously intransitive or duration-blind in ways the psychology of affect suggests. If the tracked ordering is unstable, 'the hedonic variable' may be a posit doing no work, and the single currency turns out to be several incommensurable ones after all.

*Mixed experiences.* Tragedy we enjoy, food that hurts, the pleasure of a stretch that borders on pain. If a whole experience can be pleasant while containing unpleasant components, then the positive value cannot be a property of the experience's felt character, only of a net balance across parts — which saves the currency claim but turns hedonism into a thesis about aggregation, and inherits every problem aggregation theses have.

Conclusion I'm aiming at: the question 'what do pleasures have in common?' is mis-specified. The right question is what hedonism needs pleasure to be in order to do the work it is hired for, and whether anything can do that work while also fitting the cases. My conjecture is that nothing can, which would leave hedonism not refuted but unstable — permanently one revision away from becoming some other theory.
