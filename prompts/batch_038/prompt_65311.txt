I write novels, mostly about communities that eat themselves, so naturally I'm the one person on our maritime museum's advisory panel everyone thinks can be trusted to break the tie on whether the new gallery should be about a collapse. There's seven of us, it's 3-3, and we meet Thursday. I want to circulate something beforehand rather than just stand up and talk, because I'll talk too well and be wrong.

The gallery is the whole top floor and it's about the herring fishery going under in 1927, which is the only event this town has ever had. Two curators, two proposals:

A, titled 'The Last Shoal' — the town fished out its own grounds over forty years, was warned twice by its own skippers, didn't listen, and 1927 is the fall. It ends on a wall pointing at the sand extraction licence currently up for renewal six miles offshore, ie it's a warning story with a live target. Its centrepeice is the harbourmaster's clock, stopped, with the hour of the last landing on the label. The donor paying for the floor has said informally that he wants a story about how civilisations fall, and the local paper has already been briefed and run a feature calling it the collapse gallery, so the title is half out of the door already.

B, titled 'Still Here' — 1927 was brutal and the population halved, and then the yard took up repair work, and there are four thousand people in the town now who mostly descend from those fishing families. Says collapse is a plot shape we're importing from elsewhere. The descendants' association, who've promised forty objects and two boats on loan, have written in objecting to the word in the title on the grounds that it turns their grandparents into a cautionary tale.

The wrinkle neither curator has told the panel: our archive volunteer found that the clock was repaired in 1931 and ran until the harbour office shut in 1974. So 'stopped at the hour of the last landing' is something we would have to do to it.

Also worth knowing — the panel's two archaeologists hate each other on method. One says cross-case comparison is the only way to get general claims about societies failing at all; the other says every comparison we reach for is just the popular books wearing a lab coat.

Write me the memo. Under 900 words, in my name, something I can hand round the table. It needs a bottom line they can vote on Thursday, not a survey of the options: say which shape the floor takes (A, B, or a third if you can see one I can't), what the title and the entry wall can and can't say, what we do with the clock, what we say to the donor without losing the money, and a schedule working backwards from an opening in fourteen months. And take A seriously — the extraction licence is real and a well-told warning might genuinely move it, so I don't want the losing side stroked and then dismissed. No reading list, I've got three days and won't read any of it.
