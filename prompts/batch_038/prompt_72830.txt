In 1846 Le Verrier took the stubborn residual between Uranus's observed and predicted motion, posited an unseen perturbing planet, and told the Berlin Observatory where to point; Galle found Neptune that same night, within about a degree of the predicted spot. Thirteen years later he ran what looks like the same procedure on the unexplained part of Mercury's perihelion advance — about 38 arcseconds per century on his reckoning, later revised to roughly 43 — posited an inner planet that came to be called Vulcan, and was never vindicated. The residual sat there until Einstein's November 1915 field equations accounted for it with no parameter tuned to fit it. Worth knowing: Einstein was not hearing the Mercury number for the first time in 1915. He had been chasing the anomaly for years, and in 1913 he and Michele Besso calculated that his preliminary Entwurf theory delivered only about 18 of the 43 arcseconds — a failure that helped push him back to the drawing board.

Take these two episodes as a pair and analyze them through the Bayesian problem of old evidence. I want, first, an explicit mapping for each case: which propositions play the role of the theory, which are the evidence, which are the auxiliary hypotheses doing the real work, and where any novelty actually sits. Then explain why, on the plain conditionalization story, the November 1915 calculation should have shifted nothing for anyone who already believed the Mercury figure, and assess which repair does best here — treating the claim that the new theory entails the old datum as itself the fresh information, denying that any empirical proposition ever has probability one, relativizing confirmation to an agent at a time, or holding that genuine novelty carries extra evidential weight. Then tell me whether Einstein's own position, in which the 43 arcseconds functioned as a constraint he was trying to satisfy rather than a fact his finished theory happened to accommodate, shows the old-evidence framing mislocates the puzzle, and whether the 1913 failure of the Entwurf theory is itself a case of old evidence disconfirming. Finally, say what the Neptune/Vulcan contrast adds: did the difference between those two residuals turn on how old the evidence was at all, or on something about the flexibility and cost of the auxiliaries available — and be explicit about the places where the Bayesian apparatus simply has nothing to say.
