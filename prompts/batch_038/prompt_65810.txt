Analyze the Beltracchi affair as a test case for the philosophy of forgery — not the criminal case, which I know, but the theory of value. The facts I will take responsibility for: Wolfgang Beltracchi spent decades placing paintings attributed to Ernst, Campendonk, Léger, Derain and others on the market; he did not copy known works but invented new compositions in the target artist's manner, and manufactured the provenance to match, down to a faked period photograph in which his wife appears as her own grandmother. The trade accepted these works for years — one attributed Campendonk sold at auction for close to three million euros — and the operation collapsed only when pigment analysis found a white that was not available when the painting was supposedly made. Convicted in 2011 and released in early 2015, he has since worked openly under his own name, including a series painted in the manner of well-known masters, at prices that by all accounts are a small fraction of what the forgeries commanded.

Set the case against the three main positions on what forgery costs: that a visually indistinguishable fake is aesthetically as good as the original; Lessing's claim that criticism is a historical discipline, so a forgery can succeed as appearance and still fail as achievement; and Dutton's account of the work as a performance whose value depends on whose performance it is. For each, give the mapping honestly — what counts as the achievement here, who performed it, and what the fooled experts failed to perceive — and then say where the position is strained or simply silent. I want pressure applied at three points in particular: that these were invented compositions rather than copies; that nothing looked wrong until chemistry spoke; and the post-2015 work, in which the same hand does openly what it used to do deceptively. Does the case show the earlier prices were payment for a name rather than a performance, or that the name was part of what was performed? Close with the position the episode best supports, how confident you are in that verdict, and the single point you are least sure of.
