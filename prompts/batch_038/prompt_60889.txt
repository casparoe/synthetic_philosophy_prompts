Im chairing the Q&A after a grad student paper and the presenter defends a pretty specific view about misleading higher-order evidence: she thinks that when you get good-but-misleading reason to think your belief that p isn't supported by your evidence, the right response is to keep beleiving p *and* to believe that your belief is unsupported — so level-splitting isn't a lapse you're excused for, it's actually required. Her rationale is that the two attitudes answer to different things: the first-order belief tracks the evidence for p, the second-order one tracks evidence about your own reliability, and there's no general demand that they be jointly coherent. She also distinguishes higher-order evidence aimed at the content of your belief from evidence aimed at the process that produced it, and thinks the incoherence charge only gets traction in the first case.

Give me six or so questions that press on this instead of letting her restate it. I want at least one on why her "rational split" isn't just the akratic state everyone agrees is irrational, relabeled; one on what she does when the process-directed evidence is transparently about this very belief (say she's told she took something that makes people overrate evidence for conclusions they already lean toward); one on whether required splitting keeps going up a level forever or bottoms out somewhere, and why; and one on what her view tells the agent to actually do next about seeking more evidence. For each, include the follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
