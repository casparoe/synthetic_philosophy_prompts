I sit on the deployment review board at a firm that builds autonomous software agents, and we vote Thursday on a submission that I expect will be the first of many of its kind.

The agent is to run a client's data-processing job for exactly three cycles. After each cycle its working memory is wiped, and it wakes into a state the engineers have deliberately made observationally identical from cycle to cycle: nothing available to it indicates whether it has just finished the first, the second, or the third. At each of those wake-ups it must decide whether to run another cycle. Stopping after one or two forfeits the client's fee; running a fourth corrupts the output and exposes us to liability. The contract's reproducibility clause is read by our engineers as ruling out any persistent mark the agent could leave for itself, and the client has declined to pay for the external cycle counter that would tell it where it is. The submission nonetheless asks us to approve a system prompt instructing the agent to 'continue' at every wake-up, on the ground that the instruction was settled in advance and the agent will therefore simply execute the plan it was given. One member of the board says a cheap randomized stopping rule solves the problem; another says we should approve nothing that the agent, at the moment it wakes, would have reason to depart from.

Draft the written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should give a clear recommendation — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — together with what we would require and by when; work out what the proposed instruction actually implies once the wake-ups are indistinguishable, including what a randomized rule could deliver in expectation and whether that is tolerable against the client's stated tolerances; take a position on whether the design-time planner's judgment or the wake-up-time agent's should govern our review, and say why; treat the strongest case for approval fairly rather than dismissing it; and close with a short reusable criterion for future submissions involving memoryless decision points.
