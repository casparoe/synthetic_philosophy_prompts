Mechanical engineer here — I read a lot of biomechanics and evolution stuff for fun, and over the last year I keep coming accross the same argumentative move and I can't tell whether there's a standard word for it.

Here's the move. Someone measures, carefully and with real instruments, that a body part does a job well. Then the discussion section says this shows why the part is there. Two I remember:

- a paper arguing that the ridges on our fingertips work like tyre treads, channelling water away so you can hold onto wet things. Fine, they measured the friction, I believe them. But then the ridges are treated as having evolved for wet grip and nothing further is said.
- the endless argument about the human chin, where one side measures that it does something (resists chewing loads, whatever) and takes that as settling the separate question of whether it was shaped by selection at all.

What bugs me is that the usefulness actively suppresses the second question. A part can be genuinely useful and still have shown up for an unrelated reason — because the geometry forces it, because it rides along with another part that was selected, because both sexes share a developmental recipe and only one of them needs the thing. Measured utility is compatible with all of those, and once you've got a nice number on the function nobody seems to feel any need to go check the comparative evidence.

The part I most want a handle on is what happens when someone does raise the alternative. The reply is basically "but the function is real, we measured it." An origin question gets answered with a utility claim, and everyone accepts that as a response.

I'm not asking about the case where something got started for one reason and was later pressed into service for another (I know that's a thing people talk about). I'm also not asking about armchair stories with no data. And I know there's a general -ism about assuming every trait is an optimally designed adaptation — but I think this specific step can happen even in a paper that's careful about that attitude, which is why I suspect it's its own thing rather than just a piece of that.

So: is there an established concept or literature for this exact slide from "does job J" to "is there because J," including the dialectical version where the utility measurement gets used to bat away the alternative explanation? If there is, what's the closest one, and how does what I described differ from it? If nothing fits properly, give me a name and a real definition — with boundary conditions, i.e. what would *not* count as an instance, since there has to be a legitimate way to argue from function to origin or else the term just amounts to "all functional biology is bad." I'm not after the label, I'm after the carving: exactly where the inference goes wrong, where it doesn't, and how you'd tell those two apart reading a paper.
