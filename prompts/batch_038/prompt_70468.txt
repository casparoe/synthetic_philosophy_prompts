You are a philosopher of mind with serious training in the metaphysics of realization and in the relations between psychology and the neurosciences. I am going to ask you to diagnose a stalemate rather than to referee it, so please think carefully and lay out your reasoning before you commit to a view.

I should say at the outset who is asking. I am not an academic and have no formal training. I write and certify control software for industrial machinery, and for the past several years I have been reading philosophy of mind on my own, mostly at night. I also run a small reading group of six: two of us write software, one is a nurse, one teaches secondary-school physics, and two are retired. We have spent the better part of a year on the argument that mental states cannot be identified with brain states because the same mental state can be realized in physically very different systems.

To be sure you are engaging the dispute we are actually stuck in, here is how it looks from where I sit. On one side: since Putnam first pressed the point, the observation that pain-bearers on this planet range from primates down to mollusks, that neuroanatomy diverges sharply across those groups, that convergent evolution routinely produces similar capacities out of dissimilar structures, and that we can coherently imagine machines, Martians, or a nation-sized population of people organised so as to mimic the causal economy of a single nervous system. On the other side, the identity theorists and reductionists have their replies, and they have had them for decades: that a multiply realized property can be identified with a disjunction of its realizers, or with the second-order property of having some realizer or other that plays the right role; that when you look closely the supposedly unrelated realizers always turn out to share something at a deeper level, so the reduction can still be carried through case by case; that if the realizers genuinely shared nothing, the mental property would be squeezed out of the causal story altogether and would not be worth believing in; and, more recently, that true multiple realization is simply much rarer in nature than the argument assumes. Both sides know all of this. Nobody in my group, and as far as I can tell nobody in the literature, has moved an inch.

Here is my working hypothesis, offered strictly as a hypothesis and not as something I have read anywhere. The dispute is stuck because both sides treat the same property as though it were a two-place relation settled once and for all by the world, whereas in my day job realization is always indexed to a purpose and to a set of failure conditions. Two components that are perfectly interchangeable in one circuit are absolutely not interchangeable in another, because they fail differently: one browns out gradually, the other latches into a stuck state. Which physical features of a component count as the function it realizes depends on which hazards you were asked to analyse. I suspect the same goes here, and that the two camps are answering different questions under one slogan — one asking whether psychology can be dispensed with as an explanatory level, the other asking whether the world carves mental kinds at their joints. If so, each side's central evidence is beside the point, and the argument has been circling for half a century because it is missing an argument place.

Please do the following, in this order.

1. Begin with a short brainstorm. List the candidate diagnoses you think deserve consideration — for instance a merely verbal dispute over the words property or realization; a hidden empirical question about how much real variation there is across nervous systems; a disagreement about what counts as a successful reduction or explanation; two different questions sharing a single heading; a contest over which intuitions or cases get to count as data; or the possibility that the question is ill-posed rather than merely hard. Do not evaluate them yet. Just name each one and say in a single line what would have to be true for that diagnosis to be the correct one.

2. Then argue for the diagnosis you think is right, using what each side actually says. Test my hypothesis honestly rather than flattering it: if the purpose-relativity picture is a red herring, or if it smuggles in one side's conclusion, or if it is just a restatement of something the reductionists already say, tell me so plainly. I also want you to assess a distinction of my own, which I am not sure is sound. Call the first kind substitution, where one design is replaced by another deliberately built to fill the same role, and the second kind convergence, where wholly unrelated designs independently arrive at solving the same problem, as bat wings and bird wings do. My suspicion is that substitution cases are dialectically inert, because there the shared role was imposed by a designer and the reductionist can simply point back to it, whereas only convergence cases raise a genuine threat to identity claims. If you agree, that would substantially narrow the debate, and I want to know whether you do agree and why.

3. Then tell me what would count as progress, given your diagnosis: a new distinction, a formal result about property identity or the realization relation, an empirical finding about nervous systems, a change of question, or nothing at all. If nothing, explain why an impasse of this duration is what we should have expected rather than evidence that someone has been slow.

4. Finally, say what a group like mine should do next, bearing in mind that none of us can check the technical literature and that we would rather stop going in circles than cover more ground.

This is not a request for a survey of the positions; I want the diagnosis and the reasons for it. Please do not give me a reading list, a bibliography, or citations — I cannot look them up, and I would rather have your reasoning than your references. Where you are unsure of an attribution, describe the view instead of naming its author. Write formally and at whatever length the argument genuinely requires; a substantial answer is welcome provided every paragraph is doing work.
