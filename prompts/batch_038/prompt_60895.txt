Acting as a professional philosopher of physics here — specifically someone who works on probability in Everettian quantum mechanics and who has actually read the decision-theoretic arguments rather than the popular write-ups — I want you to do a consistency audit on my own views. Think carefully before answering, and start with a short brainstorming pass: list every pair of my numbered commitments below that might pull against each other, including ones that turn out to be harmless, and only then narrow down to the ones that actually hurt. Please don't lecture me about what the many-worlds picture is or how decoherence works, I know that part cold.

Some background: I retired four years ago after about 35 years running an optics and laser spectroscopy lab, and I've finally got around to reading philosophy, which I'd put off my whole career. I've gone pretty deep on Everett lately, mostly because I've watched decoherence eat interference fringes in real apparatus for decades and the no-collapse story feels like the honest one. A physicist friend of mine says my package is incoherent but couldn't say where, and I can't locate it either, so I'd rather you find it than take his word for it.

Here's where I've landed:

1. Unitary quantum mechanics is the whole story. No collapse, no hidden variables, nothing added on top of the universal wavefunction except the ordinary derivative stuff (tables, cats, retirees) that supervenes on it.

2. Decoherence settles the preferred-basis worry and I don't want a separate solution to it. The branch structure is approximate and smeared at the edges and I'm fine with that, it's like asking exactly how many grains make a heap. The vagueness is in my description, not in the wavefunction, which stays perfectly exact.

3. I take the Deutsch/Wallace line on the Born rule: it isn't an extra postulate. Rationality in an Everettian setting pushes you to bet by squared amplitude once you accept things like indifference to mere branch proliferation — if you split a branch into two equal halves nothing physical happened, so nothing you care about should change.

4. Probability is not in the world. I'm a Ramsey type: chance-talk is just talk about what degrees of belief a coherent agent ought to have. There is no objective chance, no propensity, nothing stochastic anywhere in nature. Everett is deterministic and I'm glad about it, I spent my career hating indeterminism.

5. But I do think the squared amplitude is a real physical magnitude belonging to a branch, not bookkeeping. My own way of putting it, and it isn't in the literature like this so please push on it: think of a beam splitter. The intensity down each arm is a real conserved thing, an energy fraction. So I say weight measures how much of me goes down each arm. There's a fixed budget of 1 and it gets carved up at branchings. Basically my own version of Vaidman's measure of existence, but tied to persons rather than worlds.

6. On uncertainty: after the split and before I look, I genuinely don't know which branch I'm on, even though they're all equally real. The self-locating move from Sebens and Carroll is the only honest way I can see to have uncertainty in a deterministic multiverse, and I buy it.

7. Branch counting is meaningless. If a device takes one branch and splits it into a million equal sub-branches, the total weight hasn't budged, so my credences shouldn't budge either. The number of worlds can't be what probability counts.

8. One concrete case and my verdict on it, because I think verdicts are where these things break. A 90/10 beam splitter, and the apparatus is arranged so that I get a fully conscious copy in each arm. Before any environment gets involved — so before decoherence really bites — we run the whole thing backwards and recohere the two arms perfectly, so at the end there's just one of me with no memory of the experiment. Question: during the interval, what should the 90% copy's confidence be that they're the one who comes out the other side? I say 90%, because that's how much of them was in that arm. I'll admit "survives" is doing something weird there, since the recoherence means both amplitudes contribute to the single output.
