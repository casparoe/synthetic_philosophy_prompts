question: Explain the argument Gongsun Long gives in the White Horse dialogue for the conclusion that a white horse is not a horse, and say whether you think that conclusion is true. (10 marks)

student's answer:

Gongsun Long was a Mohist logician and the White Horse dialogue is the most famous bit of the School of Names. He gets asked whether it's admissible to say a white horse is not a horse, and says it is. His first move is that 'horse' is what designates the shape of a thing and 'white' is what designates its colour, and what designates the colour isn't what designates the shape, so the two names can't be the same name.

His second move is the one I find convincing: if you ask someone for a horse they'll bring you a yellow one or a black one, but if you ask for a white horse they won't. So 'horse' and 'white horse' have different extensions, one class of things is bigger than the other, and if the classes are different the terms can't mean the same thing. That's exactly his point, that the white horse class isn't the horse class.

So I think the conclusion is true as long as you read it carefully. It isn't that a white horse isn't an animal of the horse species, obviously, it's that the name 'white horse' picks out something narrower. People who say he's just being silly are confusing intension and extension, which is the whole mistake.

He backs this up with the separateness of properties: whiteness and horseness are seperate because when you look at a white stone you see the whiteness but you don't see the hardness, so each property can be picked out on its own without the others. That's why the Mohists attacked him — they said hardness and whiteness are both in the stone and can't be pulled apart, and if they're right about that then the white horse argument fails too.

Overall a clever piece of philosophy of language, definately not just a word game, tho the conclusion only comes out true if you accept his way of using names.
