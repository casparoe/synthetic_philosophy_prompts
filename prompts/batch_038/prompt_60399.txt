I am helping a historian finish a monograph on responsibility for the outbreak of the First World War, and I need to anticipate how he will rule on a case he has not yet reached. His verdicts so far, in his own grading: Germany bears the preponderance of responsibility, having settled on war as a live option years before 1914 and then pressed Vienna towards action; Austria-Hungary bears substantial but derivative responsibility, since she wanted a local war against Serbia and merely acquiesced in a general one; Russia is culpable notwithstanding her defensive intentions, because general mobilisation made German escalation all but unavoidable; France is mildly culpable, her July assurance to Russia being imprudent rather than a plan; the Serbian government is not culpable at all, since the Sarajevo plotters acted against its wishes and it did give Vienna a warning, however oblique; and Britain is close to exculpated, having spent the crisis mediating and having breached no obligation. He writes, explicitly, against a recent revisionist revival that spreads the blame evenly among the powers.

The unwritten chapter concerns the Ottoman entry of late October 1914. The facts as he sets them out: the empire's approaches to Britain, France and Russia for an alliance had been rebuffed for months; two dreadnoughts built for it in British yards had been requisitioned by the Royal Navy in August; the ships that bombarded the Russian Black Sea ports on 29 October had been German until that summer and still carried German crews under Ottoman colours; the raid was devised by the war minister together with the German ambassador and admiral after attempts to win over the cabinet had failed; the grand vizier and the finance minister opposed involvement and ministers protested afterwards; the war minister blocked any official apology; and entry opened the Caucasus, Mesopotamia and the Dardanelles.

Predict his verdict and its grade. Which earlier ruling is the closest analogue, and which pulls the other way? Name at least two further rules that accommodate all six earlier verdicts just as well but diverge on this one, and say what each yields here. Then propose a case — in this war or a constructed one — that would settle which rule he is running, with what each candidate predicts about it. Finally: does his Serbian precedent commit him to treating the war minister's clique as something other than the state, or can a nation be held responsible across that division without abandoning the principle that let Serbia off?
