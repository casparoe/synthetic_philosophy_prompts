Write a joke about the frame problem. The punchline should turn on the content of the debate rather than on a pun that trades on the word frame, or on a stock gag about robots being unable to cope with ordinary life. It should also make clear that I know what the problem actually is — the difficulty of stating what an action leaves unchanged, so that a formalism doesn't have to be told explicitly that pulling a wagon doesn't change the location of a bomb sitting on it — rather than confusing it with the ramification problem or the qualification problem.

Ideally the joke exploits a mismatch. On one side there is the version that matters in AI research, where the frame problem looks like a tedious but in-principle tractable bookkeeping chore: supply a frame axiom for every fluent and every action, or find some nonmonotonic or circumscription-style device that supplies them by default. On the other side there is the version that took hold in philosophy of mind, where the complaint is that nothing less than holistic relevance-determination could ever settle which of indefinitely many things an agent believes bear on the situation in front of it, so that no amount of axiom-writing is even the right kind of solution. Extra credit if the joke lands on the fact that these are nominally one and the same problem, which allows each side to insist that the other has not understood what the problem is.

The joke should be short enough to tell out loud, and it should still work — differently, but recognizably — for someone who has only encountered the term in passing.
