I'm on the collections committee of a county archives and Thursday we have to decide where the next three years of processing time goes. I want a short memo I can circulate beforehand that actually lands somewhere — a recommendation, not a both-sides.

The choice. Option A: the estate of the family that owned the cannery that employed this town from the 1890s to the 1970s is offering about 60 linear feet — payroll books, personnel files, correspondence, plant photographs — plus a small endowment that, as a condition of the gift, has to be spent processing that collection. Option B: a community-documentation project with two neighborhoods whose families did the cannery work, producing oral histories, church and mutual-aid records, and household papers. No funding attached, so it eats the same staff hours.

What's got me stuck: when I pulled our holdings stats, essentially every item we already have about those workers was created by the company, about them. So A adds detail but not authorship — more of the same voice, including personnel files with supervisors' annotations on who was trouble. B creates first-person records for people who have none, except the participants want a 50-year closure on the interviews and some want to review their transcripts before they open. Half the committee says that control is the entire point and we shouldn't repeat the extraction; the other half says a sealed tape in 2075 is still a silence for every researcher alive now, and we'd be swapping one gap for a better-feeling one.

So recommend, with reasons the losing side could actually answer, and treat their strongest point seriously rather than strawmanning it. Then give me the conditions you'd attach — A's donor restriction and the living-persons problem in the personnel files, B's closure terms — because 'just do both' doesnt survive this budget. Add three or four criteria the committee could apply to the next such decision without relitigating this one.

Finally, say what would change your recommendation: which fact or argument would flip you, and where do you think your own reasoning is most likely to break.
