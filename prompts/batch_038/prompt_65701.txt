A friend and I argued until nearly two in the morning last night about the moral equality of combatants, and toward the end he restated the Walzerian position back to me in terms I could not rebut on the spot. He said it comes to this: "The doctrine holds that a soldier's cause makes no moral difference whatever, so a Wehrmacht conscript and an RAF bomber crewman stand on precisely the same footing and either may permissibly be killed. Walzer gets there by treating war as a contest: pick up a weapon and you forfeit your right not to be killed, the sole ground of the permission being that you are now dangerous. So the justice of the war drops out of the ethics of fighting altogether — which is why Walzer is prepared to call soldiers on the aggressor's side morally innocent, and why nothing beyond war crimes is left to condemn. And because the whole apparatus is an extension of ordinary self-defense law, he has to apply it uniformly, including in a supreme emergency." From which he concluded that anyone who regards aggressive war as the gravest of crimes is obliged to reject the moral equality of combatants outright.

I suspect he is flattening the position, though I got tangled when I tried to say where, and I do not think he invented it out of whole cloth either. Please go through his summary claim by claim: which parts a careful Walzerian would endorse, which are misrepresentations or conflations, and which remain contested even among those who accept the equality. Two specific points I could not settle. First, is supreme emergency really a case of applying the self-defense logic uniformly, or does it cut the other way and import considerations about the justice of the cause into the rules of conduct? Second, what does the equality actually rest on — dangerousness and status, some notion of reciprocity or mutual consent, or a convention that soldiers themselves endorse? Finally, once the summary is corrected, does his conclusion survive: does the revisionist insistence that liability to attack requires responsibility for an unjust threat bite any harder against the accurate version than against his caricature?
