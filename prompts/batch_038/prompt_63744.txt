I am fixing the terms of evaluation for a survey article on the paradox of tragedy, and I want the standard of adequacy settled before any accounts are canvassed. Set out the desiderata an account of our positive response to tragic fiction must meet to count as adequate: for each requirement, the case or consideration that motivates it, and a mark of whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or a demand imported by one particular camp. The purpose is adjudication among the existing families of response rather than the defense of one, so the list should ideally be something all camps could accept — which is exactly why I expect several of the usual demands to fail that test, among them that the distress be a real emotion rather than a make-believe one, that the pleasure attach to the emotion itself instead of to something accompanying it, that the account also cover horror and real grief, and that it explain why some people simply avoid sad films. Then identify any subset of the requirements that cannot be satisfied jointly, and say what forces the choice between them. Finally, name the single requirement that disqualifies the largest number of candidate accounts. A table is acceptable for the list itself. End with a line of the form "Verdict: <requirement>".
