I am one of two readers for a short-essay examination in an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar, and I would like an independent assessment of one answer before scores are entered. Please grade it and write the comments that would justify the grade to the student.

**The examination question (100 points):**

> Set out Dennett's taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. For each rung above the lowest, state what capacity it adds to the rung below it, and identify where the information that fixes the creature's behavior comes from at that rung. Then take a position on this question: is any non-human animal a Popperian creature? In defending your position, apply the counterfactual-cost test introduced in seminar — a creature is to be classified at the highest rung whose signature capacity it can still deploy when the task departs from the conditions under which that capacity was installed, whether the installer was natural selection, an individual reinforcement history, or an enculturating community — and explain what that test is meant to accomplish that a purely behavioral criterion of classification cannot. The test is a device of the seminar's own and is not to be found in Dennett.

**The student's answer:**

> Dennett sorts minds according to how the design of their behavior is fixed. Darwinian creatures are the products of natural selection alone: their responses are built in, and there is nothing an individual Darwinian creature can do to acquire a new one. Bacteria and the simpler invertebrates are Darwinian creatures. Skinnerian creatures add operant conditioning, so that the consequences behavior has had shape it. The environment thus contributes information to the design of the creature in much the way selection does, only within a single lifetime rather than across generations; the rat and the pigeon in the box are the paradigm cases. Popperian creatures go further still: they can generate candidate actions internally and evaluate them before executing any of them, so that what gets killed off is a hypothesis rather than the animal that entertained it. Gregorian creatures, at the top, supplement all of this with tools and language drawn from a surrounding culture. What distinguishes them is that these designs come from outside, and it is this external sourcing, rather than any further change in the architecture of the brain, that produces conscious thought.
>
> The four kinds are successive evolutionary stages. Each appeared after the one below it, and once a lineage has become Popperian it is no longer properly described as Darwinian or Skinnerian, so classification is always a matter of determining which stage a species has reached. Nothing outside the hominid line is Popperian, and no species alive today is merely Darwinian.
>
> I will argue that no non-human animal is a Popperian creature. Under the counterfactual-cost test, a creature counts at the highest rung whose signature capacity survives a departure from the conditions in which it was installed. A rat's capacity to choose was installed by a reinforcement history tied to a particular box, a particular lever, and a particular schedule of delivery. Move the animal from a fixed-ratio to a variable-interval schedule and its organized pressing falls apart: the rat that had learned the box has not learned to choose. Since the signature capacity of the Popperian rung is precisely the evaluation of candidate actions in advance, and since that capacity cannot survive a change in the conditions of installation, the rat is Skinnerian at best. Tolman's maze rats, which formed cognitive maps and showed latent learning in the absence of reinforcement, are often offered as evidence of inner representation, and this much is correct: they represented. But representation is common ground, and the counterfactual-cost test shows that what Tolman's rats had was representation without evaluation, which is the Skinnerian condition. The reasoning generalizes. Because the capacities of every non-human animal were installed either by selection or by a reinforcement history bound to a particular environment, no non-human animal has a capacity that survives departure from the conditions of its installation. Chimpanzees that fish for termites with modified stems look Gregorian, but the behavior is a fixed repertoire passed along by observation, so they belong on a rung below. The Popperian rung is populated by us alone.
