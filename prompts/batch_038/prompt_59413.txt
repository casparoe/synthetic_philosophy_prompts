Write me a dialogue between two AI-safety people stuck in a design review over the shutdown button for a pretty capable agent, who think the problem should be attacked in fundamentally different ways.

- One is a build-it-into-the-utility-function type: give the agent a continuation utility for the post-shutdown branch, calibrated so it's genuinely indifferent between the button getting pressed and not getting pressed — zero incentive to disable the switch, zero incentive to press it itself.
- The other thinks that's treating the symptom: if the agent is uncertain about what it's actually supposed to value and treats a button press as *evidence* about that — a signal from a human who knows more than it does — then leaving the switch alone isn't something you have to engineer in, it falls out of rational deference.

The thing I want the dialogue to actually turn on is a dilemma, and I don't want either of them to hand-wave past it. Indifference-style designs seem to need the press to be walled off from everything else the agent cares about. Make the press a coin flip and the math works beautifully, but nobody wants a corrigible agent that gets switched off at random — the whole point is that it gets switched off becuase of how it's been behaving. Make the press depend on a human's judgment of the agent's conduct, though, and now the press carries information about the world, and the agent can start moving the probability of that information around: curating what its overseer gets to see, managing its own record, performing corrigibility instead of having it.

Have each of them take a real run at escaping the dilemma rather than just restating it — splitting "the press as evidence" from "the causal process that produces the press," say, or restricting what the agent is allowed to condition on, or arguing that the manipulation worry is really a decision-theory problem wearing a corrigibility costume. Both should be sharp and should engage with the strongest version of the other's move, no strawmen. It's fine — honestly better — if they end in a real standoff where each has dragged a cost into the open that the other has to swallow.
