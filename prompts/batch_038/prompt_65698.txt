So the newer minimum wage research seems to have flipped things — the careful studies keep failing to find that raising the wage costs jobs. Doesn't that collapse the efficiency case against it? My thinking is: if employment doesn't fall, the deadweight loss is zero, and all thats left is a transfer from employers and consumers to low-wage workers, which is a value judgement rather than economics. And where there's monopsony power, a wage floor that raises both wages and employment is just correcting a market failure, so its a straight efficiency gain. At which point I cant see what's left of the opposition besides a distaste for redistribution. Where am I going wrong here — and does the same logic carry over to rent control? I.e. if the studies can't find a vacancy or new-construction effect either, is there anything left to complain about there?
