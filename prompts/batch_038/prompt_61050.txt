So I'm assembling a run of problem cases for a seminar on dispositions, and I've been testing them on my old supervisor, whose intuitions are unusually stable. Her record so far, in order:

1. Ordinary wine glass sitting on a shelf: fragile. Trivially.
2. A live wire hooked up to an electro-fink that would drain the charge the instant anything conductive touched it: still live.
3. A genuinely brittle glass packed in a block of styrafoam thick enough to soak up any blow it could realistically take: still fragile.
4. A steel tumbler that isn't brittle at all, but which a bystander sorcerer would shatter the moment anyone struck it: not fragile.
5. A dead wire that the same sort of device would render live if touched: not live.
6. A toughened glass whose molecular structure really is different — it springs back from any impact: not fragile.
7. A brittle glass that is both packed and hooked to a fink that would take the brittleness out of it if struck: still fragile.

She also volunteered a remark when I pushed on 3 and 4 together. She said what matters is 'what the thing is actually made of', not what would happen if you poked it — but she then insisted she isn't just a base-fetishist, that there has to be some tie to the counterfactuals or the word disposition stops meaning anything at all.

Next week I'm bringing a case I made up, and I think its going to split whatever rules she's running apart. Here it is: an object whose brittle layers have exactly the molecular structure of ordinary fragile glass — same bonding, same crack-propagation profile — but which is built, all the way through and inseparably, out of alternating leaves of that brittle material and a damping material that absorbs any impact energy before it reaches the brittle leaves. No device, no sorcerer, nothing to unwrap. The damping is what the object is; it can't be removed without destroying the thing. Strike it and it doesn't break. It never will, in any circumstance. Fragile or not?

What I'm after:

- The single rule that best fits all seven verdicts plus the remark. Formulate it properly, not as a slogan.
- At least two other rules — from the literature or invented — that fit the record just as well, and exactly where each diverges from the first one on my new case. I'd also like to know which candidate rules the record positively rules out, and which of the seven verdicts is doing the real work of ruling them out versus which are redundant.
- Your best guess at her verdict on the new case, with the reason you'd bet on it, and a rough confidence.
- The closest analogue in her existing record, and why the analogy drags in one direction rather than the other — I suspect 3 and 6 both claim it and pull opposite ways.
- If the record genuinely underdetermines her, what case should I bring the week after to settle it?
- Flag anything incoherent in that remark. She looks like she's straddling two incompatible commitments and I can't tell whether she's noticed.

Don't give me a tour of the fink/mask/mimic debate, I've read it — work with her record. And if you think one of the seven verdicts is just wrong, say which and what it costs her. British English, please.
