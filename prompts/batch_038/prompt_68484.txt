I'm a backend engineer at a small regional museum — basically I am the whole IT department, I maintain the collections database and the public site. No philosophy training at all, so bear with me, I just need to think this through before a meeting on Thursday.

About six months ago a grad student going through our boxes on a mid-century illustrator whose estate we hold (he's the famous one, his stuff is in every design history survey) found out that he spent the 60s and 70s working as a paid police informant. His testimony was the main evidence that put two men away for a very long time, and one of them is now thought to have been innocent. The researcher thinks he did it for the money and partly to point attention away from himself.

Here's the part I keep turning over. A bunch of the pieces we show, including the ones everybody considers his best work, are courtroom sketches and studies of those two men. The researcher's argument is that the only reason those drawings are so good is that he had access nobody else had, and that access came from what he did. So the thing we admire sits pretty directly downstream of the harm, its not like a separate bad thing that happened to also be done by a good painter.

Now there's a fight. One of the men's daughters wants the sketches pulled. The other family wants them kept online because they're evidence for an innocence petition that's still moving. Take them down and we might be destroying the only public record; leave them up and we're making money and prestige off a man's testimony that wrecked lives. Also, practically, we can't do a clean removal anyway — the CMS leaves a stub page for anything hidden and a school district curriculum links to the item IDs.

But my question isn't the logistics, someone above me can decide that. It's what I should be arguing for. Is there any version of "the drawings are excellent and what he did was monstrous" that actually holds up, or is that just a comfortable way of not thinking about it? And am I supposed to distrust the fact that I find them beautiful — I've been staring at these things for two years and I do — or is my reaction beside the point of what we should do with them?
