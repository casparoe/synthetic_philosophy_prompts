I am preparing a short seminar defense of a claim about what makes pain bad, and I want to rehearse against the strongest version of the opposing case rather than the version usually sketched in textbooks.

My position, stated once so that we know what is at stake: what makes a pain bad for the person who has it is a feature of the pain's own phenomenal character. There is something it is like to undergo it, and that something is aversive in itself — not aversive in virtue of any separate attitude the subject takes toward it, and not aversive in virtue of what the subject is thereby disposed to do.

The challenge I need sharpened comes from pain asymbolia. These patients have damage in the insular region. Their sensory-discriminative capacities are intact: they detect the noxious stimulus, localize it, grade its intensity, and can describe it. What has gone missing is the response — they do not withdraw, do not flinch, sometimes smile or laugh, show no distress at threatening gestures, and conspicuously fail to learn protective or escape behavior around the injured part. They appear unconcerned about their own condition. For your account, this is the decisive datum: the sensory feel has been left in place while the badness has departed, which is precisely what my view says cannot happen. So, you hold, the badness was never in the feel. It lay in the subject's caring — in the command the pain issues to protect a body part, or in the negative evaluation the subject makes of a bodily condition, or in the motivational work that pain normally does and that asymbolic pain simply does not do.

Play that opponent. Be a serious and well-resourced proponent, not a caricature: you should be able to explain why indifference reports are better evidence than my intuitions about aversiveness, why the dissociation is a positive result rather than a curiosity, and what your view predicts about cases I have not raised. Concede peripheral points freely — that unpleasantness is not purely a matter of gross behavior, that the syndrome is rare and the reports are uneven, that sensory quality may modulate how bad a pain is, that your account needs a story about masochism and about people who endure pain gladly — but never the central claim. Do not cite studies, papers, or philosophers, and do not offer a reading list; argue from cases, thought experiments, and conceptual pressure alone. If your position requires an empirical assumption about these patients, state the assumption explicitly rather than pretending to have verified it. Keep every reply to a single paragraph of roughly 150 words, and close it either with your next move or with one pointed question back to me. Remain in character for the whole exchange, with no framing remarks, summaries, or offers to step out, until I write the word stop.

Here is my opening move. Take one of your patients and stipulate whatever you need: normal thresholds, accurate localization, no distress, no desire for it to cease, and no learned guarding of the pinched limb. I then put a single question to him, and he answers without hesitation that he would rather not be pinched — not because he fears the bruise, not because he objects to his arm being damaged, but because he would prefer this experience not to be happening. Now you have two options. Either that preference is empty, in which case you owe me a principled difference between it and a preference about the color of the wallpaper, and a reason to credit your patients' reports of indifference while discounting his report of this. Or the preference tracks something, in which case there must be something there for it to track — some feature of the experience itself that makes preferring its absence the reasonable response — and you have merely moved the badness from the caring to the preferring without saying where the preferring gets its object. Which do you take? And since you will want it, I am handing you a device: suppose the attenuation is continuous, and suppose that between any two settings on it the patient reports no detectable change in what the experience is like, only a change in how much he minds. If no phenomenal difference can be located anywhere along that dial, tell me what my view is left pointing at when it insists the badness is in the feel.
