Our VP answered "if we don't build it, someone else will," and the room took that as ending the discussion. Since then I've noticed the sentence doing several jobs that don't look compatible, and a colleague made it worse: she says that if it's true of every company, then it isn't a prediction about the world at all, it's a description of the trap, and repeating it is part of what keeps the trap running.

Please separate the precise claims that one sentence could be making, varying the things I suspect are being slid between: "someone" as at least one rival versus eventually everyone; "will" as certainty versus likelihood; whether the claim is that the thing gets built anyway or only that it gets built sooner, and by us; whether it concerns our power to affect the outcome, our permission to, or merely our interest in not being the only one abstaining; whether it is causal or is instead evidence about what our refusal would signal; and whether one more entrant changes anything when the harm comes from the whole category rather than from any single builder. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, substantive and probably true, or false. Then tell me which reading the VP actually needed, what further premise that reading requires in order to justify shipping, and whether my colleague's version is one more reading or a confusion. A numbered list I could paste into a design doc would be ideal.
