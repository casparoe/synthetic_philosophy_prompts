I write the definitional sections of regulations for a state environmental agency, and a big chunk of the job is drawing lines where the concept doesn't have one: what counts as a "small" discharge, how long a "reasonable" compliance period is, at what concentration something becomes hazardous. These decide who gets an exemption and who gets fined, and they get litigated, so the line has to survive being applied to cases I never thought about.

I want a procedure I can actually run on a draft definition, grounded in whatever the vagueness literature says that's true about sorites-style borderline cases, that tells me which drafting tool to reach for and in what order to ask the questions — bright numeric threshhold, tiered scale, flexible standard with listed factors, a safe harbor, case-by-case adjudication, or some combination. It should tell me what to do about the fairness complaint from whoever lands just on the wrong side of a line I know is arbitrary, and it should be honest about which steps are only my judgment wearing a procedural costume. Constraints: about an hour per definition, and my reviewers are agency lawyers, not philosophers, so no formal semantics and no apparatus they'd have to be taught.

Then run it on three live ones: when a water body counts as "impaired," the "small generator" exemption from hazardous waste handling rules, and "reasonable time" to cure a noticed violation. At least one of the three should come out a way I'd argue with, and say plainly where the procedure runs out and can't help me. American English, please.
