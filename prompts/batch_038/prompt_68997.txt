I am a software engineer with roughly twelve years of experience, split between backend services and real-time graphics. I have no formal training in philosophy; what I know about consciousness studies comes from popular writing and from arguments with colleagues. I would like you to perform a translation task for me and then audit your own translation, and I would like you to be blunt about where it breaks.

The source material is the philosophical dispute over the Cartesian theater. As I understand it, roughly: there is a persistent intuition that somewhere in the brain there is a finish line, a place where processed information arrives and is thereby presented to a subject, such that whatever has crossed that line is what is conscious. The objection is that this posits an inner viewer, which either regresses or is never located. The rival account, the multiple drafts model, denies both the line and the viewer: there are only parallel, continuously revised streams of processing, each becoming available to influence behavior and memory as soon as it is computed, with no fact of the matter about which stream is the one being experienced at a given instant. A third position, global workspace theory, looks to me less like an abolition of the theater than a reform of it — content becomes conscious by being broadcast widely to many specialist processors, none of which is privileged as the recipient. I have also read that the dispute is usually illustrated with an apparent-motion illusion in which two lights of different colors flash in succession and the subject reports a single light traveling and changing color midway, and that the two competing explanations of that report are labeled Orwellian and Stalinesque: in one, the experience occurs as reported and the record is edited afterward; in the other, the content is reconciled before it is ever experienced. The claim is that no experiment can separate the two, since they agree on every physical and behavioral fact.

If I have any of that wrong, correct it before you translate it. I do not want a strawman of the source debate rendered fluently into my idiom.

The target vocabulary is mine, and I want you to stay inside it for as long as it can bear weight: frame composition and presentation, the swap chain, double and triple buffering, vertical sync and tearing, immediate-mode versus retained-mode rendering, render-to-texture, the commit point in a transaction, write-ahead logs and durable state, optimistic concurrency control and conflict detection, merge semantics and last-writer-wins, revision history versus current state, an event loop with reentrant callbacks, publish/subscribe with many subscribers, blackboard architectures with independent knowledge sources, and the three-way distinction among a value that was computed, a value that was persisted, and a value that was observed.

Work through the following in order:

1. Restate the thesis that there is no single moment or place at which processing becomes conscious, in terms of commit points and presentation. Say what would have to be true of a pipeline for there to be no answer at all to the question of where in it consciousness begins.
2. Restate the homunculus regress. In my vocabulary, any component that reads a finished buffer is simply another consumer, so the regress seems to halt the moment reading stops being viewing. Is that a genuine dissolution of the problem, or have I merely described a system with no viewer anywhere in it and therefore no consciousness left to explain?
3. Restate global workspace theory as broadcast to many subscribers, then tell me whether it remains distinguishable from the multiple drafts model in my vocabulary. If the two collapse into the same architecture from where I stand, decide whether that is because my vocabulary is too coarse to separate them or because the disagreement between them is less substantive than it looks.
4. Restate the Orwellian/Stalinesque fork as a choice between editing state before commit and editing the audit log after commit. Then state precisely what would make those two histories the same computation, and whether the philosophers' insistence that they are indistinguishable is a claim about observability, about persisted state, or about something my vocabulary cannot reach.
5. Say what becomes of what it is like to be such a system. I can describe every state and transition in a pipeline without ever needing a term for its being presented to anyone. Tell me whether that absence is the residue or a category mistake on my part.

Then give me the residue report in three buckets: claims that translate cleanly; claims that translate only with distortion, where you should specify exactly what the distortion costs; and claims that cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all. For each item in the third bucket, say whether the failure is a limitation of the engineering vocabulary, a limitation of the philosophical claim, or evidence that the claim was less determinate than its proponents assume.

I have two hypotheses of my own that I want tested rather than flattered. First, every term I listed is defined relative to an interface or a consumer, and a rendering pipeline always terminates in a display that somebody outside the system looks at — so my vocabulary may be structurally incapable of expressing the denial of an inner viewer, because it invariably smuggles an outer one back in. Second, the thesis that there is no single moment at which processing becomes conscious seems to hold of essentially every pipelined system I have ever built, including headless batch jobs and stream processors that nobody would call conscious, which makes me suspect it is carrying far less weight than it appears to. Tell me whether each hypothesis survives contact with the actual arguments; if either is a bad-faith reading, say so and explain what it misses.

Finally, reverse the exercise briefly: is there anything in this debate that is easier to state in my vocabulary than in the philosophers', such that the engineering framing contributes something rather than merely approximating?

I am not looking for citations, a reading list, or pointers to the literature, and I would rather you not lapse into popular science. I want the translation, the audit, and your own judgment about which side of the residue the fault lies on.
