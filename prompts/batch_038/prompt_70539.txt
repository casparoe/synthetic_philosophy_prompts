I run a volunteer forum for vintage synth repair — about 20k members, 8 of us modding it, nobody paid. Here's the actual problem, and I want an actual decision out of this, not a taxonomy of my feelings.

One member, call him D, is by a wide margin the most useful person on the site. He wrote the reference threads everyone else links to and he answers maybe a third of the technical questions. He is also unbearable to beginners. Not slurs, not harassment, nothing that trips our rules — just a steady drip of contempt: "this has been answered four times, use the search," mocking typos, telling a guy his $900 question was really a $9 question. And the cost is measurable. I went back through a year of new accounts: of the ones who got a reply from D in the beginner sub, about two thirds never posted again. Of newcomers who didn't run into him, about a quarter. So the numbers look lopsided and I should just be able to do the numbers, except I can't, and I want to know whether that's a real objection or squeamishness.

Honest state of my head. Maybe 50-60% I lean toward the view where what matters is total wellbeing, which says D's contribution to a few hundred people's evenings loses to a couple thousand quietly-ruined first days — except I don't really believe wellbeing piles up across people that cleanly, and I don't think "loses the thing he's best at and the only place he's respected" is a small cost. Maybe 25% I lean toward the view where what matters is whether each affected person could reasonably reject the rule I'm imposing, and there it's not obvious at all that D's complaint gets outweighed by a lot of small complaints from people who got dunked on once and moved to Reddit. The rest of the time I think in terms of what a good admin would character-wise do, which doesn't rank my options at all — it just says don't be cowardly and don't be cruel, and those point in different directions here.

Wrinkle: D has offered a deal. Make him a mod with a formal remit over the technical subs and he'll cut it out. My mods are split. Half say take it, it's cheap. Half say that's paying a bully, and it publicly endorses the rule that the more indispensable you are, the more of a jerk you get to be.

What I want:

1. Walk ban / take-the-deal / warn-and-see through each of the three views I described, including where the answer isn't obvious. Does the disagreement between them actually change the verdict, or is it noise at these magnitudes?

2. People have floated three ways to decide when you're split like this: go with whichever view you lean toward hardest; rank the three options under each view and add up the ranks; or take the option whose worst case is least bad. Pretty sure two of those break on my third view, which refuses to rank anything. Which one survives that, and is there a fourth move I'm missing?

3. My own idea, which I suspect is broken: pick the option that's never the worst under any view I take seriously — second best or better everywhere. I don't think it exists here, every option is worst somewhere. Is it ever a coherent rule, or does it just collapse into the least-bad-worst-case one?

4. The thing I can't shake is that I'm being asked to compare how-wrong each view thinks each option is, and I don't see how those quantities live on the same scale. If they don't, is the whole project hopeless, or can it be done without pretending the scales line up?

5. Waiting costs something — the churn keeps happening — but I might resolve my views in six months. Is "decide later" ever right, and how do I tell whether I'm buying information or avoiding a hard conversation?

One more thing: I've noticed I want the answer to come out as take-the-deal, and I'd rather you not just hand me that. If it's ban him, say ban him.
