Our meditation group's monthly ethics evening has handed me next month's session: ninety minutes, a whiteboard, about twenty regular practitioners, several of them EA-adjacent, none of them academics. I want to teach the live question of whether Buddhist ethics is consequentialist. They will arrive certain the answer is an obvious yes — karma keeps score, compassion wants less suffering, done. What I need from you is the architecture of the session, not a summary of the debate.

Lay out what has to be in place before the question is a question for them at all, and in what order. My own guess is that the first job is prying the technical sense of consequentialism loose from the colloquial "cares about outcomes," and the second is getting them to see that karma's automatic bookkeeping may be doing the opposite work from a utility calculation — a rule whose payoff is guaranteed by the structure of the universe isn't a rule you follow because you did the arithmetic. Tell me whether that ordering is right, what depends on it, and what I'd be building on sand.

Then: the single case that should carry the evening, and what it must and must not contain. If your instinct is the Mahāyāna story of the compassionate ship captain who kills the bandit aboard his vessel, say whether that case is load-bearing here or whether it smuggles in an upāya framework I shouldn't hand them yet. Map the misconceptions they'll bring and the ones they'll form mid-session — the anattā-is-really-just-Parfit move, "skillful means, so the precepts are heuristics," "the first noble truth is negative utilitarianism," trolley problems — and say at which precise point each gets headed off rather than merely contradicted.

I came to Buddhism by the Parfit route myself, so the plan should be honest about where that route is a projection onto the tradition rather than a reading of it. Close with the one question I can put to the room that separates someone who followed the argument from someone who merely agreed with me, and name what I should openly decline to cover at this level — I don't want a reading list or scholar names in the session, and I'd rather say so than imply I'm reporting settled results.
