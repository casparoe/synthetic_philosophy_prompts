Not really a philosophy person but I've been reading around the ethics of deception lately because of something that happened at work, and I keep running into a pattern I can't find a word for.

Two examples. One: my manager spent about two years making a point of saying, in front of other people, that she always gives it to you straight and that if your job were ever at risk you'd hear it from her first. Then in March I asked whether the reorg was going to touch our team and she said "nothing's been decided about your team" — which was, as far as anyone can tell, litterally true. I took that to mean we were safe. We weren't. When it came out she was visibly relieved that it hadn't been a lie, and a couple of colleagues agreed with her.

Two: a friend of mine is in a similar spot with his brother. Never once lied as far as anyone knows, but over years built up this standing expectation that he'd volunteer bad news unprompted, and then leaned on that expectation to let people reach conclusions he'd never reach out loud himself.

The pattern I mean: the false belief isn't produced by any single sentence. It's produced by a standing setup the person has carefully installed — a reputation, a promise, a repeated assurance — and then each individual utterance is true, unremarkable, and just slides along the rails the setup laid down. So the test everyone instinctively reaches for, "was what they said false?", never fires, and they get to cash in "technically I didn't lie" indefinitely. And notice the setup itself often isnt even a factual claim; it's a promise or a bit of self-description, so it doesn't look like the kind of thing that could be true or false either, which seems to be why nobody counts it.

Is there an established concept for this? I've read a little about the lying versus misleading distinction and about truthful statements that leave people with false impressions, and none of it seems to capture the fact that the misleading work here is done by the standing context rather than by anything said in the moment. So: what are the nearest existing ideas, how does this differ from each of them, and if nothing really fits, what would you call it and how would you define it? I care way more about the edges than the label — for instance, does it still count if the person installed the reputation with no plan to use it that way, or if they've since come to genuinely believe their own version of events? And what's a case that looks exactly like this but isn't?
