I am in the sixth form and I am responsible for the weekly board of scientific claims in our common room. Last month I wrote up a claim about octopus cognition that I had heard on a podcast and had not checked anywhere else. I was confident it was true, so I put it flatly, without qualification. It turned out to be correct, and a teacher who saw the board told me afterwards that I had no business writing it as I did.

What puzzles me is that I cannot locate the fault. The sentence was true; I sincerely believed it; nobody was misled. Had I hedged it — 'some research suggests' — the board would have read as evasive and, I suspect, been less useful. My teacher's objection appears to be that my grounds were poor, but they were poor in precisely the way my grounds have been for many things I have said and been right about. I would also have written the opposite, just as confidently, had the podcast said the opposite, which is what bothers me most. A classmate pointed out that another pupil had posted a claim on equally thin grounds the same week and had simply been wrong, and that I had been lucky; oddly, that makes me feel worse rather than better.

Was what I did improper, and if so in what respect, given that nothing I asserted was false and I intended to deceive nobody? And would qualifying the claim have genuinely fixed the problem, or only disguised it?
