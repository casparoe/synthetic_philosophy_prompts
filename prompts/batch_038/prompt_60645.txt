Here's a variant I've cooked up for class, call it the Transparency Oracle. At some point in my career an oracle hands me three things: (i) a consistent, recursively axiomatized system F in the language of arithmetic; (ii) a certificate, which is genuine and which I fully accept, that F is consistent *and* that F captures every mathematical judgment I will ever make, including this one; and (iii) a written-out Gödel sentence G(F) for F. I read G(F), chew on it for a few minutes, and find myself plainly assenting: true, and not provable in F.

The tension I want you to sit with: if (ii) really holds, then my assent to G(F) is one of the judgments F is supposed to capture, which seems to push toward F proving G(F), which collides with the consistency certificate. So either the stipulations can't all be jointly satisfied, or something subtler is going on. Note that the usual escape hatch — the oracle's testimony is fresh information, so I'm really running F-plus-oracle rather than F — is precisely what (ii) is designed to slam shut.

And the Hofstadterian wrinkle: on the strange-loop picture, a system that represents itself well enough to cross levels is exactly the kind of thing that would look, from the inside, like it was seeing the truth of its own Gödel sentence. So the datum the anti-mechanist leans on might be what mechanism predicts — the loop commenting on itself — rather than evidence of stepping outside it.
