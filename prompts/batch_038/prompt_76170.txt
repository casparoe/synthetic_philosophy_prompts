ok so I just spent three hours in a pub with my friend Ravi (physics PhD, quantum foundations, thinks philosophy is a waste of time except when it isn't) arguing about the Principal Principle, and I've come away more confused than when I sat down, so I want to lay out my actual verdicts case by case and see whether any principle survives them. He kept saying I was being incoherent and I kept saying I was being ordinary, and neither of us could produce the principle that would settle it.

The cases. (1) A friend rolls what she swears is a fair die and tells me the chance of a six is 1/6, and I know nothing else relevant: my credence in six is 1/6. Obvious. (2) Same setup, except I'm leaning over the table and I can see the die tumbling and it's plainly coming up six: my credence is about 1, not 1/6. So the deference rule can't be unconditional; some evidence is off-limits. (3) She rolls that same die thirty times and I watch six come up every single time. On roll 31 my credence in six is way above 1/6, and I've also stopped believing her that it's fair. So a record of outcomes can override a chance claim. (4) But now: my stats prof says a certain radioactive atom has chance 1/2 of decaying in the next hour, and I'm only 0.7 confident her theory is correct. I compute 0.7 × 1/2 = 0.35 and that feels like the right answer — I'd be genuinely surprised if you told me my credence should just be 1/2. So here I *am* letting my opinions about the chance itself in, which seems to clash with (1), where the announced chance gets swallowed whole. (5) The one Ravi wouldn't let go of: a machine flipped a coin inside a sealed box an hour ago, I'm told the machine's chance of heads was 1/2, and I've seen nothing since. Credence: 1/2. I'm sure of that. But I'm equally sure that the chance of heads isn't 1/2 anymore — the coin is lying on the felt right now, it's either heads or it isn't, and if you asked me for the chance *now* of that toss having come up heads I'd say 0 or 1. So I'm deferring to a chance that has, on my own view, expired, and I cannot articulate why that isn't just a confusion. (6) Related: I want to say that in a fully deterministic world a well-made die still has chance 1/6 and I should still defer to it. And I also want to say that if determinism is true then every chance is really 0 or 1, because that's what chance is supposed to be. I hold both, Ravi says incoherent, I say it's just how the word gets used, no one budged. (7) Everett. I'm told the Born weight on the spin-up branch is 1/2 and that both outcomes definitely happen. I still think credence 1/2 is correct — not a useful fiction, not a betting strategy, actually correct — and I have no account at all of what the 1/2 is deferring *to*. (8) And this is what ended the argument: Ravi says if you turn the deference rule into a general recipe you follow, it can make you change your mind about what the chances are without acquiring any new evidence, and I said that's plainly absurd, no evidence no change. Then I remembered (3), where I happily change my mind about the chances. I still don't know what the difference is supposed to be.

So: is there one principle, or a small set of competing candidates, that gets as many of these as it can? For each candidate, say which clause is doing the work on which of (1)–(8), and which verdicts it forces me to give up; don't be polite about it.
