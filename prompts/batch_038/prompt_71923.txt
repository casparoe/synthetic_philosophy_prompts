I'm on the residents' association for a pretty run-down estate and last Thursday's meeting went sideways in a way I've been chewing over ever since. Two things were on the agenda: (1) whether we should organise an unpaid volunteer night patrol — pairs of us, torches and radios — because the police response to the burglaries has been effectively nil, and (2) whether we should sign a joint letter with a local campaign group describing the stop-and-search operation here as a form of state violence. The same bloke sank both proposals with the same line: the state has a monopoly on legitimate violence, that's just what a state means. Against the patrol he meant that our walking around would be violence-adjacent vigilantism the state hasn't authorised. Against the letter he meant that force carried out by the police under law isn't the same kind of thing as violence at all, it's legitimate force, so the letter confuses categories. Everyone nodded both times, which is whats annoying me.

I know the line is Weber, roughly that the state is the human community which successfully claims the monopoly of the legitimate use of physical force within a given territory, and I know well enough that he wasn't saying the state is the only body that ever does the hitting. But that hasn't got me anywhere useful in the room.

So can you lay out, as a menu I can actually hold in my head, the different precise claims the slogan can be making? For each reading I want: what "monopoly" is being read as (exclusive practice, exclusive legal right, sole source of authorisation for force that others carry out, successful suppression of rivals?), what "legitimate" is doing (morally justified, legally authorised, widely believed to be authorised — and whether the slide between those is where the trick lives), whether the claim is about a type of organisation or about this particular territory, and whether it comes out as a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, an empirical claim that's flatly false in plenty of places, or something confused. Say which reading he needed for the patrol argument and which for the stop-and-search argument, whether one person can coherently deploy both, and where private security firms, self-defence, and — realistically for us — a crew that already runs the night-time economy round here would sit on each reading. Is there any reading on which the slogan gets you from a definition to a presumption that non-state force is wrong, and what would have to be true for that inference to work? And lastly, does it on its best reading have any actual bearing on whether policing here should be reformed, defunded or abolished, or is it a definition being passed off as a verdict?

Bottom line in your first sentence please, then the reasoning. No preamble and don't restate any of this back at me.
