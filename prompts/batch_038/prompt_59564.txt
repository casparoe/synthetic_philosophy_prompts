Story prompt. I've been chewing on the Sleeping Beauty setup — coin, amnesia drug, waking up on Monday and then maybe again on Tuesday, the whole thing — and every version of it I read turns out to be a philosophy essay wearing a lab coat. I want an actual story instead.

Here's the conceit to build around: the lab that runs the protocol has a night porter who empties the bins in the observation room. At each awakening the subject is asked to write her answer to the question on an index card, and the cards are supossed to be shredded for blinding reasons. They don't get shredded. They get binned, and the porter has been quietly keeping them in a shoebox for years, along with a tally scratched into the back of a storeroom door. So he's the only person in the building holding the complete dataset — every awakening, seen from outside, undamaged by any drug. Meanwhile the two camps of researchers who argue about what she ought to say when she wakes have hardened into departments with their own budgets and their own interns, and neither camp will go near the shoebox.

Write it so the story actually turns on what that tally can and can't settle. I want the ending to land on the gap between counting awakenings and having a belief from inside one of them — the scene should do it, not a speech.

A few constraints, and I'd like them obeyed pretty strictly:
- No jargon anywhere. Don't use the words credence, self-locating, halfer, thirder, or anthropic. Let a reader who knows the debate spot it anyway.
- Stay in the porter's point of view, or close to it.
- Give the cards a physical life — handwriting that changes, a card with something other than an answer on it, that sort of thing. Details matter more than exposition here.
- Hard cap: 700 words for the whole thing, and please actually count. Put a one-line logline at the very top, then the story.
