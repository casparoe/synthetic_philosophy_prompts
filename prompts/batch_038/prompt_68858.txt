Hannah Arendt died in 1975 and never confronted synthetic media. Suppose she were asked to assess a current electoral campaign in which a political organization publishes a continuous stream of generated audio and fabricated documents, and in which, whenever genuine incriminating material surfaces, its spokespeople decline to deny it and instead suggest that it is probably synthetic.

I want two things. First, her likely assessment — but worked out from her specific claims in 'Lying in Politics' and 'Truth and Politics' rather than from a general appeal to propaganda: the treatment of lying as a mode of action that presupposes an agent; the thesis that the modern danger is not that people come to believe falsehoods but that the factual texture on which the distinction between true and false depends is dissolved; and her reliance on historians, journalists, and storytellers standing outside the apparatus of power as the witnesses of factual truth.

Second, and more difficult: in this campaign no individual knowingly asserts a falsehood, because the generated output is never reviewed and the officials themselves do not know which of their records are real. Does Arendt's insistence that lying is deliberate action leave her unable to name what is wrong here, or do her discussions of image-making and of the self-deception of the problem-solvers give her the resources to do so? Commit to one answer, and mark clearly where you are extrapolating beyond what she actually wrote.
