When I ask which claim he's actually making he says it doesnt matter, they all amount to the same thing. I'd like to show they don't.

So give me the full menu of precise claims that one sentence could be standing for: the ones that are true but empty, the substantive ones that might actually be right, the ones that are just false, and the ones that quietly assume an answer to where property rights come from before any taxman shows up. Then tell me which of those readings does the work he needs it to do — making redistribution look like it owes a special justification while the pre-tax baseline stays innocent — and which reading, if any, the Chamberlain argument actually delivers. Does getting Chamberlain buy you 'theft', or something weaker and less useful to him? And if some version of the slogan survives all this, I want to know exactly what it costs him to hold it.
