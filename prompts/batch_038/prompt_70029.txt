I'm 71, spent my working life in soil physics, retired three years ago, and only recently started reading philosophy properly — at the moment its some Margalit on memory and Rieffs case for forgetting, and I cant decide which of them is right.

Here's the situation. Our department closes its archive at the end of the month. Nothing famous: ninety years of field notebooks, instrument logbooks, a wall of thesis drafts with annotations, and the rainfall records my own late supervisor kept by hand for forty-one years. The paper goes to a records centre two hours away; a contractor scans "the historically significant subset"; the rest is pulped. Miriam, who has run the room for twenty-six years, retires the same afternoon.

Her view, which I think is right and which I don't know how to argue for: the point of the place was never retention. It was that the department stayed in a position to be contradicted by its dead — anybody could pull a notebook and find that a thing we all say is not what we actually did. She reckons a scanned subset that nobody opens is worse than the pulping, because it lets everyone feel they remembered. Taped inside the door for years was her one rule: nobody in there says "as everyone knows."

The dean's view, which I also partly feel and have said out loud: you can't run a living department under the gaze of people who died in 1962, and forgetting is how an institution keeps the ability to change.

I've been asked to give the toast at the drinks in the archive room on that last afternoon. About four minutes, maybe forty people: Miriam and two former staff, a couple of emeritus types who appear in those notebooks, the dean, and around twenty grad students who have never been in the building and came for the free wine.

Write it. It has to honour Miriam without becoming a protest that costs her the goodwill she actually wants, and it cannot lean on any version of "the material will live on" — she'd know I don't believe it, and so would everyone over sixty. I want the two views above to do real structural work in it rather than get mentioned, in the language a soil physicist would use and a grad student would follow. And I'd like it to end on something that isn't a resolution, because I don't have one.
