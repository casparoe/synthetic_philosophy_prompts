I'm on a six-person student life committee at a small residential college, and we're stuck. The administration asked us to come up with a recommended alcohol policy for the themed residential houses by the end of the month, and the committee has basically split into a dry-campus wing and a "nobody enforces this anyway, so let's at least make it supervised" wing. One of my colleagues, a classicist who's on the committee as a faculty rep, circulated a paragraph that she says shows the split is a false one, because Plato already solved it. Her paragraph went something like this:

"In the Laws Plato bans wine entirely for anyone under eighteen, on the grounds that young people are already excitable enough and alcohol pours fuel on the fire. From eighteen to forty he permits it only in closely supervised group settings run by an older, sober presider whose job is to steer the drinking and stop it before it goes bad. What's striking is his argument for the supervision rather than the prohibition: wine works like a truth serum, it dissolves the inhibitions that normally keep people's characters hidden, so a man who behaves decently while drunk has passed a real test. That's also why he calls wine the gift of Dionysus, a medicine that relaxes the tension of age and loosens people up enough to be honest. Plato's point is that the supervised setting doesn't just control drinking, it improves the drinker, because the test is also a training."

She's clearly not making it up out of whole cloth and I don't want to just dismiss her, but a couple of things in there feel off to me and I can't pin down which. Can you go through her paragraph claim by claim and sort it into three piles: what a Plato scholar would sign off on, what's a defensible simplification of something more complicated, and what's actually wrong or backwards? I'm especially unsure about the age brackets she gives, about whether the whole point of the supervised drinking is really that it's a test of character, and about that line where wine is described as relaxing people and loosening them up, because I have a vague memory that Plato thinks wine makes you more intense rather than calmer, which would flip her argument. Also she says wine is treated like a truth serum, and I'd have thought the interesting thing is closer to the opposite.

Then, once you've done the audit, I need the actual deliverable: a short memo I can circulate to the committee before our next meeting, maybe 700-900 words, with a concrete recommendation about what our house alcohol policy should be. If your recommendation is conditional, say what the conditions are and who has to be able to deliver them.
