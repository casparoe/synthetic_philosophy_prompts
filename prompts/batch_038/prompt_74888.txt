My friend Ruth keeps telling me whether various sequences of choices count as rational, and I've been logging her verdicts because I cant work out the rule she's running.

1. A guy prefers a kayak to a bike, the bike to a camera, and the camera to the kayak. He starts out holding the camera, and a broker offers him one paid swap at a time, $20 a pop, always to something he prefers more, until he's back where he started and $60 lighter. He'd seen the whole schedule up front. Ruth: obviously irrational.
2. Same kind of cycle, but the offers arrive one at a time with no way of seeing the shape of it ahead of time. Ruth: not irrational. Each choice was fine, he just got fleeced.
3. Someone who knows every Monday that Friday-him will talk him out of the gym, resolves every Monday, caves every Friday, for years. Ruth: irrational, and "but Friday-me genuinely doesn't want to go" is no defense.
4. A person who knowingly lets herself acquire a taste — one concert, then the rent money goes on the band — and afterwards is delighted and wouldn't undo any of it. Ruth: that's a life, not a mistake.
5. Someone who binds herself to a decision rule, the thing she bound herself against never turns up, and the binding costs her plenty. Ruth: rational, that's just the price of doing business with yourself.

Here's the new case. Nadia has known for ten years that every November she wants to buy a boat and every March she wants to sell it. Last June she gave herself a considered policy: no boats between October and January, plus a 30-day cooling-off period her partner knew about. This November she revoked the policy — not from the itch, she insists, but because she came to think it was paternalistic toward her future self and that her boat Novembers had been among the best of her life. She bought, sold in March at exactly the $8,000 loss she'd predicted, and says she'd pay $10,000 to run it again.

Will Ruth call that irrational? I want to know which rules are still live after her five verdicts, what each of them says about Nadia, your best guess with the reasons for it, and one further case I could put to her that would settle which rule she's actually using.
