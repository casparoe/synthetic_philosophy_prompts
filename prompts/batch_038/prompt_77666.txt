Write me a parody customer-support FAQ — the help center page for a subscription service that will do your explaining for you, deadpan corporate helpdesk voice, about 600 words under a few headers with Q&A items. The thing being sent up is inference to the best explanation as a *method*, not any particular person: don't name real philosophers and don't cite anything. The jokes should come from the company being completely upfront about the standard problems in a tone of ordinary helpdesk boilerplate, so a philosopher of science laughs while a lay reader just thinks it's a weird firm.

Beats I want hit:

- plans are tiered by loveliness, and there's a customer asking what the difference is between loveliness and likelihood — the answer should cheerfuly admit they come apart and that only one of them is billed.
- some small print conceding that we guarantee you the best of the lot of candidate explanations we happened to generate, and make no claim the lot contains the truth.
- a customer asks how we know the method is reliable; the reply is that its reliability is the best explanation of its track record, and then the helpdesk changes the subject rather than noticing the loop.
- warranty question — my explanation turned out false, do I get a refund? Answer should note that the previous incumbent was the best available at the time of purchase.
- a question about a cheaper competitor that just conditionalizes on the evidence and never explains anything, and why we think that's an inferior product.
- contrastive mode: customers have to name the foil, extra foils are extra.
- final item: an escalation from someone holding two rival explanations that are exactly as loveley as each other, and the helpdesk visibly has nothing left to say.

Stay deadpan all the way through — no winking, no aside to the reader about how absurd this is.
