I'm on the work group revising the anxiety chapter of our diagnostic manual, and Thursday we vote on whether to keep, rename, or drop the provisional category we've been calling "anticipatory dysregulation" — chronic low-grade dread with no specific object, plus the checking-and-rehearsing habits that go with it. Two problems. First, the draft criteria leaked a year ago and the term has taken off online; alot of people now describe their inner life in exactly our vocabulary, including the bits we meant to be technical, and our field-study sites report patients arriving already knowing the criteria by heart. One member says that spread is the best evidence we've cut at a real joint — the category was there all along, we just named it. Another says it's evidence of the opposite: we're manufacturing the very cases we'll later count as confirmation, and every year the label exists it gets harder to tell what we'd have found if we hadn't. Second, dropping it would strand people who currently use it to get school accommodations and insurance coverage.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. Pick one — keep, rename, or drop — with whatever specifiers, thresholds, or transition arrangements you think are needed, and give the reasons. It has to take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously rather than strawmanning it, and it should be something the group could actually act on Thursday, not a survey of the debate.
