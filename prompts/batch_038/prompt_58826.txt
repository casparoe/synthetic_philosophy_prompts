I keep trying to work out when I should take a bet that someone else offers me, and my gut is inconsistent. Some cases:

- Guy at a bar, fair deck, neither of us has seen the card, he offers me even money that its red. I refuse, and I think refusing is right, not just timid.
- My brother offers me even money that his team takes the series. We've watched the same games, read the same stuff. I take it happily, and afterwards neither of us budges on our odds.
- A broker calls with a tip he swears he's trading on himself. I still hang up — him telling me is itself information.
- I pay an insurer a premium to take a risk off my hands even though they know way more than me about how likely the thing is. That one I think is just sensible.
- People tell me the price already reflects everything public, but I also figure if that were literally true nobody would bother doing research, so somebody has to be doing it.
- Two coworkers who disagree about a policy put fifty bucks on it. I approve, and I think the bet actually sharpens both their estimates.

Is there one principle that generates all of these? The pair I can't reconcile is the bar guy and the insurer: both are better-informed strangers offering me terms, and I flip. And what does whatever principle you land on say about a case I haven't listed — my sister, who's smarter than me at this and has no reason to mislead me, offers to swap our positions in two funds because she thinks it helps us both. Do I take that?

If some of my confusion here is already a worked-out result rather than a personal quirk, tell me which parts, and be explicit about which parts are settled and which are still live arguments among people who work on this — don't hand me a contested view as the consensus. And say plainly which of my verdicts the cleanest principle forces me to give up.
