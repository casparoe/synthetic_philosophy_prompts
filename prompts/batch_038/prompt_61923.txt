**The student's answer, unedited:**

> For deflationists, the whole content of the truth predicate is captured by instances of the schema: to assert that snow is white is true is just to assert that snow is white. So truth has no underlying nature, and there is nothing to theorize about. The predicate exists mainly to let us avoid redundancy when the same claim comes up twice in an argument.
>
> The generalization point is the important one. If a witness makes a hundred assertions, I cannot endorse them by conjoining them, since I may not know what they are, and in any case there might be infinitely many. "Everything the witness said is true" does the job in one sentence, and it works because each instance of the schema lets me move back from the endorsement to the claim. Without a truth predicate I could only produce an infinite disjunction, which no language can hold.
>
> On the two versions: Horwich is a minimalist, so for him truth isn't a property at all — "is true" is just a logical device, and that is what makes his view deflationary. The prosententialists say truth is a property, but a purely logical one with no nature behind it. That is the substantive disagreement.
>
> One consequence is that deflationism makes truth a cheap notion, so the deflationist is free to identify truth with whatever plays the role of correctness for assertion, and there is no pressure to resist the Liar, since on this view paradoxical sentences simply fail to say anything.
