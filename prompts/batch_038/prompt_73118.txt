A friend and I argued until two in the morning about survival and copying, and I would rather have the disagreement audited than resumed. Here is my position as I wrote it up afterwards:

1. What matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness, provided it has the right kind of cause. Identity is not what matters.
2. There is no further fact about personal identity beyond physical and psychological continuity; identity claims are just claims about those continuities.
3. Fission is not a catastrophe. If my psychology continues in two people, I have what matters, doubly, and neither successor is more genuinely me than the other.
4. Replacing my neurons one at a time with synthetic equivalents, over the course of months, preserves my survival, even though none of the original matter remains at the end.
5. A teletransporter that scans me, destroys me, and assembles an exact duplicate somewhere else does not preserve my survival, however complete the psychological continuity it produces.
6. What is missing in case 5 is that the duplicate's psychology is caused by a record rather than by an ongoing life; the process is interrupted, and that interruption is what does the work.

Which of these are in genuine conflict, and which merely look that way? Please rank the conflicts by severity, and for each say what would have to be weakened or abandoned and what the loss would be. I am not after the survey of positions I could get anywhere; I want to know whether this particular set can be held together, and if not, which revision is cheapest. Under 500 words, most severe conflict first, no preamble.
