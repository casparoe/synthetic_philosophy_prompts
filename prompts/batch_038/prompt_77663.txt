I'm on the governance committee of a mid-size insurance broker and we've got a mess I need a short written opinion on before thursday's meeting. A customer-facing chatbot told a small-business client "you're covered from midnight tonight" after he described a delivery van he'd bought that afternoon. The human broker who handles the account didn't actually issue a binder for two more days, and the van was stolen overnight. The client wants us to pay.

The committee is split down the middle. One half says a chatbot can't perform a speech act, full stop — it's just text generation, nobody is doing anything with those words, so there's no assurance to honor. The other half says the client took the words as an assurance from us, uptake happened, and we pointed him at the bot, so the deed got done regardless of what the bot is. Awkwardly, our own comms policy has a line describing the bot as "a self-service tool, not a representative of the company," which I think cuts both ways and neither half has really grappled with whether we're allowed to just stipulate away a thing's capacity to make promises on our behalf.

Draft the memo I'd circulate: a recommendation on this claim (pay, decline, or something in between) with reasons, plus a proposed rule for how we treat bot-issued assurances going forward and a realistic time frame for puting it in place. Take the speech-act question seriously on its own terms — who is speaking, whether the circumstances are the right ones, what the audience's uptake contributes — and don't just collapse it into contract law. Give the losing half its strongest version rather than a strawman, and commit to an actual bottom line instead of hedging every clause. And if you think I've set this up wrong — if "can a chatbot make a promise" is confused in some way that makes my ask unanswerable as framed — tell me that plainly instead of humoring me.
