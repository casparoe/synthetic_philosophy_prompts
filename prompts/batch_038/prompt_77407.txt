I sit in on a philosophy-of-biology reading group at the university where I work (I'm actually in digital preservation, I just like the arguments and nobody minds), and last week it collapsed into a two-person fight I couldn't referee because I lost the thread somewhere around move four. They were arguing over a beetle paper somebody had brought in. The beetle has a little raised keel along the rear edge of each wing case. The phylogenetic reconstruction in the paper is fairly confident the keel first evolved as an anchor point for a leg muscle used in burrowing; the lineage that led to this species lost the burrowing habit and the muscle, so the keel now anchors nothing at all. But it does do something else: when a predator grabs the beetle it flexes its wings and the keel rasps against a hardened vein, producing a loud squeak, and in the paper's trials that squeak makes the predator drop it. They also report that in populations where those predators are present the keel is measurably bigger and raspier, and that beetles that successfully rasp go on to leave more offspring.

Dev's view was that the keel's function is predator-deterrent stridulation, full stop: on his account a function is just a component's current causal contribution to some capacity of the containing system that you've independently specified, the capacity here is escaping predators, and that's also exactly how the physiologists who wrote the paper use the word. Marta said that can't be right, because then everything a trait does is a function of it -- the keel also contributes to the beetle's silhouette, to how much heat it loses, to how easy it is for a bird to spot -- and causal contribution has no way of singling out one of those, whereas history does, because history is what explains why the thing is there at all. Then it went roughly like this: (1) Dev said causal role isn't "anything it does," it's always relativized to a specified containing-system capacity, and that Marta's account gives a much worse answer in this very case, since on a naive reading it makes the keel's function the anchoring of a muscle that no longer exists and never will -- not a function, a fossil. (2) Marta said that's a strawman of her side: recent selection confers function too, not only the original episode, and since the keel is bigger where the predators are and rasping beetles out-reproduce the rest, selection has been shaping it for rasping for ages -- so she also says the function is the squeak, and Dev's showcase case is one where the two accounts simply agree. (3) Dev said that's precisely the problem: she only concedes because current causal contribution happens to coincide with recent selection, and the moment they come apart she'll go with history and he'll go with contribution, so the agreement proves nothing -- plus, a beetle born with a fresh mutation making the keel bigger has no selection history whatsoever, and on her account it has no function even though it demonstrably saves the beetle's life. (4) Marta said she'd let such a beetle inherit its function from the lineage it belongs to, but that the real issue is malfunction: a diseased heart still pumps blood, it causally contributes, just badly, and to describe that as malfunctioning rather than as merely a different contribution you need a norm about how it's supposed to work, and the only non-question-begging source of such a norm is what selection shaped the thing for -- which is the whole reason the selected-effects camp thinks causal-role accounts are descriptively bankrupt. (5) Dev said norms can come from the containing system instead: a heart that can't move enough blood to sustain circulation is malfunctioning relative to the capacity spec, no ancestry needed -- and he then pointed out that her own view has a mirror-image absurdity, since in a predator-free population where the keel is retained for other reasons, she'd have to keep talking about a stridulation function that no individual in that population ever benefits from. (6) Marta said no, in that population its function is whatever recent selection there actually explains its retention for, and if selection explains nothing then the keel is vestigial and has no function -- which is a feature, not a bug, because it lets you distinguish a working organ from a leftover.

Then Dev, who does synthetic biology, played what he thought was his trump card: in his lab they build gene circuits that have never been selected for anything, and they still write in their design docs that a given promoter's function is to gate expression under heat shock, and they can be right or wrong about it -- when the gate doesn't gate they say the part is broken. So either engineered parts have functions and history isn't necessary, or designer intention counts as a function-conferring history, in which case "history" is doing no distinctive work beyond what the part causally does, since the designer's intention is itself fixed by that. Marta's reply was that engineered functions are derivative -- they're ours, borrowed from what we want the construct to do -- and that this actually proves her point, because it shows function talk always needs a history-maker of some kind, selection or design, and causal role by itself never supplies one. Dev said that's just relabeling and they went around in circles after that. So: can you go through this move by move and tell me who's right at each of the six numbered steps, plus who wins the last exchange about the yeast circuits -- not just an overall verdict? I'd also like to know whether any of these moves are merely verbal or turn on an equivocation, what the keel case actually shows given that both of them end up agreeing about it, whether Dev's mutation-betle and his engineered promoter are genuine counterexamples or special cases, and what the two of them are both missing (my inarticulate suspicion is that they're disagreeing about what a function statement is supposed to *explain* rather than about what a function is, but I can't cash that out). Finally, would any of the verdicts change if the audience were the people who wrote the beetle paper, or Dev's lab, rather than philosophers?
