I'm a second-year PhD student, nominally in philosophy, but my actual project sits in a lab that measures affective responses in animals — mostly rats, some farm species, a bit of work on human infants — and my supervisor has told me to stop hedging and commit to an account of pleasure before I can say what any of our measures are measures *of*. So I want the spec first. Not a survey of positions, not a defence of any of them: the list of requirements that any answer to "what do all the pleasures have in common?" has to meet to be usable by someone in my position, with the case or consideration behind each one, and a view on which of them are actually negotiable.

Seed material, use what helps. The list that started this for me: the pleasure of scratching an itch, of cold water when you're parched, of the exact right pun landing, of an oboe line in a piece you love, of a hot bath, of relief when the phone doesn't ring, of finally getting unstuck on a proof you've been grinding on for a week. Whoever says those share one felt quality owes me an account of why its so hard to introspect; whoever says they share nothing owes me an account of why "pleasure" isn't just a grab-bag word we could trade in for six unrelated ones. Also in the mix: the wanting/liking split I keep hitting in the affective neuroscience literature, where as I understand it you can knock out one and leave the other intact in an animal, plus the human cases where someone keeps compulsively pursuing something they report no longer enjoying at all; pleasures you're ashamed of (I still catch a flicker of delight at a rival's humiliation and I don't approve of it, but it doesn't feel like a lesser pleasure for that); pleasures whose object turns out to be misdescribed — being delighted by a wine you were told was rare and then learning it's supermarket plonk — and I want to know whether the theory has to say something about that case or whether its a philosopher's toy; and the mixtures, chilli heat, the ache after a hard workout, nostalgia, where pleasure and pain seem to arrive as one thing rather than two things co-occurring. Then there are the ones I suspect matter most for my purposes and can't state cleanly: the account must not make pleasure depend on conceptual or linguistic sophistication, since most of my subjects aren't people; it should tell me whether "more pleasure" is a coherent quantity at all, i.e. whether it licenses comparisons within one creature and across creatures, because if it doesn't then a lot of what my lab publishes is unintelligible rather than merely uncertain; it has to fix what makes a particular episode the pleasure it is, since I need to count episodes and not just detect a sign; and I think there should be some requirement about not smuggling welfare value into the definition, because a theory that makes pleasure good by stipulation can't be turned around to argue that pleasure is good.

For each requirement, say why it's a requirement and mark it non-negotiable or camp-prejudice — I already suspect "there must be a single common phenomenal property" is the latter dressed up as neutral, but argue it rather than grant it to me, and do the same for the ones I'm fond of, especially the no-smuggling one, which may be doing more work than I'm entitled to. Then the part I most want: tell me whether any subset of these is jointly unsatisfiable, which pair specifically, and which single requirement knocks out the largest number of candidates. Give me your view on whether the question is even well posed — maybe the honest answer is "nothing in common, here's the family resemblance," in which case say what has to be true for that not to be a cop-out, because a cluster account that can't tell me why some new experience belongs to the family looks untestable from where I'm sitting. Finish with a short pass, a paragraph each at most, on how the felt-quality or dimensional-affect picture, the being-pleased-at attitude accounts, the desire-based accounts, and the nothing-in-common view come out against your list. No reading list and no citations, please — I have stacks of those and not two of them agree on anything.
