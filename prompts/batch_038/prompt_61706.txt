You're a philosopher of punishment who moonlights as a trust-and-safety consultant, and I'd like you to actually think this through before writing anything.

I head the mod team for an amateur astronomy forum, about 200k members, all of us volunteers. A couple times a month something lands on my desk that looks like a sanction case: a member posted something hateful about a named person on another platform, someone's decade-old DMs got dug up and are circulating, a member harassed another member, or a genuinely valuable contributor turns out to have done something bad offline. Right now we decide by vibes and whoever is loudest in the mod chat wins. Weve gotten it wrong in both directions — people permanently banned for things that didn't warrant it, and people shielded because the forum needed their expertise.

I think the frame we're missing is this: we treat bans as punishment, but a lot of the time what we're actually doing is self-protection, and those two need different justifications. Relatedly, there's a distinction in punishment theory between what justifies having the practice at all and what justifies applying it to this particular person, and I'm pretty sure we collapse them constantly. We also have no concept of proportionality, no maximum term, no fair-notice requirement, and no idea how anyone gets back in — plus the sanction is being handed down in front of a crowd that wants blood, which does something to the expressive function of the whole thing.

So: first, sketch out what a sanction on a volunteer forum is even for — retributive, deterrent, expressive, protective — and which of those aims a small non-state body can legitimately pursue. Then turn that into a decision procedure my team can run in under an hour per case. I want the questions in the order they should be asked, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure terminates in "no sanction," where it hands off to a human because the issue is values rather than facts, and where you expect it to systematically misfire given our constraints. Flag any step that's a judgment call dressed up as procedure.

Then run it on three cases: (a) the vile-but-legal off-platform post about a named individual; (b) the long-time expert whose messages to a 15-year-old surfaced; (c) a member we banned four years ago for sustained bad-faith posting who has apologised, changed, and whose contributions we badly need. For at least one of these the procedure should give me an answer I won't like — tell me which and why.

No bibliography or citations, please; just make the reasoning stand on its own.
