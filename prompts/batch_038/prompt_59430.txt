I'm on the grants board of a small foundation (about $12m a year) and we have a problem I can't articulate without sounding paranoid. Our climate portfolio can be run three ways: multi-year core grants to a fixed set of orgs, matched project grants awarded competitively each round, or annual prizes judged after the fact. Which pairing of these looks best to us shifts depending on what happens to be on the agenda that month, and some of the grantees have clearly figured this out. Over the last 18 months we've gone core→prizes→matched→core, and every one of those moves cost real money in unwinding commitments, matching obligations we couldn't cancel, and staff time — we're back where we started with roughly $400k less to show for it. One trustee wants us to adopt a binding three-year rubric that locks the structure in advance and defers any change to a single scheduled review; another says that's nuts, because each individual switch was a sensible response to what we'd just learned, and you can't condemn a decision that was right when it was made just because of how the sequence turned out. A third has floated a look-ahead rule instead: before voting on any switch, we write down what we'd want to do two rounds later, and refuse switches we'd predictably undo.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before next weeks meeting. It needs an actual bottom line — adopt the rubric, adopt the look-ahead rule, keep case-by-case discretion, or some conditional hybrid — plus reasoning the trustees can argue with rather than a summary of the debate. Take the losing side's strongest argument seriously rather than strawmaning it, and say something about when it would be legitimate to break a rule you've bound yourself to, since that's the first question that'll come up in the room.
