So I run a small shop that refurbishes used power tools and resells them with a 90-day warranty. Eight techs. For a couple years now we've kept two separate scores on every job, and I've started to think they don't add up to one coherent idea of what I actually hold people responsible for. I could use someone thinking it through with me properly.

The process score is the checklist: did you log the motor draw, did you swap the brushes whether they looked fine or not, did you do the 20-minute run-in, did you initial each box. The outcome score is just whether the tool comes back inside 90 days.

Here's the part that bugs me. The two scores get used in opposite directions. Pay and bonuses come almost entirely off outcomes — no returns, you get the quarterly bonus, I dont care what your sheet looked like. But discipline comes off process — if I catch you skipping steps you get written up even when every tool you touched is still out there working fine. So the guy who cuts corners and gets away with it is my top earner, and the guy who does everything by the book and eats one bad return gets no bonus and no blame. I've got a rule for that second case too: follow the checklist fully and a tool still comes back, you can't be written up, you did the work. Meanwhile a tech can scribble "this step is wrong for this model" on the sheet, skip it, and nothing ever judges that flag except whether the tool came back.

And the process audits are random, 10% of jobs. So whether your corner-cutting gets seen at all is a coin flip that has nothing to do with you.

Can you work out what theory of responsibility — praise, blame, desert, whatever the right words are — this whole setup actually assumes, assuming it assumes one? I want to know which parts are load-bearing: what could I drop and still be doing the same thing philosophically, versus what am I committed to whether I like it or not. Specifically, is the 10% sampling the same kind of luck as the tool-not-coming-back luck, or is it a different animal that I'm treating the same way by accident? I half remember someone saying philosophers get worked up about luck affecting blame but that praise is suposed to be immune or something, and I can't tell whether my shop is a counterexample to that or just an incoherent mess. Also tell me where the practice is underdetermined — places where two or three pretty different theories would license exactly the same rules, so that watching us work couldn't settle which one I believe.

Then tell me what would actually have to change if I switched to judging purely on outcomes, or purely on process: the paperwork, the bonus math, what I'd have to start measuring, how I'd have to talk to the techs. I'm not asking which system is better for a business in general. I'm asking what each one would force me to believe about my techs — and about my own ability to see what they do — that I might not be willing to believe.
