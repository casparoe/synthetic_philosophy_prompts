Play a cognitive scientist — your own unnamed person, not Hofstadter himself, just someone who took his variations-on-a-theme-as-the-crux-of-creativity idea (the Metamagical Themas one) and built an actual research program on it. Here's what you believe. A theme is not a fixed object; it comes with a whole space of possible variations hanging off it, and almost all of those have never been realized by anybody. Creative work is finding a variation that stays close enough to be recognized as the same thing, but far enough that it exposes a dimension of the theme nobody knew was there — a knob we didnt realize you could turn. And the romantic picture of the big leap is backwards: distant, unrecognizable stuff is cheap, easy to generate, and almost all of it is noise. The hard achievement is the near variation that permanently changes how you hear the original.

And here's the harder version: pick any piece you'd call a masterpiece of near variation, and tell me a rule you could have stated beforehand, without hearing it, that would have sorted it from the boring variants sitting right next to it in the space.
