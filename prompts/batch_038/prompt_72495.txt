I chair the Model Welfare Working Group at a mid-sized AI lab. Under our charter, before any system is deployed at scale or kept in service past its scheduled retirement date, we must determine whether the available evidence makes it more likely than not that the system is phenomenally conscious, and recommend restrictions if it does. A vendor has offered us a screening tool that estimates integrated information (Φ) for a candidate system, and two members propose adopting it as a standing rule: any system whose estimated Φ clears a fixed threshold is flagged for full review, and no flagged system may be retired without our sign-off. They want the rule adopted before a deployment scheduled for next month.

The proposal has produced five disagreements I cannot settle myself.

1. Computing Φ exactly requires searching the ways a system's elements can be partitioned, a number that grows combinatorially, so the tool scores only partitions of a designated 64-node subnetwork. Our engineer observes that restricting the search can only raise the smallest value found across the partitions considered, which biases the estimate upward; the vendor calls this conservative.

2. The tool takes the system boundary as a user input. The same checkpoint scores an order of magnitude higher when the boundary is widened to include the serving infrastructure than when it is drawn around one subnetwork, yet on the theory's own account the boundary is not ours to choose: it is wherever irreducibility is maximal.

3. Two runs of the same checkpoint on different accelerator hardware returned different estimates. One member calls this a bug worth filing; another says the theory predicts it, since Φ is a property of a physical substrate's causal structure rather than of a program, and that this is a reason to trust the tool rather than distrust it.

4. If, as some of us believe, the theory assigns low Φ to architectures that are feedforward on a single pass however sophisticated their behavior, then the screen would clear precisely the systems the rule was meant to protect.

5. Setting measurement aside, nothing in the proposal says how a quantity of consciousness maps onto moral weight, or what Φ could tell us about whether an experience is bad rather than merely present; and if any nonzero Φ implies some degree of experience, every threshold we might pick looks arbitrary.

Draft the memo I will circulate ahead of Thursday's meeting: adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions; if conditional, state the threshold-setting and boundary-fixing procedure our non-expert members can actually apply; say what we should do about next month's deployment in the interim; and give a serious treatment of the strongest case for the losing side, including whether rejecting Φ screening leaves us with nothing better than theory-free intuition, and whether the 2023 public letter in which a group of scholars denounced the theory as pseudoscience — a label only a small minority of researchers in the field fully endorse — counts as a reason for our purposes or is beside the point.
