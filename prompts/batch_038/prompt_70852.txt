I'm a PhD student in political philosophy working on the ethics of state wrongdoing, and I want to interrogate the procedural architecture of covert-action authorization in the United States rather than any particular operation. Under the governing statute, such an operation must be authorized in advance by a written finding signed personally by the president; that finding must be reported to the intelligence committees before the operation begins, or, in extraordinary circumstances, to a handful of congressional leaders who are then bound to secrecy; a finding may not authorize an operation retroactively; it may not authorize anything that would violate the Constitution or a federal statute; and covert action is itself defined by the intent that the government's role never be apparent or publicly acknowledged. Reconstruct the theory of political wrongdoing this apparatus presupposes: who, on this view, can change an act's moral character by signing, is the wrong at issue moral, legal, or merely political, and does the signatory incur anything he could later be blamed or punished for? Which of these features can a purely instrumental account of secrecy explain — non-acknowledgment as a way of protecting operatives, containing escalation, and limiting diplomatic costs — and which only make sense if the act is taken to be genuinely wrong in a way that survives its being legal, necessary, and personally authorized? I'm especially struck by the fact that the framework was built to eliminate the president's deniability toward Congress while preserving the state's deniability toward the public and toward other governments; is that a single coherent commitment about accountability, or two incompatible ones? And what would have to change in the apparatus if we replaced the implicit view with a realist one on which reasons of state simply displace ordinary morality, or with one on which such acts are flatly impermissible? Please distinguish throughout what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely sits comfortably with, and end with a line beginning "Verdict:" giving, in a phrase of a few words, the single commitment the practice most strictly presupposes.
