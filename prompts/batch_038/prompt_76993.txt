In a constitutional theory seminar I attend, a classmate defended judicial review with the familiar precommitment image: a democratic people is like Odysseus lashed to the mast, the constitution is the rope, and unelected judges are the crew, instructed in advance to disregard the sailor's pleas to be released once the Sirens begin to sing. On that reading, judicial review is not an intrusion on democracy but democracy's own calmer, earlier self holding the line against a passing majority's passions. My classmate then offered a variant he believes defuses the objection that the whole arrangement merely lets the dead govern the living: suppose the ropes are not tied once and for all, but retied at regular intervals by a vote of the crew, so that the binding authority is periodically renewed by those subject to it — though never by the sailors at the moment of temptation.

I would like a careful audit of how much weight the analogy can bear. Please set out the correspondence table explicitly — the ship and its voyage, the destination, the Sirens, the ropes, the earlier self that does the tying, the crew, the standing instruction to disregard pleas, the sailors themselves — and say what argumentative work each element performs. Then separate the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion from those that are merely decorative. Four points in particular deserve pressure: whether the identity that makes self-binding legitimate, namely that one and the same agent both ties and is tied, survives when binders and bound belong to different generations; whether the distinction between passion and considered judgment is as sharp in politics as the distinction between a song and a course, given that courts must decide substantively which legislative acts amount to singing; whether the crew's supposed indifference about the destination has any counterpart in a judiciary that inevitably exercises judgment about what the voyage is for; and what the retied-rope variant genuinely changes, given that it renews the constraint without ever consulting the sailors under temptation.

Finally, state what the source domain quietly contributes that the target lacks — an identifiable and external temptation, a destination fixed in advance, a binding act that costs the agent nothing but freedom of movement, an enforcer with no view of the merits — and conclude whether the classmate's claim survives once those imports are on the table. If the analogy fails at a load-bearing joint, propose a better one; if no analogy of this kind can carry the argument, explain why, and say what sort of argument would have to replace it.
