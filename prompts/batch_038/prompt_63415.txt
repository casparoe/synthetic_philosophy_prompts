I have spent the better part of two decades in this corner of the internet: I was reading the sequences when they were still posting on a schedule, I have argued about inverted spectra in comment threads that outlasted the marriages of people I know, and I have, more than once, watched a meetup devolve at 1 a.m. into whether a sufficiently organized weather system could be embarrassed. I am helping assemble a small printed booklet for our group's tenth anniversary, and I would like a poem about functionalism and multiple realizability in which the form, not the subject matter, carries the argument.

The conceit is this. Write a poem of six quatrains in a fixed rhyme scheme of your choosing, identical in every stanza, in which the manner of realization changes from stanza to stanza while the scheme itself is never violated. Stanza one rhymes outright. Stanza two holds the same positions by assonance or consonance alone. Stanza three holds them by eye, orthographically, so that the rhyme is real on the page and only approximate in the mouth. Stanza four holds them by sense, with words that answer each other in meaning rather than sound. Stanza five holds them through some further displacement you invent, provided a reader can still verify that the same slots have been filled. The claim the poem should enact is the one that made the view compelling in the first place: the role is preserved while the stuff is swapped, and anyone who says the scheme has stopped being the scheme has to say why. A reader who knows the literature should recognize what is happening before they can state it, and a reader who never notices should simply find the stanzas increasingly strange.

Then the turn. In the sixth stanza the scheme still lands, every position still fills, and something is absent. I want the objection that is supposed to be decisive against exactly this kind of poem — the one where a billion people are each handed a two-way radio and asked to stand in for a neuron, so that the whole distributed system is functionally impeccable and, the objector insists, dark inside. Render it as an image, and let it hurt. But do not resolve it, and do not concede it either: the poem should carry the functionalist's rejoinder, that an intuition this strong about the substrate is precisely the sort of intuition the whole view was built to distrust. Leave the final line standing on the premise one of the two sides would have to surrender.

Constraints. No names and no titles anywhere in the poem, and no bibliography; the ideas must arrive as images and structure. One oblique nod is permitted to a Martian whose pain is wired differently but is pain anyway, and one to an arm that thinks for itself. Sound must not be sacrificed to scheme: the first quatrain should be worth reading aloud on its own merits. Avoid any line that collapses into a slogan about patterns and substrate, or the mind being what the mind does; those are the fortune-cookie versions and I will notice. Twenty-four lines total, give or take nothing.

After the poem, add a short prose paragraph, outside the verse, mapping each stanza to the way it realizes the scheme, so that I can check whether the structure survives being read aloud by someone else at the party.
