I've been arguing on and off for months with my flatmate, who's doing a philosophy PhD, and I've finally decided the only way to settle it is to make him translate. I'm a control engineer, about fifteen years designing fuzzy logic controllers — building management systems, battery chargers, that sort of thing. My whole working vocabulary is membership functions, triangular and trapezoidal shapes, hedges, fuzzification of a crisp input, Mamdani and Takagi–Sugeno inference, centroid defuzzification, rule base tuning. And my honest position, the one he keeps saying is naive, is that the sorites paradox is a fake problem that my field solved fifty years ago: "bald" and "tall" and "hot" are just vague predicates, you give them a graded membership function, you stop pretending there's a sharp cut-off, and the paradox dissolves because nobody ever claimed a one-hair difference changes anything — it changes the membership value by some tiny amount, exactly as you'd want.

He says I'm confusing a modelling device with a theory of truth, and that the live view I should be worrying about is epistemicism — roughly, that vague predicates really do have sharp boundaries, that there is a last man who is bald and a first who isn't, that bivalence holds, and that our ignorance of where the line falls is a special kind of ignorance explained by how the meanings of our words get fixed by use. I don't buy it, but I can't argue with it in his terms and I'd rather not have to learn them. So here's what I want: restate epistemicism as faithfully as you can manage in my vocabulary, using the machinery I actually have on the bench. Treat the boundary claim as a claim about my membership functions — what has the function got to look like if the epistemicist is right? Is it a step function with an unknown threshold, a very steep ramp, a rule base that has been sharpened somewhere downstream of where I can see it? Where does the "we cannot know" bit go — is that an unobservable parameter, an identifiability problem, a limitation of the training data, something about how the rule base was actually fitted by a community of users rather than designed by me? What does bivalence correspond to? What happens to hedges — if "very tall" is just the membership function squared, what is the epistemicist saying about that operation? Go claim by claim and be as literal as you can without turning it into a joke.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. Tell me which pieces of epistemicism translate cleanly, which ones only translate if you distort one side or the other, and which have no expression at all in my framework. Be scrupulously honest about direction — don't quietly make epistemicism look absurd by rendering it as "fuzzy logic but with bivalence asserted for no reason", and equally don't smuggle my own presuppositions in and call them translation, especially the assumption that a membership function is a useful engineering fiction rather than a report on how things are. If I've mischaracterised something about fuzzy logic in order to set the question up, leave it as I stated it and flag it separately at the end, because that gap is itself data. Finally, give me your verdict on the residue: is what fails to translate evidence that my vocabulary is missing something real — that control engineers have a hole where a theory of truth should be — or evidence that epistemicism is less substantive than its defenders think, a way of restating an ordinary modelling choice in metaphysical costume? Please write the whole thing in British English.
