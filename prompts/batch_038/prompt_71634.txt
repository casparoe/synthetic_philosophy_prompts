Our department is hosting a Q&A with a scholar whose reading of the White Horse Dialogue runs against the standard Platonist interpretation. She denies that Gongsun Long is positing separated universals: on her account, Classical Chinese nouns behave like mass terms, so horse and white horse name portions of stuff rather than classes, the dialogue's real argument is mereological, and the passage observing that a request for a horse can be filled by a yellow or black animal while a request for a white horse cannot is what does the work. She is a graceful debater who concedes minor points in order to protect the overall picture. Please write five questions, as a numbered list with no preamble or closing remarks, that tighten around her in that order; each item should be one sentence posing the question followed by one sentence giving the reply you expect from her and the follow-up that reply invites. Begin by granting her the mass-noun premise so that you can make her pay for it — what survives of the reading if Classical Chinese turns out to have ordinary count nouns and classifiers? — work through whether differing conditions for filling a request show two different things or only two different ways of picking out the same thing, and end by asking whether her interpretation rescues the argument from paradox only by leaving Gongsun Long with nothing to say that a horse trader would not already know.
