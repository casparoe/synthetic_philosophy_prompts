I've been asked to write down my actual position on indicative conditionals and probabilistic updating for a small workshop, and a colleague suggested I get it stress-tested before I present it. So here is the whole package, in my own words. Tell me where it creaks.

(1) The Equation. My credence in an indicative conditional "if A, C" just is my conditional credence in C on A. I hold this as a general thesis about my degrees of belief, not as something restricted to some special register or context.

(2) Conditional probability is nothing over and above the ratio. P(C|A) = P(A and C)/P(A), full stop. I don't believe in primitive conditional probabilities, and I don't believe in conditional events as some third ontological category sitting between propositions and nothing at all.

(3) Conditionals are propositions. They live in the same Boolean algebra as everything else: I can negate them, disjoin them, and stick them in the antecedent or consequent of further conditionals, and any of my credence functions can be conditionalised on any of them.

(4) Modus ponens and modus tollens are valid for indicative conditionals; hypothetical syllogism is not. I'm quite happy with that asymmetry, I don't think it's an embarassment or a sign of bad faith.

(5) When I learn a flat conditional outright — say dispatch radios "if she's in the north half she's in the north-west zone" — the correct response is to conditionalise on the corresponding material conditional, N ⊃ W.

(6) Antecedent preservation. Learning anything of the "if A, ..." shape shouldn't move my credence in A one bit, whether what I learn is a flat conditional or a constraint on a conditional probability. It's just not evidence about A. This is my main reason for thinking the Judy Benjamin sort of case has an easy answer: the poor woman's credence that she's in the north half stays exactly where it was.

(7) Jeffrey conditionalisation is the only permissible way to respond to evidence that doesn't arrive as a certainty. But for a Judy Benjamin-style input — where you're handed a constraint on a conditional probability rather than a partition plus new numbers for its cells — I don't think there's any uniquely correct revision rule. Anything coherent is allowed.

Here's the concrete case I plan to present, my own setting rather than the original one. A hiker is missing in a valley. North half and south half, each split into a west zone and an east zone. My prior: 1/2 on the north half, and conditional on north, 3/4 on north-west and 1/4 on north-east. South is 1/2, split evenly between south-west and south-east. So 3/8 north-west, 1/8 north-east, 1/4 south-west, 1/4 south-east. Then dispatch radios the sentence in (5) — that's the whole message they recieved, one sentence, from a source I take to be reliable and to have said something true. I'm confident that after updating, my credence that she's in the north half is still 1/2, per (6), and I'm equally confident the Equation survives the update intact.

Now audit it. Which of these genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only look like they do, and what has to go? I want the tensions ordered by severity rather than dumped on me as a flat set of equally bad problems, and for each one I want to know which commitment is cheapest to give up and what giving it up costs me — including costs elsewhere in the list, not just locally. On the worked case I want the actual number, not a gesture at one: if my 1/2 is wrong, say it's wrong and show me the arithmetic, and then tell me whether the Equation is still standing afterwards.

And be committed about it. I don't want a menu of respectable ways to go with the choice left to me — tell me which revision you'd make, why that one and not the others, and what you think the resulting package looks like. No bibliography, I know the literature; I want your verdict.
