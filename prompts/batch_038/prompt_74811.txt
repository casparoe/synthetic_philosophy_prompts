I want to build a paired Frankfurt case, but for a narrower purpose than the usual PAP fight: I'm testing whether alternate possibilities, if they matter at all, matter to the *degree* of blame rather than to whether there's any responsibility at all.

Version A: an ordinary plant manager, call him N, learns a component has a defect that will injure some customers, decides to ship anyway for the usual career reasons, deliberates freely, signs. Version B: exactly the same down to N's thoughts and the ink on the signiture, except a neurosurgeon has secretly implanted a device that would have driven N to sign had he shown any sign of changing his mind. It never fires. N doesn't know it's there, and nobody else does either.

Constraints: one short paragraph per version; the implant is the only sci-fi element allowed and everything else stays mundane (no trolleys, no torture); and the target intuition is that B deserves somewhat less blame than A, not that B is off the hook. So you can't get there by making B's harm bigger or B's character worse — the whole point is that nothing in the actual sequence differs.

Then run the check for me: what a hardline actual-sequence theorist has to say about the comparison and why, what a flicker-of-freedom defender says, which single feature of B is generating the degree intuition, and what happens to the intuition if you delete that feature. End with the actual-sequence theorist's best attack — I assume it's that we're picturing the device firing; is that really the best on offer?

If you think no case of this shape can produce a genuine degree difference without rigging the verdict, say so bluntly instead of complying politely. Under 400 words, and put your verdict on whether the pairing works in the first sentence.
