For some years I have been tracking what looks to me like a permanent stalemate between two literatures that appear to be arguing about the same phenomenon and never actually meet.

On one side, the behavioural economists: akrasia is dynamic inconsistency; human discounting is quasi-hyperbolic rather than exponential; model the agent as a sequence of temporally local selves and you have explained gym memberships nobody uses, why precommitment works, and why naive agents are exploited by their own future preferences while sophisticated ones plan around them. On the other side, the philosophers of action: a discount function describes choices, it does not tell you what it is to act against one's own all-things-considered judgement; and to fold a resolution into a long-horizon preference is to lose precisely the thing that makes weakness of will worth a theory. Some of them go further and deny that weakness of will is akrasia at all, holding instead that it is an over-ready revision of one's intentions, so that the economist and the philosopher are not even tracking the same failure.

I have read both camps — the akrasia sequences on the rationalist blogs and the discounting reviews in the journals — and my working hypothesis is that there is no substantive disagreement at all: the two sides answer different questions, one descriptive and diachronic, one normative and first-personal, under the single word 'self-control', and the appearance of conflict arises only when one camp writes as though its model disposes of the other's.

Diagnose this, please. Is the split verbal, a difference about what counts as an explanation, or is there a real disagreement underneath — for instance about whether a better judgement can itself just be another desire with a longer horizon, or whether a resolution is representable in a discounting framework at all? I want reasons drawn from what each side is actually committed to rather than a survey of the positions. Then tell me whether any hidden empirical or formal question could decide it — the stability of measured discount rates across elicitation methods, whether self-control behaves like a depletable resource or a skill, or some consistency result about time-indexed preferences — and what, on your diagnosis, would count as progress as opposed to more volume. Please write in British English.
