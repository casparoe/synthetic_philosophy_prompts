Ok so here's a view I've talked myself into over the last few months and I want it attacked properly, not summarized at me.

I don't think money pumps show that intransitive preferences are irrational. What they show is narrower: you can't be intransitive *and* myopic. Hand the agent ordinary foresight — they see the whole decision tree, they plan ahead, and they're allowed to change their mind at a node at no cost — and the pump dissolves, because backwards induction lets them refuse the very trade that starts the drain. On my view there's no synchronic coherence requirement hiding behind the pump argument at all. Transitivity comes off the list of rational requirements, and what replaces it is a purely diachronic one: be able to form a plan and stick to it. Sophistication is the whole virtue, and preference consistency is at most a special case of it — a derived thing, not a basic one.

I know this smells like letting intransitivity off way too easy. So: what would have to be true for it to be false? Give me the considerations that would actually move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Specifically I'm after

- cases or results where the foresighted agent still gets drained, and places where sophistication fails to determine a plan at all — I suspect ties and indifference are where this bites, and I want to know how deep the problem goes once you allow weak preference and cycles in the induced ranking over plans;
- whether my two freebies (foresight costs nothing, changing your mind costs nothing) are doing illegitimate work — i.e. whether I've smuggled in a commitment device and then pretended it was free. If the sophisticated agent needs something like a precommitment to carry out the plan, does my view just become resolute choice with extra steps, and is that a concession or a disaster?
- whether the substitute requirement collapses back into transitivity applied to the ranking over whole plans, in which case I haven't abolished the constraint, I've moved it upstairs and lost the argument;
- what my view predicts about real sequential choice behaviour that the standard view doesn't. If the two are indistinguishable in practice, that's a mark against mine and I want it counted as one, not explained away;
- and whether the position risks anything whatsoever, or whether I've made it unfalsifiable by retreating to "well, that agent wasn't sophisticated enough" every time a pump goes through. If that's the diagnosis, say whether it's fatal for a view of this kind or just a normal cost of it being a view about rational structure rather than an empirical hypothesis.

Two more things. Keep apart what a defender of the pump argument would press from what I'd have to concede on my own terms — I'd rather know which of these I can live with and which ones I can't. And skip the bibliography, I know the terrain, I want the arguments and the constructions. If your honest verdict is that the position is well insulated and that insulation is fine, tell me that; it doesn't have to lose. But I do want the map of where it's exposed, so I can go check those spots myself.
