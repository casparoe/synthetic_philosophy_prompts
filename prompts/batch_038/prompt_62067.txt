I'm trying to work out whose values a general-purpose AI should answer to, and my own verdicts won't line up.

A voice assistant used mostly by speakers of a small regional language: it should follow that community's norms rather than the national majority's, even though the majority is a hundred times bigger. A system allocating hospital beds across a whole country: it should follow the country's values, not those of the region it happens to be deployed in. The local community wins one and loses the other, and I can't say what separates them.

A personal assistant asked to draft messages that will wear down an elderly relative's resistance to selling her house: not acceptable, though the user is the one paying and no stranger is hurt. The same assistant asked to help that user find a casino: acceptable — unless he had earlier asked it to refuse exactly this, in which case I'd side with his earlier self. Sometimes, though, not always.

If 95 percent of the people affected agree on something, I'm happy for the machine to go along with the majority; if it's 51 to 49, I don't want it to take either side. The size of the majority seems to decide whether the majority counts at all, which I know is strange.

A small group that cares desperately should beat a vast majority that barely notices — except when the small group holds views I happen to dislike, and then I want the majority after all.

My guess is that the values which count belong to whoever has to live with the consequences, weighted by how much they stand to lose. Does that survive these cases, or does it only hide the trouble? I'd like the best principle you can give me stated exactly, with the case that motivates each part of it, what it decides about a case I haven't listed, and a plain statement of which of my verdicts it forces me to give up.
