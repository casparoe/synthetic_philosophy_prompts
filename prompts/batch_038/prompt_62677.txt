I work in agricultural and social policy rather than in philosophy, though I have read some of the literature on social construction on my own time. Over the past few years I have been tracking a pattern for which I cannot find an adequate term, and I would like to know whether one exists.

Two cases, both of which I have worked on directly.

First, a wine appellation. A boundary is drawn on a map by a regulatory body, originally for fraud prevention and tax purposes, along lines that everyone involved concedes were somewhat arbitrary — it follows a river in one stretch, a road in another, an old cadastral survey in a third. Nothing about the soil or the climate changes at that line. But within a generation the rules attached to the boundary change which grape varieties may lawfully be planted, which irrigation and pruning practices are permitted, and what the resulting wine may be called and therefore sold for. Growers inside the boundary replant; growers ten meters outside it, working essentially identical ground, plant something else entirely, because their wine cannot carry the name. After thirty or forty years the wine inside the boundary reliably differs from the wine outside it — in variety, in chemistry, in style — and the difference is robust, measurable, and replicable across vintages. Commentators then write about the region as though it had always possessed this distinctive character, and the regulatory line comes to look as if it had been tracking something real all along.

Second, an eligibility threshold. A program sets a numeric cutoff for benefits, chosen because of rounding conventions and budget arithmetic rather than because anything happens to a household at exactly that number. Over time the cutoff shapes circumstances directly — through what people qualify for, what they are instructed to do, and what data is collected about them — and the population just below the line comes to differ from the population just above it in ways a researcher can detect statistically and even exploit as a research design. Nobody involved believes the cutoff describes anything.

What makes the pattern hard for me to name is the mechanism. The usual story about classifications changing what they classify runs through people's minds: a group is labelled, its members come to understand themselves under the label, and their behaviour changes accordingly. That is not what happens in either of my cases. Self-understanding is doing no work at all. A grower who has never heard the word "appellation" and holds no beliefs whatsoever about regional character still ends up growing something different, because the legal and commercial machinery built on top of the classification channels what is available to him. The category becomes real through paperwork, infrastructure, and incentives rather than through anyone's conception of it — including, and this is the point I want to stress, the conception of the very people who fall inside it.

A second feature I keep encountering, which I do not think the familiar stories capture, is that the kind outlives its justification. Once the scaffolding is in place, the regularity persists after the original reason for the boundary has been abandoned or shown to have been mistaken. Repeal the rule and the pattern decays only across a generation, because vineyards are long-lived, because records continue to be kept in the old categories, and because downstream institutions keep reporting along the historical line. By the time the justification is gone, the evidence for the distinction exists in the world independently of it, and the distinction can then be defended empirically without reference to the decision that produced it.

So: is there an established concept for classification-made-real-by-institutional-scaffolding that does not depend on anyone's beliefs about the kind? I have heard the slogan that categories of this sort are socially constructed yet perfectly real, and I have heard about self-fulfilling prophecies, but neither seems to fit — the first is a claim about status rather than mechanism, and the second requires belief. If there is a concept in the neighbourhood that I am missing, I want to know precisely how what I have described differs from it, and in particular whether my two additional features (belief-independence, and persistence after the justification is withdrawn) are already part of the standard notion or are genuine departures from it. If nothing in the literature captures it, say so plainly and coin a term: give me a definition, tell me what would count as a boundary case falling outside it, and tell me how it relates to the nearest neighbours, so that I can use it in a report without being accused of reinventing something.

I am not after a reading list; I can find that myself once I know what to look for. What I want is the carving — what exactly this phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.

Please begin with the bottom line in your first sentence: either the name of the established concept that fits best, or a clear statement that there is none. Then give the reasoning. No preamble, and no restatement of my question.
