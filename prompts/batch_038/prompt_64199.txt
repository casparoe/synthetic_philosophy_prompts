I build backend stuff for a small health app where people with chronic pain log their flares. Two sliders per entry: "how intense was it" (0–10, how strong the sensation felt) and "how much did it bother you" (0–10, how much it wrecked your day / how much you minded it). We added the second one after support kept getting tickets from users saying the first number was measuring the wrong thing. Six months of data later the two are only loosely correlated — lots of 8s paired with 2s, and some 3s paired with 9s.

At the review where I showed that, our clinical advisor Dana and a staff engineer named Marcus went at it for like forty minutes and I mostly just watched. Here's the exchange as best I remember it.

1. Marcus: it's one variable plus noise. Intensity just *is* how awful the thing feels, and "bothered" is contaminated — people score it low because they've habituated, or because they're stoic, or because they're rating it hours later and reconstructing. Coping better isn't evidence the pain got less bad. So kill slider two, it's a redundant instrument and it's making our numbers look incoherent.

2. Dana: the divergence is real and it tracks a distinction people have been arguing about for decades. Look at pain asymbolia — usually after a stroke in a region around the insula, where patients tell you they feel the pain fine, they can localize it and describe it, but they don't mind it. Some grin or giggle while it's being applied. They'll let you repeat the noxious stimulus over and over, they won't pull away. If intensity and badness were the same thing, that whole profile would be impossible.

3. Marcus: asymbolia supports me, not you. Those patients are not doing well. They don't protect the limb, they'll stand on a fractured ankle, they get burned and cut, they don't learn to avoid what's damaging them. Pain's whole job is to make you stop, and in asymbolia that job is broken. So "doesn't mind it" isn't the badness going away, it's a different and worse badness arriving. Slider two is basically measuring "are you going to do something about this," which is not the same as "is the pain less bad."

4. Dana: you're sliding between two senses of bad and not marking the shift. Bad-for-the-person — agreed, the asymbolic is worse off. Bad as in "the experience itself is less awful to have" — by their own report it is, and the report is the only evidence anyone has ever had about what an experience is like. If you overrule the report you've made a claim nobody could check: nobody is ever a reliable witness to their own pain.

5. Marcus: reports aren't infallible just because they're first-person. These patients often have other stuff going on — flatter affect, blunted emotional responses generally, some don't seem to care about much of anything. So "I feel it but it doesn't bother me" might be coming out of a system that's bad at reporting affect across the board. We don't accept "I'm fine to drive" from someone who's three drinks in. First-person data, sure, but low-confidence data.

6. Dana: fine — but then your skepticism eats slider one too, since it's the same mouth producing both numbers.

Somewhere in there Marcus floated a hypothetical that neither of them let go of: suppose we could ship it. A stimulator or a drug that produces the asymbolia profile on demand for people with genuinely intractable pain — keep the sensation, delete the minding. He called that a cure and said we'd be crazy not to want it. Dana called it a mutilation, said it "works" only by breaking the part of the person that looks after their own body, and that a user who came back and logged 10 intensity / 0 bothered would not thereby be reporting a pain that isn't bad.

I honestly couldn't referee any of it and I don't have the vocabulary. Can you go through the six moves one at a time and say who's right at each — including where someone equivocates or where a reply just doesn't land on the thing it's answering? Then: is the disagreement in move 1 actually settleable by our app's data, or is there no experiment we could run on those two sliders that would decide it? And what are both of them missing that would change the shape of the argument? No need for citations or a reading list, I just want the reasoning spelled out.
