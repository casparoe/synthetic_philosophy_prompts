After her death, Mother Teresa's letters to her spiritual directors were published against her expressed wishes, revealing that for decades she experienced prayer as met with silence and emptiness and felt unwanted by God — while continuing to work, pray, and profess belief without interruption. Analyze this as a test case for Schellenberg's argument from divine hiddenness.

State compactly what the argument requires: that a perfectly loving God would see to it that anyone capable of a personal relationship with God and not resisting it believes that God exists, so that non-resistant non-belief counts as evidence against such a God. Then do the mapping. Is her condition non-belief, resistant belief, or a third thing the argument has no slot for? If she believed throughout, what does that show about the argument's chosen variable — is the deprivation that matters the absence of belief, or the absence of felt reciprocation within a relationship that belief alone does not secure? Give Schellenberg's best reply and say whether it preserves the argument or quietly concedes the point.

Next, consider the dark-night reading, on which prolonged divine absence is a stage of spiritual formation rather than evidence, and ask whether that response is available here in a way it is not for ordinary non-believers — and what that asymmetry costs.

Finish by saying whether the episode confirms the argument, is an anomaly for it, or exposes a distinction it was never built to handle, and be explicit about where the theory is silent, including about what correspondence published against the writer's wishes can legitimately be made to establish.
