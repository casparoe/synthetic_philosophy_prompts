Not a philosopher, I just watch a lot of arguments and there's a move I keep running into that I've never been able to find a word for.

Setup: someone gets blamed for a failing — blew a deadline, relapsed, took the money, kept quiet about something — and the person doing the blaming has genuinely never done that thing, not once. The reply they get isn't "you do it too." It's the opposite: the fact that they've never done it is what supposedly disqualifies them. "You've never been that deep in it, so you don't get to weigh in." "Easy for you to say, you never had the itch." And sometimes there's a counterfactual attached — "if you'd been in my position you'd have done worse than me" — which is doing alot of work, because now the blamer's actual clean record is treated as beside the point and what matters is how they'd have behaved under conditions that never obtained.

Instances I've actually seen:
- someone who's never been addicted telling a friend that relapsing was a choice, and getting back that a person who's never had cravings can't possibly know what that decision looked like from the inside;
- a team lead who has never missed a delivery pulling a junior aside about a slipped deadline, and the rest of the team closing ranks on the grounds that the lead has never been underwater on three projects at once and therefore has no idea;
- a country with no history of the particular kind of corruption being described lecturing one that does, and the answer being that the lecturing country simply never faced those choices, so it can't judge them.

What I can't work out is what this is *supposed* to be an objection to. Sometimes it reads like an informational point — you're missing facts about what the situation is like. Sometimes like a point about who's allowed to condemn anyone, which doesn't obviously follow from missing facts. And sometimes it's the counterfactual version, where the claim is that there's no real difference between the two people at all, just a difference in what they happened to be exposed to. Those feel like three different objections wearing one coat.

So: is there an established name for this? I've read up on tu quoque and its clearly not that — tu quoque says you did it too, this says your not having done it is the problem. I know there's a whole philosophical literature on whether hypocrites get to blame people, but my blamer isn't a hypocrite, they're the reverse, and the objection seems to bite harder the cleaner their record is. I also know the "you were just lucky, not better" idea has a name somewhere in ethics and I suspect it's related, but on its own that doesn't capture the part about who gets to say anything.

What I want isn't just a label — I want the carving. Which of the three readings (missing information, missing authority, no real difference) has the best claim to be the underlying thing? Is the counterfactual version doing something different from the experiential one, or is it just the experiential one with the teeth showing? And under what conditions is the objection actually *right* — because there does seem to be a genuine case where the person who held out did get lucky and it's not obvious they're better placed to judge. If there's no established term, coin one, define it, and tell me what it isn't: what seperates it from tu quoque, from ordinary hypocrisy objections, from the luck-based one, and from the general "you need lived experience to have an opinion" demand, which I think is a broader and blunter thing.
