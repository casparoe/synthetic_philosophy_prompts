I chair a nine-person funding panel for a mid-sized foundation. We meet monthly, roughly fourteen applications a meeting, and each one is scored against three written criteria — soundness, significance, feasibility — that are supposed to be conjunctive: fund only if all three are met. We keep walking into the doctrinal paradox. Last month a majority voted yes on each of the three criteria separately, and a majority voted no on "fund," and the applicant's lawyer wrote to us pointing out that our own published criterion-findings contradict our decision.

Two more complications. Members recuse on individual criteria rather than on whole applications, because our conflict rules are criterion-specific, so the eligible electorate differs from criterion to criterion. And we currently take the criterion votes first, project the tallies on the screen, and only then vote on funding — several members now say seeing the tallies changes how they vote, and I cant tell whether that's corruption or just people updating on information.

Write me a procedure I can actually run in the room: the order of ballots, who is eligible at each step, what to do when the premise-majority and the conclusion-majority come apart, what gets written into the appeal file, and where the procedure has to stop and hand the call to a human because it's a question of values rather than arithmetic. Ground it in whatever judgment aggregation genuinely delivers that's usable at nine voters and fourteen cases a month, and be straight about which steps are judgment calls wearing a procedure costume. Then run it on two cases: one where a criterion-level recusal flips the paradox, and one where the procedure forces an outcome I'll think is substantively wrong.

Finish with a table of the procedure, columns: `step` | `question put to the panel` | `who votes` | `what counts as a sufficient answer` | `what happens if it fails` | `judgment_call` (no, or yes plus what the judgment is about).
