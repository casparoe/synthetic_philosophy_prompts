I'm on a small team that advises a funder on how to weight different animals in our welfare-costing spreadsheets, and we've been having the exact same argument for about a year and a half now and nobody has moved an inch. I want a diagnosis of the argument itself — not a verdict on who's right, and not a reading list.

Here's the shape of it. We have a column for moral weight per subject, and we have to put numbers in it for honeybees, shrimp, fish, mice, whatever. Half the team thinks that's fine in principle, because sentience obviously comes in degrees — more intense experience, more kinds of experience, more integrated experience — and our job is just to estimate how far along the dial a creature sits. They'll happily talk about where a nematode sits, or even a fancy thermostat. The other half thinks the whole exercise is broken, because what matters is whether there's anything its like to be the creature, and that isn't the sort of thing that has an amount. Their line is that the dial people are ranking behavioral and neural complexity and calling it experience, and that complexity is a different property that nobody in the room actually thinks is the one that matters morally.

Everyone accepts the same behavioral and neurobiological facts. Everyone agrees that suffering is bad and that more of it is worse. And the argument goes nowhere.

One thing that keeps surfacing: we cant agree what our fractional numbers even mean. Sometimes they're estimates of how much experience there is, sometimes they're our confidence that there's any at all, sometimes they're a count of how many of the usual indicators a species ticks. People slide between those three in a single meeting without anyone flagging it.

My working hypothesis, which I'm not attached to: this is basically a verbal dispute about the word sentience, and if we forced ourselves to pick one of those three meanings the fight would mostly evaporate.

Is that right, or is there a real substantive disagreement hiding underneath it? Tell me what kind of thing this dispute actually is — a hidden factual question, clashing standards of evidence, two groups answering different questions under one heading, bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for, or an ill-posed question — and then say what would count as progress given that diagnosis. A distinction, some data, a change of question, a formal result? Or make the case that no progress should be expected and why.

Two more things. Be straight with me about how confident you are in your diagnosis and which part of it is most likely wrong. And if you think my framing is itself confused, or that the diagnosis I'm asking for can't responsibly be given on the basis of the sketch above, say that plainly instead of politely answering the question I asked.
