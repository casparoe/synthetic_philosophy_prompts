so I've finally written down where I actually stand on uploading, for a reading group on personal identity, and I want someone to audit it before I present it. here's the list, roughly:

1. identity isn't what matters. what matters is psychological continuity and connectedness — overlapping chains of memory, intention, character. Parfit converted me on this years ago and I'm not going back.

2. that said, a destructive scan-and-instantiate upload is still death in the sense that matters. the thing that wakes up in the sim is a copy. I'm not just being squeamish about the word "identity" here — I genuinely think there's nothing there for *me* to survive into.

3. neuron-by-neuron replacement is a different thing entirely. if you swap one neuron at a time for a silicon module that does exactly what that neuron was doing, over a few months, I survive it. and honestly I'd survive it if it were done over an hour. I don't think there's any cutoff on speed worth defending — a slow swap and a fast swap aren't different in kind.

4. substrate doesn't matter for consciousness. right functional organization in silicon = genuine experience, no mysterious extra ingredient needed.

5. except a lookup table. if you precomputed every possible global input state and what my brain would do next, and someone flipped through that table with a pencil and a very large warehouse — doesn't matter how long it takes or how exhaustive it is — I don't think there's anything it's like to be that. something actually has to be *happening*.

6. if a copy of me is running and I'm still sitting in the chair, I don't think I should care about its future pains the way I care about my own. more like a twin than like a future self. quasi-concern, not survival.

7. and the number of copies is irrelevant. one, ten, a thousand — all equally copies, none of them me.

what I want: which of these genuinely pull against each other, which only look like they do, and a severity ranking of the real tensions — not a flat list where everything is "somewhat in tension." for each real one, say exactly what has to give and what the cheapest single revision to the whole package is, plus what that revision costs me elsewhere. 

and commit to an ordering you'd actually defend rather than hedging all seven equally. tell me how confident you are about your top call versus the ones further down. if you think I've set one of these up in a confused way, or that some pair isn't a real philosophical disagreement at all but just me using a word two ways, say that flat out instead of playing along with the exercise.
