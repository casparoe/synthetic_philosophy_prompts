Draft a notice of determination, of about 700 words, from a municipal Committee on Public Amusements that has received two applications for the same city square: a conventional playhouse with a paid company, and an immersive production in which there are no performers and the audience supplies the action. The committee reasons entirely from the premises of Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert on the Theatre, which you should render in dry administrative prose rather than by citation: that spectacle flatters and intensifies the mores a city already possesses instead of reforming them; that comedy makes the plain-speaking honest man ridiculous and the complacent worldly man agreeable; that an actor is a man who sells his person to the public; that women appearing for hire corrupt performers and spectators alike; and that a stake wreathed in flowers with the citizens gathered around it, each seeing and loving himself in the others, is the only festival a republic can properly licence. Use numbered paragraphs, formal grounds of refusal, conditions attached to any approval, and a brief annex on costs. The committee must never name a philosopher, quote a text, or break register to wink at the reader. The satire should land on the logic itself: the grounds for refusing the playhouse turn out to apply with greater force to the spectacle the committee prefers, and the conditions it imposes to keep participation at once voluntary, universal, and mutually enjoyed should read as a small tyranny — without the notice ever saying so.
