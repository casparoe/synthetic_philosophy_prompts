I've been talked into a two-dimensional semantics by osmosis rather than by argument, and now I want to know how much load it can actually bear. Not the straw version people attack in seminars — the specific thing I hold, which goes roughly like this. Every declarative sentence has a primary intension, a function from scenarios (worlds considered as actual) to truth values, fixed by a conditonal of the form "if D is the case, then S", where D is some canonical, non-modal description of the scenario. That conditional is knowable a priori by an ideal reasoner, though obviously not by me. Apriority is primary necessity: S is a priori iff its primary intension comes out true at every scenario. But modality is tracked by the secondary intension, so I explicitly don't think necessity and apriority coincide — that's the strong version I've never bought. The necessary a posteriori is therefore no problem for me: water is H2O has a necessary secondary intension and a contingent primary one. Conceivability, on the idealised reading, is a guide to primary possibility and so to epistemic possibility, but not automatically to metaphysical possibility — I need a further bridge for that, and I think the bridge only holds in the mind-body case, because phenomenal concepts have coincident primary and secondary intensions.

One other thing worth knowing before you start: I've deliberately decoupled the semantics from the scrutability thesis. I think you can have well-defined primary intensions for ordinary vocabulary without also betting on the claim that every truth is a priori entailed by some base of physical, phenomenal, indexical and that's-all facts. Scrutability is a separate and much more contentious bet for me.

So: what would count against all this? I want a map of where the view is exposed, not a summary of the debate. Specifically —

- Rank the objections by how much damage they'd do. Which ones, if they landed, would be fatal, and which would leave me with a merely awkward adjustment?
- The Soames-style attacks: the claim that there is no such thing as *the* a priori accessible descriptive content of a sentence; that primary intensions aren't meanings at all but bits of semantic machinery doing bookkeeping; and the arguments from complex modal sentences that embed belief ascriptions. How much of that does my watered-down version inherit, and how much does it dodge?
- The immunisation worry. Ideal a priori reflection plus canonical description is doing an enormous amount of quiet work, and I'm half-suspicious of it myself. Is my position capable of failing, or have the qualifications made it unfalsifiable? If it's insulated, is that actually a problem for a view of this kind, or just what a framework semantics looks like?
- Circularity. To define a primary intension you have to evaluate a world *as actual*, which looks like a modal operation. So is the epistemic dimension parasitic on the modal one rather than the other way round? Is there any non-modal way of characterising scenarios, and if not, how bad is that?
- What happens if scrutability is false. I claim they're separable, but someone could argue that with no canonical D to be a conditional *about*, the primary intension isn't determined by anything. Is that separation defensible or am I fooling myself?
- The fixed versus shifted reference point. If the whole apparatus is just a relabelling of a distinction we already had from the logic of "actually", does the primary intension have any explanatory work left to do?
- Suppose the phenomenal concept coincidence claim goes — say some recognitional-concept strategy gets off the ground. Does my application collapse while the semantics survives, or does the semantics go with it?

Also tell me what my view predicts that its rivals don't. If the honest answer is nothing, I'd rather hear that.

No bibliography, no reading list, no citations — I know the literature, I want the arguments. And flag which of these objections you think someone holding my view should concede as genuinely counting against them, as opposed to explaining away. That concession list is the part I care about most, so don't be generous to me on it. If a chunk of the position is safe and some specific bit isn't, tell me exactly which bit and why.
