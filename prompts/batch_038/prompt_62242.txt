I keep circling back to the "is DNA literally a code or is that just a metaphor" fight and I genuinely can't tell whether anything is being decided. You've got the code-biology types saying the codon assignments are arbitrary and there's an actual translation apparatus doing the decoding, so it's a code in the same sense Morse is; the Maynard Smith line, which grants all that but says what does the work is an evolved signalling function rather than the arbitrariness itself; and then the deflationists, Sarkar-ish, who say strip the rhetoric and you're left with template-directed chemistry plus binding affinities, and 'information' buys you nothing. Meanwhile my own corner of biology keeps handing everyone awkward data: variant codes where UGA reads as tryptophan in mitochondria and as stop elsewhere, and now whole bacterial genomes rebuilt from scratch with codons reassigned and the redundant ones folded out — i.e. the mapping demonstrably gets rewired, both by evolution and by grad students.

My working hypothesis, which I'm not married to: the camps are answering different questions and not noticing. One side is asking why this particular sequence-token reliably yields this product (a historical/causal question), the other is asking whether the sequence-to-product relation has the right structure to be semantic, and every argument that looks like a refutation is just an answer to the other question. Is that right, or is there a substantive disagreement buried in there that the verbal gloss is hiding? Give me the diagnosis grounded in what each camp actually claims, not a survey, plus an honest take on whether the recoded-genome work could ever settle any of it or whether its orthogonal to both questions.
