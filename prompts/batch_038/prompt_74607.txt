I am drafting a paper on the epistemology of long-run prediction, and the argument that was meant to occupy its middle section now appears to prove considerably more than I can defend. It runs as follows.

(1) Evidence bears on a theory only through the predictions that theory generates.
(2) A long-run theory predicts outcomes that, if they occurred, would be produced by processes operating across periods in which many independently varying factors intervene.
(3) Whenever such a prediction fails, the historical record supplies at least one of those factors, independently documented, that accounts for the failure.
(4) An auxiliary appeal to an independently documented factor is not ad hoc; it is routine scientific practice.
(5) In the Malthusian case the factors are of precisely this kind: synthetic nitrogen fixation, the demographic transition, the cultivation of New World land, fossil-fuel energy, and Boserupian intensification induced by population pressure itself.
(6) So two centuries of rising per capita income do not disconfirm Malthus: the positive checks were postponed rather than refuted, and the theory stands ready to be vindicated later.
(7) Nothing in (2) through (6) is special to Malthus. History never runs out of documented intervening factors, so any long-run theory whose predictions fail is rescuable in the same way.
(8) Hence no long-run theory can be disconfirmed by the failure of its predictions.
(9) Given (1), no long-run theory is answerable to evidence at all.
(10) If answerability to evidence is what makes a body of claims empirical, then (9) places demographic transition theory, integrated assessment models in climate economics, and long-run extrapolation in evolutionary biology and cosmology in the same category as Malthusianism.

I am unwilling to accept (10), and I am not prepared to grant that the escape from the Malthusian trap told us nothing about Malthus's theory and everything about his timing. Tell me which step to reject and what the rejection costs; I would most like to keep (1) and (5). I also want the account you give to respect a constraint I do not see how to satisfy: it must condemn the Malthusian rescue while acquitting the inference from the anomalies in the orbit of Uranus to the existence of Neptune, since that rescue is universally regarded as legitimate. Finally, on the view you favor, what would count as evidence against a long-run theory, and is any coherent content left for the idea that a theory is correct but premature? Please write in American English.
