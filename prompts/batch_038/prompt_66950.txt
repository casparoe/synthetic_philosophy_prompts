I'm trying to figure out whether "democratic backsliding" names a real thing in my head or is just what I call my side losing. Verdicts first, then a principle I've been carrying around that I suspect is broken.

Expanding a constitutional court by five seats and filling all five before the newly elected parliament has even convened: backsliding, no argument. A national senate leaving a vacant supreme court seat empty for a year without a single hearing: also backsliding, and this one bothers me because nothing illegal happened and no written rule was broken. Lowering the voting age to 16: obviously not. Barring citizens who've lived abroad more than fifteen years from voting: I won't call it backsliding when a stable old democracy does it, but I would call it that when a new government does it this year, and I can't tell you why those aren't the same act. Prosecuting a former head of government on ordinary charges with real evidence behind them: not backsliding, though I flinch. Inventing the charge after the conduct: backsliding. Redrawing districts so a party takes 60% of the seats on 45% of the vote: backsliding now, but the same trick run by the other party thirty years ago I wouldn't have called that, and the only difference I can name is that the tools got sharper. Turning public broadcasting into a mouthpiece: backsliding. Subsidizing independent outlets so they survive at all: not.

My principle is: a move only counts if I'd call it backsliding whichever party did it, and it has to make the current rulers less likely to loose an election and hand over power. Everything else is just politics.

I know the senate case and the fifteen-year rule each break that somewhere. Give me one or two candidate principles stated precisely enough that I could actually test them, say which of my verdicts has to go to keep them, and tell me what the survivor says about a case I didn't list: an opposition-run city whose local elections get postponed indefinitely over a corruption emergency. No headings, bullets, bold, or tables please — continuous prose, like an email.
