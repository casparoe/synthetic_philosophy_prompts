I'm on the board of a city social-services department and Thursday we vote on whether to expand a pilot. Right now about a third of a claimant's monthly benefit gets loaded onto a restricted card that won't work at liquor stores, the casino, or fast-food places — the rest comes through as ordinary cash. The evaluation came back last month and its awkward: no measurable difference in nutrition, spending, or job starts between the restricted group and the control. Fraud referrals did drop, mostly because the balance is harder to convert to cash. But the polling is the striking part — support for the program as a whole went up eleven points in the districts where people knew about the card, and the council members who saved it in the last budget fight tell me privately that's the only reason it survived.

One board member has proposed keeping the card but shrinking the restricted share to a token amount, which strikes me as an admission of what it's actually for. Our claimant advisory panel is split right down the middle: two members call the card humiliating, one says she doesn't care a bit as long as the total went up.

Can you draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote? I need a clear bottom line — expand, expand with specific changes, or drop it — plus the reasoning behind it, and a real answer to the strongest case on the other side, which I think is the argument that walking away from a rule we know is humiliating costs us the whole program and the people it keeps afloat.
