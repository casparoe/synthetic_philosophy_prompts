I've been on the science-and-ideas beat for about six years now, and I keep coming back to the same dead end with the whole "is there life out there" question, so I want a diagnosis rather than another explainer.

Three episodes have stuck with me. The phosphine-in-Venus's-clouds business, where the argument very quickly stopped being about the spectrum and became about whether anyone was allowed to claim a molecule that hard to make abiotically. The interstellar cigar-rock, where one well-known guy said it might be alien hardware and most of the field said the lightcurve was weird but not that weird, and both sides seemed to be reasoning from a prior about how many probes a civilisation would send rather than from any observation. And the recent dimethyl-sulfide hint on that sub-Neptune, where a follow-up team got a low-significance non-detection and the two papers were, as far as I could tell, both doing their stats honestly. Then there's the Drake-equation wing, where the people who think life is probably common and the people who think we're almost certainly alone agree on every single term except the one about abiogenesis, and they argue about that term forever without moving.

My working hypothesis — tell me if it's wrong, I want to be wrong if I'm wrong — is that the abiogenesis term isn't really an estimate of anything, because there's no reference class: we have one data point, and we're the sort of observers who'd have one data point whether life was easy or nearly impossible, so the timing of life showing up early on Earth doesn't licence the inference people want it to licence. If that's right, a big chunk of the disagreement is about what a probability claim on a unique unrepeatable event even means, not about planets.

So: is this one dispute or two (the parameter dispute and the biosignature-dispute are maybe structurally different)? Verbal, substantive, a clash of priors nobody can argue for, or two camps answering different questions under one headline? Diagnose it using what each side actually says. Then tell me what would count as progress — a distinction, an observation, a formal result, a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected and why. And be specific about what a single finding like a second independent origin of life would and wouldn't settle.

No reading list, no catalogue of Fermi paradox solutions, and please don't sand off the disagreement for a general audience — I'll do the simplifying. Around 900 words.
