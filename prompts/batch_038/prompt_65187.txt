Third year PhD in a social evolution group — I work on public-goods virulence factors, mostly siderophore production in bacteria, cheater strains, multiple infection, the whole apparatus. A friend of mine who went into industry keeps sending me essays about something called the competitiveness constraint and the alignment tax, and last week I actually sat down and read a pile of them instead of skimming. The argument as far as I can reconstruct it: doing safety work properly costs a lab something real — time, compute, people, a delayed release — and that cost makes it relatively worse off against rivals who skip it, so even labs that would rather be careful get pushed toward the cheap-and-fast strategy, and voluntary restraint doesn't hold because the ones who defect are the ones who win. Then there are two rejoinders people make. One is that the tax is near zero because safety and capability are complements rather than substitutes, so being careful mostly makes you better and the dilemma dissolves. The other is that the constraint is soft because there are only a handful of serious players, which makes coordination feasible in a way it wouldn't be with a thousand.

Here's what I want from you. Translate that whole thing — the constraint, the tax, and both rejoinders — into the vocabulary I actually work in, as faithfully as you can manage: producers and cheaters, the cost/benefit ratio and the assortment term, multiplicity of infection, well-mixed versus structured populations, privatization, pleiotropy and other private benefits, policing and sanctions, metabolic prudence, within-host versus between-host selection, the transmission–virulence tradeoff. Start with a correspondence table — the lab that invests in safety is what, the racing lab is what, what plays the role of relatedness, what plays the role of diffusion scale, what plays the host — and then go claim by claim and sort each into three bins: translates cleanly, translates only with distortion (say exactly what got bent and in which direction), and has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Don't make my side look cleverer than it is. If a mapping only works because you quietly imported something my framework assumes, flag that rather than passing it off as translation. I want you to be especially blunt about whether calling safety a public good is a translation or a smuggled assumption, since a big chunk of my field is about how few of these supposed public goods stay non-excludable once you look hard at privatization and private benefit.

Then the part I actually care about. For everything that lands in the third bin, tell me whether the failure is a limitation of the evolutionary vocabulary — something real about the AI case that selection-among-strains genuinely cannot represent — or evidence that the original claim was vaguer than it sounded in English. A few specific things I want a position on rather than a menu. What, if anything, plays the role of r? Is the honest answer "nothing, this is a well-mixed population and that's the whole problem," or are there real assortment mechanisms hiding in the argument — personnel moving between labs, shared published methods, a few frontier systems sharing ancestry — that the essays just never name? Does the complements-not-substitutes rejoinder map onto pleiotropy and private benefit the way I suspect it does, and if it does, does that change what would count as evidence for it, since in my field you'd settle that by measuring the private benefit instead of arguing about it in a blog post? Does policing have a counterpart, and if it does, is the pessimism of the original argument about sanctions being absent or about sanctions being impossible — those feel like very different claims and I think people slide between them. And what's the fitness currency here: I can't see anywhere that competitive position and safety cost get commensurated into a single number, and I suspect that missing currency is doing more work than anyone admits. I'm not asking for citations or a reading list, just your best reconstruction and a careful account of where it breaks. Finish with one paragraph on whether my reflex — that this is the tragedy of the virulence commons with a few extra steps — is a useful compression, or exactly the kind of flattening that erases the part worth arguing about.
