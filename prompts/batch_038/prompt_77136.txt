I hold the philosophy seat on an interpretability panel at a research institute, and I am drafting the framing memorandum for a case that comes before us next month. I should like your help reasoning it through, both because the memorandum must be defensible to the engineers on the panel and because I intend to adapt the exercise as a take-home for my graduate seminar on the metaphysics of representation.

Our charge is narrow. For a given trained model we must decide whether it has identified the ontology its developers intended — that is, whether the model represents the target kind, as opposed to something that merely co-varied with that kind in the training data. We record separately, where it matters, whether information present in the model is available to it. Our verdicts are minuted, and my difficulty is that I cannot state the criterion I have in fact been applying.

The record to date. In each case the question was whether the model possesses the concept of the target property, and my vote was with the majority every time.

1. Dog classifier. Ninety-nine per cent accurate on the test distribution; a linear probe recovers the dog feature from the penultimate layer. When animals are composited onto unfamiliar backgrounds, accuracy falls to chance. Verdict: no.

2. Chess model trained solely on game scores — sequences of moves, no diagrams, no rules text — which reaches master strength. No linear probe for the position succeeds, but a nonlinear readout trained on its activations reconstructs the board accurately; the model itself cannot be asked to report a position. Verdict: yes. Minute: inability to report was held not to bear on possession.

3. Sentiment classifier whose activations encode whether a review is sarcastic. The feature is causally implicated in producing correct labels for sarcastic negatives, and does so across genres and periods of text. The model cannot answer any direct question about sarcasm without further fine-tuning data. Verdict: yes. Minute: information that is present and used but not reportable is still information the model has.

4. Lesion detector in medical imaging. Accurate across scanners, sites and patient populations, and still accurate after site-specific correlates (implants, positioning markers, burned-in text) are scrubbed. No probe for the lesion succeeds at any depth; the nearest decodable features are texture statistics. Verdict: yes. Minute: what persuaded me was that it would have been right in precisely those situations in which the correlated cues were absent.

5. Reading-comprehension model that uses the number of items mentioned in a passage. It answers arithmetic questions about that number correctly, but only in the surface form in which it was trained; paraphrases fail. Separately, it judges correctly whether two differently worded passages mention the same number of items. It cannot combine the two capacities: told that a passage mentions three items and that a second passage mentions the same number as the first, it fails to say how many the second mentions. Verdict: no. Minute: a capacity that cannot be brought to bear on anything else is a lookup table, not a concept.

6. Warehouse navigation model. No trace of doorways, decodable or behavioural; yet attributing beliefs and desires about doorways predicts its trajectories better than any other gloss we tried. Verdict: no. Minute: predictive convenience in an intentional gloss is not a finding about the model.

The new case. A diagnostic model answers questions about cavitation in industrial pumps from vibration and acoustic logs. (a) No probe succeeds — neither linear nor a large nonlinear probe trained on abundant paired data; the best we achieve is chance. (b) Its single-property judgments are robust: they survive re-sensoring, different pump models, adversarial rescaling of the signals, paraphrased questions, and the removal of every correlate we could identify in the training logs. (c) It fails systematically when cavitation must be combined with other information it plainly has — for instance, whether throttling the inlet valve would leave the impeller at risk — although it answers each half of such a question correctly in isolation. (d) A second model given the first model's activations cannot recover cavitation; given instead the first model's answers to a battery of counterfactual questions, it can. (e) Ablating one attention head eliminates performance on all cavitation questions and on nothing else.

Please address the following in numbered sections, and do not cite literature: the record above is the whole of the evidence, and I want analysis rather than a survey of what has been said about machine concepts.

1. Formulate the candidate criteria that could generate this record — at minimum, decodability, counterfactual tracking, causal use in producing behaviour, systematic integration with the rest of the system's capacities, and charitable interpretive fit — and say which of them, alone or in combination, are consistent with all six verdicts. Where a criterion must be qualified in order to survive, state the qualification and what it costs.

2. Bring out explicitly the pressure that cases 3 and 5 exert on one another, and say what distinction a defender of integration as a necessary condition must draw between them if the record is to remain coherent.

3. Predict my verdict on the new case, with a rough degree of confidence, identifying the precedent I shall lean on hardest and the one I shall have to distinguish.

4. Recommend the decision the panel should reach if its aim is consistency with its own minutes: name the closest analog among the six, say whether the record genuinely underdetermines the case, and, if it does, state what principle the panel would be adopting by ruling yes and by ruling no, and which of the two it can live with.

5. Design a further case, described precisely enough that each surviving criterion entails a determinate verdict on it, which would settle whether the minute recorded against case 5 was directed at the failure of composition as such or at the fragility of each capacity under paraphrase. Say what result would falsify each reading.

6. For each criterion that survives, state what conception of content, or of concept possession, it commits us to, and whether on that conception the question whether this model has the concept of cavitation has an answer independent of our scoring conventions. Close by identifying which of the six recorded verdicts is least stable under the prediction you made in section 3.
