I spent thirty-eight years in experimental biophysics, and only since retiring have I read philosophy properly; the communitarian writing on personhood coming out of sub-Saharan Africa has unsettled me more than anything else I have encountered, chiefly because I cannot tell whether I actually believe it. Below are my verdicts on cases. I would like to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

A man who has lived alone in a cabin for thirty years by his own choice: full moral standing, certainly, yet something in him seems atrophied — and I would not say that of a merely lonely man in a city.

A castaway who writes letters to children who will never read them: fully a person, and somehow more so than the hermit, though neither of them is answered.

An infant: not yet a person in the developed sense, but its claims are unconditional. I will not accept that its standing waits upon being welcomed.

A patient in a persistent vegetative state whose sisters visit and speak to him daily: I accept that his personhood is now sustained by their regard. A non-verbal autistic adult with one devoted carer — structurally the same situation: I find the identical conclusion repugnant, and I cannot say why.

A chatbot with which one grieving woman has a relationship of exactly that shape: not a person, on my view.

A guard who spent twenty years degrading those in his charge: I want to say he has diminished himself, and equally that nothing he did can strip him of his protections.

My tentative formulation is that a person is a being whose ceasing to exist would wrong at least one other being. Please test it against these cases, repair or replace it, and state the result clause by clause, giving the case that motivates each clause. Tell me plainly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, and then apply it to two situations I have not raised: a nun under a perpetual vow of silence, and the last surviving speaker of a dying language. Finally, and most importantly: do my verdicts make me a relational theorist at all, or a capacity theorist with relational decoration?
