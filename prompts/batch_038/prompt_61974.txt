I'm running a 90-minute workshop with a weird mix: half the room uses interpretability tooling every day and has never thought about what they're committed to, half have read philosophy about representation and never opened a transformer. What I want out of you is a decision tree that sorts an attendee into a named position on whether neural nets have representations — starting only from claims they'd happily assent to, never from the labels, so people get sorted by what they believe rather than what they call themselves.

Work from the logical space first, names second. The branch questions I *think* matter: whether a state has to be capable of misrepresenting to count as a representation at all; whether content is fixed by the state's causal role inside the model or by its correlation with the training distribution; whether polysemanticity is a defeater or just measurement noise; and whether realism about features requires the direction to actually be doing causal work in the computation. Rearrange, cut, or add branches if you think mine are the wrong ones, and tell me which one has to sit at the root and why.

Leaves need to include, at minimum: activations-are-representations-by-fiat, functional/causal realism, information-without-aboutness deflationism, flat-out eliminativism, instrumentalism about features, and feature-realist-but-circuit-skeptic — plus any cell you think is empty, with a diagnosis of whether its empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unclaimed.

Then flag honestly which pairs of leaves no question can separate, and which questions feel diagnostic but aren't. And be committed, not even-handed: tell me which leaf most working ML people actually land in once you run them through it, and where in your own tree you're making a judgment call rather than tracking a real distinction.
