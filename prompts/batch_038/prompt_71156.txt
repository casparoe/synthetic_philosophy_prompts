I finished my dissertation last spring on credal sets and lower previsions — the whole imprecise-probability apparatus — and spent four years happily arguing with philosophers about whether a rational agent's beliefs have to be a single real-valued function. Then in January I took a job as a methods analyst in a hospital's patient-safety unit, which is where it stopped being abstract. Three weeks in I got handed this: a single lot of a vascular catheter, 41 documented uses, zero confirmed bloodstream infections. Except the unit's own audit says event capture is lossy — some infections get coded as sepsis-of-unspecified-origin, some patients transfer out and we never see the follow-up — so the honest statement is that we observed zero *reports*, and the true reporting rate is somewhere between, very generously, one-in-four and one. My proposal to the safety committee was to not collapse that into a number: treat the reporting rate as ranging over a set, get a set of posteriors for the true event rate, and report the interval of upper bounds rather than a point. With zero events in 41 uses the usual crude upper bound is around 7%, but if only a quarter of events actually reach us the same calculation is more like 29%, and the committee's action threshold sits right in the middle of that range. So the entire decision hinges on a nuisance parameter the data cannot settle, and I'd rather say so than smuggle in a point estimate of it.

Next Thursday I give the monthly methods forum talk — 25 minutes, then questions, and the room decides whether this becomes a real piece of work or gets shelved. It's about twenty people: a dozen clinical epidemiologists and trialists who think exclusively in operating characteristics, coverage, and whether a figure can survive being put in a letter to the manufacturer; the unit director who has to actually sign that letter; two senior Bayesians from the biostat group, one of whom builds hierarchical models for a living and considers anything short of putting a hyperprior on the reporting rate to be a cop-out dressed up in notation; and one philosopher-of-statistics person who does read the imprecise-probability literature and will be the most dangerous person present, because she'll know precisely where my argument is thinnest and she will not be rude about it. My argument, roughly: a single number here buys its precision with an unstated point estimate of reporting; representing reporting as a set yields a set of posteriors whose spread is a feature, since it isolates exactly the part of the question the evidence can't touch; you can still act, using something like maximality or gamma-maximin instead of pretending the spread evaporates; and the useful deliverable for a director is "keep versus pull is not rankable without a judgement about capture, and here is the judgement that would flip it," not a spurious 7%.

So: build me the prep sheet. Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually field from *this* audience, ranked by how much damage each would do in the room — and please don't give me the philosophy-seminar hits, because nobody here has heard of a Dutch book and I do not want to spend my question period defending coherence theorems to people who care about coverage. For each objection I want who it comes from and how they'd phrase it, since the trialist version and the hierarchical-Bayes version can be aimed at the exact same soft spot and sound nothing alike; a two-minute answer I can say out loud without hedging every clause; and a blunt line about where that answer is weakest. Mark which ones I should concede inside the first five minutes to buy credibility, which ones I cannot concede without losing the whole proposal, and the single reply that has to land for this to survive the meeting. I already know the dilation question is coming — that more data can widen the interval — and I'm honestly scared of "why not just put a prior on the reporting rate and be done with it," since I don't have a crisp answer for why that isn't strictly better. Finish with the question I'm dreading, the one I'm praying nobody asks, phrased the way it would actually be asked, and what I say if it lands anyway.
