A colleague of mine is writing a book defending Descartes' doctrine that non-human animals are automata — no thought, no language, and, on the strict reading she favors, nothing it is like to be one. Her verdicts so far are these. A dog with a crushed paw that howls, limps, and guards the limb for days: mere machinery registering damage. A six-month-old infant in pain: genuinely feels it. A stroke patient with global aphasia who can no longer speak or understand speech but plays chess, solves novel puzzles, and communicates by gesture: unquestionably a thinking being. An African grey that names shapes and colors accurately and asks for the particular food it wants: a very elaborate clock. A child born deaf-blind who acquires tactile signing: obviously a mind.

Two cases have been put to her for a seminar panel. First, a large language model: bodiless, not human, and unlike the parrot never trained on any narrow labeling task, yet it answers arbitrary novel prompts appropriately. Second, a patient diagnosed as vegetative years ago who, under neuroimaging, activates motor areas just as healthy controls do when asked to imagine playing tennis, and who can now answer unfamiliar yes-or-no questions by imagining tennis for one answer and walking through her childhood home for the other.

Predict her verdicts on both. Which criteria are consistent with all five earlier rulings — flexible use of language, possession of a rational soul or membership in the human kind, general-purpose problem solving, second-order awareness, or something else the data support — and where do those candidates come apart on the new pair? Commit to a best guess in each case, explain why it beats the alternatives, and name the further case that would separate the surviving candidates. Finally: Descartes' own answer to the automaton objection was that no machine could be made to respond in appropriately varied ways to whatever is said in its presence. Does the first case defeat that reply, and did her five prior rulings already commit her to giving it up — or can she absorb the language model with the doctrine intact?
