Okay so I spent like three hours last night arguing with a friend — population geneticist, insufferable about it — over whether "function" in molecular biology has to mean selected effect, and I came out the other side convinced I can make my case in public. Which is a problem, because I'm giving a 25 minute talk in six weeks and the room is not friendly in the way I'd planned for.

The workshop is on genome annotation. Roughly: a dozen people who curate Gene Ontology annotations for a living, a couple of computational biologists who do homology-based transfer, three philosophers of biology (two of them real teleosemantics people who will hate me on principle), and at least one person who was in the room for the 2012 ENCODE mess, where the consortium announced ~80% of the genome had a 'biochemical function' and got absolutely torn apart by the evolutionary crowd for conflating activity with function.

My thesis, sketch only: function talk at the molecular level is licensed *exclusively* by causal-role evidence, and this isn't the usual pluralist shrug about both accounts having their place — it's an epistemic claim about what could possibly support what. My argument is from the evidence-code vocabulary GO actually uses. IDA, IMP, IEP, the high-throughput versions: all of these are causal-role support, you show what the thing does in some system. There is no code for "selection shaped this activity," and there couldn't be one, because the comparative stuff — conservation scores, dN/dS, the IBA/IBD annotations that come off the phylogenetic trees — licenses claims about a *sequence lineage*, not about an activity being a selected effect. A term can be conserved across a tree for reasons that have nothing to do with fitness contribution. So etiological ascription is systematically underdetermined at molecular scale, and better phylogenetics doesn't fix it. Then my positive move: functions are two-place, always relative to a specified background system (cell type, condition), and that's what ENCODE actually got wrong — not that activity is too weak a basis for function, but that they never pinned down the background.
