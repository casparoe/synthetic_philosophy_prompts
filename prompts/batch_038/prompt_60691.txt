So the no-trade theorem — Milgrom and Stokey, right, common priors plus common knowledge of rationality means speculative trade can't happen. Doesn't that basically settle what any given trade means? If two people swap shares, one of them has to be wrong about where the price is going, and since the theorem says rational agents with the same priors would never do it, whatever trade I actually observe must be driven by somebody who's either irrational or being forced to move for non-information reasons. Given that, how should I use it? If I'm thinking about buying and I can't articulate any private information I have, should I just assume I'm the sucker and sit out — or is there a way to tell from price and volume which side of the trade is the uninformed one?
