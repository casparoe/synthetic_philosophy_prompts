I am a philosopher of language by training, and this term I have had to take over two lectures on free will and moral responsibility in our second-year ethics module at short notice. To prepare, I read a chapter of a recent popular book on the subject, which presents Galen Strawson's Basic Argument as follows:

"Strawson's argument is a new way of pressing the old problem of determinism. If everything we do is caused by the past, our choices are merely links in a chain that began before we existed, and we cannot be blamed for them. Strawson's twist is to push the chain inside the agent: we act as we do because of the way we are — our character, desires and dispositions — and since we never chose our character, we cannot be responsible for what follows from it. To be responsible for an action you would have to be responsible for the character that produced it, and for that you would have to be responsible for whatever produced that character, and so on, until you would have to have made yourself out of nothing. That is impossible, so free will is an illusion: the libertarians are wrong, the compatibilists are merely changing the subject, and any institution that treats people as deserving praise or punishment rests on a mistake we ought to correct."

Before I build a lecture around this, I should like an accuracy audit. Please work through the passage claim by claim, distinguishing what a specialist would accept as a fair statement of Strawson's position, what is a simplification that could stand with qualification, and what is a misrepresentation. Two things in particular trouble me. First, whether the argument really needs determinism to be true at all, or whether Strawson takes it to work either way. Second, whether the regress is properly described as running over *characters*, since I suspect that formulation is exposed to an objection — about deliberate self-formation — that Strawson's own version is meant to block. Finally, tell me whether the closing claims about compatibilism and about correcting our institutions follow from the argument or are additions to it, and what Strawson would say about them. Where the popular version goes wrong, please give me a correct formulation short enough to put on a slide.
