**Draft: "Does the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction survive its own test?"**

*1. The phenomenon.* Present two dots of different colours, one after the other, at spatially separated locations. At the right combinations of separation and interval, subjects report a single dot crossing the gap and changing colour partway along its path — and they report the change as having already happened before the second dot was lit. This is the colour phi phenomenon, described by Kolers and von Grünau, who took it up in response to a question from Nelson Goodman about what a colour change would do to the ordinary phi effect. The philosophical sting is that the report concerns a time at which the information required for it was not yet available to the subject.

*2. The two standard answers.* Either the experience as it unfolded was of a dot moving while keeping its first colour, and the report was assembled later from a record that had been edited after the second dot arrived — in which case the subject is an unwitting fabulist about her own immediate past (the Orwellian option); or conscious content was simply withheld until the second stimulus arrived, so that what the subject had was the composed sequence all along, delivered a little late (the Stalinesque option). Dennett pressed the pair against Cartesian materialism in *Consciousness Explained*: if there were a single place where it all comes together for the subject, the two options would have to differ in what she would find there, and since they do not, there is no such place.

*3. What I want to add.* The dichotomy is defined over a premise that is usually left implicit. Call it the **content-bearing assumption**: for any report, there is some state that carries the content being reported, and that state either did or did not carry that content at the time of the experience. Given that assumption, revision and delay exhaust the space. Without it, they do not. A third possibility is that the state from which the report is generated carries no determinate content about colour-at-a-point-on-the-trajectory at all, and acquires one only in the course of being interrogated. Call this the **query-time option**. On it, nothing was rewritten and nothing was delayed, because there was never anything of the right kind to rewrite or release.

This is not Dennett's own conclusion in different clothes. His conclusion is deflationary: the distinction makes no observational difference, so drop it. Mine is that the distinction *does* make an observational difference — but only conditionally, on the content-bearing assumption, and that assumption is separately testable.

*4. A test.* The three options predict different **shapes of error** under interference, not different rates of illusion. Introduce a backward mask or a concurrent interference task at varied delays after the second dot, and ask about colour and motion in varied orders and response formats.

- *Orwellian:* the pre-revision content persists in some buffer, and revision overwrites it. Interference timed to damage the overwritten record should let the earlier content leak: reports drift back toward motion-without-colour-change, biased toward the first colour, at better-than-chance confidence.
- *Stalinesque:* composition precedes delivery, so interference degrades the composed percept wholesale. Reports collapse toward two static flashes, with no structured bias toward the first colour.
- *Query-time:* errors track the interrogative context itself. Reports differ in kind, not just in noise, depending on whether colour is asked about before motion and on how the response alternatives are framed.

*5. The worry I have about my own paper.* All of this may rest on a mistake: perhaps when the experience occurred is not the sort of thing that has a determinate answer independently of an account of report generation, in which case section 4 tests reporting architecture and not the timing of experience, and my title promises something the argument cannot deliver. I want to be a realist about the question — otherwise there is nothing to test — but I am sympathetic to the deflationary line, and I cannot currently say what keeps those two attitudes from contradicting each other.
