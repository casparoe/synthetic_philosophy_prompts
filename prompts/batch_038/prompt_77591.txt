I'm auditing a historical sociology seminar and we've spent the last two weeks on Weber's The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism. My prof keeps insisting that Weber is *not* arguing religion caused capitalism, but the text honestly reads like exactly that argument to me, so I'm trying to work out what he thinks he's refusing to claim. Here's the mechanism as I've got it: Calvinists were taught God had already fixed who was saved, which produced enormous anxiety since nobody could know where they stood; they started reading worldly success in their calling as a sign they were probably among the elect; so they worked and saved far harder than other people did; capital accumulated; and eventually the religious scaffolding fell away and left behind the hardened shell of methodical work discipline that Weber says we're stuck in now.

Two things bug me. First, strip out the theology and isn't this just the claim that beliefs influence behaviour, which is true of every religion that ever existed and cant be what makes the book a classic — so either I'm missing what the actual content of the claim is, or its a big deal mostly because of how it was packaged. Second, and this is the one I want the most help with: if the causal engine is anxiety about an uncertain salvation, then it should fire anywhere a religion makes salvation uncertain, and Catholic peasants were plenty anxious about their souls without inventing rational accounting and systematic reinvestment. So the thesis looks either trivially true or trivially false depending on how loosely you read the word spirit, and I don't see a third option. A classmate said Weber's escape is elective affinity rather than causation, but that sounds like a dodge to me, and I don't see how it differs from just noticing that two things happened to vary together.

Can you lay out what the non-trivial version of the mechanism actually is, what Weber's own comparative work does about the why-not-everywhere-else objection, and whether elective affinity is doing real explanatory work or is just a hedge? Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five points, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion.
