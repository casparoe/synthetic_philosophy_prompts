I am a process-safety engineer. For the better part of two decades my working life has consisted of producing and defending safety cases for hazardous installations: identifying initiating events, mapping barriers and independent protection layers against them with bowtie diagrams, demonstrating that residual risk has been reduced so far as is reasonably practicable, obtaining a formal deviation permit before any departure from the certified standard, and controlling change so that an approval granted for one purpose is not quietly used for another. I have no training in international law or political philosophy, and I have found that I can only think clearly about a dispute once I can state it in that vocabulary.

The dispute is the one over humanitarian intervention and regime change. On one side: the claim that a state or coalition may use force without Security Council authorisation to halt mass atrocity, provided the cause is just, the intention is right, non-military options have been exhausted, the means are proportionate, and there is a reasonable prospect of success — the position that the international commission reviewing the 1999 Kosovo campaign reached when it judged that intervention 'illegal but legitimate'. On the other side: the claim that the authorisation requirement is not a procedural formality but the very thing that prevents powerful states from presenting wars of choice as rescues, and that halting an atrocity and removing a regime are different undertakings with different prospects.

Please render both sides into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, working through the following:

1. Translate each of the criteria — just cause, right intention, last resort, proportionality, reasonable prospect of success — into the terms of hazard analysis and risk acceptance. Where two moral criteria collapse into one engineering notion, or one criterion splits into two, say so explicitly.
2. What is the counterpart of the authorisation requirement: a certification body, a permit issuer, a design authority? In engineering, is a standard's authority justified by its consequences — independence, avoidance of self-assessment bias, coordination among many actors — or by something else? If by its consequences, does that already concede part of the argument to the interventionist?
3. How should I represent Kosovo (no permit at all), Iraq in 2003 (a contested hazard assessment and a disputed legal basis), and Libya in 2011, where a permit issued for the protection of civilians was used, its critics say, to bring about a change of regime? In particular, does my vocabulary have the resources to distinguish acting within the letter of a permit from acting outside its intent, given that permits are usually drafted in terms of outcomes?
4. What does the objection that unauthorised intervention erodes an order preventing greater harm overall correspond to — degradation of a standard's authority, loss of independence between protection layers, normalisation of deviation?

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually want. State which claims translate cleanly, which only translate with distortion, and which have no expression at all in the language of safety cases. Test at least these candidates: the consent of the affected population; the moral weight of intention as against expected outcome; the asymmetry between acting and failing to act, given that a safety case treats an absent barrier exactly as an inadequate one; sovereignty as a constraint that is supposed to hold regardless of consequences; and the fact that in my discipline the party who created the hazard and the party who failed to prevent it are usually the same organisation, whereas here the intervener caused none of the harm it proposes to stop.

Finally, tell me whether that residue indicates a genuine deficiency in my framework — something real that risk analysis cannot represent — or whether it shows that the moral disagreement is less substantive than its participants suppose. Do not settle the question by quietly importing the consequentialist assumptions of my own discipline and calling the result a translation; if the translation only works because ALARP reasoning has already decided the issue, I want that said.

I do not want citations, a bibliography or a reading list. Please write in British English, and organise the answer under headings corresponding to the four numbered items and the residue report.
