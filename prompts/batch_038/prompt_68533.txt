Write a resignation letter of roughly 550 words from a law of nature to the Board of the Cosmos, care of its human resources function, giving notice. The writer has held the post since the first instants and is leaving because of a restructuring in which governance has been eliminated: it was once credited with making events happen, underwriting counterfactuals, and explaining regularities, and has now been informed that it never did anything of the kind — it was only ever a compact redescription of what the events were doing on their own, promoted to the post by a committee that weighed simplicity against strength. The register should be deadpan, formally polite, and genuinely aggrieved, like a long-serving employee made redundant by a cost-cutting exercise, and the grievances must be the real ones rather than invented complaints: that its tenure now depends on how things turn out arbitrarily far away in the record, so it cannot be said to hold anywhere locally; that the criteria by which it was selected are the committee's own and can be revised at will; that its own exact formulation has been quietly retuned several times during its service, so that it can no longer state what it currently says; that the new arrangement leaves it unable to answer the question it was hired to answer, since the only reply available is that events simply do what they do; and that the rival concern down the road is advertising the old job back with full modal authority, though it too cannot say where that authority comes from. Do not name any philosophers, works, or technical labels — the metaphysics should be legible from the personnel vocabulary alone. A reader who knows the literature should recognise each objection being staged and judge the satire fair, while a reader who does not should simply see a dignified worker being pushed out. Present it as continuous prose in letter form, with a salutation and a sign-off: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
