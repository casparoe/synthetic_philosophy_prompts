"Harsanyi's theorem proves that utilitarianism isn't a moral assumption at all, it's a consequence of minimal rationality. Here's the setup: each person's preferences over gambles can be represented by an expected utility, and society's preferences over the same gambles can be too. Add one ethical condition — if everyone prefers A to B, society must prefer A to B — and out comes the result: social welfare is a weighted sum of individual utilities. And since nothing in the setup privileges one person over another, the weights are all equal, so social welfare is just the sum of everybody's utility. That's classical utilitarianism, derived instead of asserted. Notice what it rules out too: because the only ethical premise is unanimity, the sole route to egalitarianism is to break one of the axioms, usually by refusing to let society maximize expected welfare — which is why Rawls ends up with a theory that can't cope with risk at all. And the theorem answers the old objection about interpersonal comparisons. It doesn't assume utilities are comparable across people; it shows they have to be, because the conclusion wouldn't mean anything otherwise."
