You are advising a small animal-welfare foundation that has committed roughly $2 million over four years to work on the ethics and practice of insect pest control. Its stated goal is not to protect crop yields and not, primarily, to save insects, but to reduce the total suffering that agriculture generates in them — including, if that turns out to be where the leverage lies, the suffering caused by the methods we use to kill them. The staff has narrowed the field to five candidate research programs and wants to know which two should get the money:

1. **Markers.** Work that would sharpen behavioral, neuroanatomical, and pharmacological evidence about which insect orders have something like valenced experience and which very probably do not.
2. **Method comparison.** A systematic ranking of control techniques — contact neurotoxins, ingestion baits, growth regulators that interrupt molting, species-specific viruses, pheromone mating disruption, irradiated-male release programs — by the likely duration and intensity of dying per individual killed.
3. **Numbers.** Better estimates of how many insects a hectare of cropland supports, how many are killed directly by control measures versus indirectly by habitat conversion, and how suppressing one field displaces populations into others.
4. **The sign question.** Whether the average insect life in an agricultural landscape is net positive or net negative for the insect, given that most individuals die young and die badly — parasitized, starved, desiccated. If lives are net negative, swift killing is a benefit and the method ranking nearly moot; if net positive, every reduction in abundance is a harm and the whole enterprise inverts.
5. **Uptake.** What regulators, extension services, and large growers would actually adopt, and which of the findings above would have to exist before they would adopt it.

Deliverable: begin with a bottom line of no more than three sentences naming your two picks and the single consideration that most drove the choice. Then at most 750 words of justification. I want the ranking defended rather than declared: state the criteria you are applying (how far upstream a question sits, tractability within four years by a small team, likelihood of changing what actually happens in fields, and robustness to the sign question resolving either way), explain how you traded them off against one another, and be explicit about which program you are deprioritizing most firmly and why its answer would not change much downstream. Close by naming the one or two pieces of evidence that would most plausibly make you reorder the list, and what you would fund instead.

Constraints: this is an argument about allocation, not a literature review. Do not supply a bibliography, a reading list, or citations to particular studies; if you invoke empirical findings, describe them generically. Assume I am already familiar with the standard arguments for and against insect sentience — I want your judgment, not a survey of the debate.
