I am not an academic — I manage a small freight depot on the edge of the city, and for the past four years I have been the only non-professional member of the board of a charity that runs a night shelter. I am writing because of something I keep running into, in myself, that I do not have a word for, and I want to describe it as plainly as I can, because I suspect that if I reached for a familiar term I would be pointed toward a body of writing that is not quite about my case. The first time it happened, the board was deciding whether to keep funding a daytime drop-in service or to move the money into more overnight beds. One of our members is a woman who spent twenty-five years as a trial lawyer, and she made the case for moving the money. She was patient with me, she answered every objection I raised, and by the end of the meeting I agreed with her completely. I still agree with her, six years on. But if you ask me now to say why, all I can do is reproduce her sentences, in something like her order, and I cannot tell you whether I am holding a view because it is correct or because I could not keep up with the person presenting it. Nothing she said struck me as shifty. As far as I can judge, she was honest and probably right.

Since then I have collected other instances, and they are what make me think this is a pattern rather than a private failing. My brother-in-law is like this at family dinners: after an hour with him I find myself asserting things at work the following week that turn out, on inspection, to be his phrasing and his evidence and nothing I can support. A few years ago I listened to a long interview with a commentator whose politics I distrust and whose manner I dislike, and he gave an account of something that has lodged in my head ever since; I have never been able to argue against it, and I have never been able to check it either. What connects these cases, and what I want to ask about, is a structural point that I have not seen anyone state. In almost everything else I defer about, checking is much cheaper than producing, and that is what makes deferral survivable. I could not rebuild a gearbox, but I know within a fortnight whether the truck has actually been fixed. I could not cook like a chef, but I can taste the result. With this kind of case the relationship seems to be inverted: the thing I am being given is easier to produce than it is for me to evaluate, and the failure does not announce itself when it happens. A case I cannot follow feels, from the inside, exactly like a case that is sound. I have no instrument that distinguishes them at the moment of assent, and afterwards the two leave the same trace in my head.

This is why I have been turning it over in connection with the arguments now going on about machines that will talk to us and will be very much better at talking than we are. Everything I read on that subject treats the danger as a variety of dishonesty — the machine flattering you, framing things, concealing, steering you toward something it wants. My own experience suggests there is a version of the trouble in which nobody is dishonest and nothing is concealed, where the other party is telling the truth and helping you as best it can, and where you are still left holding beliefs you did not earn and cannot audit. So: is there an established concept for this — for a situation in which someone's case simply exceeds your capacity to evaluate it, so that your agreement is manufactured rather than arrived at, and the problem lies in the shape of the encounter rather than in anyone's intentions? Please tell me what the nearest recognised ideas are, and for each one, exactly why it does not quite cover this, and then which of them comes closest. If nothing covers it, say so plainly and give it a name of your own, with a definition tight enough that I could tell whether a new case falls under it. I care much more about that carving than about the label: where the edges are. Does it apply to an excellent teacher, or to a book I can reread for a month at my own pace? Does it require that the other party be more able than me, or only that the case arrive faster than I can test it? Does it disappear in cases where I can check, and what happens when neither of us can? And is there any real difference, on my side of the table, between the honest version and the dishonest one — because from where I sit they are the same experience, and that is the part that frightens me. Finally, tell me how I would know if I am fooling myself here: if there is no distinct phenomenon at all, just the ordinary indignity of being out-argued with a story wrapped around it. Please answer in continuous prose, the way you would write a letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — and put your direct answer in the first paragraph, so that I know straight away whether there is an established name for this and what you take the thing to be.
