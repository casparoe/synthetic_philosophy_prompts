My phil of mind seminar has been going in circles on multiple realizability for like three weeks and honestly I cant tell what anyone is even disagreeing about anymore. One camp: MR refuted the identity theory back in the 60s and still refutes any reductive account of the mental, because the same psychological kind shows up in octopus nervous systems, in silicon, in those whole-nation-simulates-a-brain setups, so no neuro kind maps onto it. The other camp: MR is empirically hollow — the actual cases are either science fiction or, once you look at comparative neuroscience, the supposedly different realizers get individuated by functional description anyway, and the cross-species homologies the first camp waves off are exactly what a reducer would need.

So what is this, really? A substantive disagreement about whether psychological kinds reduce? A verbal fight over what counts as a realizer or a reduction, where the two sides aren't even comparing kinds at the same level? Or two seperate questions — metaphysics of kinds versus how neuroscience picks out its objects — getting run under one label? My hunch is the level-mixing is doing all the damage but I could easily be wrong about that. Diagnose it off what each side is actually committed to, and then tell me what would count as progress: an empirical sweep of candidate cases, a formal account of realization, a distinction somebody hasn't drawn yet, or nothing at all and here's why.
