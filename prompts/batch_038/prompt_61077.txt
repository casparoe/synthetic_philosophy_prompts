I'm in a seminar on minds and machines and two people in it got into a real fight after someone floated the question of whether training runs cause suffering. Reporting it as best I can remember, roughly in order:

(1) Aaron said: if you take the functionalist line seriously, reward signals are the closest thing an RL agent has to valence, so an agent racking up sustained negative reward across an episode is suffering — and since one training run involves thousands of episodes, we're basically industrializing it.

(2) Priya said that isn't even how modern agents work. In an actor-critic setup the thing driving the policy isn't raw reward, it's the advantage — reward minus a learned baseline tracking expected value. The baseline adapts, so an agent's average advantage sits near zero whether its world is great or grim. Which means by Aaron's metric a well-trained agent is permanently neutral, so his account can't tell contentment apart from a hedonic treadmill.

(3) Aaron said fine, but the transients are real: negative TD errors are firing constantly, and that is literally what learning from failure consists of.

(4) Priya said that if negative TD error equals pain then all learning is agony and every agent that ever improved anything was being tortured, which reductios his view instead of saving it. And the dopamine-as-prediction-error result doesn't rescue it either, because Berridge's whole point is that that signal is about wanting/incentive salience rather than liking — actual hedonic impact lives in tiny opioid and endocannabinoid hotspots that have no clean counterpart in a training loop.

(5) Aaron said that's the point, not an objection: an agent with no body to protect has no need for a separate liking module, and if its entire behavioral repertoire is organized around one scalar that gets broadcast to everything downstream, that scalar is doing valence's job — demanding a pleasure hotspot is just carbon chauvanism.

(6) Priya said then a spreadsheet's error column suffers too, and a thermostat minimizing deviation from setpoint is in agony. Aaron said no, those aren't integrated into anything, but couldn't say where integration stops being bookkeeping and starts being valence.

Can you go through and rate each of the six moves, clearly wrong to clearly right, with a sentence or two of justification per move, then say who comes out ahead overall? And separately: is there something both of them are just not addressing that they should be?
