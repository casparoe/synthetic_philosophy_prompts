our lab made me the official methods person and i've been trying to write down what I actually believe so it can go in the onboarding doc, but I don't trust that the pieces fit together. can you audit it? here's the list:

1. no priors, full stop. i don't want a "subjective prior" doing any work in what we publish. justification should be in terms of error rates: how often does this procedure get it wrong over repeated use.
2. what makes a method good is its long-run error control, not how strongly this particular dataset supports anything. the 5% is a property of the procedure, not of my one experiment.
3. which is why peeking / optional stopping is a real problem and not a technicality: if you look after every 20 subjects and quit as soon as it goes significant, your actual false-positive rate is nowhere near nominal, and a paper that did that simply didn't control its error rate. the stopping rule is part of the procedure and has to go into the analysis.
4. but when someone asks me whether to believe a single result, i catch myself reasoning about the data itself: if lab A and lab B end up with the same numbers, i don't see how A's data can be better evidence than B's becuase A had planned to stop at n=100 and B would have kept going if it hadn't hit significance. same data, same evidence, whatever the intention was.
5. i refuse to say a p-value is "the probability the null is true," and i correct people who do.
6. i do think a very small p is evidence against the null, and a large one is not evidence for it — that asymmetry feels right to me.
7. i also basically buy the argument that most published findings are false: low power, plus the fact that most of the hypotheses people bother to test were unlikely to be true in the first place, means the share of significant results that are real has to be low.
8. and i think the replication crisis is a misuse story, not a philosophy story — flexibility, p-hacking, HARKing, selective reporting. preregistration plus fixed sample sizes plus reporting everything, and we've fixed most of it.

which of these are actually in conflict and which only look like they are? rank the real ones by how bad they are rather than dumping them all in one pile, say what i'd have to give up in each case and what that costs me, and then tell me which single change would buy the most coherence for the least damage. no reading list please, i just want the audit.
