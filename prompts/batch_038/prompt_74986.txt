Got into a three hour arguement with a friend last night about whether the agricultural revolution was a mistake, and I came away less sure than I went in. My position: for basically everyone actually alive during the first several thousand years of farming, life got worse — more work for a narrower diet, worse health, and the beginning of real hierarchy — and the only reason we call it progress is that it allowed far more people to exist, which I don't think is the same thing as making people better off. I lean on three things: the old forager work-week numbers (the fifteen-to-twenty hours a week figure from the !Kung and Arnhem Land ethnographies), the skeletons (shrinking stature, dental caries, enamel defects, infectious lesions), and the fact that hereditary inequality and stored surplus seem to show up right alongside farming. He came back with: those ethnographic foragers had already been pushed onto marginal land by farming and herding neighbors, so they're not a fair baseline; the fifteen-hour figure excludes food processing, and adding it back gets you somewhere around forty; something called the osteological paradox, which supposedly means a cemetery full of healed lesions can indicate a healthier population rather than a sicker one, so my bone evidence proves nothing; and absurdly rich Paleolithic burials blanketed in beads, which would put inequality tens of thousands of years before farming.

Tell me what would actually count against my position. I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, ranked from "this sinks it" down to "merely awkward," and for each one say whether someone committed to my view would concede it as damaging or wave it off. Be honest about whether the position has been padded with escape clauses — nobody chose it, it was ratchet-like, bad for individuals but good for the species eventually — to the point where it can't fail, and whether that's a genuine problem for a claim of this kind. And tell me whether my view predicts anything the pro-agriculture side doesn't, or whether it's mostly a mood about the past dressed up as a finding.
