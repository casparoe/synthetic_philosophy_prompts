hey — I'm in a reading group on probabilistic reasoning and I've been asked to put in writing where I've landed on updating with conditionals, so everyone can take a swing at it. Before I circulate it, can you check whether the package actually hangs together? Here's what I'm committed to:

(1) Adams' thesis, for indicatives whose parts aren't themselves conditionals: the probability of an if-then just is the corresponding conditional probability.

(2) Lewis's triviality results settle it against truth conditions for conditionals. So I'm a non-cognitivist about them — an indicative conditional doesn't say anything true or false, and what we sloppily call its probability is really the conditional credence. I read Lewis as having proved my view right, not as having shown that I should water down (1).

(3) Which is why, in the Judy Benjamin case, I go with van Fraassen's rule. Prior: half on red, half on blue, and within each half the top and bottom quarters equally likely. She's told by someone she takes to be 3/4 reliable that if the card is in the red half then it's in the top quarter. The rule says leave the 1/2 on red exactly where it is and move the conditional to 3/4, so you end up with 3/8 on red-top, 1/8 on red-bottom, 1/2 on blue.

(4) More generally I'm a minimal-revision person: Jeffrey conditionalize on whatever partition the incoming information actually speaks to and change nothing else. I'm automaticly suspicious of any updating rule that isn't forced by the information.

(5) That said, I don't think any part of a credence fuction should be insulated from evidence by stipulation. If the informant's decision to bring up the red half at all carries information — and I think selection procedures usually do — then the update has to register that somewhere.

(6) Credences supervene on total evidence. No pragmatic fudging, no shaving numbers down for modesty's sake, nothing moved except by evidence.

So: which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which pairs only look bad until you spell them out properly? Rank the real tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally worrying, say for each one which commitment has to go or get qualified and what you'd replace it with, and then tell me the single revision that would clean up the most.
