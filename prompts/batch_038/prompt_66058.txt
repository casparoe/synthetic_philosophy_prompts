ok so I'm wrapping up a seminar week on the KK principle and the anti-luminosity argument, and instead of a summary handout I want to end the last session with a poem the students will argue about on the way out. 19 lines, proper villanelle, two rhymes all the way through, the usual refrain pattern.

here's the conceit, and it's the whole point so please don't soften it: refrain one is "I know, I know." It has to do double duty — read as the KK iteration, knowing that you know, and read as somebody brushing you off. Refrain two is a first-person report out of a slow series, word for word identical every single time it comes back while what it's about has drifted underneath it. Not the feeling-cold-and-slowly-warming-up one, everyone's done that one. Give me something else gradual and perceptual — light coming up a windowsill, a noise getting closer on the road, a smell leaving a room, your call — as long as the report can honestly be true at the start and honestly false at the end with no moment anywhere in between where the speaker could have caught it changing.

then the structural part, which is what makes this worth doing instead of just a villanelle with epistemology name-dropped on it: each tercet moves one notch further down the series, and I want a student who has actually worked through the argument to be able to put a finger on which tercet is the margin-for-error step — the one where saying "I know" costs you the case next door — and which one is the induction, the notch where the returning refrain stops being reassuring and starts being the problem. The closing couplet lands the two refrains side by side and just leaves them there. No resolution, no verdict on which principle dies, and please don't let the last line wink at the reader.

also, the thing I most want the poem to turn on is that KK is really just luminosity pointed back at knowledge itself — the same machinery that eats the slow series eats "I know that I know" — and I want that to show up in the fact that the two refrains are doing the same kind of work, never in anything stated. So no jargon in the poem at all: no sorites, no luminous, no margin, no epistemic, no names, no titles. Someone who's never heard of any of this should be able to read it as a poem about lying awake while the light comes up. Someone who has should be able to rebuild the argument from the refrains alone.

keep the two rhyme sets clean, don't cheat with near-rhymes on the refrains, and dont tack on an explanation afterwards — I don't want a note about the poem, I want the poem. One-line title if you feel like it, thats all.
