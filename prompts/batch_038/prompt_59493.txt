I am drafting a long post on indicative conditionals for a forum where the regulars will pull it apart line by line, so I would like the package audited before I publish it. Over years of reading the sequences and the subsequent arguing I have settled on the following: (1) indicative conditionals express propositions, so my credences in them are credences in something that is true or false; (2) for my own credences, P(A→B) = P(B|A) whenever P(A) > 0 — Stalnaker's thesis taken as a claim about me, not about every probability function in the space; (3) I update by strict conditionalisation, and I hold that the thesis in (2) should continue to be satisfied by my credences after any such update, since learning ought not to leave me worse placed than I am now; (4) conditional excluded middle holds for indicatives: for any A and B, either A→B or A→¬B; (5) I have plenty of intermediate conditional credences that I am not prepared to surrender — my credence that the train arrives on time given that it departs on time is roughly 0.85, against an unconditional 0.7 — and I count any view that collapses such a credence into the unconditional one, or drives it to 0 or 1, as refuted.

Two things already worry me, and I want to know whether they are real: that Lewis's triviality results are proved for arbitrary probability functions and so my restriction in (2) buys nothing; and that (4) is doing no work at all in the package. Say which pairs genuinely conflict and which merely look as though they do, rank the conflicts from most to least severe, give the cheapest repair for each together with what it costs, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Reply as exactly five numbered items of one or two sentences each, in descending order of severity, with no introduction or conclusion.
