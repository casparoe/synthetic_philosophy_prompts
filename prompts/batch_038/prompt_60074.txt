I have run a floatation studio for four years, and last spring a philosopher who floats with us weekly offered a framing that we subsequently put into our practitioner-training materials and, less defensibly, onto two pages of our website: that the isolation tank is a modern flying man. His case to my staff came in two moves. First, he said, the tank reinstates the conditions Avicenna stipulates — a body suspended in a medium that gives no perceptible support, sight cut off, sound cut off, limbs held apart so that none of them feels another. Second, he said, what a floater reports in that situation is what Avicenna predicts: an awareness of existing that carries no information about the body at all. So the tank, on his account, is not merely an illustration of the argument but evidence for its conclusion — that whatever it is that is aware of itself is not the body.

A graduate student who attended our open evening has written to object that the comparison cannot carry the second move at all, and that her reason compresses into a single sentence: the tank subtracts, whereas the flying man never had anything to subtract. I want to answer her properly, or concede properly, before we print anything further.

Could you audit the analogy for me? I would like a correspondence table taking each stipulation in Avicenna's setup and identifying what, if anything, plays its role in a tank, with a verdict on how faithful each pairing is; the disanalogies, separated into those that are merely picturesque and those that actually damage the intended use; an account of which features of the thought experiment the tank comparison quietly imports into a situation that lacks them; and a separate judgement on each of the two moves. In particular: is there a weaker claim about floating that the analogy can legitimately support, and can a tank serve as evidence for the stronger claim about the self, or does that inference require something no actual tank, however well built, could supply? If the analogy fails, tell me what should replace it — a better comparison for what the tank really does deliver, or a candid statement that nothing in the empirical world instantiates the flying man. I am not after references or a reading list; I want the reasoning.
