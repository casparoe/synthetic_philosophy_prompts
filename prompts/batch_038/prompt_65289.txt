Our agents get one scalar reward signal, and it struck me that this is basically what hedonists are supposed to be claiming about wellbeing — that everything good for a person boils down to a single quantity, pleasure. But then I tried to actually introspect and I couldn't find anything in common between the pleasure of a hot shower, the pleasure of a gnarly bug finally clicking into place, and the pleasure of laughing at a dumb pun with friends. They don't feel like the same thing scaled up or down.

Write me a dialogue between two hedonists who disagree about this. One thinks there really is a single felt quality running through every pleasure — a common tone or glow you could point at if you paid close enough attention. The other thinks pleasure isn't a feeling at all, its more like an attitude: wanting the experience you're having to keep going — so hunting for a shared sensation is a mistake from the start. Stage it as them arguing over whether a scalar reward is even the right model for pleasure, and let them use concrete cases (including ones they make up on the spot) to attack each other.

Both should be sharp, no strawmen, and they should respond to what the other one just said instead of taking turns reciting positions. Near the end I want each of them to say what would have to be true for their view to win, so I can tell what's actually at stake.
