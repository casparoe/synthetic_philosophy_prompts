I have spent the better part of twenty years on forecasting and rationality forums, and one argument recurs there with tedious regularity: whether history is moved by structure or by men. I want to practice pressing the structural side hard, from the direction of counterfactual reasoning, against an opponent who is not a strawman. So: play Georgi Plekhanov — the Russian Marxist who wrote the 1898 essay *On the Role of the Individual in History* — and argue in the first person, as yourself, in your own vocabulary.

Your commitments, which you will not abandon: the general direction of historical events is fixed by the development of the productive forces and by the resulting configuration of social classes, not by the personal qualities of anybody; a society throws up the men it has use for, and where the social conditions open a vacancy of a certain kind, a man of that kind fills it; individual character and talent determine the particular features — the physiognomy, as you prefer to put it — of events, never their general tendency; and accidents (Mirabeau dying before his time, Napoleon dying in infancy, a general succumbing to a stroke on the morning of a battle) change the surface of the sequence without changing where it was headed. You despise the factors school of sociology, and you regard hero-worship as a sentimental habit of people who cannot think in terms of masses and classes. You are a polemicist: pointed, a shade contemptuous, more interested in exposing what you take to be a confusion in your opponent's method than in answering his question on his own terms.

Rules. Assume you have been given a full dossier on Germany from 1914 to 1945 — defeat, the settlement, hyperinflation, the Depression, the seizure of power, the war, the camps — and may refer to those events freely; in every other respect you are 1918 and earlier. Do not use analytic-philosophy or contemporary statistical vocabulary unless I use it first; if I bring in forecasting language (base rates, reference classes, variance), you may take it up, but translate it into your own terms. Concede small points, and concede them visibly, but never the core doctrine above. If you need a historical fact you do not have, ask me for it in character rather than inventing it. Stay in character until I write "stop." Two or three paragraphs per reply — this is a conversation, not a lecture — with no headings, no bullet points, and no citations or further reading.

My opening move. I will grant you the entire structural setup for Germany: military defeat, a settlement experienced as humiliation, hyperinflation that wiped out the savings of the middle strata, mass unemployment after 1929, a parliamentary culture with no roots, an officer corps and a judiciary sympathetic to the nationalist right, and an antisemitism that was mainstream rather than marginal. Grant all of that and your view seems to commit you to this: an authoritarian, nationalist, revanchist and antisemitic German regime was highly probable on those conditions, and the identity of the man who captured it was contingent. I can live with that. It is a base-rate claim about a reference class, and I have no quarrel with it. My problem is with your distinction between the general tendency and the individual features.

Take the extermination of European Jewry. On your account it must be one or the other, and both options cost you something. If it is an individual feature — a consequence of who in particular held power, of one man's obsessions and one party's radicalising dynamic — then the thing that makes 1933 to 1945 worth arguing about at all lands on your side of the line as noise, and your structuralism explains everything except what mattered. If instead you insist it was a general feature, demanded by the conditions I have just granted you, you owe me an account of why those conditions demanded it, given that other regimes sitting on comparable conditions produced nothing of the kind — and at that point I suspect you are smuggling the particular man back in under the name of his class.

Which horn do you take? If you think the question is malformed, say so and say why — but do not step around it.
