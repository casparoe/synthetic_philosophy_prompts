I sit on an internal review board at an engineering firm, and we have been arguing about whether to attach named individual accountability to sign-offs whose downstream harms are foreseeable. A senior colleague closed the discussion by invoking the banality of evil: his position was that perpetrators never look like perpetrators, so screening for bad character is futile, and that blame properly belongs to the division of labor rather than to the people occupying its slots. The same colleague also deploys the phrase in the opposite direction, as a warning that each of us should scrutinize ourselves, since atrocity is carried out by ordinary people doing their jobs.

Please lay out the distinct claims that sentence can be making, as a menu rather than a single interpretation. For each reading: state it precisely; say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or false; and say whether it actually licenses dismissing individual accountability, licenses the self-examination use, or licenses neither. I especially want the following kept apart: the descriptive claim about the perpetrator's psychology (thoughtlessness, cliché-ridden speech, careerism, absence of sadism or of conviction); the metaphysical claim that evil has no depth and is not radical, as Arendt had earlier described it; the structural claim about bureaucracy, role fragmentation, and euphemistic language; the epistemic claim about our ability to recognize future perpetrators in advance; and the normative claim about where culpability lands. Flag whether any single reading can do both of my colleague's jobs at once, or whether he is equivocating.

Two further points. First, which of these readings would Arendt herself have rejected, given that she insisted Eichmann was answerable for what he had done? Second, which readings are hostage to the accuracy of her portrait of the man: historians working from the transcripts of the interviews Eichmann gave a Dutch ex-SS journalist in Buenos Aires in the 1950s have argued he was boastful and fanatically convinced rather than thoughtless. Tell me which readings collapse if that is right and which survive it intact.
