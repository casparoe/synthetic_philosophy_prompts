A national advisory panel on climate intervention has spent fourteen months deadlocked over whether to authorize the first phase of a stratospheric aerosol injection program, and its two most opposed members have agreed to submit a single joint statement instead of competing minority reports. One is a Precautionary Advocate, who holds that an activity must not proceed where there is a scientifically credible pathway to harm that cannot be undone within the lifetime of the decision, however low the probability assigned to that pathway. The other is a Symmetry Advocate, who holds that the risks of acting and the risks of not acting must be assessed on a single footing, and that a principle which systematically privileges inaction is not a principle of caution but a concealed preference for the present arrangement of things.

Take the following as stipulated by the panel's own modeling, accepted by both members for the purposes of the exercise and not to be relitigated in the statement: decarbonization alone will not hold warming below the threshold at which two regional monsoon systems are projected to fail; a program of sufficient scale to hold that line would, after roughly a decade of operation, be such that terminating it returns the masked warming within a handful of years rather than over the century it took to accumulate; a limited instrumented test would mask nothing but would, in the Precautionary Advocate's assessment, create institutional momentum making later full authorization close to automatic; and the populations most exposed to monsoon failure have asked through their representatives that the program not proceed, while the populations most exposed to test-related deposition have asked that it not be tested.

Write the joint statement, in this order:

1. **Agreed claims.** Numbered. Tag each as an agreement about a matter of fact, about a normative principle, or about the correct formulation of a question, since the panel needs to know which kind of consensus it has in each instance. Where a claim can be signed by both only under a stipulated meaning, state the stipulation inline rather than letting one sentence do double duty. Flag separately any claim on which the two agree in wording but for reasons each regards as incompatible with the other's, and record why each is nonetheless willing to sign it.

2. **A reversibility ledger.** For each candidate course — full authorization, a limited instrumented test, indefinite deferral with standing review, and accelerated decarbonization alone — the members' shared estimate of the horizon within which that course could still be abandoned without leaving the world materially worse than it would have been under the leading alternative, resolved into physical, institutional, and epistemic reversibility. Mark every cell as jointly signed, signed by one party under protest, or disputed. The disputed cells carry most of the information, so state for each one exactly what the two take themselves to be disagreeing about.

3. **A table of disagreements.** Each row to contain: the disagreement stated once in a formulation the Precautionary Advocate would accept as their own view and once in a formulation the Symmetry Advocate would accept as theirs; what each takes the other's position to imply that the other denies; the crux, being the sub-question whose settlement would actually move at least one of them; and, for each crux, the argument, observation, or case both accept as bearing on it, with each party's prediction written down in advance. At least one crux must concern the baseline against which harm is measured, given that the trajectory the panel is being asked to preserve is itself one of rapid and possibly catastrophic change. At least one must concern whether irreversibility attaches to beginning a process, to continuing it, or to ending it. At least one must concern whether the principle each is invoking functions as a decision rule, as an allocation of the burden of proof, or as a constraint on how a program must be designed in order to remain exit-capable. If you conclude that some portion of the fourteen-month dispute was about words rather than about the world, identify it, display the two readings side by side, and say what substantive residue survives their removal.

4. **Falsification commitments.** For each member: what they would have to learn, or observe in the record of comparable programs authorized in stages, in order to revise the general principle they brought to the panel and not merely their verdict on this one; and whether there exists anything both accept as capable of moving them. If one of the two cannot state such a condition without ceasing to hold the position that defines them, say so plainly — the panel is entitled to know which of its members is reasoning from a commitment that is not, even on their own account, hostage to evidence.

Keep the whole document in the register of something two adversaries would sign: no adjudication between them, no survey of how others have framed the question, no policy recommendation. Address it to the remaining panel members and to the minister who commissioned the review. Roughly 1,300 to 1,800 words.
