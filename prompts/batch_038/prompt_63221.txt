I teach an upper-level seminar in philosophy of science and philosophy of history: eighteen students, no geology background, no pre-reading, one seventy-five-minute session. I want a structured in-class simulation in which they adjudicate the start date of the Anthropocene and, without being told, discover that the answer tracks the decision criterion rather than the evidence.

So that you need not rely on recall, here are the materials I want in play: the Anthropocene Working Group's formal proposal centred on the sediments of Crawford Lake in Ontario, keyed to the bomb-test plutonium signal, with a start date around 1950; a competing proposal built on the dip in atmospheric carbon dioxide around 1610 recorded in Antarctic ice and attributed to post-contact depopulation and reforestation in the Americas; industrial-era soot and fly ash; early agricultural land use; the broiler-chicken bone as a marker of a reconfigured biosphere; and plastics, concrete and other "technofossils." In 2024 the responsible subcommission voted the epoch proposal down, chiefly because the sedimentary record is too shallow and the proposed start date too recent. Withhold this last fact until the debrief.

Please deliver: the printed materials (marker dossiers with their evidence profiles, and a set of candidate decision criteria — global synchronicity, permanence of the signal in the archive, detectability across independent archives, magnitude of departure from the Holocene baseline, clarity of causal attribution — distributed so that no group holds them all); roles, including at least one participant whose standing objection is that the term universalises responsibility among "anthropos" when the record is attributable to particular societies and institutions; explicit rules and a minute-by-minute timeline; and a voting procedure that enforces the constraint that a golden spike is a single point in a single section, so that averaging or splitting the difference is procedurally impossible. Then a debrief that maps specific moments in the session onto the criterion-relativity of periodisation, the question whether such a vote reports a fact or enacts a stipulation, and the question of who has standing to decide; a candid list of what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle each; and an honest note on which feature of the real controversy the simulation cannot reproduce and which I must supply myself.
