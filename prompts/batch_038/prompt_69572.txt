Our philosophy-of-science centre has secured funding for three researchers over three years, and the steering committee has agreed to spend it on the underdetermination of theory by evidence — the cluster of claims usually associated with Duhem and with Quine. My job is to turn that into an actual work programme, and I would rather not spend the first year discovering that we have selected a question whose answer makes no difference to anything else. What I want from you is a defended prioritisation of the open questions, not a survey of the literature and not a reading list.

Here is the candidate list as I have drafted it. Please treat it as provisional and tell me where I have framed something badly.

(a) *The existence question.* Are there documented historical cases of two theories that were genuinely empirically equivalent and mutually incompatible, in the strong sense the thesis requires? Or does the debate rest on artificially constructed rivals and on curve-fitting examples that no working scientist would call rivals at all? If no such cases exist, does the thesis lose its content, or does it survive as a modal claim about how the evidence might have looked?

(b) *The individuation question.* What makes two formulations count as distinct theories rather than notational variants of a single theory? The thesis seems to presuppose a criterion of theoretical identity that nobody has managed to supply. Is that criterion a precondition for stating the thesis, or can the thesis be made sense of without it?

(c) *Local versus global.* Does the claim that the whole of science is underdetermined by the totality of possible evidence have any consequences? Since the evidential base in that formulation already includes every possible observation, my suspicion is that it is true and inert. Argue me out of that if you can.

(d) *The Bayesian relocation worry.* Does a Bayesian treatment dissolve underdetermination through the priors, or merely shift it into the choice of priors and likelihoods? Is there anything more to be said about priors than that they are conventional?

(e) *The ad hoc question.* How much of the work in rescuing a threatened theory is actually done by auxiliary hypotheses, and is there a principled criterion for when a rescue is ad hoc, or is that ultimately a sociological fact about what a community tolerates?

(f) *The observational-content question.* Can the thesis be stated at all without some workable distinction between the observational and the theoretical parts of a theory, and if that distinction cannot be repaired, does the thesis become immune to challenge by stipulation?

(g) *The live-cases question.* Where underdetermination is invoked in politically charged model-based science — climate sensitivity, epidemiological projections, econometric identification — is it local and in principle resolvable by further data, or structural? What would have to be true of a modelling situation for the second answer to hold?

Now the constraints on the programme. One of the three researchers is a historian of physics with no formal background beyond first-order logic; the other two are formally trained. The agenda should be one that this particular combination can execute. Second, one member of the steering committee believes the entire topic is a verbal dispute dressed up as epistemology, and she is not going to be moved by the fact that eminent people have written about it. That yields the criterion I care about most: I want the questions that would still be worth three years even if she turned out to be right, and I want you to say explicitly which of the items above fail that test.

So: rank them, or a narrowed version of them, and defend the ordering. For each item you retain, tell me what depends on it — which of the others cannot be answered until it is settled — how tractable it is for a group of this composition, and what a credible first-year result would look like. Then tell me which to cut and why, and what you would add that I have not listed. Finally, specify what evidence or development over the next eighteen months would move items up or down your ranking.
