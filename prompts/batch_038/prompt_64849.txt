I run a tiny political-theory podcast — couple hundred regulars, no budget, I record in a closet — and next month's guest is a theorist who's spent a decade defending a pretty radical answer to the boundary problem, i.e. the question of who belongs in the demos. Her position, roughly: the franchise shouldn't be all-or-nothing citizenship but a continuous voting weight proportional to how much a given collective decision affects your interests; the demos is decision-relative, so it dissolves and reforms around each issue (a climate vote includes affected foreigners, a zoning vote includes commuters and sixteen-year-olds, a foreign-policy vote excludes both); and she thinks this dissolves the "who decides who decides" regress, because you don't need a prior demos to fix the boundary, you need a competent measurer — on her account a standing independent commission that models ex ante affectedness and certifies the electorate for each decision. She also argues the all-subjected principle is just a special case of hers, since subjection to law is one channel of affectedness among many.

Write me nine to eleven questions for her. Nothing generic — no "what drew you to this," no "how do you answer your critics." I want questions she can't get out of by restating the view, and I want at least one each on:

- how you're supposed to get a single scalar out of "affectedness" at all, given the baseline problem (affected compared to what?) and the fact that the estimate depends on the preferences of the people being measured;
- the regress, which I suspect shes relocated rather than removed — who constitutes the commission, and does the commission's own appointment count as a decision that affects people, in which case who gets weight there;
- endogeneity: if weight tracks affectedness, what stops people from becoming affected on purpose, or from bargaining today to shape the electorate for decisions down the line;
- a decision with hugely diffuse tiny effects versus one with concentrated catastrophic effects, where her arithmetic hands the win to the diffuse side;
- whether the franchise is an interest-tracking device at all rather than a matter of standing, and what she says to the resident non-citizen who gets full weight on policing and zero on everything else.

For each one give me a follow-up keyed to the answer she's actually most likely to give, not to a strawman — she's quick, and she has a canned move where she concedes the measurement is rough and then says any monotone proxy beats the binary franchise. Where a question needs a concrete case to bite, put the case in the question. Then finish by telling me which two she'll find hardest and why.
