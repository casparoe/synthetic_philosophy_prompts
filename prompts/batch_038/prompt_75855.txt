I'm a second-year PPE student on a module about technology, risk and public policy, and my tutor has let me run one session of our student-led ethics workshop: 75 minutes, roughly 24 people, mixed second- and third-years, no background in decision theory, and no budget beyond paper and tokens.

I want to build a game in which the group runs into the unilateralist's curse for themselves — the situation where many well-informed, morally serious people each hold private evidence about whether some powerful course of action is worth taking, each judges it honestly, and the action therefore goes ahead whenever any one of them judges it favourably; so that adding more careful actors makes the collective outcome worse rather than better. They should hit this by playing, not by being told.

Please design the activity in full: the rules, the information structure (exactly what each participant knows, what is hidden from them, and how the true value of the project is fixed), a payoff scheme using tokens or points rather than money, the number of rounds, and a minute-by-minute timeline that leaves room for the debrief. I'd like at least one round in which the asymmetry flips — where the hazard is that nobody acts and a real benefit is forfeited — so that they feel the cost of restraint as well as the cost of zeal. The name of the problem and any literature should be withheld until the debrief, where you should map particular moments of play onto the underlying structure: dispersed private information, correlated and independent errors, why the probability that someone acts rises with the number of actors, and what the usual remedies (a higher threshold for acting, deferral to a collective judgement) actually buy and what they cost.

Finally, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — where students will game the rules, where the numbers will feel arbitrary, where one confident participant will steamroller the rest — and how I should handle each; and be frank about which feature of the curse a classroom game cannot reproduce, so that I know what I have to supply by hand.

Please write your response in British English.
