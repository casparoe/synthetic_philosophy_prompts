I sit on the board of a small independent research institute, founded in 1911, and we have to decide whether to sign a fifty-year lease on a new building. Two trustees have approached the question from opposite directions, and I cannot tell which of them is reasoning better.

The first is confident. There are European universities founded in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, he points out, that are still teaching students today; scholarship belongs to the category of things that, once established, essentially do not die, and an institute that has already come through two world wars, a depression, and the loss and later recovery of its endowment plainly belongs to that category. The second thinks this is fantasy. The honest comparison, he says, is with the whole population of American independent institutes and small private colleges, a great many of which have closed, merged, or been absorbed into a larger institution within fifty years of their founding; on that footing he puts our chances of still existing in 2075 at something like a third.

What makes the disagreement hard to adjudicate is that both trustees insist they are reasoning in the same way — from what has happened to comparable institutions rather than from our own special circumstances — and both arrive at a number. Neither is merely flattering the institute.

Two further complications. First, none of the institutions in the second trustee's comparison group had taken on an obligation of the kind we are considering, and I genuinely do not know whether a fifty-year lease makes an institute likelier to persist, because it anchors it in one place, or likelier to fail, because it cannot retreat when its funding thins. Second, several board members suspect that the group one compares oneself to is settled by the conclusion one already wants, and that the arithmetic afterward is only decoration.

So: is there a principled way to determine which past institutions ours ought to be compared with, or are the skeptics right that the choice is arbitrary? Where does that leave the two numbers now on the table — are either of them usable, and if not, what would a usable estimate have to look like? And what should my memo actually recommend about the lease?
