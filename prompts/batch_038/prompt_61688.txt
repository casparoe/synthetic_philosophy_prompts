So here's a thing I've been sitting on for months and I don't have anyone to talk to about it. I work as a legal assistant at a mid-size firm and a huge chunk of my job is drafting those class action settlement notices that go out in the mail — you know the kind, *if you purchased X between such-and-such dates you may be entitled to cash*. A while back I fell down a rabbit hole on the nonidentity problem (Parfit's version, where a policy can be terrible but nobody is actually harmed by it because the people who end up existing wouldn't have existed otherwise, and their lives are worth living) and now I can't read one of these notices without twitching, because half the boilerplate stops making sense the second the class members' *existence* depends on the lawsuit getting settled. The opt-out is the part that really gets me: you can't opt out of a settlement that determines whether there's anyone around to opt.

Could you write me a parody court-approved settlement notice — the whole thing, standard headings and an FAQ at the end — for a made-up class action brought on behalf of future people against some company whose conduct changes who gets concieved? Around 700-800 words, deadpan legalese, and please no winking: I want it to read like something that actually came off a printer and got mailed to households. Invent the case name, the court, the defendant and the product; don't use any real case, real judge or real person, and don't cite anything. The funny has to come from the philosophy and the legal machinery being right, not from being wacky, so hit the eligibility definition, the claim form, a deadline that falls before anyone in the class is born, opt-out instructions, objector rights, the fairness hearing date, the release language, plaintiff's counsel's fee request, what happens to the leftover settlement money, and an expert declaration concluding that no class member was made worse off. Someone who works in this area should be able to see that the piece knows the difference between same-number and different-number choices, knows what work "a life worth living" is doing in the argument, and knows there's a wider person-affecting way of putting the objection — but my cousin should also be able to read it and laugh without googling a thing.

Then tack on one short paragraph after the notice where you step out of character: tell me where you think the piece exaggerates or leans on a contested reading (including whether real class practice would actually break down the way I'm claiming, since I might be over-reading my own job), which objection to the nonidentity conclusion you find most convincing, and what would change your mind — what argument or consideration would push you from "nobody is harmed" to "the policy is still wrong, and here is who gets to say so."
