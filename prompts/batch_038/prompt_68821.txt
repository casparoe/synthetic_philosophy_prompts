I'm in the middle of a novel set during the year my city takes down two statues and renames four streets, and the research has pushed me into an actual position I want stress-tested before I build the book's argument on it. Here's the position: monuments and street names don't assert anything. They aren't claims about the person they honor that could be true or false, and they aren't really speech at all — they're a standing, pre-propositional thank-you built into public space, a default of gratitude and deference that people absorb by walking past rather than by reading. On this view the folks who say a statue is a lie about a slaveholder are making a category mistake, and so are the ones who say taking it down censors a claim, since nothing was claimed. Removal just withdraws the default; it rearranges the furniture of attention, not the record.
