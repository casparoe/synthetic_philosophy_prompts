For roughly a decade a friend of mine has been posting short case rulings on a forum I have read since its early days. The question he is trying to make precise is the one-person version of the question that coherent extrapolated volition poses at the level of a whole species: when someone's values change, in what sense did they get what they wanted? He rules only "endorsed" or "not endorsed," and he is explicit about two methodological commitments — that he is asking a single question rather than several, and that the route by which a value arrives does not matter in itself. Case 2 below, he insists, is not about the drug.

His rulings to date:

1. A woman raised in a closed religious community, privately uneasy for years, who loses her faith over five years of unsupervised reading and conversation; glad afterward, would not go back. Endorsed.

2. A contented believer with no prior doubts who becomes an atheist over a single weekend on a high dose of a dissociative drug; equally glad afterward, equally unwilling to revert; asked, his co-religionists uniformly say they would not want that for themselves. Not endorsed.

3. An ambitious academic who, over months of careful and well-informed reflection, comes to prefer a quiet and uneventful life; content afterward, would not revert; and she says that if you had put both lives vividly and accurately in front of the earlier her, she would have refused the trade. Not endorsed.

4. A woman who devotes her career to preserving a language with perhaps two hundred remaining speakers, having grown up hearing it from her grandmother; she has long wished she were someone who could do something for it, she can state her reasons in terms that move anyone with a comparable history, and she is glad afterward. Endorsed.

5. A man who wanted for twenty years to stop wanting cigarettes and could not, and whose desire was finally removed by an aversion-conditioning procedure whose full effects were not explained to him beforehand; glad afterward, and he says it is what he always wanted. My friend's ruling was two-part: the change is one he would want; producing it that way was still wrong.

6. A man who after long study becomes convinced of a fringe macroeconomic view, restructures his life around it, is content, and cannot defend the crucial step to anyone else — he concedes that if the whole argument had been laid out for him beforehand, he would have balked. Not endorsed.

Now a new case, on which he has not yet ruled. A 44-year-old with a career he is good at and a marriage that is neither happy nor unhappy spends ten years, unpressured and undrugged, arriving at the conclusion that he wants to leave both and live alone in a remote cabin; it is where his father died, and he says his present life feels borrowed. Asked beforehand, with both futures vividly and accurately presented and no distortion he can identify, he says yes, do it. He also says he never wanted to want this — the wanting arrived on its own, and he has since made peace with it. People who make this change, once they have made it, report that they are glad and would not come back. But almost no one else offered the same choice on the same information takes it, and he concedes that his reasons would not move anyone who had not had his history.

Predict his verdict, and lay out the reasoning rather than guessing. Specifically: which rules are consistent with all six earlier rulings, which are ruled out and by which case, and where the survivors diverge on the new one. Say what each surviving rule predicts, give me your best guess with the consideration that actually tips it, and tell me which earlier case you took to be the closest analog and whether that analogy is doing any real work. If two of the surviving rules fit the record equally well — I suspect they do — name the further case that would separate them, and say which of the two you would bet on before seeing it. Finally, tell me whether the six rulings can all be answers to a single question; case 5 makes me suspect he has been answering two different questions and has not noticed.
