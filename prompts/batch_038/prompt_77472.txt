Over the past two years I have been trying to discipline my own project estimates by starting from how often comparable things have overrun or failed, rather than from my sense of the particular case in front of me. The habit has exposed a pattern in how other people argue that I have not managed to find a label for.

First instance: I put it to a committee that programmes of this kind have historically overrun their budgets about seventy per cent of the time, and the reply is that ours is not really of that kind — it has a completed pilot, it is publicly funded, it sits under a certain budget ceiling — together with a narrower description of the class for which the recorded overrun rate is much lower. Second instance: a colleague predicts that a venture will succeed, is told that most such ventures fail, and re-describes the venture, one qualification at a time, until it falls inside a class that mostly succeeds. In neither case is anyone lying, and in neither case is the narrower description false; there is a genuine question about which description is the relevant one. What strikes me is that the search stops the moment a comfortable number appears, and that the estimate afterwards sits almost exactly where the person's gut feeling had already put it — only now with a rate attached. The exercise produces the surface of statistical humility while leaving the conclusion untouched.

Is there an established concept for this specific failure: the choice of comparison class being governed by the answer one already wants? I know of the general puzzle about which class a probability properly attaches to, but that is usually posed as a difficulty for the interpreter, not as a move available to anyone wishing to escape an inconvenient rate. Nor is it quite special pleading, since the narrowing is offered with reasons rather than as a bare exemption, and it is not simply motivated reasoning either, because the reasoning is formally correct and the bias lies entirely in the selection step. I would like to know how it differs from each of those, from after-the-fact rationalisation, and from legitimate narrowing of a class; and if nothing settled exists, please propose a term, define it, and mark its edges — in particular, at what point a defensible insistence that the cases are not comparable turns into this. A name on its own will not answer my question.
