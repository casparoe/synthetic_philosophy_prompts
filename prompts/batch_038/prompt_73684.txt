**Draft: "No Draft to Revise: A Third Option on the Timing of Experience"**

**1. The datum.** Take the color phi situation studied by Kolers and von Grünau in the mid-1970s, reportedly at the instigation of a question from Nelson Goodman. A dot of one color is presented at one location; shortly afterward, and at an appropriate spatial and temporal interval, a dot of a different color is presented at a second location. Subjects report a single dot traveling between the two positions and changing color abruptly somewhere around the middle of the illusory path. The puzzle is informational: whatever settles where along the path the change occurs cannot be available until after the second dot has been presented, yet the change is experienced as having already happened mid-path.

**2. Two familiar answers.** Dennett and Kinsbourne, in their 1992 Behavioral and Brain Sciences target article, framed the space of responses as a choice between an Orwellian and a Stalinesque story. On the Orwellian story, the subject genuinely has an experience as the first dot is presented, and when the second dot arrives the record of that experience is rewritten; the subject is an honest but unreliable historian of their own mind. On the Stalinesque story, the brain composes the content — including the mid-path color change — before the subject becomes aware of anything at all, so there is nothing to overwrite and the eventual report is a faithful read-out. Dennett and Kinsbourne favored the second, and folded it into the multiple drafts picture on which there is no single privileged moment at which consciousness "happens."

**3. What both answers share.** Orwellian and Stalinesque accounts disagree about *when* the content of the experience becomes determinate. Both agree that there is such a time. I want to separate two questions that this framing runs together:

- (Q1) At what time does the content become determinate?
- (Q2) Is there any time at which it is determinate?

Orwellian and Stalinesque accounts give rival answers to Q1. My proposal is to answer Q2 in the negative for at least some features of some experiences. I will call this the *unfixed-content* option. It is not the Stalinesque option with the fixing pushed indefinitely later, and it is not a claim about memory reliability. It is a claim that, for the color phi subject, the question "was the change experienced as occurring at the midpoint before the second dot appeared?" has no answer, in the way that "where exactly did the heap stop being a heap?" has no answer once the counting is fixed but before anyone asks.

**4. What the option costs and buys.** It costs an explanation of the striking cross-subject stability of phi reports, and it owes an account of what makes a report about one's own experience true at all, if the content reported was not settled at the time of the reporting. It buys the following: no rewriting mechanism is needed, no theatre is needed, and no appeal is required to a special epistemic privilege attaching to the moment of report.

**5. How it could be tested.** I want to commit the view to something. I propose what I will call the *probe-sensitivity signature*: if the content were determinate before the subject is asked, then a probe should return a noisy reading of a fixed value — variability that averages out toward a stable mean. If the content is unfixed, a probe should return readings that covary *systematically* with the parameters of the probe itself: its temporal resolution demands, the size of its response set, and the attentional resources available when it is delivered. Variance is not the mark of the unfixed-content option; systematic covariation is.

**6. An experiment.** Standard color phi, with the dependent variable being the reported *location* of the color transition along the inter-dot path. On each trial the subject indicates the location by touching a point on a blank reproduction of the path after the motion sequence. Four conditions are crossed: (a) the transition-location question is cued either by a probe flashed on the path during the sequence or only after the trial ends; (b) the response set is coarse (two halves of the path) or fine (eight positions); (c) a concurrent auditory discrimination task either occupies or does not occupy resources during the interval; (d) the color pair is either rapidly discriminable or slowly discriminable. My prediction: the indicated transition point migrates systematically toward the second dot as probe demands increase, and the during-probe and after-probe conditions dissociate in a direction and magnitude not attributable to noise. On the Stalinesque account, content is composed by the time of report, so during-probe and after-probe should agree up to noise. On the Orwellian account, editing likewise produces agreement, with the during-probe reports biased toward the first dot.

**7. The harder case: Libet.** In Libet's work with neurosurgical patients, awareness of a cutaneous or directly evoked cortical stimulus appeared to require sustained cortical activity lasting on the order of half a second, yet subjects dated the onset of the felt sensation to the time of the first pulse rather than to the end of the required activity — the so-called backward referral. This looks worse for me than color phi does, because referral seems to require a determinate date to which something is referred. My response: the referral is not the brain stamping a record with an earlier time. The date becomes determinate only when the subject is asked, and the form of the question — "when did it begin?" — is itself the probe that fixes it. Backward referral is a property of the probe, not of the record.

**8. Conclusion.** The Orwellian/Stalinesque dichotomy is presented as exhaustive, but it is exhaustive only given the fixation assumption. Dropping that assumption yields a third position that is neither skeptical about memory nor committed to pre-composed content.

**What I would like comments on.**

1. **Distinctness.** The single most important question: is the unfixed-content option genuinely a third position, or is it a redescription of what Dennett and Kinsbourne already said under the multiple drafts model? If it is a redescription, please identify precisely which sentence in sections 3 or 5 fails to add anything they did not already have. If it is genuinely distinct, please say what work the distinction is doing that their account cannot do.

2. **The argument in section 3.** I move from the observation that both rival accounts answer Q1 to the claim that they therefore share a commitment to Q2's having an answer. Does that inference go through? A referee has suggested in passing that a Stalinesque account could deny Q2 as well while still making sense of composition, and I could not tell whether this was a real possibility or a confusion.

3. **Section 5's evidential principle.** I claim that a determinate content would yield noise around a stable mean while unfixed content would yield systematic covariation with probe parameters. Please press on this. In particular: could a fully determinate content nevertheless produce systematic covariation with probe parameters, for reasons having nothing to do to with determinacy? If so, my signature is not diagnostic, and I want to know which of the two views — determinate-content-with-probe-artifacts or unfixed-content — is the more parsimonious explanation of such a pattern.

4. **Section 6's design.** Tell me whether the proposed experiment would discriminate the three accounts, and whether any of the four manipulations is redundant, confounded, or incapable of being interpreted. I am especially unsure about condition (d), since slow discriminability may just increase latency rather than change what is fixed.

5. **Section 7's handling of Libet.** Is my response to the backward-referral material coherent? Note a worry I have not resolved: I elsewhere insist that the subject's report is the only evidence available about the experience, yet in section 7 I treat the report's content as an artifact of how the question was framed. Is that a straightforward inconsistency, or can the two be held together?

6. **Accuracy.** Flag any place where I have misdescribed the color phi data, the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction, or the Libet material. I care more about misdescription of the positions than about imprecision in dates and publication details.

7. **Structural.** The draft is intended to run to about four thousand words. Tell me which section is doing the least work and should be cut, and whether section 7 belongs in the paper at all or should be deferred to a reply to an anticipated objection.

Please be blunt and specific. A comment of the form "section 5's principle is too strong, because such-and-such" is far more useful to me than a general assessment of the project's merits.
