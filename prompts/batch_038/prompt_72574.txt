I chair a five-member departmental committee that must decide whether a doctoral candidate's central argument is sound enough for the dissertation to be sent out to external readers. A colleague has proposed that we enlarge the committee to twenty-five, drawn from across the faculty, and that members refrain from discussing the case with one another before voting — on the grounds that a large, mutually uninfluenced majority is mathematically guaranteed to land on the right answer, whereas discussion merely lets the most forceful speaker contaminate everyone else's judgment. He adds that, on this reasoning, there is no need to seat anyone who actually works in the candidate's area. I sense that something in his argument is right and something badly wrong, but I have not managed to say what. Where does the reasoning hold up, where does it break down, and what should I propose to the committee?
