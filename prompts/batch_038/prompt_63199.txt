So I volunteer on the board of a small arts nonprofit (about 40 employees, mid-size city, we meet over Zoom mostly) and last month I chaired the five-person search committee for a new program director. Our bylaws — which are basically a template somebody downloaded years ago and nobody has ever read closely — spell out how the committee's recommendation works, and its weirdly mechanical: we vote separately by majority on three questions, (1) does the candidate have the field experience the job calls for, (2) does her portfolio show she's a good mission fit, and (3) did she come across well in the structured interviews — and the recommendation is "recommend" only if all three pass. Fine, whatever, we did it.

The votes came out 3–2 on all three questions, but not the same 3–2. On experience it was me, Priya and Yolanda yes; on mission fit it was me, Dan and Marcus; on interviews it was Priya, Yolanda and Dan. So every criterion cleared, which means per the rule we recommended her, and I signed the letter that went up to the full board, and they made an offer, and she starts in March. But afterward, mostly as a gut check, I went around the table and asked each of us straight up "so, would you have recommended her?" and every single one of us said no. Not one of the five would have voted to hire her if that had been the question actually on the table. Marcus has been kind of rattled by it — he says the committee decided something none of us think, and then he also points out he personally voted against two of the three criteria, so his own hands are clean if it goes south in a couple of years. Priya says that's all nonsense, a committee is a committee, the rule was followed, it's our decision, we own it, and the blame lands on all five of us equally. Dan doesn't see that there's anything to explain: he voted the way he voted on each question, the bylaws did the rest, and at no point did "the committee" ever decide anything at all. I'm the one who signed the letter, so I don't find Dan's version especially comforting.

Here's what I want: your actual verdict, not a menu of options. Is this recommendation ours in any real sense — did the committee decide to hire her, or did five separate people each decide something else and a rule manufacture an outcome nobody endorsed? Given whatever you say about that, what should I do now, with the offer already out and me having chaired the thing? Tell me which of Marcus, Priya and Dan is closest to right, and whether I should be pushing to rewrite the bylaws before the next hire or whether that's just rearranging deck chairs. Keep it under 350 words, bottom line in the first sentence, and tell me how confident you are in the verdict since I'm probably going to act on it. And please be straight with me: if you think my framing is muddled, or that the honest answer is "this is a bookkeeping problem, stop hunting for a deep one," say that plainly instead of giving me a diplomatic version. Same goes if one of my assumptions doesn't actually hold up — I'd rather be corrected than agreed with.
