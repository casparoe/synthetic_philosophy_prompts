At a meetup last week a guy who does neural-net work made this pitch, more or less: von Neumann already settled the brain-is-a-computer question in the little book he was writing when he died, The Computer and the Brain. His argument, per this guy, was component by component -- the neuron is the switching element, the all-or-none spike is the 1/0, the synapse is the memory cell, the axon is the wire -- and every difference he turned up between brains and machines came out quantitative rather than in principle. Neurons are slower, but there are vastly more of them working in parallel; each one is less precise, but the system swamps that with redundancy and error correction; the machine keeps memory in a seperate store while the brain keeps it distributed in the cells themselves, which is an engineering tradeoff, not a different kind of thing. So on this telling von Neumann's own bottom line was that nothing is left over: emulation is a matter of hitting the right precision at the right scale, and anyone still worried is a vitalist in a lab coat. He then added a second step -- since the correspondence is complete and exhaustive, adding or rearranging components can't add or remove experience, so the whole consciousness question drops out.

I half bought it and half didn't, and I can't tell which half. Can you build the correspondence table properly for me? Which pairings did von Neumann actually draw, which are imports from later computer science that he never made, and for each one which side of the pairing the analogy quietly favors -- i.e. what features of the machine get smuggled into the brain just because the table looks symmetric. Then the places it breaks: I've heard he thought the nervous system was partly analog, partly asynchronous rather than clock-driven, and that whatever code it uses is structurally unlike the notation systems of mathematics. Does he really say those things, and if so how much of the table do they take out -- all of it, or just the parts the pitch leaned on? And separately, is the second step (complete correspondence, therefore no further question about experience) something the analogy could ever have supported even if the first step were airtight?

Finally, if the von Neumann architecture is the wrong source domain for the brain, what would be a better one, and what work could that analogy honestly carry?
