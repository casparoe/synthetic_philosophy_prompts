I run a small philosophy podcast, and next week's guest defends epiphenomenalism about qualia in a specific form: brain states cause her experiences, her experiences cause nothing at all, and she answers the paradox of phenomenal judgment by holding that acquaintance rather than causation is what makes her first-person reports about their objects — reliability, on her picture, comes free from the common cause that produces both the experience and the judgment. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press the actual joints of that position instead of asking her to restate it. I want at least one question on why acquaintance rather than the causing brain state fixes what her reports are about, one on the fact that her zombie duplicate would judge, report, and argue exactly as she does, one on whether she is prepared to concede that her introspective judgments are insensitive and unsafe and still call them knowledge, and one on what could distinguish her view from interactionism short of stipulation — and for each of those, a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Close with a question asking her to name a piece of evidence that would make her abandon epiphenomenalism rather than reinterpret it.
