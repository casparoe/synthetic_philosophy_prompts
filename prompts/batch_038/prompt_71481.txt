I write about science and ideas for a weekly review, and I am scripting an episode in our reconstructed-interview strand, in which a long-dead thinker is questioned strictly on what he actually wrote. Next week's guest is G. E. Moore, on the chapter of *Principia Ethica* called "The Ideal": the claim that the greatest intrinsic goods are the aesthetic enjoyment of beautiful objects and personal affection; the insistence that such enjoyment carries a cognitive element, an awareness of the object's real qualities, without which the emotion is worth little; and the principle of organic unities, on which "the intrinsic value of a whole is neither identical with nor proportional to the sum of the values of its parts".

I want questions that press the argument rather than invite a summary, and nothing generic. Please write in British English, and organise the answer in these sections, in this order:

1. **The guest in brief** (about 120 words): what that chapter actually commits Moore to, so that I do not misdescribe him on air.
2. **Main sequence** (eight questions, each 40 to 60 words). At least one must go to whether affection, valued only as the appreciation of lovable qualities, makes friends interchangeable and turns loyalty to a diminished or unlovely person into a mistake; one to the person deeply moved by a forgery, or by a sunset he describes wrongly; one to whether organic unities explains anything or merely licenses whatever ranking Moore fancies; one to his method of comparing goods in complete isolation, and to his habit of assuming that we all agree; and one to an ideal that says nothing about *who* enjoys the goods, and nothing to the young admirers who made it a creed of art and friendship while others went hungry. After each question, add **Pressure point:** in one line, and **If he replies…** with a follow-up keyed to the answer he is likeliest to give.
3. **Dodges and counters** (about 150 words): three evasions available to him, and the sentence that closes each off.
4. **Closer** (one question, about 40 words): putting to him the concession in his later preface that his non-naturalism might survive a moral definition of good, and asking what that costs the chapter.

No references or bibliography, please; I will check the text myself.
