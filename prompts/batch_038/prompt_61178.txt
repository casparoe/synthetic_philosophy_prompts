The room: a stochastic thermodynamics group seminar, maybe 25 people. Mostly folks who work on fluctuation theorems and feedback-controlled small systems; two of them have written on the generalized second law with mutual information; one experimentalist who's measured single-bit erasure costs on a colloidal particle in an optical trap; and a visiting philosopher of physics who does foundations of stat mech. They're polite, but they will not let conceptual hand-waving slide, and at least half of them think the demon question was nailed down decades ago and find it slightly tiresome to reopen.

My thesis: Landauer's principle doesn't exorcise Maxwell's demon, it relocates him. Argument sketch — (1) the erasure-based resolution only bites if the demon actually resets its memory, so it's really a claim about cyclic demons; (2) a demon whose memory just keeps growing never erases anything and so never pays the kT ln 2, it defers, and deferring isn't paying; (3) to close the books on the deferred case you have to say where the blank bits came from, and a standing supply of blank bits is itself a low-entropy resource, so the cost migrates out of a mechanism and into a boundary condition; (4) hence the demon is only exorcised once you've already granted something shaped like the low-entropy assumption you set out to explain away.

What I want is a prep sheet for the question period, not a defense of the view. Give me the seven or eight objections this particular audience would actually raise, ranked by how much damage they do, each in the register of whoever's raising it — the fluctuation-theorem people, the experimentalist and the philosopher are not going to phrase things the same way — with a two-minute reply and a blunt note on where the reply is soft, where it quietly concedes more than I'd like, and whether the objection is fatal or merely embarassing. Tell me what to give away in the first five minutes so the hour isn't spent relitigating it, and what I have to hold onto for the talk to be worth giving at all. And include the question I'm dreading: the one suggesting my position is formally indistinguishable from just renaming the memory's entropy and calling it a bill that comes due later.
