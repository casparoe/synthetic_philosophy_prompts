My flatmate is deep into honest-signalling theory and I've been keeping a list of her verdicts. Peacock's train: an honest signal, and honest precisely because it's expensive to carry. Red deer roaring in a contest: honest, but she says no stag is choosing to be honest — his body won't let him fake the rate. House sparrow's black bib: honest only in a crowd, since a cheat gets beaten up by rivals it'll meet again. Bowerbird's bower: honest, cost tracks the builder. A pure Fisherian ornament that nobody pays anything for: she calls it arbitrary — it can still be prefered, but it says nothing about the bearer. Nestling begging calls: honest, but only because the cost lands on the whole brood.

Here's the case I've built to stump her. A lizard where every male pays exactly the same fixed bill for his dewlap — same joules, same pigment, and nothing mechanical stops a scrawny male from paying it. But a scrawny male's whole budget is a fifth the size, so the identical bill eats a much bigger slice of what he's got. And they're solitary: two males meet once, bluff or fight, and never see each other again, so there's nobody around to punish a faker afterwards.

What does she say — honest or not? I want the rule she's actually running, the other rules that fit her whole list just as well but give a different answer here, and the one further case that would finally tell them apart.
