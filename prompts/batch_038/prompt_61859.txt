The context is this. I am revising my graduate seminar on mental causation and want to state one point precisely before asserting it in lecture. My reconstruction of the dialectic runs as follows. Nonreductive physicalists hold that mental properties are realized by, and therefore determined by, physical properties. But realization just is a determination relation, and determination is causal: the realizer brings it about that the realized property is instantiated. It follows that a realized mental property stands to its base as an effect to its cause, and an effect cannot cause anything on account of what already causes it — that would be causation running back down the realization relation. On this reading, the exclusion problem is fundamentally a problem about the direction of causation, and the premise ruling out systematic overdetermination is doing no substantive work: it is a gloss on the impossibility of a mental property causing what its own realizer causes.
