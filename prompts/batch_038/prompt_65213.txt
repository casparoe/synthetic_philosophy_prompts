I work in policy analysis for a local authority in the north of England, and our staff reading group has just finished a much-praised trade book about the erosion of shared facts. One member, who read philosophy as an undergraduate, said the chapter on the American theories of truth was "basically right, only compressed", and I have been arguing with him about it ever since; I read James's lectures when I was eighteen and retain an affection for him, so I cannot tell whether my suspicion that the chapter is unfair to James and correspondingly generous to Peirce is judgement or nostalgia. The passage in question runs as follows: "Peirce and James agreed that the meaning of a belief lies in its practical consequences, but they split over what to do with truth itself. Peirce, the more rigorous of the two, defined a true belief as one that investigators would converge on if inquiry were carried far enough, which makes truth on his account a matter of what the scientific community eventually settles on rather than of how things are. James abandoned even that constraint: for him a belief was true just to the extent that holding it paid off for the person holding it, which is why he was quite content to say that belief in God is true if it makes your life go better. Peirce was appalled, and coined the deliberately unattractive name pragmaticism for his own doctrine so that it could not be confused with James's; the disagreement between the two came down in the end to temperament rather than substance, the logical Peirce against the psychological James. The decisive objection arrived almost immediately from critics such as Bertrand Russell, who pointed out that plenty of false beliefs are useful and plenty of true ones ruinous, and pragmatism has never really recovered. Since the theory makes truth a function of what works for us, it is hardly surprising that the intellectual culture which absorbed it now struggles to say why a comforting falsehood should be resisted."

Could you audit this passage for me, sentence by sentence?
