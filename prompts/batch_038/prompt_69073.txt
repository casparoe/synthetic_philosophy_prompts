People always run Williams's integrity objection (Jim and the Indians, George the chemist) together with the one-thought-too-many point from the drowning spouse case, as if it were the same complaint twice. Compare them properly. Here's the test I care about: imagine a George who is genuinely delighted to take the weapons job and feels zero conflict about it, and a husband who saves his wife while cheerfully reporting that he'd weighed up the stranger's greater usefulness first and only then noticed the current made the stranger unreachable. Does each of Williams's two complaints still land on these agents, or does it need the inner fricton to bite at all? Which of the two is really a claim about the agent's psychology and which is a claim about the structure of utilitarian justification — and does that split them apart cleanly, or does one turn out to be a special case of the other?

End with a last line starting "Verdict:" that says whether these are one objection or two, and which of them (if either) is the more fundamental.
