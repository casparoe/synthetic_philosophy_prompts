I am a secondary-school music teacher with no academic training in philosophy, but I have kept a dream journal every morning for about six years and, for the last three of them, practised lucid dreaming — the reality checks through the day, the intention-setting at bedtime, the whole routine. A few of us meet fortnightly at the local library to argue about whatever someone has been reading, and next month it is my turn to set out what I actually believe about dreams so that the others can attack it. One of them warned me beforehand that there is a serious argument, apparently from a philosopher, to the effect that dreams are not experiences at all, which I dismissed on the spot as obviously absurd; but I have since realised that I cannot say precisely which of my own beliefs makes it absurd. So I would like an honest audit of the following set before I present it, rather than a defence of it.

Here is the package, as far as I can state it cleanly. (1) Dreaming is something that happens to me while I am asleep: an episode with a beginning and an end, during which there is something it is like to be me. (2) My journal entry records an episode that is already over; the report is evidence about the dream rather than what makes it the dream it was, so a dream I forget utterly still happened, and there is a fact of the matter about it that I have simply lost access to. (3) I cannot be wrong about what my dream was like — if it seems to me on waking that I was being chased through a multi-storey car park, then I was, and nobody could correct me: not my husband, who was lying beside me and watched me sleep, and not some scan of my brain that showed me dreaming of a beach instead. (4) In a lucid dream I make genuine choices — to test whether I am dreaming, to fly, to stay in rather than wake — and I have found that running through a difficult guitar passage in a lucid dream leaves me measurably better at it in the morning, which I take to show that something was actually done in the night, not merely narrated afterwards. (5) Dream time is squashed: an episode that feels as though it ran for the best part of an hour may occupy only a few minutes of sleep, and I do not think the felt length has to match the clock length. (6) A dream is not a perception of anything; what I seem to see, hear and touch in it is manufactured rather than detected. (7) All the same, things from the room sometimes get folded in — the alarm becomes a fire engine, a draught becomes a winter street. (8) Deep sleep is genuinely blank: there are stretches of the night in which I have no experience at all, not a dim one or a fleeting one, none. (9) And sometimes I wake with a powerful conviction that I have just been dreaming and no content whatever — a mood, or a single image that dissolves the moment I reach for it — which I treat as evidence that there was a definite dream, even though I could not tell you anything about it.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, ranked from the most serious downwards rather than presented as a flat list of complaints. For each real tension, say what would have to give, what the cheapest revision is, and what that revision would cost me in practice — I should tell you that I find (3) the most obviously true item on the list and (2) the one I would most hate to lose, since the entire journal habit assumes it, but I am not committed to preserving either at all costs. Equally important, please identify the tensions that only look like tensions and explain why they dissolve, because I suspect I am conflating at least a couple of things that a careful person would keep apart — in particular, whether there is a difference between being right that something happened and being right about what it was like, and whether that difference does any work for me. Finally, tell me whether my dismissive reaction to that argument about dreams not being experiences was justified given this package, or whether the argument is in fact aimed at exactly the commitments I hold, and which single revision would tidy up the most damage at once. Please write your answer in British English.
