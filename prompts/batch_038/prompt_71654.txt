ok so i want a parody piece — the full script for one of those mandatory annual compliance e-learning modules that every employee has to click through, except it's from a made-up public agency that has codified agent-centered moral constraints into enforceable workplace rules. call the agency whatever you like. roughly 800–900 words, laid out like a real module: title slide, learning objectives, several content slides with the narrator's voiceover written out, one scenario walkthrough with a branching "what would you do?" click, three knowledge-check questions with deadpan explanations of why each distractor is wrong, a "common misconceptions" callout box, and the certificate blurb at the end.

the author of the module is completely sincere — no winking at the reader, no asides. and the humor has to come from the ethics being right rather than from bureaucrats being dumb, so a normative ethicist laughs instead of winces. which means working in the actual machinery: the doing/allowing distinction; agent-relativity, including the fact that constraints aren't values to be promoted so the agency definately doesn't track a total-harm metric and is quite proud of that; double effect with the intended vs merely-foreseen line and its proportionality condition; whether the constraint has a threshold and what an employee is supposed to do at it; the de minimis problem; and above all the paradox of deontology itself — the module has to teach staff that they may not commit one violation in order to prevent five others, and it has to contain the drafter's straight-faced answer to the employee who asks why not, ideally in the misconceptions box.

constraints: no trolleys, no footbridges, no transplant surgeons — the scenario should be a boring realistic institutional one (inspector, dispatcher, procurement officer, ward clerk, whatever you pick). don't name or quote any real philosopher and don't include any references, further reading, or citation-shaped stuff; invent everything. don't let the module lapse into consequentialist reasoning anywhere, including in the answer explanations — the internal consistency is part of the bit, so wrong answers should be wrong for the agency's own reasons and not because they're stupid.

last thing: keep it fair rather than a dunk, and remember the target is a position and a practice, not a person. at least one knowledge-check item or the branching scenario should land so that a reader who's been smirking feels the pull of the other side — the module should get to state the strongest version of its own view, in its own voice, before the last slide.
