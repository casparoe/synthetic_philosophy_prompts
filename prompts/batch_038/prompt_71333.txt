I'm running an upper-level seminar that's half philosophy grad students and half econ undergrads — 18 people, 75 minutes, everyone's had intro stats but nobody's had formal epistemology. I want to build an in-class game around Aumann's agreeing-to-disagree result, but not the toy version where I state the theorem and then illustrate it; I want them to run into the failure themselves, before anyone says the phrase common prior out loud.

Here's what I have in mind, and what I need you to make actually workable. Two rounds of the same estimation task. In round 1 the only thing that differs between students is which signals they get to see, and a full disclosure protocol — everyone announces their probability, in order, over and over until nothing changes — should grind them into agreement. In round 2 the protocol looks identical from the inside but visibly stalls. The catch is that I don't want to just tell them they now have different priors, because the interesting part is that they cant tell whether the stall came from different priors, from different likelihoods, or from a finer/coarser partition of the same evidence. My hunch is you can build the materials so that no amount of post-hoc interrogation separates those three, which would either refute Harsanyi's doctrine in front of them or show it's unfalsifiable — tell me straight if that hunch is wrong, because I'd rather know before I design around it.

Give me: the exact estimation task and payoff scheme (grading credit or candy, no real money), what each info slip says in each round, a disclosure protocol that generates genuine common knowledge rather than just mutual knowledge — that distinction should bite mechanically, so build it into the rules — a timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief mapping each stall onto which hypothesis of the theorem broke. Then: what predictably goes wrong the first time you run it (I'm mostly worried about arithmetic errors and one confident student steamrolling the announcements), and which feature of the result the game just can't reproduce, so I know what I have to supply by hand.
