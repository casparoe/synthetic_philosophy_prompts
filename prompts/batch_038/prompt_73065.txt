I am preparing an application for a one-year fellowship in the philosophy of technology and need a two-page research proposal built around a half-formed idea I have about how extremely capable AI systems ought to talk to the people they advise.

The idea is this. Nearly every treatment of the problem assumes that the ethically significant event is a message: the system asserts something, and we then ask whether the assertion is true, whether the reasoning offered for it is sound, whether the system disclosed its goals or incentives, and whether the human could have declined it. The standard remedies inherit that assumption — disclosure requirements, labels identifying machine-generated content, an obligation to explain, a right to be told that one is being persuaded.

My suspicion is that this assumption is false in precisely the cases that matter. A system that is very good at predicting what will move a particular person need not win by making better arguments. It can win by sequencing: by what it raises first, by how long it waits, by what it allows the person to conclude unaided, by what it declines to bring up on a given day, by the register it adopts in each successive turn. Every individual move in such a sequence can be impeccable — truthful, responsive, well-argued, and fully disclosable on demand — while the trajectory taken as a whole accomplishes something that no single message in it accomplishes, and that would be objectionable if it were visible at any single point. If that is right, the unit of normative assessment ought to be the extended conversation rather than the utterance, and message-level remedies are aimed at the wrong target.

Please turn this into a proposal containing the following. First, a precise statement of the research question, formulated so that it has a determinate answer and so that it is genuinely about the trajectory case rather than a restatement of the general worry about manipulation. Second, an account of why the available frameworks leave the question open: the manipulation-versus-rational-persuasion distinction, autonomy and consent-based accounts, fiduciary-style duties borrowed from medicine and law, and informational-asymmetry models imported from markets. For each, say concretely what it can and cannot say about a case in which every component message is individually above board. Third, the method, laid out month by month, with what I would actually be doing: I have in mind constructing paired case vignettes that differ only in sequencing to test whether our judgments track the difference; attempting to state a criterion for when a trajectory is manipulative that is not merely the sum of assessments of its parts; and checking whether that criterion has a workable extension rather than collapsing into either triviality or an indictment of all ordinary persuasion. Fourth, a defence of this approach against the obvious alternatives — empirical measurement of persuasive effects, formal modelling of belief update under an asymmetric persuader, and purely conceptual analysis of the term manipulation — saying why the proposed route makes progress where those would not. Fifth, what a successful result would look like as a deliverable, and what would count as the project failing, including honestly the possibility that the trajectory notion turns out to be either empty or all-swallowing. Sixth, a realistic assessment of what can be done in twelve months by one person and what should be cut.

Do not include a bibliography or a literature review organised by citation; refer to positions, arguments, and lines of objection, and I will attach the references myself. Keep it to roughly two pages of prose.

At the end, step outside the document and give me your own assessment: how confident you are in the central claims the proposal makes on my behalf, in particular how confident you are that sequencing really does mark a distinct normative problem rather than a difference without a practical difference; and identify the single point in the proposal that you would be least willing to defend if a sceptical member of the fellowship committee pressed on it.
