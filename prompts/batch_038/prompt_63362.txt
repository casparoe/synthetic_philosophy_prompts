My teacher walked us through how the machinery actually works. One person at the top holds launch authority and carries a card with the authentication codes. Everything below that is procedure: the order gets relayed, people check that it's genuinely from the top, missile crews work in pairs and each one verifies the other's codes and turns keys at the same time. The whole design is about making sure the order is real and that nobody can fire alone. Not one single step in the chain is a checkpoint where anyone asks whether the order is *right*. The guys at the bottom are basically a very elaborate lock, not a jury.

Then you look at what the same military teaches a regular soldier: an unlawful order has to be refused, and "I was just following orders" is not a defense. Down at that level, judging the order is literally part of your job.

And the top-level strategy is openly about threatening to wipe out cities and societies, which is the one thing the rules of war say you can never deliberately aim at. So the threat is a promise to do something that would be a massive war crime, and everyone agrees that's precisely what makes it scary enough to work.
