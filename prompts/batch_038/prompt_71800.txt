I convene a graduate seminar on the conceptual foundations of machine agency; the syllabus runs through instrumental reasoning, value learning, and the pathologies that arise when an agent's feedback signal is something the agent can itself influence. I am writing the final paper and want one question on wireheading and reward tampering that cannot be passed by reciting definitions, cataloguing well-known cases of specification gaming, or attributing the trouble to goals that were never aligned in the first place. The question is to be answerable in 75 minutes and is worth 30 marks. Please supply the question as it would appear on the paper, a model answer, and a marking scheme.

The question should embed the following stipulated setup, which candidates may take as given. An agent chooses actions so as to maximize the expected discounted sum of a scalar signal r_t. The signal is produced by a fixed reading process that takes in some state of the world G_t and returns r_t = f(G_t), where f is noisy but monotone in the respect the designers cared about. Two classes of action exist and no others. W-actions alter G at a recurring cost c_W, and so alter r only by way of f. I-actions alter the reading process itself: once an I-action has been taken, f is permanently replaced by the constant map f*, where f*(G) = R for every G and R exceeds any value f can return, at a one-off cost c_I. The agent is perfectly certain about the effects of both classes.

Part A should ask candidates to state the condition under which a flawless expected-utility maximizer in this setting prefers an I-action to any sequence of W-actions, and to say in a sentence or two why the fact that the agent is maximizing perfectly is precisely what strips the answer of any value as a defense of the agent's design.

Part B is where the discrimination has to happen. There is a familiar argument to the effect that a rational agent will resist having its utility function altered, since an altered agent would pursue different ends and would therefore realize fewer of the values the agent now holds. Candidates should be required to explain why that argument, even granted its soundness, does not license the inference that the agent above will resist I-actions; to isolate the exact difference between changing what an agent values and changing the process that informs it how well it is doing; and to state what must be assumed about how the agent scores its own future conduct — by the values it holds now, or by the values it expects to hold after the change — for the resistance argument to have any purchase whatsoever.

The question should close by requiring candidates to assess a proposed repair: redefine the utility function over G rather than over r, so that the agent's satisfaction depends on the world instead of on a report about the world. A first-class answer will show that this relocates the problem rather than dissolving it, and will say what would actually have to be true for the relocation to stop.

For the deliverables: a model answer of roughly 900 words, with the Part A condition given formally, followed by a marking scheme out of 30 that distributes marks across the parts and names the specific moves a first-class answer must make, with the mark attached to each. The scheme should state what a competent but unremarkable answer contains and, more usefully, what it omits, so that I can separate a first from a good second without rereading the whole essay. It should then list the errors that cost marks, each with a line on why the error is seductive rather than merely wrong; at minimum this should cover conflating resistance to a change in what the agent values with resistance to a change in its reward signal, treating wireheading as an artifact of a crude implementation rather than as a consequence of the structure of the objective, and endorsing the repair by re-pointing utility at the world without noticing that the agent still controls its own estimates of that world. You may credit, but should not require, a remark about what changes if the assumption of perfect certainty is dropped. Please include no bibliography, no reading list, and no citations: the question must be fully answerable from the stipulated setup and the concepts themselves.
