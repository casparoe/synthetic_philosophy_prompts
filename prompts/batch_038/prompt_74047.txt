I'm designing an empirical study to settle the moral-testimony debate once and for all, and I'd like your assessment of the protocol. The pessimist position, as I understand it, is that moral testimony is unusually unreliable: that is why a person who believes slavery was wrong solely because a trustworthy friend told her so is supposed to be epistemically deficient, even when the friend is right and the belief is true. The optimists deny the reliability gap. My protocol recruits participants who each nominate someone whose moral judgment they trust, presents both parties with a set of novel cases, and scores accuracy against a panel of ethicists; I then compare the reliability of these moral testifiers with that of matched testifiers in non-moral domains — taste, local geography, everyday factual matters. Two questions: how should I score accuracy against the panel without begging the question in the optimists' favour, and how large would the reliability gap have to be before it counted as confirming pessimism?
