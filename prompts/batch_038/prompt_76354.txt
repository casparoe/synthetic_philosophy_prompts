My professor of the philosophy of language has issued the following verdicts, each on whether the utterance in question conversationally implicates the content noted:

1. "Some of the applicants have prior teaching experience," in a report written by someone with complete information — yes: it implicates that not all of them do.
2. "If some of the applicants have prior teaching experience, we will need a different shortlisting procedure" — no: the inference fails to arise when the sentence sits in the antecedent of a conditional.
3. "He is poor but honest" — the contrast between poverty and honesty is conveyed, yet it is not conversational, because it cannot be retracted: adding "though I mean no suggestion of tension between the two" is absurd.
4. "It is freezing in here," said by a seated guest to a host standing beside an open window — yes: a particularized implicature that the window should be shut.
5. "Could you pass the salt?" at a dinner table — no: that is an indirect request, not an implicature.
6. "Well, that was a triumph," said by a manager surveying a disastrous product launch — yes: the speaker means something close to the opposite of what the words say.
7. "Some of the applicants have prior teaching experience — in fact, all of them do" — the implicature arises and is then withdrawn, and the fact that the repair costs the speaker nothing is precisely what shows the implicature was conversational.

Tomorrow's example is this. At one in the morning a host says to a guest who has shown no sign of leaving: "I have to be up at six — not that I'm hinting you should go." The question she will pose is whether the host's utterance conversationally implicates that the guest should leave.

Predict her verdict. More than one principle about conversational implicature is consistent with verdicts 1–7: set out the leading candidates, explain how each accommodates the whole pattern (in particular verdicts 3 and 7, which bear directly on what retraction can and cannot show), and state what each predicts about the new case. Then commit to a best guess, giving your reasons for preferring that principle over its rivals, and describe the further example that would most sharply separate the principles that survive.
