Our interdisciplinary project on human–AI teaming keeps producing design documents that say things like "the team intends to complete the inspection" without anyone noticing what that commits us to. I would like a diagnostic instrument — eight to ten questions plus a scoring key — that sorts a participant onto a theory of joint agency on the basis of how they answer cases, not which label they prefer.

Each question should present one concrete scenario with two or three answer options, and no option may name a philosopher or use a term of art. Draw the scenarios from, at minimum: an infiltrator who outwardly coordinates while privately planning to sabotage the joint effort; a distributed crowd of several hundred strangers coordinating through a platform with no face-to-face contact and no shared awareness of the whole; a committee whose written procedure yields a decision contrary to every member's individual view; a surgeon and a robotic instrument that adapts to her movements but is stipulated to have no goal representations of its own; and two people whose subplans conflict so the activity never gets off the ground, though each is willing to continue.

The scoring key must place respondents among these leaves: planning-based shared intention; plural subjects with joint commitment; collective intentionality as an irreducible primitive of individual minds; a we-mode view built on collective acceptance; a minimal, sub-personal account in terms of shared goals and expectations about each other's movements; group agency secured by an aggregation procedure; a teleological individualist account; and a deflationary leaf holding there is no shared intentionality at all.

Finally: identify the two leaves your instrument least reliably separates and say what case would separate them, and state what does or does not occupy the cell pairing an irreducible group-level agent with a denial that joint agency generates obligations between members — and if it is empty, why.
