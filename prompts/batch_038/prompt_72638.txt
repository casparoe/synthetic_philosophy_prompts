my friend runs a peer-support group and she has one line she keeps going back to: depression is like diabetes. it's a physical condition, nobody blames a diabetic for their pancreas, treatment is a medical job, so all the talk about meaning and childhood and reframing your thoughts is like telling a diabetic to think positive about her insulin. she's using it for two jobs at once -- killing the stigma, and closing down the question of whether circumstances and talking actually matter.

meanwhile I've been reading Szasz, who thinks the phrase mental illness is a myth because disease is something bodies have -- a lesion you can point at -- so the diabetes comparison is exactly the wrong one to reach for.

can you audit the analogy? I want the mapping spelled out (what plays the pancreas, the insulin, the glucose reading, the heritablity, the lifestyle stuff, the lifelong management), then the disanalogies, but sorted into the ones that bite on what she's using it for and the ones that are just furniture. two things I care about most: whether the destigmatizing conclusion survives even if the stronger it's-medical-all-the-way-down one doesn't, and whether Szasz gets a free ride here -- the analogy seems to assume somatic disease is value-free and lesion-defined, and isn't the cutoff that makes you diabetic a committee decision that's moved over time? end with what a better analogy would be if there is one, and with what would change your mind about the verdict -- what finding or argument would make you say the mapping holds up better than I'm assuming, or that I've got Szasz's criterion wrong.
