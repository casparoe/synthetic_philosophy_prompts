ok so background: I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training at all, and I keep getting dragged into the same argument with two coworkers. One insists DNA is literally a program — real source code, and the cell is just the runtime. The other says "information" in biology is a borrowed metaphor and biologists should quit using it. I know enough Shannon theory from work to put a number on how much uncertainty a sequence removes, but that is genuinely the extent of it, and I have never seen the actual debate laid out anywhere I could follow.

For an internal tech talk at our London office, I want a decision tree that sorts the positions on whether DNA is a code. Please write the whole thing in British English.

Shape: plain text, indented tree, seven to nine branch nodes, eight to twelve leaves. Every node has to be a question someone could answer without already knowing which camp it leads to — pinned to a concrete case, not to a slogan like "do you think genes have meaning?". Build it out of the logical space first and place the named positions afterwards, not the other way round.

Cases I'd like worked in somewhere along the branches:

- a synthetic DNA strand storing a photo or a video file. It is DNA, it definitely carries information, nobody would call it a gene — does it get to count?
- lab strains of bacteria whose code table has been rewritten so a stop codon now specifies an amino acid. If you can recompile the lookup table, what does that say about the mapping being a convention?
- the near-arbitrariness of codon to amino acid, the picture where it is a historical accident that got locked in very early, versus any chemistry-forced reading of it. Arbitrariness feels like the thing that earns something the label "code" in the first place, but then who laid down the convention?
- alternative splicing and overlapping reading frames: one string, several outputs, depending on what is doing the reading. Is the output really a function of the string alone?
- cell-free translation in a test tube: no cell, no selection history, no purpose in sight. Does the message still mean anything in there?
- transmission that is information-ish but not in the sequence at all — prions, or epigenetic inheritance — and whether that breaks the picture or just relocates it.
- whether the genome and the cellular machinery that reads it can be separated the way a compiler and the code it compiles can, given that the reader is itself built from the thing being read.

Each leaf should carry: the standard name of the position if it has one (and at most one person associated with it — but no references, no bibliography, no reading list, I can't check any of that anyway and don't want to pretend to), one or two sentences on what it actually claims, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Then after the tree, two short sections: which combinations of answers are logically available but nobody actually holds, and why — incoherent, just unappealing, or merely unexplored — and which pairs of leaves your questions cannot really tell apart. I would much rather see that flagged than have the tree look sharper than it is.

Aim for something a room of engineers can follow without a philosophy degree but that would not make a biologist wince. About 1200 words, dont pad it.
