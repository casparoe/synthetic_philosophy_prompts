Before I read another proposal about the transitivity of betterness, I want the specification settled. Fix the purpose as follows, since adequacy is relative to it: the comparative relation the theory supplies is to be used by a single agent that chooses repeatedly over time, that retains memory of its earlier choices, and that can construct and honour commitment devices — precommitments, restricted menus, self-binding arrangements. It is not a ranking published once in a table, and it is not required to be recoverable from the aggregated pairwise judgements of a committee.

Against that purpose, write the desiderata for a theory of comparative value. For each item, four things. First, the requirement, stated precisely. Second, the case or consideration that motivates it, developed far enough that I can feel the pressure it exerts — I have in mind the kinds of material the debate runs on: continua of sounds or of lives along which every neighbouring pair looks indistinguishable or trivially ordered while the endpoints look incomparable; trades between a short interval of severe suffering and a much longer interval of slight discomfort; spectra of outcomes running from the best things available down to existences barely worth continuing; and the cycles that such continua can be made to generate. Third, a verdict on whether it is a hard requirement under the purpose above or a demand imported from a camp with a different purpose — and if the latter, whose purpose, and what changes once you drop it. Fourth, whether it constrains the relation itself (transitivity, completeness, determinacy, Sorites-resistance, applicability to the pairs actually presented for choice) or constrains the account of the relation (what the theory must explain about our judgements, including why we are misled at every step of the continuum rather than only at its endpoints).

Then the part I most want: identify any subset of the requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied. Do not assert the conflict — exhibit it. Give the construction step by step: the sequence of pairwise comparisons, the trade-off principle or indifference-licensing assumption that carries each step, and the point at which it either closes on itself or collapses into triviality. For each such conflict, say which requirement you would surrender and what the theory loses by surrendering it — and in particular whether the loss actually costs my agent anything, given that it can precommit. If you conclude that the anti-cycle requirement is negotiable for this agent but not for others, argue that rather than assuming it.

Say also which single requirement does the most exclusionary work: the one that, once insisted upon, leaves most of the field in trouble.

Close with a short scorecard, three sentences at most per view, covering these four: a view that denies transitivity for all-things-considered betterness while retaining it for betterness in a given respect; a lexical or threshold view; a view that treats the relevant continua as vague, or as containing incomparable pairs; and a view that preserves transitivity by denying that the trade-offs driving the argument are genuinely available at all. For each, name the requirement it breaks, and say whether it breaks that requirement deliberately or as collateral damage.

No bibliography, no citations, no titles of works, no dates. I want the specification, not a reading list, and I would rather have fewer requirements argued for carefully than a long inventory of them.
