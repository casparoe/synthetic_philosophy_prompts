ok, I've been deep in the insect-sentience rabbit hole for a while now and I want a piece of satire, not another essay.

Write a mock application to an institutional animal care and use committee — the kind of protocol form a lab files before it's allowed to do anything to a vertebrate — except the applicant is a homeowner who needs to deal with a yellowjacket nest under his deck, and he is filling the form out in complete earnest. Around 700 words, formatted as the form itself: numbered sections with headings (Purpose of the Procedure, Number of Animals Justified, Alternatives Considered, Pain and Distress, Humane Endpoint, Non-Target and Environmental Effects, Consultation With an Expert), his answers written underneath in his own voice, plus at least three spots where the committee has clearly written back in the margin and rejected something.

The jokes have to come from getting the ethics right rather than from him being an idiot. He should be doing the things a good applicant does: invoking replacement, reduction and refinement; arguing over whether a soapy-water flood is really more humane than an insecticidal dust, given how long either one takes; estimating a headcount for the colony and then hedging it into mush; weighing the chance that hymenopterans are sentient against the chance that they come inside and sting his kid. Somebody — him or the committee — should raise the awkward fact that a temperate wasp colony dies off in the fall anyway with only the new queens overwintering, and the applicant then has to say whether that makes his intervention cost-free. There should be one moment where the probability-weighted sentience math gets applied completely straight-faced and produces an answer nobody is happy with. And in the environmental section let him try to claim credit for all the caterpillars the wasps would otherwise have eaten, with the committee calling out the double-counting.

The target is the whole practice of running pest control through a formal harm-benefit calculus, and the bureaucratic genre that's supposed to house it. Don't let him be a strawman and don't let the committee be stupid either — the funny version is both sides doing there best inside a form that was never built for this. Stay deadpan: no winking, no sentence that explains the joke, and no objection stated outright instead of enacted by a form field. Also no real citations or bibliography; if the form references a regulation or a guideline, invent one.
