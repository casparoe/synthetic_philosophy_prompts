I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and each autumn I lead a three-hour workshop for roughly eighteen early-career reporters who cover courts, regulation, and public health. This year's theme is precommitment: how institutions bind themselves in advance, and what actually makes those bindings hold. Design a game we can play in the room, with no lecture beforehand and with the relevant terms of art withheld until the debrief, that gets the group to feel the difference between tying yourself down and depriving yourself of the ability to hear the case for untying.

I want the engine of the game to be the part of the Homeric episode that nearly everyone skips. The crew's ears were stopped with wax; Ulysses was lashed to the mast and given a standing order that if he begged to be released they were to bind him with yet more bonds; and the arrangement therefore worked only because the enforcers could not be persuaded and had been told in advance to discount the principal's later expressed wishes. The constraint was interpersonal and epistemic rather than physical, and that is the thing I want eighteen people to run into, not be told about. In play, they should first discover that a rule they wrote unanimously is close to worthless once someone in the room has standing to interpret it, and then discover what has to be added to make it hold — and notice on their own what that addition costs them by way of being able to object.

Please give me the rules, the roles, the materials, a scoring system that functions without real money, a minute-by-minute plan for the three hours including the debrief, and a mapping of particular moments in play onto the distinction between self-binding and self-insulation, and onto the delegation problem that arises when those who enforce a constraint are not themselves subject to it. Close with what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to recover in the moment, and with a frank note on which feature of constitutional entrenchment the game cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply in words.
