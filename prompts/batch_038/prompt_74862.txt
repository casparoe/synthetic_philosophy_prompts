Ok, weird situation and honestly I don't know who else to ask, none of this is my normal job.

I'm a staff engineer at a mid-size data company. Last year we acquired a smaller competitor's product team, ~20 people, mostly for their pipeline tech. Three weeks ago someone from that team had a few drinks at an offsite and told me that for about two years before the acquisition they'd been scraping a third party's data in a way that clearly broke that site's terms and was probably illegal too — and some of it still feeds a model we ship. Only two or three people actually knew at the time; everyone else just used the output.

Our CEO wants to "get to the bottom of it" and floated this: open a two-week window where anyone from the old team can come forward and describe exactly what happened and where the data ended up, with a written promise they won't be fired or reported for it. Once the window shuts, anyone who knew and stayed quiet is fair game. A couple of the senior people from the acquired team are furious about this — they say its basically a bounty on your own coworkers and turns the juniors into informants, and that you should just deal with the two or three who actually decided anything, directly and quietly, no circus. Others say if you don't do it in the open the truth never surfaces and we'll still be shipping tainted data in three years.

I'm the one who has to audit the pipeline either way, so I get a real vote here. Which design actually gets you closer to the truth, and is either one fair? Two things keep bugging me. The people whose data got taken aren't in the room, get no say, and can still sue us no matter what we promise anyone internally — so who exactly are we granting this to? And protecting people in exchange for talking feels both completely necessary and kind of gross at the same time, and I can't tell whether that gross feeling is tracking something real or if I'm just being squeamish.
