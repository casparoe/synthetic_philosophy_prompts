I'm teaching an interdisciplinary seminar — historians, political theorists, two theologians — and every year someone says the arc of history bends toward justice with total confidence while meaning something completely different by it from the person sitting next to them. I want to build a sorting instrument that catches the disagreement before any labels get invoked: five questions a student can answer without knowing any of the isms, which between them drop them onto a named position.

Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of movements. The distinctions I actually care about are whether the claim is predictive or regulative, whether whatever does the bending sits inside history or outside it, whether human agency is required for the tendency to hold at all, whether the payoff lands within history or beyond it, and whether the arc is supposed to be visible from where we stand or only reconstructable backwards. No question may name a philosopher, an ism, or God, and none of them can be a bald 'do you believe in progress?' — make each one a bet, a counterfactual or a case.

Leaves I need: a Popperian anti-historicism, a providential reading, a dialectical-necessity reading, a plain empirical-progress reading, a cyclical-decline reading, and at least one cell nobody occupies, with a line on whether its empty because it's incoherent, unattractive or just unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your questions cant really separate rather than papering over it.

Format: exactly five numbered items, no introduction and no conclusion. Each item is one question in a sentence or two with its answer options, every option carrying a short clause on which leaf it points toward, and a closing line noting either an unoccupied combination or an unseparated pair. British English please.
