Here's what I'm tabling at your committee. It's my invention, not any real proposal, so don't go looking for precedents:

**Outcome Contract Rule.** The federation stops legislating means altogether. For any program it legislates only (a) an outcome target, defined by a federal measurement instrument, and (b) an audit procedure. The target itself is set by federal statute with Bundesrat consent, so the Länder choose the yardstick collectively and can't pick easy ones. Then: any Land may substitute its own scheme for the federal one once the federal audit office certifies that the Land's own scheme met or beat the target for two consecutive cycles; from certification onward the federal statute simply does not apply in that Land. If a contracted Land then misses the target in two consecutive cycles, certification lapses and the federation runs the program directly in that Land for one cycle through its own civil service, bypassing the Land government. Länder may also pool into a joint contract with a single shared target, so the small ones get scale without going it alone.

My opening move:

You keep saying the federal standard protects the least mobile. But that standard is precisely what the median Land can afford once the Bundesrat has finished with it — so in a poor Land it's an unfunded promise, and in a rich Land it's a ceiling nobody is allowed to break through. And my rule doesn't generate a race to the bottom, because there's no exit, no foot-voting, no tax competition: the target, the yardstick and the auditor are all federal. So tell me concretely what the least mobile citizen in the poorest Land actually loses under the outcome contract that she has today. Don't give me the principle, give me the person.
