**The student's answer:**

> The hole argument is aimed at the view Earman and Norton call manifold substantivalism: the claim that the points of the differentiable manifold M are genuine constituents of the world, existing independently of the metric and matter fields defined on them, and individuated independently of those fields. Spacetime, on this view, just is M, with the metric a field that M happens to bear.
>
> The argument starts from a model of general relativity, a triple (M, g, T) satisfying Einstein's field equations. Pick a bounded open region H of M — the "hole" — and a diffeomorphism d: M → M which is the identity everywhere outside H but moves points around inside H. Because the field equations are generally covariant, applying d to the model yields another model, (M, d\*g, d\*T), which satisfies the same equations. d\*g and g coincide everywhere outside H, since d is the identity there, but they disagree inside it: for p in H, the value g takes at p is taken by d\*g at d(p).
>
> Now let Σ be a spacelike hypersurface lying entirely to the past of H. Σ is outside the hole, so the two models assign exactly the same metric and matter field on Σ; they agree on all the initial data. Yet they differ at points of H, which lie to the future of Σ. The data on Σ therefore fail to pick out a unique subsequent evolution. If, as the substantivalist insists, d\*g and g represent two numerically distinct physical situations, then general relativity is indeterministic — and not merely in some special case, since the choice of d was entirely arbitrary.
>
> Earman and Norton present this as a dilemma. Either the substantivalist swallows the indeterminism, or she denies that diffeomorphically related models represent distinct situations, the denial being what they call Leibniz equivalence.
>
> I think the second horn is much the better one, and that it costs the substantivalist nothing. Two coordinate descriptions of a single geometry are descriptions of the same physical situation on any view — nobody supposes that a chart is part of the furniture of the world — and Leibniz equivalence, so far as I can see, is nothing over and above the general covariance we already invoked in building the argument. We noted that d\*g is a solution because the equations are invariant under diffeomorphisms; Leibniz equivalence merely draws the evident consequence that two solutions related by such an invariance cannot be physically distinct. So the substantivalist who adopts it introduces no new premise. She keeps the manifold and its points, which is all that substantivalism asserts, and what she abandons is only the habit of reading a difference in the labeling of points as a difference in the world.
>
> The first horn, by contrast, is not really live. An indeterminism of this unconstrained kind is not one a theorist can absorb: it says that a complete specification of the state on Σ leaves open what obtains at p, and no further data about Σ can close the gap, since the two models already agree on all of them. The substantivalist who takes this horn is committed to brute facts about which of two models that coincide on everything outside H obtains — and such facts are unknowable in principle, because any observation is itself a matter field and every matter field is subject to the same dragging. She has paid for the failure of determinism with facts that can make no empirical difference whatever.
>
> So the argument is best read as a reductio not of general relativity but of a way of reading it. What survives is a picture on which the points of M exist but are not identifiable apart from the fields spread over them, and the physically real content of a model is the pattern of coincidences among those fields. It is worth noting, finally, that this conclusion is available only because the metric is dynamical. In a theory with a fixed background geometry, dragging the matter fields would alter their relation to that background, and the dragged triple would not be a model of the theory at all. That is why the hole argument is specific to general relativity, and why it does not threaten special relativistic field theories on Minkowski spacetime, even though those too can be written in generally covariant form.
