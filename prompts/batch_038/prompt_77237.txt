I built this argument for a cultural psych seminar and the conclusion is one nobody there will accept, but I genuinely can't find the broken step.

(1) How tight a culture is — how strict its norms are, how hard it punishes deviation — tracks the threats it historically faced: famine, disease, natural disaster, chronic conflict.
(2) Groups in high-threat environments that had strong norms and punished deviants hard got through those conditions better than looser groups alongside them.
(3) So a lot of tight norms exist because they helped the societies that have them cope with threat.
(4) If a norm is there because it helped its society cope with its conditions, then it's doing work for that society, and a norm that does work for the people who have it is appropriate for them.
(5) So the tight norms — harsh punishment of deviance, restrictions on who you may marry, rigid gendered division of labor — are appropriate in high-threat societies, and calling them oppressive from where I sit is a kind of imperialism.
(6) Same route the other way: rich low-threat societies' looseness is appropriate for them, and pushing looseness on high-threat societies undercuts those societies' ability to function.

Half the seminar will say the problem is obviously 4→5, some is/ought slide, and I dont think that's it — (4) doesn't morally praise anything, it just says the norm fits the conditions, and I don't see how a fit claim becomes a praise claim by accident. Also (3) is only a functional story about persistence, and functional stories about norms had better have some normative upshot, or the whole enterprise of explaining norms by function is pointless.

So which step is actually broken? Tell me why it looked fine to me, and what I pay for rejecting it. If you land on (4), say whether that leaves functionalist explanation with literally zero normative implications, because that strikes me as too strong — and if you think it does leave some upshot, spell out what the acceptable version of (4) would look like and why (5) still doesn't follow from it. Also flag anywhere I'm relying on (2) being true as stated. And say how confident you are in the diagnosis overall, plus the single point you're least sure about.
