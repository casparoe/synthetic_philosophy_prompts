so I'm two years into a development econ PhD and I've been staring at our cross-country growth regressions long enough that something has started bugging me, and I want to know what the whole apparatus is actually assuming.

The standard setup in my group: the dependent variable is growth in GDP per capita over some window, and the regressors are schooling, investment share, income at the start of the window, an index of institutional quality, and — this is the one — a culture variable, which is the share of that country's survey respondents who say most people can be trusted, pulled from one of the big cross-national values surveys. The write-up then says culture has an independent effect on growth. Three things don't sit right with me.

(1) The same group's policy reports treat culture as untouchable. The recommendations are always about incentives, property rights and state capacity, and the standing line is that you can't legislate what people value, so it isn't a lever. Which means culture is a cause in table 3 and not a cause in chapter 5.

(2) When a referee pushed on endogeneity, the fix was to instrument trust with some deep historical variable — whether the society plough-farmed, say, or how dense missionary activity was a century ago. So the causal claim ends up resting on the assumption that the historical thing affects growth today only by way of what people answer on a survey today.

(3) For my own chapter I tried to isolate culture proper by first regressing trust on institutions, income, schooling and the trust of neighbouring countries, and then using the leftover residual as my culture measure — the part of the value that nothing else explains. My advisor said that was backwards and I couldnt say why he was right.

What picture of culture does all this presuppose? I mean, what has to be true of it for one scalar attached to a country to be among the causes of that country's growth — that values are a stable property of a society rather than of situations, that they're separable from the institutions they're supposed to explain, that asking people a question reports them, that they work by being held by individuals? And which rival picture does it rule out: the Weberian sort, where you explain conduct by the reasons a belief gives the person holding it rather than by a variable pushing on an outcome, or the materialist sort, where ideas are just a trace of what already happened economically? Also tell me where the practice is underdetermined — which of our coefficients would come out exactly the same if culture weren't a cause at all but a proxy for something else, and whether (1), (2) and (3) are all leaning on one assumption or on three different ones.

Answer as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each: what the regression setup presupposes about culture, what rival view it excludes, then one point each on (1), (2) and (3). No introduction, no conclusion.
