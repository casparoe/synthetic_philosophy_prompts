So I TA a seminar on bargaining and fairness and I'm building an exercise around the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution vs the Nash one, and I want a sanity check on whether the thing actually has the teeth I think it has.

Setup: two firms, A and B, splitting the gains from a joint venture. Payoffs are $M above each side's walk-away position, so the disagreement point is d = (0,0). Feasible set is the triangle with corners (0,0), (100,0), (0,100), i.e. the frontier is x + y = 100. Both rules then say (50,50), which is why I picked it as a baseline — nothing up my sleeve.

Four moves A (or B, in one case) can make before sitting down. Take each on its own, starting from the baseline:

1. **Moonshot buyout.** A acquires a logistics network that only pays off in configurations where B gets squeezed, which adds the point (140,20) to the feasible set — so the set becomes the hull of (0,0), (100,0), (140,20), (0,100).
2. **Ceiling trim.** B signs an exclusivity deal with A's key supplier, so A can no longer do better than 60 in any arrangement. Feasible set is now the hull of (0,0), (60,40), (0,100) — i.e. just the stretch of x + y = 100 with x ≤ 60. My gut says this shouldn't change anything for a fair rule, since it only deletes deals nobody was ever going to accept anyway.
3. **Pie expansion.** A mediator finds an efficiency gain and the frontier moves out to x + y = 120.
4. **Aspiration padding.** A's board publishes a "best case" standalone valuation of 130 for itself — its not actually feasible, but the mediator's spreadsheet builds the utopia/ideal point out of each side's stated best case, so u becomes (130,100) while the feasible set is unchanged.

What I want:

- For each move, A's payoff under the Nash solution and under KS in the modified problem. Ballpark to one decimal is fine, just show the line of reasoning briefly.
- Rank the four moves by how much they raise A's payoff under each rule, and then separately by how much they change A's *share* of the joint surplus under KS (share rather than absolute dollars, since move 3 changes the size of the pie).
- A tier list (S/A/B/C) of the four moves for A if the mediator uses KS, and then again if the mediator uses Nash.
- Rate each move 0–10 on "how embarassing is this to KS as a fairness standard," and give the parallel score for Nash, one line of justification each.
- Say whether move 4 counts as gaming the concept or just gaming the inputs, and whether that distinction also rescues KS from moves 1 and 2.

Then flag which of your numbers you're least sure of, and tell me what would change your mind: what argument would convince you that these moves aren't genuine objections to KS at all, just artefacts of the toy geometry I picked.
