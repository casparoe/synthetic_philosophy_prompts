I work at a community org, and a lot of the people we serve are long-term residents without citizenship, so they're barred from voting in any local election. When they object to something — the new parking enforcement, a school boundary change — somebody always answers "if you don't vote, you don't get to complain." Same line gets thrown at the 16-year-olds in our youth group, usually by people who also oppose making voting compulsory. Can you lay out the different precise claims that one sentence could actually be making? I want the full menu: a forfeiture-of-standing claim, a no-causal-influence claim, a hypocrisy or self-consistency claim, a you're-not-informed-enough-to-object claim, some kind of legitimacy or authorization claim, whatever else is hiding in there. State each as a real thesis rather than a vibe, say which are platitudes, which are false, which are substantive but defensible, and which of them even could apply to someone who's legally not allowed to vote. And does any reading survive the observation that a single vote basically never decides an outcome anyway?
