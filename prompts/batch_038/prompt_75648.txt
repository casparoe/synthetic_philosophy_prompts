I work as head of sensory evaluation at a specialty coffee roastery. I run a trained panel of eight tasters who score and describe green and roasted lots, and a large part of my job is keeping their palates comparable to one another over time. In the past year two of my panelists have drifted, and they have drifted in opposite directions, and I have found that I cannot tell which of them is the one who needs recalibrating.

Panelist A trained as a neuroscientist before moving into flavor work. He says his sensitivity is unchanged: he still sorts the same lots blind, in the same order, with the same descriptors, and his discrimination scores and ranking correlations are statistically indistinguishable from last year's. What has changed, he says, is his liking. The profile he used to chase now strikes him as flat, and he explains this as his standards having moved — the experience is the same, his reaction to it is not. Panelist B came from audio engineering. He says the reverse: his preferences are intact, he still wants that profile and still judges it as one judges a good thing, but it no longer tastes to him the way it used to; he suspects something in his wiring has gone and that the experience itself has degraded. Behaviorally the two are, so far as I can measure, the same person in two descriptions. Their self-reports are flatly incompatible.

This sent me to Dennett's 1988 paper "Quining Qualia" and its two coffee tasters at the same company, and from there into the wider stalemate between eliminativist and illusionist accounts of qualia on one side and qualia realism on the other — a dispute that has been running for four decades with the same arguments and no movement I can detect.

What I want from you is a diagnosis of why this dispute has stalled, not a verdict on it and not a tour of the positions. Specifically:

1. Is it a substantive disagreement about the world, a verbal one about what the terms qualia, experience, and seems are being made to cover, or two different questions traveling under a single heading — say, one about what first-person reports license and another about the metaphysics of consciousness?
2. Which of the following is doing the real work: competing standards for what counts as evidence (introspective testimony versus discrimination behavior versus functional and causal explanation), disagreement about which cases count as data at all, or bedrock intuitions about there being something it is like that neither side can defend from neutral ground?
3. Where does the coffee-taster case sit in that structure? Does it show only that introspective reports are unreliable, or does it purport to show that there is no fact of the matter about which of my panelists is right? Realists respond that it trades on an ambiguity between how things seem and how one takes them to be. Tell me whether that reply is a genuine escape or a restatement of the dispute.
4. Here is my working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis to be tested rather than as a conclusion: the impasse is not a hard empirical question but a disagreement about the job description of a theory of consciousness. The eliminativist holds that a candidate property earns its place only by explaining behavior, report, and discrimination; the realist denies that this exhausts what a theory of experience owes. Pressure-test this — including whether it is too charitable to both sides.
5. Given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, a reformulation of the question, a class of empirical findings (anosmia and parosmia, blindsight, adaptation and recalibration studies), or nothing at all? If nothing, say so and explain why the stalemate is stable rather than merely unresolved. And tell me honestly whether anything measurable in my own laboratory could bear on the question.
