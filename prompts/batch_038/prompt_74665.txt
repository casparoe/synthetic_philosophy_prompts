You are advising the steering committee of a small interdisciplinary center on knowledge and automation at a research university: four postdocs, one resident philosopher, a three-year runway, and a funder who wants both publishable conceptual work and guidance the university can use when it rewrites its rules on AI in coursework. Please work inside the following frame. Distinguish three ways of outsourcing epistemic labor: offloading storage and calculation, where the agent can still reconstruct the result (writing, calculators, search engines); deferential outsourcing, where the agent cannot follow the reasoning but has standing reasons to trust the community producing it (the point Hardwig pressed, that large-scale science rests on rational trust in testimony); and generative outsourcing, where the system supplies the candidate thoughts, formulations, and framings themselves. On this picture the live crux is whether verification competence can survive the loss of generation competence — whether one can keep the ability to tell good output from bad after losing the ability to produce it oneself.

Given that setup, which open questions in this area should the center prioritize over its three years, and in what order? I want a defended agenda, not a survey. For each question, say why it sits upstream of the others, whether it is tractable by conceptual and philosophical method rather than requiring large empirical studies the center cannot run, how neglected it already is, and what it would deliver on the coursework-policy side of the funder's brief. Then tell me which currently fashionable questions the center should decline to take on, and whether they are overrated or merely premature. Finally, say what evidence or argument over the next two or three years would move each item up or down your ordering. Argue from the concepts rather than from citations; no bibliography, please.
