I am editing a public-facing explainer on behavioural genetics, and I need a rigorous audit of one analogy that our consulting geneticist keeps reaching for. Whenever a reviewer objects that heritability estimates are systematically misread, he replies that heritability is simply the R-squared of the genetic model: a variance-explained statistic like any other, no different in kind from reporting that a regression accounts for 80 percent of the variation in house prices across a city. He uses that analogy to defend three claims in the draft.

Claim A: writing that human height is "about 80 percent genetic" is no more misleading than writing that our model explains 80 percent of the variance in house prices.

Claim B: heritabilities are comparable across traits and across populations in the way R-squared values are comparable across models, so a trait estimated at 0.8 can meaningfully be called more genetic than one estimated at 0.4.

Claim C: where the estimate is high, environmental policy has little room to move individual outcomes.

I want the analogy audited, not restated. First, map the elements explicitly: what plays the role of the outcome variable, the predictors, the fitted model, the sample, and the residual. Then show where each correspondence breaks down, attending in particular to what happens when the "predictor" is an unmeasured random effect rather than a column of data; when the estimate is broad-sense rather than narrow-sense; when genotypes are correlated with, or actively select, the environments they land in; and when the population's environmental range shifts while the genotypes stay fixed. Identify separately which commitments the regression framing quietly imports that the variance-component ratio does not carry — above all the suggestion that the non-genetic share has been positively characterised rather than arriving as a leftover residual. Finally, judge Claims A, B and C one at a time, and say whether a better analogy exists for the same explanatory purpose; if none does, say that plainly and explain what a non-specialist could correctly be told instead.

No bibliography and no citations, please; I will supply references myself.

Deliverable: the audit in prose, followed by a single JSON object conforming to this schema.
