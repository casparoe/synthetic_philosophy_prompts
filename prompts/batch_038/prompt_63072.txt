I'm a novelist and I'm on the jury for a mid-size memoir prize — five of us, we meet next week to cut the shortlist down to a winner. One book has become the whole fight. It's a memoir built around memories of early childhood abuse the author recovered in her thirties during guided-imagery therapy; before that she had no recollection of any of it, there's no corroborating witnesses and no records, and her family denies it flat out. She is utterly convinced and she writes those recovered scenes with real force. Our rulebook is useless here: it says entries must be a truthful account of the author's own life and requires a signed statement of good faith, and says nothing about verifiability. Two jurors want to drop the book as effectively unfalsifiable, one wants to keep it on the grounds that memoir was never in the business of court-admissible fact and sincerity is all the rule asks for, and the chair has asked me to circulate something we can all react to.

Draft that memo — a recommendation we could actually vote on, with the reasoning, not a summary of both sides. Two things I need it to do. First, pull apart the different things everyone in this argument sloppily calls introspection — noticing what's going on in your own mind right now, versus having grounds about your own past, versus your felt confidence in either — and say which of those her certainty actually licenses and which it doesn't. Second, tell me whether the introspection-skeptics' stuff (people confidently inventing plausible explanations for their own behaviour and then recieving them as memories) genuinely undermines all memoir including the ones we've all admired, or whether it only bites under specific conditions — and if it's conditions, what marks them off, in terms a juror could apply to a manuscript rather than to a lab study.

Take the pro-book juror's position at its strongest, not as a strawman, and if keeping-or-dropping is the wrong frame entirely, give me a fallback we could vote on instead. Under 900 words, readable by a smart non-philosopher, and please no citations or reading lists — I don't have time to chase anything down.
