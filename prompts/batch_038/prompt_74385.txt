I spent forty-odd years in biophysics and only retired last spring, so these days I read philosophy for fun and I've gotten properly tangled up in the consciousness literature. What hooked me was Dennett's attack on the "Cartesian theater" — the point that you can't ask *when* something becomes conscious without quietly assuming there's a place where the watching happens. But half the people I read afterwards seem to keep the theater anyway, just relocated.

Could you build me a decision tree that sorts theories of consciousness by what they're committed to about *timing*, rather than by how they define phenomenal consciousness? I want the entry point to be a temporal anomaly — the color-phi kind of case, the flash-lag effect, the rabbit illusion you get tapping up someone's arm, Libet's business about the brain antedating a stimulus — and every node should be a question my reading group could answer by thinking about what would show up in the lab, without already knowing which theory they're steering toward. So things like: is there a fact of the matter about the moment an event enters experience at all; does that moment have to coincide with when the information becomes available to the rest of the system; can the content be edited after it was supposedly already experienced; does anything have to be true at a single time and place.

Leaves should be named positions — multiple drafts, global workspace, higher-order thought, naive "it happens here and now," whatever else genuinely belongs — and I'd also like the unoccupied corners marked, with one line each on whether the combination is incoherent, just unappealing, or simply nobody's thought of it yet.

It's for about eight ex-colleagues who are smart but haven't read the sources, so keep it to six or seven nodes. And please be honest about which pairs of leaves the tree can't actually separate — I have a strong suspicion two or three of these are the same view wearing different clothes, and I'd rather be told that up front than discover it in the middle of the meeting.
