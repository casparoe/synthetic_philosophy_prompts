A chapter of a popular cosmology book I've been reading handles the Boltzmann-brain problem as follows:

'Here is the argument. Boltzmann's statistical mechanics tells us that matter in thermal equilibrium is always jostling, and given a long enough time those random motions will, purely by chance, assemble any structure you like, however improbable. Wait long enough and one of those fluctuations will produce a fully functioning human brain, complete with false memories of a life it never lived. And because producing a single brain is a far smaller departure from equilibrium than producing an entire ordered observable universe, such brains should be incomparably more numerous than ordinary observers like us. So if your cosmological model predicts an eternity of empty de Sitter space, it predicts that nearly every observer who ever exists is a Boltzmann brain — which means, statistically, that you are one. But you have genuine memories and a genuine past, so that prediction is false, and the cosmology that entails it must be false as well. This is why such models are described as self-refuting: they cannot be believed, because on their own account the reasoning that led you to them was an accident. The standard repairs all shorten the universe's future — vacuum decay or a recollapse cuts the fluctuation process off before brains have time to accumulate.'

The author is clearly not a crank, and much of this matches what I have read elsewhere, but two spots struck me as too fast and I cannot tell whether my unease is justified or merely unfamiliarity. Could you audit the passage for me? Please sort its claims into those a physicist working on foundations or a philosopher of physics would sign off on without qualification, those that are right in spirit but framed so that they misdescribe what the argument really depends on, and those that are wrong or question-begging — and flag anywhere a live dispute is presented as settled. Then two questions. Does the concluding move work at all: is "we have real memories, so any theory implying we are fluctuations is false" a legitimate inference, or must the argument run differently, and if differently, how? And is the whole business just brain-in-a-vat skepticism in a physics costume, as I suspect, or does it bear on particular cosmological models in a way skeptical hypotheses never could?
