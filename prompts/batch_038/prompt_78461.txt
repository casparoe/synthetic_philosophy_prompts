So I keep running into the same move in arguments about animals in the wild and I've never managed to find a word for it. Some cases that made me notice:

A friend of mine will pull over for a pigeon that's been hit by a car, drive it two towns over to a wildlife rehabber, and be visibly shaken about it for a day. Same friend, hiking in October, comes across a fawn with its jaw wrecked by parasites, starving, covered in ticks, and her whole reaction is — well, that's nature, that's just how it works out here. Nobody in the conversation suggested the fawn is suffering less. Its obviously suffering more. The only thing that changed is what caused it.

Second one: somebody floats vaccinating wild foxes with oral bait, or deworming a wild ungulate population, and the reflex objection is that you can't just start intervening in ecosystems, that's playing god, we should leave nature to sort itself out. The identical intervention on livestock or on pets gets funded without a discussion, often by the same people.

Third, a smaller one: I argued that the sheer volume of suffering in wild animal populations is a reason to do something about it, and the reply I got was basically that a world containing that much suffering is what the world is, so there's nothing to conclude from it — like the fact that it's been going on forever without us is itself a reason it isn't urgent.

Is there an established name for this? I know the general neighborhood — naturalistic fallacy, status quo bias, the doing/allowing distinction — but none of them seem to be the thing. It isn't a claim that natural is good or that nature is in balance; the people making the move usually agree readily that the suffering is bad and pointless. It isn't doing vs. allowing either, because the same person will happily allow a fawn to die and yet think failing to help a car-struck pigeon is a dereliction — the line isn't act/omission, it's human-caused vs. not-human-caused. And it isn't just status quo bias, because it survives being told that the suffering isn't anyone's chosen arrangement, it's just the accident of how the causes happen to be distributed.

What I'm after is the carving, not a label. What exactly is the phenomenon, which nearby concept comes closest and at what point does each one stop fitting, and is there any recognized term for this specific provenance-based discount on harms? And if nobody has named it, name it — give me a definition tight enough that I can tell what falls inside and what doesn't, plus a few boundary cases worked through. Does it cover reluctance to help wild animals merely because they're wild, with no claim about naturalness? Does it cover treating a flood as a lesser tragedy than an equally destructive flood caused by a dam failure? Does it cover the way we treat disease in wild populations differently from disease in human populations, or is that a separate bias doing separate work? Please answer in American English.
