My aunt has late-stage dementia and at a family meeting my cousin said "a person is a person through people" to explain why we shouldn't be looking for whats left of her capacities -- her personhood is held in our relations with her, not in her memory. It moved me, but I keep thinking it backfires: if being a person depends on being woven into a community, what does that say about someone nobody is in relation with anymore, or a person whose family has all died?

Can you lay out the different precise claims that proverb could be making -- metaphysics of what persons are, a claim about how personhood is acquired or comes in degrees, a norm about how we're obliged to treat people, some combination -- and for each one say whether it's plausible, whether it actually gets my cousin his conclusion, and whether it drags us into the problem I'm worried about. A menu of readings with verdicts, not a defense of whichever you like best.
