I need a two-page proposal for a twelve-week independent study, to be submitted to my department's graduate committee, on a question about Sen's liberal paradox that I cannot yet state cleanly. My hunch is that the paradox is standardly presented as a clash between a minimal condition of individual liberty and the weak Pareto principle, whereas the mechanism actually doing the work is that the social-state space allows one person to hold preferences about another person's private feature, which unrestricted domain then imports into the social ranking. The familiar responses — rights understood as protected choices rather than as decisiveness over outcomes, restrictions on which preferences count, and formulations of rights over strategies in a game form rather than over social states — each blunt the result, and each attracts a standard complaint: the first makes a person's rights hostage to what he happens to do, the second looks reverse-engineered from the theorem, the third severs any link between rights and how well people fare.

What I want to investigate is whether some restriction on cross-personal preferences of this kind can provably dissolve the impossibility, is not equivalent to simply deleting one of the two conditions, and can be motivated independently of the paradox rather than from it.

Turn this into a proposal that: states the research question in a single opening sentence, precise enough that a result could confirm or refute it; explains, by describing the arguments rather than citing them, why the responses above leave that question open (no bibliography and no reference list — I will supply citations myself); lays out a week-by-week method, including which formal results I would attempt first, what I would do if the natural restriction turns out too strong or too weak, and how I would test a candidate restriction for principledness rather than ad hocery; says what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project's failing; and gives an honest assessment of whether twelve weeks is realistic, naming the stage most likely to overrun.
