my reading group is doing Simone Weil's essay on abolishing all political parties next week and I want to open the session with a poem rather than a summary. write me a pantoum, six quatrains or so, ending the usual way (the first stanza's 1st and 3rd lines coming back as the last stanza's 2nd and 4th) — because I want the repetition to be the argument. a reader should be able to watch a real question, the kind a person actually has (is this true, what would be just here) get inherited by the next stanza and come back already owned by the party: will this position help us grow, can it get me elected. work in her defintion of a party — machine for manufacturing collective passion, organization for putting pressure on individual minds, thing whose only real end is its own limitless growth, totalitarian in germ — but no quotes from her anywhere, just paraphrase. and don't let the ending turn into a sermon about banning parties; the darker note is that the recycling is a relief, that not having to think alone is comfortable, which is pretty much why she wants them gone instead of reformed.
