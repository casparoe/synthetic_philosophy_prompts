Write a dialogue between two moral philosophers who disagree about whether pleasure and pain are commensurable.

Fix the two positions as follows. The first is a threshold negative utilitarian: there is a level of intensity-and-duration above which suffering is lexically bad, so that no quantity of pleasure, however large or however widely distributed, can compensate for a single supra-threshold episode; below that level, suffering is fungible with pleasure at a determinate exchange rate. The second is a cardinal hedonist: all experience lies on one continuous welfare scale, so every agony has a finite value that enough mild pleasure outweighs, and every pleasure a finite value that can justify enough agony.

Set the argument at a hospital research ethics board weighing a trial that would spare a large population a modest chronic ache by exposing a handful of patients to brief, severe, and irremediable episodes. Have each press the objection that does the most damage to the other: the cardinal hedonist on where the threshold sits and whether the view survives a sorites series of gradually intensifying pains, and on whether a million sub-threshold lives jointly outweigh one supra-threshold one; the threshold theorist on whether the single scale is a measurement or a stipulation, what the exchange rate could be derived from, and what the view says when both available options contain supra-threshold suffering. Neither may be a strawman — each must be given its best answer to the hardest form of the objection, and neither may be allowed a clean victory. Close with the two agreeing on what would settle the disagreement and on whether that thing is available in principle.
