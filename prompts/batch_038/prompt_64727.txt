So I'm prepping a seminar on Schopenhauer's aesthetics and I keep hitting a snag I can't shake, and I want your number on it rather than a survey of the literature — which I know, and which you'd get wrong anyway.

Setup. In Book III he says music is unlike the other arts: they copy the Platonic Ideas, whereas music is an unmediated copy of the will itself, which is why it goes straight at us in a way painting and poetry don't. Fine. My objection — call it the articulation problem, I'm sure someone has run it before, but I want it stated my way — is this: the will, on his own account, is blind, unitary, partless, one and the same in everything, with plurality and order belonging only to its manifestations. Music meanwhile is about the most internally articulated art form there is: tonic and dominant, tension and resolution, theme and recapitulation, a strict hierarchy of parts with rules about what can follow what. So what is music copying, exactly? If the will has no structure, a structureless copy is what you'd expect, and all that intricate form has to come from somewhere else — convention, the physiology of hearing, or the Ideas after all. And if you say the will does have the relevant structure, you've quietly re-admitted the stratification he was routing around, and music ends up copying something organized, which is basically the Ideas with the name filed off.

The obvious rescue is his own bit about the layers of a piece: the grumbling bass as crude inorganic matter, the middle voices as the intermediate grades of objectification, the melody on top as the intellectual life and endeavor of a human being. That looks like exactly the graded articulation my objection says is missing, and it's sourced from the will's own objectification rather than from the Ideas. I want you to weigh that rescue honestly — it's the strongest card he's holding and I think people wave it away too fast.

Question: what's your credence that Schopenhauer's account of music, as he actually gives it, survives the articulation problem? Meaning: a competent contemporary analytic philosopher of music who is granted all the rest of the metaphysics for free — the will, representation, the Ideas, the whole system — would still judge his claims about music to be non-vacuous and doing real explanatory work. One number.

Then decompose it. Give me seperate rough probabilities for at least these four: (a) the will in his system genuinely lacks the structure my objection says it lacks; (b) the bass-to-melody stratification supplies the structure without smuggling the Ideas back in; (c) whether the copying claim is even the sort of thing that could be non-vacuous, or whether its doing purely expressive/rhetorical work no matter how the metaphysics shakes out; (d) how load-bearing the music doctrine actually is for the rest of the system versus how decorative — i.e. whether someone who accepts the metaphysics is under any pressure to accept the music part too. Then say how you're combining them: multiplying, taking a min, weighting by something? And say which of the four your final number is most sensitive to.

Last bit. A colleague of mine is at about 0.7 that the account survives; I'm at about 0.25. We've read the same book. Tell me which of (a)–(d) you think is doing most of the work in that gap. And tell me what argument or what kind of evidence would move your number the furthest, and in which direction. Don't hedge with "it depends how you read the copy relation" — that's precisely the thing I want priced out.
