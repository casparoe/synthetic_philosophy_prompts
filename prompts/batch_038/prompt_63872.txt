I'm a novelist, fourth book, and for about a year now I've been doing research for one that's set in a clinic that prescribes stimulants to perfectly healthy people — which sounds dystopian until you notice the waiting list is six months long. Anyway. Last week I got into a long argument with a friend who used to teach bioethics, and she made a claim I can't shake: that the whole therapy-versus-enhancement debate is a dead end, because "treatment" just means bringing someone up to normal and "enhancement" means going past it, and the normal baseline keeps sliding around (reading was an enhancement in 1400; glasses; IVF; vaccination). Her proposal is to scrap the pair entirely and ask instead whether an intervention is *positional* — whether its benefit depends on other people not having it too. Literacy stopped being positional once everybody got it, she said; being six inches taller, or ten points up on the curve, never does. On her account enhancement only becomes objectionable when it's a race where everyone pays to run and nobody ends up ahead of where they'd have been.

So: write me an essay on whether she's right. I want the positional analysis taken seriously as a replacement, not bolted on as one consideration among many — does it actually do the job the old distinction was hired for, or does it quietly change the subject? Because there's a case I keep coming back to that seems to break it, and I'd like you to wrestle with it rather than route around it. Her own daughter, seventeen, has been on an SSRI for anxiety for two years; it works; it is about as non-positional as an intervention can be, since nobody loses anything if she's calm. And yet my friend admits she sometimes wonders whether the girl's writing — she writes well, uncomfortably well — would have more in it if she were anxious again, and whether the drug took something from her that neither "it's restoring her to normal" nor "it's not a status race" can name. If the therapy test says fine and the positional test says fine and something still feels unaccounted for, what is the missing category, or is the residue just aesthetic squeamishness on my part?

Give me your verdict in the first sentence and then defend it — no both-sides mush at the end. Under 900 words please, I've got a deadline of my own staring at me.
