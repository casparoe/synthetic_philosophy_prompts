I work on an applied alignment team building a mechanistic anomaly detector, and I want to pin down what a line one of my senior colleagues keeps repeating actually commits us to. Our setup, briefly: we record internal activations (feature-space summaries of the residual stream at a fixed set of layers) on a reference battery of prompts where we have decent independent reason to think the model is doing the straightforward thing — deliberately easy tasks on which the honest answer is also the easiest one for it to compute — and then, at deployment, we flag inputs whose internal activity sits far from that reference distribution under a distance metric we have chosen. His line, as I would render it, is that we are not looking for lying, we are looking for difference. He puts that sentence to work in three distinct places. First, when I proposed that we settle on an operational definition of deception before choosing the reference battery, so that we would know what we were trying to keep the model from doing, he replied that the definition is unnecessary: any departure from the reference will show up, so conceptual work upstream is a waste of the quarter. Second, in a design review, he used it to reassure a skeptical reviewer that an obfuscated or strategically concealed failure mode cannot slip through, on the grounds that because we test for no particular behavior, a mechanism trained to disguise itself still has to compute something, and that something will differ from the reference. Third, in a writeup intended for outside readers, he used it to argue that the method is failure-mode-agnostic and model-agnostic: nothing in it depends on knowing what the bad thing is, so the same detector should transfer to any model, any task, and any failure we have not yet imagined.

I suspect the sentence is doing at least three different jobs and that at most one of them is defensible, but I cannot say which, and I would rather not walk into the next review with a vague objection. Could you lay out, as a numbered menu I can bring to that meeting, the precise claims it could be making — from the near-tautological (a detector can only ever register deviation from some baseline) through the substantive (deviation in the right internal feature space is a better signal than deviation in behavior) to the strong and possibly false (no account of the target failure mode is required anywhere in the pipeline)? For each reading, state exactly what it asserts, whether it is trivially true, contingently true given design choices, or false, and which of the three uses above it could support and what would have to be added for it to support them. Please be explicit about the two notions the sentence leaves open, since I think most of the disagreement lives there: what counts as a difference, given that difference is relative to a metric, a comparison class, and a level of granularity — a mechanism can be identical at the level of the algorithm it implements and different at the level of the features realizing it — and what it means for the reference and the deployment computation to be the same mechanism at all, whether that is sameness of weights, of circuit structure, of computed function, or of the features causally involved in producing the output.

Finally, I would like the best reading tested against two cases we actually have. In the first, a deployment input appears to route through essentially the circuitry the model uses on the reference battery, with the departure concentrated in a small number of feature directions that matter a great deal for the output; the difference is small in extent but consequential, and our current distance metric barely registers it. In the second, a benign input differs from the reference battery in topic, length, and formatting, and produces a large distance under the same metric even though nothing of interest has changed. Is there a reading of "we are looking for difference, not for lying" on which both cases come out the way we want — the first flagged, the second not — without smuggling back in precisely the account of what counts as the relevant failure that the first use says we do not need? If there is not, tell me which of the three uses has to be dropped, and what would replace it.
