Our county flood control district votes next month on a five-year contract for the model that predicts river levels for our flood warnings. The incumbent is an old statistical thing a state university built in the 90s — it assumes the river changes smoothly and that the last sixty years is a reasonable guide to the next ten. The challenger is a startup whose whole pitch is that their system makes no assumptions about the future whatsoever, it just finds whatever pattern is sitting in the data. One of our board members has done a lot of reading on her own and pushed back with that no-free-lunch result, the one where no learning method comes out ahead of any other once you average across every possible way the world could be. The vendor's answer was that that's precisely their point: the old model's assumptions are just as arbitrary, theirs simply doesn't hide them. Then we ran both against our own historical gauge records as a blind backtest and they came out roughly even, and the vendor is now using that tie as evidence they're right.

Half the board thinks the startup is being admirably honest about uncertainty, half thinks it's a con dressed up as humility. There's also a wrinkle nobody's noticed yet: our procurement policy forbids awarding a contract on the basis of assumptions about future conditions that cant be proven — which if you actually apply it disqualifies everybody, including just keeping the incumbent.

Write the memo I'll hand out before the vote. Say what we should do and by when, with reasons we could actually argue back against. I want it to take a real position on whether the vendor's no-free-lunch line is a legitimate argument or a dodge, and to fix the policy problem rather than just note it.
