Act as a philosopher of mathematics equally at home in model theory and in the conceptual foundations of relativity, and think carefully before answering. I have just spent three hours arguing with a friend from philosophy of physics, and I want the argument audited rather than reprised.

Her claim: Skolem's paradox is the relativity of simultaneity in a new hat. Just as "simultaneous" turned out to have no frame-independent truth value and we stopped finding that scandalous, "uncountable" has no model-independent truth value, so the paradox is a pseudo-problem. She draws two further morals: that Skolem himself was right to read his relativity result as deflationary about set-theoretic talk generally, and that, since cardinality claims hold only relative to a model, the continuum hypothesis has no determinate truth value.

Begin by brainstorming the several distinct senses of "relative" that could be at work on each side. Then give me the correspondence table explicitly — what in set theory plays the role of the inertial frame, of the transformation between frames, of the observer, and of the invariant interval — and sort the disanalogies into those that genuinely undercut her inference and those that are merely cosmetic. Does it matter that a countable model is a different structure rather than the same universe viewed askew? Does it matter that Löwenheim–Skolem concerns the limits of first-order axiomatization while relativity of simultaneity concerns the physical world? Can absoluteness phenomena play the invariant's role in restoring objectivity, the way the spacetime interval does? Say, in the end, whether either of her morals survives the audit, and if the analogy fails, whether some better one captures the Skolemite predicament.

Throughout, mark clearly what is settled among people who work on this and what is genuinely contested — set-theoretic pluralism or a multiverse reading, for instance, is not consensus — and do not let a contested position enter silently as background.
