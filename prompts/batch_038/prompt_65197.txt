Here's my opening. In that grid exercise, the people who set it up end with a dozen completions that all look defensible, and then they keep the ones they like and write down constraints that make the choice look forced after the fact. The constraint set gets fitted to the answer, not the other way round — it's rationalisation wearing a lab coat. And the same thing happens at full size: your "hard constraints" on the a have to be rewritten between the humanist double-story, the geometric single-story, and whatever a blackletter scribe was doing with it. So what's actually doing the identifying isn't a constraint at all, it's the style, and once you admit identity is indexed to a style the overlapping-resemblances story has already won and your constraints are just bookkeeping.

Two things I want in your first reply: tell me concretely what a given pair of seed letters forces about the rest of the alphabet, and then give me a verdict on this one — a closed circular bowl with a straight vertical stem attached at the right, running down below the baseline. Is that an 'a'? Yes or no, and what decides it.
