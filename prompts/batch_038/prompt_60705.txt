I am helping to design a comparative research program on consciousness in systems without nervous systems — plant signalling, cortical organoids, distributed computation of the slime-mould kind — and one candidate metaphysical backdrop for that program is panpsychism, in whichever version can actually do the work. Before I either commit the program to it or set it aside, I want the specification such a theory must satisfy. Write the desiderata for an account of phenomenal composition: what a theory has to deliver in order to be usable in that setting, and in order to count as adequate at all.

For each requirement, give the consideration or case that motivates it, and then classify it as (i) non-negotiable for any theory in this area, (ii) negotiable in general but non-negotiable given the purpose above, or (iii) a prejudice of one particular camp that should not be imposed on the others. In the third class, name the camp doing the imposing.

The list should cover at least the following, and you should add whatever I have missed:

- What the theory must say about which composites are subjects and which are not, and whether the criterion offered is internal to the theory or a threshold calibrated to fit the human case.
- The demand that the character of a macro-level experience be explicable from the micro-level ones rather than merely correlated with them, together with the prior question of whether that demand is even coherent.
- The choice between treating micro-experiences as parts or constituents of a macro-experience and treating them as its causes, and the distinct problem each route generates.
- The asymmetry whereby we can apparently attend to the unified state but never to its alleged constituents, and what the theory owes by way of explaining that asymmetry rather than brute-stipulating it.
- Whether the micro-phenomenal properties are permitted to be causally inert, and whether the permissibility of that differs for a panpsychist than for a physicalist.
- The counting objection — that the view posits an extravagant population of subjects — including the question, prior to it, of whether counting subjects is a well-defined operation at this level.
- Behaviour under partial disruption: anaesthesia, callosal section, gradual degradation of integrating machinery, and any case in which a system loses half of what supposedly binds it.

Then give me what I most want to see: whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement eliminates the most candidates. If the constitutive route and the causal route are each knocked out by different members of the list, say so plainly, since that would mean the real choice is not among panpsychist theories but between panpsychism and something else.

Finally, audit the list for symmetry. A specification that pressures bottom-up views while leaving the top-down alternative — one mind whose states are differentiated, rather than many minds whose states are aggregated — untouched would be question-begging. Tell me whether any of the requirements has that defect, and whether the differentiated-unity view faces a mirror-image problem of comparable severity.

Score the main contenders briefly at the end — constitutive bottom-up panpsychism, the top-down alternative, and neutral or Russellian-style monism insofar as it competes for the same job — but keep each verdict to a few lines; the weight belongs on the requirements and their motivating cases. No bibliography, no citations, no reading list: refer to positions and arguments descriptively and I will supply the references myself.
