So I'm running a 2.5 hour workshop at a community philosophy festival, a couple of months out — maybe 22 adults, real mixed bag, about a third have taken a philosophy class at some point, the rest are just curious, and nobody in the room will know each other. I want to build the session around Bostrom's singleton idea and I need help designing it as an actual game rather than a talk.

What I want them to walk out seeing is not "one world authority = scary" and not "one world authority = the obvious fix," but the genuinely uncomfortable thing in the middle: that the very features which make a single coordinating authority good at solving problems nobody can solve alone (nobody can defect, nobody can opt out, one enforcement mechanism) are the same features that make it catastrophic if the values it happens to entrench are the wrong ones, with no external check left to correct it afterwards. I also want them to feel that the decision to create the thing is made under real uncertainty, by people who will be bound by it whether they voted for it or not.

The bit I care most about: they should run into it before anyone says the word. My strong preference is a two-phase structure where the payoff landscape literally changes between phases, so the institution they bargain for in phase one turns out to have different properties in phase two than the one they voted for. I'd also like a mechanism where whoever gets there first writes the standing rule and that rule then can't be touched by the ordinary amendment procedure — so lock-in and path dependence happen in their hands instead of on a diagram. And please build in some kind of dissenting faction for whom opting out is genuinely attractive, arranged so that the singleton's interest in compelling that faction becomes visible from the inside rather than being asserted by me.

Give me the roles and how to divide 22 people among them, whatever props or tokens I need (I can buy cheap stuff, but no real money in play), a point scheme that makes the tradeoffs actually bite, a minute-by-minute timeline for 150 minutes including a ten minute break, the text I'd physically hand to participants — which mustnt use the word singleton or anything like "global governance" — and then the debrief: the sequence of questions that gets them from "that was fun/infuriating" to the actual argument, plus which moment in the game maps onto which piece of the idea.

Two more things I always forget until it's too late. Tell me what will predictably break the first time I run it — someone always finds an exploit, and my groups tend to be either pathologically nice or savagely competitive — and how to patch it mid-session without breaking the point. And be honest about which feature of the idea the game fundamentally can't reproduce; I suspect it's timescale, since a singleton is supposed to persist for generations and mine lasts 90 minutes, but tell me what I have to supply verbally to paper over the gap.

Format request, sorry in advance: write the whole thing as continuous prose, like an email to me — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, even for the rules and the point scheme. I know that's perverse for a rules document, but I want to read it in one sitting with a pen.
