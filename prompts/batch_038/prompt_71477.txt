So I make little card games for my family and I've fallen down a rabbit hole that I don't think board game forums can help me with anymore, because it turned into a philosophy thing.

One of my games is called Lantern & Lock. Two players, the Burglar and the House, each secretly plays one of three cards and then you compare. The reference card has the victory points on it (Burglar first, House second — they always add up to 8):

House plays →   Hound    Lantern   Trap
Chimney          4,4      8,0      0,8
Window           2,6      3,5      3,5
Cellar           4,4      0,8      8,0

Chimney and Cellar are the flashy gambly ones. Window is the sensible middle card and for years everybody in my house plays Window basically by default, me included, because it never looks bad. And you can't point at any single card that beats it: Chimney gets crushed by Trap, Cellar gets crushed by Lantern.

Then my older kid, who does some math at school, told me that if I just flip a coin between Chimney and Cellar I get 4 points against Hound, 4 against Lantern and 4 against Trap, guarenteed — which beats Window's 3 no matter what the House plays. So Window isn't beaten by a card, it's beaten by a coin flip between two other cards. That felt like a magic trick and also like I've been playing my own game wrong for a decade.

That sent me reading about deleting dominated strategies and now I've got an argument going in my head I can't settle.

One side says: this is about as solid as it gets, because the coin flip doesn't need me to know anything at all about my opponent. My nephew always plays Trap like clockwork, and the coin flip still beats Window against Trap. So it can't be some fancy idealization, its just arithmetic.

Other side says: something feels off. I'm not really *choosing* the coin flip — the coin flip is a way of describing what the other guy can't predict, so calling it a "strategy" is a bit of a swindle. And once you delete more than one card you need the other player to be rational, and to know that you're rational, and to know that you know, and nobody at my kitchen table is like that. Plus I read that if you use weak dominance instead of strict, the answer depends on what order you delete things in and you can accidentally throw away a perfectly good equilibrium — so maybe the whole deletion business is shakier than it looks and I shouldn't feel bad about Window.

Write me an essay that takes this argument seriously from both directions and then actually lands somewhere. I want a verdict, not a shrug. Specifically:

1. Is "never play something that's beaten by a mixture of other things" a genuine requirement on a rational player, or is it an artifact of how the math is set up?
2. Does the fact that the coin flip beats Window even against a totally predictable nephew settle the issue, or am I missing what the worry was supposed to be?
3. Does the order-dependence / lost-equilibrium problem with weak dominance infect my Window case too, or are they really separate issues?
4. One thing that nags me: the numbers on the card are a fiction anyway. In real play Window might be worth 3.5 or Chimney 4.5, the table is fuzzy. Does fuzziness undermine the argument?
5. Practical: should I cut Window from the game?

Conditional verdicts are fine as long as you're explicit about the conditions. Don't bother with references or a reading list, I've never read any of this properly and just want your reasoning.
