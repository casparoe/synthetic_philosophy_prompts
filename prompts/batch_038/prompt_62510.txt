I run a bioethics block for second-year med students — 24 of them, one 110-minute session, none have done any philosophy, a few have already been on ICU wards. Design an in-class role-play that makes them collide with the problem of having two seperate criteria for death: irreversible loss of function of the entire brain, and irreversible cessation of circulatory and respiratory function. They should run into it themselves. Nobody says "dead donor rule" out loud until the debrief, and no lecture beforehand.

Here's the spine I want. Split them into three "determination committees," each working from a different rulebook: one that accepts whole-brain criteria, one that only requires brainstem death, and one where the family has exercised a religious objection to neurological criteria, so only circulatory determination is legally available to them. All three get the same packets, so the comparison happens at the end.

The bit that really matters: each committee has a recorder whose job is to write, in a fixed one-sentence template, what the patient's body is no longer doing that makes them dead. Collect the sentences. The reveal is them reading their own round-1 sentence next to their round-3 one.

Cases, roughly: (a) warm, pink, ventilated, own heartbeat, no brainstem reflexes, no cortical activity, family asking whether he's still in there; (b) brainstem infarct, apneic and areflexic, but reactive EEG — this one should split the committees; (c) controlled donation after circulatory death, with a fight over whether two minutes of no-touch or five is enough, an auto-resuscitation worry, and a plan to restart regional perfusion after declaration that the family asks whether it'll send blood back to the brain. Give one committee the knowledge that the transplant team is down the hall and that their paperwork gets audited.

Deliverables: role cards, the packets written out in full, the ledger sheet, a minute-by-minute timeline, a debrief mapping moments onto the problem, what predictably goes wrong the first time and how to steer out of it, and an honest note on which feature it can't reproduce. Also a version that works if only ten show up. No citations or reading lists — just stuff I can print.
