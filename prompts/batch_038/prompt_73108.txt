my school's philosophy club does this thing where you write down your actual position on a topic and then everybody else tries to break it, and I got stuck with the uploading / gradual replacement one. I want someone to check my list before I hand it in Thursday because I have a feeling parts of it don't line up but I honestly can't tell which. Here's what I believe:

1. Identity isn't really what matters. What matters is that whoever wakes up has my memories, my personality, my projects, all the psychological stuff hooked up in the right way. I'm fine with that coming in degrees — more connected = more of what matters.

2. But if they scan my brain, destroy it, and print an exact functional copy that wakes up mid sentence like nothing happened, that's death. The copy is a different guy who's convinced he's me.

3. If instead they swap my neurons out for silicon ones that do exactly the same job, one at a time over a couple months, I survive all of it. There's no moment where I stop existing, and that's kind of the whole point — there isn't some cutoff where the person disappears.

4. From the inside what I care about is that the lights stay on. Somebody is home the entire time, there's never a stretch where nothing's happening.

5. That said I obviously accept that sleep and anesthesia are fine, you don't die every night, so a gap in experience can't be fatal by itself.

6. My own proposal for how you'd actually run the swap: you can't replace more than like 1% of the units in a day. Go faster than that and something goes wrong. I can't say exactly what goes wrong, but I'm confident there's a rate you shouldn't exceed, and I'd want it written into the rules.

7. A case I made up for the club: every time they pull a neuron out they keep it alive in a tank, still wired to the other removed ones, and over the months the tank slowly reorganizes and starts having coherent memories too. At the end there's a silicon head and a tank of wetware and both of them claim to be me. I'm confident the answer is obviously the head, and the tank is just spare parts that got confused.

So — which of these actually contradict each other, and which ones just look bad sitting next to each other but are fine once you spell them out? Rank the real problems by how bad they are, I don't want a list where everything is flagged as equally broken. For each one tell me what would have to give, what the cheapest fix is, and what I lose by making that fix. Then tell me the single change that cleans up the most damage at once. Don't be nice about it, I want the damage report, not encouragement.
