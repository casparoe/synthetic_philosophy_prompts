So me and my friend Arun got properly into it last night about Berkeley's Querist — two hours of it on the walk home, and neither of us budged an inch. His take: the whole money business is immaterialism wearing a waistcoat, that when Berkeley asks "whether the true idea of money, as such, be not altogether that of a ticket or counter?" he's doing to coin exactly what he does to matter in the Principles, stripping it of any being except what it has in relation to a mind that counts it, so money is signs all the way down and the economics is just metaphysics keeping a ledger. My take: no, it's a standalone argument about function — circulation, velocity, the fact that a guinea locked in a drawer does nothing for anybody — and you can swallow it whole while thinking immaterialism is nonsense. He says I'm cherry-picking, I say he's over-reading. We agreed we'd each write something and compare, except I can't write poetry to save my life, so I'm cheating.

What I want is a poem in Berkeley's own form: a run of numbered querries, every single line interrogative, not one declarative anywhere in it, "Whether..." openings and all. Say twenty-five lines or so, no rhyme, no meter to speak of, since he didn't bother with either. The content has to actually track his argument in roughly his order — start from wealth being industry rather than treasure, and from the situation of a country with no mines of its own and plenty of hunger, which is the whole occasion of the book. Then get to the ticket-or-counter claim, and then hit the two moves I keep insisting are good economics and not metaphysics at all: "less money swiftly circulating, be not, in effect, equivalent to more money slowly circulating," and the one about the denominations being retained even if all the bullion were gone, with things still rated and bought and sold and commerce still going round. Work in at least one query pointed at hoarding and one at paper credit or a bank, and don't let any of them sound like a modern textbook — no "fiat," no "money supply," no M-anything.

The trick, and the reason I want a poem instead of an essay, is that every query has to be readable both ways: by someone who hears a hard-nosed point about how money's only job is to move labour around, and by someone who hears the metaphysics humming underneath. So plant the resonance without naming it — no "esse est percipi," no "immaterialism," no "ideas" as a term of art. Do it sideways, with something like a query about whether a ticket that nobody reckons with is a ticket at all, or about what a counter counts when the counting stops. Someone who's read the Principles should smirk somewhere around the last four lines; someone who hasn't should just get a decent poem about money. And the ending has to stay a question, because that's the point of the form — he never once commits, which is definately why Arun and I are still arguing about it. Give it a title, keep the diction period-ish but not costume-drama, and no "thee" or "thou" unless it earns its place.
