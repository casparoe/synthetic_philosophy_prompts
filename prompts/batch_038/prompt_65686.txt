Could you write me a two-hander dialogue — just the two speakers, no narrator, no stage directions — for a dataset I'm putting together?

The setup: a regulatory panel has to fill in one blank on a standard form. It's a "hedonic offset schedule" used to score public projects: for every unit of net pleasure a project is expected to create, how many units of suffering averted must it deliver before the whole thing counts as an improvement? One panellist is a negative utilitarian of the lexical-threshold sort, who thinks the blank can't take any finite number at all — pleasure and pain aren't on one scale, so the schedule is either incoherent or should just read "no exchange rate". The other is a committed commensurabilist: one scale, one currency, and they reckon the negative utilitarian's refusal gives up exactly the continuity you need for anything to be measurable, comparable or choosable under uncertainty — plus a lexically prior pain will happily bankrupt civilisation to spare somebody a hangnail.

Neither should be a strawman, and they have to actually answer each other rather than take turns reciting their positions. I'd like the exchange to get properly into the weeds on at least three of these: whether the continuity/Archimedean objection really bites or just assumes the conclusion; where the threshold is meant to sit — intensity, duration, or the kind of experience — and whether a sorites over ever-smaller pains wrecks it; how any of this aggregates across different people rather than within a single life; and the extinction worry, i.e. whether the lexical view makes an empty universe the best one going. Let them shift ground a bit — a concession, a refinement, someone admitting a case they cant handle — but dont resolve it, and don't let either of them win.

Hard cap: 700 words. Speaker labels only (give them names, not A and B), and keep each turn short enough that it reads like two people in a room rather than two interleaved essays.
