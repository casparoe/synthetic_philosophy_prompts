I am a junior in high school. For two years I have taken comparative government, and for three years I have done nearly all of my serious thinking about systems through software: I write Python and TypeScript, I maintain my robotics team's drive and sensor stack, and last summer I built a small distributed job queue with one scheduler process, heartbeats, and a retry policy for workers that stop answering. I am not a professional architect — I learn from documentation and from breaking things — but words like coupling, interface, single point of failure, deadlock, watchdog, rollback, bus factor, and graceful degradation are genuinely native to me, considerably more so than the vocabulary of political science.

What I want is a translation, and then an accounting of what the translation fails to carry.

Please restate the standard case that parliamentary government is more durable than presidential government in the vocabulary of my software work, claim by claim, as faithfully as you can manage. The claims I would like handled individually are: that a separately elected executive and a separately elected legislature both speak for the same electorate and there is no agreed procedure for settling which one wins when they disagree; that fixed terms make the system rigid, because a failing executive cannot be replaced before the next scheduled release and a crisis cannot be resolved by calling an early election; that presidential contests are zero-sum in a way that leaves the losing side with nothing and raises the stakes of every contest; that candidate-centered campaigns favor outsiders and personalist leadership over disciplined organizations; and, on the other side, the rebuttal I have read arguing that a Westminster-style system is in fact the more winner-take-all arrangement, because a disciplined single-party majority can govern without building any coalition at all, and because a party can remove its own leader between elections without asking anyone.

For each of these, give me the mapping, and then a fidelity verdict: does it translate cleanly, does it translate only if I let a term stretch beyond its engineering meaning, or is there no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? Separately, and just as important, list every point at which the mapping only appears to work because you quietly imported an engineering assumption — that obstruction is a defect, that throughput is what a system is for, that a component no one can replace is a liability, that a monitoring layer should be independent of what it monitors — rather than because the two domains genuinely match. I suspect this translation will smuggle in an efficiency norm that the political argument does not share, and I would rather see that flagged than absorbed silently.

Two puzzles of my own I would like you to address inside the translation rather than around it. First: everything I have been taught says never to build two components that can each block the other with no tiebreaker and no timeout, because that is deadlock by construction — yet a presidential system seems to have exactly that shape, with the next election as the only timeout. Is that a real disanalogy, or am I missing what the deadlock is doing? Second: the confidence mechanism that parliamentarism is praised for is not an independent watchdog. It is triggered from inside the majority that supports the executive, so it is closer to a process monitoring itself, which I would normally call the weaker design. Does the software vocabulary capture that inversion honestly, or does it make parliamentarism look better than the argument deserves simply because fast feedback sounds good in my language?

Finally, my own hypothesis, which I would like you to evaluate rather than endorse: that the whole dispute reduces to how many independent actors must approve a change of state and what happens when they refuse, so that presidentialism and parliamentarism differ in parameter values rather than in kind. Does my vocabulary state that reframing more cleanly than the standard typology does, or does stating it that way lose something the typology was tracking?

End with the residue report as a verdict, not a summary. When something in this debate has no expression in how I think about systems, tell me whether that exposes a limit in my framework or reveals that the original claim was less clear than it sounded. I am not looking for a set of pleasing correspondences; I want the honest account of where the translation breaks, because I expect that is where the interesting part is.
