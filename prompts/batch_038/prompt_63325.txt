Now change the valence, so the program helps the environment and the chairman says he doesn't care about that either, and only 23% say he helped intentionally. Nothing in the story has changed except whether the side effect is good or bad, so the difference has to be coming from the subjects' attitudes rather than from any fact about the chairman. That is a decisive refutation of the idea that intentional action is a neutral, descriptive notion you apply on the basis of what the agent wanted, what she believed, and what she did. It shows that when we say someone did something on purpose we are not describing the world at all, we are voicing blame — and because the asymmetry turns up in preschoolers and in samples from lots of different cultures, it is a fact about human minds rather than an artifact of the English word 'intentionally.' Philosophers have been treating their own intuitions as evidence for two thousand years. Knobe showed that those intuitions are just moralized snap judgments, which is why conceptual analysis now has to be handed over to psychology."
