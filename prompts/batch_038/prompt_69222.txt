I cover science and ideas for a mid-size magazine — evolution, cognition, the occasional philosophy of biology thing — and I'm working on a piece about the units-of-selection fight, the genes-versus-organisms-versus-groups argument that's somehow still running. My editor says I can have one sidebar that's just a joke, as long as its the kind of joke where the biology *is* the punchline rather than the set dressing. No "science is complicated, har har," no dumbing-down gags.

So: a job advertisement, about 700 words, for the position of Unit of Selection. Employer listed only as "the population," or something you think is funnier. Deadpan HR-and-academic-hiring voice all the way through — role summary, essential criteria, desirable experience, disqualifying circumstances, compensation and benefits, how to apply, and a grievance-history box at the bottom.

The comedy has to come from the actual structure of the debate, and none of it should ever be explained. Please work in:

- the requirement that the successful candidate be something that gets copied with heritable variation in how many copies get made, and can be *credited* with its own contribution rather than with whatever the neighborhood it happened to sit in did;
- the long-serving incumbent who has apparently held the post for most of the history of life while working remotely, denies ever applying, and points out that the copies are what persist and the job title is a bookkeeping convenience;
- an applicant from the group level whose references are all drawn from situations where competition inside the group was somehow switched off, and whose candidacy keeps falling apart the moment a recruiter asks how the group itself gets reproduced rather than merely out-competed;
- an HR policy of pluralism, under which every applicant is told the role has been split into two shares that are both being filled simultaneously and that the disagreement was never really substantive;
- a small-print clause covering what the employer will do if a cheater turns up inside a group whose application has been shortlisted.

For compensation, riff on immortality-by-proxy: the successful candidate will never strictly die, although essentially every copy will. I'd also like three or four lines in the "unsuccessful applicants" note about a species-level candidate rejected for failing to demonstrate that it exists in any form the recruiter could audit, and one line in the grievance box about a very old claim that the title was stolen from an organism by a consortium of smaller entities that have been doing the work by proxy ever since.

Target is the debate and the way it's argued, not any living person. Don't use real biologists, real papers, or real institutions — invented names welcome, but nothing that implies anybody endorsed this. A smart non-biologist should laugh at the surface while a biologist laughs at the mechanics. American English, please.
