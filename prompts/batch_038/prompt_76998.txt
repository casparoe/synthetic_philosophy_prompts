Quick one before I lock in slides for my seminar on the epistemics of solution concepts. I've been telling the students that iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies — where you let mixed strategies do the dominating — is really just belief talk in disguise: a strategy drops out exactly when no belief about the other players could justify it. Then a student asked whether it makes any difference if we let players hold *correlated* beliefs about the others' joint moves instead of independent ones. I said it cant, on the grounds that a correlated belief is just a weighted average of product beliefs, and if your strategy is a best response to the average, it had to be a best response to at least one of the things being averaged. So the two belief assumptions give you the same surviving set. She looked unconvinced and I couldn't press the point any further in the moment.

So — am I right? If not, where exactly does the inference break, and what does a game have to look like for the two assumptions to genuinely come apart (and why does the number of players matter here, if it does)? Also tell me what would change your mind on this, or where you think your own reasoning is most likely to be slipping.
