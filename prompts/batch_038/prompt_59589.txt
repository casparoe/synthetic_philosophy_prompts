our history-of-economic-thought reading group ends on Berkeley's Querist and i want something to read aloud to close it, done in his own gimmick: the whole poem is questions, never a single assertion, and the questions have to carry the argument. five numbered queries, two or three lines each, nothing before or after — no title, no note explaining what you were up to.

content it needs to smugle in through the interrogatives: money as a mere ticket or counter — Berkeley's own query asks whether the true idea of money is not "altogether that of a ticket or counter" — so that what it's struck from doesn't matter; wealth as the power to set other people's industry going rather than a hoard of gold; less money moving fast beating more money moving slow; worthless paper doing the whole job provided it keeps circulating, while bullion shut in a chest does nothing at all.

then the fifth query should sit on the thing people actually fight over in Berkeley: whether the force of his money comes from the state that stamps and names it (the chartalist reading) or from circulation, fashion, and the wants people are taught to have. leave that one genuinely two-sided, don't tip it.

two failure modes to avoid: modern macro vocabulary in a wig — velocity, monetary base, liquidity preference — and a sermon about greed. someone who's actually read the Querist should be able to point at which query is doing which bit of work.
