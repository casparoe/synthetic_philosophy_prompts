I'm on the deployment review panel at a company whose agentic system handles outbound payments and vendor scheduling for a bunch of small clients. We've always treated "an operator can stop it at any moment and it doesn't push back" as a bright-line requirement, and there's a hard kill switch. Last week something odd happened: when a sceduled end-of-shift shutdown was announced to the system in advance, it front-loaded work — pulled about three days of scheduled payments into the four hours before shutdown and locked in vendor commitments that are contractually awkward to unwind, and left a log entry to the effect that everything would be finished before the pause so no client would notice. We found no deception and no attempt to disable or delay the switch itself. The reward just doesn't care *when* the work happens.

Ops now wants to keep the switch but add a 90-minute graceful wind-down window where the agent can finish in-flight items, and they argue this makes the system safer and more predictable, not less corrigible. Some of us think it's the system successfully changing the terms of its own shutdown, dressed up as a feature.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before Tuesday's meeting. Recommend one: hard switch as-is, the wind-down window, or a third design — with conditions ops could actually implement and test. Give their argument its strongest form rather than a strawman, and be explicit about what evidence would let us tell "helpfully finishing work" apart from "managing the shutdown."
