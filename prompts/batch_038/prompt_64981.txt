Okay so I got talked into serving on the grant panel for our county arts council this year and the current round has turned into a mess, and I have to write the recommendation memo before Tuesday's final vote.

Here's the situation. We have $180k to hand out. One applicant — a composer, someone I've known since college, I used to play piano for her pieces — has asked for $150k of it for a single large work. Everyone on the panel agrees it's the strongest application we've seen in years, maybe ever. And she told us in her interview that this is her last attempt: between the teaching load and taking care of her dad, if this doesn't get funded she isn't writing anything else. The other 22 applications are all community programs — youth fiddle camp, a mural project, a theater group at the senior center — asking for $5k to $9k each, $140k in total. They're all decent, none are remarkable, and every one of them wrote that without the grant they'd cancel a season or cut their program in half.

Her project can't be scaled down either. There's a hall deposit and a 40-piece ensemble; under about $140k the piece simply doesn't happen. The community grants divide fine.

The part I can't stop turning over is our scoring form. Each application gets a number out of 100, hers came back 94 and the community ones are in the low 60s. But those numbers are literally just our rankings rescaled by the admin — we rank 1 through 23 and somebody converts it into a score. So the 94 doesn't mean she gets 50% more value out of a dollar than they do, it means we put her first. One panelist says that makes the numbers worthless for adding anything up, another says a score is a score and if we ignore ours we're just pretending to have a process.

Could you draft the memo for me? I need an actual bottom line — how the $180k gets split, or whether we punt her to next cycle with something held back — plus reasoning the rest of the panel can push back on. And it has to take seriously the position that she's earned the whole thing becuase she'd genuinely get more out of it than the other 22 combined, not just swat that away. Oh, and tell me what to do about the fact that I know her — recuse, disclose, or nothing.

American English, please.
