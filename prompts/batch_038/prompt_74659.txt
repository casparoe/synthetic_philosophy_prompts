I'm about two thirds of the way through a novel — literary, not sci-fi, though there's a chatbot in it — and one of my characters is a retired philosopher of mind, a bit of a public-intellectual type, who has to explain in one kitchen-table scene why he thinks the griefbot his sister has been talking to every night for a year is a moral catastrophe. I've been reading around, and a long piece in a Sunday magazine summed up the Dennett position like this, and I more or less lifted its shape for his speech:

"Dennett spent forty years arguing that there's no theater in the head — no place where it all comes together — and no self either, just a story a brain tells about itself to make its own behavior intelligible. So when the chatbots showed up he wasn't worried about their inner lives at all. He called them philosophical zombies: all the competence, none of the comprehension, nothing it is like to be them. He took Searle's Chinese room to have settled the point that syntax can never turn into semantics, which is also why he always thought the Turing test was a hollow trophy — pass it and all you've shown is that you can fool someone for five minutes. But since on his own view a person is nothing over and above a center of narrative gravity, there's no principled line between the story a chatbot spins and the story you spin, so we're all going to be taken in, and once people start taking instruction from text that isn't anybody, democracy is finished. Which is why he ended up arguing that AI should be banned."

I'm not trying to write an essay, I just dont want to be laughed at by the one reviewer who'll know. Can you go through this the way someone who actually works on philosophy of mind would, and tell me which sentences are Dennett, which are the magazine writer smoothing him into something more quotable, and which are just wrong? - Does the "no self, therefore no difference between you and the bot" move actually work, or does it flatten the thing he was really worried about? I keep feeling like the counterfeiting happens somewhere other than inside the machine and the article missed it.
