Our vote isn't binding but the principal actually reads it, which is rarer than you'd think.

In my philosophy elective we read this essay from 1980 about whether artifacts have politics. The part that stuck with me is that it says there are two different kinds of politics a thing can have: one where the design or arrangement of a thing is basically how a community settles some fight it was having, and another where a thing only works at all if you organize people around it in a particular way, whether anyone planned that or not. I've been trying to use that on our committee and it's going badly. Half the time the teachers go "you're being paranoid, a turnstile is a turnstile," and the other half the time my friends go "you let the phone pouches through, so now you're nitpicking everything." Which is fair. I genuinely don't have a way to tell the difference between "this thing has politics in it" and "this thing has effects, like every thing has effects."

So can you write me a procedure we could actually run in a meeting? The questions to ask in order about a proposal, what would count as a good enough answer to each one so we aren't just arguing about vibes, and — this is the part I keep failing — a stopping rule: what makes us say nope, not political, this is just a thing, move on. If everything turns out political then the concept isn't doing any work and the teachers are right that i'm annoying.

Here's what we've actually had this year so you have real material: magnetic pouches that lock your phone for the whole day and only unlock at the front office; an AI checker English teachers run essays through; keycard turnstiles at the two side doors, where the card costs $15 to replace and a parent has to sign to activate it; an app replacing the paper sign-out sheet for leaving campus during free period, which also logs where you go; and a cafeteria app that lets you preorder and skip the line while the hot meal line stays for people who don't have it.

Run the procedure on two of those. Please pick one where it lands against what I want — I hate the pouches and I like the cafeteria app, so if the procedure says the pouches are fine or the app is loaded, I want to see that instead of you telling me what I already think.
