My friend has this little one-woman zine and the running feature is a column where she rates one creature or machine a month as either SPHEXISH or NOT SPHEXISH — sphexishness being her word (she got it from Hofstadter) for that wasp-style trap where the routine keeps running even though the point of it has evaporated. I've been keeping a list of her ratings, and here they are:

- The digger wasp itself. It drags its paralyzed cricket to the burrow entrance, leaves it there, goes in alone to check the nest, comes out to drag the prey in. Shift the cricket a few inches while it's inside and you get the whole loop again, and again, apparently forever. SPHEXISH.
- A robot vacuum of the dumb variety. It bumps, turns, bumps, and it happily goes over the same square metre of floor for an hour with a patch of spilled cat litter sitting right there. SPHEXISH.
- A 2009-era sat nav. Miss your turn and it replans cheerfully. Except its map is years out of date and there's no live data, so it sends you straight into a road that's been closed for six months, every single time, with total confidence. Her verdict was hedged: not sphexish in its driving, sphexish about its map.
- An Excel macro she inherited that runs all fourteen steps whether or not step three failed, and reports success at the end. SPHEXISH.
- A top chess engine she played fifty games against. It never once learned anything about her. Game fifty was analysed by exactly the same evaluation as game one, and it found resources in every game that she would never have seen. SPHEXISH.
- A text generator that produced a passage she could not trace to anything it had been trained on, and which, when she fed the passage back to it a day later, did not recognise as its own. NOT SPHEXISH.
- A nurse working a sepsis checklist who keeps going down the checklist in order even though the patient in front of her has changed in a way the checklist doesn't have a box for. SPHEXISH.
- A guide dog that refuses to cross at the curb because a car is coming, disobeying the owner's command to do exactly that. NOT SPHEXISH.
- A warehouse sorter that deals with objects it's never seen before, then jams on a box two centimetres over the size limit, retrying the same chute eleven times and unable to flag a human. SPHEXISH.
- A learning thermostat, three weeks in. It starts pre-heating on the mornings you're actually home, stops on the ones you're not, and eventually pops up a message saying your pattern seems to have changed. NOT SPHEXISH.

The new one for next month, which I've already sent her: a hospital robot porter. Its navigation is genuinely lovely — it yields in corridors, waits for the trolley, backs off and takes the long way round, handles stuff it's never encountered. But if the ward door is locked when it arrives, it waits exactly ten minutes, then carries the load back to the lab it came from and leaves it on a shelf there. Nobody at the lab is told it's coming. The load in question is a biopsy that needs to be kept cold and got to theatre within the hour, and the shelf isn't cold. It does this identically every time, and the ten-minute wait-and-return is in the manual as a deliberate failsafe.

So — what do you think she'll print? I'm not really after the answer, I'm after the machinery. What rule is she actually running, what other rules fit all ten of those ratings just as well, and what does each of them say about the porter? Give me your best guess with the reasons you'd prefer it over the rivals, name which of her ten ratings is doing the most work in discriminating between the rules, and tell me one further case — something she hasn't rated yet — that would settle it if you showed it to her. And if you think the porter one is a coin flip for her, say what she'd be committing herself to whichever way she lands, especially given that she already split her verdict once on the sat nav.
