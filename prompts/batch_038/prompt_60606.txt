Our series is built on time travel, and I'm the continuity coordinator who has to answer the writers before a pitch turns into a draft. I get two or three of these a week. A wrong yes costs us a rewrite six weeks later; a wrong no costs us a good story. The showrunner signs off on everything, so my note has to be something she can veto in one line, and the writers have no philosophy training — they argue in terms of "can you change the past or not."

Our canon is fixed and not open for renegotiation: the past can be changed; when it changes, only the protagonist remembers the previous version; and whatever he is holding at the moment of change comes through with him.

Write me a procedure I can run on a pitch in about thirty minutes: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, and which verdict each branch ends in — clear, clear with a condition attached, needs rewrite, or not a logic problem at all but a creative call I hand up to the showrunner. Draw on whatever the philosophy and physics of time travel actually has that survives contact with a writers' room, and tell me which distinctions you are keeping, which you are dropping as unusable on a shooting schedule, and where my own judgment is doing the work while looking like a rule.

Then run it on three pitches we keep circling back to:

(a) a character travels back, meets his mentor as a young man, and hands him the technique that the mentor is shown teaching him in season one;

(b) the protagonist prevents his parents from ever meeting, and we cut to him in the present, unchanged and still remembering, standing in the house he grew up in;

(c) a traveler kills the antagonist as a child in 1994, in a season whose present-day scenes — already shot — treat the antagonist as alive and working until last month.

Be honest about where the procedure will misfire: the pitches it waves through that it shouldn't, and the ones it kills for the wrong reason. I want one procedure you would stand behind, with your reasons for the calls it makes, not a survey of what each theory of time travel would say about our show.
