I'm trying to settle a long-running disagreement with my younger sister, and I'd like a second opinion on whether the experiment we ran actually proved anything. Background: we were raised in a nondenominational evangelical church, I drifted away in college, and for about a decade my sister has alternated between grieving my unbelief and pressing me to come back. Last January I proposed a deal, half in earnest and half to end the argument. I would spend six months doing what she said a sincere seeker does: twenty minutes of prayer each morning, said aloud, asking God to make himself known to me if he exists; church every Sunday; a chapter of Scripture daily; and a journal in which I recorded each evening whether I had felt anything at all that I'd be tempted to read as a response. We agreed in advance, in writing, that if at the end of six months I could not point to anything in that journal that looked like an answer — a felt presence, a coincidence I couldn't explain, an unmistakable pull — she would accept that I had genuinely tried and stop bringing it up, and I would treat the question as settled in the negative. I work as a data analyst, so I was rather pleased with myself for having pre-registered the whole thing.

The six months are up. There was no felt presence and no unmistakable pull. There were two events I'd have to be dishonest not to mention: a morning in April when I felt an unexpected wave of calm during prayer that lasted several minutes, and a week in June when three separate people, unconnected to one another, told me to be patient about something I'd been anxious over. I said beforehand that neither would count, and I say the same now — the calm is roughly what twenty minutes of slow breathing in a quiet room produces, and all three of them knew me well enough to guess what was weighing on me. My sister says the opposite: that the calm was the answer, that the protocol was satisfied and I'm moving the goalposts, and that in any case I went in with the verdict already written, which she says voids the whole thing because God is not obliged to perform on demand for someone who has decided in advance what will count. She adds that plenty of people she considers far holier than either of us have gone for years feeling nothing whatsoever, so the absence of felt presence proves nothing even on my own terms. Her pastor, separately, has told her that my unbelief is a matter of the will rather than the evidence — that I am not really unable to believe but unwilling to.

Here is what complicates my own side of the ledger. Around month four I noticed that I was opening the journal each evening with a kind of dread, and I realized I was partly hoping that nothing would happen, because a null result was the outcome that won the argument for me. I told myself this was mere competitiveness and that I was still performing every step of the protocol faithfully. I mention it because I genuinely cannot tell whether it is a confession or a red herring. So, three questions, and I'd like you to be blunt rather than diplomatic. First, how much evidential weight should I actually assign the null result? I assumed at the outset that a loving personal God who wanted a relationship with me would not stay silent through six months of open, sincere asking, and that the silence would therefore count heavily against his existence; I'm now unsure whether either of us was entitled to that assumption, since it looks like precisely the thing her tradition denies. Second, is her objection — that fixing in advance what will count as evidence renders the test worthless — correct, or is it an escape hatch that makes her position impossible to disprove? Both seem true at once and I don't know how to hold them together. Third, given that the deal has settled nothing, what should I actually say to her, and is there anything worth saying to the claim that my difficulty is volitional rather than intellectual? I am not looking for reassurance in either direction; I want to know whether I reasoned badly, and where.
