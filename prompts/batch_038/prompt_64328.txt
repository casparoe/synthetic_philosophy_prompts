I never studied philosophy; I picked up a used copy of Nozick's *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* years ago and the Wilt Chamberlain chapter is the part I've gone back to most often. As I understand it: start from a distribution that has whatever shape you think is fair — call it D1 — then have a basketball star arrange to collect twenty-five cents from every ticket, have a million people happily pay it over a season, and he wakes up with a quarter of a million dollars and a new distribution, D2, that no longer matches the pattern. Nozick's point, as I read it, is that you can only get back to the pattern by undoing the voluntary transfers or by repeating that undoing forever, since the same thing happens again next season. The detail that keeps me up is the third part of his own theory: he admits that justice also requires rectifying holdings that were taken unjustly in the first place, which means the tidy D1 the argument starts from was never actually clean.

I would like a poem that puts this in a pantoum, roughly six quatrains, so that the form can carry the argument instead of just describing it. The second and fourth lines of each stanza should reappear as the first and third lines of the next, and the final stanza should close the loop by bringing back the opening stanza's first and third lines as its own second and fourth lines. What I'm hoping the repetition will show is that the pattern is always being rebuilt out of the material of its own disruption, and that restoring it lands you exactly where you started, needing to do it once more; each reused line should sit in a new place and pick up a slightly different sense. Please move from D1 through the box of quarters to D2, and let the closing loop be where the rectification problem surfaces — the hint that the arrangement at the beginning was already standing on stolen ground. Keep the jargon out (no "end-state principles" or "historical entitlement"), render the distinction in images, and don't resolve the tension in favor of either side; someone who knows the argument should be able to see which move each stanza is making.
