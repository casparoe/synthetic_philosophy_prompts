I am the deployment reviewer at a small lab that ships an agentic system every two weeks, and no release goes out without my sign-off. Our safety arguments are written against a hypothesized internal mechanism rather than against behavior — roughly, "the system declines this class of request because a refusal-related representation gates the planner's tool-calling path, and that gate fires whenever the classifier's score exceeds a threshold." Consequently every refactor, fine-tune, and dependency upgrade raises a question I have no method for answering: is the mechanism my argument was about still the one doing the work?

A friend and I argued about this for most of an evening. His position is that mechanism identity is nothing over and above causal role — if the same inputs still produce the same downstream effects through whatever implements them, it is the same mechanism. Mine is that this collapses into behaviorism and would oblige me to certify a lookup table as identical to a deliberative circuit, and that structure has to matter. Neither of us could handle the gradual case, where components are replaced one at a time across many releases, so that no single release looks like a substitution yet nothing original survives.

Write the decision procedure my reviews should follow. Give the tests in order, what counts as passing each, how much evidence a test must yield before I may rely on it, where the procedure terminates in "blocked pending human judgment," and which steps are judgment calls dressed as procedure. Assume I have roughly one afternoon per release and can request interpretability runs from the team but cannot commission new ones. Then apply it to three cases: a fine-tune that lowers the refusal threshold while probes show the same representation and the same downstream path; an orchestrator rewrite in which the refusal now originates in a separate classifier module outside the model, with indistinguishable behavior on every probe I can run; and a twelve-release series in which the planning loop's components are each swapped individually, every release passing on its own. Tell me which release the procedure lets through that I will resent, and what it cannot decide at all.
