I spent 34 years doing trace-metal analysis for an agricultural lab — mostly graphite furnace work, mostly mud — and since retiring I've fallen into philosophy, which my wife says is worse than the golf habit I never took up. Our reading group did Hume's essay on the standard of taste last week (the 1757 one, the dissertation in Four Dissertations) and we spent two hours shouting at each other about whether beauty is objective. Then I got volunteered to judge the village photography prize and the wine club annual tasting in the same month. Which is why Im asking you for a piece of satire.

Write a parody job advertisement, about 650 words, from an invented accreditation body — name it whatever you like, something bland and institutional — recruiting a certified judge of taste. Deadpan HR register throughout: about us, the role, essential criteria, desirable criteria, salary and benefits, assessment task, how to apply. No winking, no authorial elbow in the ribs; the reader should be able to tell what's being sent up from the content alone. The target is Hume's solution and the whole business of taste-certification, not any real person.

The essential criteria should be Hume's marks of the true judge rendered into recruitment boilerplate — good sense, delicacy sharpened by practice, a wide habit of comparison, freedom from prejudice — and the jokes should come from the philosophy being got right rather than from "art is subjective, ha ha." Things I want squeezed in, in your own words:

- the circularity: the board certifies a judge by whether his verdicts match the panel's, and the panel's verdicts are the standard, with a frank admission that there's no independent way of telling who qualifies;
- the rareness: properly qualified judges are vanishingly scarce, so the post has been vacant for years, and the budget line for practice and comparison should definately look obscene;
- the test of time: only works that have been around a couple of centuries are eligible for verdicts, which needs its own clause about new work;
- beauty existing in the mind that contemplates it, and the existence of real general principles of taste, stated as two separate bullets with a note that the board sees no tension;
- the prejudice clause, in which the sitting panel's own prejudices get recorded as settled findings;
- an assessment task built out of Sancho's story about the two relatives and the hogshead of wine — retell it inside the ad, and ask candidates what evidence would have changed the relatives' verdict.

Don't invent quotations and don't give me a bibliography or reading list; everything you need is above. Two sections, in this order: "The Posting" (the ad itself, ~650 words, including a short sidebar box about the board), then "What I Was Poking At" (~200 words), telling me which actual feature of Hume's argument each of those clauses exaggerates, so I can check you know the essay and aren't just doing generic art-world satire. I don't want to be unfair to him — the exaggerations should be real features, pushed until they hurt.
