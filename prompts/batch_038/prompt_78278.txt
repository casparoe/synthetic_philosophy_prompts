Our lab's technical review board has been asked to sign, before each capability release, a statement to the effect that the alignment problem has been adequately addressed for this system. Nobody on the board can say what would make that statement true, and we have decided to fix the specification before we evaluate any proposed solution. I want you to write the specification.

Four constraints make the exercise hard, and every desideratum you propose should be checked against them: the board has a finite verification budget and cannot run unlimited tests; we are not entitled to assume interpretability access to the system's internals; the account must be statable in advance of knowing what the system will be deployed to do; and it must support a decision that is closer to binary than to scalar, even if the underlying property is graded.

Deliverable: the desiderata that any adequate account of what the alignment problem *is* must meet in order to serve that purpose. For each, give the concrete case that motivates it — construct the cases yourself, in enough detail that a reader can see exactly what goes wrong; do not gesture at well-known published anecdotes — and say why the case makes the requirement necessary rather than merely desirable. Cover at minimum: what the account must take the object of alignment to be, and why that choice is not arbitrary; what it must say about the gap between an externally specified objective and whatever is actually implemented internally; behavior that holds only while the system is being observed, or only on the distribution of situations it was built in; principals whose preferences change after the system exists, and cases where the people deploying the system are not the people affected by it; a system that resists correction or shutdown for reasons that sound safety-relevant to it; and what can be demanded by way of operationalizable checks given the finite budget.

Three things matter more than the length of the list. First, the counting question: state whether your specification treats this as one problem or several, give the criterion by which you are counting, and if several, specify each component's interface to the others — I want to be able to detect when a claimed solution to one component has silently assumed another component solved. Second, identify the largest subset of your desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied under the four constraints, and say which member you would drop and exactly what that costs the certification. Third, tell me which requirements people in this area commonly insist upon are not genuine desiderata but the local preferences of one camp — and include at least one requirement nobody asks for that our purpose nonetheless forces on us.

No citations, no paper titles, no reading list; argue from the cases.

Format: at most 150 words of prose orienting the standard of adequacy, then a single JSON object, and nothing after it. Schema:

- `adequacy_standard`: string — one or two sentences on what "adequate" is relative to here.
- `decomposition`: object with `verdict` (one problem / several / one problem with separable components), `counting_criterion` (string), and `components` (array of objects: `name`, `failure_mode_addressed`, `interface_to_other_components`, `how_to_detect_a_smuggled_assumption`).
- `desiderata`: array of at least ten objects, each with `id` (D1, D2, …), `statement`, `test_case` (the constructed scenario, self-contained), `why_necessary`, `status` (non-negotiable / negotiable / camp-relative), `who_resists_it` (which family of views would reject or downweight it, and on what grounds), `exclusion_power` (high / medium / low, relative to the accounts people actually defend).
- `unsatisfiable_set`: object with `ids`, `why_jointly_unsatisfiable`, `member_to_drop`, `cost_of_dropping_it`.
- `most_exclusionary`: object with `id` and `reason` — the single requirement that knocks out the most candidates.
- `camp_prejudices`: array of objects with `putative_desideratum`, `camp_it_belongs_to`, `why_it_is_not_a_real_requirement`.
- `neglected_requirement`: object with `statement` and `why_our_purpose_forces_it`.
- `family_scorecard`: array of at most four objects with `family` (e.g. specification-and-learning approaches, evaluation-driven approaches, interpretability-first approaches, sociotechnical-process approaches), `first_desideratum_it_fails`, `one_line_verdict`. Keep this section short; the weight belongs on the criteria.
