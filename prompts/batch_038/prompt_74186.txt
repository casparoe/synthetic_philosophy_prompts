Can you write me a parody job advertisement for the post of "Unit of Selection," followed by the hiring panel's shortlisting notes? Setting: the vacancy sits at something like the Institute of Adaptive Bookkeeping, has been advertised continuously since 1859, and has never yet been filled.

The ad should have the ordinary sections — about us, essential criteria, person specification — and the criteria should be the real ones (high-fidelity replication, a defensible answer to where your boundaries start and stop, the ability to actually bear fitness rather than merely transmit it), stated so blandly that you only notice how brutal they are on a second read.

Then the shortlist. Applicants I want in there: the Gene, self-nominated, insisting it's the only serious replicator, but with an awkward CV gap around how it individuates itself across recombination and a habit of claiming credit for the beaver's dam; the Organism, the incumbent, who genuinely faces the environment and resents the job title; a Trait Group, which submits a reference letter asserting that it reproduces by fission and which HR cant verify; and a Species, screened out at the first pass but allowed a paragraph of appeal arguing that sorting is still selection. Someone should also apply who isn't a candidate at all — a contextual-analysis contractor offering to dissolve the posting entirely by regressing individual fitness on individual and group traits.

The humour has to come from getting the biology and the philosophy right: the replicator/interactor distinction, MLS1 versus MLS2, and that Price-equation partition where almost any applicant can claim to have been promoted depending on how you carve the covariance terms. Deadpan HR voice throughout, no winking, 650-700 words. The target is the way the debate keeps sliding between 'who does the replicating' and 'who gets the fitness' while treating them as one question — but the piece must never say that out loud. Close with the panel's recommendation, which should be either an offer to everyone or no offer to anyone, and it's funnier if the reader can't immediately tell which.
