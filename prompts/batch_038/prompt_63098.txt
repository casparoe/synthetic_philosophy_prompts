I am a junior in high school, and for the past fourteen months I have been running a small resale business out of my bedroom: I buy used vintage clothing in bulk lots at estate sales, photograph the pieces, and sell them individually online. I have a decision to make this weekend that I cannot settle on my own, and I want to lay out the situation as exactly as I can, because the advice I have been given keeps sounding confident without sounding true.

I started with $600 of birthday money. My method has been to bring essentially all of it to every sale and buy the largest lot I could get for it. There have been twelve lots. Nine sold for between 1.8 and 3 times what I paid. Two roughly broke even after fees and shipping. One — a very large lot of heavily stained denim that I could not resell — returned about a quarter of its cost, and took my bankroll from roughly $1,900 down to $475. It took me five months and seven smaller purchases to climb back to the $2,400 I have now.

On Saturday there is an estate sale two hours away with a collection I have seen photographed in detail, and I am as sure as I have ever been about one of these that it would resell for close to three times the $2,300 asking price. My older cousin, who does this at a much larger scale, tells me that if I really believe that, then anything less than all-in is just refusing to make money; he says the only question worth asking is what the average outcome would be if I could run the same purchase a hundred times, and that a purchase with a good average is one you should make as big as you possibly can. My mother, who watched me lose three-quarters of my money doing precisely what he recommends, thinks I should never commit more than a few hundred dollars again. Neither of them will engage with the other's reasoning.

Two things complicate it that I do not know how to fold in. First, by June 1 I need $1,800 in cash for the deposit and airfare on a summer program I have already been accepted to and have told my parents I would pay for myself; if my bankroll drops below that, there is nowhere else for it to come from. Second, my nine-in-twelve record is twelve lots in one narrow category, and resale prices in that category have been sliding since December, so I am not certain the same ratio would come out if I ran it again.

So I would like to know: is my cousin right that the average across a hundred imaginary repetitions is what I should be tracking, is my mother right that the size of the number matters more than the odds, or are the two of them answering different questions from the one I actually have? And what should I put down on Saturday — preferably a rule I can reuse on the next lot rather than a single figure, along with an honest account of how much that rule should shift given that my track record is short and the market is cooling.
