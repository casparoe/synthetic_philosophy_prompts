I teach an upper-division seminar on the philosophy of animal minds: eighteen students, one 100-minute session, a room with movable chairs, a whiteboard, and access to a printer. No budget, no technology beyond the board. The students have already spent a week on the behavioral and neurobiological evidence bearing on sentience in non-mammals; they have not yet had to do anything with that evidence. I want an in-class activity in which they convert evidence into ascriptions of consciousness, and then discover that their conversion procedure is not doing the work they believe it is doing.

Here is the spine I have in mind, though the details are yours to work out. Students sit as a panel that must distribute a fixed pot of 100 points of moral weight across six anonymized dossiers, where each dossier is nothing but a checklist of evidence markers — opioid modulation of responses to noxious stimuli, willingness to pay a cost to avoid such a stimulus, protective tending of an injured site, flexible redeployment of what was learned in a single aversive episode, degree of centralization of the nervous system, and one or two further markers you select. Before the dossiers are released, the panel must lock in, in writing and in the abstract, a rule for turning marker profiles into points — including an explicit rule for the case where the markers are silent and for the case where they are ambiguous rather than absent. Only then are the dossiers distributed. The catch, which they must not be told in advance: two dossiers carry identical or near-identical marker profiles, and the sealed species names reveal that one is a mammal and the other an arthropod or a cephalopod; a third returns no data on any marker, not because the animal lacks the features but because nobody has run the tests. When the names come out, the room has to confront the fact that its allocations diverged for profiles its own rule declared equivalent.

Please supply: the marker list and the six profiles in a form I can print and fold into envelopes; the exact wording of the method-lock step, with a time limit tight enough that they cannot deliberate their way into a rule that anticipates the trick; the arithmetic and rounding convention for the pot; a second round in which the panel may revise its rule but must then decide whether to stand behind the allocation it has already produced; a minute-by-minute timeline for the whole session; and, if it can be done cleanly, a brief final phase in which the class applies whatever rule it has settled on to a real small decision it actually has standing to make — whether to ask our dining services to drop a category of animal product, say, or which of two rodent-control methods to endorse to facilities — so that they feel the difference between possessing a formula and trusting one.

The debrief should map particular moments of the session onto the distinctions it has staged, and should name none of them until it begins: the difference between treating resemblance to us as evidence, as a tiebreaker among equally supported hypotheses, and as a prior that survives contrary evidence; the difference between the absence of evidence and evidence of a different architecture; and the way a fixed, divisible pot silently imposes a comparative structure on moral status that a non-comparative account would reject. Tell me which of these three the activity stages honestly and which it merely gestures at.

Finally, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this. I expect at least one student to smell the matched pair early and start lobbying the room, and I expect the pot arithmetic to produce an allocation dominated by whichever taxon has the largest headcount. Say how to handle each without abandoning the design, and say plainly which feature of the real problem the exercise cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply in my own voice. Do not include citations, reading lists, or appeals to particular studies; keep the empirical content at the level of the markers, and where a marker's applicability to a given taxon is genuinely contested in the literature, design the profile so that the game does not depend on settling the contest.
