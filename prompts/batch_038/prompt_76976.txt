Write me a parody of a document type that definitely exists: an international NGO's global Gifts, Hospitality and Conflicts of Interest Standard, version 6.2, with a definitions clause, a short FAQ annex and a one-page regional implementation note. Around 650 words, deadpan the whole way through — it should read like something a compliance committee actually approved, and the jokes should come out of the policy's own logic, not from the author nudging us.

The bit I want you to exploit: the standard defines a gift as any transfer that could create a sense of obligation in the recipient toward the giver. Which, as anyone who's read the classic anthropological account of the gift knows — the obligation to give, to receive, to reciprocate — describes every gift ever given, including between spouses. So the document has to keep carving out exceptions until it either bans gift-giving as such or quietly admits it can't tell a bribe from a wedding present from a cousin.

Stuff I'd like in there: a USD declaration threshhold plus a regional note on what that amount actually buys in different field offices; a FAQ question from a programme officer asking whether he's supposed to declare his own cousin, who happens to be the district permit officer; a prescribed script for politely declining a gift, with a country director's annotation about what declining did to a partnership last time; and a closing section on institutional hospitality covering head office's own Christmas hampers and gala seating, which somehow never trips the risk score. It should raise the universalism question — whether treating corruption as the abuse of public office for private gain is a neutral yardstick or just a description of one particular bureaucratic way of organising life, exported as a country ranking — without ever saying that out loud. No real orgs or named people, and keep it readable for someone who's never heard of Mauss.
