I am writing a chapter of a dissertation in Kantian ethics that will be read by people who already know the standard material cold. Every reader has met the murderer at the door, has heard Constant's objection that no duty of truthfulness is owed to someone who threatens you, and has heard the now-familiar reply that the prohibition binds only outright lies and leaves literally true but misleading assertions untouched. My chapter argues that neither move is needed: there are ordinary cases in which Kant's own principles require a speaker to assert what she believes to be false, and require it for deontological rather than consequentialist reasons. I need a case built to order.

Construct a thought experiment satisfying all six of the following specifications.

1. Setting. Entirely mundane and realistic: a workplace, a school, a clinic, a household. No murderers, no officials at the door, no hostage situations, no trolley-style contrivances.

2. Utterance. The agent's response must be an outright assertion of something she believes to be false, made with the intention that it be taken as true. It may not be literally true but misleading; it may not be an evasion, a change of subject, an equivocation, or a refusal to answer. Construct the case so that each of those escapes is genuinely foreclosed, and state separately what forecloses each one.

3. Addressee. The person lied to must be an innocent rational agent with an ordinary entitlement to the truth. The case may not depend on the addressee having forfeited anything through aggression, threat, or bad faith, since that is precisely the move I am trying to get beyond.

4. Grounds. The reason for lying must not be a balance of outcomes. No appeal to lives saved, harms prevented, or the world going better. The permission or requirement must be derivable from what is wrong with the alternative: from how telling the truth would treat the agent herself or some third person, or from an obligation the agent has already assumed.

5. Derivation. Run the case through the Formula of Universal Law (state the maxim explicitly, universalize it, and say whether the failure is a contradiction in conception or a contradiction in the will), through the Formula of Humanity, and through Kant's claim that a lie wrongs humanity in the liar's own person. Where these tests pull in different directions, report the divergence rather than smoothing it over. You may use Kant's remark that although duties themselves cannot collide, grounds of obligation can, with the stronger ground prevailing; do not let that remark carry the whole argument.

6. Length. The scenario itself in a single paragraph, with the analysis following it.

After the analysis, identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work — the feature whose removal would let a strict prohibitionist dissolve the case — and describe what the case looks like once that feature is taken away. Then state the strongest attack a defender of the received strict view would launch, including whether she would attempt to reclassify the utterance as misleading rather than as a lie, and give me your honest verdict on whether that attack succeeds.

If you conclude that the six specifications cannot be jointly satisfied, say so plainly, identify which specification must be relaxed and why, and then give me the best case you can construct under the remaining five.

Please do not quote Kant, and do not supply page numbers, Akademie pagination, or any list of sources: I want the reasoning, not the attributions. Close by telling me how confident you are that the case as delivered actually meets all six specifications, and by singling out the one specification you are least sure you have met.
