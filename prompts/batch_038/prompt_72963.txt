Been going back and forth on this for ages and I want someone to tell me whether my intuitions add up to anything at all. I know there's a whole literature on discount rates and I'm not trying to relitigate it, I'm just trying to sort out my own head.

The cases:

**Money, me.** $500 now or $600 in a year, mine either way: I take the $500. I don't feel bad about it. I'd call that normal, maybe even rational.

**Money, a stranger.** Same choice, except some stranger gets it: I'd wait for the $600. Making them take $500 just because their money arrives later feels like pure stinginess. Lateness isn't a reason.

**My own old age.** I know I should be putting more away, I don't, and I think people who don't are making a genuine mistake. Notice this contradicts the first case. There I say robbing next-year-me is fine, here I say robbing eighty-year-old-me is not.

**My kid.** I'd cut my own retirement comforts to pay for her education without a second thought, and she's a different person entirely. So it isn't that I only care about myself, and it isn't that I refuse to discount others either — for her I'll cheerfully discount me.

**The shape of it.** Now versus next week I discount like crazy. Year ten versus year ten and a month, barely at all. And here I contradict myself twice: part of me says "now" is a genuinely special position, not an illusion. Another part says I should sign things in advance that bind future-me precisely because present-me can't be trusted. I don't see how both are true.

**Past versus future.** I'd much rather have had a brutal procedure yesterday than a milder one next week. Feels completely natural, no anguish. But when it comes to leaving waste that's dangerous for a few thousand years, I don't think "it's in the future" carries a shred of weight. Same lateness, opposite verdicts.

**Climate, 2200.** I won't discount their wellbeing for being later. But I will discount for the chance they never exist at all, and I'll discount some more for the fact that they'll probably be much richer, so a given unit of consumption matters less to them. I'm pretty sure those three are seperate reasons and only the first is disreputable, but I want someone to check that I'm actually keeping them apart rather than flattering myself.

And my tentative principle, which is what I most want attacked: I think the right amount to discount tracks how connected the later person is to the one doing the deciding. Memories carried forward, projects still live, character recognizable — the more of that, the less discounting is warranted. Trouble is it predicts exactly backwards from what I said above. Next-year-me is still solidly me, so I shouldn't touch his $600; eighty-year-old-me is practically a stranger, so underinvesting in him should be no big deal. My gut goes the other way on both counts. So either the connection idea is a dressed-up rationalization of impatience, or those two verdicts are wrong, and I can't tell which.

So: is there a principle, or a small handful, that gets all or most of these? State it precisely and tell me which of my cases motivated which clause. Then run it on two or three cases I didn't mention and say what it delivers — including whether the past/future asymmetry survives as a personal preference but dies as policy, which is where I suspect this is heading. And be straight about which verdicts it can't save. I'd rather hear "you have to give up the kid one, it's incoherent" than get handed a baroque six-clause principle that keeps everything. If the connection idea can be repaired with a tweak, give me the tweak; if it can't, say so plainly.

Finally, tell me what would change your mind. Which argument or consideration would push you to the other verdict on the me-versus-stranger asymmetry in particular, and where is your reasoning here most likely to be weakest? I don't want a confident answer, I want a calibrated one.
