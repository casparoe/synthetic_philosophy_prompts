I've been reading this fight for years and I want the document it should have produced. Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps about Aumann's agreement theorem.

Camp A ("Aumann-rationalists"): the theorem is a substantive normative ideal, not a curiosity. Two people who share a prior, know each other's credences, and know the other is reasoning well can't rationally sit at different posteriors — so a disagreement that persists through honest exchange of reasons is itself evidence that someone is failing somewhere (bad priors, motivated reasoning, not actually trading reasons), and the common prior assumption should be adopted as a working default.

Camp B: that's a category error. The hypotheses — common prior, common knowledge of the posteriors, common knowledge of rationality, costless unlimited communication — are jointly so idealized that no real argument satisfies them, and there are independent reasons to deny that rationality pins down a unique credence anyway (imprecise credences, rationally permissible differences in priors), so genuinely rational agents can agree to disagree with no defect at all.

Give me: (1) a numbered section of claims both camps can sign — what the theorem does prove, what it doesn't, and what would have to be true for it to bite on an actual argument; (2) a disagreements table, each row phrased so that the side it describes would accept the wording used for it; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling them would actually move at least one camp, and for each crux a case, argument, or thought experiment both agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction written down in advance. I suspect "is the common prior assumption normative or just an idealization?" is at least half a words fight, so (4) tell me which parts of the dispute were verbal and rewrite them so they stop being one.

Then the reflexive bit, which is the part I actually care about: have them apply their own signed criteria to themselves. After the collaboration, do Camp A and Camp B converge on whether the theorem has normative bite? If not, which of their own agreed claims licenses them in still disagreeing — and does the document have to concede that the two of them are a live counterexample to their own positions? If either camp wants to lean on the Blackwell–Dubins "opinions merge in the limit" result, fine, but say explicitly whether the other camp grants that it bears on any crux.
