I'm in a political ethics seminar trying to pin down what actually makes something corrupt by just listing my gut calls, and they are not adding up. Here they are.

- Paying a posted "expedite fee" at a passport office in a country where every clerk expects it: not corrupt, that's just the price of the service.
- A pharma rep buying my uncle, a GP, a $30 lunch, which there's decent evidence nudges his prescribing: not corrupt. The same rep paying him $3,000 as a "consulting fee" for a meeting that plainly never happened: corrupt.
- A councillor voting to rezone a parcel she owns without disclosing: corrupt. A councillor voting for a rezoning that raises the value of her own street, disclosed, everyone knew: not corrupt.
- A village chief taking a goat from a family because refusing it would be a real insult, then siting the new well where the hydrology says rather than near their field: not corrupt. Same goat, well sited near their field: corrupt.
- A donor giving a university $500k with no strings, and two years later admissions takes his marginal kid: corrupt.
- A judge letting a bar association fly her to a conference, then years later ruling for one of its member firms — correctly, everyone agrees the ruling is right: not corrupt.
- A cop taking free coffee from a diner and later not writing the owner up: corrupt.
- A politician giving a donor's kid an internship over better applicants, in a place where a politician's kin obligations are the only safety net anyone has: honestly don't know. And I notice I'd call the exact same thing corrupt back home.

My tentative rule is that a gift or payment is corrupt iff it actually changes what the official does, and the change makes the outcome worse on the merits. But that condemns the passport fee (I jump a queue, somebody waits longer), and I don't think it seperates the goat from the $500k, or the judge from the cop.

Can you state a principle — or two competing ones — that captures most of this, say which case motivates each clause, then tell me what it comes out as on a couple of cases I haven't thought of, and be straight with me about which of my verdicts it can't save? No reading list please, I just want the principle.
