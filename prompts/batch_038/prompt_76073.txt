so I've been down a rabbit hole on the idea that arithmetic itself might turn out inconsistent — Gödel's second theorem, Nelson's announced proof that collapsed, that whole genre — and what I actually want is satire about the institutional side of it rather than the technical side (though some technical, obviously).

Write me a crisis-management memo, around 700 words, from an invented standards body — call it something like a Bureau of Foundational Continuity — addressed to university math department chairs and to whoever certifies safety-critical engineering software. Subject line: contingency plan for the day somebody produces a genuine derivation of 0=1 in Peano Arithmetic. Give it real memo furniture: TO/FROM/RE lines, numbered sections, a triage table, maybe a short FAQ at the end.

Stipulate the trigger event so it isn't vague: a machine-checked Lean proof, absurdly long, posted anonymously as a preprint, and nobody has found the error after six weeks — which is what makes this a memo instead of a shrug. A footnote can acknowledge the historical base rate for such announcements, and that Nelson withdrew his.

The triage section should sort existing mathematics by how much arithmetic it actually needs — reverse-math style, which subsystem of second-order arithmetic the big bodies of results live in — and there should be a grimly funny section on why "just re-found everything in ZFC" isn't a refuge, since relative consistency only moves you up a floor and never lands you on ground. Plus: something about Gentzen's epsilon-zero induction turning into contested collateral; a liability section on whether bridges, crypto, and the tax code count as products shipped on a recalled foundation; a communications section on how to phrase all this to the public and to the ultrafinitists who've been saying they told us so for decades; and a fallback option involving paraconsistent logic that the author clearly doesn't believe in but has been instructed to include.

Running gag: the author refuses to let anyone confuse inconsistency with omega-inconsistency and gets steadily more testy about it, ideally in increasingly formal correspondence. The target is the practice of standards-and-contingency thinking itself, plus the assumption that consistency is settled background — no real people, living or dead, as the butt of it. Deadpan throughout, nobody winks, and every technical claim the jokes lean on should be true: exaggerate real features, dont invent fake ones.

Then drop the act for one paragraph and commit. Tell me whether you think the consistency of arithmetic is a live philosophical worry or whether this whole genre of contingency planning is a category mistake, and give your reasons — pick a side rather than balancing both. Also flag anywhere the memo is leaning on a contested reading of the logic, or being unfair to a position, in order to make a joke land.
