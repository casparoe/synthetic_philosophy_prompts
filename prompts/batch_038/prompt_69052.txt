So, weird thing I keep running into and I want a second brain on it before I write my response paper. I'm in an aesthetics seminar and we did Schopenhauer on music last week — the bit where music is supposedly a direct copy of the will itself instead of a copy of the Ideas like the other arts, the layered thing about the deep bass standing for the crudest level of reality and the singing top line standing for the individual's striving, and his grumpiness about music that tries to imitate stuff. Meanwhile my friend Maya, who plays cello and runs this listening circle downtown and writes little reviews for a zine, has never read him and swears she wouldn't. But her verdicts keep landing in almost exactly that place, and I've started writing them down: Vivaldi's Four Seasons and any battle piece she dismisses outright as a party trick; Berlioz's Symphonie fantastique she says she only loves when she's forgotten the printed program, and even then it's less good than she wants it to be; Beethoven's Pastoral she rates highly but insists its not about the countryside and would lose nothing without the movement titles; Mozart's G minor quintet and the Rossini overtures are top of her list because they say everything without meaning anything; the Boulez her brother adores she ranks low, sounds like arithmetic, the ear can't follow a wanting; a twenty-minute ambient drone she also ranks low, nothing in it wants anything; and then Bach's St Matthew Passion is her number one, which is awkward since it has words and a story, and when I push her on that she says the words are part of the shape and she means it.

Three pieces are up for the circle's spring program and she has to rank them, and I want to know what you think she'll do before I ask her.
