You are a philosopher of perception who also reads the cross-modal vision literature, and I would like you to think carefully before answering: sketch two or three candidate diagnoses of the stalemate first, then commit to one and argue for it.

I have just been asked to co-teach a seminar on Molyneux's problem alongside a vision scientist, and at our planning meeting we both insisted the same empirical result settles the question — in opposite directions. The result is from the sight-restoration work on children and adolescents treated for dense congenital cataracts: within roughly two days of surgery they could visually discriminate objects nearly as well as they could identify them by touch, yet matching a seen object to a felt one was barely better than guessing; those retested at five days, seven days, and five months had climbed to something like 80–90 percent. My colleague reads this as the empiricist vindication: there is no ready-made amodal representation of shape, the touch-to-sight link must be built, and it is built by learning. I read the very same numbers the other way: transfer that arrives this fast and this completely looks like two modalities reading off a single spatial content, whereas the Berkeley-style picture, on which the connection is only a learned correlation, should predict a slow grinding-in over months. Neither of us moved, and I suspect the pattern recurs right back through Berkeley, Leibniz, and Reid — each camp treating whatever data arrives as confirming what it already held.

My hypothesis, which I want you to test rather than adopt, is that the disagreement is not about the data at all but about which question Molyneux was asking: one about the content of a perceptual state at an instant, the other about the standing capacities of a perceiver — with a third camp holding that the question is ill-posed unless it specifies what the subject is being asked to do with the cube and the sphere. Is that right? Tell me what each side's position actually commits it to, where the restoration results genuinely bite and where they merely look decisive, and what would count as progress: a distinction, a differently designed study, or a reformulation of the question. Please no bibliography — I want the diagnosis argued from the positions themselves.
