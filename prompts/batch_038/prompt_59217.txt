I teach a graduate seminar in the philosophy of music, and I came to philosophy by way of music theory: before my doctorate I spent years doing harmonic and contrapuntal analysis, and I still think about pieces in figured-bass terms — function, register, interval size, contrapuntal obligation, structural level. I want you to take the celebrated section of Book III of Schopenhauer's *The World as Will and Representation* devoted to music, and restate it in that vocabulary of mine. The material to be translated is roughly this: the claim that music, unlike the other arts, is not a copy of the Platonic Ideas but a copy of the will itself, and therefore speaks a universality unlike that of concepts; the parallel he draws between the fundamental bass and the lowest grades of the will's objectification — crude, inorganic mass — with the filling or inner voices corresponding to the plant and animal kingdoms and the high, singing principal melody to the intellectual life and striving of the individual; his remarks that the bass is confined to the low register and moves slowly and in large steps while the melody moves freely and quickly; his treatment of dissonance and its obligatory resolution, including the way a delayed cadence heightens the satisfaction when it finally arrives, as an image of striving checked and then appeased; and the remark that the composer lays bare the innermost essence of the world in a language his reasoning faculty does not understand. I don't want an analogy or a popularization. I want a genuine translation, thesis by thesis, into claims about tonal practice that a competent analyst would recognize as descriptions of music rather than of metaphysics.

I have the Payne volume on my desk, so please do not quote, cite, or supply a bibliography — paraphrase is exactly what I want, and I will check it against the text myself. What I do want is maximal honesty about the costs. Two suspicions drive the request. First, I suspect that much of what reads as metaphysics in those pages is simply an accurate description of common-practice tonal syntax — that the "hierarchy of tones" is really the functional stratification that any figured-bass-trained musician of the 1820s took for granted — and I want to know how much of the doctrine loses its metaphysical ambition once it is rendered in my terms. Second, I suspect that some of it cannot be rendered at all: my vocabulary has no expression for a will that is not a psychological subject, no sense of "copy of" other than structural resemblance, and no way to speak of a piece's content except as a listener's expectations. So where you find yourself translating "the will" as tension, expectation, or felt tendency, stop and mark the substitution rather than absorb it; that quiet psychologizing is precisely what I want exposed.

Then test the translation against repertoires Schopenhauer never considered, and tell me which claims survive the contact: a four-part chorale in which the bass is the most active voice and the soprano moves largely by step; a long pedal point beneath rapidly changing upper voices; a cello line that climbs above the second violin; monophonic chant with no bass at all; gamelan music with clear registral and functional stratification but no fundamental-bass harmony; and a post-tonal context in which dissonance carries no obligation to resolve. Please organize your reply in these sections, in this order. "The theses as I take them" (about 150 words: a neutral restatement, so I can check that you have not already begun translating). "Translation ledger" (a table, one row per thesis: the Schopenhauerian claim, its best rendering in tonal-theoretical terms, and a status of clean, distorted, or inexpressible). "Clean survivals" (about 250 words). "Distortions" (about 350 words, naming in each case the particular feature of my vocabulary that does the violence). "Inexpressibles" (about 300 words). "Stress tests" (about 300 words, working through the six repertoires above). "Verdict" (about 200 words), in which you tell me plainly whether the residue indicts Schopenhauer — showing that he read his metaphysics off the local conventions of the style he knew — or indicts my vocabulary, showing that tonal theory simply lacks the resources to state what he is claiming. I would rather have a deflationary verdict than a diplomatic one.
