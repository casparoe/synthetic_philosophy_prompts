I have spent the better part of two decades in and around the rationalist blogosphere, and roughly as long writing firmware and compiler-toolchain code for a living. I have read the usual exchanges on the Chinese Room and on Dennett's two-state machine that one program can read as the moon and another as a quarter, and I suspect both camps are arguing about an artefact I am fluent in, using a vocabulary I am not. I would like you to test that suspicion by translating.

The assignment: restate the dispute between Searle and Dennett over original versus derived intentionality in the vocabulary I actually use — instruction sets and ABIs, calling conventions, linkers, memory-mapped registers, union types and type punning, microcode, and the standing distinction between a value and the bit pattern that happens to carry it. Go claim by claim rather than writing a general essay, and keep the two sides distinct: what Searle says symbol manipulation cannot deliver, and what Dennett says the physics of the machine leaves unsettled.

Work through this case, because I think it is the two-bitser with the mystique removed. One firmware image, built once, flashed onto two physically identical boards. At a fixed address sits a sixteen-bit word. On board A a driver writes it and the rest of the system reads it as cabin temperature in tenths of a degree; on board B a different driver writes the same word and it is read as two packed bytes, fan speed and door state. In some runs the silicon ends up in the same electrical state under both readings. Now add two complications. First, the image was compiled from a single source that declares the word as a union, so both readings are recorded somewhere in the artefacts — in a language nobody is currently executing. Second, board A drives a damper motor: what it reads changes what the room does, in a closed loop. Board B only logs; nothing downstream ever acts on its value.

Then answer these, in order:

1. Which of my two boards, if either, is closer to what Searle says is missing from the room, and why. In particular, does his appeal to the brain's causal powers have a counterpart in the closed-loop-versus-logging asymmetry, or is that a category error dressed up as an engineering distinction?
2. Is there anything in the stack that is not itself interpreted by something below or beside it? The program interprets the word; the ISA interprets the program; microcode interprets the ISA; charge interprets nothing. If interpretation runs all the way down, does original versus derived go vacuous, or does some residue survive the descent — and if it survives, where exactly does it land?
3. What happens to the union? There is a reading recorded in an artefact that is not being executed by anyone. Is that derived intentionality, potential intentionality, or nothing at all, and does the fact that the source file was written by a person rather than generated change the answer?
4. A residue table, which is the part I actually want. Columns: the claim as the philosophers put it, in your paraphrase; the nearest object in my vocabulary; a verdict of clean, forced, or no counterpart; and what the forcing cost. Be explicit where a claim admits two renderings and the two renderings support opposite verdicts.
5. How much of the dispute is verbal, and which part. I have in mind that Searle treats the ascription of computation to a system as observer-relative while also treating the brain's production of intentionality as observer-independent; if you think that combination is unstable, say what would have to be given up to stabilise it, from either side.

For the record, my intuition is this: meaning is a property of a reading, not of a bit pattern; the stack is syntax all the way down, each level interpreted by the next; and therefore the Chinese Room fails for the same reason the question whether that word is really a temperature fails — it has no answer until you fix a reader, and once you have fixed one it is trivial. Tell me whether that is correct, correct but incomplete, or a move Searle has already diagnosed. Do not flatter it.

Two ground rules. Do not smuggle my framework's assumptions in and present them as translation: if you must distort, mark the distortion and say what it cost. And I have no web access and no interest in a bibliography, citations, or paper titles — paraphrase both philosophers rather than quoting them, and give me argument instead of references.
