I'm giving a 20-minute paper at an interdisciplinary workshop on long-run growth next week, and I'm the only non-economist on the program. My thesis is that Malthusian theory wasn't refuted by the demographic transition — it was right but early, and I try to make "right but early" precise instead of leaving it as a vibe. The criterion I defend: a long-run theory earns a regime-change escape from falsification only if it is, in my term, sealed in advance, meaning (i) it states up front the observable conditions under which its own parameters shift regime, (ii) those conditions are generated by the same mechanism that produces the in-regime predictions rather than by a seperate post-hoc story, and (iii) the escape it licenses is one a rival couldn't have taken without paying a comparable cost. I argue the formalized Malthusian model of pre-industrial economies clears all three, because the escape from the trap runs through the very technology-population feedback that produced the stagnation, so unified growth theory is a conservative extension of the model rather than a replacement for it.
