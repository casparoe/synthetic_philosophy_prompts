I serve on the editorial board of a university press series in global history, and we must decide whether an incoming proposal counts as Eurocentric. Our own record to date: we accepted a manuscript built as six parallel regional narratives that meet only after 1500; we rejected a survey whose first half is devoted to Europe and whose second half treats other regions mainly as theaters of European expansion; we accepted a study of European industrialization that argued its case by sustained comparison with the lower Yangzi and with Japan, taking the divergence itself to be what requires explanation; we rejected a book that gave non-European societies a great many pages but cast them as reservoirs of technique, crop, and demand on which Europe subsequently drew; we accepted a monograph that omits Europe for three quarters of its length in favor of Indian Ocean exchange networks; and we rejected, narrowly, a history of science that ran a single line from antiquity through Renaissance Europe to the present, admitting other traditions only at the points where they fed that line.

The new proposal devotes roughly a third of its chapters to Europe; it portrays colonial officials, merchants, and states as agents with their own reasons and their own internal dynamics; it organizes the whole book under a tripartite periodization — ancient, medieval, modern — applied uniformly to every region it treats; and its closing chapter asks why the institutions of the modern world came to be recognizably Western in form.

Tell me how I should expect the board to vote, and which of our six precedents is the closest analog and why. More important, lay out the candidate criteria the board might be running — share of coverage; whether non-Europeans appear as agents or as objects; whether European categories serve as the universal scaffolding; whether the narrative converges on a Western terminus — and say of each whether it is consistent with all six precedents or which precedent it contradicts. Then say where the surviving criteria diverge on the new case, which way I should vote if consistency with our own record is the governing consideration, what principle the board would be adopting by voting each way, and what further proposal we could put before it that would separate the criteria decisively.
