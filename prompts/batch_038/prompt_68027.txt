I'm in the middle of a novel — literary, though it flirts with genre — whose two leads are decision theorists who used to be married and have been arguing about the same thing for years, and the third act turns on a document they finally co-sign after an adversarial collaboration. I want you to write that document, becuase my own drafts of it keep sliding back into dialogue, and what the scene needs is the flat, joint, slightly lawyerly thing instead. The characters: she's an updateless decision theorist, who thinks the real object of choice is a policy picked from the perspective of whatever information you had before Omega modelled you, and that once you've learned which branch you're in it's too late to re-decide; he's a plain causal decision theorist who updates, maximizing causal expected utility given everything he now knows, and who thinks talk about what you "would have done" only ever earns its keep when there was an earlier moment at which you could actually have committed.

So that they aren't recycling the cases their grad students have all seen, their collaboration is about one case, which I invented and want treated as a fixed datum rather than something to re-litigate: ten years ago Omega scanned her brain and flipped a fair coin; the deal was that in the tails world she'd have been handed $10,000 yesterday, but only if the scan predicted that she'd hand over $100 today, in the heads world. Today it's heads, she's been told all of this out loud, and she's holding a hundred-dollar bill. The reason I made it up — and the reason I want the document to actually engage with it rather than settling the case as a bog-standard mugging — is that she is not the person who was scanned: the scan predicted the choices of a twenty-three-year-old who cared almost exclusively about her own money, and the woman standing there now weights other people's welfare very differently. He says the decade between the scan and today was a wide-open choice point at which she could have shaped herself into someone who pays, so nothing exotic is required; she says that standing inside her current values, the question of which prior to be updateless *with respect to* doesn't even have his answer.

Draft the joint statement the two of them sign. I want the claims both can accept about the case and about what each of their theories says the correct move here is; the disagreement stated in wording each of them would accept as a description of their own side (I'll be quoting it in the book, so it has to read like something a real person would put their name to, not a strawman); the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would genuinely move at least one of them — I suspect they include whether his ten-year choice point does the work he wants it to, and whose prior she should be reasoning from; for each crux, some variant case, argument, or test both agree would bear on it, with each of their predictions written down in advance; and a line on which parts of their fight turn out to have been merely verbal. Give it to me as exactly five numbered clauses of a sentence or two each, with no introduction and no conclusion — terse and a bit cold suits the both of them.
