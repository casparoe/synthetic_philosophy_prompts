A friend and I spent most of an evening arguing about process supervision versus outcome supervision for AI systems, and I have been unable to reconstruct my own side of it since. He assembled the following chain, which I could not answer at the time, though I remain convinced its endpoint is false.

(1) A reward is a scalar assigned to a segment of a system's behavior; whatever else it is later used for, it is a score of that segment and carries no content beyond it.
(2) Scoring each intermediate step of a solution is therefore scoring a very short segment of the solution: an outcome signal applied to a small piece of the trajectory.
(3) Hence process supervision and outcome supervision differ not in kind but only in the granularity of the segment scored. The distinction is a dial, not a fork.
(4) If it is a dial, then everything claimed on its behalf — that process supervision limits reward hacking, that it improves credit assignment, that it catches reasoning which is fluent but invalid — amounts to a wager about which granularity happens to work on which task, and there is no principled reason to turn the dial one way rather than the other.
(5) Moreover, step-level labels come from annotators judging whether a step looks right, and a step looks right insofar as steps like it tend to occur in solutions that end up correct. The label is thus a proxy for the expected correctness of the final answer.
(6) So a process reward model is an outcome model at one remove: it inherits every pathology of outcome supervision and adds annotation noise.
(7) Generalizing: all supervision of any learner, artificial or human, is outcome supervision at some segment length, and nothing that goes by the name of teaching a method is anything other than rewarding results.
(8) But (7) is absurd. A teacher who watches a student reach a correct answer by dividing through by an expression that may equal zero corrects the student, and that correction is not about the answer.
(9) So (1) through (6) conceal a mistake somewhere.

If I had to choose, I would rather surrender (6) than (8), and I suspect (5) is doing something quietly illicit — but when I said so, he replied that (5) is merely an empirical remark about the provenance of the labels, and I could not explain why provenance should matter to what the resulting model is a model of. Which step do you reject, and why did it look unobjectionable while the argument was being run? I would also like the alternatives priced: if one keeps (2) and (3) and instead denies (4), what would have to be true of step-level rewards for the dial to have a principled setting? And what would change your mind here — what should a defender of (3) say to force you to a different diagnosis, which of your own moves do you think is most likely to fail, and is there any evidence about how process reward models behave under sustained optimization pressure that would settle the dispute in his favor rather than mine?
