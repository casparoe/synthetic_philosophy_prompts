A friend and I argued past midnight about the fact that we have never detected any sign of intelligence beyond Earth, and he finished with a case that seems to demolish the entire question rather than answer it. Here is his argument as I have reconstructed it.

(1) If technological civilizations arose at all often in our galaxy, then at least some of them would have expanded far enough, over the billions of years available, to leave signatures we could detect.
(2) After decades of searching, we have detected no such signatures: no probes, no engineered structures, no transmissions.
(3) So technological civilizations do not arise often in our galaxy.
(4) The only reason anyone ever expected them to arise often is that nothing about our star, our planet, or our history looks exceptional; we are an ordinary product of ordinary conditions, and ordinary conditions produce us.
(5) So the expectation driving (1) rests entirely on our being ordinary.
(6) But (3) denies that expectation. On his own account, we did what ordinary planets do not do.
(7) Therefore we are not ordinary: either we are the first, or we are alone, or everyone else is destroyed at some step we have luckily passed. And since the same reasoning applies to the observable universe as a whole rather than merely to the galaxy, we are alone there too.
(8) An argument that assumes we are unremarkable and concludes that we are remarkable has not discovered anything. It has only exposed a contradiction between two things we assumed, with no independent way of telling which to give up.
(9) So the silence supports no conclusion at all. It is a see-saw: you can buy any result you like by choosing which end to stand on.

He is very pleased with (9). I am not. The silence strikes me as evidence of something, and (9) has the air of an escape hatch that would work equally well against any observation anyone found inconvenient — yet I cannot locate the step where he goes wrong, and (4) through (6) in particular seem hard to deny. Which premise or inference would you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? In particular, does your answer commit us to one branch of the trilemma in (7), to none of them, or to some further possibility he never listed? Please tell me how confident you are in the diagnosis you settle on, and single out the point in your own answer that you are least sure of.
