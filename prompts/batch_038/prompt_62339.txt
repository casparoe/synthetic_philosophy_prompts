Longtime LW/EA-Forum-adjacent reader here, and I've been chewing on futarchy for years — Hanson's thing where you vote on the goal and let prediction markets pick the means. There's a failure mode I keep running into in those threads and I genuinely can't tell whether it already has a name, so I'll describe it clumsily and hope somebody recognizes it.

Setup: you've got a market on a conditional contract, something like "if we adopt plan A, the metric clears X." The price is supposed to be the input to a decision. What I keep noticing is that the moment the market gets hooked up to an actual decision — the moment the price is what tips the choice — the price moves, and it moves toward whichever side the traders want to win. Not because anyone is lying, and not because anyone is paying to move the price so they can profit on some outside position. The traders are sincere: ask the same question off the record and they'll hand you the same credence they're betting. Its just that when the price decides something, buying the contract stops being purely a way of reporting a belief and becomes also a way of casting a ballot, and both motives arrive in the same wallet at the same price.

Cleanest version I've seen is a side-by-side. Same question, two markets: one that decides nothing and just pays out on the fact of the matter, and one whose price feeds into whether the thing happens. The second runs systematically in the direction people want, and the gap widens the more rides on the decision. Unhook it from the decision and the gap closes back up.

Second instance, and this is the bit that makes me think it isn't just ordinary manipulation: in a two-branch futarchy setup ("value if A" versus "value if B"), only one branch ever resolves. If B doesn't get chosen, the B market never pays out, so nobody who talked B up ever gets scored. The drift never gets corrected on the branch that lost — and the losing branch is also the thin one, so it's least accurate exactly when the comparison is close enough to matter.

So: does this have an established term? I've tried filing it under several and none sit right. Goodhart's law is about a measure becoming a target, but here the measure isn't being optimized by an outsider — it's being used as a voting device by the same people reporting into it. Reflexivity and self-fulfilling prophecy are close, except there the world changes to match the belief; here the belief didn't change, only the price did. Performativity in the sociology-of-finance sense is adjacent for the same reason. Expressive voting is in the ballpark, but that's usually about cheap, non-pivotal expression, and here the expression is pivotal and costs real money. And plain "prediction market manipulation" assumes either an outside position or someone betting against what they believe.

Don't just hand me a label — I want the carving. What's the actual mechanism, where does this stop being its own thing and start being manipulation or reflexivity, does the decision-relevance have to be public and binding for the effect to show up, and if there's no existing term, what would you call it and what are the boundary cases? Bonus points if you can say whether anyone has proposed a fix that doesn't amount to "just don't let the market decide anything."
