I am two years into a philosophy of mind PhD, and last month I changed labs in a way that has made an old argument of mine uncomfortably personal. My training was in first-order representationalism — my master's thesis defended the view that what makes a mental state conscious is its having the right kind of representational content, with no need for any further state directed at it — and I wrote a seminar paper arguing that higher-order accounts either collapse into that view or posit experiences nobody could be said to have. My new group does psychophysics: metacontrast masking, confidence ratings, type-2 signal detection measures of how well subjects track their own discrimination performance. The PI has asked me to draft the theory section of a grant whose framing is frankly higher-order, on the assumption that awareness of a stimulus consists in a suitable representation of the state carrying that stimulus, and she has asked me to make the section philosophically defensible rather than merely gestural. The problem is that I cannot yet state precisely where the two families come apart. The examples she reaches for — blindsight, inattentional blindness, unilateral neglect — look to me like cases both sides can absorb, since the higher-order theorist can always say the higher-order representation is degraded rather than absent, and the first-order theorist can say the degraded first-order representation explains everything anyway.

So please build me a minimal pair of thought experiments — one and the same paradigm, one and the same subject, with the stimulus history, attentional load, arousal, and memory access held fixed — such that a higher-order theorist of the Rosenthal or Lau variety and a first-order representationalist of the Tye or Dretske variety are forced to give opposite verdicts, both on whether there is something it is like for the subject at the relevant moment and on what that something is like. The design has to satisfy the following constraints. First, no stipulated verdicts: the divergence must be derived from each theory's own commitments, and you should show me the derivation step by step rather than announce the outcome. Second, no science fiction — no neural implants, no zombies, no inverted spectra generated by fiat; keep the machinery to things a vision laboratory or a neuropsychology clinic could plausibly instantiate, whether that is masking, transcranial stimulation of a named area, a lesion patient, or a syndrome such as Anton's. Third, the evidence in the case must be report-independent: objective discrimination, confidence or type-2 measures, choice behaviour, physiological readouts. If you do use verbal report anywhere, tell me what the case looks like once the subject cannot speak, since the report confound is precisely what I am trying to design around. Fourth, and this is the constraint I expect to matter most: in the arm where the higher-order representation exists without an appropriate first-order target, the design must explain why we should read it as generating a genuine conscious state with a determinate phenomenal character rather than a mere false belief that one is having an experience — identify the feature of the case that is supposed to do this work, and say honestly how much weight it can bear. Fifth, keep each scenario to roughly a paragraph.

After the two scenarios, give me a numbered check that the pair really is minimal — which single variable differs, and confirmation that nothing else relevant differs. Then, for each scenario, what the higher-order theorist says, what the first-order theorist says, and which specific premise of each theory does the work. Then the attacks: two or three that each side would launch against the other's arm, including the dispositionalist higher-order move and the deflationary move on which there is no fact of the matter about the targetless case, each with a note on whether the attack actually defeats the case or only relocates the problem to a further question. Finally, a short statement of which empirical result in this paradigm would be genuinely asymmetric — evidence that could only have been produced if one of the two theories were true, so that I can tell my PI whether her proposed dependent measure can discriminate between the views at all. Please phrase each theory's commitments in wording its own advocates would sign; I want to hand this over without it reading as a strawman of the position I used to hold. No citations or references — what I need is the argument laid bare.
