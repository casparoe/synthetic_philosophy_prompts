I spent about thirty-five years doing field entomology and then a stretch on water-quality work, retired two years ago, and have fallen unexpectedly hard for moral philosophy — specifically the doing-harm-versus-letting-harm-happen stuff, which turns out to be almost word-for-word the argument we have once a month at the land trust I volunteer for. So I thought I'd put a real upcoming vote to you rather than a textbook case.

The trust's chair — call her Marion — has a record I've been trying to reverse-engineer. She's articulate, but her stated reasons never quite collapse into one principle. Four decisions from the last six years:

1. Brutal snow year, a herd of elk on one of our preserves was going to starve by the hundreds. Someone proposed a sharpshoot cull of about sixty animals to spare them the slow version. Marion voted no. Her line in the minutes was that watching them starve is horrible, "but that isn't the same thing as putting the bullet in them."

2. A wood-boring beetle thats arrived on shipping pallets was killing off a rare native pine, and with it the habitat of a bird down to a few hundred pairs. Treatment was a systemic insecticide plus cutting and chipping infested trees, which would have killed beetle larvae by the thousand and would also have taken out some nest trees and a rare lichen. Marion voted yes, on condition the crews phase the cutting so no occupied nest tree came down that season. Killing the beetles didn't bother her at all. Losing the nests nearly did.

3. A landslide silted in a stream and wiped out one of six populations of a frog that's perfectly secure everywhere else. Staff proposed a captive-breeding and reintroduction program, expensive, five years. Marion voted no, saying the trust's job is land, not doctoring wildlife, and that a natural wipeout in a secure species isn't ours to fix.

4. Feral goats left over from an old ranching operation were eating an island scrub down to dirt. Eradication by helicopter shooting was the cheap option. Marion pushed through live-trap-and-relocate instead, at maybe eight times the cost, and said for the record that she'd pay any price to avoid killing them. The goats went, the habitat was saved.

Now the case in front of us next week. Staff recieved the vets' report last month. There's a valley holding the only wild population of an endangered mountain ungulate, about forty animals. Ten years ago a herding development program put domestic goats in the same valley, and our vets are confident the goats brought in a respiratory infection. Mortality is running around a third and the deaths are ugly and slow. No vaccine exists, fencing the herds apart is impossible in that terrain, and handling animals for treatment kills some of them from stress. The only thing staff can offer is test-and-cull: shoot every animal testing positive plus its close contacts, roughly fifteen to twenty of the forty, on a model saying the remainder then recover. Doing nothing means the herd crashes to a dozen or fewer, which is functionally extinct. Nobody's found a non-lethal version this time, though they looked.

So — what does Marion vote? I'd like you to work out which rules are actually compatible with all four past votes. I can see candidates in the neighbourhood of "never kill, letting die is different," a means-versus-side-effect rule, something about the moral status of what's being killed, something about whether the threat is our fault, something about extirpation weighing against individual deaths, and a "we manage places, not animals" rule. Tell me which of those the record genuinely supports, which ones need patching to survive it, and which are really the same rule wearing different clothes. Then say what each surviving rule predicts for the ungulate case, give me your best guess at her vote and what you'd bet on it, and — the part I care about most — describe the hypothetical proposal I could put in front of her that would split the rules that currently agree. Finally, if the rest of the board lines up behind her either way, what principle would we be adopting that we haven't adopted before?
