I help run a Stoic practice group in my city — all of us are amateurs, nobody's done philosophy — and we've been going in circles for about two years on the control idea. Roughly: one camp says Epictetus gave you exactly two buckets, what's up to us (our judgements, our choices) and what isn't (body, property, reputation, other people), and adding anything else dilutes the whole exercise. The other camp says that's useless in real life because half of what actually stresses us out is only partly up to us — a job interview, whether your kid studies, whether your flight leaves — so you need a third bucket plus the move of setting your target internally (do my best) instead of externally (get the job). Each side thinks the other is either butchering the text or butchering the practice, and nobody has moved an inch.

My hunch, which I want you to test rather than endorse: this isn't one disagreement. Part of it is verbal — the same phrase doing two jobs, causal control in one mouth and where-to-point-your-attention in the other — and part of it is two different questions hiding under one heading, one about what the ancients meant and one about what the exercise actually does to a person who runs it for six months. If that's right, what would progress even look like: a distinction, a translation point, some evidence about how the practice performs? And if I'm wrong, tell me which bit and what the real crux is. Also say what would change your mind about your own diagnosis, and where you think your reasoning is shakiest.
