My half-formed idea: the "chaos theory makes determinism untestable / empirically empty" argument is stuck because both sides treat prediction as a single relation between model and world. But there are at least three distinct targets — tracking the trajectory pointwise, getting the invariant measure or long-run statistics right, and calling whether some coarse-grained event happens within a stated tolerance — and a chaotic system can fail the first completely while being excellent at the second. That's roughly Lorenz's own distinction between predictability of the first kind and the second kind, which people on both sides seem to forget. Second half of the idea: shadowing-type guarantees get proved for hyperbolic systems and are known to fail in plenty of nonhyperbolic ones, so even "the simulated trajectory is meaningless" is system-relative, not a blanket consequence of sensitive dependence.
