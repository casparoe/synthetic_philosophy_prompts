Read it against the Schellenberg-style non-resistant nonbelief argument -- the claim that a perfectly loving God wouldn't allow anyone genuinely open to a relationship with him to be without belief, since belief is a precondition of that relationship.

What got us stuck is that Tomas isn't an ordinary unbeliever. He's a pastor who's been going through the motions for years, and at the end he holds the three o'clock service to a near-empty church anyway. When Algot the sexton floats the idea that the worst part of the Passion wasn't the physical pain but God not answering from the cross, Tomas just says yes. So which box is he in: non-resistant nonbeliever, believer whose God has gone missing, or something the taxonomy has no slot for? Does the empty-church ending undercut the premise that belief is necessary for relationship with God, or does it quietly concede it?
