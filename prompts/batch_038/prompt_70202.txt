I'm putting together a screening night for our epistemology reading group and we're doing *Doubt* — the 2008 Shanley film, not the stage play — and I want to frame it through pragmatic encroachment and contextualism without it turning into the lame "ooh, the movie is about evidence" conversation. Can you give me an actual reading I can argue with?

Some scaffolding in case the beats are fuzzy: 1964, a Bronx parish school. Sister Aloysius is the principal, Father Flynn the parish priest, Sister James the young teacher, Donald Miller the school's first Black student. James notices Donald come back from a private meeting with Flynn in the rectory upset and smelling of alcohol on his breath, and later sees Flynn slipping an undershirt into Donald's locker. Confronted, Flynn explains the boy was caught drinking the sacramental wine and he'd been keeping it quiet to protect him. Aloysius is unmoved. She goes to Donald's mother, who reveals that Donald is probably gay and that his father would kill him if he knew, and who begs Aloysius to get rid of Flynn rather than Donald so the boy can finish the year. Aloysius then tells Flynn she's spoken to a nun at his last parish and found a pattern; asked for proof, she admits all she has is her certainty; he requests a transfer and leaves with a farewell homily. Afterwards she tells James the nun story was a lie — her reasoning being that the bluff wouldn't have worked if it were false — and then breaks down and says she has doubts.

The thing I actually want you to chew on is a variant of encroachment I've been kicking around. The standard view says whether you know p depends on what's riding on your acting on p: the same evidence that gives you knowledge when little is at stake doesn't when a lot is. My variant — call it proxy stakes — says that when the consequences of your acting on p land mainly on somebody else, it's *their* stakes, not yours, that set the threshold. So on this picture Aloysius's own risk (she tells Flynn she's willing to be thrown out of the church herself) is beside the point; her evidential bar should be set by what happens to Donald and to Flynn. Trouble is the view needs a rule for whose stakes count and under which description of their interests, and the film seems to be about that disagreement rather then about evidence at all: Aloysius and Mrs. Miller are both orienting themselves around Donald's interests and coming out in opposite places, with Mrs. Miller holding far more of the relevant information. Mrs. Miller is the case that wrecks me, actually — she acts, she asks for Flynn to be removed, in a way that looks rational whether or not Flynn is guilty, and she never seems to want to *know*. If encroachment is supposed to tie rational action to knowledge, what is she doing?

Where I want you to go:

- Is there a clean contextualist arc here — a possibility gets raised, salience goes up, and knowledge claims that were true become false, including Aloysius's own? Flynn's opening homily is about doubt binding a community the way faith does, and his next one is about gossip. Is he doing context-shifting work, and does the film know it?
- The undershirt is the best prop for this: the identical observation is either nothing or everything. Does that support salience-sensitivity, or does it just show the evidence was always ambiguous and I'm projecting my machinery onto it?
- The bluff. Aloysius manufactures what she then counts as evidence, and her action also destroys the only route by which she could have found out. Encroachment tells you to gather more evidence when stakes are high; it doesn't seem to have an account of the agent who takes the step that makes further evidence impossible. Is that a real gap, or am I over-reading one plot point?
- And the honest part: where does this reading strain? I worry I'm flattening a parable about certainty and cruelty into a case study. Tell me where the film refuses to be about knowledge at all — including that it never lets the audience find out whether Flynn did it, which might be the whole point — and what Shanley gives the encroachment debate that the journal articles don't.
