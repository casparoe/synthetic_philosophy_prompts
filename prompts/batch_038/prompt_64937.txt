I write the 80-word blurbs for a small independent cinema's weekly program. Films get booked months out and I only ever manage to see maybe half of them before copy deadline — for the rest I work from critics' reviews, the programmer's notes and the director's statement, and then write as if I'd been there. Someone wrote in last week about a film I'd called quietly devastating: she said she felt misled, not because I got it wrong (she agreed with the verdict) but because I'd clearly never sat in the room with it, and hadn't said so. Thats the part I can't shake — she isn't disputing the accuracy, she's disputing my standing to say it. Meanwhile I do the neighborhood restaurant picks for a local newsletter in exactly the same secondhand way and neither I nor any reader has ever minded. Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up: is she onto something real, and what explains the difference between the two cases?
