Next month I want to do iterated elimination of dominated strategies, but specifically the version where a strategy gets knocked out because it's beaten by a *randomization* over other strategies rather than by any single one, and then you keep going. I know the mechanics fine, what I don't know is how to teach them. My last two sessions went okay but the room visibly glazed over the second I started deriving things at the board, so I want a plan, not a derivation.

Here's the game I built for it (Alice picks the row, Bob the column, Alice's payoff listed first):

```
              L         C         R
   T      (1, 1)    (0, 2)    (3, 0)
   M      (0, 2)    (1, 1)    (3, 0)
   B     (0.4, 0)  (0.4, 0)  (0.4, 5)
```

My intended line: B isn't beaten by T (T gets 0 against C) and isn't beaten by M (M gets 0 against L), but a 50/50 flip between T and M gets (0.5, 0.5, 3), which beats B's (0.4, 0.4, 0.4) in every single column, so B goes. Then Bob's R loses to L in both surviving rows, so R goes, and you land on the top-left 2x2, which is matching pennies — each player at half-half, no pure answer. First ask: is this example actually sound? I sketched it on a napkin and I'm worried there's a flaw I can't see myself, like B being strictly dominated by some pure strategy after all, or R already being eliminable in the full 3x3 by a mixture of L and C, which would wreck the "you have to go in this order" story I'm telling.

The real question is the lesson plan though. Some specific things I'm stuck on.
