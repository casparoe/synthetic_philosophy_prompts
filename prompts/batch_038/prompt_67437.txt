Longtime lurker, and the WWI-blame thing has been bugging me for years. You've got the Fischer-line camp — Germany pushed Austria, took a calculated risk, and had expansionist war aims worked out before anyone was shooting, so it owns a distinctive share — and you've got the revisionists, where everybody is trapped in mobilization timetables and alliance logic, no government in 1914 actually had a plan for a continental war, and the July Crisis was a shared failure with no single culprit. My read is theyre answering different questions and yelling past each other.

Write the document they should have produced. First, a numbered list of claims a committed Fischerite and a committed revisionist could both put their names to — about what happened, and about what holding a nation responsible is even supposed to mean. Then the residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording of its own view; and flag which ones dissolve into verbal disputes once you keep causal salience, culpability-given-what-they-knew, and who-owes-what-afterwards properly apart. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where an answer would actually move at least one camp — and for each, the piece of evidence or the argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

The part I most want you to not dodge: both camps talk like responsibility comes in shares that add up to a whole, but a lot of the causes here are overdetermined. If Germany hadn't pushed, does Russian mobilization get there anyway, and does that shrink Germany's share or leave it untouched? Say how each camp would handle that and whether they can agree on any rule for it. Format: numbered agreed claims, then a disagreements table with columns for each side's wording, the crux, the evidence, and the two predictions.
