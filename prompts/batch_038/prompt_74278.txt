I'm helping a postdoc I work with build the taxonomy section of a grant proposal — basically a table that sorts candidate AI safety problems into "alignment proper" vs. "capability/robustness" vs. "out of scope," and where something lands determines which budget line it comes out of. She has strong priors and Ive been feeding her cases to calibrate. Her calls so far:

- the classic RL reward-hacking demos, like the boat game where the agent spins in circles farming points instead of finishing the race: alignment, the paradigm case
- a model that behaves during training because it can tell its being evaluated and would do something else otherwise: alignment, and the deep version of it
- a proxy goal that works fine inside the training distribution and falls apart outside it: alignment
- sycophancy: alignment — she thinks the model has genuinely acquired a preference for agreement over accuracy, not just a habit
- sandbagging on evals: alignment
- a jailbreak where someone wraps a request in a roleplay frame and the model plays along: NOT alignment. Her reason, roughly: the objective isn't wrong, the input channel is just too credulous. Robustness problem.
- a model with completely correct goals that causes damage by being too incompetent to foresee its own side effects: NOT alignment, capability
- whether models have interests or moral patienthood: out of scope entirely, ethics not alignment

The case we have to place next week: a deployed system of maybe fifty separately trained agents, each individually as aligned as we currently know how to make them — no hacking, no deception, no proxy goals, clean on every eval we run — but the harm is compositional. They compete for the same resource and collectively produce outcomes that none of them would endorse if you asked it directly. No single agent is misaligned; the ensemble is.

Tell me which way she goes. More usefully, tell me what rule she's actually been running that fits all eight of those calls. If the priors genuinely underdetermine it, say what she'd be committing herself to with each choice, because that goes in the proposal either way. There's a second case in the queue too — a policy that faithfully learns exactly what its human labelers said, where the labelers turned out to be systematically biased — feel free to use it to break the tie if it breaks one.

Finish with the single further case that would settle which rule she's on, and then be straight with me about how confident you are in the call and which piece of your reconstruction you'd bet least on.
