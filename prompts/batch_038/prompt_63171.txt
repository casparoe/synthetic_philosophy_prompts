I am a novelist, midway through my second book, and its central people speak a language I invented, inside which I have now been living for three years. Four of its properties bear on my problem. Every finite verb carries an obligatory evidential — seen, told, inferred, or dreamed — and the dreamed evidential has no English reflex at all, though an entire chapter turns on it. Bare generic nouns do not exist, so a speaker cannot say that wolves hunt in packs, only that these wolves hunted, that night, before someone's eyes. Spatial description runs on absolute bearings, which means no character in the book can be told that a thing lies to their left. And there is no tense; in its place the grammar marks whether an event's consequences are still open or have settled, a distinction my characters attend to constantly and my sentences almost never manage. My English renderings are all hacks: free indirect discourse pressed into service for the evidentials, sentence-initial hedges, aspectual periphrasis, italics I dislike, and a recurring particle that has begun to read as a tic.

Here is what I want. Take the debate over linguistic relativity — the strong deterministic formulation, the modest experimental version, and Slobin's reformulation under the label thinking for speaking, along with the cases that carry the empirical weight: exact enumeration among the Pirahã, spatial memory and recall in Guugu Yimithirr and the other languages that orient by cardinal bearings rather than left and right, and the categorical discrimination findings for the two Russian blues — and restate the whole of it in the vocabulary I actually work in. Not popular exposition: my vocabulary. Focalization, register, voice, diction, syntax as an index of character, scene against summary, the unreliable narrator, what a reader can be made to feel without ever being told, and the difference between what a language permits and what a writer chooses.

Then give me the residue. Which of their claims survive intact in craft terms? Which survive only under distortion, and precisely what distortion? Which cannot be expressed at all? My own suspicion is that craft vocabulary is structurally unable to host the strong claim, because it treats language as a menu from which an author selects, whereas the strong claim requires language as a limit no one chose; and because on the page there is no cognition anterior to its rendering, the psycholinguist's independent variable has nowhere to stand. Tell me whether that suspicion holds, and whether the residue indicts my vocabulary or signals that language shapes thought is quietly collapsing into language shapes what gets reported.

Format: exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, every item prefaced with the tag TRANSLATION or RESIDUE. Items one through three carry the translation, with the strong formulation, the modest formulation, and the thinking-for-speaking reformulation each claiming one of them in whatever order you judge most honest. Item four is the residue report. Item five answers the question I cannot put to my editor: does a language like mine, rendered into English prose, actually deliver the strong thesis, or is it decoration laid over the weak one? No introduction, no conclusion, nothing before item one or after item five, and no references or reading lists — I want the translation, not the apparatus.
