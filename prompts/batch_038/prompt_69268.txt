Ok so I've talked myself into a fairly ambitious version of two-dimensional semantics and I want a proper audit of how exposed it is, rather then the usual summary of the framework.

The view, roughly: every declarative sentence has a primary intension — a function from centred epistemically possible scenarios to truth values — sitting alongside the ordinary secondary intension, and that's all the machinery you need. The payoff is that the Kripkean necessary a posteriori turns out to be a kind of illusion. "Water is H2O" is necessary when you evaluate it the second way and contingent when you evaluate it the first way, so there's no such thing as a truth that's genuinely necessary but only knowable a posteriori. Necessity and apriority come cleanly apart, the modal intuition behind the zombie argument survives, and nothing gets lost.

What would count against this? I want the defeaters that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be — fatal versus merely awkward — and for each one, whether a committed two-dimensionalist would concede it or dismiss it, and on what grounds.

Some things I'd particularly like taken seriously:

The jade case, which is my own worry and I may have it wrong. Jade isn't one kind. It's an umbrella term covering two mineralogically unrelated things — nephrite, an amphibole aggregate, and jadeite, a pyroxene — and neither of the things people actually call jade is straightforwardly a mineral species rather than a rock. The term worked perfectly well, in several unrelated traditions, for thousands of years before anyone sorted this out. So what is the primary intension of "jade is a mineral"? If the framework has to hand back a disjunctive one to get the extension right, then its not a priori that jade is either jadeite or nephrite — that's about as a posteriori as it gets — and the epistemic transparency that was the whole selling point is gone. If instead it hands back something non-disjunctive, it gets the truth value wrong. Which horn is worse, and does the squeeze generalise to other mass nouns and kind terms, or is jade just a pathological case the framework can quarantine?

Empty scenarios. What happens to a primary intension when no scenario satisfies the reference-fixing conditions at all — does a chunk of ordinary empirical discourse come out knowably false a priori, and is that a cost or a feature?

The collapse worry. Is there any reason to think the two-dimensional apparatus earns its keep, as opposed to a one-dimensional semantics plus a wholly independent account of epistemic possibility? What would a genuine collapse result have to establish before I'd give the view up, and how near has anyone got to one?

And what does this view explain or predict that its rivals don't? I'd especially like to know whether there's any linguistically or psychologically checkable consequence — something about how speakers manage partial understanding, incomplete belief ascriptions, presupposition failure — because honestly that's what I'd find most persuasive, far more than another thought experiment.

Finally, be blunt about whether the view has been qualified into vacuity. If every counterexample can be absorbed by re-stipulating which scenarios there are or how they're centred, is that fatal, or is that simply what a general semantic framework is supposed to be able to do? I can live with either "it's insulated and that's fine" or "it's insulated and that's a real problem" — I just want the reasoning laid out.

British English please.
