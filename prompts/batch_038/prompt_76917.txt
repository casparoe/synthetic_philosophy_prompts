Been turning something over in my head and I can't tell if it's a real category or just me being squeamish. A while back my brother and I made a deal: he'd switch off buying bottled water by the case, which I think is indefensible, and I'd register and actually vote in municipal elections, which he thinks is indefensible that I skip. Both done, nobody got cheated. Then I made a bigger one with a friend: she gives fifty bucks a month to a group I'm convinced makes things worse, and I volunteer Saturdays at an animal shelter she thinks does more harm than good. We swapped — she dropped the donation, I dropped the shift.

Both times I came out ahead by my own lights, which is why it's hard to complain. But here's what sits wrong with me: I still believe exactly what I believed before. I still think the shelter was worth my Saturday. I just sold off the part of my life that showed it, and now my actions and my views don't line up anymore even though nothing about my views changed. Worse, I catch myself inventorying my beliefs now, looking for ones that cost me a lot to act on but that somebody else would pay to switch off.

I know the general practice has a name, moral trade or something like it, and I know selling out and hypocrisy are in the neighborhood, but neither fits — I'm not pretending to believe anything and nobody's got a gun to my head. Is there an established concept for the specific thing where the exchange leaves the belief intact but strips out the behavior that used to express it? And where's the edge: would taking a paycheck from a company whose product you think is bad count, or is it only about two-sided swaps? Does donating the proceeds back to the cause fix it? If there's no name, what would you call it, and define it so it isn't just hypocrisy with extra steps.
