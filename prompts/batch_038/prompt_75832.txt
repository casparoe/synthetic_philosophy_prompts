The question was:

"State the argument from special relativity to the conclusion that spacetime is a 'block' in which future events are just as real as present ones (the Rietdijk–Putnam argument). Give its premises explicitly, say what it is supposed to establish, and then discuss one objection and whether you think it succeeds."

The student wrote:

"Special relativity makes simultaneity relative: two observers moving with respect to each other disagree about which distant events are happening at the same time as an event they both agree on. The usual illustration here is a pair of passing spaceships, but the popular version uses Andromeda. If you walk briskly toward Andromeda, your plane of simultaneity slices through Andromeda's history a couple of days later than the plane of someone standing still on Earth, and if you walk away it slices a couple of days earlier. Andromeda is about 2.5 million light years off, so a tiny velocity makes a big difference, which is why the argument always uses distant things — locally the effect is negligible.

The argument then needs two further premises. First, no privileged inertial frame: a moving observer's 'now' is no less legitimate than mine. Second, transitivity of the reality relation — if x is real and y is real relative to x, then y is real. So: take an event e in my future, far away. There is some inertial observer passing me right now for whom e is simultaneous with this moment. That observer is just as real as I am. So by transitivity e is real relative to me too. Since e was arbitrary, every event in spacetime is real, and the future is as real as the present: the block universe. Presentism comes out false, because it holds that only what exists now is real and we've just shown that things in my future are real.

Note what this does to freedom. If the future already exists then it is already settled what I'll do tomorrow, so there's no sense in which I could do otherwise. Determinism comes out of the spacetime physics itself, with no assumptions about laws needed.

On objections: one popular reply is that the argument equivocates, because 'real' should be indexed to a single point-event rather than to a whole simultaneity slice, and nothing is real relative to a point except what lies in its past light cone. I think this reply succeeds and dissolves the argument, since once reality is point-relative there's no fact of the matter about a whole slice of the future being real. And the presentist can't escape by re-reading the physics either, since the only way to get absolute simultaneity back is to deny the Lorentz transformations, which are among the best confirmed results in all of physics. So the block stands."
