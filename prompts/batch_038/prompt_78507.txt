I work on a signup flow at a mid-size SaaS company and I've been thinking about our "I agree" checkbox in a way that's probably not normal for an engineer.

Here's the setup. New user hits the form, there's an unticked box saying they agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, both linked. The ToS is about 14,000 words — I counted, its roughly 45 minutes of actual reading — and section 11 has an arbitration clause and a class-action waiver buried in it. We hash the exact version each user agreed to and keep that log for years, which we treat as a real artifact, not a formality. Then, separately, the same document says we can change it whenever we want, we'll post the new version, and continued use counts as acceptance.

Meanwhile for marketing emails we run a totally different and much more careful opt-in flow, and I'm pretty sure a pre-ticked box wouldn't count as consent under GDPR anyway. Anything touching payments or health data gets extra explicit confirmation steps. And we say you have to be 13+ but do literally nothing to check.

So what theory of consent or agreement does the checkbox practice actually presuppose? It can't be "this person understood and chose these terms," because we know for a fact essentially nobody reads it and we've A/B tested the checkbox placement and wording specifically to raise the tick rate. But it also isn't pure theater that everyone agrees to treat as theater, because we build serious infrastructure around it — the hashes, the retention, the "which version did you accept" support tickets — like the moment of assent is a real event with real content.

Is it presupposing that agreement is about outward behavior rather than inner state? About allocating risk? About who bears the cost of reading? Which rival accounts of consent does the practice quietly rule out, and where is it internally inconsistent — especially given that one party can rewrite the thing and your continued use counts as a yes, which doesn't look like assent to me at all.

Also: reconstruct what would actually have to change in how signup flows get built if we swapped in a standard requiring genuine comprehension. And please separate what the practice strictly presupposes from stuff that merely fits it nicely — I'd guess several pretty different theories license the exact same checkbox, and I want to know which parts are underdetermined.

Tell me how confident you are in the reconstruction overall, and single out the one claim in it you're least sure about.
