Suppose I grant you, for the sake of the exercise, the following factual background: some AI capability that would be civilization-ending if deployed carelessly becomes reachable within eight years by any one of roughly five hundred independent, well-resourced teams; no legal enforcement of any kind exists; and the only governance instrument on the table is a voluntary reputational norm under which deployment requires the affirmative assent of at least two of a standing panel of five designated assessors, those assessors being drawn from the research community with the deepest interest in the capability itself.

The proposition I want a credence on is this: under those stipulated conditions, the two-of-five assessor norm would reduce total existential risk relative to a baseline of individually responsible judgment, where "reduce" means that the risk removed by gating reckless deployments exceeds the risk added by delaying protective or corrective deployments and by displacing activity toward actors who never sign on. I am not asking whether the norm is admirable, or whether some better instrument exists; I am asking whether this instrument, in this setting, is net risk-reducing.

Give me a single number, and then the decomposition that produces it. At minimum I want terms for: how much of the catastrophe risk in this setting really is generated by the many-actors asymmetry the norm is designed to blunt, as against concentration in one or two dominant teams for whom the norm is irrelevant; the probability of high compliance by the teams that matter most; the probability that two assessors' errors are independent enough for their agreement to carry evidential weight, given that all five are selected from the population most enthusiastic about the capability; and the probability that the induced delay raises risk more than the gating lowers it — the mirror image of the curse, in which the same structural asymmetry that makes dangerous action cheap makes protective action expensive. Add or substitute terms where you think mine are the wrong ones, but state what each term is a probability of.

Then: how the terms combine, which one your final number is most sensitive to, which one you are least confident in, and what single piece of evidence or argument would move the number furthest. My own credence is about 0.75 and a colleague's is about 0.30; tell me which of your terms most plausibly accounts for that gap, and whether it is a disagreement about a probability or about the framing of the proposition itself.
