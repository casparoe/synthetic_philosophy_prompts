My reading group in philosophy of language has spent a year talking past itself about machine translation, and it's really two camps. One follows Hofstadter in Le Ton beau de Marot: a word carries a penumbra of associations built out of having a body and a life, and translating means re-making that penumbra in another culture, so a system with no such life can only mimic the surface however fluent it sounds. The other says that's unfalsifiable goalpost-moving, that there's no seperate faculty called understanding to point at, and that competence under hard enough constraints is all the notion ever meant.

Draft the joint statement they could both sign. I want a numbered section of claims both accept (about what current systems do well, about what translation involves, about why fluency is weak evidence); a disagreements table with each row giving the claim in wording each side would sign for its own position; and the cruxes, each with a test both would accept and both sides' predictions written down in advance.

Work at least two of these in as candidate cruxes. One, the anomaly case: a source text with a slip in it — a date that can't be right, a referent who wasn't there, a sense that only lands if you know something the writer assumes. Human translators stall, flag, sometimes repair; a system renders the slip confidently. What do each of them predict if you fine-tune on editor-corrected translations, and does a system that learns to flag count as having understood, or as having learned a new surface trick? Two, constrained poetry: get predictions about K distinct renderings of Marot's Ma mignonne that keep the tight line-length and rhyme, judged blind by poets — how many are acceptable, and how many are genuinely distinct rather than near-duplicates.

No crux may be phrased as 'it just needs more scale' or 'it's the Chinese Room again' unless you say what observation would distinguish them. And tell me which bits of the row are verbal — I suspect shallowness and understanding are smuggling work — and if so, which side's usage is worth keeping and why. British English please; it's going out as a workshop handout.
