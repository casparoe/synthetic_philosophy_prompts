I'm drafting a quals answer on van Fraassen's Reflection principle and I've cornered myself. Here's the argument I keep reconstructing, and it seems to show that a rational agent can never learn anything.

(1) Reflection: if you're certain now that at some later time t your credence in H will be x, then your credence in H now should be x.
(2) I conditionalise, and I'm certain that in a year's time, having read the evidence, my credence in H will be some definite value — call it x. (Either it'll be definite or it won't; if it won't, I've nothing to reason with, so assume it will.)
(3) By (1) and (2), my credence in H now should be x.
(4) Generalise: whatever credence I'll end up with after a year of inquiry is the credence I should already have.
(5) But if inquiry can only ever move me to credences I should already have had, it can't improve my doxastic state — there's nothing left for the evidence to do.
(6) And since the same reasoning runs forward from every stage, any change I'm certain I'll make is one I should already have made, so a rational agent's credences are static over time.
(7) So Reflection, together with ordinary conditionalisation, rules out learning, and one of them has to go.
