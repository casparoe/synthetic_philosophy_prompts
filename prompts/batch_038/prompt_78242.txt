Ok so, third-year philosophy PhD here, im writing a chapter on the metaphysics of interpretability and I want to stage a confrontation with Quine in it, and I could use a sparring partner before I hand anything to my advisor.

Quick setup, in case you want to check I've got the dialectic right. Some people doing mechanistic interp are straight-up realists: sparse autoencoders, activation patching, steering vectors — these uncover genuine structure, features and circuits that were in there all along, the way the periodic table uncovered real elements rather than inventing them. Other people think that's inflated. On their view the features are artifacts of the researcher's choices (which layer, which corpus, which sparsity penalty, which dictionary size), and any of a huge number of mutually seperate feature sets would support exactly the same intervention predictions, so there's no fact of the matter about whether some direction in activation space is really a feature.

Here's the wrinkle I actually care about, and its not the standard version of the debate, so please engage with this specifically instead of the generic realism/anti-realism thing. Suppose two labs fit sparse dictionaries to the same layer of the same model, with different regularizers. The resulting feature lists overlap maybe a third of the way. But each dictionary is just as good as the other at predicting what happens under intervention — patch in either one and you steer the model the same way — and neither is more economical all-in, since what one lab saves in dictionary size the other saves in the complexity of its decoding rule. On top of that, the model itself, when asked in plain language, gives fluent and apparently accurate self-descriptions that shift depending on which of the two vocabularies the question happens to be phrased in.

Given all that: what would Quine have made of it? Don't give me the greatest hits — I can recite his line about being a value of a variable and the bit about posits being on a par with the gods of myth just as well as you can. I want you to actually run his machinery on the case: ontological relativity and relativity to a background language, proxy functions, underdetermination of a theory by all possible evidence, his pragmatic criteria for choosing between empirically equivalent theories, and his deep distrust of first-person authority coming out of the radical translation stuff. Would he call the features real? Would he think the question is malformed rather than hard? Does the self-report twist move him at all, or would he just file those reports away as more verbal behavior to be predicted?

And the one I most want pressure-tested: would his verdict differ in any way from what he'd say about quarks, or about sets? Interp feels special because the 'observation' is itself produced by a theory-laden artifact — the dictionary — rather than by a bubble chamber, and because the object of study talks back. Is that a real epistemic difference for a Quinean, or just a difference of degree that his holism swallows whole?

Take a side and argue for it. I'd much rather read a committed case than a survey of options. American English please, and somewhere around a thousand words is fine. No need for references or a reading list, I've got that end handled.
