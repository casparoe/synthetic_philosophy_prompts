My area is judgment aggregation, and I've found I can no longer explain the discursive dilemma to non-specialists without a whiteboard. I'd like to try the other route. Write a story, 1,800–2,500 words, on the following stipulated case; take every detail of it as given, including the arithmetic.

A five-person landmarks commission decides whether to list a disused pumping station that a developer wants to level. Its charter provides that listing follows if the building has either architectural significance (p) or documented historical significance (q), and that the commission votes separately on p, on q, and on the listing itself. The votes come out: one commissioner finds the station significant on both grounds, one on the architectural ground alone, one on the historical ground alone, and two on neither. So p is rejected 3–2, q is rejected 3–2, and the listing is carried 3–2. Aggregating across the premises denies the listing; aggregating across the outcome grants it — the reverse of the direction the familiar conjunctive case runs. No commissioner is individually inconsistent; the inconsistency sits in the set the commission holds as a body.

Three things the story must do.

First, stay in the third person, limited to the chair, who sets the order of voting and who holds a preference about the outcome she never voices. The commission has no rule for choosing between procedures, so she must choose one in practice; leave the reader unable to settle whether her reasoning is an argument or an appetite.

Second, have one commissioner misreport a premise to move the outcome. Treat this as a small, ordinary act of dishonesty with a face attached, not as an illustration of a manipulability result.

Third, end after the vote, in the drafting of the published decision: the commission owes a single justification for a listing whose grounds a majority rejected. Let the last line of the story be a line of that document.

No philosophers among the characters, no passages of exposition, and neither "discursive dilemma" nor "doctrinal paradox" appears anywhere in it. The logic should be exact and invisible.
