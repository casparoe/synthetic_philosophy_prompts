A friend and I spent most of last night on a single question, arrived nowhere, and I would now rather have a third party's number than more dialectic. The terrain is Dennett's 1988 case against qualia, and in particular his pair of coffee tasters who, some years into a job ensuring the constancy of a house blend, both announce that they can no longer stand it — with the blend itself demonstrably unchanged and their colleagues insisting it tastes exactly as it always has. One taster attributes the change entirely to his own cultivated preferences: the taste experience is precisely what it was, but he has grown out of liking it. The other attributes it entirely to his perceptual machinery: his preferences are untouched, and if only his tasting apparatus still worked as it used to he would love the coffee as much as ever. Dennett's point is that neither man has, or could have, anything that tells him which description of his own case is correct.

My friend concludes from this that the concept is empty: if no difference in the world could correspond to the difference between the two hypotheses, they are not two hypotheses. I think the argument proves less than that, because the impossibility it exploits depends on the absence of a comparison class, and that absence looks contingent rather than conceptual. To test my own side, I constructed a variant, and the variant is what I most want your number on.

Call her the third taster. From the day she is hired she maintains, under seal and out of her own reach, a running log containing (a) her own reports about the blend at fixed intervals, and (b) her performance on forced-choice discriminations — which of two cups is sweeter, which more bitter, whether two samples are the same or different — with the ground truth recorded by whatever instrument generated the pairs. Six years in, she hates the blend, exactly as the other two do. But the log can now be opened and cross-checked against a fresh battery of the same discriminations, and suppose the result is this: her present discriminatory profile has drifted in a specific and structured way, she has become reliably worse at exactly the distinctions her earlier self made reliably, and her reports have shifted only where her discriminations have shifted.

Does that settle which of the two taster-hypotheses is true of her? If it does, does talk of qualia thereby acquire empirical content — or has the log merely pinned down a change in a discriminatory and dispositional profile, which is what a functionalist said the whole dispute was about from the beginning?

Here is the proposition I want a credence on, stated as tightly as I can manage. Call it Q:

Q: There is no property P that is simultaneously intrinsic (non-relational, not constituted by its relations to other states or to behaviour), ineffable (not fully capturable in any description), private (accessible to its subject alone), and directly or immediately apprehensible (known non-inferentially, with a presumption of incorrigibility), while also being the very property we are aware of and talking about when we report how an experience seems to us.

I want your P(Q), and I want it derived rather than declared. These are the terms I have in mind; add, drop, or redraw any of them, but say why:

- P(the four marks are jointly unsatisfiable — that no property whatever can bear all four).
- P(a property could bear all four yet be idle: the incoherence lying not in the marks being impossible but in their being unable to latch onto anything our reports, discriminations, and theories are actually about).
- P(introspection constitutes a distinct, non-inferential source of knowledge, reliable enough to ground first-person present-tense claims of the form "it seems to me that things are thus-and-so").
- P(a case like the third taster's would genuinely adjudicate the quale-changed versus disposition-changed question, rather than merely refining a functional description).
- P(a frank epiphenomenalism — qualia doing no causal or explanatory work at all — is a stable retreat for the realist rather than a self-undermining one; note that this term cuts against Q).
- P(the dispute is substantially verbal, so that a realist and an eliminativist who agree on every functional, behavioural, and neural fact are not really disagreeing about anything).
- P(a residual, non-traditional notion of phenomenal character — something like the property picked out by attending to and judging of a state — counts as saving qualia rather than as changing the subject).

For each term: a number, one or two lines of justification, and then the combination into P(Q). Be explicit about the arithmetic you are using — a product, a disjunction over the ways Q could hold, a weighted mix — and flag any dependence between terms that makes a naive product misleading; I suspect several of these are far from independent, and I would rather you say where than pretend otherwise.

Then the diagnostics: which single term is your final number most sensitive to, which term are you least confident in your own estimate of, and what one piece of evidence or argument would move the total furthest. Finally, if you had to say where my friend's position and mine actually diverge — which of these terms she and I are most likely to score very differently, given the way I have described our views — say which, and whether the third taster case resolves that divergence or merely relocates it.
