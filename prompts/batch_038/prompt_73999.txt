I moved from Aarhus to the Bay Area in the summer to take up a research post, and in the months since I have found that I cannot hold my own views about the celebration of success steady. My partner is American; our daughter is nine and has begun to speak the local idiom of exceptionalism with unsettling fluency; my family in Jutland responds to my new position with a politeness I recognize as disapproval. I would like an audit of the position I actually hold, because I suspect it does not cohere and I cannot tell which piece I ought to surrender.

The set, stated as carefully as I can manage:

1. Admiration is a fitting response to excellence. To admire an outstanding piece of work, and the person who made it, is a form of attention that good lives include, and a culture that has extinguished it cannot recover what it has lost by other means.

2. The Law of Jante is satire, but it is not mistaken. Sandemose's town, with its injunction against supposing yourself anything special, describes a real discipline, and that discipline protects a real good: equality of standing among people who must go on living with one another. The reflexes I was raised in — deflecting praise, declining to make a fuss, treating conspicuous self-promotion as a breach of manners — are not merely resentment dressed as modesty. What Australians describe as cutting down the tall poppy looks cruel from where I am sitting, but it appears to me to be doing something.

3. I am a fairly thoroughgoing luck egalitarian. Native ability, temperament, health, family, and the accident of being born at the right moment in the right economy are brute luck, and the returns to them are not deserved in any sense capable of grounding a claim.

4. Praise and blame should track what is up to the agent. Where an outcome is not attributable to the agent's choices, the fitting response is appreciation of a good result, not credit, and certainly not rank.

5. Envy is a vice and I have no wish to vindicate it. But the resentment of those who have been left behind is frequently a reliable signal that something in the arrangement is unjust, and should be treated as evidence rather than as a symptom to be managed away.

6. Publicly honouring the successful is worth what it costs, because it supplies aspirational models: a child who sees someone from her own background recognized for doing difficult work acquires a possibility she would otherwise never have entertained. This seems to me a considerably better defence of the American idiom than the defence from desert, and I want very much to keep it.

7. My own addition, which I flag as idiosyncratic: admiration should be proportioned not to the height a person reached but to the distance they travelled from where they started. Call it the counterfactual-distance principle.

What I am after is an audit, not a survey of the debate. Please tell me which of these commitments are in genuine tension with one another, ordered by severity rather than presented as equally troubling; for each genuine tension, what the least costly revision would be and what that revision would cost me in the rest of the set; and which of the conflicts I have probably imagined, since I expect at least one of the pairs above is only apparently at odds and would rather be told that plainly than have all seven flagged. I would also like to know whether (7) is earning its place here or is doing nothing that some other member of the list does not already do, and if the latter, whether it inherits that member's difficulties.

Two further requests. First, tell me where your own analysis is most likely to be wrong: which of your verdicts about these tensions rests on the shakiest step, and what kind of consideration would move you to rank them differently or to dissolve a tension you are here calling real. Second, if there is a distinction I have not thought of that would let more of this set stand together than your analysis permits, I would much rather hear it than be told to choose — but please do not manufacture one for my comfort. I have no attachment to any particular member of the list and no preference about which survives. I only want to know which of them cannot stay.
