My parents confirmed it, my father took it considerably harder than I did, and we've all more or less landed somewhere livable. I want to be explicit that I'm not asking for the usual reassurance about who a "real" parent is. I've heard that speech, I largely accept it, and it isn't what's troubling me.

My mother's response was that it couldn't matter as much as I think, because "it would have been you anyway — if the clinic had used a different donor that day, we'd still have gotten you, just with different eyes." My roommate, a genetics major, told her this was nonsense: if a different sperm had been used, a different child would have been born, and I would never have existed at all; there is no version of events in which I come from anything other than the exact egg and the exact sperm that made me. My roommate's claim sounds rigorous, but it seems to imply that the person I am was settled by a microscopic event in a plastic dish, and that if it had gone slightly differently no one would ever have noticed, because someone indistinguishable from me would have been sitting at my parents' table in my place. I also can't get a grip on the scenarios themselves. The clinic used a technique in which a single sperm is injected directly into a single egg, so "which sperm" was a determinate event; my mother produced fourteen eggs that cycle, so "which egg" was too; and my roommate added that even holding both fixed, the embryo could have been cultured a day longer, or could have split in two, in which case she wants to know which of the resulting twins would have been me. Meanwhile I've met five of my six half-siblings, all of them produced by the same man's sperm and none of them remotely me, which feels like evidence for my roommate's side without my being able to say why.
