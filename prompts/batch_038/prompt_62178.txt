For three years I've run my working life on commitment devices: money auto-transfers into an account with a thirty-day withdrawal delay, a paid site-blocker runs during my work hours, and my sister holds five hundred dollars that she keeps if I miss a client deadline. The devices aren't broken — I keep disarming them. Twice I've paid the early-withdrawal penalty on the grounds that this particular month was genuinely exceptional. I switched to a blocker whose admin password only I know. I've talked my sister out of both forfeits with reasons that struck me as sound at the time. Each defection was calm, planned in advance, and accompanied by a case for why the rule I'd set didn't actually cover this situation.

Two things I can't work out. First, if the version of me that overrides these rules acts deliberately and with what feels like better information about the present moment, is it possible the devices were never evidence of a weakness I was correcting but of a goal I don't in fact hold — that is, that I was the one who was wrong, not my later self? Second, does it make sense to keep building stronger ones, given that the same judgment that designs the lock also decides when to pick it? My therapist tells me to stop treating myself as an adversary and simply want better things, but I can't see what that would consist in over and above what I end up choosing when the moment arrives. Tell me where the criticism should actually land, and what you'd have me do differently.
