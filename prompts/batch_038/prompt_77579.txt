I am preparing the closing session of an upper-level metaphysics seminar on modality, and I have assigned Everything Everywhere All at Once (2022) in place of a paper. A colleague warns me that the session will do nothing but entrench the undergraduate habit of treating David Lewis's modal realism as a colorful version of the Everett interpretation, so I want a reading that keeps the two rigorously apart without draining the film of philosophical interest. Please write it as continuous critical prose I can adapt for the seminar, taking Lewis's program in On the Plurality of Worlds seriously on its own terms. I am not after a survey, and I do not want a bibliography, citations, or page references: I want the reading and the reasoning.

Work through the following, in roughly this order.

First, the film's actual modal commitments. Lewis's worlds are spatiotemporally interrelated sums, isolated from one another, with no causal commerce across the boundary and no trans-world identity; the film's universes are connected by verse-jumping, and skills, dispositions, and even stretches of memory appear to travel along those connections. Is the film committed to a branching-time or Everett-style picture, to literal trans-world identity, or to something Lewis never contemplated? I do not want the flat verdict that the film is metaphysically wrong. I want to know precisely which of Lewis's commitments each of its devices violates, and which violations are dramatic licence that a Lewisian could absorb as talk of counterparts.

Second, where the film may nonetheless be Lewisian. My own hunch, which I would like tested rather than flattered, is that its central emotional movement — Evelyn's discovery that she is the version of herself who has failed at everything, and her subsequent decision to remain in the laundromat universe anyway — dramatizes Lewis's thesis that actual is an indexical, true of whatever world its speaker occupies and carrying no intrinsic privilege. The film's disorientation also strikes me as self-locating rather than ordinary ignorance: Evelyn does not lack facts about the pluriverse so much as a fact about where she stands within it, which is what Lewis's centered contents were introduced to handle. Does the film sustain that reading, and what does it make of the corollary that every Evelyn, from inside her own universe, is the actual one?

Third, the everything bagel. Jobu Tupaki's nihilism appears to run: because every possibility is realized somewhere, no outcome is significant, and annihilation is therefore the only coherent response. The film answers with kindness, laundry, taxes, and two rocks wearing googly eyes in a lifeless universe. A Lewisian reply seems available — significance is world-bound, and the fact that things go otherwise in other worlds is no more a reason for despair than the fact that things go otherwise in distant regions of this one — though I am not certain Lewis ever pressed it in that form, so please say whether you are reporting his reply or reconstructing one his commitments license. Does the film actually make that reply, or does it substitute a change of attitude for an argument? And is the bagel's pull a live objection modal realism never answered, or a confusion the film exposes?

Fourth, a distinction I have been using and want sharpened or rejected. Through the first two acts the multiverse functions as an arsenal: alternate selves supply skills and escape routes, and plenitude is a resource. In the third act it functions as an audit: alternate selves supply standards against which this life scores badly, and plenitude becomes a comparison class. Only the second seems to me to generate a genuinely Lewisian problem. Is the distinction real, does the film mark it structurally, and does its resolution amount to what I have been calling localism about significance — the view that the weight of an act is fixed by what happens in the world where it is performed?

Fifth, recombination. Universes with hot dogs for fingers and raccoons piloting chefs look like exactly what Lewis's principle of recombination licenses, but as I understand it the principle recombines individuals into a spatiotemporally connected whole and leaves the laws to supervene on what a world contains; it does not permit a world to house a device that reaches into another. Does the film respect that limit, or does its plenitude outrun anything Lewis would grant it?

Sixth, where I must abandon the reading. Tell me plainly which of the above is my imposition, which scenes and relationships resist it — I suspect Waymond's remark that in another life he would have wanted nothing more than doing laundry and taxes with Evelyn cuts against the localism I want to find — and what the film would have to be for the Lewisian reading to be honest rather than convenient.

Close with two short answers. What is the single most damaging misconception about modal realism this film is likely to leave my students with, and what is the one thing it shows about plenitude, indexicality, or the significance of choice that argument alone does not show? I am prepared to be told that the answer to the second question is nothing, though I doubt it.
