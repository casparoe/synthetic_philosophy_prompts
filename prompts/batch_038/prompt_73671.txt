Please compose a piece of corporate satire of roughly 850 to 900 words, in the form of an internal policy document issued by the Office of General Counsel of a fictional multinational trading and logistics group. The premise: the firm has, by administrative decree, resolved that every contract, notice, memorandum, and standard term it issues shall be drafted in strict conformity with a Russellian analysis of definite descriptions, and that denotational failures are henceforth to be treated as compliance defects rather than as matters of interpretation. The document should consist of a brief cover memorandum announcing the policy and its commercial rationale, followed by numbered sections of the resulting manual — a Global Reference Standards instrument, revision four, say — and closing with an annex containing one specimen form.

The humour must be generated entirely by getting the semantics right, so please build in the following:

- A mandatory attestation, signed by a responsible officer, that exactly one entity in the relevant domain satisfies each description used in an executed document, together with an internal precedent showing this requirement breaking down for descriptions of future or hypothetical counterparties, for plural and mass descriptions, and for descriptions of the firm's own premises, which cannot be expanded far enough to secure uniqueness without rendering the instrument unusable.
- A scope-discipline section obliging drafters to record, on the annexed form, whether a description occurring within the scope of a negation, a modal, or an attitude verb takes the wide or the narrow reading, with the consequence that ordinary exclusion and indemnity clauses now require a rider nobody can read; the practice should look absurd but be semantically impeccable throughout.
- A fallback provision for the litigation department, founded on the thought that where a description fails to denote no statement is made and so nothing false is asserted, and therefore no representation can have been breached — and the risk committee's expressed concern that opposing counsel will answer that this excuse trades on what the speaker meant to convey rather than on what the sentence says, with a proposed remedy that makes things worse.
- A section governing descriptions used to pick out a particular individual, whoever that individual turns out to be, as against descriptions used to say whatever fits, and the firm's attempts to police the difference in drafting practice.
- A disciplinary provision concerning a sister office in another jurisdiction that persists in treating proper names as tags that pick out the same bearer in every counterfactual circumstance, and the resulting cross-border conflict in which a vessel named in one clause and described in another behaves differently under the same force majeure analysis. Play this entirely straight.

Observe these constraints. Maintain a deadpan administrative register from first word to last: no winking, no asides to the reader, no confessing note, no indication anywhere that the firm considers itself ridiculous. Invent all parties, matters, clause text, and precedent references; do not quote, or paraphrase closely enough to be recognisable as a quotation, from any published philosophical work, and supply no references, bibliography, or citations of any kind — the manual's only authority is its own precedent file, which may include one venerable matter in which a sovereign sought confirmation of whether a certain gentleman had in fact written a certain novel, and the file's failure to dispose of it. The target of the satire is a practice and a style of discourse — the imposition of a semantic theory on drafting by decree, and the treatment of ambiguity as a defect to be certified away — and not any philosopher, living or dead, none of whom should be named. A reader with no philosophy of language should still find the bureaucracy funny; a specialist should recognise that the existence and uniqueness conditions, scope interaction with negation, the incompleteness problem, empty descriptions, presupposition failure, the referential-attributive distinction, and rigidity have all been handled correctly. Close with a self-undermining beat in which the instrument's own title and signature block breach its provisions, so that on its own analysis the policy issues from no one, and leave it genuinely unclear whether the firm regards that as a joke or as a finding.
