I assess claims for a UK advertising standards team: roughly forty complaints a week over whether an advertisement, packaging line or online banner misleads, and my recommendation goes to a two-person panel with no training in linguistics or philosophy. Our code prohibits claims that mislead by implication as well as claims that are literally false, but it offers nothing on how implication is generated or when it can be undone, so we argue case by case and advertisers reply that they never said the offending thing.

Please write the procedure we could run on any claim, grounded in what Grice actually establishes about what is said versus what is meant, conversational implicature and the maxims. I want an ordered set of tests, each with a line on what counts as a sufficient answer: how to fix what the claim literally says; how to reconstruct step by step the implicature an ordinary consumer would derive, and how to tell when there is no such derivation to be had; how to distinguish a generalised implicature from one that depends on the particular context, and why that distinction changes what we may hold the advertiser to; and whether footnotes, asterisks and a closing phrase such as "terms and conditions apply" genuinely cancel an implicature, given that a theorist's cancellability test is not the same thing as a speaker's disclaimer. State what the procedure assumes about whether an audience treats commercial messaging as cooperative in the first place, and what changes if it does not. Mark the stages that are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and the point at which the question stops being about meaning — materiality, consumer expectation, remedy — and goes to the panel. We have twenty minutes per claim.

Then run it on three: a savings advertisement headlined "5% interest" where the rate is capped at balances under £1,000, disclosed in small print; an energy tariff promising savings "up to £312 a year" on terms almost no customer satisfies; and one claim of your choosing that clears the procedure intact but that the panel will be certain is a trick. For the last, say plainly what our reasoning fails to capture, and finish with what this procedure cannot be used for at all. Please write the answer in British English.
