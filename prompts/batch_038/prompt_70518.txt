Before I commit to a position in writing, I want to be sure I understand how special relativity bears on the dispute between endurantism and perdurantism, because on my reading the physics simply decides it, and I cannot see what is left to argue about.

My understanding runs as follows. A perdurantist holds that a persisting object is a four-dimensional aggregate of temporal parts, none of them any less real than the others; that is a commitment to all times and their contents standing on a par, which is to say a commitment to eternalism. An endurantist holds that an object is wholly present at every moment at which it exists, and that requires there to be a determinate fact about which events are co-present with any given moment, which is to say a commitment to presentism, or at least to a privileged simultaneity relation. The relativity of simultaneity denies that there is any frame-independent such fact. So endurantism is incompatible with our best-confirmed physical theory, and four-dimensionalism is what remains standing once that theory is accepted.

What I would like is an account of what the live disagreement could consist in once that much is granted. If the physics forces temporal parts on us, then endurantists who continue to defend their view must either be quietly rejecting relativity or must mean something by their central claim other than what I am taking them to mean. I suspect the latter, but I cannot identify a reading that is not merely a relabeling of perdurantism in endurantist vocabulary. So: what can an endurantist who accepts special relativity without reservation actually say about how objects persist, and why is it not a distinction without a difference? I would also like to know whether the two views are genuinely rivals about the world or merely about how best to describe it, and if they are rivals, what would have to be observed to tell them apart.

Finally, is there any further empirical evidence short of a new theory of spacetime that could bear on which view is correct, or has the question been permanently insulated from physical input by the arguments above?
