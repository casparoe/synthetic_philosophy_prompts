so here's something I want you to actually take a side on rather than survey the literature at me.

the standard story: Maxwell's demon got exorcised by Landauer's principle plus Bennett's observation that a demon running a closed cycle has to wipe its memory eventually, and the erasure of one bit costs at least kT ln 2 of dissipation, which exactly cancels the work the demon seemed to get for free from a single heat bath. Second law safe, information is physical, everyone goes home.

I think there's a premise doing all the work in there that basically never gets argued for, and I want you to argue my reading of it. Call it the **finitude premise**: any device that reliably converts information about a system into work against a single heat bath must, over the long run, either reset some finite memory or burn down a finite non-equilibrium resource that it didn't manufacture. Notice Landauer gives you the *price* of a reset. It doesn't establish that a reset is *inevitable*, and it certainly doesn't establish that whatever the demon is spending is denominated in the same currency as the second law's ledger.

argue for this position: the Landauer/Bennett line isn't a resolution of the demon, it's a relocation of it. Once you write the finitude premise down explicitly, the interesting question turns out to be about the physics of *correlations* — how it is that the demon's record comes to stand in a relation to the gas that's monetizable at all, and why that relation can't be bootstrapped into a perpetual motion machine on its own — and Landauer's principle is just an accounting rule you're entitled to apply only after you've already assumed the thing worth explaining.

keep it concrete. work through a Szilard-engine style setup: one particle in a box, partition inserted, one bit measured, up to kT ln 2 of work extracted, partition removed, cycle repeats. be explicit about where in *your* account the cost lands, and about why that's a different place than where Bennett puts it — not just a redescription of the same place. if you can make the case that a demon with an unbounded memory register that never resets is either physically unavailable or else paying in a currency nobody's tracking, that'd be the strongest version of what I'm after.

on the epistemic side: I want you committed. give me your best argument for the position above, don't sandbag it into "the standard view is also defensible, reasonable people differ." then at the end flag the one or two steps where you think you're most likely just wrong, and say what a defender of Bennett would have to show to break the argument.
