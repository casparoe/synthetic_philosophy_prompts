My sister Ruth died in March. She was a metaphysician — grounding and fundamentality were her whole life — and I'm a novelist, so we spent thirty years arguing about whether anything really needs a reason. She left instructions that I give the eulogy, and one of the instructions was: no ungrounded claims.

Here's what I want. She was a priority monist: she thought the whole is more fundamental than its parts, that parts are grounded in the whole they belong to, and — this mattered enormously to her — grounding is definately not causing, so nobody in that room gets to say she "made" us who we are. Her last unfinished project was about whether grounding has to bottom out at all, or whether there could be endless descent with no fundamental level anywhere. I want that used honestly, and I don't want it smuggled into being a heaven.

The room will be half her grad students, who'll catch every sloppy "in virtue of," and half our family outside Cleveland, who have never heard the word grounding. Four or five minutes. The philosophy can't be decoration — the monism has to actually carry the comfort, so that somebody who's never read a page of it still feels it. And nothing she'd have called a brute fact.
