Write a dialogue between two people arguing over whether the intentional stance is a legitimate crane when applied to a large language model.

Side A (roughly Dennett's line) says describing the model as having beliefs and goals earns its keep: it predicts what the model will do next better than any tractable mechanistic account, and its not positing anything mind-first, so it's a crane rather than a skyhook. Side B is a greedy reductionist who says the only real explanation is gradient descent over next-token statistics, and that taking the model's self-report ("I refused because I took you to be asking for medical advice") at face value smuggles understanding in from nowhere — a skyhook wearing a humbler name.

Neither side gets to win cheap. B has to actually answer: fine, then why does the intentional stance predict so well, and where does that predictive success come from if not from something belief-shaped? A has to actually answer: how is a crane that's doing all the explanatory work any different from the skyhook you claim to be ruling out? Have them pick at each other's specific moves instead of delivering parallel mini-essays, and don't let either one convert by the end. Around 700 words.
