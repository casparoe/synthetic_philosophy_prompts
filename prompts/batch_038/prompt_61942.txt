Our reading group is 14-ish adults, 90 minutes, and about half of them have never read philosophy on purpose. Next week is Mencius vs Xunzi on human nature and I want them to hit the disagreement before anyone hears either name. No lecture up front.

The design I keep circling: two rounds of the same public-goods task at a table. Everyone privately splits a fixed pile of tokens between a private pot and a group pot; the group pot gets multiplied and split evenly, so the group total is the score. Round A has no monitoring, no sanctions, and the group sets its own norms out loud first. Round B is identical except contributions get posted where everyone can see them and there's a real punishment option that costs the punisher something. Then the reveal: the only thing that changed between rounds was an assumption I was privately making about them — decent-needs-the-right-conditions in A, self-interested-needs-constraint in B — and which round got which treatment was a coin flip I didnt mention. What I want them to feel is that neither result is evidence about human nature, because the institution manufactures the behavior that afterwards looks like it confirms the institution's premise. Xunzi-shaped rules make Xunzi-shaped people, Mencius-shaped rules make Mencius-shaped people, and you can't tell which way the arrow went.

Two problems I can already see, and I want you to solve them or overrule me. First, I expect round B to actually score *higher*, which reads as a straight win for Xunzi instead of a demonstration of underdetermination — and then Mencius's side owes an explanation of why the sprouts didn't carry it without an enforcer. Do I lean into the asymmetry, change what's being measured, or something else? Second, punishment sometimes backfires and people punish the generous ones, which is a third outcome I haven't scripted for.

Give me the full rules and materials, a point scheme that needs no real money, minute-by-minute timings, the reveal script, and the debrief questions in the order I should ask them — mapping back onto the four sprouts and the child at the well, stripped Ox Mountain, and the barley-seed comparison on one side, and bad nature, wei as deliberate artifice, and the warped-wood-and-whetstone on the other. Plus what predictably goes wrong the first time I run it and how I recover in the moment, and one honest line about which feature of the debate the activity can't reproduce and I'll have to supply by talking.

And tell me where this is weakest: what would change your mind about whether the design tests anything at all versus just staging my own conclusion? Is there a version of the reveal that hands them real evidence instead of a rigged demo, or is the reflexivity point untestable by construction?
