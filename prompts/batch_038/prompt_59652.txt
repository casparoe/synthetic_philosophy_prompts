I do monitoring and evaluation for a small NGO, and we're about to pitch a project in a highland valley where roughly 300 families still get by on a mix of shifting cultivation, some herding, and a fair amount of wild foods. The package is a higher-yield rice variety plus a microcredit line for seed and fertilizer. My own model says calories per person go up about a third inside five years, which is why the donors love it.

I spent three weeks up there last spring and two things won't leave me alone. People work way less than I do — four hours a day, sometimes less — and nobody I met was hungry or skinny. And when I asked how they handle a really bad argument, the answer was always some version of: one family packs up and joins relatives over the ridge. Nobody holds title to land and nobody can build up a store, because it all gets shared out or spoils. But the loan needs collateral, the new seed wants a fixed plot and a store shed, and the shed wants a lock. Some households did tell me they want it, the young couples especially.

My boss says Im being romantic, that you don't get to veto a 33% calorie bump on vibes, and that people have been farming for ten thousand years so what am I actually suggesting. Is my unease tracking something real? And if it is, what exactly — I want to be able to say it in the meeting instead of mumbling about dignity.
