My sister and her brother-in-law got into a long argument over dinner at my parents' about a proposal where they live to allow regulated paid surrogacy -- right now the country bans it outright and won't recognise arrangements people travel abroad for. He's for it, she's against, and I was just sitting there taking notes. Roughly how it went:

1. He said the ban is paternalism, full stop: a woman who can carry a pregnancy can decide whether to be paid for it, and banning it doesn't stop surrogacy, it just exports it to places with weaker protections for the woman carrying.
2. She said that isn't paternalism, it's commodification -- some things shouldn't be for sale even with consent, and consent offered by someone who needs the money isn't the kind of consent that legitimises a market.
3. He said then you'd have to ban the roofer, the night-shift cleaner and the miner too, since they also work for money they need and also risk their bodies. If poverty voids consent, it voids it there.
4. She said disanalogy: a roofer can walk off the job at 11am. A pregnant woman can't just stop. And since no court can order specific performance of a pregnancy, the contract's central promise gets backed up instead by custody fights and damages claims -- so the market is selling something it legally can't deliver, and substituting pressure for performance.
5. He said that's an argument for regulation, not prohibition: unenforceable handover clauses, her own lawyer, a guaranteed right to keep the child, screening, escrow. And he added that if she'd accept unpaid surrogacy, her objection can't be about the nine months or the entanglement, only about the payment -- which is odd, since paying someone more for the same risky, irreversable activity is supposed to make it worse.
6. She said there is a reason: payment changes who absorbs the damage when something goes wrong. Between people who know each other, a disability or a change of heart gets absorbed by the relationship; in a market it becomes a breach. And she said he'd already conceded this, because his rules strip out the only part of the contract that could be enforced -- at which point it's a gift with a fee attached, and the fee's main function is recruiting people who otherwise wouldn't do it.

Neither budged. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right at each step, not just who wins overall? Two more things I'm curious about: which single move is the crux that the rest hangs on, and is either of them smuggling in a claim about paid egg donation or other paid bodily use that they'd have to give up to stay consistent?
