I am setting the final examination for a masters module in philosophy of biology whose students have been taught the arithmetic of variance partitioning but are unreliable in interpreting it. Please write one question on heritability, worth 40 marks and answerable in 90 minutes with a calculator, together with a model answer that shows the arithmetic and a marking scheme that allocates marks by part and names the particular misinterpretations that should cost marks.

The question must be driven by data rather than by exposition. Give the students this table: the same panel of 40 genotypes of a grain legume is grown at three sites, and dry yield per plant (grams) has the following variance components and means — Site A, an irrigated research station: V_A = 28, V_D = 8, V_E = 4, V_G×E = 0, mean 55; Site B, rain-fed smallholder fields: V_A = 28, V_D = 8, V_E = 32, V_G×E = 12, mean 40; Site C, the same fields under a uniform irrigation-and-extension package: V_A = 28, V_D = 8, V_E = 4, V_G×E = 0, mean 55. Tell the students that the components are stipulated as constant across sites for simplicity.

Build the question out of five parts: (a, 8 marks) compute narrow-sense heritability at each site, and explain why a broad-sense figure is unproblematic at Site A but turns on a convention at Site B, giving both candidate values and saying what the choice does to the reported number; (b, 8 marks) predict the response to a selection differential of 10 g at each site, and explain why identical additive variance buys such different responses, including whether the same 10 g differential represents equal selection intensity at sites with such different phenotypic spreads; (c, 10 marks) assess a ministry official who, told that heritability at Site C is 0.70 against 0.35 at Site B, concludes that the environment has now been "taken care of," that further investment in uniform provision cannot raise yield, and that breeding is the only remaining lever; (d, 8 marks) assess a donor agency that proposes shipping the top-performing lines identified at Site A to Site B on the grounds that yield is 0.90 genetic at Site A, so winners there will be winners anywhere; (e, 6 marks) assess a seed company that reasons that since broad-sense heritability at Site A is 0.90 and Site A's mean exceeds Site B's by 15 g, roughly 90% of that gap is genetic.

The design has to make the numbers do the work: a candidate who recites only that heritability is population-relative and does not describe individuals should fail to reach a pass. State explicitly in the marking scheme what separates an A from a B, and I expect that to include noticing that heritability at Site C doubles precisely because the environment was improved and equalised, that the shared germplasm already refutes the inference from within-site heritability to the between-site mean gap, and that holding V_A fixed across environments is itself a simplification worth flagging. The question must be self-contained, requiring no outside literature.
