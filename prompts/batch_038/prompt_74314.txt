My colleague and I have been going in circles for months about what the "what" in equality is supposed to be, and I've decided to stop asking her what she believes and start reading her verdicts instead. Here's what she's on record about:

- Two wheelchair users with identical incomes and identical savings, one living in a town with step-free kerbs and a lift-equipped bus fleet, the other in a town with neither: the second one is worse off, and not just because of some consideration outside equality.
- Someone who starts from exactly her neighbour's bundle and sinks half of it into rare wine, and is left short, while the neighbour is perfectly happy on cheap beer: no complaint that justice ought to recognise.
- A woman with a rare metabolic condition who needs an eye-wateringly expensive diet, versus a foodie spending the same sum on equally unnecessary luxuries: the first has a claim on the rest of us, the second doesn't, even though the amounts are identical.
- A well-off man who decides to live on rice and lentils and gives the rest away: not worse off at all, even though his actual standard of living is miles below his neighbour's.

The new case I want to spring on her: a woman in a poor district who draws the same pay as her brother, has the same years of schooling available to her on paper, says she's genuinely content with her life, and was never steered toward the apprenticeships or the jobs he was steered toward — she insists she doesn't want them. Is she worse off than he is?

So: predict her verdict. Then tell me which metrics are still live candidates given those four verdicts — I reckon at least three survive all of them and I cant work out how to tell them apart — say which one you'd bet on and why, and give me one further case I could put to her where her own answers would come apart rather than let her stay neutral.
