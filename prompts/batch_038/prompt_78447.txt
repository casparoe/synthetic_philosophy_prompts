I teach a graduate seminar on fundamentality, and over the past year I have been assembling my own settled verdicts about what grounds what, in order to discover whether they are jointly explicable by a single account. I should like you to attempt that discovery on my behalf, and then to repair or replace the provisional principle I have been working with.

The verdicts follow. I record them as conclusions I have defended at length, not as passing intuitions.

(1) Where Socrates exists, the singleton of Socrates exists as well, and each entails the other. Still, the member grounds the singleton and not the reverse. Necessitation here runs in both directions, so whatever grounding amounts to, it cannot be a direction of entailment.

(2) The vase's being crimson grounds its being coloured. Its being coloured does not ground its being crimson.

(3) That it is raining grounds that it is raining or that the volcano erupted, notwithstanding that the second disjunct is false. Moreover, if both disjuncts were true, I maintain that each would ground the disjunction — and yet I am unwilling to concede that the disjunction is thereby grounded twice over, or that it admits of two competing complete explanations.

(4) That snow is white grounds the truth of the thought that snow is white; the truth does not ground the fact.

(5) Arithmetical and logical truths — that two and three make five, that it either rains or does not — obtain, but nothing grounds them, and no portion of the world explains them. At the same time I cannot bring myself to call them fundamental in the sense I reserve for the electron's charge or for the distribution of physical magnitudes at a time. So I posit a third status, and I shall call it autonomy: such facts are true in virtue of nothing whatsoever, being neither grounded in anything nor themselves grounds of anything. I regard this category as indispensable.

(6) That an act would inflict agony for its own sake grounds its being wrong. But the further fact that agony of that sort grounds wrongness is not itself grounded in any natural fact; I take it to be fundamental. Hence some fundamental facts mention properties that are themselves derivative. I am also drawn to a purity constraint — that the fundamental facts, taken jointly, should be statable without mentioning anything derivative — and I confess that I do not know how to reconcile that attraction with what I have just said.

(7) Every fact is either fundamental or grounded; there is no third option. Grounding chains are irreflexive, asymmetric, and finite, terminating in facts that are grounded in nothing.

(8) Nothing grounds itself. But neither do I want my account to generate self-grounding from mere notational variants: that the vase is crimson and cold and that it is cold and crimson are one fact, not two mutually grounding facts.

My provisional principle is this: x grounds y just in case (i) x and y both obtain; (ii) x metaphysically necessitates y; (iii) y is more general than x, in that there are more ways for y to obtain than for x to obtain; and (iv) y does not necessitate x.

I know already that it fails. Clause (iv) wrongly excludes (1). Clauses (ii) and (iii) wrongly make every fact whatever a ground of the truths in (5), since anything necessitates a logical truth and anything is more specific than one. Clause (iii) threatens (8) with a spurious ordering among notational variants. And the principle is simply silent on (6) and (7).

Please now deliver the following.

(a) A repaired principle, or a small set of genuinely rival candidates, formulated precisely enough to be applied mechanically rather than by taste. For each clause of each candidate, identify which of my verdicts motivates it and what work it performs that no other clause can. State explicitly which notions you are treating as primitive, and whether the repair requires a new primitive — essence or nature, relative naturalness, an irreducible idiom of holding-in-virtue-of — beyond necessitation and generality.

(b) A candid cost analysis. Is there any account that preserves all of (1) through (8)? If not, I want the choice put sharply: the baroque principle that saves every verdict, against the parsimonious one that costs me at least one. Recommend one, and say which verdict it surrenders and why that is the cheapest. I expect that (5) and (7) are the pair that cannot both survive, and that (6) with its purity attraction is a second such pair; tell me whether I am right about either.

(c) Verdicts, with the responsible clause named in each instance, on cases I have not listed: whether the general fact that every fact is fundamental, grounded, or autonomous itself enjoys one of those three statuses, and which; whether a necessarily existent concrete totality, which necessitates everything, thereby grounds everything; whether the grounding facts are themselves grounded, as in the question of whether the bridge fact in (6) is grounded in the natures of the properties it relates; and whether a fact taken together with an autonomous truth grounds what the fact alone grounds, or whether the autonomous component contributes something.

(d) An assessment of whether autonomy as I have characterised it is a stable category or collapses, and if it collapses, into which of its neighbours and with what consequences for (7).

(e) A closing statement of which of my verdicts your recommended account cannot accommodate, together with the least revision to my set of judgments that would restore consistency.
