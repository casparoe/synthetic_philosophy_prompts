im in my third year of a history degree and for the last discussion section of our nationalism seminar the prof let me run whatever I want, so I want to make a "which theory of the age of nations do you actually hold?" sorting quiz — 8 to 10 items, each a concrete case or a claim about a case, two or three options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions.

the whole point is that someone who has never read Anderson or Gellner or Hobsbawm should still land in the right leaf, so no item is allowed to just ask whether nations are ancient or modern, and none of them should contain a position name. cases should be the kind of thing we argue about in seminar: a kingdom whose vernacular only got standardized and mass-printed in the 1800s, a border drawn in a european chancellery that later produced a population ready to die for it, a stateless people with shared myths and genealogies and a remembered golden age but no institutions, a national dress/festival/anthem you can trace to a specific committee in the last 150 years, an administrative unit with continuous records back to the middle ages, a diaspora that keeps the myths alive with no territory.

the distinctions that actually have to do the separating work, as far as I can tell: whether shared myth and memory is enough for a nation to already exist or whether it takes standardized literacy and mass communication; whether an invented tradition needs a cynical inventor or can grow up anonymously and be sincerely experienced as ancient; whether the nation depends on the state or the state on the nation; whether a community's conviction that it is very old is part of what makes it a nation or just false consciousness to be explained away; whether quiet everyday reproduction does more work than founding moments.

leaves need to include primordialism, the medievalist/perennialist option, ethnosymbolism, industrialization-driven modernism, print-and-administrative-vernacular modernism, top-down invented tradition, and banal nationalism as its own thing rather than a footnote — plus every combination of answers that nobody holds, with a one line diagnosis of whether each empty cell is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. and flag any pair of leaves my quiz turns out not to be able to seperate.

one more thing: please don't build the tree on the civic vs ethnic axis. our prof spent a whole session arguing it's a dead end and I don't want my instrument quietly smuggling it back in through the case descriptions.
