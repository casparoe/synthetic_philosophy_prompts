I'm in a grad seminar this term on the game theory of AI agents, and this week's topic is program equilibrium — the setup where instead of picking an action you hand in a program, and the mechanism runs your program with read access to your opponent's source code. We covered the Tennenholtz folk theorem for program games and then the provability-logic work on robust cooperation (the FairBot / PrudentBot line), and the seminar collectively got annoyed at how brittle the constructions look, so we invented a variant and now my reading group partner and I cant agree about it. Variant: one-shot prisoner's dilemma, T=5 R=3 P=1 S=0, but instead of getting the opponent's raw source you get a sandbox where you can execute the opponent's program as many times as you like against a hard budget of, say, 10^9 steps total; blow the budget and the mechanism kills your process and you're stuck with whatever partial output you had. Everyone knows the budget, and everyone knows that a nested sandbox gets a proportionally smaller one, so the "I simulate you simulating me" regress has to bottom out somewhere instead of just never terminating.

The claim I want a number on: there exists a pair of programs such that (i) they mutually cooperate, (ii) that profile is a program equilibrium under unilateral deviation — neither designer gains by swapping in a different program while the other's stays fixed — and (iii) the cooperation is robust rather than brittle, meaning it doesn't require the opponent's code to be exactly what you predicted: you still cooperate against any program that would cooperate against you, and you never cooperate against one that wouldn't.
