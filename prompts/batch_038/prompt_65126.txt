got into a three-hour argument with a friend about the prisoner's dilemma last night and neither of us budged, so I want a neutral audit of where I actually stand. here's the package:

1. one-shot, anonymous, no future interactions: you defect. I'm completely unmoved by "but the social optimum is better" — that's not an answer to the strategic question.
2. if there's even a small chance the other player isn't straightforwardly rational, say they're a reciprocity type, then cooperating can be rational even in a short finitely repeated game. my friend says 1 and 2 can't both be true; I think he's wrong.
3. infinitely repeated: the folk theorem basically says any halfway decent payoff profile can be sustained, so I don't think there's a fact of the matter about which equilibrium gets played.
4. what makes repeated cooperation work at all is credible threats — you actually have to be willing to carry the punishment out, otherwise its just noise.
5. tit for tat is the real discovery in all this. Axelrod's tournaments weren't a parlor game, they told us something about the world: nice, retaliatory, forgiving, legible.
6. I think about it evolutionarily: strategies that do well spread, full stop, with no group selection smuggled in anywhere.
7. and yes, I'd tell my kid to play tit for tat in real life.
8. I'm aware there's stuff about generous variants and about win-stay-lose-shift doing better when there's noise, but I take that as a detail, not a revision.
