I am a consultant psychiatrist with no real philosophical training beyond what one picks up in a medical ethics course. Most of my working life is spent on psychiatric advance directives and self-binding arrangements: patients with bipolar disorder or recurrent addiction who, in a cool interval, ask us to hold them to a decision they will want to reverse later. I think in terms of states versus traits, capacity at the moment of decision, insight, adherence, enforceability, and whether a commitment will still feel like the patient's own when the time comes to honour it.

I would like Parfit's hitchhiker and the associated idea of rational irrationality restated in that vocabulary, as faithfully as it can be done. State the case first, in your own terms: someone stranded in the desert whose ride to town is conditional on the driver's being able to tell that he really will hand over the money afterwards, and for whom that payment, once he is safe in town, is pure loss. Then translate two claims into mine — (a) that the agent who decides case by case, on the merits of the situation he actually finds himself in, is the one who ends up worst off, and (b) that it can be rational to bring it about that you have a motive or belief you know to be irrational; use the variant in which a third party offers the stranded man a pill that would make him someone constitutionally incapable of reneging.

For each, tell me what carries over unchanged, what survives only by being bent, and precisely how it is bent, and what has no clinical expression whatsoever. Do not smuggle: substituting "therapeutically adaptive" or "good for adherence" for "what he has most reason to do" is not a translation, and I want to be told where you were tempted to do it. End with a definite verdict rather than a menu of interpretations — either the residue shows that clinical reasoning is missing a normative concept it should have had all along, or it shows that the doctrine of rational irrationality is doing less work than its presentation suggests. Pick one and give me your reasons.
