im in a philosophy of time course and my tutor has a move he deploys pretty much every time someone in seminar tries to defend presentism. he says: insisting that only the present exists is exactly like insisting that only what's within arm's reach of me exists. "now" is the same kind of expression as "here" — it just locates you on the map, it doesn't carve reality up into what's real and what isn't. so the presentist isn't putting forward a deep metaphysical thesis, they're committing a sort of provincialism, and the knockdown argument is just "you don't believe that about places, so you'd better not say it about times." he thinks this makes presentism a non-starter rather than a false theory, which is a much stronger claim, and he treats anyone who keeps going as either confused or doing it for the aesthetic.

the thing is i find it very persuasive when he says it and not at all persuasive an hour later, which makes me think it's the analogy doing the work rather than an argument. can you audit it for me properly? i want the actual correspondence spelled out — what in the time case plays the role of "here", of "there", of spatial distance, of my being able to walk over and check, of the fact that a complete description of the world never needs to include the word "here" — and then where the mapping snaps.

and then sort the breaks into two piles, because that's the part i really need: which disanalogies actually damage the conclusion he's drawing (that presentism doesn't even get off the ground), and which ones a presentist could cheerfully concede because they'd bite only on a weaker claim. a few i keep circling without being able to settle:

- what about the moving spotlight people who want the whole block AND a metaphysically special present? does his analogy have any purchase on them, or does using it against them just assume the thing in dispute?
- is there a version of the analogy where it's genuinely an argument rather than a restatement — i.e. can you make it precise enough that a presentist could be shown to be committed to something they reject about places?
