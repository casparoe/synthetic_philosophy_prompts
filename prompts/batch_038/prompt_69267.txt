A friend of mine who's just finished a philosophy degree sent me his verdict on moral dilemmas, and I'd like a sanity check before I repeat any of it to anyone else. He wrote:

"Genuine moral dilemmas are impossible and the proof is short. Suppose you ought to keep a promise and you ought to tell the truth, and you can't do both. Ought implies can, so whichever of the two you can't do, you were never really under an obligation to do it — nobody is obliged to do the impossible. What people call dilemmas are just clashes between prima facie duties, and Ross's whole point was that prima facie duties aren't actual duties, so once you've thought it through properly there's always exactly one thing you ought to do all things considered. That's also why Sartre's student — the one torn between staying with his mother and joining the Resistance — isn't a counterexample: Sartre simply hadn't done enough moral thinking to see which of the two options was right. And the leftover stuff everyone makes such a fuss about, the guilt and the apologies and the compensation you owe whoever you let down, actually settles the matter: if you'd done what you really ought to have done there'd be nothing to apologise for, so the residue proves you did something wrong. Either way you never get the dirty-hands situation people like to talk about, where whatever you choose you've chosen wrongly."

I'm fairly confident at least some of this is too quick, but I can't say where. Could you go through it claim by claim and tell me which bits a defender of genuine dilemmas would happily sign up to, which are fair simplifications, and which are just mistakes? Two things especially: whether the guilt-and-apologies move cuts the way he thinks it does, because I have a vague memory of it being used in exactly the opposite direction; and whether his two conclusions — that there's never a real conflict of duties, and that there's never a case where whatever you do you do wrong — are actually the same claim or whether one of them is doing all the work. You don't need to cite anything or point me at readings, I'm not writing this up, I just want to know what to believe. Please answer in British English.
