I'm writing a feature for a general-interest science section on why the apparent fine-tuning of the constants keeps driving physicists toward the multiverse, and I'd rather open it with a joke than with another recitation of how improbably small the cosmological constant is. What I want is a joke whose punchline turns on the actual disagreement rather than on the general weirdness of the subject: the objection that inferring many universes from one conspicuously life-friendly universe commits the inverse gambler's fallacy — that a man who strolls into a casino and finds double sixes on the table has learned nothing whatever about how long the player has been there — together with the standard reply that the cosmological case is disanalogous, since we could only ever have been ushered in after the double sixes came up. It should work for a reader who has never encountered the phrase and land harder for one who has, and it must not depend on a pun or on the word God. Finally, add one sentence telling me whether the joke is philosophically clean or is quietly cheating somewhere, so that I find out before the letters page does.
