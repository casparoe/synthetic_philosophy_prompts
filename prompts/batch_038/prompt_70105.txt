The industry lawyer said you can't pull a chemical off the market without evidence that it harms people — that's "innocent until proven guilty," bedrock stuff. The public health advocate said the whole point is to flip it: chemicals should be presumed guilty until proven innocent, and she talked as though she were invoking the exact same principle, just applying it correctly. Both of them acted like the slogan settles something, and I left the room with no idea what it could even mean in this setting.
