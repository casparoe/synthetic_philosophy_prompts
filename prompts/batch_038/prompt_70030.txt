My brother and his wife chose their embryos. They conceived through IVF, ran polygenic screening, and have told the whole family without hedging that they picked the one with the best odds. I am giving the toast at the baby shower in six weeks, and the room will include an aunt who is a clinical geneticist and considers all of this an obvious good, and a close family friend who does disability-rights law and considers it an obvious wrong. Neither intends to say so aloud, and both will be listening to me carefully.

Write a toast of about five minutes that welcomes a child whose arrival involved ranking possible children against one another. I want it built on a distinction I have not seen anyone state well: that a toast addresses someone as irreplaceable, which is precisely what a selection procedure cannot say about the person it selects, so the two acts are less in competition than in different registers. The speech should also carry the asymmetry worry honestly — a decision made about a person, in advance, that they can never contest or reciprocate — without becoming a verdict, and without using the non-identity point as an escape hatch. No one may leave hearing that this child is the output of a choice among interchangeable candidates, but no one may hear me pretend the choice did not happen. It should sound like a brother talking, not a seminar. If you judge that some of these constraints cannot all be satisfied in a single toast, say which one you dropped and why.
