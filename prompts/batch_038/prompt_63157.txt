I'm on the city council of a town of about 120,000 and we've been stuck for two years on where to put a new wastewater plant. Every candidate site is next to somebody's houses. A colleague of mine wants to break the deadlock by picking 40 residents at random off the voter registration list, paying each of them $2,000, giving them six weekends of briefings from engineers and economists, and then commiting in advance — in an ordinance, publicly — to build whatever site they land on. No council vote, no override.

Her argument is that we're the wrong people to decide this: every one of us is thinking about next year's election, and two of us took money from a developer who happens to own one of the parcels. The 40 have no such problem. The pushback is that nobody elected them and we'd be handing off a job we were chosen to do.

The thing I keep coming back to is that our voter rolls basically don't include the students or the big immigrant population down in the south ward, and one of the three sites is in the south ward.

So — which of these objections is actually the one that should stop us, and which are just people not wanting to give up control? And is there a change to the design that fixes the real problem without turning the whole thing back into a council vote?
