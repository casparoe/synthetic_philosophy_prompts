ok so i'm in this weird cross-listed seminar — half cognitive science of concepts, half type-design studio — and the running assignment is the seed extrapolation thing: you get three lowercase letters drawn on a coarse grid out of a fixed set of legal segments (ours is 4 wide by 6 tall, diagonals have to be 45°) and you have to draw the rest of the alphabet "in the same spirit." this week's reading was the Letter Spirit project, where the whole point is that what makes an 'a' an 'a' isn't a feature list you can write down.

i want to build the counter-piece into my response essay: a parody product-launch page plus FAQ for a startup that claims to have SOLVED letter identity in the dumbest possible way. their pitch is that they enumerated every glyph you can build on the grid, ran a huge panel rating each one for "a-ness," "g-ness," etc., and now letter identity is a database lookup — they have the complete extension, so there's no essence left to theorize about.

please write it, ~700-800 words, deadpan startup marketing voice, absolutely no winking. i want a hero tagline, a spec bullet list, three pricing tiers where the conceptual distinctions get commodified (free tier = membership only; paid tier = extrapolation to grids they never enumerated; enterprise = "family resemblance as a service" or something equally gross), an FAQ, and one 1-star review from a working type designer together with the company's response to it.

the FAQ has to cover these, and in every single case the joke should be that the company's answer is technically correct and completely useless:

- someone asks what makes a glyph an 'a'. they sell an explainability add-on that returns a feature list, and i want the list to be obviously necessary-but-not-sufficient (or sufficient-but-not-necessary), and they are PROUD of it
- what happens when the grid changes or a new legal segment gets added — they own the entire extension for one grid and it buys them nothing for the next one
- the panel data: the 'a' ratings came out bimodal (double-story 'a' people vs single-story 'a' people) and they averaged it, so now both clusters sit around 0.5 and nobody's actual 'a' scores well. include an email from a customer asking why their perfectly good 'a' got flagged
- descenders — g, j, p, q, y hang below the box, and the answer should be that they're a seperate SKU
- a glyph that is definitively on the 'a' list but that everyone who looks at it says is a 4

hard rules: no philosopher names anywhere, not even as a joke — this marketing copy would never say "family resemblance" out loud. don't state the objections; let the FAQ stumble into them and walk away. and i want one contradiction the company never notices: they insist the list is the complete and final answer to what a letter IS, and then the roadmap section at the very bottom promises "essence" in Q3. that should be the last line.
