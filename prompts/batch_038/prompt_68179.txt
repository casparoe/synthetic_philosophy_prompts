Two years on the conflict committee at my housing co-op, and last month I ended up on the other side of a complaint for the first time, which is a hell of a way to finally see the pattern from both ends.

Instance one: the long-running laundry room schedule dispute. The guy who filed it never once said he wanted to win. He said things like "that made me feel dismissed" and "I don't feel safe in that space anymore." But when we offered him roughly what he'd nominally asked for — a private apology, a rewritten schedule, an acknowledgment in writing — he turned it down and escalated to the whole membership, becuse a private fix didn't count. What settled it wasn't repair, it was the other person losing standing in front of everyone. The injury language stayed identical the whole way through; the stakes moved.

Instance two, and this is the one that stuck. A newer member had been on the receiving end of the same guy. When we asked her, she said clearly that she wasn't hurt, didn't think anything had happened, didn't want anything done. The committee overruled her: she'd been harmed whether she registered it or not, and her not registering it was itself a sign of how bad it was. So she got handed a status she'd declined, and her declining it counted as evidence for the handoff.

Here's what I don't have words for. Its not that anyone's lying — I think people genuinely feel the injury and genuinely need the public win at the same time, and neither one is a costume for the other. And it isn't really the thing I keep seeing where societies get sorted into ones that run on reputation, ones that run on inner worth, and ones that run on harm. That taxonomy sorts whole cultures, and I can't find a slot in it for what I'm actually looking at: talk out of one column and practice out of another, inside a single person and a single thirty-person institution, where the practice can recruit the talk and can even be applied to someone who refuses it.

Is there an established concept for this? What's nearest to it, and where does mine come apart from each of the candidates — I can already think of strategic framing and of culture-as-toolkit ways of talking, and I suspect both of them miss the part where the person isn't picking the vocabulary instrumentally. If nothing fits, name it for me, with a definition and a couple of boundary cases so I can tell when it's operating and when I'm just watching people be complicated. I'm not after a reading list, just the carving — in about three weeks I have to draft our procedures document, and the whole thing turns on whether a stated injury is evidence about someone's condition or about their position in a contest, and I think the honest answer is both at once, which is exactly the thing I need a handle on.
