I'm a third-year physics undergrad sitting in on our department's philosophy of math reading group this term — they let non-majors audit as long as you actually do the reading. We just wrapped the unit on platonism versus nominalism and I'm stuck on something that I'm pretty sure is obvious to everyone else in the room but nobody unpacked when I half-raised it (we were already over time and I didnt want to be the guy). I'm not after more reading recommendations, I've got a stack, I just want to know whether my reasoning is off somewhere.

Here's the sticking point. Everybody treats Hartry Field's *Science Without Numbers* (1980) as the existence proof that you can be a nominalist and still do serious physics: he rebuilds Newtonian gravitation out of purely geometric primitives with no numbers or functions in it, Hilbert-style, and then shows the mathematics you bolt back on is conservative, meaning it can't yield any physical conclusions the nominalistic theory didn't already have. But when I tried to actually follow the construction it looks to me like he never eliminates the abstract machinery, he just relocates it. To pin down a continuum of spacetime points you need a second-order language where you can quantify over arbitrary collections of points, and the conservativeness result itself gets established by ordinary set-theoretic model theory. So the platonism got pushed up into the metalanguage, which is arguably worse because now it's out of sight. That's pushed me toward thinking a real nominalism has to be *finite*, and I've been sketching one where the only things my language ranges over are finitely many concrete measurement records, so there's no infinite totality anywhere in the ontology.

Two questions, and please tell me straight if the second one is embarrassingly confused. First: given all that, why does the group still talk about Field as a demonstration that nominalism is viable rather than conceding the point to the platonists — is there a principled reason that what happens in the metalanguage doesn't contaminate the object theory? Second: is my finite version the right repair, and am I hitting walls because I'm bad at this or because those are the real costs? Because I keep running into things that feel less like deep obstacles and more like I've broken my own language — I can't state anything like "the temperature passes through every value between these two," I can't say that a bounded increasing sequence of positions converges, and I can't write down most conservation laws at all since they quantify over times. If doing nominalism properly really does cost you all of that, shouldn't the debate have been settled in the platonists' favour forty years ago?
