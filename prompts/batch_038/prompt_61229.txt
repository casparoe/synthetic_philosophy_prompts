Been reading LW and its spinoffs since the Sequences and there's a register in mech interp / evals writing that nobody ever stops to examine, so I want someone to do the examining.

The practice: people who open up networks talk about the internals as if they had mental content, and not as a loose metaphor they'd retract under pressure. A direction in activation space *detects* the Golden Gate Bridge. A circuit *represents* whether the code is Python or Java. A probe can *read out* what the model really has available, and the finding everyone gets excited about is the gap — internally Paris, out loud Marseille. When Anthropic cranked the bridge feature and shipped Golden Gate Claude, the fact that the resulting model couldn't stop steering every conversation to the bridge was treated as settling what that feature meant; the clamp is the arbiter. Same vocabulary in safety work: a model that behaves differently because it takes itself to be in an eval is called deceptive, and deception obviously requires a gap between what it represents and what it emits.

Heres the part that bugs me. All of that presupposes there's a fact of the matter about content, and about *mis*representation. Detecting X has to be something that can go wrong while still being detecting X — otherwise the frog that snaps at a BB isn't misperceiving a fly, it's correctly registering a small dark moving thing, which is all it was ever causally hooked up to. And the moment error has to be possible, covariance alone won't do the job; you need something that sets the norm. Which sounds exactly like teleosemantics with gradient descent swapped in for natural selection: a mechanism means what it was selected to track, and it was selected becuase it cut the loss.

But if thats the implicit theory, the practice looks like it's running two different criteria for what a feature means without noticing. One is the clamp — intervene, see which behavior moves, call that the meaning: a causal-role test. The other is the data and the task — what the mechanism got rewarded for tracking: a selection-history test. Those come apart. The recurring vision result, where your cat classifier turns out to be keying on fur texture and ear geometry and on texture rather than shape generally, is a case where the honest causal description is an ugly disjunction and the selected-for description is cat, and the field just says cat without arguing for it. Meanwhile a small adversarial patch flips the same thing to ostrich, which on a pure covariance story means it was never about cats to begin with.

So:

1. What theory of content does this practice actually presuppose? Is teleosemantics-with-SGD forced on it, or would weaker accounts license exactly the same talk — plain detection/covariance, conceptual-role semantics, Dennett-flavored instrumentalism where the attributions are useful fictions, or a deflationism on which everything cashes out into loss curves and activation statistics and no content talk survives?
2. Which of those does the practice rule out? I'd bet a lot of the talk is compatible with instrumentalism, so name the specific claims that would have to be dropped if you went that route, and tell me which findings could only be stated under a realist account.
3. Where is it internally inconsistent? My candidate: they'll say a model believes something false, but nobody says a smoke detector believes there's smoke when it goes off on shower steam, even though we call that a false alarm and it has a function in precisely the designed sense. Second candidate: the clamp settles meaning in one paragraph, the training data settles what the thing is a detector *of* in the next. Is there any single account under which both moves are licensed?
4. If you replaced the implicit theory with a rival — say, straight interpretivism — what would concretely change in the methodology? Does the feature ontology survive? Do probes still read out what the model thinks, or only what's linearly decodable, with the extra claim about thought doing no work at all? Does anything in the eval-awareness argument survive?

Not looking for a literature tour, just the argument. The test I actually care about: be concrete about which sentences in the interp register would have to get rewritten under each rival account, and which ones wouldn't change at all.
