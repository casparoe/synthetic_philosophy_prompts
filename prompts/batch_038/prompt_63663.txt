A friend of mine who reads a lot of econ twitter posted this summary of Sen's impossibility of a Paretian liberal in our group chat:

"Sen proved mathematically that you can't have both individual rights and efficiency. In his example, one dirty book and two people: give each of them the right to decide whether they personally read it, and the rights plus unanimity generate a cycle, so a society that scrupulously respects each person's private choices ends up ranking an outcome that both citizens agree is worse than the one where nobody reads anything. Liberty is provably inefficient — that's why the laissez-faire line that free choice and Pareto efficiency always go together is just false. Nozick said the whole thing misses the point, since rights are side constraints and not social preferences, but Sen answered that a side-constraint view can't tell you what to do when rights conflict, so the paradox stands."
