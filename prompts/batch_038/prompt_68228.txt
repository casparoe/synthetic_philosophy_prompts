I'm one of eight members of my school's student council, which votes on whether the school issues official statements. Our school opened in 1961 on land cleared from a Black neighborhood whose residents were never compensated, and it did not admit Black students until 1971. Almost no student here now has any family connection to either group. A community organization made up largely of descendants of the displaced families has asked the school to apologize at the anniversary assembly in May, and three difficulties have come up that I can't rank.

Several council members hold that the school cannot apologize at all, because none of us was alive for any of it, and an apology from people who did nothing wrong amounts to a performance rather than an apology. The principal will agree to a statement saying the school regrets that era, but not to one saying it apologizes, because the district's lawyers advise that the second could be used against us in a lawsuit. And the organization's spokesperson has said that an apology with no scholarship fund behind it would be an insult and that she would rather the school say nothing at all — which some members take as a reason to say nothing.

Which of these three is a genuine obstacle and which rests on a mistake? What should we vote, and why?
