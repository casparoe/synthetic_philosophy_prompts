For thirty-five years I worked as a condensed-matter physicist, and a good part of that career was spent deciding which of my field's posits were real and which were convenient bookkeeping. Since retiring I have taken up philosophy of science in earnest and now host a small podcast. My guest next week is a mechanistic-interpretability researcher — please treat her as an invented figure rather than any real person — who is an outspoken realist about her results: when her group reports a direction in activation space that tracks some concept, she holds that they have located a genuine representation inside the network, not a useful gloss on its input–output behavior.

Please write eight to ten interview questions, in American English, that engage the substance of that realism instead of asking her to describe her workflow. I would like coverage of at least the following: the evidential asymmetry in causal interventions — clamping or steering a direction demonstrably changes a model's outputs, but why should that establish that the model represents the content we use to label the direction, rather than that we have found a workable handle on its substrate; superposition and polysemanticity, and what licenses the single direction as the unit of representation given recent arguments that many purported features are irreducibly multidimensional; non-universality — features and circuits that fail to recur across seeds, scales, and architectures — and whether her realism concerns natural kinds or artifacts of a training distribution; the analyst-relativity of feature discovery when the labels used to identify clusters are themselves produced by another model; and whether such a state can misrepresent anything at all, since a representation that cannot be wrong may not deserve the name.

For each question, add one follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give — she will probably reach for the entity-realist intuition that whatever you can intervene on is real, so be ready for that — and close with a question about what specific result would oblige her to give up the realist reading. No requests for citations or reading lists, and no soft questions about her career.
