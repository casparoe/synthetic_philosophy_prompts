Been going back and forth on the last section of Turing's 1950 paper, the one where he takes up extrasensory perception, and I've talked myself into a reading I want stress-tested. Warning up front: I'm paraphrasing from memory and deliberately not quoting anything, so if I've garbled the text, say so.

The gist as I remember it: after working through the mathematical objection, the consciousness objection, Lady Lovelace and the rest, Turing ends by saying he can't give the ESP evidence the cold shoulder — on his telling the statistical case for telepathy at least is overwhelming, and telepathy is the most important of the paranormal phenomena — and that once you admit telepathy the imitation game has to be tightened up, by something like screening the room so the contestant can't simply be read telepathically.

Usual treatment in the commentaries: cute embarrassment, symptom of Turing's odd priors, skip it. My position is stronger and I think under-appreciated. That section isn't about minds, it's about instrumentation. Turing is conceding that the game's verdict is only as good as the channel running between interrogator and contestant, and that an unmodeled side-channel doesn't add noise, it makes the test measure the wrong thing entirely. The telepathy-proof room is a containment move, not a metaphysical one. And on that reading the section generalizes in an obviously modern way: training-data leakage, an evaluator who's seen the transcript before, a system that can infer it's being tested — each breaks the game by the exact mechanism Turing named, which would make these the most load-bearing pages in the paper rather than the most skippable.

What would count against that? I want the exposure map, not a defense. Which defeaters are decisive and which are merely annoying, and which ones would a defender of the boring reading grant me themselves.

**The boring reading** (~150 words): the best case that Turing just thought the 1950 card-guessing statistics looked impressive and meant nothing deeper by it, or was being wry for a Mind readership.

**Decisive defeaters** (~350 words): what would actually kill the instrumentation reading — things he says elsewhere in the paper, the historical context of parapsychology at the time, or structural collapse (does my reading reduce to the truism that experiments need controlled conditions?). Rank them.

**Merely awkward** (~200 words): evidence that stings but that I could absorb without giving up the position, and what that absorbtion costs me.

**Insulated?** (~200 words): can my reading explain away anything you throw at it, and is that a problem for a claim about a historical text as opposed to a claim about the world.

**Fidelity check** (~100 words): where my paraphrase is probably wrong or overstated.

**Bottom line** (3-4 sentences): how significant is the ESP section actually.
