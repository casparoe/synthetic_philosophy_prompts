Thirty-five years doing analytical chemistry in a government lab, retired two years ago, and philosophy is still a hobby I do badly -- mostly I read whatever the library has and get confused in a slow, methodical way. So this may have an obvious answer that I've just missed.

My brother-in-law is deep into alternative news sites and I used to think we had nothing to talk about. But when we actually sit down and go through his claims one at a time, it turns out I agree with most of them. Four or five companies own nearly all the local papers. The local stations run the same national feed and the same stories land within hours. Advertisers get skittish when coverage gets hot and the hot coverage stops. A small number of big asset managers sit on the boards of companies that are supposedly competitors. And people who push back too hard find their careers quietly stall. I'd sign off on every one of those. What he adds at the end is the bit I can't accept: that therefore somebody is running it, that there's a room where it's decided. I say nobody's in the room, it just falls out of the structure, and he looks at me like I'm the naive one in the family.

Here's what I want a word for. Not his view -- mine, or rather the shape of the thing that sits between us. It's the position where you grant every individual premise about coordinated-looking behaviour, the concentration and the aligned incentives and the career costs of dissent and the eerie speed of convergence, and you still insist no one planned any of it. Functionally the two pictures seem identical to me: same expectations about what gets covered and what doesn't, same account of why the output is uniform, same list of who benefits. The only difference is whether there's a meeting. And I've started to worry that when I tell him there's no meeting, I'm not disagreeing with him about anything checkable -- I'm just refusing to draw the little figure with the moustache at the top of the org chart. I've tried the economist's move on him, that a market produces order with nobody designing it, and he shot back that a market has a correction mechanism because bad prices get exploited, whereas in his case the correction mechanism is itself part of what's captured -- which I thought was a genuinely good answer and didn't know how to come back at.

So: does this have a name, in philosophy or in the social sciences or wherever it lives? I'm not after a bare label, I want to know where the edges are. Specifically -- is a structural account that makes exactly the predictions a conspiracy account makes just a conspiracy theory with the agent deleted, or is there something real that separates them? Is there any observation that could in principle tell the two apart, or is the difference purely about how you describe the same data? And my instinct from thirty-five years at a bench is that "it works out nicely for such-and-such a group" is not a mechanism unless you can point to the loop that keeps making it work out nicely, so I'd also like to know whether anyone has pressed on that objection, and whether my side of the argument is even obliged to supply such a loop. If nothing in the literature covers this exact configuration, please say so plainly and then coin something -- with a definition, and some boundary cases that show what the term excludes, because I'd like to be able to use it on him at Thanksgiving without making things worse.
