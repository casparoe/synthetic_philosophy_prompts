I'm applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and need a two-page proposal built around an idea I cannot yet state cleanly. The familiar treatments of Keynes's 1930 "Economic Possibilities for Our Grandchildren" divide into two camps: those who say his fifteen-hour week failed because he underestimated relative and positional wants, and those who say the abundance arrived and we merely misallocated it. Both, I suspect, read the essay as making a claim about the content of the good life, when its claim may be only about the conditions for one.

The complication I want the project built around is Keynes's own 1938 memoir "My Early Beliefs," read to the Memoir Club and published posthumously, in which he retrospectively faults the Moorean ethic he and his circle had adopted: they kept the view that the good consists in certain states of mind — love, aesthetic appreciation, the enjoyment of beauty — while discarding any account of duty, of rules, and of the states of the world on which such states of mind depend. Hence the question I want funded: does that self-criticism supply the missing account of what leisure is for, and thereby repair the 1930 argument, or does it reveal that the fifteen-hour week was never a failed forecast but an incoherent aspiration, since Keynes had no determinate conception of what would occupy it? A third possibility the proposal should hold open rather than resolve in advance is that the two texts answer different questions altogether, and that treating them as continuous is precisely what generates the standard objections.

Write the proposal with a precise statement of the question, an account of why the existing positions leave it open, a month-by-month plan of what I would actually do, what a successful result would look like, and an honest statement of what would count as failure — including the risk that the work degenerates into intellectual biography or into a quarrel about Moore that is not Keynes's. No bibliography and no literature review by citation; I'll add those myself.
