Her line: the "is it parroting or does it actually believe it" debate has a clean answer — you attribute a genuine belief to a system only if the content survives perturbations that hold informational content fixed while changing surface form (paraphrase, translation, reordered context, persona framing, register). Fail the test, it's mimicry, not belief. She's sharp and will tie you in knots if you let her, and I've read one too many LW megathreads where this exact move gets waved through.

Write me eight to ten questions that actually bite, each with a follow-up aimed at the answer she's most likely to give (say what that expected answer is in a clause before the follow-up). I need at least one on sycophancy: people flatter and cave under social pressure too, so does her criterion demote most human beliefs to mimicry, and if she says that's fine, what's doing the work in the word "belief"? And one on non-assertable belief: the criterion only ever gets at the state through what the system says, so what about internal states that steer behavior but never surface in output?

No softballs about what drew her to philosophy of mind, and don't let anything rest on introspection reports, since she'll say those are just more perturbable surface form. End with a question about what specific result would falsify the view, and build in a follow-up for when she tries to park it as an empirical matter for future work.
