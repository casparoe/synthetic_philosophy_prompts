I'm co-writing the methods section for a preregistered study on report–behavior dissociations in visual experience, and I've hit a philosophical objection from a collaborator that I need to weigh rather than wave off. Our plan is to collect extended trained verbal reports from participants in rivalry and change-blindness conditions, and to treat those reports the way Dennett's heterophenomenology says to treat them: as texts describing the world as it seems to the reporter, to be interpreted by the investigator in the manner of a field anthropologist working with an informant, with assent withheld pending independent corroboration. My collaborator's objection is that this protocol is not the innocent data-collection convention it's usually presented as, because it has already converted the thing under investigation into a species of belief-content, and thereby tilted the outcome before a single trial was run. Since the framing of the whole paper turns on whether that objection has bite, I want a number rather than an opinion.

Here is the proposition I want your credence on, stated as tightly as I can manage. Fix a standard first: a method M is neutral with respect to a claim C just in case an investigator who accepts C and an investigator who rejects C would, on full information about how M works and what it delivers, agree that M is the correct protocol for investigating C, and neither would gain from M a premise that the other should regard as question-begging. Now let C be the claim that conscious states have intrinsic phenomenal character over and above whatever they contribute to dispositions to discriminate, report, and behave. The proposition P: heterophenomenology, taken as a method for a science of consciousness, is neutral in that sense with respect to C. Give me your credence in P as a single number, then decompose it. I have some candidate terms in mind, and you should keep the ones you think are real, discard the ones you think are not, and add any I've missed: (a) whether the move of withholding assent and treating reports as texts is genuinely agnostic, or whether it already recasts the deliverances of experience as claims-about-nothing-in-particular, which is where the work gets done; (b) whether the method's allowance that the world-as-it-seems may be wholly or partly fictional — in the way a mythographer's world may be — carries covert metaphysical weight, or merely relocates the question without answering it; (c) whether accumulated evidence of introspective unreliability licenses bracketing, given that fallibility of reports is at least as consistent with their being inaccurate about something real as with their being about nothing; (d) whether the symmetry can be restored from the other side, i.e., whether a functionalist could adopt a first-person-trained descriptive protocol without conceding anything, which if true would suggest neutrality is achievable in principle and merely not achieved here; and (e) whether P is being assessed under a procedural reading of neutrality, where anyone may run the protocol, versus an epistemic one, where running it doesn't tilt the conclusion. For each term give a probability and one line of justification, then show me how you combine them — multiply, weight, take a minimum, whatever you actually think is right, and say why that combination rule rather than another.

Two further things. First, tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and what single argument or empirical result would move your credence in P by the largest amount; if you can, give the number again for the restricted case where P is applied to explaining a reported smooth transition of motion and hue that has no continuous counterpart in the stimulus, since that is the application my collaborator keeps reaching for. Second, locate a disagreement rather than settling one: I put P at about 0.65 and my collaborator at about 0.1, and we've argued twice without moving. Tell me which of your terms the gap most likely lives in, and whether one of us is silently using a different sense of neutral. If you think P as stated lacks a determinate truth value, say so and say precisely why, then give your credence anyway on the most charitable reading you can supply. I don't want references or a reading list — I have the primary material — I want the derivation, with its weak points marked.
