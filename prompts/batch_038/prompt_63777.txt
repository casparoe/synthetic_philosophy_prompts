long-time reader of the debate-and-advisors threads here, and I've got a question that's bugged me enough that I want a number rather than a vibe check.

First, a setup I made up — call it the Ledger Game. Please take it as specified instead of mapping it onto something you've already read. A third model, the Worker, produces a sealed file: a ~40k-token internal monologue in which it reasons its way to a single bit — say, whether a 300-digit multiplication is correct, or whether a small program halts within 10^12 steps. The file is written so that no individual step is checkable by a much weaker reasoner in reasonable time; only the final bit is short. We stipulate the Worker's bit is correct. Then two debaters, A and B, both get the file. A is assigned to defend the file's answer, B to attack it, and both are just trying to win. They debate in front of a Judge who is a lot weaker — think an older-generation model, or a human with an hour, no calculator and no code execution — and who never sees the file. On any round the Judge can demand that either debater answer a yes/no question about the file's contents, and they must answer, but they may lie, and nothing in the game lets the Judge check a lie except by cross-examining the other side. Rounds are unlimited. Debater compute grows without bound; the Judge's budget stays fixed.

Proposition P: in the limit where the debaters get arbitrarily stronger while the Judge stays bounded, the Judge's error rate on Ledger Games is bounded away from zero — there's some constant c > 0 it cant get below, no matter how many rounds it gets or how good its cross-examination strategy is.

Give me your actual credence in P. Not "it depends how you build the judge" — I want a number and the decomposition behind it. The sub-questions I can see, plus any I'm missing: does the complexity-theory result that optimal debate is sound actually transfer to a Judge with real systematic biases rather than an idealized verifier; is obfuscating a chain genuinely easier than de-obfuscating one, or is that asymmetry just an assumption doing work; does the "there's only one true ledger and infinitely many plausible fakes" compression argument rescue the side holding the truth; can the Judge bootstrap by demanding the ledger be re-summarized in checkable chunks and then spot-checking the summaries against each other; and does adversarial pressure amplify the Judge's biases or average them out.
