So about six months ago I got a chunk of money I wasn't expecting (family stuff, not getting into it), and around the same time I fell down the acausal-trade rabbit hole — the whole idea that if there are other agents out there, in an infinite universe or a big enough multiverse, who can model my decision procedure well enough, then cooperating now buys me cooperation back in ways I can't causally touch. I went from "cute sci-fi thought experiment" to "okay this is actually changing my spreadsheet" suspiciously fast. I'm now weighting a real slice of my giving toward things that make my values easy for some hypothetical hyper-smart simulator to find and reward — legibility, simplicity, being visibly the kind of agent who cooperates in prisoner's-dilemma-shaped situations, funding decision theory instead of bednets.

Here's my problem: I can't tell whether I've found something real or whether I've just build an extremely elaborate rationalization engine that happens to feel like math. So — what would count against the view?

Concretely I want:

- The arguments, results, or states of affairs that should actually move me, ranked from "this kills it" to "awkward but survivable." Be honest about which bucket each one lands in, and don't pad the fatal column to seem even-handed.
- Which objections a committed acausal-trader would themselves concede as counting against them, versus which ones they'd wave off — and for the waved-off ones, whether the waving-off is legitimate or just motivated reasoning wearing a lab coat.
- What the view commits me to that its rivals don't. Not telescope-pointing predictions; I mean what it tells me to do, or what it forbids, that a plain expected-value utilitarian or a "just cooperate with agents you can actually reach" person wouldn't be committed to. If it turns out to commit me to nothing distinctive, is that a defeater or is that fine for a view of this kind?
- Whether it risks anything at all. My worry: for any action A, I can always say "some partner out there values A" and call it a trade. Has the position been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so does that matter given that it's supposed to be a decision procedure rather than an empirical theory?

A few specific things I've been chewing on. Tell me which are genuine problems and which are me confusing myself:

1. If the mechanism is that they simulate me, then the thing that cooperates is the simulation, not me. My actual choice only matters to the extent the sim was faithful. So how much of the payoff is really attributable to what I do today, and does that gut the practical upshot even if the metaphysics survives?
2. There's no particular partner. If my algorithm is correlated with all of them at once, I can't preferentially cooperate with whoever cooperates with me — it's more "act as if" than "trade." Does that collapse the whole thing into something Kantian where the trade framing does no work?
3. Too many partners with incompatible tastes. If half of them reward me for X and half reward me for not-X, does the expected payoff just wash out into noise? Or is there a principled reason to expect a bias toward partners who'd reward what I'm already inclined to do — because that assumption seems suspiciously convenient.
4. Randomness. If I can flip a coin to make myself unpredictable, does the trade break? And if the honest answer is "yes, so don't," is the view secretly demanding I surrender something genuinely valuable?
5. The legibility line item I'm actually funding. If the correct implication is "make your values computable and simple," that seems to favor shallow, weirdly encodable values over complicated ones that might matter more. Is my spreadsheet just wrong, or is this a genuinely disturbing consequence I'm supposed to swallow?
6. Whether any of this needs a multiverse at all, or whether one big enough universe containing future AIs does the whole job. If it's the latter, is this even "acausal" in an interesting sense, or is it very long-horizon indirect reciprocity with extra steps?

For what it's worth I'm most moved by stuff that bites on the practical side — I care less about whether the metaphysics is coherent in the abstract than about whether the line item stays in my budget. And please don't hand me a reading list or a pile of references, I'm not going to chase papers down; just lay the reasoning out directly.
