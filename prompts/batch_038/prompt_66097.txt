ok so I'm taking philosophy of time this term and we just did the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and my roommate (physics, third year, insufferable about it) has turned it into a fatalism proof I genuinely can't get out of. He walked me through it on the way to dinner and every step seemed fine when I pushed on it. Best reconstruction I can manage:

(1) For any inertial observer, the events that exist now — that are fully, presently real — are exactly the events on that observer's plane of simultaneity, i.e. the ones sharing the observer's time coordinate once you've synced clocks with light signals the Einstein way.

(2) Special relativity says no inertial frame is physically privileged. There's no experiment that picks out mine.

(3) Reality isn't a matter of perspective. Either an event is real or it isn't; "real for the probe" and "real for me" can't turn out to be two different, incompatible inventories of what there is.

(4) So: I'm real, and the probe is real, and if everything real-for-the-probe is just what sits on the probe's now-plane, then what sits on the probe's now-plane is real too. Reality can't depend on who's doing the bookkeeping.

(5) Concretely. A survey probe is inbound toward Earth at 0.1c, and by my clocks right this second it's 30 light years out. The tilt of its simultaneity plane is vL/c², so 0.1 × 30 = 3: the event on Earth that is real for the probe at that moment is me walking into an oral exam three years from now.

(6) By (3) and (4), that exam-hall event is real simpliciter — now, while I'm standing here in the corridor.

(7) If a future event is already real, then it's already the case that it happens, and nothing can make it not happen.

(8) So there is nothing I can do now that affects whether I walk into that exam hall.

(9) And it scales: for any future event of mine, T years out, I can find a subluminal inbound observer far enough away (L = Tc²/v) whose now-plane contains it. No faster-than-light signals, no exotic physics, just distance.

(10) Therefore nothing anyone does affects anything that's going to happen. Fatalism, from a bit of algebra.
