I run a reading group that turned out accidentally interdisciplinary — six philosophy grad students, two clinical psychologists, a color-vision postdoc, and an anesthesiologist who mostly comes for the arguments — and this term we're doing the consciousness-skeptic side of things, starting with Dennett's quining-qualia material. For the first meeting I want to hand people a sorting instrument instead of doing the usual round of "so, who believes in qualia?", because when you ask it that way literally everyone says yes and means something completely different by it, and we burn an hour discovering that. So: write me a "which position on qualia do you actually hold?" quiz, nine or ten questions, two or three answer options each, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto named views. We've got the room for ninety minutes and I'm the one who has to tally the sheets afterward, so the key needs to be simple enough that I can do it with a spreadsheet and one coffee.

The frame I want it built on is the old four-property definition: that qualia are supposed to be ineffable, intrinsic, private, and directly or immediately apprehensible. Every question should be a concrete case or a forced choice rather than a vocabulary check, and the word qualia shouldn't appear in any of the questions at all — the four properties should only show up in the key. What I want the key to report is which of the four someone is actually willing to give up and at what price, so that a person who drops incorrigibility but keeps the rest lands somewhere different from a person who trades intrinsicness for a story about representation. Work through the logical space first: which subsets of the four anybody could be prepared to sacrifice, which of those cells are occupied by a view someone defends, which are empty, and for each empty one whether it's empty because it's incoherent, because it's just unappealing, or because nobody's bothered looking. Cases I need used: Dennett's two coffee tasters (the one who says the taste hasn't changed but he doesn't like it anymore, and the one who says the taste changed), an inverted-spectrum swap that leaves every discriminative ability and every verbal report exactly as it was, a pain-asymbolia-style case where the pain is reported as present but no longer unpleasant, and one case where a subject is confidently wrong about whether their own experience just changed.

Leaves I want in the tree: straight-up qualia realism of the property-dualist kind, outright nothing-there eliminativism, the representationalist who keeps what-it's-likeness but denies intrinsicness, a higher-order-theory leaf, naive realism or disjunctivism, and a grab-bag leaf where the four properties pick out a pile of heterogeneous things that were never one kind to begin with — plus room for unnamed combinations if the space has any a respondent could actually land in. Also tell me which pairs of leaves your instrument can't really separate; my suspicion is that eliminativism and illusionism will collapse into each other, and if that's right I'd rather the key say so and offer one follow-up question that at least narrows it, then paper over it. Two things on tone. Commit to a tree, don't hedge every branch with some-philosophers-would-say, and mark the two or three branches you think are genuinely shaky and why. And if you think the whole thing is misframed — say, if the four-property gloss isn't where the real disagreements actually live, or if a quiz like this can only ever re-sort people by the label they already liked no matter how carefully the questions are written — say that plainly up front instead of building me something you think is worthless. No bibliography, I just want the instrument.
