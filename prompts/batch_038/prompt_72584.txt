I've followed the debate over naturalizing mental content for years, mostly through rationalist-adjacent essays that pick it up whenever someone asks what it would take for a trained network to genuinely represent anything. One widely circulated post I ran across frames teleosemantics this way:

"The teleosemantic proposal is that a state's content is fixed by the function it was selected for: the frog's bug-detector means fly because detecting flies is what the mechanism evolved to do. But the theory cannot escape the following problem. Those same cells fire at small dark moving dots in general, including the BB pellets experimenters flick past the frog, and since the frog is rewarded either way, selection history is indifferent between FLY and FLY-OR-PELLET. Content is therefore underdetermined exactly where the theory needed it to be determinate, so misrepresentation goes unexplained -- which is why the field eventually moved on to purely causal accounts."

I suspect this is at least partly unfair. My half-remembered impression is that the theory has a clause about normal conditions that is supposed to do real narrowing work here, and that the pellet case is ordinarily described as one where the frog is *not* actually fed -- but I don't trust my recall, and I'm not equipped to tell which of my two suspicions (if either) is right.

Could you audit the passage for me? I'd like it separated into what a working teleosemanticist would happily sign, what is fair simplification, and what is wrong or question-begging. Specifically: is the empirical premise about the pellets stated correctly, and does the underdetermination argument even need it; does the objection engage the machinery the theory was built to handle, or does it assume that machinery away; is the gloss on 'the function it was selected for' doing justice to how content is actually derived on the leading versions, given that there seem to be producer-oriented and consumer-oriented variants; and does the closing claim about where the field went follow from anything accurate in the summary?
