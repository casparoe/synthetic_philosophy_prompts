Give me a number and its derivation. Consider Thomson's lamp: a lamp switched on and off at intervals that halve, so that infinitely many switchings are completed within a single minute. Let P be the claim that a complete and true description of the lamp's state at every moment before the minute ends can leave it genuinely undetermined — as a matter of the world, not of our information — whether the lamp is on at the end, and that this fact shows nothing whatsoever about whether infinite tasks can be completed.

What is your credence in P? Decompose it into at least the following terms, each with a number and a line of justification: the probability that the correct diagnosis is underdetermination of the final state rather than the conceptual impossibility of the task; the probability that the urn case — add ten balls, remove the lowest-numbered one, at every step — differs in kind from the lamp, in that a determinate answer survives there while none does here; the probability that "genuinely undetermined" names a real metaphysical category in this setting rather than a disguised complaint that the description omitted the switching rule or the exact timings; and the probability that ordinary physical constraints render the dispute moot. Then combine them, identify the term to which your final number is most sensitive, and say which single consideration would move that number furthest.

For reference: I sit near 0.7, while a colleague who takes such tasks to be conceptually impossible sits near 0.1. Tell me where our disagreement most plausibly lives.
