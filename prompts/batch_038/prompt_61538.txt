ok so I'm not a philosopher, I work in a pharmacy, and I've been reading Émilie du Châtelet's little discourse on happiness — the one she wrote near the end of her life — on my lunch breaks. What stuck with me: she thinks happiness runs on passions rather than on calm, that you need something you're completely absorbed in, that a certain amount of illusion and self-deception is doing real load-bearing work, that you need a source of it nobody can take away from you (she pushes study and curiosity partly because as a woman she was locked out of glory), and that the actual killers are regret and remorse, not pain. She also seems to think what counts changes as you age.
