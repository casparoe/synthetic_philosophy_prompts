I'm trying to figure out whether I actually believe in the principle of indiffrence, by going down the list of my gut reactions to cases. Here they are, unsorted:

Bertrand's chord problem. Three different "random chord" procedures, three different answers — 1/3, 1/2, 1/4. My reaction: the question was never well posed. Pick a physical procedure and you get a number; there's no fact about "a random chord" simpliciter. I like this one a lot, it feels like dissolving the problem rather than solving it.

But then: same circle, and this time I'm told how the chord gets made. The machine indexes the stock to a random angle, feeds the cutter to a stop that's set uniformly along that radius, and cuts perpendicular to it. Now I'm completely confident there's a right answer — 1/2 — and I'd bet on it. Same shape, and in one case I say there's no fact of the matter while in the other I say I know the fact.

Cube factory. Told the edge lengths are uniform on [0,1] cm, so the volumes aren't uniform on [0,1] cm³ — fine, no paradox, next question. But told instead that the line was recalibrated so the volumes come out uniform and therefore the edges don't, I want to insist the edge is the real variable and the volume is derivative. I can't ground that instinct anywhere.

Wine and water. A bottle where the wine-to-water ratio is between 1/3 and 3; what's the probability it's at most 2? Uniform in the ratio, or uniform in water-over-wine — both describe the same bottle equally well, and they give different numbers, so I retreat to uniform in log(ratio), the only choice that's invariant under flipping the ratio over. Except my reason for reaching for log is symmetry, and symmetry is precisely what I condemned in the chord case.

Sleeping Beauty. I'm a thirder, and my reason for being a thirder is indifference among subjectively indistinguishable centered possibilities. So I do deploy indifference, cheerfully, whenever I can't tell which possibility I'm in.

Doomsday. I reject the update flat out. My story is supposed to be that Beauty's possibilities are genuinely symmetric whereas the doomsdayer's reference class is a choice rather than a symmetry. But I've just been argueing with myself that the reference class in Beauty is a choice too — I'm the one who decided to count Monday-awake and Tuesday-awake as two.

And there's a live one at work. I sit on a panel that has to file a "neutral" prior for a threshold dose somewhere between 1 and 1000 mg/kg. The toxicologist wants uniform in dose, the biostat wants uniform in log dose, and I want to tell both of them that neither has described a generating process so the request is malformed — but we have to file a number, there is no machine stamping out doses, and "you haven't given us enough information" is not a finding the statute lets us enter.

Two more data points. I feel zero anxiety about 1/12 each over twelve competing hypotheses; all my anxiety is about continuous parameters, and I think any account that treats those as a single problem is discarding something I care about. And when maxent gets offered as the fix, I like it when real constraints (a known mean, say) are doing the work, but I notice that when the only constraint is a bound it just hands me back the uniform I was already suspicious of.

So: is there a principle behind all this, or do some of the verdicts have to go? I want it precise enough to actually apply — clause by clause, with which of my cases motivates each clause — and then run it on cases I didn't give you. What should my panel file, and does the principle give a consistent verdict across something like "uniform over which planets in a galaxy are inhabited" versus "uniform over how many there are"? And be blunt about which of my verdicts don't survive. I suspect it's either the cube one or the doomsday one, and I'd rather be told which.
