Fix a finite choice dataset over two goods: a handful of observed price–income situations together with the bundles chosen, all satisfying the generalized axiom of revealed preference. Such a dataset is rationalizable, but it does not pin down the indifference map. It delivers only bounds — some bundles must lie strictly above the indifference curve through an observed choice, some strictly below — and leaves a nonempty middle region on which the data imposes nothing. Consider two readings of that region.

(A) Underdetermination: the agent has determinate preferences there; the dataset simply fails to reveal them.

(B) No fact of the matter: the middle region is not unrevealed but empty. "Preference" gets whatever content it has from choice data, so where the data imposes nothing, the question has no true answer, and utility functions that differ only in that region are not rival descriptions of anything.

Give me your credence in (B), with the derivation rather than the number alone. I want at least these terms, each with a rough probability and a line of justification: whether a dispositional analysis of preference is correct as against a mental-state realist one; whether determinate counterfactual choices in the middle region — suppose there are some, stable across framings — are compatible with (B) or instead collapse it into (A); whether part of the dispute is verbal; and any term you think I have left out, including what happens in cardinal settings such as choice under risk. Then say how you combine them, which term your final number is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move it furthest.
