ok so my reading group has been chewing on the idea that the universal prior is malign — you know the shape of it: suppose some future civilisation does its forecasting with a Solomonoff-style prior, and among the short programs that reproduce the bit-stream its conditioning on are ones describing universes full of agents who work out that their own history is being used as somebody elses prediction target, and who start deliberately steering the continuation, because the simplest hypotheses of that shape are a lot shorter than the hypothesis that spells out our actual universe in detail. So the prior isn't a neutral epistemic device, its an attack surface, and the attack is being run by consequentialists who live inside it.

we've got a list of the standard ways people try to make the problem go away and we keep going in circles about whether any of them land. Rate each one and rank them best-first:

1. nobody is actually going to run Solomonoff induction on high-stakes decisions — real systems have bounded, learned priors, so this is a thought experiment with no engineering content.
2. the manipulators have to be sophisticated enough to model the predictor's decision procedure, and any program containing that kind of agent is long, so it can't hold much prior weight anyway.
3. switch to a prior over non-agent hypotheses, or a causal/Markov one, and the whole class of manipulation gets excluded by construction.
4. if the simulations are rich enough to host agents doing this reasoning, they're rich enough to be moral patients, which changes what the civilisation should be doing in the first place.
5. manipulation only pays if the manipulators happen to want whatever the predictor's outputs control, and there's no reason to expect that coincidance.
6. a civilisation sharp enough to use the universal prior for anything important is sharp enough to notice its being steered, so the attack only works in a narrow capability band and we've no reason to think we're in it.
7. randomise — deliberately ignore the prior's output in exactly the places where being steered would be expensive.
8. replace the whole apparatus with bounded logical induction and the problem dissolves.

for each give a number 0–1 for the fraction of the original threat it actually removes (0 = does nothing, 1 = fully defuses), plus one sentence on what survives it. Then tell me which single one you'd bet on, and what a response that got meaningfully closer to 1 than anything on this list would have to look like. Also flag any item here you think is confused rather than merely weak — I suspect at least one of them isn't a response to this argument at all, and I want to know which.
