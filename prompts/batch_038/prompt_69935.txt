What I need is a proper case analysis, not the textbook survey — I know Wright and Cummins cold, and I know the selected-effect vs. causal-role distinction, Millikan, Neander, Godfrey-Smith's proper function / modern history split. Don't recite any of that back at me except insofar as it does work on the case. No citations, no reading list, I have all of it.

The facts as I've got them, so you're not guessing: the 2012 round of consortium papers defined function operationally as biochemical activity — transcribed, bound by transcription factors, or carrying a chromatin signature — in at least one of the assayed cell types, and the headline number that came out of that was something like 80% of the genome. Critics (Graur, Doolittle, Palazzo, Gregory, roughly that crew) said activity isn't function and that the mutation-load argument caps the functional fraction somewhere near 10%; the junk tradition runs back through Ohno and the C-value paradox to the 1980 selfish-DNA papers, with neutral theory supplying the mechanism that lets slightly deleterious junk hang around, and Cavalier-Smith dissenting from the other direction with the nucleoskeletal stuff. One of the critics ran a reductio comparing the consortium's reasoning to counting the heat a TV set gives off among the set's functions.

First: do the mapping, and be disciplined about it. Who are the players and what is actually being disputed — the word, the concept, the measurement, or a prior about genome architecture?
