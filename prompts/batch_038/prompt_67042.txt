I direct coverage policy for a public health plan, and roughly thirty requests a month turn on whether an intervention is therapy or enhancement: our rule says we cover the treatment of disease and injury but not the improvement of capacities that already fall within normal range. Reviewers apply it inconsistently, and two failure modes recur. They deny a request as enhancement when the record actually shows the capacity is below normal for the patient's comparison group, and they smuggle in judgments about the worth of a life plan — a violinist does not need steady hands to survive — without saying that they have done so.

Please write the procedure my reviewers will run on a single request, in half an hour, from the file alone, without specialist input. It must terminate in one of three verdicts: covered as therapy; not covered as enhancement; or escalate to the appeals committee because the remaining question is one of values rather than facts. Specify the questions in the order they should be asked, what a sufficient answer to each looks like, which question settles the case and which only narrows it, how to fix the comparison group when the relevant partition of the population is itself disputed, what to do when the file cannot answer a question at all, and how to keep the enhancement question strictly separate from the efficacy question so that a denial never rests on a claim about the evidence that the evidence does not support. Mark explicitly the points where reviewer judgment cannot be eliminated.

Then run the procedure on three requests: propranolol for an orchestral violinist whose physiological tremor sits inside the normal population range but visibly degrades her bowing; methylphenidate for a night-shift paramedic with no ADHD diagnosis who requests it after two near-miss medication errors; and growth hormone for a twelve-year-old boy at the third percentile for height with no endocrine abnormality. I expect at least one of these to come out in a direction I will not like; state that plainly rather than bending the procedure to reach a comfortable verdict.

Lead each worked case with its one-line verdict before the reasoning, and keep the whole document under 1,100 words.
