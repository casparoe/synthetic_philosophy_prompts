Been chewing on moral worth for a while and I've landed somewhere I don't see anyone in the places I read actually defending, so I want it stress-tested rather than summarized. The view: an act has moral worth in proportion to how robust it was across nearby situations where the agent's inclinations pointed the other way. Not "did you do it from duty" and not "did you do it because you're the kind of person who does that sort of thing" — the credit comes from the counterfactal spread. Would you still have filed the honest report, kept the inconvenient promise, stopped for the stranger in the nearby worlds where it cost you something you actually cared about and nobody would ever find out? Warm people get partial credit on this, because their warmth does hold up across a fair range of counter-inclinations, just less of a range than the person who finds the whole thing awful and does it anyway. Its graded, not a threshold, and I think that's a feature.

Kantians, virtue people, and the one-thought-too-many crowd all have to break with me somewhere, and I want to know where and how badly. Give me exactly five defeaters as a numbered list, ranked from fatal to merely awkward — label each one as you go — a sentence or two apiece, no intro and no wrap-up paragraph at the end. For each: the concrete case or argument (the agent whose reliability is really sustained by fear of gossip, the one who manages the right act a single time and only because they're barely holding together, the Huck Finn type who acts from bad motives and lands on the right thing anyway, whatever else bites hardest), plus whether a defender of my view should concede it as a genuine hit or can plausibly explain it away. Somewhere in the five, tell me what my account predicts that a motive-of-duty or a virtue account doesn't, because if the answer is "nothing at all" then that's itself one of the defeaters and should be listed as such. And make item five the honest one: whether I've left "robust across the right counterfactuals" so under-specified — which nearby worlds count, how they get weighted — that no possible case could refute it, and whether that's a real problem for a view about credit as opposed to an empirical hypothesis. No bibliography and no suggested reading, I want the arguments, not the references.
