hey — i run a reading group that's half game theory, half moral philosophy, and we're closing out a unit on bargaining. instead of another slide of axioms i want to end on something funny. could you write me a fake app-store listing plus its customer review thread for a consumer product that sells "bargaining solutions as a service" to people splitting up? call it whatever, something with an absurd SaaS name (not splitwise, thats taken). around 700 words.

the product has tiers: a free plan, then premium plans named after the well-known solutions (the Nash one, the Kalai–Smorodinsky one, plus an egalitarian and a utilitarian option). users pick which notion of fairness they subscribe to and the app spits out an allocation.

the important thing is that the jokes have to come from getting the properties right — no generic tech-bro satire, no "the algorithm is biased" shrugs. things i definitely want in there:

- a one-star review from a guy who found out mid-divorce that his ex can use thier land in a way only she can (she's a contractor, some development-permit thing), which raises her best-case payoff and leaves his untouched. under his plan, his share went DOWN. he's apoplectic, and support's reply is technically correct and completely unhelpful. get the direction of that right, it's the crux of the whole piece.
- a five-star review from someone on the Nash tier gloating that nothing you drag to the table changes the answer, with a reply from another user pointing out exactly why that should worry him.
- something about rescaling: a reviewer whose therapist helped her convert everything into "happiness units" at a different rate than his coach did, and the free plan changed its answer while the premium plans didn't. feel free to work this into a FAQ or sidebar — "rescaling-proof" as a paid feature.
- the emotional center: a long review from someone who spent six months arguing about what counts as the walkaway point — whose job offer counts, what the house is really worth, whether the dog is a cost or an asset — and then noticed that the app just takes whatever two numbers you type into those boxes, and everything elegant downstream only decides how to divide what's left above them.
- two or three developer responses, deadpan, that never once acknowledge the last point.

target of the satire: treating the choice between these solutions as a purely formal matter and never asking where the disagreement point comes from. don't state that anywhere — the reader should feel it, and the last line should just be the app's star rating. no real people, no citations, no footnotes. readable by someone who's never seen a bargaining diagram, but a game theorist should actually laugh at the monotonicity bit rather than wince at it.
