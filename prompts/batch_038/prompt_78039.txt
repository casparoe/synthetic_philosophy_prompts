I've written down the six commitments that make up my working position on supervising AI systems, and I want the package checked before I circulate it.

1. The case for supervising the process rather than the outcome is that, at the frontier, the final result frequently exceeds what a human evaluator can check, while the individual steps leading to it frequently do not.
2. What actually convinces me empirically is the head-to-head literature: in domains where ground-truth final answers are available for labeling and scoring, step-level reward models substantially outperform outcome-level ones at selecting correct solutions.
3. I take the reward-model overoptimization results seriously — a learned reward degrades as the policy is pushed far from its initialization — so the remedies are small KL budgets, early stopping, and ensembles of reward models.
4. I nevertheless hold that process supervision is structurally less exposed to Goodharting than outcome supervision, because a grade distributed over many intermediate decisions leaves less to be won by gaming any single one.
5. I also believe chain-of-thought transcripts are frequently unfaithful: models shaped by pressure to produce derivations that look right often reach their answers by routes the transcript does not report.
6. For evaluation I trust only outcome measures — accuracy against checkable answers — and I am skeptical of any benchmark that scores the quality of a model's intermediate reasoning.

Some of these strike me as pulling against each other, and I suspect that at least one pair only looks that way. Please audit the set and rank the tensions by severity rather than reporting them all as equally bad. For each genuine tension, identify which commitment has to weaken, what the minimal weakening is, and what I lose by accepting it. For the pairs that are merely apparently inconsistent, explain why they survive inspection and whether anything residual is left over. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what claims I would have to stop making if I adopted it.
