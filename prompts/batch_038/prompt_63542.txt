A regional payer is drafting the rule that will decide which conditions count as diseases for purposes of publicly funded treatment, and I want the standard fixed before anyone picks an underlying account. Write the desiderata any concept of disease must meet to serve that job: the phenomena it has to explain; the cases it has to get right — asymptomatic genetic risk, conditions whose statistical abnormality is itself produced by earlier treatment (a reference range for, say, blood lipids that drifts once most of the comparison population is medicated), and states the affected community denies are disorders; the distinctions it may not collapse (disease / illness / impairment / disability, disorder-as-kind versus disorder-in-this-person, being disordered and being merely different); and the operational constraints, including that the rule must return a verdict when clinician and community disagree, and must specify what would count as evidence for each of its terms. For every requirement give the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are local preferences of a camp — statistical-function accounts, hybrids that add a harm condition, and views on which disorder is socially conferred. Flag any subset you believe cannot be jointly satisfied; if the list turns out to be a result in its own right, that is what I most want to see. Close by naming the single requirement that eliminates the most candidates and scoring the three camps briefly against it.
