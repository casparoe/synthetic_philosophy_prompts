> **Question 3 (20 marks, 1000 words).** Explain why Schopenhauer holds that music alone among the arts is a copy of the will itself rather than a copy of the Ideas, and say what this implies for the place of music in his ranking of the arts. Then, in a seperate final section, respond to the objection raised in the week 5 seminar: since nothing in experience could confirm or disconfirm the claim that music copies the will, Schopenhauer's account of music is not really a theory about music at all, but an unfalsifiable redescription of his metaphysics. Here's the script:

---

Schopenhauer divides everything into will and representation. The world we deal with day to day — bodies, objects, even our own actions as we see them from outside — is representation, and it is structured by space, time and causality, which between them are just the principle of sufficient reason at work. Prior to all of this is the will: a single blind striving with no goal and no ground, which is what Kant's thing in itself turns out to be once Schopenhauer shows that we meet it from the inside in our own bodily action, where we are not spectators of an event but the event itself.

The will does not appear in individuals straight away. It objectifies itself in stages, and these stages are what Schopenhauer, following Plato, calls the Ideas. The arts are then ranked by how high up that scale the Idea they copy happens to sit. Architecture copies the lowest Ideas — weight, rigidity, resistance, light — which is why it can only show us dumb forces locked in conflict. Gardening and landscape painting come above it, then animal painting and sculpture, then poetry in its various kinds, with tragedy at the top of the poetic arts, since its subject is the Idea of humanity shown most completely, the will in conflict with itself.

Music is the exception, and the exception is total. It copies no Idea. It copies the will itself, and this is why Schopenhauer says it stands wholly apart from the rest: it is a copy of the will in just the way the world is a copy of the will, so it is independent of the world of appearance and would in a sense still be what it is even if that world were gone. Its universality is also not the universality of concepts, which is abstract and empty; music is universal and yet completely determinate. That is the reason an untrained listener grasps it as well as a composer does, and the reason it needs no words — indeed Schopenhauer thinks it inverts the proper relation to treat music as a means to a text.

Now, since music copies the will, and the will is the highest Idea in the scale of objectification, music sits at the top of the same ladder the other arts climb. It is simply the highest rung, above tragedy rather than outside the ordering. And this has a consequence Schopenhauer accepts rather than evades. Aesthetic pleasure everywhere else comes from will-less contemplation: the subject stops being a striver and becomes a clear mirror of the object. But the content of music is striving itself, and its effect is famously to take hold of us, to hurry the pulse and pull us along. So musical pleasure cannot be will-less in the way tragic pleasure is. Music is the one art that rouses the will instead of quieting it, and his system has to treat our response to music as a different kind of thing altogether from our response to tragedy. This is also his answer to the seminar objection. The will is not known by observing it but by being it, so there is nothing to confirm or disconfirm from the outside in the first place; music moves us because we are the will, and no experiment could be evidence for or against that.
