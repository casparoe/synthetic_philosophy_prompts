I am setting the four-hour take-home for a graduate seminar that has just spent three weeks on Parfit's reductionism and on the Buddhist reductionist alternative to it, and I want one question that cannot be passed by reciting the chariot simile from the Milindapañha, listing the five aggregates, or intoning that persons are conceptual fictions. The crux I want the question to force the students onto is asymmetry. Parfit's Relation R is symmetric and he is perfectly content for it to hold one-to-many in fission cases; karmic continuity, by contrast, has to run in one direction and to a single successor stream if moral responsibility and rebirth are to mean anything at all. So the Buddhist reductionist who wants to keep karma while denying a self appears to need a non-branching clause added to causal-mental continuity — and any such clause starts to look like a substantial person smuggled back in through the back door. I want the question to make the students confront that clause directly, using a case in which one mental continuum divides into two successor streams, both of which are then ordained, and one of which commits a serious transgression: which stream ripens the fruit, and if both do, has the karmic ledger been doubled rather than merely relocated? The second half of the question should ask whether the pudgalavādin's person, which is held to be neither identical with nor distinct from the aggregates and therefore inexpressible, is genuinely better equipped for this problem than Vasubandhu's reductionism, or whether it simply moves the same difficulty up a level.

Please write the question itself, a model answer of about 900 words, and a marking scheme out of 25. The scheme should name the specific argumentative moves an A answer must make — at least one of which must involve the student reasoning through the fission case rather than reporting a position — and should list separately, with mark deductions, four characteristic mistakes, each annotated with a line explaining why it is a philosophical error and not merely an omission.
