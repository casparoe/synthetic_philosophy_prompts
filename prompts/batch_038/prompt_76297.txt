- the fizzle view: hypocritical blame isn't a genuine instance of blame at all. The act misfires, sort of like a promise made with your fingers crossed behind your back. So there's no real blame on the table to assess morally, and whatever's wrong here is wrong in the way deception is wrong.
- the recusal view: it IS a real piece of blame, and it can even be exactly right on the merits (the target did it, deserves it, no excuses), but this particular blamer is barred from delivering it. Like a judge who has to recuse himself — the verdict he'd reach is correct, he's just not the one who's allowed to issue it.

My TA called the second one the correct-but-impermissible puzzle, and that's the part I really want unpacked. If the blame is true and deserved, what exactly is doing the wrong-making work? Unfairness (you're enforcing a norm you exempt yourself from)? A failure to treat the person as your equal in some specific sense? Usurping a status you don't have? So first: explain the difference between the two views in a way that makes it obvious they're genuinely incompatible and not just two descriptions of one thing. Ideally give me a test case where they'd come apart in what they predict.
