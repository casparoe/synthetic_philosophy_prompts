I'm putting together a few cases for a reading group on collective intentionality and I want one that isolates Bratman's meshing-subplans condition, because everyone I know just nods at that condition and noone's ever actually had to test it.

The target: a two-person cooperative activity that goes off without a hitch — they finish what they set out to do, they're mutually responsive throughout, each knows the other's intentions and knows that the other knows, and neither is lying to or withholding anything relevant from the other. But there's no way to fill in both agents' partial plans so that the fill-ins are compatible. So on Bratman's analysis there's no shared intention here (one condition fails), while Searle would say there was a genuine we-intention the whole time and Gilbert would say there was a real joint commitment, obligations and standing to rebuke included. The point of the case is that the *success* of the joint activity doesn't diagnose meshing, so nobody can wave the case away by pointing at how smoothly it all went.

Constraints:

- realistic, ordinary-life scale. two people doing something people actually do together — no sci-fi apparatus, no exotic institutions, no brain-scanning.
- the scenario itself in one paragraph.
- the incompatibility has to be load-bearing, not an artifact of how you describe the plans. This is the hard bit and the part I most want you to be careful about: you can't get the effect by handing each of them a fallback triggered by some condition that just happens never to obtain, since a Bratmanian will say conditional plans with untriggered antecedents mesh perfectly well. Whatever the clash is, it has to survive being written out explicitly as conditionals, and you have to explain why it survives.
- mutual responsiveness, common knowledge, and commitment to the joint activity are all fully intact. If the case only works because somebody is distracted, misinformed, or quietly backing out, it doesn't count — I want meshing to be the sole failure.

Then after the scenario:

1. Run Bratman's conditions down one at a time and say which are satisfied and which isn't.
2. Write out each agents partial plan as fully as you can, offer at least one candidate completion of each, and show that no completion of the pair is jointly consistent. If you can't actually show that, say so rather than hand-waving.
3. Searle and Gilbert on the same case: what each says and why — and tell me whether they really come out aligned here, or whether one of them has a problem of its own that I should be ready for in discussion.
4. Which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what the three verdicts look like if you remove it.
5. The best Bratmanian reply — the one claiming my case is really about mere dispositions rather than subplans, or re-describing the plans so that they mesh — and whether it goes through.

And if you think the spec as I've written it can't be met, don't fake it: tell me which constraint has to go and give me the closest case that does the same job.
