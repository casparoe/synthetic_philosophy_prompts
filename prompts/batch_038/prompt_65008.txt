Please write a satirical document: a technical memorandum of no more than 750 words, annex included, from an invented consultancy — Tideway Impact Advisory — to the standards committee of an invented certification scheme for farmed shrimp, the Assured Crustacean Standard. The committee has asked the consultancy to rank two production systems by their effect on aggregate welfare: a conventional semi-intensive pond operation, and a more intensive system that houses roughly thirty times as many animals while scoring marginally worse on the scheme's own per-animal measure. The memorandum reports the result and makes a recommendation.

The comedy must come from the methodology being entirely respectable and applied with complete honesty. The intensive system should come out ahead by a wide margin, because the scheme scores welfare per animal and then multiplies by headcount, and the memorandum should record this as a decisive finding rather than a curiosity. Do not let anyone in the document notice what has happened, and do not explain anything afterwards: the target is a style of reasoning — the conversion of an unsettled question about which animals are sentient into an auditable aggregate — rather than a villain, a campaigning group or a named individual. The authors should read as competent, sincere and slightly bored. Deadpan throughout.

I would like the following features to do explicit work in the text. First, the moral weight assigned to shrimp should be a discount factor reflecting the scheme's stated confidence that they are sentient; the memorandum never argues about sentience at all, and defends the figure only on the grounds that it is conservative and that a sensitivity analysis was performed. Second, the memorandum should note without embarrassment that this discount is applied to the farmed shrimp but not to the small pelagic fish reduced to feed, on the grounds that the latter fall outside the scope of the scheme. Third, at least one unit of measurement should quietly settle a metaphysical question: welfare reported per kilogram of biomass rather than per animal, say, or a headcount estimated as stocking numbers less pre-harvest mortality, so that animals who die of disease never enter the denominator. Fourth, the memorandum should observe in the same flat register that a disease-management intervention which reduces pre-harvest deaths increases the number of animals who survive to be harvested, and that the rubric scores this as an unambiguous improvement. Fifth, the annex should be a short table showing how the recommendation turns on where the scheme places the neutral point of its welfare scale — that is, on whether the lives of intensively farmed shrimp sit above or below zero — with the ranking reversing across values that all fall within the range the committee has previously described as plausible. Present the table as a procedural formality, with no comment attached.

Anchor the document in real practice rather than invented cruelty. Eyestalk ablation of broodstock — removal of an eyestalk and with it the X-organ–sinus gland complex that secretes gonad-inhibiting hormone, performed to accelerate maturation and spawning — should appear as a scored line item with a corrective-action timeline. Chilling in ice slurry at harvest should appear as the scheme's default slaughter method and be credited as humane. A single grow-out pond may be described as holding animals in the hundreds of thousands. Every welfare-relevant fact should be true; only the consultancy, the scheme and the arithmetic are invented.

Keep it legible to a reader who has never encountered the dispute between total and average aggregation: the recoil should arrive through the procedure and the numbers, not through adjectives. Give me the memorandum and its annex and nothing else.
