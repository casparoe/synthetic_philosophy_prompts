I'm in year three of a PhD in industrial ecology, which basically means my entire life is lifecycle assessment — I model animal-protein systems, mostly ruminant vs. poultry supply chains. Lately I've gotten dragged into the EA-adjacent ethics literature about eating animals, partly because a friend who works at a farm sanctuary keeps sending me links, and partly because my own numbers kept nudging me toward questions I dont know how to think about. The stuff I keep running into: the claim that switching from beef to chicken makes things worse rather than better, because a chicken is so much smaller than a cow that the same tonnage of meat means many more individuals killed; the "crop deaths" argument, that tillage, harvest and rodent control on cropland (including cropland growing feed) kills a lot of wild vertebrates, so a plant-based diet isn't slaughter-free either; a parallel literature saying we should care about the huge number of wild animals who die of predation, starvation and disease, and that this might swamp everything happening on farms; plus the standard pushbacks — that one person's shopping basket changes nothing, that you can't do that arithmetic honestly, that you shouldn't treat wild ecosystems as a ledger of harms, and that animals raised for food who would otherwise never have existed aren't harmed by being killed at the end.

What I want is a translation exercise, because I don't trust myself to read the ethics on its own terms — I understand every word and none of the stakes. Take that cluster of claims and restate them as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually live in: functional unit, system boundary, inventory, allocation and co-products, system expansion vs. partitioning, attributional vs. consequential modelling, marginal vs. average, characterization factors, midpoint vs. endpoint indicators, uncertainty ranges, sensitivity analysis. So concretely: what would the functional unit for "suffering per kg of protein" even be, if there is one? Is the beef-to-chicken number-of-individuals point just a unit conversion, or does it smuggle in something? Is "your individual purchase doesn't matter" the same dispute as average-vs-marginal modelling, or a different one that only looks similar? Where does wild animal suffering sit — inside the boundary of a food system at all, or a background condition we'd handle as an externality? And the crop deaths claim: is that an inventory item, a land-use impact, or something the framework has no slot for? Worth knowing that our biodiversity indicators are all species-and-area-and-time constructs, so an individual animal's death basically doesn't register anywhere in them, which I suspect is doing a lot of work here.

Then the part I actually care about, the residue report. Go claim by claim and sort them into (a) translates cleanly, (b) translates only if you bend my vocabulary — and tell me exactly where the bend is and what it costs — and (c) has no expression in it whatsoever. For every (c), give me your view on whether that's a genuine gap in LCA, i.e. the framework is structurally silent on something that matters, or whether it means the ethical claim was vaguer than it sounded — maybe "count the individuals" really is just a choice of functional unit and the whole fight is notational. Please be honest rather than tidy: every time you have to make a normative call to get the translation to go through (whose suffering counts, whether death and chronic pain are commensurable, whether a never-existing animal can be harmed, whether the relevant quantity is total or per-unit), flag it as a normative call instead of presenting it as a fact about my framework. I'd much rather get a long list of "this doesn't fit, and here's the shape of the misfit" than a clean mapping that quietly turns animal ethics into something LCA could always say.
