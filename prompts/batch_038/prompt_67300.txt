Write a piece of fellowship literature — roughly 700 words, in the numbered-steps, first-person-plural register of a twelve-step pamphlet — for a recovery group whose members have given up explosion and now live with a few true contradictions. The disease is the Law of Non-Contradiction; the First Step is powerlessness over a liar sentence and an unmanageable consequence relation.

Keep it entirely deadpan: no glossary, no aside for outsiders, no wink. The humor should come from the program having honestly absorbed the objections, so that a classical logician reading it recognizes every standard complaint as a step, a caution, or a relapse. Require the following beats, in whatever order works: a clear distinction between mere paraconsistency (a practice, available without believing anything) and dialetheism (a view about truth), with one member who has worked all the steps and still insists she has never accepted a contradiction; relapse framed as the strengthened liar, which keeps coming back after each inventory; trivialism as the bottom, with a testimonial from someone who came in believing everything and is now grateful to believe only a laminated list of sentences; a caution about Curry-style conditionals the fellowship refuses to sign; and an answer to the charge that a member can no longer deny anything, worked into the pamphlet's own idiom of denial.

The literature cannot settle how many steps there are; treat the discrepancy as a dialetheia rather than resolving it. Close with a short reading in the manner of a meeting's closing prayer, mapping serenity, courage, and wisdom onto which contradictions to accept, which to reject, and how to tell them apart. Do not name any living philosopher; refer to the founders only by their initials.
