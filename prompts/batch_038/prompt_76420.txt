I've been defending a property-dualist line for some years now and a colleague reckons my package is unstable — that I'm entitled to at most half of it at a time. Before I go back at him I'd like a proper audit rather than a recital of the standard objections. Here's the set, more or less in the order I'd defend it:

1. Physical causal closure is non-negotiable: every physical event has a sufficient physical cause.
2. Mary learns something genuinely new on release, so there are non-functional phenomenal properties over and above the physical ones.
3. Hence neural states cause qualia and qualia cause nothing whatsoever — not behaviour, not other mental states, not each other.
4. I know all about the paradox of phenomenal judgment and I think I've handled it: introspective knowledge needs sensitivity, not causation by the fact known, and since the quale and its neural base are nomically tied, in the nearest world where I'm not in pain I don't believe I am. Common cause, problem dissolved.
5. Beliefs are individuated by their causal roles. I'm a functionalist about belief, just not about consciousness.
6. The pairing of qualia with functions isnt a brute cosmic accident — awfulness lands on damage-detection, colour phenomenology on wavelength discrimination, and thats not arbitrary.

Which pairs genuinely pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the real ones by severity, give the cheapest repair for each and what the repair costs, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most damage. Be blunt about whether (4) is as safe as I think. British English, please.
