I cover archaeology and the science of complex systems for a general-interest desk, and across several years of reporting I keep meeting a pattern I have no name for. Whenever a past society is said to have collapsed, the verdict depends entirely on which level of description you inspect, and the levels can point in opposite directions at once.

Two instances from stories I have handled. A Bronze Age palace system in the eastern Mediterranean: the administrative tablets stop, the monumental building stops, the rich burials disappear, and the number of people who can write shrinks to a handful of specialists — yet in the ordinary settlements that follow, skeletal and burial evidence is no worse and in places better, and the same language and the same gods carry on for another thousand years. A Mesoamerican lowland kingdom: its rulers stop commissioning dated monuments and the great centers lose most of their inhabitants, while a few hundred kilometres away other centers enter their most spectacular building phase within the same decades. Catastrophe at the level of institutions and elites, continuity or even improvement at the level of the population, both descriptions true of one and the same event, neither reducible to the other.

Is there an established term for this? Not for the loose observation that "collapse" is a loaded word; I want the level-dependence specifically — the fact that the register in which you measure determines whether anything collapsed at all. I am repeatedly pointed toward resilience theory and regime shifts, toward complexity loss or decomplexification, toward the argument that collapse can be a rational response to diminishing returns on complexity, and toward Simpson's paradox. Each looks adjacent and each is about something slightly different, the last because it treats the reversal as a statistical artifact rather than as a genuine fact about two distinct things being measured. Which of these is nearest, how precisely does what I have described differ from each, and if none fits, coin and define a term — including the boundary cases. What if the population-level register deteriorates too: does that make it a real collapse, or merely one in which the levels agree? At what point does the label stop doing work? And is there a name, established or otherwise, for the corresponding rhetorical move — describing a situation in one register while evaluating it in another?
