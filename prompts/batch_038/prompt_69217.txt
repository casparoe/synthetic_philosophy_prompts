Question they were answering:

"State the simple conditional analysis of a disposition ascription, and explain what 'fink' cases are meant to show about it, using Martin's electro-fink. Then explain how masking cases are supposed to differ from fink cases, and whether Lewis's reformed conditional analysis handles both. Finish with your own view of whether the project of reducing dispositions to conditionals can be rescued."

Student's answer:

"The simple analysis says that for x to be disposed to give response r to stimulus s is just: if x were s, x would r. So a glass is fragile iff, were it struck, it would break. Finks defeat this because the truth of the conditional depends on what happens after the stimulus, not just on what the object is like beforehand. Martin's electro-fink is a wire that is live — it would shock you if you touched it — but a device senses the touch and kills the current first, so the conditional comes out false even though the wire really was disposed to shock. The lesson is that extrinsic interferers can falsify the conditional without anything being wrong with the disposition, so the analysis is too weak.

Masking is basically a fink that arrives later instead of sooner: something removes the disposition right at the point where it was about to manifest, so it's still a case of the disposition going away. Our own datacenter is like this — the heat sensors are wired into the suppression system, but an on-call runbook flips a global maintenance flag the instant any sensor reads hot, so the sprinklers never fire. The flag deletes the sensors' disposition to trigger suppression, which is why the analysis fails.

Lewis responded by tacking a ceteris paribus clause onto the conditional, roughly 'all else being equal', which is an admission that you can't reduce dispositions to conditionals without a hedge and therefore that they have to be taken as basic, unanalyzable properties.

My own view is that this is bad news for dispositions full stop. A masked disposition could be intercepted in every single situation it ever faces, and a disposition that is never manifested anywhere is indistinguishable from there being no disposition at all. So the conditional analysis and realism about dispositions sink together."
