Background. My town, which I'll call K—, is in a weird spot. There's an unincorporated stretch of road on the eastern edge, maybe 400 people, that the council wants to annex for the tax base. Last month at a public meeting the two got tangled into a single proposal: hold a first referendum open to literally everyone who lives inside the watershed — annexation targets, 16-year-olds, undocumented residents, tourists, even the people who just commute in for work — whose *only* job is to decide who gets to vote in the second referendum, which then decides annexation and the franchise extension. The pitch was that the existing demos can't be the body that decides who joins the demos, so you convene a bigger, residence-based body to do that one job and then dissolve it.

Two people I know, Nadia and Ben, argued about this in the parking lot and then at some length over email, and I ended up with the whole exhange. Roughly:

1. **Nadia:** Any decision about who counts as a member of the people has to be made by, or at least be acceptable to, everyone it applies to. Otherwise the demos constitutes itself by fiat and there's no non-circular answer to why this boundary rather than that one. So the two-stage thing is at least pointed at the right problem, however clunky.
2. **Ben:** That's an infinite regress. If every boundary decision needs a prior demos to make it, and that demos needs one before it, you never get started. Every actual democracy on earth was founded by a group with no right whatsoever to found it — which is presumably why Nadia gets to vote today. So the regress can't be a real objection, it's just the price of having politics.
3. **Nadia:** Fine, but notice what you just conceded. If the regress is unavoidable, the boundary question isn't a democratic question — it sits outside the procedure and gets settled by something else. So let's stop pretending it's a vote and start asking which boundary is just.
4. **Ben:** Then you've dropped the thing you were defending. And just by what standard? 5. **Nadia:** That's why I don't use affectedness. Use subjection: whoever is subject to the town's coercive rules gets a say in making them. 7. **Nadia:** Stage one is a kludge, I'll grant that. But it's a kludge in the right direction, and it beats letting the current council decide, which is just the incumbent demos voting to enlarge itself on terms it picks.

Conceding the regress doesn't hand you might-makes-right.
