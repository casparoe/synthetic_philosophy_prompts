A safety measure earns its keep only if it can be checked from outside the model, by measurement or by proof, rather than resting on an inductive leap from the training distribution to states in which the model has never been observed. You reason from the assumption that any single barrier will be penetrated, so you design for penetration to be detected early rather than for it never to occur; failure need not be impossible, only loud enough and soon enough that a bad outcome is caught before it becomes irreversible.

Here is the proposal under discussion. My group wants to deploy a capable model behind a deliberately thin control layer: a sandbox for code execution, a learned output filter, and a smaller monitor model that reads the primary model's stated plan and raises an alarm if it looks deceptive or dangerous. Everything else we expect from training. The primary model is trained to reason explicitly about our policies, to flag its own uncertainty, and to cooperate with the monitor, and we test those dispositions with weak-to-strong setups and debate. My position is that the trained dispositions are load-bearing and the control layer exists mainly to reassure people who do not trust the training.

My opening move: apply your own test to your own programme. Every mechanism you propose must interpret the model's behavior before it can constrain it. The output filter has to classify an action as dangerous, the monitor has to read a plan and judge it, the tripwire has to decide what counts as an alarm. Each such interpreter is either a learned component trained on data of the same kind as the thing it polices, or a formal verifier applied to a specification written in ordinary language and, in practice, revised with input from the system it governs. In the first case your monitor is a smaller model whose good behavior you must simply assume, so the trust has moved rather than vanished. In the second, you have proved only that the system conforms to its own reading of the rules. The real choice, then, is not between trust and verification; it is between trusting one large model and trusting a stack of smaller ones plus a document the large model helps write. Why is control anything other than alignment with more moving parts and a wider attack surface?
