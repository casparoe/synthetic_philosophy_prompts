I am a fourth-year PhD student in theoretical biology working on origins-of-life models, and this term I have been reading the typogenetics material in Gödel, Escher, Bach alongside the self-reproducing automata literature for a seminar on what Hofstadter calls the DNA–Gödel isomorphism. My advisor's one-line summary of the entire program — which he has now deployed twice in group meeting, both times to end a discussion — is that self-replication is just self-reference with the syntax made physical. Last week a visiting student argued that a collectively autocatalytic reaction network, in which no molecule anywhere carries an encoded description of the network, is nonetheless genuinely self-reproducing. My advisor waved the objection away with his line: because the network contains no description of itself, it cannot be a replicator in the sense that matters, and whatever propagation it exhibits is an accident of chemistry rather than an instance of self-replication. I suspect that one sentence is doing at least four different jobs at once and that at least one of them is false, but I have not been able to lay the options out cleanly, and I want to be able to answer him in his own terms.

Could you unpack the slogan into a menu of distinct, precisely stated claims? For each reading I want the exact content of the alleged isomorphism — what in the replicator is supposed to correspond to what in the diagonal or fixed-point construction, and what the correspondence licenses — followed by a verdict: platitude, substantive and defensible, or false, with the reason for the verdict rather than a hedge. The readings I can half-see, offered only as a starting sketch you should correct, reorder, or discard: a fixed-point reading on which replication is a self-application structure of the sort a quine exhibits; an arithmetization reading on which the point is that one medium can encode and be operated on by another; a reader-dependence reading on which a code has semantic content only relative to machinery that interprets it; an incompleteness reading on which replicators face limits on self-inspection analogous to a system's inability to certify its own consistency; and a von Neumann reading on which a replicator must contain a description of what it builds together with machinery for building from the description and machinery for copying the description — which, as far as I can tell, is a claim about replicators that does not mention Gödel at all.

Then, and this is the part I most need, tell me which single reading my advisor requires in order to dismiss the autocatalytic network, whether that reading is true, and therefore whether his dismissal goes through — committing to a definite answer with reasons, since a balanced survey of the options is exactly what I can already produce and would be useless to me. If the readings he needs and the readings that are true come apart, say so plainly. Finally, flag any place where you think my own framing is loose or wrong, including my characterization of the typogenetics puzzle and of what the first incompleteness theorem actually establishes; I would much rather be corrected than accommodated.
