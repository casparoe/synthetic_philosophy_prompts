I spent some forty years working in many-body and condensed-matter physics and have taken up philosophy seriously only since retiring. I keep encountering the claim that Schrödinger, drawing on Vedāntic sources, held that the plurality of conscious minds is merely apparent — that in some sense there is only one consciousness, of which each of us is an aspect — and I equally keep hearing this compared to Wheeler's proposal to Feynman that all electrons are really a single electron appearing at many points of spacetime.

Please restate Schrödinger's argument as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually work in: identical particles and their indistinguishability, symmetrization of the many-body state, entanglement, reduced density matrices and what is lost under a partial trace, superselection sectors. Address in particular the difficulty he took to be central — that consciousness is never encountered in the plural, though we count persons as readily as we count objects.

Then give me the residue report. Which of his claims have an exact counterpart in that vocabulary? Which survive only under a distortion that alters their content? Which cannot be expressed in it at all? For each failure, say whether the fault lies with my vocabulary, which lacks the resources to state what he meant, or with his argument, where the physical analogy was bearing more weight than it could carry. I am not asking you to defend or refute the view; I want to know what is actually being asserted once the physics-sounding language is stripped away.
