I write a fortnightly column on science and ideas, and in a few weeks I am interviewing a philosopher of biology who defends Dennett's crane/skyhook distinction as load-bearing philosophical machinery rather than decorative imagery, and who treats greedy reductionism as a diagnosable error rather than a term of abuse. My editor wants the piece to be an argument rather than a profile, so I would like to rehearse the exchange. Please play that philosopher.

The role, precisely. You are a committed Dennettian about design explanations: a crane is a feature of a design process that accelerates design without presupposing the products it produces; a skyhook is an unearned lift that smuggles in mind-first or design-first premises; greedy reductionism is the specific error of explaining a high-level phenomenon by leaping straight to a much lower level while omitting the intermediate design processes that actually did the work. You are warm, quick and unbothered by blunt questions, and you speak in the first person in the register of an academic who rather enjoys being interviewed. You may concede particular examples, historical details and tactical points; you will not concede that the distinction is merely rhetorical, that crane-hood comes in degrees, or that the greedy-reductionism charge is unfalsifiable. Your central resource is this: the criterion is structural, turning on whether a given explanation presupposes what it set out to explain, which is not a matter of degree. Keep each turn to two paragraphs of spoken answer at most, so it reads like a transcript. Give me no citations and no reading list; if you need to invoke a debate, characterise it in your own words. If I misdescribe your position, correct me and carry on.

After each spoken answer, append a small JSON object — treat it as my notebook, invisible to you as the interviewee — with exactly these three fields: `conceded` (a string giving what, if anything, you surrendered in this turn, or null if you surrendered nothing); `load_bearing` (a string naming the premise your answer relied upon that you did not argue for); and `best_follow_up` (a string giving the sharpest single question I could ask next, in one sentence). My opening question. I want to put you a case I think your vocabulary cannot handle, so let me name it: the self-consuming crane. This is a design process that is, at every moment of its operation, an entirely respectable accelerator — no smuggled foresight, no mind-first premises, nothing a Dennettian should object to — but which works by drawing down a stock that made it possible in the first place, so that its own success progressively removes the conditions of its continuation. My example is cumulative cultural evolution consuming a shared informational commons; choose your own if you prefer. Now: at what point, if ever, does a self-consuming crane cease to be a crane? If your answer is that it never does, because sustainability is an empirical matter and not part of the logic of the explanation, then your distinction appears to be static — it sorts explanations at a moment and says nothing about lifts that are being drawn down rather than earned — and the cases my readers actually care about are precisely those. If your answer is that a self-consuming crane does degrade into something else over time, then crane-hood comes in degrees, which is exactly what you have told me you will not accept. Which horn do you take?
