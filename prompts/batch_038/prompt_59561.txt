I am applying for a departmental summer research fellowship — eight funded weeks, no teaching obligations — and need a proposal of roughly 1,000 words followed by a week-by-week plan. The reading committee is mixed: two philosophers of biology and one philosopher of medicine, none of whom specializes in the other's subfield. My project concerns the dispute between selected-effect (etiological) and causal-role accounts of biological function, but I want to approach it from a direction I have not seen developed, and the proposal has to argue that the direction is both non-standard and tractable in eight weeks.

My half-formed idea. The dispute is usually prosecuted by testing each account against a small stock of standing cases — malfunction, exaptation, a genuinely novel trait, a trait whose present contribution has drifted from what it was recruited for — and then asking which account returns the intuitively correct verdict. My suspicion is that this methodology cannot separate the accounts, because for any single trait under stable conditions the two largely agree, and where they disagree each side can redescribe the case in its own vocabulary and claim the intuition. What should distinguish them is their verdicts across a systematic class of cases in which the selection regime and the current causal contribution come apart wholesale rather than incidentally: organisms whose environment has changed faster than selection can track; lineages that have passed through domestication or a severe bottleneck; traits whose bearer or context has been replaced, as with a transplanted organ or a device that has taken over a physiological role; and managed or engineered populations. For these regime-shift cases, the interesting question is not merely what each account says the function is, but whether each account has the resources to say when a function is *lost*, when it has *changed*, and when it is *merely unexercised* — and whether the answer it gives is one that a practice which must act on function ascriptions can actually use: clinical judgment about whether an organ is functioning properly, conservation and restoration decisions about which traits matter, the functional-trait vocabulary of ecology.

A second strand I want braided in. These practices already appear to deploy different notions of function, and to deploy them indexically: a clinician assesses present performance, an evolutionary-medicine explanation appeals to what the trait was for, and both may be describing the same organ in the same patient. If so, pluralism looks attractive — but pluralism is cheap unless one can specify which notion governs in which context and what to do when the notions issue in conflicting verdicts about one and the same case, for instance an organ that discharges its present contribution superbly while having been displaced from what it was selected to do, or the reverse.

Please structure the proposal so that it contains: (i) a precise statement of the research question, precise enough that one could say what an answer to it would look like; (ii) an account of why the standing positions — including the more recent naturalistic approaches that try to ground function in disposition, in organization, or in contribution to persistence rather than in selection history — leave that question open rather than merely disagreeing about it; (iii) the method: what I would actually do, week by week, including how the case class would be assembled and what would count as a fair test of an account against it rather than a test rigged in advance; (iv) an argument for this approach over the obvious alternatives, namely arguing directly for one account against the other or retreating to pluralism; (v) a description of what success looks like, and separately what would count as failure — I want genuine failure conditions that the project could meet, not a restatement of the aims; and (vi) a candid assessment of scope, identifying which parts are too ambitious for eight weeks and what should be cut.

I will supply all references myself, so please include no bibliography and no literature review organized by citation; referring to positions, arguments, and test cases by description is precisely what I want. Please also spare me any paragraph on the general importance of the topic or on how the project will benefit wider audiences.
