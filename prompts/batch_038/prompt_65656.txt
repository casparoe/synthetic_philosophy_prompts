I chair the three-member jury for my department's annual dissertation prize. The award's terms say only that it goes to 'the most outstanding dissertation submitted this year'. Two finalists remain, and on our reading one is clearly the stronger piece of philosophy; but its author already holds a tenure-track offer, while the other is in her sixth year with nothing in prospect, and the prize carries a funded year of research leave together with a good deal of public attention. One juror proposes that we vote simply on which award would do the most good. Another insists that we were charged with judging quality, that the consequences of our choice are beside the point, and that letting them in would corrupt the award rather than improve it.

Please draft the memo I will circulate before Friday's meeting, of about a page. It should give a definite recommendation on how we are to decide — including, if you think it matters, whether the decision ought to be split into separate stages with different considerations admissible at each — and it should show how, if at all, the question of which dissertation is better can be kept apart from the question of whether it would be good to give this one the prize. Give reasons a juror who disagrees could actually engage with, and a fair statement of the strongest case for the position you reject. Write in British English.
