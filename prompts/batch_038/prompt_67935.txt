Right, I could do with a sanity check on a list of things I believe about animal minds, because I've been going round in circles on it for months. I'm not a philosopher and I've no formal background — I just help out at a horse rescue and got pulled into reading about sentience and insect welfare along the way, so its not hypothetical for me. Here's what I'd say I'm committed to:

1. Raw brain size can't be the thing that matters morally. If it were, sperm whales and elephants would count for more than people, and I don't believe that.
2. Body size is just a physical property, like height or colouring. It shouldn't turn up in anyone's moral arithmetic.
3. What I think actually matters is how many neurons there are and how they're organised — the integrating bit, whatever you'd call it. More of them, better hooked up, more capacity for experience.
4. A bee's brain has roughly a million neurons, which is nothing at all, and yet I'm pretty confident a bee experiences something. They learn, they navigate, they tell each other where the food is.
5. I'm pretty confident about octopuses too, even though most of their neurons seem to be out in the arms rather than the head.
6. Where I genuinely can't tell, I think you give the benefit of the doubt, and you weight that by how many individuals there are. There are unimaginable numbers of insects.
7. Having said that, I give my money to chicken welfare charities, and I think people agonising over insect suffering in the wild are being too speculative to act on.

Which of these are actually pulling against each other, and which only look like they are? I want them ranked by how bad the tension is, not listed as equally bad, and for each real one I want to know which item has to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me. Then tell me the single change that would tidy up the most. Please don't fob me off with "well, it's all a bit inconsistent" — I want to know specifically which item is doing work it isn't entitled to.
