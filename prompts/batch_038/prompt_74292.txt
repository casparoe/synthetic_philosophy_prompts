Each has the same size, temperature, color and composition, and every relational property the one has, the other has too, since the only thing either is related to is a sphere exactly like itself. So they're indiscernible and yet obviously two, and the principle is false. What Black's case really shows is that things can differ in number alone, solo numero, which is precisely what Leibniz's principle rules out. Modern physics sides with Black regardless, because quantum mechanics says elementary particles are literally indistinguishable — no possible measurement tells electron 1 from electron 2 — so even the empirically informed version of Leibniz's principle fails."
