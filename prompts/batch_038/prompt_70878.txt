I've been talked into running a two-hour session for our university's EA/rationality society — about 25 people, mostly STEM undergrads plus a couple of grad students, all of whom have read a blog post about the Kelly criterion and can recite f* = edge/odds at you. Advertised topic: geometric vs arithmetic, or why expected-value maximization gets weird when the bets are sequential instead of parallel. The problem is that half the room will hear this as a gotcha against EV and the other half will hear it as log utility with extra steps, and both reactions are wrong in ways I don't know how to preempt.

So: don't explain the material to me, plan the two hours.

What I need is the dependency structure. Which pieces genuinely have to be in place before the next one makes sense — e.g. does the time-average vs ensemble-average distinction have to land before they ever see a single path go broke, or is the ruin path the thing that makes the distinction meaningful and I have it backwards? Give me the order and say what each step is load-bearing for.

Second, I built a device and I can't tell whether it's helping. Two sheets projected side by side: one is 100 parallel people each taking the same bet 40 times, the other is one person taking it 40 times, same random seed. Tell me whether to keep it, what it teaches that writing the formula on the board doesnt, and what it actively pushes them toward — my worry is it makes the whole thing look like a sampling quirk that more rounds would fix, and it hands the sharpest person in the room the objection "but you can pool across people, so the ensemble is the one that matters, and that's exactly what a foundation or a movement does."

Third, the carrying example may be miscalibrated and I want your opinion. Right now: each round you stake some fraction of your bankroll, even odds, a win pays you 50% of what you staked and a loss costs you 40% of it. All-in, that's +5% expected per round but about −5% per round geometrically, and full Kelly is a quarter of the bankroll. Trouble is a quarter-bankroll Kelly grows so slowly that over 40 rounds the noise eats the drift and the demo lands as a shrug. Should I reparametrize it, and to what? I want it visible inside the session but not so lopsided that they smell a rigged setup — and I'd rather it stayed a bet someone in that room would recognizably take.

Fourth, the misconceptions in the order they'll actually surface, and the exact point in the sequence where I head each one off. The ones I'm braced for: "Kelly is just log utility so it's a taste preference, not a fact about the world"; "ergodicity is a fancy word for compounding"; "the mean path catches up eventually, you just need more rounds"; and the one I dread, "so this is an argument against any big irreversible bet, which would rule out starting things, going into debt to study, or donating aggressively." For each, tell me whether it's a thing to block early or a thing to let them say out loud and then turn.

Fifth, one question I can ask at the end — not a slogan check, something where a person who has the structure answers differently from a person who only has the vibes.

And tell me what to cut. I'm assuming fractional Kelly for estimation error, the continuous-time treatment, and anything resembling the ergodic theorem proper are all off the table at this level, but say what you'd drop and what I should say out loud about having dropped it, because this crowd will notice the gap and fill it themselves.
