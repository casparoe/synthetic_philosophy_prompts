Our Sanskrit reading group has been stuck on the pramāṇa-count dispute for months and I've come to think we're not actually having one argument. Quick sketch of the positions as I understand them: the Buddhist side (Dignāga, then Dharmakīrti) says there are exactly two pramāṇas, perception and inference, and that the reason is metaphysical — there are only two kinds of knowable object, the particular and the general — so testimony about a pot, or about impermanence, just is an inference from an utterance to a fact, and comparison and postulation are inferential too. The Nyāya side says four: perception, inference, comparison, and testimony, where testimony is the utterance of a trustworthy person and is irreducible, because the cognition it produces has its own distinctive causal shape. And then the Mīmāṃsakas push the number back up again — Kumārila to six with postulation and non-apprehension, Prābhākara folding absence back into perception.

My hypothesis, which nobody else in the group will accept: "how many pramāṇas are there" isn't one question, because the two sides are individuating along incompatible axes. The Buddhists count by content — what kind of object the cognition is about — so two objects gives you two pramāṇas. The Naiyāyikas count by causal route — how the cognition got produced — so every genuinely distinct generative process earns its own slot. Each axis reductios the other: count by route and you should get indefinitely many pramāṇas (seeing a pot vs. seeing a cloth, my mother's word vs. the Veda's), count by content and Nyāya's four collapses. If that's right the debate is partly verbal, but I don't think that's the whole story, because the count has downstream work to do — wether the Veda can be authoritative without an author, whether the Buddha's word is a pramāṇa at all, whether absences are knowable.

So: diagnose it. Is the axis mismatch the real engine of the impasse, or is there a substantive disagreement hiding underneath that survives once you separate the two questions? Where would you put comparison and postulation in your diagnosis? And given what you land on, what would count as progress — a distinction, a test case that breaks the tie, or a reason to think no tie-breaking case is even available? Be as committed as you can be about which of these it is, but tell me explicitly what would change your mind, and where you think your own reasoning is most likely to break.
