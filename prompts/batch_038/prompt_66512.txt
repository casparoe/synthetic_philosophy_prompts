Here is the paragraph as submitted:

"Moral testimony is the position that you cannot acquire moral knowledge on someone else's say-so. Suppose a moral philosopher tells you that what you are about to do is wrong, and suppose she is right; on this view you still do not know that it is wrong, because in morality there is nothing for testimony to transmit. Unlike the weather or the past, moral truths are not the sort of thing anyone is in a position to detect, and moral philosophers are in any case no more reliable than the rest of us about what is right. There is also a second problem: a belief you hold because an authority asserted it cannot be the basis of a virtue, since virtue consists in acting for the right reasons — hence the word we have for people who take their ethics wholesale from someone else. The pessimists' conclusion is that the only way to improve morally is to think harder for yourself, which is why philosophers so often insist that reading novels and arguing with friends beats reading moral theory."

It also means that moral philosophers who appear before legislative committees are, strictly speaking, committing a fraud, since on their own account they have no knowledge to hand over."
