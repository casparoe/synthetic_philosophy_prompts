I do behavioral evals at a lab, and there's a senior person who ends a certain kind of discussion by saying these systems "only have as-if beliefs." Last week it killed a proposal to describe a model as wanting to avoid being shut down in an incident report — his line was that wanting is just a manner of speaking, so the report shouldn't use the word. What bugs me is that for that same model, the belief-ascription predicted held-out behavior across three unrelated task families better then anything we could say at the level of architecture or training data, so "manner of speaking" started to feel like it was doing a lot of unearned work.

Can you lay out the different precise claims that sentence could be making, from the platitudinous to the ones with real metaphysical teeth? For each reading, I want two things: whether it's compatible with that predictive success, and whether it actually licenses shutting down the report question. A hint at one axis I keep tripping on: some readings seem to be claims about the system, others about what we're permitted to assert, and I suspect the argument only goes through if you slide between them.

Give me the menu with verdicts, not a defense of your favorite reading. And please mark which of these are settled and which are live disagreements among people who actually work on this — don't hand me one camp's view as the consensus.
