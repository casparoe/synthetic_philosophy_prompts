I am building a theory of blameworthiness and need its responsibility-relevant core to be an account of the control an agent must have over an action. Before I take any candidate account seriously, I want the specification it would have to meet. The purpose is what fixes the standard: the account must be statable to a philosophically trained non-specialist; it must be applicable to coercion, addiction, direct manipulation, negligence, and the conduct of children and the cognitively impaired; and it must yield a determinate verdict on whether causal determinism threatens desert. Write the desiderata for an account of the control condition — the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the results it must be consistent with.

For each item, give: the requirement itself; the case or consideration motivating it; what an account that violates it is thereby committed to saying; and its status — non-negotiable, a preference of one camp passing itself off as a requirement, or something to be struck from the list.

The territory the list must cover includes the following, though my enumeration is not the answer and you should add what I have omitted:

1. The verdict on the standard counterexample structure: an agent who acts for his own reasons while an intervener stands ready to guarantee the outcome and never acts, so that he is responsible despite being unable to do otherwise.
2. An irrelevance constraint: whatever the intervener's presence changes about the assessment must be traceable to something in the agent's own course of events, since the two scenarios are otherwise identical up to the moment of action.
3. The residual-alternatives objection — that such cases leave trivial leftovers, a moment of hesitation or an earlier opportunity — together with the question whether a robustness condition on alternatives can be imposed at all without begging the question against the incompatibilist.
4. The dilemma facing any such case: if the intervener's trigger is determined by the agent's prior state, the case cannot be pressed on someone who denies that determinism leaves alternative possibilities; if it is not determined, the agent apparently retains one.
5. The distinction between guiding one's own behaviour and having options open, and an explicit statement of which the account requires.
6. Compatibility with the exculpating force of coercion, compulsion, and ignorance, which no account may explain away.
7. Cases of manipulation constructed to be neutral between determinism and indeterminism.
8. Whether control must come in degrees to match degrees of blameworthiness.

Adjudicate, in addition, three requirements I am unsure whether to admit, saying for each whether it is genuine or a prejudice: assessor-accessibility, the demand that the condition be one whose satisfaction can be determined on the evidence available to people who actually blame, since blaming is a practice and not a metaphysical audit; counterfactual austerity, the demand that responsibility not turn on facts about what hidden interveners would have done; and explanatory depth, the demand that the account say why the kind of control it identifies is the kind that bears on desert rather than merely relabelling the intuition.

Then stress the list against this case, which I do not believe is in the literature. The mechanism monitors the agent's own deliberation and fires only if that deliberation is about to conclude against what the intervener wants; when it fires it does not cause the action but eliminates every alternative course at that moment, so the agent's reasoning and his act are internally exactly what they would have been, while absent the mechanism he would have concluded otherwise. Say which requirements this strains, and whether accommodating it forces a historical condition on how the actual sequence arose.

Finally, three short conclusions: which single requirement eliminates the most candidates; whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable, and if so which member must be abandoned and why — I expect the deliverable to be part impossibility result rather than a shopping list, and that is what I most want to see; and, in no more than a paragraph each, how a leeway theory retaining a weakened alternative-possibilities principle, a source or guidance-control compatibilism, and the view that responsibility survives determinism whatever else turns on it fare against the list. No scorecard, and no references.
