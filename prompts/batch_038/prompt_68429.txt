I cover science and research policy for a general-interest outlet, and I'm drafting a piece about what a reporter is entitled to conclude from one unverifiable tip of a particular logical shape. The shape is van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin case, transposed onto my beat. There are three live possibilities for how a contested drug application resolves: the sponsor withdraws (W), or files and gets a fast review (F), or files and lands in the standard queue (S). Before the tip I had them at roughly a third each. An anonymous source I regard as accurate on facts but opaque on motives tells me: if they file, it's a fast review with probability three-quarters. So the tip fixes a conditional, P(F | not-W) = 3/4, and leaves P(W) formally unconstrained.

Here is the package of commitments the piece will argue for. I'd like an audit of whether it hangs together before I send it to a philosopher friend who will otherwise take it apart in public.

(a) Learning is not always learning that some proposition is true. When evidence shifts my degrees of belief across a partition without settling any cell, I use Jeffrey's rule, and I treat ordinary conditionalization as the limiting case in which one cell goes to 1.

(b) A tip that fixes a conditional is handled by the same variational principle: among all posteriors satisfying the constraint, take the one closest to my prior in relative entropy. Infomin is not a rival to Jeffrey's rule but its generalization, which is why I can present (a) and (b) as one doctrine rather than two. As a consistency check, when the constraint tightens to P(F | not-W) = 1 the minimizer collapses onto plain conditionalization on the disjunction W-or-F.

(c) I take the triviality results seriously: no proposition has as its probability the corresponding conditional probability. So I do not treat my source's sentence as making me confident in some conditional entity inside my algebra; the conditional enters only as a constraint on the posterior.

(d) Running the minimization on my own numbers moves P(W) from about a third to about 0.36 — a nudge. I think that nudge is too small, and that the familiar objection, namely that infomin behaves like regression to the mean and systematically under-reacts to the strength of what it is told, has real force.

(e) Still, I don't want to trade (b) for some other measure of closeness, because the unity with (a) is what makes my numbers look principled rather than tuned.

(f) Whatever rule I use must be order-independent. If two tips of this kind arrive in the same week, my final probabilities should not depend on which I processed first. I regard that as the difference between a rule and a recipe, and it is what makes a number safe to print.

(g) To convert a tip into a constraint at all, I have to suppose that my source's choice of what to mention carries no information — that the framing isn't itself evidence. Yet in my reporting practice I hold close to the opposite about anonymous sources: they select their framing strategically, the first question I ask is why they are telling me this and why now, and I would never let a desk treat a tip as a neutral sample of what the source believes.

(h) I want one determinate posterior, not a family of them. Imprecise probabilities may be more honest, but a newsroom needs a single figure.

Please treat this as a package rather than lecturing me on any one of the topics. Specifically: which pairs are in genuine tension, ordered by severity, and for each, what would have to be given up and what the loss costs the argument? Which pairs only look like they conflict — I suspect at least one does, and I would rather be told which and why than have it flagged indiscriminately alongside the rest? And if I were allowed a single revision, which one buys the most consistency? I'm not after references or a reading list; I want the reasoning, and where a claim turns on arithmetic — whether the minimizer in (d) really is that timid, or whether (f) really does fail for constraint-based updates — I want the calculation shown. If the package survives only by dropping something I have listed as non-negotiable, say so plainly, and say which one.
