I'm on my city's housing authority board and Thursday we vote on renewing a five-year master lease with a private landlord covering about 200 scattered-site units. The lease lets him end any individual tenancy on 30 days notice with no cause given; in nine years he's done it twice, both times to tenants who'd filed complaints about repairs, though he swears one of those was a genuine need to house a relative. Legal staff say its fine because the clause isn't being enforced against anyone day to day, and our own tenant survey came back 78% satisfied and dont want it changed. The only competing bid is from a nonprofit whose leases are fixed-term and cause-only, but it runs about 9% more per unit and they can only supply 120 units — so 80 households get nothing this round.

Draft the short memo I'll circulate before the vote. It needs a clear recommendation (renew, don't, or renew with conditions spelled out concretely), reasoning that a board member who disagrees can actually argue back against, and a real treatment of the strongest case for the staff position — that a discretionary power nobody ever suffers under isn't a harm worth leaving 80 families unhoused over. Please don't name-check political theorists or lean on labels; what I want argued out on the merits is whether the bare existence of the landlord's power to evict at will is itself a cost to the tenants even when he mostly doesn't use it, whether the two past uses change the analysis, and whether the tenants' reported satisfaction settles the question or might not be the right thing to go on here.
