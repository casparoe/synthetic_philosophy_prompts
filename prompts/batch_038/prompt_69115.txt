Write a satirical legal-administrative document in American English, using the spelling, idiom, and formatting conventions of United States federal practice.

**The setup.** A federal district court has certified a plaintiff class arising from the classic aggregation dilemma: a choice between torturing one person for fifty years and giving each of an astronomically large number of people a momentary dust speck in the eye. The choice was made. The speck recipients now seek relief, and the court has appointed a settlement administrator. Your document is the administrator's *Notice of Proposed Class Action Settlement and Instructions for Filing a Claim*, as it would appear in the packet distributed — or, as the notice will have to explain, not distributed — to class members. Take the class size to be 3^^^3, expressed in up-arrow notation, and treat that numeral as a fact of the case.

**Form and length.** Roughly 800–1,100 words. It should be laid out like a genuine notice: a fictitious caption with court, parties, and docket number; numbered sections under the standard headings (for example, a section addressed to anyone who received a dust speck, a section on what the settlement provides, a section on the final fairness hearing, a section on excluding yourself); a claims procedure; a schedule of compensation; and a short list of frequently asked questions, no more than five. Invent every name, date, statute, and citation; do not quote or cite any real rule of procedure, case, statute, or philosopher, and do not attribute the dilemma to any real author.

**Where the comedy must come from.** Deadpan throughout, and the humor should come from getting both the philosophy and the procedure right — an absurdity that is simply a real feature of the dispute run through the machinery of class administration. In particular:

1. The notice must try to state the class size in plain English for lay readers, and fail conspicuously while remaining formally correct.
2. Because individual notice is impossible, the administrator has adopted a substitute method that is itself a small harm — for instance, funding the notice program by administering one additional dust speck to each class member — and must justify it as net-beneficial under the settlement's own arithmetic.
3. A de minimis threshold and a schedule of damages require a line to be drawn between one speck and many. Draw the line. Do not let the notice appear to notice that it is drawing one, or that the drawing of it is the whole problem.
4. The per-capita distribution is so small that it must be rounded; state the rounding rule, and state what the rounding does to the total.
5. Address the single torture victim: whether he is a class member, a subclass with interests adverse to the class, or excluded outright; the adequacy-of-representation question that creates; and the difficulty of paying his counsel and class counsel out of the same fund.
6. Summarize at least one objector's filing — invented name, invented ground — holding that no quantity of specks can ever be traded off against the torture, and give the administrator's reply, which should be procedurally impeccable and philosophically empty.
7. Provide for the unclaimed residue by cy pres, the residue being very nearly the entire fund, and name the recipient.

**Discipline of voice.** The notice is written by someone who sincerely believes the arithmetic settles the question and by whom the document is meant to be read by people who do not. It must never break character, never acknowledge a reader outside the fiction, and never wink. The satire's target is a practice — the administrative habit of resolving incommensurable magnitudes by means of thresholds, forms, rounding rules, and boilerplate — not any named person or living author. It should be fair enough that a reader who holds the aggregationist position recognizes the reasoning as their own, and a reader who rejects it recognizes their own objections filed, docketed, and disposed of. Finally, keep it legible to a non-specialist: someone who has never encountered this literature should still enjoy it as a piece of bureaucratic satire, without needing the philosophy explained to them anywhere in the text.
