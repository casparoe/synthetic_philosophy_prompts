I run a small philosophy-of-biology reading group (mostly grad students, plus two actual biologists who get visibly bored during methodology talk) and the next session is on Popper's awkward relationship with Darwinism: the fact that for years he told people natural selection wasn't a testable empirical theory but something more like a metaphysical research programme, and then, in the first Darwin lecture at Cambridge in the late seventies, publicly withdrew that and said evolution was testable after all. I want a sorting instrument for the session, not a lecture. Specifically: a decision tree that takes someone who starts out with only the vague feeling that "survival of the fittest" sounds circular, and walks them question by question to a named position on what the scientific status of selection theory really is.

Build the space first, then hang the names on it.

1. Five or six branch questions, each yes/no (three-way only where you genuinely need it). Every question must be phrased in terms of what someone believes about evidence and explanation, not in terms of labels: no question may contain words like falsifiable, tautology, propensity, instrumentalism, research programme, or any position-name. The person answering shouldn't be able to tell which leaf they're steering toward, and no question should smuggle in its own answer.

2. Then eight to ten leaves. I want at least these somewhere in the tree: Popper's earlier verdict; Popper's post-retraction verdict; the view that reinterpreting fitness as a disposition rescues testability; the view that it's a definition and definitions can still do serious scientific work; the view that evolutionary biology is a historical science that was never in the market for laws; the split view on which population genetics has laws but selection theory proper is a lawless sketch; an instrumentalist or many-models treatment; and a deflationary leaf holding that the whole dispute is verbal, about how you carve up "the theory." Add anything else you think belongs.

3. For each leaf: one sentence on its most awkward commitment, plus one concrete piece of biology — industrial melanism, drought-driven beak shifts in finches, pesticide or antibiotic resistance, the long-term E. coli experiment, sickle cell and malaria, whatever you like — that the position would accept as a genuine test of selection. If a leaf cant name one, say so and say whether that's a bug or a feature of the view.

Then the honest parts. Flag any pair of leaves your questions can't actually separate, and say what further question would separate them if there is one. Most paths through a five- or six-question tree are empty; pick three or four empty cells that are interesting rather than merely vacant, and diagnose each — is nobody there because the combination is incoherent, because it's coherent but unattractive, or because it's genuinely unexplored and somebody should be sitting in it? And tell me which single branch question is load-bearing: if I deleted it, which leaves would collapse into each other.

One thing I definately care about: Popper's change of mind should be visible as a path in the tree, meaning two leaves reached by the same answers to almost everything except one question, and I want you to say whether that question marks a substantive philosophical difference or just a disagreement about how broadly you construe the target. If you think the retraction overshot — that he conceded more than he had to — make that its own leaf rather than a footnote.

Plain indented text is fine, questions numbered. No bibliography, no citations, no reading list, and please don't invent page numbers; standard position-names as leaf labels are exactly what I'm after.
