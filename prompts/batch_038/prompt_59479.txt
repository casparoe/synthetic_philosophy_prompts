Write me a dialogue between Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett arguing about whether dreams are experiences. Don't let them trade the stock examples though — hand them this case to fight over instead: a subject in a sleep lab gets woken partway through a REM period and gives a scrappy report ("a kitchen, somebody counting"). They let her drop back off and wake her again from the same stage twenty minutes later, and now she reports a continuation: the counting got to nine and then the kitchen flooded. She's adamant it was one dream that just carried on.

Malcolm's line is that a dream is the impression on waking, so strictly nothing was going on in her head during the gap; Dennett's is that the narrative gets composed and inserted into memory at the moment of waking, which lands in a similar place but for completely different reasons. Have each of them press the other on what those twenty minutes are supposed to show — Malcolm on whether "the same dream continuing" even has a meaning, Dennett on whether his account can generate a continuation report out of a memory trace of the first report instead. Both should be at the top of their game, no strawmen and no caving early. End with the two of them trying to pin down what they actually still disagree about, and whether anything could settle it. No citations, just argument.
