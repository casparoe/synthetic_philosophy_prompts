So, no philosophy training here, just a year or so of reading two old Chinese books on my own — the one who thinks we show up already furnished with the beginnings of decency, little sprouts of compassion and shame that only need tending, and the one who thinks human nature is crooked timber and every scrap of goodness in the world is steam and pressure applied from outside. I've come down hard on the second side, and not on the mild reading of it. My actual view is the strong one: left to itself, spontaneous human impulse isn't just untrained or blank, it's pointed *against* morality, so all the be-yourself / trust-your-gut / it's-society-that-ruined-you talk is an invitation to get worse, and the only reason any of us is decent is the accumulated artificial scaffolding of rules, shame, habits and institutions that dead people built and we're too squeamish to keep repairing. My day job feeds this — I've been a corrections officer going on nineteen years, and what I see every shift is people with the scaffolding stripped off. I know that might not be evidence at all, I might just be stationed at the wrong end of the distribution, which is part of why I'm asking.

What would count against me? I want the map of where this thing is exposed, not a pep talk either way. Specifically: rank the defeaters, fatal ones first and merely awkward ones last. Which of them would somebody who actually holds my view concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they wave off — I can feel myself already reaching for the wave-off hand, so show me where it's justified and where it isn't. Then a few things I keep circling and can't resolve on my own:

- If "nature" just means whatever isn't taught, is my claim even risking anything? It starts to look like it can't lose, since any decent behavior gets filed under "see, that's the scaffolding working" and any rotten behavior under "see, that's the nature." Tell me straight whether that's fatal, whether there's a version of the claim that does put something at risk, and whether being that insulated is actually a problem for a view like this or just normal for philosophy.
- Does the strong version collapse into the sprouts version without admitting it? My worry: the steam-bending press has to grip something. Ritual and punishment only work on material that cares about approval, flinches at shame, and can be recruited — and if that's true, isn't the responsive-ness the whole show, with my "crooked" just meaning "not yet built"? Say whether that's a real collapse or a cheap trick.
- The regress thing. If everybody's nature is crooked, where did the first straighteners come from? Somebody had to invent the rites without being raised in them. I know this gets raised against my side a lot — is it a knockout, or does the view have a respectable answer?
- Is the bigger threat to me actually the third option I keep meeting in the same book, the one that says nature is neither good nor bad, more like water that runs whichever way you cut the ditch? That would let people be fully shaped by culture while denying my "pointed against" claim. Would I rather fight the sprouts guy or that guy, and why?
- What observation would actually discriminate "untrained" from "actively hostile"? I suspect those two get run together constantly and my whole position rests on keeping them seperate, so I want the concrete stuff: what would have to be true about babies and toddlers, about what cruelty costs people versus what kindness costs them, about which impulses survive institutional collapse and which get built from scratch, for me to be right rather than just cynical. And what does my view predict that the sprouts view doesn't — plus anywhere it's arguably already been caught out or confirmed.
- Give me two or three cases I can run in my head, the kind where the two views tell you to expect different things, including one from inside a prison or a place where authority has stopped working, since that's my home turf and I want to know if I've been misreading what I see every day.
