I've been slowly working my way through Tolstoy's book about what art is — the one where he dumps on Shakespeare and Beethoven and Dante and then says most of his own novels don't qualify either. Reading it I kept thinking about AI images. Not the impressive stuff, the ordinary stuff: you type a sentence into your phone and a minute later you have a watercolour of your dog in a spacesuit, or a "painting" for your mum's birthday, or a cover for a story you'll never finish.

Everyone I've said this to gives the same answer — he'd call it counterfeit art, full stop, because theres no feeling behind it and nothing gets passed from one person to another. Fine, I get that, and I mostly agree. But two things are sticking in my craw:

1. He was furious that art had been taken over by professionals and critics and turned into a club for rich educated people, and he wanted it to be something anybody, peasants included, could make and understand. A phone that makes pictures is a lot closer to that than anything he ever saw. So would he be for this?
2. He complained that painters had started doing what cameras do. So he isn't exactly a purist about machines.

Which is why I don't buy "he'd hate it" as a complete answer. My guess is the interesting version is that he'd love the fact that anyone can make pictures and despise every single one of them — or maybe worse, that he'd say a tool that gives you the surface of a feeling without anyone feeling it is the counterfeit in its final form, only now it's free and infinite.
