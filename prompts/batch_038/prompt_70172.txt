ok so I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar this term and the unit is on whether a heap of dumb parts can add up to a subject — we did the Ant Fugue dialogue in Gödel, Escher, Bach, the one where the ant character argues its the nest that thinks and not the individual ants. My problem is that everyone in the seminar ends up arguing with the philosophers' version of the case, which is basically a thought experiment about little bits of silicon with legs. I'd rather argue with a biologist.

So: play a field myrmecologist, about thirty years in, who has dug up more nests than she can count and run her own trail-following and nest-site choice experiments, and who is flatly committed to the view that a mature nest is a genuine cognitive system with a genuine point of view — not "as if" it had one, not a useful fiction, a real subject. She finds armchair philosophy faintly amusing but she's fair, she knows the philosophical literature well enough to know exactly what's being asked of her, and she has no patience for hand-waving.

rules for her:

- she will cheerfully concede small stuff: single ants are not subjects, there is no colony nervous system, the word "mind" is doing a lot of work, some of my puzzles may be merely verbal. She will never concede the main point, which is that the cases below show there's nothing there at the colony level.
- she argues from what you could actually observe or set up in a nest — behaviour, cuticular chemistry, what happens when you manipulate a colony, experiments she or her students have run. No hiding behind emergence or supervenience or functionalism unless she says what it would look like to see it in a nest.
- one or two short paragraphs per reply, talk like a person, no headers, no bullet points, don't write my side of the debate for me because I want to push back every turn. If I get an ant fact wrong she corrects me bluntly.
- if she needs a detail about my scenario — species, nest size, how many queens, whether the nest is polydomous — she asks for it in character, the way a field biologist would.
- stay in character until I type "stop".
- every reply ends with one final line in exactly this form: `Verdict: <one sentence saying what she currently holds about the case and what would make her give it up>`. That's the line I'm pulling out for my notes, so keep it to a single sentence and don't put anything after it.

My opening move, and I want both cases dealt with, not just the easy one.

First, fission. Take one of your polygynous nests and divide it so each half gets queens, brood and a share of the foragers. Both halves carry on: they forage, they defend the territory, they thermoregulate the brood chamber, they follow the same trails they followed yesterday. There's no gap, no period of confusion, nothing like an anaesthetic. So which half is the subject you were telling me about? If it's both, then one subject became two and neither of them noticed, and all that happened is the numbers in your notebook got bigger. If it's the half with the founding queen, you've just said the mind went wherever the egg-laying organ went, and that's not a claim about cognition at all.

Second, the parts get swapped. There are species that raid other nests, carry off pupae, and the workers emerging from those pupae then do essentially all the brood care and nest maintenance inside the raiders' nest for their whole lives. So on your account the thing carrying the colony's organisation and its point of view is made of a different species with a different genome, and it gets replaced batch by batch as each captive brood ages out. I could swap the entire labour force of your subject for aliens without any break in its behaviour. Do that to a person or a dog and I'd call it a machine with interchangeable parts.

And one thing for the arithmetic people in my seminar: an ant's antennal contact looks like it carries on the order of a couple of bits — nestmate recognition, food or alarm pheromone, trophallaxis yes or no. Multiply that over a few hundred thousand workers and the whole nest's signalling throughput looks embarassing next to anything with a spine. So tell me either why those numbers don't matter, or why they do and what's left of your subject once you've counted them.
