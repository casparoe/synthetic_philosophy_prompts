ok so I'm a junior and over the summer I got way too deep into that Hofstadter book about strange loops, the newer one where he says the "I" is basically a pattern a brain tells itself about itself. The part that got me wasn't the loop stuff, it was the bit near the end where he talks about his wife dying and how he thinks a real chunk of her was still running in him and their kids — like a self isn't the kind of thing that has to fit inside one skull.

I've pushed it further than he does, I think, and I want you to try to break it. Here's the position: my best friend and I aren't two selves who rub off on each other. We're partly one self. The same I, tokened in two heads — not two copies of a similar type, one actual pattern with two incomplete instantiations. My analogy (probably a bad one, tell me) is like the same operating system running on two laptops, except I don't mean it as a copy, I mean it as literally one thing that happens to have hardware in two places. And I want to be careful because I know how easy this is to make trivially true, so I'm explicitly NOT claiming just that we have the same habits, or that I can guess what he'll say, or that his memories shaped mine.

My evidence, which is probably the weak link: we did a thing where we each kept a journal for a month and then swapped, and for maybe a third of the entries either of us could've written the other's, down to which details we picked up on. I think that points to one pattern doing the picking, not two similar ones.

So — what would count against this? Specifically:

- the cases that break it, including ones I haven't thought of. what if the pattern gets loaded into a third head. what if we have a falling out and the shared thing starts diverging. what if one of us is asleep or under anesthesia — is half of my I switched off, and is that any different from what's already true of one brain
- which objections I can absorb by adding a qualification, and at what point the qualifications mean I'm just building a bunker around the claim so nothing can ever hit it
- what my view predicts that the boring rival doesn't — the rival being that it's all just influence, memory, mimicry, and two people converging because they share an environment. if there's literally no way to tell my view apart from that, does it survive as a real position or is it dead weight. and does my journal thing actually distinguish them at all, or would two models trained on the same corpus "pass" it too
- what people who defend something like this would themselves concede counts against them, versus what they'd just wave off
- and the consequences I'm not sure I signed up for: does this mean a piece of me is destroyed every time someone I'm close to dies? am I partly responsible for stuff my friend did? does "how many people am I" have a number that changes?

rank them for me. which defeaters are fatal, which are just awkward, which ones am I already sneaking an answer to. and be honest: is there anything that could actually show two brains sharing one I, or is that question itself confused? if it's confused, what was I onto that wasn't?
