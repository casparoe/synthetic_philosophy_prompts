ok so my school has an ethics discussion group and next session is on moral desert — like what actually makes it fair for someone to suffer or get rewarded. i got asked to write up my real views first so people can attack them, and i want a sanity check before i do bc i have a suspision that some of these don't fit together and i cant tell which.

here's roughly where i've landed:

1. i believe in karma, but as a natural law kind of thing, not a punishment system run by a god or a judge. nobody is out there handing down sentences. actions just have moral consequences the way stepping off a wall has physical ones.
2. that's the main reason i find it convincing — it explains why decent people get cancer at 30 and horrible people die rich in bed. suffering isn't random, it's tracked to what you did.
3. for human punishment (prisons and stuff) i only think it's justified by what it does going forward — reforming the person, or keeping everyone else safe. payback on its own makes no sense to me, adding pain just because pain happened doesn't produce anything.
4. i also don't believe in a permanent self. whatever gets reborn isn't literally identical to the one who did the deeds, its more like one candle lighting another, a continuation of a stream.
5. but i think that's still enough for fairness. someone being born into a genuinely hard life because of what happened before is just — they earned it in the only sense that matters.
6. desert has to attach to the person who acted. if you make someone pay who isn't the one who did it, that's punishing an innocent, and no amount of cosmic bookkeeping turns that into justice.
7. i still think we should work hard to relieve other people's suffering even when its supposedly their karma, and i don't see that as interfering with any of the above.
