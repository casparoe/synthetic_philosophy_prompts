I'm a second-year doing politics and philosophy and my module this term is formal political theory — we hit Gibbard–Satterthwaite a couple of weeks back (the one that says any deterministic, non-dictatorial voting rule over three or more options, with ranked ballots, is manipulable by somebody in some preference profile). Since the lecture I cant stop noticing something about how elections are actually run here, and I want it properly taken apart rather than being told the theorem again.

Three things, all from the same electoral system:

1. Official voter guidance — the Electoral Commission's how-to-vote material, the notices in the polling station — frames the ballot as a *report*. Rank the candidates in your order of preference; vote for the person you actually want. And separately, it's a criminal offence to pay someone for their vote, or to pressure, threaten or otherwise lean on them to vote a particular way. The stated rationale is always a version of "the result should reflect what voters really think."

2. In the very same election, newspapers, charities and single-issue campaign groups publish tactical voting advice — in your seat, vote for X even though you'd rather have Y, because that's how you stop Z. This is treated as good citizenship. Nobody in the official guidance warns against it, tactical voting websites get linked approvingly, and people who follow the advice are still counted as having voted properly.

3. Meanwhile inside parliament and in committee, the order in which amendments and motions are put to the vote is tightly regulated by standing orders, precisely because everyone accepts that the order changes who wins. So agenda manipulation gets procedural armour, but the ballot itself gets nothing — just a leaflet asking you to be honest.

So: what theory of the ballot does this whole package presuppose? Not "GS shows sincerity can't be incentivised" — I got that in the lecture. I want to know what the *practice* is committed to. Specifically:

- Is the leaflet's honesty framing doing real work, or is it folk decoration on top of a system that doesn't actually care about sincerity?
- Here's my own hypothesis, which I'd like you to try to break rather than flatter: maybe the criminal law isn't tracking the *direction* of your vote at all, it's tracking *alienation* — the vote being sold or surrendered for someone else's private benefit. Bribery and undue influence are about the vote ceasing to be yours; tactical voting leaves the vote entirely yours, just aimed instrumentally. If that's right, the package is consistent and the "vote honestly" language is a red herring. Is that reading available, does it survive contact with how the offences are actually described and prosecuted, and what does it imply about what a ballot *is* on this view?
- Which rival accounts of the ballot does the practice quietly rule out — e.g. a ballot as a causal intervention on expected outcomes, or as a piece of expressive signalling with no reference to a collective preference at all?
- Is there anything the practice *strictly* presupposes, as opposed to just sitting comfortably with? I suspect a lot of it is underdetermined and I want to be told where.
