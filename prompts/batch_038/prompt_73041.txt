I am assembling a diagnostic instrument for a graduate methods seminar whose members — mostly sociologists, two historians, an economist, and a public-health researcher — are all happy to say that race, gender, and money are socially constructed, and who, I am convinced, do not mean the same thing by it. What I need from you is a decision tree that sorts them by what they actually believe, not by what they call themselves. Please observe the following specifications.

**Form.** Ten to twelve branch questions, each phrased as a concrete scenario or as a claim about a scenario to be accepted or rejected. A participant must be able to answer every node from their own intuitions about the case, without knowing which position the answer routes them toward; consequently, no node may contain a term of art — no constitution, conferral, status function, looping, performativity, or amelioration in the wording of any question. Nodes must be answerable in the affirmative, the negative, or with a principled split answer (for example: yes for the concept, no for the individuals).

**The axes the tree must be built from.** Arrange these in whatever order makes the branching most economical, but all of them must be doing structural work:

1. What exactly is supposed to be constructed — the concept by which we classify, the kind itself, or the individual instances falling under it — and whether a participant can answer differently for each of the three without inconsistency.
2. Whether the social element operates causally (society sorts, channels, and shapes people until they come to fit the kind) or in the stronger sense in which the kind's continued existence depends on people's taking it to exist.
3. Whether the kind's boundaries require ongoing recognition and institutional backing, or would survive their withdrawal.
4. Whether construction is treated as entailing arbitrariness, recency, or changeability at will — and which of those three a participant actually endorses.
5. Whether the classified population changes in response to being classified.
6. Whether the kind is nonetheless real, causally efficacious, and apt for explanation, or is instead a candidate for elimination or replacement.

**Required leaves.** Each leaf carries the standard name of the position (or a coinage if there is none), one sentence stating what it takes to be doing the explanatory work, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. The leaves must include at least: eliminativism and conservationism about race, alongside realism about race as a social kind; a role-based account of race in which the role is regulated by prevailing cultural representations; gender as a position within a material structure of relations of subordination and privilege; gender as produced through repeated citational practice rather than expressed by an antecedent identity; gender as norm-guided self-identification; a general conferralist account of social kinds; money as a status collectively accepted and thereby enabled to perform a function it could not otherwise perform; money as a state-defined unit of account sustained by the demand to settle tax liabilities; money as a commodity elevated to medium of exchange by its comparative saleability; and money as credit and debt relations predating any medium.

**Vignettes.** These four must be threaded through the whole tree, not appended to it:

(a) A child raised in a community with no race vocabulary whatsoever, who is nonetheless fed, housed, disciplined, and hired in systematically patterned ways.
(b) A currency stripped of legal-tender status by decree, which continues to circulate and to settle debts in the border town that uses it.
(c) A psychiatric diagnosis whose recorded incidence rises sharply once the diagnosis becomes publicly available to the very people receiving it.
(d) A hereditary title abolished by statute, whose former holders continue to be addressed and accommodated deferentially two generations later.

After the tree, report for each vignette which leaves yield the same answer to it, and flag every pair of leaves your instrument cannot in practice separate, saying whether the failure is a defect of your question set or a genuine convergence in the positions.

**The logical space.** Then step back from the tree and lay out the answer combinations your nodes permit, including the unoccupied ones. For each unoccupied cell, diagnose why nobody is there: incoherence, instability under pressure, explanatory or political unattractiveness, or simple neglect — and where the diagnosis is neglect, sketch in two or three sentences the view that would occupy the cell.

**Self-criticism.** Close with one kind you believe your own tree misfiles — class, disability, unemployment, the household, or another you prefer — and identify which branch question would have to be rewritten to accommodate it, and how.

**Constraints.** No bibliography, no reading list, no citations, no article or book titles, and no attributions of specific claims to specific works; the standard labels for positions are all the sourcing I want. Do not end by adjudicating which leaf is correct — the instrument's purpose is sorting, and a verdict would defeat it.
