Been sitting on our research-integrity panel for a year and next month I'm the one redrafting the sanctions grid, and the entire thing pivots on a single word: intentional. Fabricate data intentionally and you're out; do it carelessly and you get a corrective action plan. The problem is I've watched this panel call identical conduct intentional when the fallout was ugly and accidental when nobody got hurt, and it isn't just us being sloppy -- people do it systematically, right down to that old pair of vignettes where the chairman is told the new program will boost profits and also affect the environment, says he doesn't care about that one way or the other, and gets judged completely differently depending only on whether the side effect was harm or help.

So before I write a line of policy I want the spec. What does a notion of intentional action have to deliver to be fit for a job like this? Give me the requirements list: the cases it has to get right (foreseen-but-unwanted side effects, the skilled marksman versus the lucky incompetent one, acting under duress, acting on a compulsion you endorse, the person who ends up ignorant through their own earlier choices), the distinctions it can't collapse (intentional vs deliberate vs voluntary vs merely foreseen, and intentional vs blameworthy, and whether it even comes in degrees), and the facts about how people actually apply the word that a theory has to either explain or explain away -- a line on why each item earns its place on the list.

Then tell me which of these are genuinely non-negotiable and which are just the tastes of one camp, and whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable. I have a suspicion that "matches how ordinary people actually talk" and "doesn't shift with the moral valence of the outcome" can't both be had, and if that's right it's the most useful thing you can tell me, because it would mean the grid can't be built on the folk concept at all. Also: which single requirement knocks out the most candidates?

Finish with a sentence each on how a plain mental-state account, a deep-self or reasons account, and the deflationary view that "intentionally" is just how people voice blame do against the list. And if you think what a sanctions policy needs is something other than any of them, say what it is.
