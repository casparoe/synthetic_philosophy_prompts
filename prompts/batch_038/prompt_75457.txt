I cover science and ideas for a general-interest mag, and next month I'm putting my own view on the record in a column about whether "better than" has to be transitive. Before I file, I want you to act as a professional philosopher who works on value theory and population ethics — not as a summarizer. No literature survey, no reading list: an audit of my package. Start by brainstorming how the pieces interact with each other, then give verdicts.

Here's what I hold:

1. My spectrum verdicts: a world of a billion very good lives (call it A) plus — nobody else affected — a trillion lives that are just barely worth living (A+): A+ is not worse than A. A world where everyone sits at a middling but equal level is better than A+. And a world of nothing but zillions of barely-worth-living lives (C) is worse than A. I'm completely fine with that chain being intransitive.

2. There's no vagueness or imprecision hiding in any of it. When I say "not worse than" I mean either better or exactly equally good — sharp, not a confession of ignorance.

3. The idea I actually want to float in the column, which I'll call the proportional-threshold view: a difference only counts as betterness if it clears a threshold that scales with how much is at stake. Small stakes, small threshold; astronomical stakes, astronomical threshold. Below the threshold, the two outcomes are exactly equally good.

4. It's one relation everywhere: comparing whole worlds and comparing stretches within a single life are the same kind of fact, governed by the same relation.

5. The money-pump objection doesn't touch me. A cycle in betterness doesn't oblige me to accept a string of trades that leaves me worse off; I'd just refuse the trades, and refusing is perfectly compatible with holding the ranking.

Does this hang together? I suspect somethings off but I don't know where, and I'm deliberately not telling you which of the five I'd be happiest to drop — that's for you to say. Rank the tensions by severity: which pairs genuinely can't coexist, and for each, what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost me? Be equally clear about which ones only look like problems and dissolve once you spell them out. Think carefully — I'd rather two sharp findings than ten flags.
