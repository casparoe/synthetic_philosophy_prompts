I'm putting together a talk for a philosophy-of-biology reading group and I want to run one real scientific fight through Dennett's cranes/skyhooks machinery, mostly to see whether the machinery survives contact with it. Can you do the analysis for me?

The episode: the ENCODE results of September 2012 and the argument that immediately followed. The facts I'll need you to work from, so you don't have to guess:

- The consortium published about 30 papers at once across Nature, Genome Research and Genome Biology, reporting a huge survey of the human genome — RNA transcription, binding sites for something like 120 transcription factors, histone modifications, methylation, chromatin accessibility — across 147+ cell types.
- Protein-coding sequence is a bit over 1% of the genome, in roughly 20,000 genes. ENCODE assigned "some sort of function" to around 80% of it, including 70,000-plus promoter regions and something like 400,000 candidate enhancers, where "function" was cashed out operationally as reproducible biochemical activity in at least one cell type.
- The press coverage ran with the death of junk DNA. Then evolutionary biologists pushed back hard — the best-known rebuttal being Dan Graur and colleagues shortly afterwards — on two grounds: (a) that biochemical activity is not the same thing as evolved function, since most transcription is noise and much of the genome is transposon debris and selfish DNA whose activity serves the element, not the organism; and (b) an old population-genetic argument going back to Haldane and Muller, that if 80% of the genome were really under selection, the load of deleterious mutations each generation would be more than a human population could bear.
- ENCODE defenders mostly answered that they were being descriptive, not claiming selection had shaped every element, and that the 80% was always a provisional upper bound.

So here's what I want. Take that fight and analyze it with cranes vs. skyhooks and greedy reductionism, being disciplined about the mapping rather than waving the metaphors around. Specifically:

1. Which side, if either, is reaching for a skyhook? The obvious read is that ENCODE imports a design-flavored notion of function — parts doing jobs for the whole — without paying for it with the crane (selection) that would earn the word, so that "function" is doing teleological work with no lifter underneath. Pressure-test that. Is it a skyhook, or is it just a badly chosen label for a legitimate lower-level finding?
2. Does greedy reductionism apply, and in which direction? Dennett's charge is normally about collapsing a higher level into a lower one in a single leap. Here the collapse seems to run the other way — molecular chemistry gets promoted to a design-level claim. Say whether the concept still has a grip on this case or whether I'm stretching it past its useful range, and if it does have a grip, name exactly which level gets skipped and what would have to be built to not skip it.
3. What does the lens explain well about the episode — in particular why this felt to participants like a category error rather than a dispute about data, and why both sides could look at the same assays and disagree about whether anything had been discovered?
4. Where does it misdescribe things or go quiet? I suspect at least three spots: the fact that the actual technical disagreement has its own well-developed vocabulary in philosophy of biology about two senses of function, which doesn't need Dennett at all; the adaptationist-just-so-story tradition it descends from, where the same complaint was made decades earlier without any skyhook talk; and the funding-and-headline incentives that made a big consortium want a big number. Tell me if you think those are fair charges or if the lens can absorb them.
5. Verdict, running in the other direction: does this episode confirm the crane/skyhook distinction, is it an anomaly, or is it a case the distinction was simply never built to handle? And would the lens have let you predict how the dispute resolved — i.e. that the field largely kept ENCODE's data and quietly dropped the 80% framing?
6. If you think a different frame does better work on this case, say which one and what it costs you to switch.
