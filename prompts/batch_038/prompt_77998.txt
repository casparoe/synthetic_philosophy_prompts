I am three-quarters of the way through a novel whose final section is told in close third person from inside the head of a foraging animal — an unfashionable device, I am aware, and one my editor has already argued against — and the book's counterweight is a comparative psychologist who has spent thirty years defending the claim that animal perceptual states have genuinely representational contents, not merely reliable covariations. Her marriage has dissolved in parallel, and I want the reader to understand, without my ever stating it, that she has for decades been systematically and *usefully* mistaken about the man she lives with: the mistake has kept her functioning, which is precisely why she could never have corrected it. The animal material has to carry that weight honestly rather than decorate it, because at one point she quarrels with two former colleagues at a conference dinner, and I cannot write that scene until I know what each of the three positions actually commits her to.

So: please construct a thought experiment to specification. I need a real or plausibly real animal, together with a describable selection or learning history, and a single token perceptual state triggered in a described situation, such that three accounts of content deliver three distinguishable verdicts about that token in this pattern. First, a causal-informational account — content fixed by what the state reliably indicates, or reliably indicated during the period in which the correlation was established — says the content is satisfied in the situation, so the animal makes no error at all. Second, a producer-side teleosemantic account — content fixed by the conditions the detecting mechanism was selected to be triggered by — says the state has a determinate content that is false in the situation, so the animal misrepresents. Third, a consumer-side or success-oriented teleosemantic account — content fixed by what the downstream systems that use the state were selected to gain from it — assigns a *different* content that comes out true. The feature I most need, and the reason I am commissioning the case rather than reaching for a familiar one, is the twist: the animal's false representation must be on the whole fitness-enhancing in the changed circumstances, so that the case cannot be waved away as a malfunction selection will promptly weed out. Constraints: no frog snapping at BB pellets, no Swampman, no Twin Earth duplicates, no inverted or shifted spectra — I want something a reader could look up in a field guide. The scenario itself must be statable in no more than three paragraphs. The misrepresentation must be stable, meaning the animal is in no position to correct it by learning, and you should say explicitly why not. No exotic biology, no engineered chimeras, no organ that would make a behavioural ecologist put the book down. And the animal's interests must be describable in ordinary terms — food, safety, reproduction, thermoregulation — so that the consumer-side account has something definite to appeal to.

After the scenario, walk me through it theory by theory: identify the token state and its triggering condition, list the historical facts each account leans on, state the content each assigns, and explain why the verdicts diverge rather than merely differing in vocabulary. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work — I suspect it is the wedge between what the detector was selected to detect and what its consumers were selected to gain — and show me what happens to all three verdicts if I remove that feature: does the case collapse into a routine misrepresentation story, or into one where the three accounts agree? Finally, give each theorist a counterattack at full strength, since the dinner scene is only worth writing if nobody scores a cheap point: how would the causal-informational theorist redescribe the content so as to recover genuine error without giving up indicator semantics, and how would the producer-side teleosematist argue that what downstream systems stand to gain cannot retroactively alter what a state means? My character is the producer-side teleosematist, and I need her defence stated better than she would state it herself. A case that simply stipulates the three contents, rather than deriving them from each account's own machinery, is useless to me — she must be able to hold her ground by argument. When you are finished, append a table with exactly these columns: Account; Content assigned to the token state; Veridical or misrepresenting in the situation; Historical fact the content turns on; Verdict once the pivotal feature is removed; and Strongest objection that account's defender can raise against the case. Three rows, one per account.
