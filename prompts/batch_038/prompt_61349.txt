Grant everything you want about Bob and his hair: it's neither true nor false that Bob is bald. Now look at that sentence itself — 'it's neither true nor false that Bob is bald.' So if your theory sentences come out determinate while the object language goes gappy, the line between 'determinately bald' and 'not determinately bald' is perfectly sharp, which is exactly what you accused me of smuggling in. I've just moved the cutoff upstairs, and you're an epistemicist about the second order.

And it looks like you can't have it both ways. Suppose you insist on the T-schema, that 'Bob is bald' is true just in case Bob is bald. Then if it's indefinite whether Bob is bald, it's indefinite whether the sentence is true, so the metalinguistic sentence is vague too — and now the claim that there are truth-value gaps is itself gappy, and you can't state your own view definitely at all.
