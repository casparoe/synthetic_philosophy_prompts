Setup: I'm sympathetic to origin essentialism about people, the Kripke-ish line that you couldnt have come from a different sperm and a different egg than the ones you actually came from. I like it precisely because it seems modest — a small, almost common-sense claim about identity, not a theory of everything. Nia says it isn't modest at all, that it snowballs, and she wrote the snowball out on my whiteboard like this:

1. Necessarily, for any person x, x originates from the very pair of gametes x in fact originates from. Different sperm or different egg, different person (or no person at all).
2. A gamete is the very gamete it is only if it's produced by the very meiotic event that actually produced it. There's no such thing as "the same sperm, made by a different cell division" — that's just a description of a different sperm.
3. An event is the event it is only if it has the causes it actually has. Otherwise events are just undifferentiated blobs and we can't tell one cell division from another.
4. Therefore, necessarily, a gamete exists only if its entire actual causal ancestry obtains. Nia's name for this principle is **ancestral closure**: whatever is essential to something that's essential to you is essential to you, all the way back.
5. Therefore, necessarily, a person exists only if the entire actual history of the world up to their conception obtains, in every detail.
6. Therefore no world that differs from actuality at a time t contains any actual person conceived after t. Since nearly everyone alive was conceived after any remote change you care to name, most counterfactual histories are simply empty of us.
7. So it is never true that if my grandmother hadn't missed that train in 1946 I'd have been born in Warsaw. That sentence describes a world with no me in it, and no sister, and no parents. Nia calls this the **Conception Wall**: divergence can only ever occur after the last of us is conceived, so the "could" in "things could have gone differently with all of us still here" is empty.
8. Now either (a) that's just necessitarianism in a lab coat — you've made the whole actual past necessary while claiming to be talking about sperm — or (b) if you insist the past is genuinely contingent and that some world with a different past does contain people, then step 1 fails in that world for those people.
9. So origin essentialism is either a disguised thesis about the necessity of the past, or it's false.
