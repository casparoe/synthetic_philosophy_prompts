So this has been rattling around in my head for months and I don't have anyone to talk to about it who wouldn't just pick a side.

I work at a small logistics software outfit, about 40 people. Our founder is intense about the mission — he thinks the product is genuinely going to cut trucking emissions and he talks about it like it's a calling. Last year he announced a $10,000 "conviction bonus" for anyone on staff who, in a one-on-one sit-down with him, he judges to actually believe in the mission. Not to say they believe it. To believe it. Hes weirdly good at spotting fakers, so most people didn't even try.

I wanted the money. Rent went up. So I decided to work on myself: I read the two books he makes everyone read, I sat in on sales calls where drivers complain about fuel costs, I kept notes on why the work matters, I thought about it on my commute. About five months in something clicked and now I really do believe it. I got the bonus in December and I was thrilled.

Heres the part that bugs me. My girlfriend says I let somebody buy a belief into my head and that I'm one step from a cult. But when I check myself, the belief feels exactly like my other beliefs. I can give you reasons for it that have nothing to do with the money, and I honestly think if the bonus had never existed I'd have wound up believing it anyway. Except I also know I never would have started down that road without the $10k sitting there.

So is my belief somehow worse, even though I can't tell the difference from the inside and I think its true? Did he do something sketchy by offering it? And practically — should I give the money back, or stop reading about the mission for a while to see if the belief survives on its own?
