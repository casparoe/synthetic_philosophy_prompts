In my epistemology seminar we did a couple of weeks on whether there are mental states you can't be wrong about being in, and then an argument with a sorites flavor that's supposed to show there aren't really any. OK, fine. But there's a third case I keep running into that the yes/no framing doesn't have a slot for, and I don't know if it has a name.

Some states are ones you can check on demand, and the check is basically infallible — except that doing the check disturbs what you're checking. Whether I'm actually enjoying the song I'm listening to: the only way to find out is to turn my attention onto the enjoying, and that makes me self-conscious and the enjoyment thins out, so what I learn is about a state I've already stopped being in. Same with whether I'm bored in lecture — asking the question makes me alert to my own boredom, which is a different mental event than just unreflectively being bored. And with a mild itch, attending to it makes it worse and it sort of migrates.

So it's knowable at a moment but the knowledge is always slightly stale, and you can't hold onto it across a stretch even tho each individual check works. Is this a recognized thing? What's the closest established concept, and how is mine different from plain introspective unreliability, or from a state that's supposed to be its own evidence? And if nobody's named it, what would you call it and where does it stop — does the embarrassment-that-grows-when-you-notice-it case count?
