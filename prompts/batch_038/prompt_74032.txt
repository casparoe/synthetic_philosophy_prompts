For my senior humanities seminar paper I am reading Of Mice and Men through the Knobe effect, and I want a genuine critical reading rather than a summary of either.

I am working with three rival explanations: that moral considerations are built into the concept of intentional action itself; that the urge to blame distorts an otherwise accurate concept; and that we call an act intentional when it expresses the agent's deeper values, which would explain the asymmetry because indifference to harm reads as self-revealing in a way that indifference to help does not.

Which of these does Steinbeck actually put under pressure? Please work through Lennie killing Curley's wife (a harm that seems to express nothing in Lennie's deeper self, since what he most wants is the rabbits), George shooting Lennie by the river (a harm that seems to express everything he values), and Carlson's shooting of Candy's dog. Then tell me which of the three explanations the novel's structure best supports, name the one scene where my lens distorts the book rather than illuminating it, and say what Steinbeck reveals that these vignette studies cannot, given that they hand the reader no history with the agent they are asked to judge.
