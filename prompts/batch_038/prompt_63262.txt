Our reading group is doing evolutionary debunking arguments and I tried to write out what I thought was the cleanest version as a numbered argument, and when I did, the conclusion blew way past what I wanted. Here it is:

1. If some process P produces beliefs about a domain D, and P's operation is fully explained by facts that have nothing to do with the D-facts, then P's outputs aren't justified — you've got a complete causal story in which the truth of the D-facts never does any work.

2. The processes that produced our moral beliefs are fully explained by facts about pleistocene selection pressures — kin altruism, reciprocity, reputation management inside small coalitions — none of which mention moral properties.

3. So our moral beliefs aren't justified. That's the debunking I was expecting, fine.

4. But exactly the same goes for arithmetic and logic. Counting, comparing quantities, and tracking what follows from what were useful for foraging, trading, and settling disputes, and you can tell the whole story of why we came to hold that 7+5=12 without any arithmetical fact ever getting a look in.

5. So by (1), our arithmetical and logical beliefs aren't justified either.

6. That's absurd, and worse, it's self-undermining: (1) is itself a normative epistemological claim arrived at by reasoning, so (5) pulls the rug out from under the argument that generated it.

7. Hence (1) is false — explanatory independence from the facts in a domain isn't enough to defeat justification.

8. But once (1) goes, (3) goes with it, and the debunker is left with no premise that generates skepticism about morality without generating it about arithmetic too.

Someone in the group says the fix is to restrict (1) to domains whose facts are causally inert and contingent, but I can't see how to draw that line without either assuming the moral realist is wrong or excluding arithmetic by hand — arithmetical facts are causally inert too, and if anything more necessary than moral ones. So: which step do I actually give up, and what does giving it up cost? I'd rather keep the verdict that arithmetic is fine, so I want to know where the asymmetry between the two domains has to come from and whether it can be principled rather than ad hoc. Also tell me what would change your mind — which consideration or argument would push you to a different verdict about where the failure is, and where do you think my own reasoning is most likely to have gone wrong?
