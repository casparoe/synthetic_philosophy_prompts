I am setting the assessed take-home for a graduate seminar in formal epistemology. The paper is closed-book and self-contained: students are given no texts, and no answer may cite literature. I want you to write one question on the regress problem — foundationalism against coherentism against infinitism — that cannot be passed by correctly naming and summarising the three positions. The difficulty should come from a formal model supplied in the question itself, which the students must then manipulate and interpret.

Write the question as it would be printed on the paper, using the following model exactly as I have specified it. You may polish the wording, but do not alter the substance, the notation, or the values.

**Stipulations to be printed for the students.**

1. A *support structure* is a pair ⟨S, R⟩, where S is a set of doxastic states and R ⊆ S × S, with tRs read as "t is a reason for s." A *justification assignment* is a function J: S → [0, 1].
2. A state is *spontaneous* if nothing bears R to it.
3. **DT (discounted transmission).** If s has exactly one reason t, then J(s) = r · J(t), where r is a fixed real number with 0 < r < 1.
4. **RA (redundant aggregation).** If s has exactly n ≥ 2 reasons t₁, …, tₙ, then J(s) = 1 − ∏ᵢ (1 − r · J(tᵢ)).
5. Every spontaneous state has J = 1.
6. An assignment is *admissible* for a structure if it satisfies clauses 3–5 at every state.
7. *Infinitist* structures contain no spontaneous states, every state has at least one reason, and R is acyclic. *Coherentist* structures may contain cycles and need not contain spontaneous states. *Foundationalist* structures are acyclic, contain no infinite descending R-chains, and every maximal descending chain terminates in a spontaneous state.
8. Throughout, take r = 3/4 unless a part asks for general r.
9. Four structures are designated: **T∞**, the infinite binary tree in which every state has exactly two reasons, those two have their own two, and so on without end, no two branches ever rejoining and no state spontaneous; **Δ**, three states each of whose reasons are the other two; **Γ**, two states each of which is the sole reason for the other; **F_d**, a finite tree of depth d in which every non-terminal state has exactly one reason and every terminal state is spontaneous.

**The parts the question must set.**

(a) Prove that in any acyclic structure with no spontaneous states in which every state has exactly one reason, every admissible assignment is identically zero. Then identify which of three ingredients is load-bearing — the absence of spontaneous states, the fact that r < 1, and the one-reason restriction — and for each state the minimal change that would admit a non-zero admissible assignment, together with whether an infinitist can make that change without ceasing to be an infinitist.

(b) For T∞ under RA, show that the identically-zero assignment and the uniform assignment J ≡ (2r − 1)/r² (for r > 1/2) are both admissible, identify them as the least and greatest fixed points of the monotone operator that RA induces, and evaluate both at r = 3/4. The question must then ask what principle, if any, could select one semantics over the other, and must note that iteration from the bottom never leaves zero while iteration from any positive starting value climbs to the greater value — so the choice is not settled by the dynamics.

(c) Determine the admissible assignments for Γ and for Δ at r = 3/4. The question must then ask whether Δ's non-zero greatest fixed point, in a structure containing no spontaneous state and no contact with anything outside the triangle, is a refutation of the model or a formal statement of the isolation objection, and must require the student to say what a coherentist could concede here without abandoning coherentism.

(d) Compute J at the root of F_d and explain why foundationalist structures admit a unique assignment where T∞ and Δ do not. Then ask what clause 5 smuggles in: what the model is structurally unable to say about spontaneous states — for instance about defeaters, and about whether a seeming's authority might itself depend on where it sits in the network.

(e) Ask the student to evaluate the model rather than work inside it: is the collapse in (a) an artefact of treating justification as transmitted along a single channel rather than a genuine problem for infinite chains? Require at least one escape route for the infinitist, an account of what the infinitist gives up by taking it, and a verdict on whether the indeterminacy in (b) afflicts coherentism specifically or any view that permits unanchored support cycles.

**Deliverables.**

- The exam question itself, printed with the stipulations and the five parts, with a mark allocation for each part summing to 25 of the paper's 50 marks, and a stated word limit of 1,500 words for the student.
- A model answer of roughly 1,500 words that stays within that limit and shows every step of each derivation rather than reporting results. Where the model answer attaches a philosophical gloss to a formal result, it must say explicitly which formal feature the gloss depends on.
- A marking scheme listing the must-hit points, the marks each carries, and the specific errors that should cost marks. It must distinguish an A from a B answer by naming the moves each contains: I expect the difference to turn on whether the candidate treats the choice between least and greatest fixed points as a substantive epistemological commitment rather than a technicality, and on whether the candidate notices that Γ collapses under DT while Δ does not. Include a one-line note on the most common way candidates fumble each part, and state explicitly that a fluent taxonomy of the three positions with no derivations earns no more than a bare pass.

Do not include citations, a bibliography, a list of further reading, or references to named philosophers anywhere in the question, the model answer, or the marking scheme. Everything needed must be in the stipulations or follow from them.

Finally, after the marking scheme, tell me how confident you are in the derivations you have put in the model answer and in the philosophical glosses attached to them, and single out the one claim in the whole package that you are least sure of, with a sentence on what would settle it.
