I am the grader for a graduate game theory course, and I need a full assessment of one student's submission to a problem-set question on two-person bargaining. The question is worth 25 points, allocated as marked. Please give me a grade and the written comments that justify it.

**Question 3 (25 points).** Consider the two-person bargaining problem with feasible set

S = {(u₁, u₂) ∈ ℝ² : u₁ ≥ 0, u₂ ≥ 0, 2u₁ + u₂ ≤ 6}

and disagreement point d = (1, 1). Utilities are von Neumann–Morgenstern. Recall the two solutions from lecture: the Nash solution is the point of S maximizing (u₁ − d₁)(u₂ − d₂); the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution is the furthest point from d along the ray from d toward the ideal point a(S) = (max u₁ over S, max u₂ over S) that still belongs to S. A solution f satisfies independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA) if whenever S′ ⊆ S, d(S′) = d(S), and f(S) ∈ S′, then f(S′) = f(S).

(a) [8 points] Compute the Nash solution and the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution for this problem.

(b) [9 points] Which of the two solutions satisfies IIA? Justify your answer. If you claim that a solution fails IIA, exhibit the contraction S′ ⊆ S that demonstrates the failure; if you claim that it satisfies IIA, explain why no such contraction can exist.

(c) [8 points] A mediator proposes to settle the dispute at the Kalai–Smorodinsky point, arguing that it is the fair outcome because "each side receives the same share of what it could realistically hope for." Critically assess this justification, and say what a party could do before negotiations begin to shift the settlement in its own favor.

**Student's submission.**

(a) On the frontier u₂ = 6 − 2u₁, the Nash product is (u₁ − 1)(5 − 2u₁) = −2u₁² + 7u₁ − 5, whose derivative vanishes at u₁ = 7/4. The Nash solution is therefore (7/4, 5/2), with gains of 3/4 and 3/2 over the disagreement point. For Kalai–Smorodinsky, a(S) = (3, 6), and the ray from d is (1 + 2t, 1 + 5t); substituting into 2u₁ + u₂ = 6 gives 3 + 9t = 6, so t = 1/3 and the solution is (5/3, 8/3). Each player's gain is exactly one third of that player's own gap from d to a: 2/3 out of a possible 2, and 5/3 out of a possible 5.

(b) The Nash solution satisfies IIA: if x maximizes the product over S and x ∈ S′ ⊆ S, then x also maximizes the product over S′, so f(S′) = x. Kalai–Smorodinsky satisfies IIA as well. The KS point is determined by just two features of the problem, d and a(S): once the ray is drawn, its crossing with the frontier is fixed. A contraction that deletes unchosen alternatives leaves d unchanged, and it leaves a(S) unchanged too, because a is a property of the frontier — the best each player could ever obtain — and removing points that no solution would have selected cannot lower anyone's best possible payoff. With d and a(S) both fixed, the ray is fixed and the crossing cannot move. So neither solution is sensitive to the presence or absence of unchosen alternatives.

(c) The mediator's description is literally accurate: at the KS point both gains are the same fraction t of the respective gaps from d to a, which is the sense in which KS equalizes proportional rather than absolute gains. Where the problem is symmetric the two solutions coincide, so nothing is lost by adopting KS in that case. The reason to prefer KS in the asymmetric case is that it satisfies everything the Nash solution satisfies — Pareto efficiency, symmetry, invariance to affine rescalings of the utilities, and IIA — and monotonicity on top of these, making it the unique point that meets all four of Nash's conditions plus one further requirement. Because the solution is scale-invariant, moreover, the mediator's talk of shares is an ordinal matter: the same settlement would be selected however the parties' preferences were re-described, provided the re-description preserves order. As for shifting the settlement in advance, KS responds to the fallback position, so a party should invest in its outside option; in the problem above this is precisely why player 2, whose fallback is the stronger, ends up ahead of player 1 relative to the Nash solution.
