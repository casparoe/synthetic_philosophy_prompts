I sit on the Program Review Panel of a state energy office — seven members, three of them from outside government — which in six weeks must decide whether a demand-response rebate program's two-year conditional approval converts to permanent status. Our deputy director has proposed that this cycle we abandon our usual format (two half-day meetings, a staff presentation, open discussion, a vote) in favor of what she calls a factored protocol. The overall question is decomposed into fourteen narrow sub-questions — eligibility design, modeled uptake, per-household kilowatt-hour savings, persistence of savings after the rebate ends, administrative cost per unit saved, incidence of the subsidy across income bands, and so on. Each is assigned to a single reviewer who receives only her slice, a data packet attached to it, and a template, and who returns a score on a one-to-nine scale plus a stated confidence. Reviewers are explicitly instructed not to ask what the sub-question is for, on the theory that knowing the overall question would contaminate their local judgment. No reviewer sees anyone else's input. A fixed weight vector, drafted by the deputy director, combines the fourteen scores into a single figure; conversion is recommended if that figure clears 6.0 and no individual score falls below 3. Only the aggregate reaches the panel.

Her stated rationale is not efficiency but epistemics. Our format, she argues, has documented failure modes: the loudest member sets the anchor in the first ten minutes, we spend two hours on whatever data happen to be in the staff memo and no time on the data that are not, and we converge early to avoid an open split. She points to the 2023 cycle, when we renewed a weatherization pilot over staff objections largely on the strength of one member's anecdote about his own contractor, and the pilot ultimately delivered about a third of its projected savings. She presses the point further: no individual on this panel can competently hold the whole question anyway, because the evidence base is simply too wide, so the real choice is not between a factored protocol and a well-informed deliberator but between a decomposition and a pretense. Several members are unpersuaded. Two hold that the weights do the substantive work: a sensitivity analysis circulated last week shows the recommendation flipping under two other plausible weightings, one of which raises the equity slice from its assigned 0.05. One member, a program administrator, objects that nobody can sensibly score, say, persistence of savings without knowing what those savings are supposed to establish, so the instruction not to ask about the whole guarantees shallow answers rather than unbiased ones. My own worry is adjacent to hers but different in kind. Under the protocol, no one in this agency will ever hold the judgment that the program merits permanent status. We will publish a number, and if the program fails in three years, there will be nobody who can tell a legislative committee what we believed or why. I do not yet know whether that is a real cost or a piece of sentiment that the 2023 record makes difficult to defend.

Please draft the memo I will circulate to the panel ahead of the meeting — roughly two pages, written for members who have skimmed the proposal but not the sensitivity analysis. It must reach a bottom line: adopt the protocol for this cycle, reject it, or adopt it with modifications stated concretely enough to be moved from the floor. Give the reasoning that gets us there, including an explicit account of what the protocol would have to assume in order to inherit the guarantee the deputy director claims for it, and whether those assumptions plausibly hold for a question of this shape. Treat the strongest case for the position you argue against in terms its holder would recognize, and say precisely where the 2023 cycle does and does not support her. Finally, specify what we should examine after this cycle to judge whether the protocol helped: name the observable signals that would count in its favor, those that would count against it, and any that would remain ambiguous however they turned out.
