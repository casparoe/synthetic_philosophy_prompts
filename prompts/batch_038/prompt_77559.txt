I'm presenting a paper at a workshop on AI safety governance next month. Audience is maybe 30 people: trust & safety leads from two frontier labs, a handful of HCI researchers who study conversational agents, one legal academic who writes about platform liability, and a couple of philosophers who do applied ethics. My thesis is that assistants shouldn't refuse paternalistically at all — they should impose friction instead. A delay, plus making the user restate what they're asking for and what it costs them, with the length of the delay scaled by how irreversible the downstream action is. Never say no, just make "yes" expensive in time and attention. The one exception I grant is irreversible harm to identifiable third parties, where I do let the assistant hard-refuse. I argue this beats refusal policies because it doesn't require the lab to decide what a good life look like, it degrades gracefully when the model misreads the situation, and it still catches the 3am cases.

Give me the Q&A prep sheet. I want the objections this particular room will raise, in their vocabulary — the liability person and the trust & safety people will object for completely different reasons and I don't want them flattened into one generic list. Order them by how much damage they'd do to the view. For each: how they'd actually phrase it, a two-minute reply, and where that reply is soft. Also flag which objections I should concede ground on up front in the talk itself, the single reply that has to land or the whole thing collapses, and the question I'm praying nobody asks.
