I keep the minutes for a twelve-member rules club that meets on alternate Thursdays to play a game in the Nomic tradition — a game in which altering the rules is itself a move, so that the only permanent content is the procedure for changing it. At the start of term the members unanimously adopted the following rulebook, which I transcribe exactly as it stood before last night's meeting. Rule 1 (immutable): every player must comply with every rule then in effect, in the form in which it is then in effect. Rule 2 (immutable): Rules 1 through 5 are immutable and may not be amended, repealed, or superseded; all other rules are mutable. Rule 3 (immutable): any mutable rule may be amended, repealed, or replaced, and new mutable rules may be added, by a vote of at least two-thirds of the players present and voting; an adopted change takes effect at the close of the round in which it was voted on. Rule 4 (immutable): no change operates retroactively; each applies only to events occurring after it takes effect. Rule 5 (immutable): no change may be adopted that contradicts the rulebook as it stands at the moment of the vote. Rule 6 (mutable): a player scores one point for each of their own proposals that is adopted, and loses one point for each round in which none of theirs is adopted. For context, Dara was leading on points under Rule 6 at the time of the vote, and Noor was second; all twelve members were present and voting.

Here is the dispute, in the order it occurred. (1) Dara proposed an amendment to Rule 6 that left the scoring sentence untouched and appended one further sentence: "This rule may not be amended or repealed." It carried 8–4. She argued that Rule 5 asks only whether a proposal contradicts the rulebook at the moment of the vote, and at that moment nothing in force forbade entrenching a mutable rule, so the entrenchment was cleanly adopted. (2) Noor then proposed to strike the appended sentence and change the scoring, on the ground that the entrenchment was a nullity from the outset: Rule 3 confers a general power over every mutable rule, Rule 6 is mutable by Rule 2's numbering, and a mutable rule cannot switch off a power that an immutable rule confers — a tenant cannot amend a lease by writing on the wall. On her view the appended sentence is at most a declaration of intent, and her proposal is properly before the club. (3) Dara replied that this proves too much: if a mutable rule cannot limit the amendment power, then no rule can limit anything, since a limitation is itself just a rule, and Rule 3 contains no words making its grant indefeasible. What the club adopted under Rule 3 was a particular exception to Rule 3's general grant, and Rule 5 was satisfied when it was adopted, so Noor is invoking Rule 5 against the product of Rule 5. She added that the only workable reading of Rule 5 is procedural — it asks whether the act of adoption is authorized, not whether the resulting text is free of tension, since every ordinary amendment "contradicts" the sentence it replaces, and a reading that counted that would have frozen the game on the first night. (4) Noor said that reading is available to Dara only because it suits her, and that Dara should pick a reading and live with it. If Rule 5 asks merely whether the act is authorized, then the club may adopt a proposal reading "no change shall hereafter be adopted," because nothing presently in force forbids it; that proposal would be valid, would bar its own repeal, and would extinguish the club's rule-making capacity while leaving the club sitting at the table. On the other reading — that what must be free of contradiction is the rulebook as it will stand — the entrenchment was never adoptable at all, since it flatly contradicts Rule 3, which says Rule 6 may be amended. Either way, she concluded, the appended sentence should be declared void from the beginning. (5) Dara said she accepts the consequence in (4) without embarrassment: a group that cannot bind itself has no rulebook but a series of coincidences, and if twelve people choose to seal their rules shut, their later regret does not make the sealing invalid, since regret is not a lack of competence. She also noted that Noor's remedy is self-consuming — a new rule reading "no proposal may be adopted that would impair the amendment power" would itself impair the amendment power, by barring its own repeal — so Noor needs an unwritten doctrine of implied limits with no textual home, and Rule 4 forbids the backward reach involved in treating the appended sentence as though it had never existed. (6) Priya, who had voted with Dara in (1), proposed a compromise: keep the appended sentence but add that it may be removed by a three-fourths vote, on the theory that turning an absolute bar into a graduated one lets the club settle the matter by rule rather than by argument. Both Dara and Noor objected that Priya's proposal could not even be put to a vote — on Dara's view because it amends an unamendable rule, on Noor's because the entrenchment is already void and the compromise is unnecessary. I declined to rule as chair, and the round closed with Rule 6 in limbo and two competing score sheets in circulation.

I would like a move-by-move adjudication rather than a survey of the issues. Please rate each of the six moves — and, where a move contains two or more separable claims, rate those claims individually — on a scale from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a sentence or two of justification for each rating. Then answer the following. (a) Who is right on the central question, whether the appended sentence is in force, and does the answer turn entirely on which reading of Rule 5 the club adopts, or is one reading independently better supported by Rules 1 through 4? (b) What is the strongest point each side makes that the other never answers? (c) What are both of them missing — in particular, is Rule 3 best understood as conferring a power that the club may use to divest itself of that power, or as describing a standing procedure that no single rule can switch off, and does drawing that distinction actually decide the case or merely restate it? (d) Is the rulebook as transcribed inconsistent, merely indeterminate, or is there a third diagnosis — that it is perfectly consistent but determinately generates a question it cannot answer? (e) State the smallest textual change to Rules 1 through 5 that would have prevented last night's dispute, and say honestly whether your change merely relocates the problem one level up; also say whether the club could adopt that change from inside its own rulebook given Rule 2, or whether it would have to step outside the game and reconstitute it. If the underlying difficulty has a standard name, one clause identifying it is welcome, but I want the reasoning worked through on these texts as given, not references or a summary of the literature.
