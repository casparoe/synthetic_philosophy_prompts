My roommate built this argument to show you can get P≠NP out of facts about understanding rather than out of algorithmics, and I think somethings broken in it but I cant find the exact bad joint. Here it is as close to his version as I can reconstruct:

(1) If a system gets the right answer only by putting out candidates and discarding the ones that fail a check, then its success is no evidence that it understands the problem.
(2) Any finite system, machine or person, that solves a search problem is doing exactly that: emitting candidates and rejecting failures.
(3) For any problem where a proposed solution can be checked quickly, enough compute plus (2) will produce a solution. So solving those problems never took understanding, only patience.
(4) Lots of central mathematical problems are like that: hand me a proof and I can check it line by line, hand me a factorization and I can multiply it out.
(5) So if a machine ever "proves" a hard theorem or cracks a big composite, nothing at all has been shown about its mind — it was just patient in the right direction.
(6) Human mathematicians are finite systems, so by (2) they are also just emitting and filtering. The feeling of insight is a report on how fast thier filter runs.
(7) Either humans understand mathematics or nothing and nobody does. Humans do.
(8) So (2) or (3) is false. But (2) is close to analytic, so (3) is false: there are quickly-checkable problems that no amount of compute solves.
(9) Therefore P≠NP.

He thinks the fact that (9) drops out of premises about minds instead of premises about algorithms is the whole point and not a bug.
