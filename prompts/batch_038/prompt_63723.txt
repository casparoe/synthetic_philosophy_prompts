My supervisor has been handing out one-word verdicts on which systems have minds for the best part of a decade and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the rule, because she won't state it. Our lab scores every candidate on two things: internal integration (roughly, how much of the causal traffic between a system's components stays inside it) and exclusion (how much leaks outside — how entangled the thing is with its environment and with other candidate systems). Both 0 to 1. She uses the numbers in seminars while insisting she doesnt believe in them.

Her record so far, integration / exclusion:

- adult human: 0.90 / 0.90 — yes
- octopus: 0.85 / 0.55 — yes, and she holds this even though most of its neurons are out in the arms and the arms do a lot of their own problem-solving
- honeybee: 0.80 / 0.80 — yes
- one of those giant single-celled slime moulds: 0.60 / 0.95 — yes; asked why, she said one cell, one subject, which is not a reason
- beehive: 0.45 / 0.75 — no
- an ant colony, the Aunt Hillary sort: 0.35 / 0.70 — no, though she called it the best attempt nature ever makes
- a person's immune system: 0.45 / 0.20 — no
- a high street bank: 0.25 / 0.60 — no
- the national economy: 0.30 / 0.30 — no, obviously

Here's the case I'm stuck on. We've been doing some work with an air traffic control unit — a sector: a room of controllers, one shared picture of the sky, handover protocols, a single voice on the frequency that speaks for the whole thing, plans that no individual controller could state, and staff turning over at every shift so the sector carries on regardless of who's in the chairs. At peak load it scores 0.72 / 0.35. Separately, we helped build a system that does the identical job out of one rack: same scope, same handover structure, same voice on the frequency, nobody in the loop. It scores 0.90 / 0.85.

So: predict her verdict on each, with reasons. Then tell me what rule her record actually supports. I can get integration-alone to fit all nine of her verdicts if I put the threshold at 0.5, and I can get the product of the two numbers to fit them all if I put that threshold around 0.4, and I can't tell whether she's running either, or something that doesn't show up in the table at all — no subjects made of subjects, one contiguous organism, capable of being harmed. Say which you'd bet on, note where the fit is fragile (the octopus makes me nervous), and say what she'd have to give up to keep it, including whether either threshold is just fitted to the data.

Then give me the case or cases that would separate whichever rules you think are still live. I've got candidates — the sector at three in the morning with two controllers instead of twenty, the same sector with a four-second delay bolted onto every handover, twins joined at the skull who share a bit of neural tissue, a man o' war, which looks like one animal but is a colony of them — but I can't work out which of these does the most work and which is a distraction.

Two costs I want priced properly. First: she says no to the immune system because it's part of a mind and no to the ant colony because it's made of minds. If the sector is a mind made of minds, is she committed to counting every controller's suffering twice, and can she block that without also blocking the octopus arms, which she's clearly happy to treat as semi-autonomous? Second: whichever way she goes here she swallows something. Yes to both, and minds turn out to be cheap and load-dependent — a Friday-evening mind that stops existing on Sunday morning. No to the sector and yes to the machine, and she has to explain why the same work, done with the same organisation, differs in status only because of what it's built out of. Tell me which horn is cheaper and what it costs.
