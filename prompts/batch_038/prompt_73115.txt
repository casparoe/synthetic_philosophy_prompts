DRAFT, section 2–4: **"Real For Whom? Indexicality in the Rietdijk–Putnam Argument"**

The argument runs roughly as follows. Take Rietdijk's 1966 version and Putnam's 1967 version together, since I think for present purposes they share a skeleton. (P1) All and only the events simultaneous with my here-now are real. (P2) No observer's frame is metaphysically privileged — this is supposed to be the philosophical reading of the relativity principle. (P3) R, the reality relation, is reflexive and transitive. From these: pick a distant observer moving relative to me; by the Lorentz transformations their plane of simultaneity tilts with respect to mine, and by tilting far enough out it slices into what I call my future. Since their here-now events are real (P2 plus the fact that they are a perfectly good observer), and since reality is transitive, the events on that tilted slice are real for me too. Repeat with the opposite velocity to get the past. Hence the future is already real, hence there is no objective becoming, hence four-dimensionalism.

My claim is that the load-bearing premise is P3 and that P3 rests on an equivocation. Transitivity is uncontroversial for a monadic property: if a exists and existence accumulates, fine. But the argument needs cross-frame transitivity, and the only transitivity that is actually licensed by the physics is within-frame. Within a single frame, simultaneity is an equivalence relation and transitivity is trivial. Across frames it is exactly what is in question. Notice what P2 actually says: the frames are on a par. Being on a par is not the same as being additive. If R is relational — if "real" is more like "real-for-o-at-p" than like "exists" — then it should behave the way other indexed relations behave, and indexed relations routinely fail to be transitive. A is a short flight from B and B is a short flight from C does not make A a short flight from C. So the argument slides from the monadic reading, where P3 is trivial but P1 is false (nobody thinks only the present slice exists simpliciter, including the presentist, who thinks the present slice is all there is but not that "real" means "on the current slice"), to the relational reading, where P1 is the interesting thesis but P3 is unearned.

The positive view I want to defend: treat reality as three-place, R(e, f, p) — event e is real relative to frame f as of point p — and then note that everything the physics gives us is invariant, so what is real simpliciter is just the manifold and its structure, the whole four-dimensional totality considered as a set of events with no privileged slicing. I want to call this the indexical block. It is not presentism, because I'm not denying that the future events are there. It is not the block universe of the argument either, because nothing on it answers the question "which slice is now" objectively, and that question was doing all the work. The worry, which I should probably state rather than hide, is that this is a verbal settlement: if "real simpliciter" just means "in the manifold," then the four-dimensionalist wins by definintion and my view has no content. My answer is that the content lies in what is denied — that there is any further fact about which events are real-now — and that denying this is not empty because the argument's conclusion is supposed to license fatalist inferences, and those inferences require the further fact.

Now Stein's objection, which I think points the same direction as mine but by a different route. Stein imposes conditions on R: it must be definable from the metric structure alone, and it must be the same relation for every observer, i.e. unique and invariant. Given those constraints the only candidate that survives is one built out of the light-cone structure, so what is real for a here-now is that point together with its causal past. That gets becoming back, at the cost of what people have called the solipsism of the present moment. **My reading is that Stein's definability and uniqueness conditions are simply a demand that reality be monadic in disguise, so his objection and my proposal are the same objection arriving from different directions.**

Finally, two loose ends I know about. First, cosmology: the standard models seem to hand us a preferred slicing via the cosmic time function, which threatens P2 and might make the whole dispute moot in the actual universe rather than in Minkowski spacetime — I have one paragraph on this and it is thin. Second, the Penrose-style Andromeda scenarios: **since simultaneity is conventional, there is no fact of the matter about which of the two passers-by has the real slice, so nothing determinate is lost when they disagree about whether the fleet has launched.** And: **because the argument only ever gets you a relation between events and frames, it can never deliver the determinism Rietdijk wanted, which is a claim about whether there are genuinely open alternatives, so the argument fails at its own stated goal even if every premise is granted.**
