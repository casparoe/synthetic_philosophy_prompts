I curate functional annotations for a gene database — someone has to decide what goes in the "function" column — and I've realized I have no idea what principle I'm actually applying when I do it. I've read enough Cummins and Millikan to know the two labels (selected effects on one side, causal role on the other) but neither one matches my gut, and my gut is what I use at work every day. So here are my verdicts on cases, and I want to know what they add up to.

1. The heart pumps blood: function, no question. Both camps agree and so do I.
2. Hearts also make a thumping sound. That sound has a causal role in the system, and a real one — a doctor can hear it, it comes from valves closing — but I'd flatly deny it's a function of the heart.
3. A heart in a body that died an hour ago and hasn't beaten in that time: I'd still say its function is to pump blood.
4. A kidney that has failed and filters nothing at all: filtering is still its function, and I want to say it's malfunctioning. Malfunction seems to be the word that drags history in whether I like it or not.
5. Feathers. They arose for insulation or display or whatever. In a pigeon, flight is a genuine function of its feathers — not a lucky side effect — even though nothing to do with flight was around when feathers first appeared.
6. Same with the panda's wrist bone that turned into a thumb: I'd say its function is stripping bamboo leaves.
7. The first heart ever, in whatever fishlike thing first grew one: I'd say it had a function the moment it appeared. No selection history behind it yet, but the intuition is strong.
8. A brand-new mutation in a flask of bacteria that confers antibiotic resistance — one copy, no generations of selection: conferring resistance is its function, I think.
9. A construct I splice into a plant with CRISPR so it glows: the function of that insert is to make it glow, obviously, and selection never touched it. Design by an agent seems to do the same work natural selection does.
10. A moth's ultrasonic escape reflex, in a lab line kept for two hundred generations with no bats anywhere: I still want to say predator evasion is its function, but it feels weirdly detached, like a word nobody uses anymore that's still in the dictionary.
11. The seedless cultivated banana. The flesh is there because it evolved to get animals to eat it and carry seeds away, and in the seedless varieties nobody disperses anything. Half of me says the flesh's function is still to attract dispersers; the other half says its function now is pleasing humans, which sounds absurd to say about a plant part.
12. The human appendix: not a digestive organ anymore, and I won't say digestion is its function. But when I read that it does something with repopulating gut bacteria I'm perfectly happy to say it has that function, and that doesn't feel like a stretch at all.

Heres the trouble. Case 4 says a function survives the complete absence of any current causal contribution. Cases 5, 7, 8 and 9 say a function survives the complete absence of any selection history. Take both seriously and function looks like "either history or causal role is enough" — but that immediately makes case 2 wrong, since heart sounds have a causal role and no history, and I'm certain heart sounds aren't a function. So my verdicts look inconsistent, or like they need something finer-grained than either camp is offering.

Is there a principle — one account, or a disciplined two-senses view, or whatever you think is intellectually honest here — that gets all of these, or gets as many as it can and then tells me plainly which ones I have to give up and why? I'd especially like to know what it says about the banana, which is the one I keep flip-flopping on, and about a case I didn't list: a duplicated gene where one copy keeps doing the old job and the other drifts for a long time and then gets recruited for something new. Which copy has which function, and at what point did it change? If your account has to say the answer is indeterminate, or that there are two functions in play at once, say so and tell me what I should type into the database column.

Please write it all as continuous prose, the way you'd write an email or a letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. I know that's awkward with a dozen cases, so just run them together in sentences and point to them by number or by description. And in American English, please.
