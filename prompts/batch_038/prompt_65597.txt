I'm two years into a philosophy PhD and I've just been handed the temporal-experience week of our consciousness seminar, which mostly means I have to explain the color phi argument, and I've ended up confused about what the argument is even arguing about. Setup as I understand it: you flash a red dot on the left and a couple hundred milliseconds later a green dot on the right, and people don't report two flashes, they report one dot travelling across and abruptly changing colour somewhere in the middle — apparently the whole thing got started because Goodman asked what a colour change would do to phi. Dennett and Kinsbourne's two models are supposed to make exactly the same predictions about that report. Orwellian: the dot really is experienced sitting there being red, and then the memory of it gets edited afterwards once the green flash lands, so the subject's report is a rewrite. Stalinesque: nothing gets to be "officially" experienced until after the second flash has arrived, so the already-edited version is what the subject saw and the report is honest. And the punchline is that no experiment could tell them apart, which its supposed to sink the idea that there's a single moment or place where it all comes together.

My working hunch, which I can't defend yet, is that the two sides aren't disagreeing about brains at all — they're disagreeing about whether a question like "what was the content of consciousness at t=250ms?" is even well posed, and the whole thing looks like an empirical stalemate only because both sides talk as if experiences come with timestamps. One of the other students pushed back hard and said the difference IS observable, because with backward masking you get effects that don't go through report — priming, forced-choice discrimination above chance on stimuli people insist they didn't see — and the two models should disagree about when those switch on, so you could settle it with a masked version of the color-phi trial instead of asking anybody what they saw. I don't know whether that's a real wedge or just relocates the problem onto a different word, "unconscious processing" instead of "experience."

So: diagnose the disagreement, don't adjudicate phi. Is it verbal — about "experience," or about what it means for something to happen "at" a time — or is there a live empirical or formal question hiding inside it? Are the two models answering different questions under one heading, one about the timing of neural processing and one about the authority of the subject's report? Is the impasse a sign the question is ill-posed, or just that it's hard and everyone's being impatient? And take the masking objection seriously rather than waving it off, since I have to answer that person in the seminar.
