Consider how a close election is actually settled. When the margin is razor-thin, the recounting body does not re-run the arithmetic on the machines' totals; it convenes a board that examines the disputed ballots one at a time and asks, of each, whether the voter's intention can be discerned from the mark on the paper — a hanging fragment, a dent with no perforation, a stray line. A ballot whose intention the board can read is counted for the candidate it reads; a ballot whose intention it cannot read is set aside and counts for nobody, receiving no partial or fractional weight. The official total is then built up entirely from these individual dispositions, and the idea of settling the contest by examining a random sample of the ballots and extrapolating to the whole is treated as a kind of cheating, an evasion of the demand that every ballot be adjudicated. Yet the very same electoral authorities, in the ordinary course, do exactly that when auditing an election: they draw a random sample of paper ballots, fix in advance an acceptable risk of failing to notice a wrong outcome, and stop inspecting the moment the sample provides enough statistical assurance that the reported winner really won. And when the count comes out precisely even, many systems resolve it by lot — a coin toss, a name pulled from a container. Over all of this sits a hard deadline: a recount completed an hour after certification is worth nothing, whatever it would have found.

I am not asking whether this is sound election policy. I want to know what it presupposes about vagueness, because the bundle looks to me like it contains commitments that cannot all be true at once.

Please address the following:

- Does the ballot-by-ballot procedure presuppose that there is a determinate fact about each individual ballot's meaning — a sharp but possibly unknowable boundary between the ballots that express an intention and those that do not — which the board is trying to track? Or is it better read as an exercise in stipulation, where the board's decision itself makes the standard precise for this election and there was no prior fact to track? Or as something involving degrees, with the board simply drawing a line where the degree passes a threshold?
- What explains the refusal to allocate partial weight to unreadable ballots? Is that a substantive commitment to there being no such thing as a half-expressed intention, or a concession to administrability that sits uneasily beside the first commitment?
- Why is sampling an insult when it determines the winner and a best practice when it checks the winner? Tell me whether that is a difference in underlying epistemology — the aggregate as constituted by its particulars versus the aggregate as an estimable quantity — or one epistemology serving two different institutional purposes.
- What does the tie broken by chance concede? That at some point there is no fact of the matter, that any of several outcomes would be equally legitimate, or only that a decision procedure must terminate?
- Does the deadline show that what the practice is really tracking is not truth about who got more votes but the existence of a settled answer, and can a practice value both without ranking them?
- Is there a sorites problem here at all? One more ambiguous ballot cannot make the difference between a legitimate and an illegitimate outcome, yet a few hundred of them plainly can. Say what kind of problem that is and which account of vagueness can absorb it.
- Now suppose the number of genuinely borderline ballots exceeds the margin of victory. Does the practice have any answer? Walk me through what each of the accounts above says about who won that election, and whether any of them yields something a losing candidate could be told without insult.
- Finally: what would have to change institutionally if the presupposition were replaced? Describe concretely what a recount built on degrees rather than thresholds would look like, and whether it collapses into absurdity or is a design we are merely unwilling to adopt.

Throughout, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably with, and flag the places where several incompatible theories would license the same procedure, so that the procedure proves less than it appears to.
