I design replicated state machines for a living: consensus protocols, leases and fencing tokens, quorum-gated reconfiguration, the safety-versus-liveness trade-off, Byzantine fault tolerance, and the impossibility results that tell me which of these cannot be had at once. My philosophy training is a few months of desultory reading.

The material I have been trying to read is the precommitment debate. Odysseus lashed to the mast, and his order to the crew not to release him whatever he says or promises once the Sirens are in earshot; Elster's use of that episode as the paradigm case of self-binding; the extension of the same idea to constitutional design, in which a founding generation entrenches rules, raises the threshold for amendment, and sometimes declares certain provisions unamendable outright; and the objection that all of this is the dead hand of the past governing the living. Running underneath is the question of which self speaks with authority — the earlier self that binds, or the later self that is bound and may have better information, different preferences, or a clearer view of the situation — plus a further class of devices that bind not by constraining action but by shaping desire, so that the later self does not want release at all.

I want that debate rendered in my own working vocabulary, as faithfully as it will bear.

**Part one: the translation.** For each of the following, give me the closest counterpart in the language of distributed systems and an explicit fidelity verdict — clean mapping, strained mapping, or nothing in my vocabulary to map it onto — together with an account of what the counterpart carries and what it drops. The items: the precommitment device itself; the standing order not to release; the crew; the two selves and the dispute over whose judgment governs; entrenchment and the raised amendment threshold; unamendable provisions; the dead-hand objection; the charge that binding forfeits responsiveness to new information; and desire-shaping self-binding, where no constraint is ever applied.

I have a working hypothesis I want tested rather than flattered: that the order to the crew is a fencing token — a device that renders the requests of a possibly corrupted component safely ignorable — and that the dead-hand objection is merely the observation that a stale configuration which blocks liveness must eventually be revisable by a quorum of healthy nodes. On that reading, the philosophers' puzzle about which self has authority is not a puzzle but a design question about who holds the amendment quorum. Tell me where this holds, where it is only suggestive analogy dressed as structure, and where it breaks.

**Part two: the residue.** List what did not survive the translation, separating claims that survive with distortion from claims my vocabulary cannot express at all. Then audit my own framework for covert normative work: that the objective is fixed by an owner standing outside the system; that safety dominates liveness as a matter of course; that a component requesting release is malfunctioning by definition; that a fault is a deviation to be tolerated rather than a possible source of information. Note anywhere the philosophy looks clearer than my engineering once these are exposed.

**Part three: the verdict.** Does the residue indict the original debate — is the two-selves question a confused way of asking who controls reconfiguration? — or does it indict my vocabulary? Address one case in particular: can my language express the possibility that the bound component sees clearly and the binder was wrong, and that honoring the earlier configuration is the error? If it cannot, say whether that is a gap worth closing or a commitment I should not be willing to surrender.

Two constraints. No references, reading lists, or suggested further reading — I want the translation itself, and I will not be looking anything up. And do not smuggle philosophical conclusions into the engineering vocabulary and then report them to me as what the engineering says; where a correspondence is metaphorical rather than structural, label it as such.
