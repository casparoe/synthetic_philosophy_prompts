I just spent two hours arguing with a friend about this and neither of us moved, so I'm outsourcing it. I sit on the standards-and-appeals committee at a state benefits agency. We make about 40,000 eligibility determinations a year off a scoring model, and each one is right roughly 97% of the time given the evidence we have — everyone agrees that's plenty to act on. My friend says our annual report should therefore just say the determinations are correct, because withholding from any individual one would be irrational. I say we can't say that: across 40,000 cases we're effectively guaranteed around 1,200 people wrongly turned down, so asserting the whole set is asserting something we know is false. Then he sprung the trap I couldn't answer — if we can't assert the set and we can't withhold from the parts, what exactly are we supposed to write?

Draft the memo I'll circulate. It needs a recommendation on the report's wording, and on whether we stand up some kind of aggregate correction process (sampling, a reopener window, whatever you'd pick), with thresholds we could actually implement. Deal honestly with his side too: an agency that hedges every single determination is no use to the people waiting on it. Two wrinkles — the errors aren't randomly spread, they cluster where documentation is thinest, i.e. exactly the claimants least able to appeal; and our appeal review is only about as reliable as the model, so a straight appeals route mostly just reshuffles who gets it wrong.
