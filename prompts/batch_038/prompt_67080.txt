I am developing an account of when a higher-level phenomenon counts as genuinely emergent rather than merely a complicated consequence of lower-level laws, and I have accumulated a set of case verdicts that I now want pressure-tested rather than defended. I give the verdicts first, then a tentative criterion of my own, then what I would like from you.

My verdicts:

1. The temperature of a cup of coffee, recovered from the statistical mechanics of its molecules: reducible, and nothing of value is lost in the reduction.
2. The irreversibility of macrodynamics from time-reversal-invariant microdynamics: not emergent. It is a matter of special boundary conditions plus coarse-graining, not of new being.
3. Continuous phase transitions, and the sharing of critical exponents by systems with utterly different microphysics — a fluid near its critical point and a uniaxial ferromagnet landing in the same universality class: emergent. That the transition is sharp only in the infinite-particle limit does not disqualify it; the idealization seems to me part of what is being explained rather than a defect in the explanation.
4. The fractionally charged quasiparticles of the fractional quantum Hall regime: emergent in the strongest sense I recognize. Here we get new entities, not merely new descriptions, and their charge is a fraction of a charge that is otherwise indivisible.
5. Phonons and stable vortices, on the one hand, and a galaxy's center of mass, on the other: I am inclined to call the first two emergent entities by the same reasoning that makes 4 emergent, and to deny it of the third, but I cannot articulate what separates them.
6. The deterministic replicator dynamics of evolutionary game theory, which is exact only in the infinite-population limit — at any finite population size drift eventually fixes one strategy, so the deterministic flow is literally false of every real population: not emergent. A serviceable idealization and nothing more. Note the tension with 3, which I want treated in the opposite way despite also existing only at a limit.
7. Pain and other multiply realizable psychological kinds: explanatorily autonomous, but not emergent in any sense that confers new causal powers. The realizer does the causal work, and I accept no downward causation here.
8. A hurricane: I do want to say that the hurricane constrains the trajectories of the air molecules composing it, and that this is a real higher-level causal influence rather than a manner of speaking. Note the tension with 7.
9. Reynolds-style flocking: everything about the collective motion follows from the local rules, I can watch it happen on a laptop in seconds, and there is no idealization anywhere. Not emergent — merely complicated.
10. A universal cellular automaton, whether Rule 110 or the Game of Life: every later configuration is a deductive consequence of the initial one, with no limit and no idealization involved, and yet I want to call the macro-behavior emergent, on the grounds that the only route from the rules to the outcome is to run the rules. Computational irreducibility looks to me like a genuine species of emergence rather than a limitation of my hardware.
11. The structure of the periodic table: it is not recoverable from the Schrödinger equation without shell-filling rules of order that are not themselves recoverable from it, and yet I refuse to call chemistry emergent. The gap in the derivation strikes me as a gap in our mathematics, not in the world.
12. The derivation of the Navier–Stokes equations from the Boltzmann equation in the Boltzmann–Grad limit, where the assumption of molecular chaos looks suspiciously like a restatement of the irreversibility one set out to derive: this is the case I keep going back and forth on. The derivation is real and hard-won, but if it does smuggle in its conclusion, I cannot tell whether fluid dynamics has been reduced or revealed as emergent.

My tentative criterion. Call a property E of a system S emergent relative to a lower-level theory T just in case: (i) E supervenes on T's ontology, since I am no dualist about any of this; (ii) no finite system governed by T exhibits an analogue of E, so that E exists only at a limit of T; and (iii) the best explanation of E is invariant under substantial changes to the T-level details. Call it the limit-plus-invariance criterion.

What I want from you:

Test the criterion against all twelve verdicts, clause by clause, reporting which verdicts it delivers, which it delivers for the wrong reason, and which it flatly contradicts. I already suspect 4, 5, the 9/10 pair, and 11 are where it breaks.

Then say whether it can be repaired, and if so by what clause, with each clause motivated by the case that requires it. Two questions matter most to me here. First, is there a principled epistemic clause that makes 10 emergent without making 9 emergent as well, or must I abandon one of those verdicts — and if the distinction turns out to be a matter of degree in compressibility, does that make it philosophically idle? Second, is there a principled difference between the infinite limit in 3 and the infinite limit in 6, or must they go the same way; and if they must, which of the two verdicts do I lose, and what does the loss cost me elsewhere in the list?

Finally, state the criterion or criteria you settle on precisely enough to be applied by someone else, and tell me what they imply for cases I have not raised: spontaneous symmetry breaking in a ferromagnet whose grain is small enough that no ordering direction is stable; the proposal that spacetime itself is emergent from some underlying non-spatiotemporal structure; the price level of a market, which no agent represents; and a hypothetical material whose bulk modulus is provably undefined for every finite N and yet is measured to six significant figures in ordinary-sized samples. Where the criterion you favor cannot accommodate one of my listed verdicts, say so plainly instead of bending the criterion to save it.
