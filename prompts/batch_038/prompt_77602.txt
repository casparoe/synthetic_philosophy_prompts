Okay so I've been chewing on Goodman's grue for years — the *Fact, Fiction, and Forecast* line, not the earlier stuff — and I've landed somewhere I want stress-tested. My view: grue isn't actually a paradox about induction at all, its basically a demonstration that you have to pick your predicates before you can do induction, plus one contingent fact about us. The fact is that "green" is entrenched and grue isn't, and that asymmetry does all the work. Once you say that, there's no leftover mystery: induction isn't threatened, we've just learned something mildly deflationary about how language hooks onto the world. I think the people who treat grue as showing induction is unsolvable are doing something like mistaking a fact about their own vocabulary for a fact about the universe.

What would count against *that*? I want the exposure map, not a refresher on the new riddle.

Some things I've been circling that I suspect are the real pressure points — use them, ignore them, or tell me they're bad:

- The entrenchment story is a claim about token frequencies in our language, not about the world. So it should be possible for the gerrymandered predicate to be the one that projects. Concretely: a classifier trained entirely on pigment samples from before a supplier reformulated, where the learned feature is extensionally grue-like over the training set and predicts correctly for everything anyone actually tests, because nobody ever tests new stock. Entrenchment says "green," the local track record says otherwise. Does that case bite, or would a defender say I've just changed the subject?
- Color science itself. Metamerism means two surfaces match under one illuminant and not another, so the "clean" predicate is always indexed to an observer and a light source — structurally the same move grue makes with time. If the predicate actually doing the work in colorimetry is response-dependent, is "green" still the un-gerrymandered one?
- The historical drift worry: the claims that color vocabularies in older languages carve the spectrum differently and that a stable word for blue shows up late. If our color terms' extensions have drifted for reasons that obviously aren't truth-tracking, does the entrenchment reply explain why we say green while *conceding* the asymmetry is local and contingent? Is that a cost or a feature for me?
