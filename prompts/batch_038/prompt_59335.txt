The version we discussed claims that a very large population of lives that are barely worth living need not be worse than a much smaller population of excellent lives, on the grounds that one can pass from the second to the first by a chain of small changes such that no step in the chain is worse than the step before it. One member of our group proposed the obvious-looking fix — deny that the relation doing the work in the chain, whether it is "better than" or "at least as good as," is transitive — and a college student who coaches our debate team shut this down in a single line: "If betterness isn't transitive, you can be money-pumped; that's just a Dutch book, and nobody wants to be exploitable." He said it as though the discussion were over, and most of the group agreed with him, but I suspect he may have been changing the subject rather than answering the argument, and I cannot articulate why.

For what it is worth, I am inclined to think the pump is at most a claim about decision procedures rather than about value, but I do not yet trust that intuition.
