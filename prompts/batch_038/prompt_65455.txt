I am preparing a twenty-minute talk for a workshop on personal identity and the extended mind, and before I commit the view to slides I would like a rigorous consistency audit of the position I intend to defend. The starting point is Hofstadter's strange-loop account of the self (I Am a Strange Loop, 2007), but the framing, the ordering of cases, and the numbered commitments below are my own, and I have not tried to keep them within what Hofstadter himself says. Here is the package:

1. A person is a self-referential pattern — a symbol system that builds, revises, and acts on a model of itself — and nothing over and above that. I am a physicalist about all of this: no immaterial soul, no nonphysical residue. Because the pattern is what the person is, and not the stuff realizing it, a person is not in principle tied to a single brain.

2. Distribution is actual, not merely possible. The people who know me well run working models of me: models that predict my behavior, that can be consulted before I am asked, that get surprised. I take those to be partial instantiations of my pattern rather than representations of it, and I take it that I am, right now, distributed across a handful of skulls at differing degrees of fidelity.

3. I mean this literally and not as a manner of speaking. When my mentor died, some part of me was destroyed — an actual reduction in the extent of my pattern, not a change in how I or anyone else feels. Symmetrically, my own death will not annihilate me all at once: I will persist, and fade unevenly, in those who carry me.

4. The ordering among carriers is a real ordering by fidelity to the pattern. My ranking is this: someone who knew my mentor intimately carries more of him than a careful biography does; a biography carries more than a novel loosely based on him; a novel carries more than a photograph; a photograph carries more than a horoscope. I do not think this collapses into a ranking of how much information is present.

5. A person is one center of perspective. Two loops running are two people, however similar. I have no use for branch identity, for the claim that both of two duplicates are the original, or for me-ness coming in degrees when rival candidates compete for the same person. I am also not a Parfitian: what matters is that the very same loop continues, not that someone psychologically continuous with it does — and none of this gives me any reason to look forward to dying.

6. My self-model is constitutively privileged, not merely better informed. What I take myself to be partly settles what I am; other people's models of me are answerable to mine, and where they diverge from it the divergence is a defect in the copy rather than a fact about me. If your model has me as generous and I know I am not, your model is simply wrong.

7. There are nonetheless regions of me to which I have no access at all, and to which my self-model contributes nothing: the child I was at seven, which survives, if anywhere, in my sister's model of me and in no one's memory including my own; the sound of my own voice; my face in motion; the person I am with people who are dead. When I ask my sister what I was like at seven, I treat her answer as evidence about me and not as evidence about her.

I am not asking for an exposition of Hofstadter, a rehearsal of the standing objections to the extended mind, or a survey of the personal identity literature — I have read all of that and can recite it. I want you to work through my list and tell me which of these commitments pull against each other, which pairs only look as though they do, and what the cheapest route back to coherence is. Assume that I do not know where the problem lies, and do not soften the exercise by treating every pair as equally worrisome or by reporting that the package needs more nuance.

Please answer in the following sections, in this order:

- **Bottom line** (about 100 words): whether the package is tenable as stated and, if it is not, which single commitment is doing the most damage.
- **The audit** (roughly 900 words): the genuine tensions, ordered from most to least severe. For each, name the pair by number, give the mechanism in two or three sentences — the specific step at which the two cannot both hold — and then say precisely what would have to give, together with what surrendering it costs me elsewhere in the list.
- **False alarms** (about 200 words): pairs that look like tensions and are not, with the distinction that dissolves each one stated explicitly rather than gestured at.
- **Repair options** (about 250 words): the two or three least costly consistent revisions of the whole package, ranked by cost, and your judgment on which single revision buys the most coherence per unit of damage.
- **Cross-examination** (about 150 words): three questions a hostile but fair commentator would put to me in the Q&A, in the order in which I would most dread answering them, each with one line on why that question is hard for this package in particular rather than for any view like it.
