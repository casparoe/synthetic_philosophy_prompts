My advisor is firmly in the moralized camp and for weeks I've been trying to build a case that embarrasses her account without anything melodramatic in it, but everything I draft turns into either a hostage scenario or a sweatshop, and she bats both away by saying I've changed the subject from consent to background justice. My partner used to run a distribution floor so I can sanity check realism, which is why I want the setting boring. Please use these two labels as I define them here, not whatever cousins of them you know. The ENTITLEMENT account: a proposal invalidates consent only if it is wrongful — it threatens to leave the person worse off than she's entitled to be, or it withholds/dangles something the proposer had no business controlling that way; if every element of the proposal sits inside the proposer's rights and duties, the consent stands no matter how few real choices she had. The OPTIONS account: consent is invalid whenever the person has no acceptable alternative to going along, whether or not anyone did anything wrong — what matters is the shape of her option set at the moment of signing, not the moral quality of the proposal. In both cases hold the other conditions fixed and satisfied: competent adult, told the truth about everything material, no lies, no omissions, no drugs, no time pressure, nobody's holding anything over her head in the dark.
