Reflection keeps coming up in a seminar I'm in and I can't tell what the disagreement actually is. The bookies say violate it and your pumpable across time, so it's a coherence requirement. The others say those books are worthless — they assume no forgetting, no drugs, no unanticipated evidence — and both sides happily agree that at 3am tonight, wrecked, I'll be certain I'm a better philosopher than I am, with nothing wrong in not believing that now. So the fight migrates into which weakened cousin gets to keep the name. Same theorem, same counterexamples, nobody moves.

The case I threw at them that both camps claimed: an agent knows a manipulator will implant a memory tonight making them believe P, and P is in fact true. Defer, says one side; you can't defer to a belief you expect to be caused that way even when it lands on truth, says the other. That felt less like a fight about Reflection than about whether justification tracks expected accuracy or the pedigree of a belief.

My hunch is the real fault line is who carries a credence — a momentary stage or a temporally extended agent — so the theorem and the counterexamples aren't even about the same object. Is that it, or accuracy vs pedigree, or just words? Diagnose it from what each side actually argues, then tell me what would count as progress: a distinction, a theorem, a case both accept, or nothing and why.
