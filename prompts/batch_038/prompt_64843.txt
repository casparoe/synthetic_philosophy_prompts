so I'm in a grad reading group on reasons and everyone had to write down their actual position and hand it over for attack. Here's mine, about reasons for action specifically:

1. Williams is right that A has a reason to Φ only if A could get to the conclusion that she should Φ by a sound deliberative route from her actual subjective motivational set S — and I read that strictly. No smuggeling in a fully informed and perfectly rational ideal self.
2. Even so, the case in Williams's own paper — the guy who wants a drink, believes the glass in front of him has gin in it, but it's actually petrol — the guy has no reason to drink it, and when I say that I don't think I'm reaching for some other sense of "reason."
3. When we tell the man who walked past the drowning kid that he had a reason to stop, I think that is literally true. Not a useful fiction, not a report of our own attitudes.
4. But I also don't think blame requires the target to have had a reason. Criticizing someone works by putting pressure on his S, getting him to care about something he didn't, and that's a thoroughly internal business.
5. Reasons for belief are a different animal — evidence settles what you ought to believe and no desire or pro-attitude is needed anywhere, so I don't think internalism about belief is even a coherent thesis.
6. The deliberation constraint is what's doing the real work for me: a consideration that could never figure in anyone's deliberation is not a reason for them, period.
7. And whether there are, say, aesthetic reasons or prudential reasons is a substantive question, not one settled by the meaning of "has a reason."

Can you audit this for me? I want to know which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which ones only look like they do. Rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each one tell me the cheapest fix, what the fix costs me, and — this is the part I care about most — why the two commitments seemed compatible to me in the first place, including anywhere I'm leaning on how elastic that deliberative-route phrase is without noticing. Then say which single revision would clean up the most damage, and whether making it turns me into an externalist who won't admit it or leaves me with a thesis too thin to do the work in (6). I know Korsgaard-style collapse worries are in the neighborhood but I can't tell which of my commitments actually triggers one.
