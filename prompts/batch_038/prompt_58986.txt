Consider the following claim, in the spirit of the transformative-experience argument developed by L. A. Paul: for certain major life decisions — having a child, undergoing a religious conversion, emigrating — the standard expected-utility model of rational choice is not merely difficult to apply but inapplicable in principle, because the choice is both epistemically transformative (you cannot know what the resulting experience will be like until you have had it) and personally transformative (your core preferences and evaluative standpoint will themselves be reshaped), so that there is no antecedently accessible assignment of subjective values over outcomes for the model to take as input.

Give me your credence in that claim as a single number, and show me the arithmetic that produces it. At minimum, decompose it into: the probability that some decision-theoretic framework requiring antecedent knowledge of outcome-value is the correct account of rational choice in such cases rather than a convenient idealization; the probability that the two kinds of transformation are genuinely inseparable in the decisions at issue rather than merely co-occurring in them; and the probability that testimony, imagination, or the value one places on discovery itself can supply the missing input. One line of justification per term, then the combination.

Now a control case, to locate the load-bearing premise. Suppose a procedure that confers a wholly new sensory capacity — you will come to know what it is like to perceive in a way you cannot presently imagine — but that leaves your character, commitments, and evaluative standpoint exactly as they are, and for which the reports of those who have undergone it are unusually rich, internally consistent, and independently corroborated. What is your credence that rational choice in the expected-utility sense remains impossible here? Report the gap between this figure and your first one, and say what that gap implies about whether the argument's force comes from the epistemic premise, the personal premise, or their interaction. Close by naming the single consideration that would move your overall number furthest, and the direction it would move it.
