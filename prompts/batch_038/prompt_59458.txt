Forty-odd years I spent in an analytical chemistry lab, and most of it, honestly, was keeping instruments honest. The rule that never budged is that you can't calibrate a device against itself. You need a standard that sits outside it — a reference weight, a certified solution — and the second you start using the readings to check the readings you've got a circle, not a measurement. Since I retired I've been reading a lot to fill the hours. Hofstadter first, I'm a sucker for that book, and then down a rabbit hole into people arguing about self-modifying agents and something called the Löbian obstacle: the idea that an agent which wants to rewrite itself first has to be able to trust what its own reasoning will conclude, and that a system careful enough to insist on a proof of that can't supply one, because that would amount to proving its own reasoning sound from the inside.

What I want is a pantoum about exactly that jam. Not about Gödel generally, and not a doom poem about AI — about the moment the agent tries to certify its future self and the certificate has to be issued by the thing being certified.

The form, since I want it to actually hold: quatrains, with the second and fourth lines of each stanza returning as the first and third lines of the next, so the poem keeps quoting itself. Six to eight stanzas. Close it the way a pantoum should — the final stanza's second and fourth lines are the opening stanza's first and third, so the loop shuts on itself. I care more about the recursion being right than about the length, because the repeats are the argument, not decoration.

Here's what I want the structure to do. The recycled lines should change meaning each time they come back: the first time through they read as a confident proof-step, and by the time they return the same words are the thing that can't be certified. The reader should end up understanding that the trap isn't "never improve yourself" — it's that demanding proof-grade certainty about your own next thought is what jams the works, and that any way out has to come from loosening that demand, or from keeping a standard outside the system. Someone who works on this should nod at the diagnosis rather than wince, and someone in my book group who has never heard of Löb should just get a good poem about a mind that can't step outside itself.

You're welcome to borrow my habits for the imagery — reference weights, a balance that can only weigh itself, a drift you can't detect from inside — but don't spell the analogy out or turn it into a lecture, and plain language is fine, no need for fancy rhyming. Oh, and don't soften the ending into a solution that isn't actually thier; if the poem can't close the loop, let it show the loop not closing.
