I teach philosophy of mind, but I came to the subject by way of a doctorate in constitutional law, and I still run a seminar each year on amendment theory. The vocabulary in which I think most fluently about change over time is therefore not metaphysical but doctrinal: constituent power as against constituted power, entrenchment and the eternity clause of Article 79(3) of the German Basic Law, the Indian basic-structure line of authority descending from Kesavananda Bharati, unconstitutional constitutional amendments, constitutional identity, the distinction between piecemeal revision and refounding, the office that survives every occupant of it, and the fiction by which the Crown is a corporation sole that never dies. I want to know what the gradual-replacement argument for survival under mind uploading looks like when it is restated in that vocabulary.

The argument to be translated, stated as its proponents state it: replace a subject's neurons one at a time with synthetic counterparts that are functionally identical to what they displace, so that at no stage is there an interruption of causal or functional organization; because there is no non-arbitrary stage at which to say the subject has ceased to exist, and because the final system preserves the functional organization of the original, the person survives the transition, and the same conclusion is taken to carry over to the scanning-and-instantiation version of uploading. Functional continuity of the right kind is doing all the work.

Please give me the following, in order:

1. A premise-by-premise translation. For each load-bearing element of the argument — the stepwise character of the change, the absence of any moment of death, the preservation of functional organization, the sufficiency of that preservation for the survival of the subject, the claim that substrate makes no difference, and the inference to the scanning case — name the closest counterpart in amendment doctrine and render the claim in doctrinal terms. Label each entry as translating cleanly, translating only with distortion, or having no counterpart at all, and say in one or two sentences why.

2. An explicit accounting of the residue. What in the uploading argument cannot be said in the language of amendment, entrenchment, and constitutional identity? Then give me your diagnosis: is the failure a defect in the constitutional vocabulary, which speaks of validity and authority and has no resources for experience, or is it evidence that the original argument was borrowing its plausibility from somewhere other than metaphysics — that when we say the subject survives we may be saying something closer to which order of rules persists?

3. A check on smuggling. Tell me every point at which the translation depends on assumptions native to constitutional theory rather than to the argument being translated: that identity is settled by an authoritative interpreter rather than by the facts, that the persisting thing is a normative order rather than an organism, that there is a people capable of binding itself and of being wronged by its own transformation. I want the stretches flagged, not papered over.

4. Two adversarial test cases in which the frameworks deliver opposite verdicts. Take the rollback scenario — the biological brain restored after gradual replacement — against the doctrinal treatment of a repeal that reinstates earlier text after intervening amendments, and tell me whether the analogy predicts the return of the original subject or something else. Then take the sufficiency claim itself: amendment doctrine is content with preserved institutional function, while the skeptic about uploading holds that function preserved is entirely compatible with experience lost. Say which of these two divergences shows the analogy is load-bearing and which shows it is idle.

5. Finally, a note in the other direction: is there a distinction in amendment doctrine — unamendability, basic structure, the difference between a valid act and one that destroys what validates it — that the uploading debate lacks and could profit from, and would importing it resolve the dispute or dissolve the question?

Structure the answer under those five headings and keep it between 1,200 and 1,800 words. Do not give me citations, a reading list, or quotations from either body of literature; I know both well and the value here lies entirely in the mapping and in what the mapping refuses to carry.
