I'm one of two external members on a national museum's collections committee, and Thursday we vote on a case thats split us badly. I need a short written opinion I can circulate Wednesday night — something the committee could actually act on, not background.

The facts. We hold a marble figure group, accessioned 1911, bought from a small parish after a fire. It was badly damaged in 1944 when the roof of the store came in. Between 1947 and the 1990s the conservation department ran repeated campaigns: every lost section was replaced with new stone from a different quarry, pinned and consolidated, all of it documented. By the conservators own estimate about 65% of the marble now in the gallery object is post-1944, including the entire head, which was recarved in 1962 from a plaster squeeze and old photographs. Meanwhile nothing was ever thrown away — the original fragments, including the head in maybe a dozen pieces, went into the study store. In 2019 a graduate seminar reassembled as many of them as would physically hold together, with armature and fills, and that second object is now catalogued as a "study assembly," roughly 40% original material.

Three things land at once. The parish's diocese has asked for the group back for the church's 900th anniversary in two years, under our policy on objects whose removal from a community of origin now looks inequitable. Our insurer wants us to name one insured object and one value. And a curator wants to show both in an exhibition about restoration. So the committee has to decide what the 1911 accession number actually attaches to.

Where everyone sits: our head of conservation says the gallery object is the work, because it carries the unbroken chain of public display and the interventions are themselves now history — you don't get to amputate 1947 to 1990. The registrar says registration has always tracked original material, so on that basis the study assembly is the real one and the gallery object is a very well-documented copy. Our curator says the question is malformed and both are phases of one thing. And the diocese's representative told me, helpfully, that the parish has never cared about the stone — what they want is the same figure, re-blessed and carried in the procession again — which seems to cut against the registrar but doesn't get us to an answer either.

One of the other external members keeps saying it's just the ship of Theseus and we should split the difference. Please don't.

Draft the memo: a recommendation the committee can vote on — what we transfer, what we keep, what gets recorded under which number, and on what timetable given the anniversary — plus the criterion you think we should adopt for cases like this going forward, and whether a percentage-of-original-material threshold can or can't be it. Take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously; whichever way you come out, at least one of those three positions is not stupid and the memo should show that you know exactly why. And tell me what the register entry and the wall label should actually read. No reading list — I need the document, not the bibliography behind it.
