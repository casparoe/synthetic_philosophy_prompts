I'm a second-year in philosophy of mind and I've been handed one session of our department's joint reading group with the vision lab — half the room actually ran these experiments and is deeply sceptical that any of it is real penetration. So I want to build a decision tree that starts from a commitment everyone in the room can share and walks you to a named position, and I want it built out of the logical space rather than out of a list of people.

Starting node: you accept that there are lab effects where what a subject knows, expects, wants, or is expert at changes what they report about a simple visual property — colour, size, slant, orientation — and that at least some of these survive the obvious controls. That's the common ground. From there each branch should be one question that genuinely splits the positions, phrased so somebody can answer it without knowing which leaf they're heading toward. The splits I care about, roughly in this order:

- is the effect synchronic (a current top-down state changes current experience) or only diachronic (learning and expertise have reshaped the module over time)? a lot of people happily grant the second and deny the first, so that needs to be a real fork and not a footnote
- does the effect survive when attention is matched or controlled, or is attention-mediation doing all the work?
- does the influencing state have content that's assessable for correctness against what's perceived, or is it a bare arousal/valence push? I want the tree to seperate penetration-by-belief from penetration-by-mood, since those get lumped together constantly in our reading group
- is the effect visible in anything other than the subject's verbal report, and if not, what work is the report/experience distinction actually doing at that node?
- then a second stage after the metaphysics: what does your leaf imply about whether perception still confers non-inferential justification, and about whether observation in the philosophy-of-science sense is theory-laden?

Leaves I want to end up at: strict encapsulation, penetration-only-via-attention (the deflationary one), graded penetration in special architectures, the everything-is-top-down view on which the question dissolves, real penetration that's epistemically harmless, plus whatever else falls out of your branches. Also give me at least two answer-combinations the tree generates that nobody occupies, and for each say whether it's empty becuase it's incoherent, because it's unattractive, or because nobody's bothered yet. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually distinguish, and — this is the part I most want — tell me which of your nodes stop making sense if you don't already accept a sharp perception/cognition boundary. I suspect the whole instrument begs the question against the dissolve-the-boundary leaf and I'd rather print that caveat on the handout than have the experimentalists point it out to me in front of everyone.

Six or seven branch nodes, seven to nine leaves, fits on one page, questions plus a sentence or two per leaf, no essays. And don't name any studies or cite anything: I'll bring the papers, the tree has to stand on the questions alone.
