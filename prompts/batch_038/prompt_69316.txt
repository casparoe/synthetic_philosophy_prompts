ok so I'm going back and forth on dirty hands in politics and I want a dialogue that actually grinds on one case instead of rehearsing the standard debate.

Here's the case, which both characters know cold: during a record flood a city's flood-control chief has maybe ten minutes to decide whether to open an emergency spillway. Opening it saves a town of forty thousand but sends the water through a farmhouse where three people are trapped with no time to get them out. He opens it. The twist is what happens after: he feels nothing. No guilt, no bad dreams, no sense of debt to the family beyond the compensation check the city sends. Years later he says it wasnt a close call, he'd do it again immediately, and that dirty hands is just language for people who want credit for hurting.

Two characters. W thinks dirty hands picks out something real: that a politically right act can leave a genuine moral remainder, and that the guilt is tracking something rather than being a mood. S is a skeptic: once you've described the conflict of duties and the consequences there's nothing left over, so the vocabulary is really an alibi that lets officials look burdened while claiming they did the right thing.

Rules for the exchange:
- S opens, and W has to deal with that opening rather then dodge it.
- W has to say whether the dirt is in the act or in the psychology of the agent. If it's in the act, W owes some account of what's wrong over and above the balance of reasons. If it's in the psychology, S should press hard that the category has collapsed into virtue talk and stopped being about dirty hands at all.
- S has to say what S's own view says the chief should feel and why, without letting "he should feel guilty" quietly smuggle the residue back in.
- Neither of them gets to just say "it's tragic" and stop there. No name-dropping and no citing anything: argue the case on its own terms.
- Both are smart and neither is a strawman, and every turn has to answer the actual point just made. By exchange five somebody concedes something that genuinely costs them.

What I'm after is whether the remainder lives in the act or in the agent, so make that the fault line the dialogue runs along.

Format: exactly five exchanges, numbered 1 through 5, each exchange being one turn per speaker labeled W: and S:, each turn 2-4 sentences so it reads like a real volley. No setup, no intro, no wrap-up telling me who you think won, just the five numbered exchanges.
