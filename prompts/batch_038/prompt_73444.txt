A metaphysics section of 24 undergraduates, one 100-minute meeting. They have encountered the ship puzzle only in its standard two-candidate form and argued about it once in section; I have lectured nothing. Design a runnable in-class activity in which they discover from what happens at the table — not from anything I say — that the criteria they are confident about cannot all be honoured at once, and that the resulting mess is exploitable by anyone who controls the order in which questions are put.

The frame should be an adjudication with real procedural teeth rather than a thought experiment read aloud. A maritime trust has died out leaving a bequest payable to "the vessel," and four claimants present, each with a one-page dossier of undisputed facts you write for me: the ship that has sailed continuously under the original name, registry and crew while every timber was replaced one-for-one over forty years; the ship rebuilt in a museum dock from every original timber that was discarded, stored and finally reassembled in the original configuration; the ship of a rival owner who bought each discarded timber as it came out and installed it, one-for-one, into his own sound hull, so that his vessel is now in continuous service and contains all and only the original timbers; and a replica launched last year to the original plans by the original builder's yard, using new timber but the original bell, figurehead and logbook. No claimant should have a fact the others lack: the asymmetries must be structural, not evidential.

The mechanics I want, which you should fill in and check: the 24 split into four panels of six, each panel holding the same six private role cards, each card fixing a criterion the holder is instructed to apply and therefore a strict ranking of the four claimants. Each panel elects a solicitor who is briefed on all six criteria and chooses the seeding of a knockout: three pairwise motions, simple majority, final winner takes the bequest. Panelists stake tokens — paper only, no real money — on each motion before it is voted, paid from the panel's bank, and the solicitor is privately instructed to maximise their own tokens by the end.

Deliver: the four dossiers and six role cards in printable form; a minute-by-minute timeline; the exact token numbers and staking rules; a verification section showing the ranking table you have specified, the pairwise majorities it produces, that the majority relation really cycles for four claimants and six voters, and at least two seedings that yield two different winners, with the solicitor's token gain worked out under each; a note on how ties are to be avoided or broken given the even panel size; and a design for the information structure that gets the four panels onto different seedings without my rigging results by hand — or, if you do rig it, how I should justify that in the debrief.

Then the debrief, mapping specific moments onto the philosophical structure: what the fourth claimant does that the usual two-way case cannot, why a relation that governs which ship is the ship had better not be order-sensitive, and what it means that panels with identical members and identical evidence crowned different ships. Mark plainly which claims are common ground among people who work on this and which are live controversies; do not let the script present any single resolution — four-dimensionalist, relative-identity, closest-continuer, epistemicist, deflationary — as the settled answer, and where the activity's own design quietly presupposes a contested position, say which and give me one sentence to flag it to the class. Name positions and arguments, not sources: no citations and no reading list.

Finish with what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and what to do about it — role-card holders voting against type, panelists declaring the whole thing a fact about voting rather than about ships, the solicitor's card being discovered early, staking collapsing into unanimity, the panel that finishes in nine minutes — and with an honest statement of the feature of the puzzle this activity cannot reproduce and that I must supply verbally.
