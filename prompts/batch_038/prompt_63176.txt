My sister and I handle the giving for our family. Every year our dad sets aside about $70k, and four or five times a year we get the cousins together for an hour to sign off on roughly $15k at a time. Nobody in this group has any training in it — I'm just the one who reads the proposals and makes a one page summary for my dad, who rubber stamps pretty much whatever we say.

Here's my problem. About once a quarter somebody pitches us something where the number involved is so enormous that any honest math says we should hand over the entire fund and go home. Last year it was a group working on preventing human extinction: their case boiled down to some unfathomable count of possible future people, so even if their odds of mattering at all were one in ten thousand, the arithmetic still flattened a local shelter that could tell us exactly how many beds it filled per dollar. This quarter its a startup doing something with carbon that claims a one in five hundred shot at a breakthrough worth a hundred times what we'd give them, and otherwise nothing. Every time, one cousin says the math says yes, another says this feels like a con, and we burn the whole hour going in circles because honestly both of them are right.

I think this shape of argument has a name — Pascal's mugging, maybe — but the name doesn't help me at a table with four people and a laptop. What I want is a procedure we can actually run on a proposal in the time we have: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each one, how to convert the giant number into something we're allowed to act on, and where the procedure should stop and say this is a values call, take a vote. My cousin who dropped math in high school has to be able to follow it. It needs a branch for when the organization wont give us any numbers at all, which is most of them.

A few things I keep tripping on that I'd like the procedure to handle explicitly:

- If the stakes really were that huge, wouldn't a lot of people already be working on it? Something feels off about a pitch where the size of the prize and the size of my personal role in it are both supposed to be enormous, but I can't say what the rule is.
- The same future-generations number showed up in three different proposals last year. If it counts at full strength for each one, we've counted it three times.
- It seems to matter whether they're unsure about how big the payoff is versus unsure about whether their intervention does anything at all, but I don't know how.
- We get maybe four of these a year. Whatever rule we adopt can't be one that says yes every time, or the fund is gone in a decade.

Then please run it on three real cases: the extinction group, the carbon startup with the one in five hundred shot, and a plain local food bank that costs us $40 a person fed and can document it. I want to see where it lands, including whichever case I'm not going to like — I've been quietly steering money to the food bank for years and I want to know whether that's defensible or just comfortable. And be straight with me about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as a checklist, and about what the procedure simply can't decide.
