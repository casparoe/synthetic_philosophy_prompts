> Kuhn's discovery was that rival scientific theories cannot be compared. He took the word "incommensurable" from Greek mathematics, where it applies to two magnitudes that share no common measure, and that is precisely the situation with competing paradigms: since each paradigm fixes the meaning of the terms in which the other is stated, there is no neutral language in which to set them side by side, and therefore no grounds on which to prefer one. Kuhn likened the shift between them to a Gestalt switch, and he was explicit that what happens in a scientific revolution is a conversion rather than a proof — afterwards the converted scientist inhabits a different world, which is why the transition has to be described in the vocabulary of religion rather than of logic. This is also why normal science matters so much in his account: during normal science the paradigm is not examined at all, it is simply applied, and it is that dogmatic closure which Popper objected to when he complained that Kuhn had described a science that had given up on criticism. Kuhn's conclusion is that science does not accumulate and does not converge on the truth; paradigms succeed one another rather as fashions do, and the special rationality of science is an Enlightenment survival. It follows that wherever rival schools coexist today — in economics, in psychoanalysis, in climate science — there can be no rational settlement, only the eventual replacement of one generation of researchers by another.

One loose thread I should hand you, since it bears on how much of the passage is outdated rather than wrong. I have heard in seminar that Kuhn spent a good deal of his later career backing away from the strongest version of the thesis — that the concept he preferred late on had more to do with the failure of translation than with the impossibility of comparison, and that he came to stress how small and local the untranslatable residue actually is, confined to a handful of interdefined terms rather than spread across an entire conceptual scheme. I cannot tell whether any of this amounts to a change of mind or merely a change of vocabulary, and I would rather not have to choose.
