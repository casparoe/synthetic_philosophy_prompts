Been going in circles on Great Man vs. structural history and I want to pressure-test it on a case I know reasonably well: Gorbachev, 1985-91, the Eastern Bloc coming apart.

The frame I want isn't the standard one. "Would it have happened anyway" bundles at least three separate claims — direction (does the bloc liberalize or break up at all), timing (1989 vs. a decade later vs. never), and manner (mostly bloodless vs. another Hungary or Prague). Sort the usual considerations — oil revenue, Afghanistan, the gerontocracy, the failed August 1991 coup — into which of those three they actually bear on, and flag every place an argument that looks like it settles the Great Man question really only settles timing.

The bit I actually care about: his key move was declining to use force, i.e. removing a constraint rather than supplying a cause. Does the structuralist test — swap the leader, same outcome — even work for negative causation like that? If most of what the structure contributed was that the tanks existed and had been used before, then pulling them off the table looks like an individual-level intervention a structural account cant absorb. Tell me if I'm fooling myself.

Then the endogeneity problem: he was picked by the same Politburo that the structural story says was already cornered, so how much variance is even attributable to him as opposed to to the selection process. And say where the analysis goes silent instead of forcing it — including whether the silence is a fact about 1989 or about counterfactual history in general. No bibliography needed, I know the lit.
