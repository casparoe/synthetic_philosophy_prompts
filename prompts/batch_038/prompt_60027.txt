I chair the disciplinary panel at a medium-security prison — three of us, a deputy governor, a wing manager and me, none of us lawyers — and we run about thirty adjudications a month, twenty minutes a case, after which we have to fill in a template certifying that the sanction was necessary and proportionate and then write one paragraph of reasons in plain English that the man himself will read and that a reviewer might check six months later. Our range is loss of privileges, days added to the earliest relase date, cellular confinement for up to a few days, and referral out for treatment. An audit just found that two panels in this same prison gave wildly different sanctions for near-identical charges, and the governor has asked me to write us a proper decision procedure. That part I can do. The part I can't do is that over the last year I've been talked into free will skepticism — I read the Caruso/Dennett debate book and came out on Caruso's side, which for me means something like his public-health/quarantine picture: nobody deserves to suffer in the basic-desert sense, what we're entitled to do is limited to what's needed to prevent harm, in its least restrictive form, with proportionality acting as a ceiling rather than a target. Two of my three panelists are Dennett's people without having read him: responsibility is a social practice, free will is a set of competences that get cultivated by being held to standards, censure is a message addressed to a person and not a technique applied to a mechanism, and a panel that never says you earned this is treating the men like weather to be managed. I don't want to relitigate that in the room every fortnight.

So write me a decision procedure for a single adjudication that a panelist who's never heard of either philosopher could actually run in twenty minutes: the questions to ask in strict order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the points where the procedure stops at no sanction, the points where it stops at a ceiling, and the points where it explicitly hands the decision over to judgment because the question is about values rather than facts. It has to be honest about admissibility — spell out which considerations come in at each step and which are barred and why, so that "he deserves days for that" gets either routed somewhere legitimate or refused outright instead of sneaking in dressed up as a risk judgment. And it needs to be one procedure both camps can use: where a skeptic and a compatibilist running the same steps would land on different sanctions, or the same sanction for incompatible reasons, mark the divergence and give me a tie-break rule (does the chair get a casting vote, does the less restrictive reading win by default, does it go to the governor?). Give me the thing as a one-page card a panel could laminate, plus the reasoning behind the card — no reading list, I've got the books.

Then run it on three cases and show the panel's working, not just the verdicts. (1) A man with fourteen months clean and working in the kitchen throws a pan of hot water at an officer during a cell search that destroyed photos of his daughter; the officer isn't badly hurt but won't come back onto the wing, and half my panel wants the maximum days. (2) A man with no charges against him in two years who quietly refuses every accredited programme and tells new arrivals the programmes are a con; the wing manager wants to book him for failure to comply, and the real case for sanctioning him is that his refusal is contagious. (3) Three men on the same wing separately refused to return to their cells during a lock-up and the wing is deteriorating, and I want one of them to take a heavier sanction to send a message. For each: the output, then what the other framework would have said about the same facts, and I want at least one of the three to come out in a way I personally will hate — better I find out now than in front of the governor. Finally tell me what the procedure can't do: where it'll still be inconsistent between panels, where it lets the regime's own effects get laundered into a risk number (boredom, crowding, the fact that dangerousness in here is partly manufactured in here), and which of its steps are judgment calls wearing a procedure's clothes.
