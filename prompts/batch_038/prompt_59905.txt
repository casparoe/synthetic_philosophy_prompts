I am putting together a reading group on the historiography of the British Raj, and we have just worked through Ferguson's *Empire* alongside Tharoor's *Inglorious Empire*. The sessions keep dissolving into a contest of atrocity and achievement — railways against famines, unified administration against the drain of revenue — and I have become convinced that what we are really quarrelling about is a single badly specified counterfactual. So I would like your credence, as a number, on the following proposition, which I will call P: *had the East India Company and subsequently the Crown never established rule over the subcontinent, and had the rest of the eighteenth-century world been held as close to its actual course as possible, the material condition of the people of India in 1947 — life expectancy, real wages and per-capita output taken jointly, judged impersonally rather than by reference to any identifiable individual — would have been better than it in fact was.*

I want the decomposition, not the headline figure. Please identify the sub-questions that genuinely carry the weight, assign a rough probability to each with a line of justification, and show how they combine. At minimum I would like separate terms for: (i) how well posed P is at all, given that there is no agreed procedure for selecting the baseline, and that a resurgent Maratha confederacy, a fragmented post-Mughal patchwork of successor states, French or Persian displacement, and an accelerated indigenous commercialisation may each imply a wildly different answer, so that P may be a weighted average over incompatible histories rather than a single claim; (ii) whether the non-identity problem defuses P in its person-affecting guise while leaving the impersonal version intact, and if so which version you have been assessing; (iii) whether the material balance is knowable on the available evidence, or whether the pivotal magnitudes — revenue extraction, the fate of Bengal's textile producers, the excess mortality of the late-Victorian famines, the interwar growth record — are contested to a degree that leaves the sign of the total underdetermined even in principle; and (iv) whether sovereignty and subjection to alien rule are commensurable with the material terms at all, such that a defender of Tharoor could accept your welfare calculation and still reject the verdict it licenses. Please do not manufacture statistics; where your credence turns on a magnitude, state what the magnitude would have to be rather than pretending to know it.

Then give me the combined number, say which term P is most sensitive to, and describe the one piece of evidence or argument that would move you furthest. Finally: my own credence is roughly 0.25, while a friend who considers Ferguson's case broadly sound puts hers at about 0.70. Tell me which of your terms the two of us most likely diverge on, and whether you take our disagreement to be substantive or a mere artefact of each of us silently filling in a different baseline — and if the latter, say which baseline each of us is probably assuming.
