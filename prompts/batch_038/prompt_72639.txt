Construct a thought experiment involving a multipolar trap — a race to the bottom — engineered to survive the removal of the two explanations people normally reach for. Its purpose is to test whether a Moloch-style trap can be generated from strategic structure alone, so a scenario that asserts the trap rather than deriving it from the players' own reasoning counts as a failure.

Specifications:

1. The setting is mundane and institutional — a profession, an industry, a platform, a department — not science-fictional, and the scenario is at most two paragraphs.
2. No player has a dominant strategy to defect, and the interaction is not a prisoner's dilemma or a resource-depletion problem at any stage; each player's best action genuinely depends on what they expect the others to do.
3. The players are rational, share the same ranking over outcomes, and correctly anticipate one another's behavior. The trap may not rest on any mistake, myopia, or ignorance.
4. There is an alternative profile that every player strictly prefers and that is feasible, including enforceable through an arrangement all of them would accept — so the case cannot be waved away as an incentive problem.
5. Full, credible, verifiable disclosure of the relevant facts does not break the trap, and the reason must be internal to the strategic structure, not that the disclosure is cheap talk or disbelieved.
6. Nothing outside the players — no regulator, no legal barrier, no exogenous shock — may be doing the work of either creating or sustaining the trap.

After the scenario, do the checking: write out the equilibrium in strategies and beliefs and verify it holds; explain how the unanimously preferred profile can be feasible and yet unreachable; give the precise mechanism by which the disclosure in (5) fails, or backfires; name the single feature of the case that is load-bearing and show what happens to the trap when that feature is removed; and close with the strongest objection available to someone who thinks monitoring and graduated sanctions dissolve the case, together with your verdict on whether it lands.
