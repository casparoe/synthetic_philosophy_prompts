I'm trying to write a short piece defending Hobbes as a philosopher of mathematics that doesn't just reduce to "he was stubborn and wrong about the circle," and I want to know where my ammunition actually is. Going through De Corpore and the Wallis business, these are the six substantive things he held, in my own paraphrase:

1. A proper definition of a geometrical figure has to be genetic — a circle is defined by saying its made by swinging a line around one fixed end — and the Euclid-style definition (a plane figure bounded by one line such that all straight lines drawn from a certain point to it are equal) isn't really a definition at all, because it tells you how to recognise the thing rather than how it comes to be.
2. Points are not unextended. A point is a little material thing whose magnitude we simply choose to leave out of account, on the grounds that motion of something with no magnitude whatsoever couldnt generate a line.
3. The whole of mathematics reduces to geometry, and geometry reduces to the kinematics of moving bodies; arithmetic and algebra are at best shorthand for geometric reasoning, not a separate or more fundamental discipline.
4. We can have genuine scientia of geometry but not of natural things, because we construct the figures ourselves and so know their causes, whereas we didn't construct the world and only ever see its effects.
5. An infinite magnitude is impossible, since any magnitude has to be generated by motion and motion only ever gets through finitely much; so any method built on infinite series or infinitely little quantities is reasoning about objects that cant exist, which is why he thought that whole approach was scholasticism coming back through the side door.
6. Symbolic algebra gives you correct answers without giving you any grasp of why they're correct.

Score each one 0–10 for how well it holds up judged on its own merits by roughly todays standards in philosophy of maths — explicitly not for how influential it turned out to be, and not penalised for the squaring-the-circle embarassment. One sentence of reasoning per score, then tell me which single one I should hang the argument on, and whether I'm being too generous about any of them.
