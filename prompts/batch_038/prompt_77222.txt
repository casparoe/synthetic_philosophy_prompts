Take France between the 1932 elections and the collapse of the Popular Front in 1938 and use it to test whether left and right name anything real, or whether they're just labels we keep reattaching to whatever coalition happens to be opposing the current order.

The puzzle I care about: in 1932 the French communists ran alone and called the socialists social-fascists — the primary enemy was the other left. By 1936 the same party was backing Blum's government from outside, a government full of Radicals, i.e. property-owning, anti-clerical bourgeois republicans the communists had spent a decade sneering at. What changed in between was the February 1934 street violence by the leagues and then Moscow's line shifting at the Comintern's 1935 congress. French workers' material interests didn't move much at all.

So what was the glue, and when did it show up? Weigh three candidate answers and tell me which one the episode actually supports. (1) Program core: each side clusters around a substantive commitment, equality versus order, and the coalition formed because the parties finally noticed they shared one. (2) Common enemy: a bloc is nothing more than the set of actors treating some opponent as the primary threat, so the left is a coalition structure rather then a value, and it can be reassembled overnight by a change in who the enemy is. (3) Indexical: left and right carry no content, they just mean whoever is opposing the dominant arrangement at that moment, so the label surviving 1932–36 is not evidence that anything survived.

Two things to press on. First, the symmetry: the leagues in Feb 1934 were just as internally incoherent — monarchists, veterans' associations, outright fascists — so if enemy-definition explains the left, does it explain the right too, and what's left of the axis if both poles turn out to be coalitions of convenience? Second, the governing record: Matignon, the 40-hour week, paid holidays — and then the thing coming apart over Spain and Munich. Did the glue hold on economics and fail on foreign policy, and what does that say about which dimension was the real one?

Say where each candidate goes silent or has to be stretched to fit, and end with the one you'd defend plus the feature of this episode that would count against it.
