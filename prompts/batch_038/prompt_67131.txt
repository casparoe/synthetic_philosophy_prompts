I'm in my fourth year of a quantitative-psychology PhD, and my dissertation concerns what happens to a screening instrument once it is deployed inside the system that consumes it — concretely, a depression-severity measure that feeds a stepped-care triage rule, so that the score determines who gets monitored, who gets referred, and who gets asked the follow-up questions that generate next year's training data. My working vocabulary is psychometric and causal-inferential: latent trait models and item response theory; configural, metric, and scalar measurement invariance and differential item functioning; Messick's unified account of construct validity; criterion contamination and label leakage; covariate and concept shift; SUTVA and interference; dynamic treatment regimes. My third committee member is a philosopher of psychiatry who has now assigned me Hacking twice and insists that my feedback-loop results are "a special case of the looping effects," and that I am missing the ontological point entirely. I have read his 1995 chapter "The Looping Effects of Human Kinds" and skimmed Rewriting the Soul, and I can follow the prose, but I cannot tell how much of it amounts to a claim I could model, test, or refute and how much is a manner of speaking.

So I would like you to restate Hacking's position in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, claim by claim rather than as a running summary. Please handle each of the following separately: the distinction between indifferent kinds and interactive kinds; the thesis that in the human sciences the classification and the people classified stand in a two-way causal relation, such that the classification becomes partly constitutive of what it classifies rather than merely tracking it; the moving-target consequence, that the stability of a human kind is a historical achievement rather than a background condition; his historical case of multiple personality, in which diagnostic criteria, interview technique, and patient communities appear to have helped produce the very phenomenon being diagnosed; and the ethical claim bound up with making up people, that new classifications create new ways of being a person and new resources for self-description. For each, give me the closest analogue in the machinery listed above. Is interactive versus indifferent simply the difference between closed-loop and open-loop deployment — that is, a SUTVA violation with a particular structure? Is constitutiveness something stronger than a feedback loop, and if so what makes it stronger, in terms I could write down? Is a time-wise invariance violation the right translation of a moving target, or does that mistranslate, given that in my field invariance failure is treated as a nuisance to be modeled away whereas Hacking seems to treat it as the phenomenon itself? I especially want you to hold apart two things my discipline routinely conflates: an instrument whose scores are contaminated by the downstream consequences of the score, and an instrument that alters the construct it purports to measure. Tell me which of Hacking's claims require the second and which can be satisfied by the first.

Then give me the residue report, and treat it as the deliverable rather than as an appendix. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with a distortion you should name explicitly, and which have no expression at all in a framework whose target is a fixed latent trait estimated from indicators? Do not stretch a metaphor to cover a gap, and do not import assumptions from measurement theory or from the causal hierarchy and then present them as what Hacking meant; I would rather have an honest list of untranslatables than a smooth-sounding translation. If you think MacKenzie's account of the performativity of financial models is a better target vocabulary than mine for some of these claims, say so, say which claims, and say what changes. Finally, take a position on the question my committee member and I are actually arguing about: is the residue evidence that my vocabulary is missing something real — something about first-person uptake, about a kind's capacity to be endorsed or resisted by the people who fall under it, about historicity — or is it evidence that, once the translation is done honestly, the looping-effects thesis collapses into the modest and already well-known observation that closed-loop deployment breaks the identification assumptions of a measurement model, wearing ontological language as costume? If the answer is both, tell me which parts fall on which side, and tell me which single claim I should press him to state in operational terms.
