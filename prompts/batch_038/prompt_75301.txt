Right, I've got a seminar in about three weeks and what I want from you is a proper Q&A prep sheet — not a defence of my view, I can do that bit myself.

The view. I'm arguing that the frame problem is still live, but not in the way it usually gets revived. My claim is that what the early AI literature was circling was never really a bookkeeping issue — how to represent the non-effects of an action without listing them all — but a relevance-determination issue, and that running the two together is exactly why everyone assumes learning methods dissolved it. The distinction I want to hold onto is between relevance that is *endogenous* (a function of the agent's own ongoing projects, of what it's trying to keep intact) and relevance that is *borrowed* — supplied from outside, by a training distribution that encodes somebody elses judgements about what mattered in a million earlier situations. On my view, trained systems are superb at borrowed relevance and have no mechanism whatsoever for the endogenous kind, which is why they come apart in the cases where the world hasn't changed but the agent's projects have: a promise made five minutes ago, a priority that just got inverted, a constraint that only exists because of who the agent is. I'm calling the demand that a real solution supply the endogenous kind the *endogeneity constraint*, and that's the load-bearing coinage in the talk, so the objections need to be aimed at it.
