Doing a philosophy of spacetime module and I'm trying to get ahead of next week's seminar by reverse-engineering our tutor's rule for when the hole argument actually bites. Her verdicts so far:

- GR on a bare manifold, read as a substantivalist so that two diffeomorphic models are two genuinely different possibilities: bites.
- The very same GR, but accepting Leibniz equivalence, so diffeomorphic models count as one physical situation: doesn't bite.
- Newtonian gravity rewritten in generally covariant form (the Newton–Cartan bussiness), read substantivalistically: bites.
- Plain old Newtonian gravity, absolute time and flat space, not covariant at all, read substantivalistically: doesn't bite — though she did note that the potential is only ever fixed up to an arbitrary function of time, and called that a cousin of the hole argument rather than the thing itself.
- Maxwell in terms of the potentials, counting gauge-related potentials as the same physical state: doesn't bite, even though you can cook up a gauge transformation that's the identity outside some small region and differs inside it.
