I'm drafting the opening section of a dissertation chapter on persistence and would like an audit of my package before I commit to it in print. Here is what I accept.

(1) Endurantism: material objects are wholly present at each moment of their existence and have no temporal parts.

(2) Eternalism, largely because I take the relativity of simultaneity to rule out a privileged present. I see no conflict with (1).

(3) Against the standard reply to the problem of temporary intrinsics, I reject the relational analysis on which "the object is F at t" means the object bears a relation to the temporally qualified property being-F-at-t: that turns every change into a merely relational one and never says how the object itself is. On my view the "at" is an unanalyzable nexus. More generally, my methodological rule is that a theory of persistence should pay for at most one primitive.

(4) The statue and the lump have genuinely different persistence conditions — the lump survives being melted down and recast, the statue does not — yet I deny that two material objects can be wholly present in the same region at the same time, and I deny that identity is ever indeterminate.

(5) Backward time travel of the Lewisian kind is possible, and I accommodate it by saying the traveller has his properties at positions in personal time, where personal time is simply an ordering of the external times he occupies.

Tell me which of these genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to. Rank the conflicts by severity rather than listing them flat, and for each give the least costly revision available to me together with what it costs in primitives or in verdicts on cases. Please do not simply recommend temporal parts: I want to know whether my own resources, under my own rule in (3), can carry the package.
