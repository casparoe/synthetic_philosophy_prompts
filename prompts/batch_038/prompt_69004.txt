My sister is a palliative care nurse and I've gotten dragged into the same argument with her and two of her coworkers maybe four times now, usually after a bottle of wine, and there's a specific shape to the way they talk that I've never been able to find a word for. Here's the setup. They hold that suffering comes in two kinds. There's the ordinary kind — a bad headache, a rough week of grief, the misery of a procedure you have to have anyway — and that kind can be traded off against good stuff: you'd accept a worse headache if it bought you something worth having, and it's fine, maybe required, to cause some of it in order to prevent more of it. Then there's the awful kind — being locked inside your own body, being tortured, some flavors of anguish — and the claim is that nothing on the good side of the ledger can ever make up for that, no matter how much happiness you pile on or how many people get to enjoy it. The line between the two isn't a matter of degree, they say, it's a difference in kind, and once something is over it the arithmetic just stops applying. I know the word "lexical" gets waved around for this two-tier business, and I've seen "incommensurable" used too, but I'm pretty sure neither of those is the thing I'm actually stuck on, which is what happens above the line.

Because the position seems to run out of scale exactly where the scale was supposed to be doing all the work. I asked: fine, so one person being tortured is bad in a way nothing can compensate — is a world with one such person exactly as bad as a world with a billion of them? My sister basically said yes, that once you're over the line badness isn't the sort of thing that adds up anymore, all you can say is that someone is over it. Her friend said no, obviously a billion is worse, it's just worse in a way that can never be cashed out in units of happiness. And those are wildly different views, but as far as I can tell they fall out of the same starting claim, and there isn't a middle option available — below the line you get normal adding-up, above it you get either flat indifference or something that can't be compared at all, and the view doesn't say which. Related second pattern, which may or may not be the same problem wearing a different hat: when I push on where the line actually goes (a month of severe nausea? a year of it? a headache that never ends?), nobody can place it, but everyone insists it's there and insists that not being able to find it isn't a problem. I also got told I was strawmanning when I tried to lay this out on a forum, so maybe I'm the one confused.

So: does the two-tier-with-a-hard-cutoff structure have a proper name, and does the thing I'm calling running-out-of-scale-above-the-line have one? I want to know how the second relates to the old thought experiment about whether you'd rather one person get tortured or everyone alive get a speck in their eye, to whatever ethicists say about trade-offs not being possible between two kinds of value, to cutoff-style views elsewhere in ethics, and to the fight about whether lots of small bads can add up to one big one. And if the above-the-line gap genuinely doesn't have an established label, say so plainly and then coin one — with a real definition, and the boundary cases: what situations fall under it, and what looks like it but isn't (I suspect "we just can't compare these two" is often a different complaint, and I want told where the line is between those). A bare name won't do me any good, I want the carving. Last thing: tell me how confident you actually are in each label you're putting forward, and point at the single claim in your answer you'd be least willing to defend if a working ethicist read it.
