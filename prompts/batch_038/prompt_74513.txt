I cover math and games for a general-interest science desk, and every column ends with a short joke that's supposed to pay off only for readers who actually know the material. This month I'm writing about the strategy-stealing argument — Nash's trick for showing the second player in Hex can't have a winning strategy, on the grounds that the first player could just plonk down any old stone and then borrow it.

Can you write me the joke? Ground rules: the punchline has to come out of the structure of the argument itself, not out of mathematicians-are-socially-awkward. Two spots I'd love it to land on, pick one — either the fact that its a pure existence proof, so you're left dead certain a winning strategy exists and completely clueless about what it is, or the quiet assumption that an extra move can never hurt you, which is exactly where the same argument dies in chess (zugzwang) and in Go once komi makes the opening asymmetric. Setup plus punchline, a handful of lines, no explainer glued on at the end. And the joke should be about the stealing, not about which game we happened to pick.
