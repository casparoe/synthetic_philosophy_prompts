So my sister runs a monthly Stoic practice group, and the centerpiece is what they call a control audit: you write down everything that's eating you and mark each item up-to-you, not-up-to-you, or partly. She's a hardliner about the old dichotomy — she's always complaining that half the group has watered it down into some trichotomy — but she still hands out the three-column sheet, and she does use the middle column, just not often.

Here's her track record across mine and her own sheets over the last several months:

- whether the flight lands on time — no
- the first jolt of anger when someone cuts you off — no, "that's weather, not you"
- whether you say the cruel thing the jolt is pushing at you — yes
- whether you get the promotion when your the obvious front-runner and the partner has already told you it's yours barring disaster — no
- whether your knee holds up over a marathon training block — partly, "you can build it and you can wreck it"
- whether your students actually learn the material — no, but she wrote in the margin "the teaching is yours"
- whether your mother's surgery goes well — no
- whether you're at her bedside every week of the recovery — yes
- whether you enjoy the party once you're there — partly, "you can set the conditions, you cant order the mood"

Now my actual situation. My mom's dementia has reached the point where I'm moving her to a care facility two hours away next month; she wont remember the move within a week, my brother thinks it's abandonment, and I've decided it's the right call anyway. The item I want to put on the next audit is *not* whether she ends up well cared for, and *not* whether my brother ever comes around — I'm pretty sure I know those marks. The item is: whether, a year from now, I'll feel like I did right by her.

Predict her mark. I want the working shown more than the bottom line: which rules are compatible with every single verdict above, where those rules actually come apart, and what each one says about my item. At least three seem live to me — the strict line that only your own assents and choices are yours, the load-the-dice line where influence earns a partly, and the aim-versus-target line where only doing-everything-you-can is yours and the target itself never is — but I suspect there's some hybrid involving whether another person's choice sits in the causal chain that explains her two flat nos on things she could obviously influence better than any of those three do. Tell me your best guess at her mark, how confident you'd be in it, and then name the single further audit item that would settle which rule she's running, and why that one rather than another. Last thing: if she marks it partly, what has she committed herself to about blame — whether I'd deserve it a year from now for feeling like I failed her? That's where this stops being an exercise for me.
