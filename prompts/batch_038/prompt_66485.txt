I've been reading about character on my own for a while now, no philosophy background, I just get obsessive about things, and I built an argument I thought was solid and then it ran off a cliff and I can't see where.

(1) A character trait has to show up across a bunch of different situations to count as a trait, otherwise "honest" just means "honest at the office." (2) The studies say it doesn't: people who happened to find a dime left in a pay phone were way more likely to help a stranger who dropped a stack of papers, and seminary students who'd been told they were running late mostly walked past a man slumped in a doorway even when the talk they were on their way to give was about the Good Samaritan. (3) So there are no broad traits, just a pile of narrow habits keyed to particular settings. (4) Which means when someone does something cruel, it wasn't them that did it, it was the setup they got dropped into. (5) And you can't blame a person for what the setup did, any more than you can blame them for the weather. (6) So nobody is ever blameworthy, and by the same reasoning nobody is praiseworthy. (7) Except then the people who ran those studies were also just doing what their labs and their deadlines made them do, so there's no reason to think they were careful or straight with us, which means (2) isn't evidence of anything after all.

I want to keep (2) and (3), I really do, I think those studies are devestating. It's (5) onward I can't live with, and I cant see how to stop it without dumping (3) too. Which step actually breaks, why did it look harmless when I wrote it down, and what do I have to swallow if I fix it that way?
