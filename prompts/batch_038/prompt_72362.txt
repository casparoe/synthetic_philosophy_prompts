My roommate and his econ study partner went around and around on the Allais choices last night and neither would budge. Both of them accept the setup: first pair is $1 million for certain versus 10% of $5 million, 89% of $1 million, 1% of nothing; second pair is 11% of $1 million and 89% of nothing versus 10% of $5 million and 90% of nothing. Both say they'd take the certain million in the first pair and the $5 million gamble in the second, and both think that's fine.

(1) Dana said that's flatly inconsistent: the 89% branch is the same for both options within each pair, so it should cancel, and once it cancels the two pairs are literally the same choice — so preferring differently in them means you're contradicting yourself.

(2) Marcus said canceling is the thing in question. Expected utility only gets cancellation from the independence axiom, and doubting that axiom is the whole point here. Showing the pattern violates it isn't showing its irrational, it's restating the axiom under dispute.

(3) Dana said okay, use a money pump instead, which doesn't presuppose anything formal: someone with those two preferences can be walked through a series of swaps and end up paying to get back where she started, so the inconsistency costs actual cash.

(4) Marcus said the pump only bites on an agent who doesn't see it coming. A sophisticated one who knows the swaps will be offered can just refuse the first, so the pump doesn't indict the preference pattern, it indicts handing your choices to a mechanism one at a time.

(5) Dana then said: fine, put the disappointment in the outcomes. If the 1% branch is described as zero dollars plus knowing you passed up a sure million, the certain option wins the first pair and the gamble wins the second, and its all consistent with expected utility after all.

(6) Marcus said that move proves way too much — you can rescue any pattern of choices by re-describing outcomes until they fit, and if every preference can be made rational by enriching the outcome space, calling a preference rational stops carrying information.

Can you go through (1) to (6) and rate each one — clearly right, partly right, or just wrong — with a sentence of justification each? Then say who comes out ahead overall, and whether there's some consideration neither of them touched that actually settles it.
