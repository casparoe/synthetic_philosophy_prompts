ok so I'm in a stochastic thermodynamics group — we run feedback protocols on a single colloidal particle in a double-well optical trap. Camera does the "measurement", the trap does the feedback, so it's basically a Szilard engine you can actually build. Everyone here talks like Landauer's principle settles the demon question and the bill is kT ln2 per erased bit, and I've mostly gone along with it, but I'm writing the intro chapter of my thesis and I don't want to assert a resolution I couldn't defend if someone pushed. So before I write anything I want the spec: what does an account of the thermodynamics of information-bearing processes actually have to deliver to count as resolving the demon problem? Give me the desiderata, with the case or consideration behind each one.

Cases I already know the list has to handle — add more if I'm missing obvious ones, and tell me if you think some of these collapse into each other:

- the randomizing demon. Instead of resetting its memory bit to a known standard state (the expensive move), it lowers the barrier and lets the memory thermalize into a random well, which looks thermodynamically free. But to use that as a blank it needs to know which well it landed in, and knowing that is another measurement needing another memory. The cost seems to get pushed down a regress instead of paid. Does an adequate account have to say where the bill is, or is it allowed to say the regress is benign?
- a reset where the entropy shows up as memory-reservoir mutual information rather than in either subsystem's own entropy, with the bath barely warming. Is "the entropy went into correlations" an acceptable place to park the compensation, or does that just hide it?
- measurement without feedback: the demon observes, does nothing, resets. Does it pay?
- feedback without measurement: an open-loop protocol that works because the particle is probably already in the well you're about to push it into.
- the infinite tape: the demon never erases, just keeps writing further along an arbitrarily long memory. Is the second law saved, deferred, or violated-until-the-tape-runs-out — and if the answer is the last one, is that a resolution or a shrug?
- a swarm of N small demons, one bit each, handing off down the line.
- erasure paid against a cold reservoir while the engine runs against a hot one. Temperature shouldn't be a global constant in the accounting.
- quantum variants: a probe that entangles instead of projectively measuring, resetting a qubit that was in a coherent superposition, and whether coherence costs anything over and above the bit.

Then the meta-level stuff, which is honestly where I think the real content is. Must the account be statable over time-reversal-invariant microdynamics, or is it allowed to posit a fundamental arrow? Is it a claim about single trajectories or about ensembles — because our data is single-run and the second law as we actually test it permits transient violations, so any requirement demanding "never a violation in any individual run" looks unsatisfiable next to "consistent with the fluctuation theorems," and I want you to say so if you agree. It must not require that the compensation be located in the demon's memory, since that's the conclusion of the house view and I don't want it smuggled in as a criterion. What counts as one bit — Shannon, von Neumann, something else — and does the account have to fix that or can it stay neutral? And how seriously should the spec take recurrence, given a finite system comes back?

Mark which of these are non-negotiable and which are camp preferences, and flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied — I'd bet there's at least one and I'd rather see that than a tidy list. Then tell me which single requirement knocks out the most candidate resolutions.

Finally, briefly, and I mean briefly: a short paragraph each on how the main contenders do against the list — the erasure-cost story, the mutual-information / generalized-second-law story, the Brillouin-style "measurement itself is the costly step" story, and the "the second law was only ever statistical, so there's nothing to exorcise" story. No bibliography, no reading list, I'll find the papers myself.
