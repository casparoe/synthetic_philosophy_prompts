In the thought experiment Avicenna calls the Flying Man, we are to suppose a human being created all at once, already fully formed and adult, with vision blocked, limbs held apart so that none of them touches another or anything else, and body suspended motionless in still air that offers no perceptible resistance. Such a person, Avicenna claims, would affirm the existence of his own self while affirming nothing else at all: neither heart nor brain, neither the length, breadth, or depth of his own body, nor any external thing. The received reading treats this as an anticipation of Descartes: the self can be conceived apart from the body, therefore it is really distinct from the body and immaterial.

Argue instead for a more modest and, I contend, more defensible reading. On this reading the Flying Man establishes something about how the self is given to its subject rather than about what kind of thing the self is: under the deprivation, the subject's awareness of himself is not a matter of singling out one item among others, and so cannot misfire in the way an identification of an object can. Call awareness of this sort non-detectorial. Avicenna has independent reason to describe self-awareness this way, since he holds it to be immediate and uninterrupted rather than the product of any operation the soul performs; the Flying Man, on the reading to be defended, demonstrates that claim rather than dualism. Dualism then requires a bridge premise — something linking extendedness or divisibility to the individuation of selves — which the thought experiment does not itself supply.

Your argument should do the following:

1. State the non-detectorial reading with enough precision that a proponent of the dualist reading could say what consideration would falsify it.
2. Name the bridge premise explicitly, and mark the exact step at which the Flying Man ceases to do any work once that premise is withdrawn.
3. Answer the objection that the deprivation shows only a failure to notice the body, and that a conclusion about the structure of self-awareness is being purchased with an absence of evidence: what the subject lacks is awareness of his limbs, and the reading on offer allegedly converts that lack into a positive claim about how the self is presented.
4. Confront the contamination worry. The intuition seems available only to readers who already know what it is like to inhabit a body, so the Flying Man may smuggle in precisely the embodied experience it was designed to exclude. Say whether this is fatal to the reading, and if it is not, what survives it.

Close by stating the strongest reason a committed Avicennian dualist would still reject your reading, and explain why, on your own account, that reason does not defeat it. Fidelity to Avicenna's actual intentions is not at issue; the reading is to be defended on philosophical grounds.
