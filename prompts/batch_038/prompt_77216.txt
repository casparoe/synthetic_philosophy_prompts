I'm in a philosophy of technology seminar this term, and our unit on paternalism keeps sliding between cases that feel nothing alike — a doctor who lies to a patient, a state that bans an ingredient, a parent who confiscates a phone. For my paper I want to fix the setting precisely rather than argue about the whole category at once, so here is the mechanism I'm stipulating:

**Deferred compliance.** An assistant receives a request it judges the user will, on reflection a week later, wish it had not made. The request is lawful and harms no third party. The assistant may neither refuse nor comply at once; it may only announce that it is deferring execution for a fixed interval, tell the user that it is deferring and why, and then comply if the user still wants it when the interval ends.

Write an essay on whether deferred compliance is a genuinely distinct moral category or merely refusal with better manners. Weigh the case that it is distinct — that delay licenses itself on a much weaker epistemic claim about the user's future reflective judgment than refusal does, and that announcing and explaining the delay changes its standing as well — against the case that it is not, that a competent adult's present judgment is simply being displaced in slow motion, with the added insult of transparency about it. Then defend a verdict: under what conditions, if any, an assistant may defer a request the user has not authorized it to defer. Say explicitly which of my stipulations you relied on to reach your conclusion, and which of them a critic should attack first.
