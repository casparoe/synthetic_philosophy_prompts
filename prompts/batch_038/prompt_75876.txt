I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am building an interactive around a claim much repeated since the dispute over a recidivism score, in which critics pointed to unequal false-positive rates and the vendor replied that its score was well calibrated: namely, that fairness is mathematically impossible. The interactive should be a decision tree that starts from a reader's acceptance of that headline and ends at a named position they did not know they held.

Please write it with eight to twelve branch nodes. Each node must be a concrete scenario or a claim about a specific trade-off that a reader can answer without any technical vocabulary: no node may ask them to choose between calibration, equalised odds and demographic parity by name. At least two nodes should be settled by a small numerical case of your own construction — a screening tool with different base rates across two groups, and a choice between two versions of it. The distinctions I want the tree to turn on include: whether the object of assessment is the score or the decision taken from it; whether the reader would accept group-specific thresholds; whether they regard unequal base rates as themselves the injustice, which a score reflecting them inherits; whether predictive accuracy is fixed from outside or can be changed by the fairness choice; and whether meeting the statistical criteria is the whole of fairness or a constraint on something else.

The leaves must include calibration-only, equalised odds, demographic parity, fairness through unawareness, an individual-fairness or similarity-based view, and the deflationary option that no statistical criterion settles the question because the choice is political. Build the space of possible answer-combinations first, then place the positions in it, and tell me which combinations are unoccupied — in each case saying whether the cell is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot really separate.

Close with a note on how confident you are in the placement of each leaf, and single out the one step in the tree you are least sure of.
