I'm about a third of the way through a dissertation chapter on the metaphysics of psychiatric kinds and I've hit a wall, mostly because the argument I'm trying to enter has been running since the eighties and nobody on either side seems to have budged. One camp says the categories track something real — there's an underlying dysfunction, or a cluster of causal mechanisms, or at least a stable enough syndrome to support induction and treatment response, and the fact that our criteria keep getting revised is just normal science converging on a kind that was there all along. The other camp says that's naive about the human sciences specifically, and leans on Hacking: once you name a way of being a person, the people so named hear the name, take it up, resist it, or perform it, so the kind and the classification of the kind mutually constitute each other — dynamic nominalism, the looping effects idea, his fugue/mad travelers case where a whole diagnosable kind appeared and then just evaporated. On this view "depression" or "ADHD" is partly made of the practices that detect and administer it, so there's no target for the realist to converge on. Both sides have been saying versions of this for decades and both keep citing the same evidence — the rising diagnosed prevalence of autism over the last few decades, which nobody thinks reflects a matching change in the underlying population; the disappointing inter-rater reliability numbers out of the DSM-5 field trials; the removal of homosexuality from the manual; the fact that a diagnosis is often the ticket to accommodations or reimbursement, so categories can be sustained by institutions that need them regardless of what they carve. Each side reads every one of those as support for itself, and the reading group I'm in has basically agreed to stop arguing about it.

My hypothesis, which I want you to stress-test rather than accept: the stall isn't a clash of bedrock intuitions and it isn't purely verbal, it's that "looping" is doing three different jobs and the two camps aren't agreeing on which one they're talking about. One channel is self-conception — being labeled changes how a person experiences and presents, which plausibly changes the kind itself. A second is institutional uptake — the label carries eligibility for services, leave, disability status, legal recognition, which can sustain a category's existence without touching whatever it's a category of. A third is detection and help-seeking — better screening and more reason to present just changes who ends up in the sample, which looks like the kind shifting but might only be our access to it shifting. If that's right, the realists are mostly answering a question about channel three (does improved detection undermine the claim that there's a kind underneath?) while the constructivists are answering a question about channel one (does the kind have identity conditions that survive its own description?), and channel two is where the genuinely non-metaphysical stakes live. There's also a second thing I suspect is hidden in here: a disagreement about the identity conditions of a kind over time. When the extension shifts from one edition of the manual to the next, the realist treats the earlier criteria as an approximation of the same kind and the constructivist treats them as a different kind, and neither argues for that assumption — they just deploy it. So maybe the crux is a question about reference across time rather than a question about realism at all.

Diagnose the disagreement: is it substantive, verbal, two or more questions sharing a name, an unargued assumption about kind-identity doing the work, or a hidden empirical question about what looping does over time — convergence to a fixed point, oscillation, or drift without stabilization? Tell me whether my three-channel hypothesis survives contact with what the two camps actually say, and if it doesn't, say so and give me a better decomposition. I want you to engage the specific moves and not write a survey; I already know the survey. Then tell me honestly what would count as progress — a distinction, a case study, an empirical finding about whether classifications stabilize or keep moving, a formal result about how categories behave under feedback, a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected and explain why. Also: please check whether the constructivist's own position is self-undermining in an interesting way (if categories are made by our naming practices, is "interactive kind" itself an interactive kind, and does that hurt or help?), since I can't tell if that's a real problem or a cheap gotcha that people who like looping effects have already absorbed.
