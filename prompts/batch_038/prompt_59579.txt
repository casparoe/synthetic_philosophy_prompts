I'm putting together a screening-and-discussion night for our phil of sci reading group and I've settled on Groundhog Day, but not for the free will stuff everyone always reaches for. I want to use it for the Humean / anti-Humean fight about laws of nature. Here's my working pitch — stress test it, then write the actual reading.

The pitch: the loop hands Phil a mosaic about as close to maximally simple as a world can get. February 2 in Punxsutawney, over and over — same song on the clock radio, same DJ patter, same insurance guy on the doorstep, same puddle on the same corner. If the best-system account is right, this is a world positively dripping with lawlike regularity: cheap to axiomatize, strong in what it lets you predict. But Phil's experience is the exact opposite of what an anti-Humean says knowledge of laws feels like. He can predict everything and explain nothing, and the film is careful never to tell him, or us, why the loop runs at all — the neurologist shrugs, the psychologist shrugs, no cause is ever offered. So my claim is that the movie stages the Humean's best case and the anti-Humean's best case inside the same hundred minutes and refuses to referee.

Then there's a distinction I've been using with the group that I half-invented and want pressure tested. I split the loop's regularities into the ones that respond to Phil and the ones that don't. The tire, the kid who falls out of the tree, the guy choking in the diner — those bend to what he does, and he learns them by experimenting. The old homeless man who dies no matter what Phil tries, food, warmth, the hospital, every single time — that one doesn't bend. My students insist the second kind is the genuinely lawlike one and the first kind is mere habit, and I can't tell whether a Lewisian is allowed to say anything like that without smuggling in governance. Can the best-system view reconstruct the distinction — through the counterfactual resilience of the generalization, or through what does and doesn't follow from the axioms, or some other route — or does the Humean have to say Phil's difference in attitude is purely a difference in his head, a feeling enlivened by repetition, with nothing in the world corresponding to it? Note the awkward bit: the intervention-respecting regularities are the ones Phil has varied the most, so if anything they have more evidence behind them, not less.

The other thing I want help with, and it's the bit I care most about: there's one regularity in this film that isn't in the mosaic of any single day, and that's Phil. His piano playing, his ice carving, his memory of previous loops — those carry across resets while the town resets. So if the whole film is the mosaic, the best system has to contain something about the observer's own trajectory that looks nothing like a law of Punxsutawney, and the thing that actually accounts for the loop ending, namely Phil becoming someone worth letting out, ends up as a theorem about a mind rather than about a place. If instead a single day is the mosaic, then the exit condition isn't in the mosaic anywhere — it's a fact only the audience is positioned to see. Is that a real worry, i.e. is the film cheating by letting us watch a governing fact from outside that no inhabitant could ever confirm, or am I over-reading a storytelling device? And a smaller version of the same worry: does a mosaic that just repeats wreck the simplicity/strength tradeoff, so that the best system comes out degenerate — trivially simple and trivially strong at once?

What I want back is criticism, not a summary. Point me at which stretches of the film actually carry the weight and which ones I'm leaning on too hard. Say where the Humean comes out ahead and where the anti-Humean does, and be specific about which flavor of anti-Humeanism the film naturally invites — necessitation between universals, a primitive governing posit that produces the mosaic rather than summarizing it, or a view on which what makes laws special is that they hold across the widest range of counterfactual situations — becuase those are not interchangeable and the film's sympathies are not neutral between them. Then tell me where the picture resists the whole framing, where I'd be forcing it, and where an honest critic has to say the movie just isn't about this. Finish with what you think the film contributes to the debate that an argument on paper couldn't, if anything. No citations or suggested further reading, I can't chase those and I don't want them. And don't take my pitch at face value: if you think the loop is a Humean's nightmare rather than a Humean's showcase, say so and show me where.
