I cover science and ideas for a general-interest weekly, and I am reporting a piece on a proposal to publicly fund preimplantation screening for a bundle of traits loosely associated with health and longevity. Interviewing bioethicists and population economists has left me with an uncomfortable discovery: my own verdicts on the underlying cases do not line up. I want to lay the cases out as concretely as I can, give you my actual reactions including the ones I am least able to defend, and find out what principle, if any, they add up to.

One, the postponement. A researcher delays trying to conceive by four years to finish a grant cycle. Because of timing, the child she eventually has is a numerically different person from the one she would have had, and there is no reason to think this child's life will go worse. I think this is obviously unobjectionable, and I do not feel I need a justification for it.

Two, the requested trait. A deaf couple, at a clinic with eleven viable embryos, asks that one carrying the genetic variant for congenital deafness be implanted, and the clinic agrees. The child's life will be, by every measure I can find, a good one. I think the clinic acted wrongly, though I cannot identify anyone the decision made worse off, since the alternative for that child was never existing.

Three, the plant, told twice. A utility selects a cheap coolant loop for a facility it knows will leak a mutagen into the surrounding aquifer. Run the numbers so the leaking begins eighteen months after commissioning: children conceived after that point are born with a chronic respiratory condition that costs them roughly a decade of life, and I think the choice is clearly wrong. Now run the identical numbers with the leak beginning twenty-five years out. Every person affected in the second version is someone who would not have existed at all under the safer design; their lives remain worth living. My condemnation does not weaken by a single degree, and that invariance bothers me more than anything else on this list.

Four, the program I am covering. A state pays couples to screen and select among embryos for predicted healthspan, which changes which children exist without making any of them worse off, and which I find myself endorsing — even though I would think it monstrous if the same selection criteria were applied to children already born.

Five, the settlement. A frontier policy encourages a population several times larger than the alternative, whose members lead lives that are worth living but not much more than that, while leaving the existing population untouched. I think this is at most mildly regrettable, not wrong.

Six, the reversal. A policy that would replace that large, marginal population with a much smaller and much better-off one. I think this is wrong, even though no one is harmed by it and no one is benefited by refusing it.

My working formulation has been: an act is wrong only if it makes some particular person worse off than that person would otherwise have been, or if it lowers average wellbeing across the population that results. I suspect this gets two and five right and loses four, but I would rather be told precisely where it breaks than reassured.

What I am after, then: the two or three candidate principles that come closest to my six verdicts, each stated precisely enough that I could apply it to an unfamiliar case without further instruction; for each, which of my cases is doing the work of motivating each clause; what each predicts about two situations I have deliberately not resolved — a couple who take a fertility drug that raises the resulting child's risk of a painful but manageable condition, without which they would have no child at all, and a government that pays couples to have one fewer child so that the smaller resulting population is better off; and a plain account of which of my verdicts each principle forces me to surrender, ranked by how much the surrender costs me. If one of my reactions is not an intuition at all but motivated reasoning dressed as one, tell me which and why.
