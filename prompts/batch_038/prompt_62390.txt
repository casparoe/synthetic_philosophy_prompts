I am responsible for the formal ontology layer of an industrial asset registry: a single database that must decide, unambiguously and on demand, which composite entities exist, when they come into and go out of existence, and whether two records denote one object or two. Everything downstream keys off that answer — depreciation schedules, warranty liability, ownership, whether a given removal constitutes theft of one item or of several. The entities we actually have to register are not the ones that dominate the philosophical literature. Among the records in scope:

- a stock of identical bearings, physically indistinguishable and swapped between sites as a matter of routine, which operations insists is one asset even though its parts change hourly and are distributed across three countries;
- a production line that is dismantled each summer, shipped to another plant, and reassembled from the same components in a different arrangement;
- a sealed cooling circuit whose working fluid is continuously replaced, where it is genuinely unclear whether the circuit is the pipework, the fluid, or both;
- two machine frames bolted together, either of which functions perfectly well alone, whose joint registration exists only for insurance purposes;
- an assembly that is sixty per cent built and will either be completed or scrapped, with no determinate fact about which until months hence.

I would like the metaphysics settled before choosing a representation, so please write the desiderata: the specification any candidate answer to the special composition question — any account of when some things compose something — must meet in order to be usable here and, more importantly, defensible in general.

For each requirement, give the consideration that motivates it and, where it clarifies, a test case drawn from the list above or one of your own construction. I want the specification to cover, at minimum: the phenomena an account must explain or explain away, including our ordinary counts of objects, sorites-susceptible borderline situations, the apparent possibility of two objects sharing all their parts, and the worry that permissive accounts commit us to arbitrary or undetectable sums; the distinctions it must not collapse, such as that between composing and being identical, between a whole existing and a whole being a convenient posit, between being one thing and being counted as one, and between vagueness in what exists and vagueness in our knowledge of what precisely exists; the results concerning vagueness and restricted composition with which it must be consistent; and which of the demands routinely made in this debate are not genuine constraints at all but the preferences of one camp. I suspect at least two of the standard demands belong in that last category, and I want to know which ones and on what grounds.

Then flag any subset of the desiderata you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and demonstrate the conflict rather than asserting it. If the list turns out to be inconsistent, that is the most useful thing you can tell me, because it would mean the registry has to be built on a choice among defective options rather than on a correct theory. Finally, identify which single requirement, if relinquished, would admit the largest number of otherwise-excluded accounts: I want to know where the least resistance lies.

Close briefly — a few paragraphs, no more — with how unrestricted composition, universalism motivated by treating composition as a kind of identity, nihilism, and contact-or-fastening accounts fare against the list, and whether any of them could serve a system obliged to return a definite answer, on request, to whether a given record denotes one thing or two.

One condition on how you present this. Mark clearly which parts of your account are settled among people who work on composition and which remain live disputes, and label your own judgment calls as such. Do not allow a position you find attractive to appear as the consensus; where the field genuinely divides, say that it divides, then say which way you lean and why.
