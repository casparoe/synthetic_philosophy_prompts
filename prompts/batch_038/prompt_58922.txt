I'm writing the framework chapter of a thesis on which social kinds are constructed, and before comparing any accounts I want the specification they will be measured against. Give me the requirements list: what a theory of the social construction of a kind has to deliver to count as adequate — the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse (among them, a concept's being socially produced versus the thing's being socially produced, a kind's being constructed versus its being merely conventional, and a kind's having no reality independent of our classifications versus its having a reality sustained by them), and the findings from history, epidemiology, or economics it must not contradict — with the motivating case or consideration behind each item. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the partisan commitments of one camp, and flag any subset that cannot be jointly satisfied: if the list turns out to be an impossibility result, that is the main thing I need to know. Say also which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates.

Two stipulated kinds, invented so that nothing depends on your recall of actual examples. Chronofatigue: first named in diagnostic manuals a decade ago; prevalence estimates vary tenfold across countries with comparable healthcare; those diagnosed reorganise their work, sleep, and self-description in ways that make the diagnosis fit better over time; retrospective biomarker studies suggest the physiological profile was detectable decades before the label existed. Prime frontage: a stretch of shops counted as prime only because those who set rents and those who pay them expect everyone else to count it so; no marker distinguishes it that is not itself a function of that expectation; when enough actors withdraw the expectation the status is gone within two years and the buildings are unchanged; while it holds, it settles which businesses can survive there and therefore who the street is for. Which of your requirements separates these two? Which would, if left unqualified, rule out calling chronofatigue constructed at all? Is there any requirement that both violate — which would mean construction claims are two unrelated jobs wearing one phrase?

Finish with two or three sentences each on how three familiar postures fare against the list: a realist constructionism on which constructed kinds are wholly real and causally potent; an error-theoretic view on which calling a kind constructed is a polite way of saying no such kind exists; and a view that relocates construction from the world to our concepts of it.
