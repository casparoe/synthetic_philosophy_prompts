so I've been chewing on Dutch book / coherence arguments on and off for years — read the old LW sequences, Skyrms, the Lewis-style dynamic thing, Christensen's critique, whatever Hájek has written on the topic, Joyce's accuracy-dominance reframing — and I've concluded that "Dutch-bookable" gets used as though it names one property with one kind of force, when I'm pretty sure it's load-bearing in at least four different ways and nobody flags which one they mean. Before I write anything more about it, I want the spec.

Here's the job the argument has to be good enough *for*, so you can calibrate: I want a coherence argument I could put in front of a smart friend who says "sure, my credences don't sum right on that partition, so what? coherence is aesthetics, and I've never lost money to it." If the argument can't answer him, it's not doing the work I need.

So: write the desiderata. What does any coherence-style argument for probabilism actually have to deliver to count as adequate — which phenomena it has to explain, which cases it has to get right, which distinctions it's not allowed to collapse, which mathematical facts it has to be consistent with. For each one, the case or consideration behind it, and a label: hard requirement, or a preference of one camp dressed up as a requirement. Things I want explicitly in there, plus whatever I've missed:

- **simultaneity.** the book needs me to accept the whole package at once. if the bets get offered one at a time and I can walk away after the first, what exactly has been demonstrated? does the spec need to say which betting protocols are in scope, or is "you'd accept them all, given the chance" enough?
- **stakes.** I think the theorem only bites if I'll take a bet at any stake. if I cap my exposure — say I never risk more than a hundred bucks — can a book still be made against me at all, or does my incoherence just cost me nothing? is "works at stakes you'd actually take" a real desideratum, or does the argument legitimately get to be an idealization?
- **the bridge.** is the argument about my degrees of belief, or about my betting dispositions? if it needs a premise connecting the two, is that premise argued or just assumed, and does assuming it make the whole thing either circular or idle? I keep feeling like every version has to pay this toll somewhere and none of them say so out loud.
- **what counts as a loss.** money, utility, expected utility — and expected *by whose lights*? related: a book gets built by a bookie who knows my whole belief set. if the bookie is limited to what I know, or if I've got a compensating false belief that makes the "sure loss" bets locally favorable, is the loss still sure? does the argument need to survive being relativized to a bookie's information, and if so, what's left of it?
- **unbounded utilities and the infinitary cases.** people claim that once utilities aren't bounded, the equivalence between no-Dutch-book and probabilistic coherence breaks and you can get unavoidable losses. what exactly has to give, and is "your utilities better be bounded" a legitimate *output* of a coherence argument or a smuggled premise? same question for countable additivity: is violating it a defect the argument has to condemn, or is it outside the argument's jurisdiction?
- **bounded agents.** I can't check coherence across a million propositions and I can't compute the violating package. does the spec need a notion of feasible/bounded coherence that still inherits the argument's force? what happens to the "you'd lose money" story when finding the book is harder than the payoff?
- **binary vs graded.** does it have to deliver *how bad* a violation is, or is "coherent or not" enough? my friend's credences are off by two percent on one partition; if the framework can't say whether that's worse or better than being off by twenty on another, that feels like a gap.
- **unbet-on propositions.** half the propositions I have opinions about nobody will ever offer me a bet on, and I have no dispositions about them at all. does the argument need counterfactual betting machinery, and if it does, is that machinery doing real work or is it just a way of restating the probability axioms in gambling cosplay?
- **diachronics.** whatever gets you conditionalization has to not also license obviously silly policies, has to handle the case where I expect future-me to be offered different bets or to have changed my mind about what I'd accept, and has to handle the fact that I'll *know* I'm about to be offered a book.

Also: I've been carving this into three notions and I want you to tell me whether the carving is real or confused — Dutch-bookable in principle (there exists a package, for an idealized bookie with full access to my mind, at any stakes), Dutch-bookable in practice (someone in my actual epistemic environment could exploit me for more than transaction costs), and Dutch-bookable relative to a bookie who only sees my behavior on some subset of propositions. Do these come apart in interesting ways, and does an adequate argument have to say which one it's establishing?

Then the part I most want: **is any subset of these jointly unsatisfiable?** if the honest list can't all be met — if, say, non-circularity plus bite-against-my-friend plus working-at-real-stakes can't coexist — then the list *is* the finding, and I'd rather know that than get a nice scorecard. Tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are camp taste (my suspicion: "must establish probabilism in full generality" and "must be non-circular" are both doing a lot of unearned work, but argue with me). And which single desideratum knocks out the largest share of the standard arguments — that's the one I'll build around.

Short scorecard at the end is fine if you want (de Finetti/Skyrms-style, Lewis's dynamic version, the representation-theorem route, Joyce's accuracy-dominance version, money-pump variants), but keep it to a few lines each; the criteria are the deliverable.

One thing on epistemics: be explicit about what's settled among people who actually work on this versus what's genuinely contested, and don't dress up a contested position as consensus — I've been burned enough by confidently-stated folklore in this area that I'd rather have the uncertainty visible. And if any of my bullets above is a confused framing rather than a real question, say so bluntly instead of answering it politely.
