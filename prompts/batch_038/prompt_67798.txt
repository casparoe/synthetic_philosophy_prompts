I sit on the board of a forty-unit housing co-operative, and next month we vote on a proposal that has split us in a way I did not expect. Twelve years ago our bylaws let any member buy vacant units at a fixed formula price; a retired neighbour bought five, taking on debt at a moment when the co-op was close to insolvency and no one else would. Two of those units she now lets to young families well below market rent. Then the city rezoned our block for a transit stop and ground-floor shops — a change our board, myself included, spent three years lobbying for — and the value of every unit roughly tripled. Under the proposal, members would be capped at two units, and half the appreciation on any unit sold would go into a common fund. She says she acquired her units honestly under rules we all voted for, with money she borrowed and risk she alone took, and that stripping them now is simply taking what is hers; several board members say her windfall is really ours, produced by our campaign, and that letting one member hold an eighth of the co-op is unfair however it came about. I find both claims compelling and cannot see how to weigh them against each other. Which of these arguments survives scrutiny, what is the proposal getting right and wrong, and how should I vote — including whether some intermediate arrangement would be better than either position? Please write in British English. After your reasoning, give a JSON object with these fields: "the_stronger_claim" (one sentence); "what_the_proposal_gets_right"; "what_it_gets_wrong"; "options" (an array of objects, each with "option", "justification" and "main_objection"); "my_vote"; and "what_would_change_it".
