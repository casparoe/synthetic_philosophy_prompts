I'm teaching an upper-level seminar on Marx and the philosophy of history -- 18 students, 90 minutes, and they've already read the 1859 preface plus the bit of Cohen where he defends the primacy thesis by way of functional explanation. I want an in-class activity that makes them feel the gap between two things the base/superstructure metaphor can be made to mean: the base as a cause of superstructural features, versus the base as a selector that gets to explain whichever legal and political forms happen to stabilize it. Here's my rough idea, improve it or throw it out: give each small group a card describing only the forces of production of a society -- say gravity-fed irrigation agriculture, a deep-shaft coalfield, a container port, a phone-based gig platform -- and have them derive the property rules and the dominant ideas that society would need, then compare across groups. If they converge, determination looks vindicated; if they diverge they'll want to say the thesis is compatible with anything, and I want them to walk into that wall themselves before anyone says the word unfalsifiable out loud. So give me: the card set (at least six, including one deliberately nasty case where its not obvious whether a feature belongs to the base or to the superstructure at all), the exact rules and a minute-by-minute timeline, a debrief that sorts what actually happened onto causal vs. functional vs. merely-constraining readings of primacy, and a straight answer about what will go wrong the first time I run it and how I recover in the moment. Then tell me which part of the view the game structurally cannot reproduce and I have to supply by hand -- I'm guessing it's the time dimension, since functional explanation needs a feedback story, but correct me if I've got the wrong limitation. Finally: how confident are you that this setup produces the realization rather than just a fun sorting exercise, and which single claim in your design are you least sure of?
