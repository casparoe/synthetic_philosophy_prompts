I'm a software engineer, about 12 years in, mostly realtime systems — netcode, streaming, latency budgets. Zero philosophy coursework, ever, so please write for a smart person who's never read any of this. Here's the situation: we had an argument in the work Slack about a news story where a witness got the order of two events wrong, and one of our PMs kept saying "your brain rewrites the tape — you're always living on a delay," which he used to conclude that a person's report of what happened first is basically worthless evidence. I said that looked like a non-sequitur and got told I don't understand how perception works. So I went down a rabbit hole that night and ended up at a demo that I think is what everyone's vaguely gesturing at: you flash two coloured dots on a screen, a few degrees apart and well under a second between them (I think like 100ms), and people don't report two flashes. They report one dot travelling across and switching colour partway — and they'll swear the switch happened before the second dot came on, at a moment when neither dot was actually on the screen. That last part broke my brain, because it looks like information from a later moment somehow altered what was experienced at an earlier one, which I'd have said was impossible.

The nearest thing I have from work is the choice you make in a multiplayer game between input delay and rollback. Input delay: you hold a frame back a couple ticks until both players' inputs have arrived, then simulate and render it, so what you show is correct but late. Rollback: you render your best guess immediately, and when the real input lands you rewind and re-render the corrected version. Those are genuinely different architectures, and in a game you can tell which one you're running by looking at the machine. My suspicion is that "your brain rewrites the tape" is doing blanket duty for several claims that aren't anywhere near equivalent — one of them is probably just the boring fact that light plus nerves plus cortex takes tens of milliseconds, which would be true and totally inert philosophically, and at least one other is something much weirder about whether there was even a determinate experience at the earlier moment for a later process to edit. I might be wrong that they're seperate claims, though.
