so I'm putting togehter my position for a workshop on whose values a publicly deployed assistant should actually serve, and I want to know whether the package holds together before I say it out loud in front of people. here's what I've landed on:

(1) ordinalism. there's no fact of the matter about how much one person values something compared to how much another does — only rankings are real, cardinal interpersonal intensities are made up.

(2) whatever rule we use has to be anonymous (nobody's input counts for more in virtue of who they are) and has to respect weak Pareto.

(3) I take Arrow seriously but I don't think it's fatal for me, because my move is to restrict the domain rather than give anything else up. my empirical bet is that people's orderings over the actual options are single-peaked along one main axis — roughly deference to traditional authority vs. individual autonomy — so the median-voter result does the work and the remaining conditions can stay untouched.

(4) but I don't want the median running roughshod, so I want carve-outs: any constituency of 5% or more gets a veto over any output that would require them to go against whatever their own ranking puts at the very top. I've been calling it the core-value veto.

(5) legitimacy is public justifiability: the *rule*, not the outcome, has to be endorseable by the people who lose under it, and endorseable from inside their own values rather than from some neutral standpoint they don't share.

(6) I'm not a value platonist — I don't think there's a standpoint-independent fact about which weighting is correct. but I do think my rule is the right one, because it's what contractors would choose behind a veil of ignorance where they don't know which constituency they'll land in, and behind that veil they'd be pretty maximin-ish.
