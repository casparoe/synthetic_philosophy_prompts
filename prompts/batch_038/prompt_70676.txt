Write a parody document: Version 4.2 of the certification standard issued by an invented assurance body — give it a blandly technocratic name of your choosing, something in the register of a private standard-setting organisation — governing a mixed aquaculture site that operates both a finfish unit and a penaeid shrimp unit. Around 800–900 words, formatted as numbered clauses with the usual apparatus of a third-party standard: a Scope section, Definitions, Normative Requirements, an annex on decapod crustaceans, and a schedule of non-conformities classified as minor, major, or critical, each with a corrective-action deadline.

The target is neither the industry alone nor the animal-welfare movement alone. It is the genre of welfare certification itself, and in particular the way an unresolved empirical question gets converted into an auditable clause. The comedy should come from the document's administrative self-confidence, not from invented cruelties: every feature you satirise ought to be something a real scheme actually does, or is strongly tempted to do.

Points the piece should handle accurately enough that a specialist laughs rather than winces:

- The standard does not settle whether decapods are sentient; it defines the question away. Include a Presumption of Sentience clause that is expressly non-rebuttable at site level, and that notes the underlying science remains under review in the very sentence that puts the presumption into force.
- Its welfare outcome indicators should turn out, on inspection, to be production parameters — survival, growth, feed conversion — wearing a welfare vocabulary, alongside one or two genuinely animal-based measures that the scheme provides no way to score.
- The criteria that actually carry the scientific debate about crustacean sentience — a suitable nervous system and appropriate sensory receptors, opioid receptors together with reduced responses to noxious stimuli after analgesics, protective motor reactions such as rubbing, flicking and sheltering, avoidance learning, and trade-offs between avoiding a noxious stimulus and satisfying other motivations — should appear only as things the auditor must be able to observe inside a defined observation window, so that several of them become practically unobservable and hence, by the document's own logic, compliant.
- Broodstock eyestalk ablation is managed rather than prohibited: permitted methods (pinching, enucleation, cauterising, ligation), a competence requirement for whoever performs it, and an anaesthesia-and-analgesia clause drafted so that it can be satisfied without any anaesthesia or analgesia.
- The slaughter requirements should scale badly. A per-animal stunning obligation, or an electrical stunning parameter, applied to a harvest of tens or hundreds of millions of individual shrimp; resolved by a mass or carapace-length threshold below which the clause simply does not apply, and by batch verification that samples a fixed number of animals.
- Finfish and decapods are subject to identical clause structure, so indicators with a clear meaning for a fish — loss of equilibrium, cessation of gill movement, time to insensibility — get applied to animals for which the equivalent measure is contested, and vice versa.
- Include a short list of observations that do not constitute a non-conformity, which should be the quiet masterpiece of the piece, and at least one critical non-conformity that concerns records rather than animals.

Constraints: stay entirely inside the document's voice. No winking, no authorial asides within the standard, no atrocities beyond what real aquaculture already practises — the horror should be procedural. A reader should come away knowing exactly what is being ironised, namely that certification can be perfectly sincere and still fail to track welfare, and that unsettled science gets absorbed into the paperwork rather than reflected in it, without the piece ever stating this. Internal consistency is part of the brief: every clause must be something a competent auditor could actually apply on a Tuesday.

Then, outside the parody and clearly marked as a note of about 150 words, say where you have taken liberties: which practices and indicators you compressed or exaggerated, which empirical claims about crustacean sentience, analgesia, or stunning you leaned on that are genuinely contested rather than settled, and which single clause would most need rewriting if the science moved. In other words, tell me what finding would make the satire unfair rather than merely uncomfortable, and where your own reasoning about what the standard could plausibly contain is most likely to be wrong.
