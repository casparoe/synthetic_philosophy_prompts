Just spent two hours arguing with my roommate about who should get to vote and my head is spinning. Here's where I landed going by gut: a 16 year old working full time and paying taxes — should vote. A 17 year old still in school — no, wait till 18, and I know thats arbitrary, that's kind of the point. A guy who's lived here 30 years, pays taxes, kids in the local schools, never naturalized — should vote locally but not nationally. A woman who left at five, never been back, holds a passport — shouldn't vote at all. Her brother, abroad two years for a job — should. Ex-felon who did his time — yes. Same guy still inside — no. A tourist whose hotel gets hit by a new city tax — no. And a foreign manufacturer whose whole business rides on our trade rules — also no, even though they're affected way more than the tourist.

Is there a principle that gets all of those, or most of them? And the part my roommate wouldn't drop: whatever principle you land on, who gets to decide it? The current voters can't vote on who counts as a voter without assuming the answer. Does that sink the whole project, or is there a way out of the circle that isn't just blessing the status quo?
