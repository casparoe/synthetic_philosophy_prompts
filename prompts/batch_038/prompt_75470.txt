I am commissioning one piece for an anthology of satirical technical writing, and I would like you to write it.

**The deliverable.** A mock international technical standard of roughly 1,100 to 1,400 words, issued by a fictional standards body of your invention, whose purpose is to certify that all instances of pleasure share one specifiable property. The document exists because a likewise invented national well-being accounting regime requires public agencies to report aggregate pleasure, and an audit regime cannot operate on a term that has not been given a single conformance-testable specification. The satire has two targets: the philosophical assumption that pleasure must be one thing if it is to carry the explanatory weight a theory of well-being places on it, and the bureaucratic discourse that assumes anything of that sort can be defined, tested, and certified. Neither target should ever be named as a target.

**The form.** Structure it as a real standard is structured, and hold the deadpan register from the first line to the last: front matter with a title, document number, edition, date of issue, and a short Foreword explaining why the previous edition's definition had to be withdrawn; Clause 1 Scope; Clause 2 Normative references, all invented; Clause 3 Terms and definitions, including the unit in which conformity is expressed and the paradigm specimens to which the definitions apply; Clause 4 Conformance requirements, with decimal sub-clauses, one per candidate specification of what pleasures have in common; Clause 5 Test methods, including a designated reference specimen against which instruments are calibrated, how that specimen was selected, and the difficulty of maintaining it; Clause 6 Nonconformity register, as a table; Clause 7 Stakeholder comments and committee dispositions, as a table; Annex A (informative) Excluded cases; Annex B (informative) Revision history, as a table.

**Where the philosophy goes.** It has to be carried entirely by the technical language, never by argumentative prose. Clause 4 must take up as competing specifications at least: a distinctive felt quality present in every pleasure; a common functional role; being such that the subject wants it to continue; being disposed to seek more of it; a common signature detectable by instrumentation; pleasure treated not as a quality shared by experiences but as a higher-order dimension along which they vary in degree; and pleasure treated as the disjunction of an enumerated list. Give each specification a conformance test, and give each test at least one documented nonconformity drawn from the specimens defined in Clause 3. Those specimens should span, among others, the warmth of a bath, the taste of something, relief that a feared diagnosis did not arrive, the satisfaction of working a problem through, amusement at a bad pun, pleasure taken in news about a person one dislikes, pleasure in remembering, pleasure in anticipating, and pleasure taken in a landscape. The register should make clear which specification fails which cases and on what ground; the revision history should show the standard's scope narrowing edition by edition as nonconformities accumulate, until the certified class is noticeably smaller than the class everyone began with.

**Two further requirements.** First, the document must handle silently, through its definitions and test methods rather than through argument, the question of whether pleasure is a sensation at all or a way of standing toward some state of affairs, since several of the paradigm specimens are plainly of the latter kind. Whatever the committee decides must be applied consistently, and it must have visible consequences for which specimens get certified and which are excluded in Annex A. Second, at some point a party must raise the parallel worry about pains, and the committee must dispose of that comment in a manner that is technically defensible and procedurally evasive, since the same audit logic applied to the pain side would unravel the standard as well.

**Restraints.** In Clause 7 include at least one comment that identifies a genuine problem with the project and is dismissed on procedural grounds, and at least one comment from a vendor or test laboratory that misunderstands what the standard is for and is answered correctly. All bodies, laboratories, instruments, officials, and vendors must be invented. Do not name or allude to any real philosopher, book, article, or famous thought experiment under its usual label, do not cite anything, and do not append a reading list; the piece must stand on its own technical vocabulary. A reader who has never encountered the debate should still enjoy it as a portrait of an institution certifying the uncertifiable. A reader who has should be able to reconstruct, from the tables alone and without being told, which account of pleasure the committee has settled on, exactly which paradigm specimens that account cannot cover, and what has been done about them.
