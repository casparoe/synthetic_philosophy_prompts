For eleven years I have run a play-by-post game in which the rules themselves are what we play with. There are eleven of us enrolled. The Charter we started from had three rules that matter here, which I will paraphrase rather than reproduce:

- C1: A proposal is adopted if, when the seven-day voting period closes, more enrolled players have voted for it than against it.
- C2: Any rule of the Charter, including C2 itself, may be repealed, amended, or replaced by an adopted proposal, and new rules may be added in the same way.
- C3: Neither C1 nor C3 itself may ever be repealed, amended, or suspended.

Two years ago the following happened. Proposal 1 read: C3 is repealed. It carried 6–5. A month later Proposal 2 read: C1 is amended so that a proposal is adopted only if at least two-thirds of the enrolled players vote for it. That carried 7–4, which was a majority but not two-thirds. For the next two years we voted under the two-thirds rule and adopted something like forty proposals under it, including one that created an officer who certifies results and one that changed how a player withdraws. Then somebody noticed that C3 forbade its own repeal, so Proposal 1 may never have been adopted, in which case C3 was still in force, in which case C1 could not be amended, in which case the two-thirds rule was never a rule, in which case the forty proposals adopted under it were never adopted either. Last month Proposal 3 was offered: the adoption of Proposal 1 is hereby ratified and confirmed, and every proposal adopted since is valid as though it had been adopted correctly. It carried 8–3, comfortably above two-thirds.

The group has split, and it is no longer really arguing about the game.

The Acceptance camp says the game never stopped and never had a hole in it. What makes a rule part of our Charter is that we play by it, and by the time anyone noticed, two years and forty proposals had gone past under the two-thirds rule without a single objection; Proposal 3 was at worst a formality and at best a genuine repair. They add that the other camp uses the word void as though we had a court, and we do not.

The Authorization camp says an act is adopted only if a rule in force at the moment of voting authorized it. C3 forbade repealing C3, so Proposal 1 was not adopted; so C3 still forbade amending C1; so Proposal 2 was not adopted; so the two-thirds rule was never in force; so Proposal 3 was not voted under anything capable of giving it force. A defect cannot cure itself, and ratification by a body that was never authorized is only another unauthorized act. They say the honest way forward is for all eleven of us to sign a fresh charter, which would be a founding and not an amendment.

I have no training in law or logic and I never went to college; I have been turning this over by myself for two years, mostly because I suspect what we stumbled into is not really about our game. What I want is the document we should have produced instead of two years of posts. Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between these two camps would end with, assuming both argue in good faith and neither is trying to win.

Please give me four parts.

Part 1: a numbered list of claims both camps can sign exactly as written, including whatever they agree about the Charter itself. I expect they both agree that C2 and C3 cannot be given full effect at the same time, and that nothing outside the game will settle which gives way. Do not manufacture agreement to make the list look substantial; any line one camp would flinch at belongs in Part 2.

Part 2: the disagreements that survive, as a table with a column giving the disagreement as the Acceptance camp would state it, a column giving it as the Authorization camp would state it, a column stating the same disagreement in neutral language that neither camp wrote, and a column marking whether the difference is substantive or only a matter of what the words are being asked to do. I have read both camps' posts for two years, so please put nothing in either camp's column that its own side would reject. If either label is one its members would not accept, propose a better one and say why both sides could live with it.

Part 3: the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one camp. Candidates I would guess at are what makes a rule part of the game, whether a rule that forbids its own repeal can be repealed under a general power to repeal any rule, and whether Proposal 3 differs in practice from all eleven of us signing a new charter today. For each crux, give a case or argument both camps would accept as bearing on it, record in advance what each camp expects that case to show if its own account is correct, and say what each camp would treat as a reason to change its mind. Flag any camp that cannot say what would count against it. These four variants of our situation have already been argued over, and you may use them or write better ones: (a) suppose C2 had said that no proposal may be adopted during the first year of play, one was adopted in month three, and everyone played under it for a decade; (b) suppose a rule appoints one player to certify results and she certifies a proposal that plainly lacked the votes, and everyone acts on the certification for years; (c) suppose a new rule says it may be amended only by a unanimous proposal, and ten of us later amend it over one dissent; (d) suppose all eleven of us sign a statement saying that the rule set we are now playing by is the Charter and always was.

Part 4: a short closing paragraph both camps could sign, saying what they are still arguing about, what they have stopped arguing about, and what they now think this game is an example of.

Three constraints. I am not asking who is right, and I do not want a diagnosis of why the argument is stuck; I want the text both sides could put their names to. Do not dissolve the whole disagreement by declaring it verbal, though if part of it is verbal, say which part and why, and tell me what is left over. And please give me no case names, book titles, authors, or reading list of any kind: I cannot check any of it, and I would rather have the reasoning than pointers I cannot follow. Plain language throughout, and American English.
