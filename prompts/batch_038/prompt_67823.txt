Background, sorry this is long. I'm not an academic in any way — I drove a forklift for eleven years and now I do community outreach for a county government, and I got put on a 7-person steering committee because they wanted "a resident's voice." We're building a chat assistant that answers public questions out of our records databases: permits, business licenses, property ownership, some court stuff. Go-live is about six weeks out and the committee has burned three meetings on a question that sounds trivial and turns out not to be.

The question: what does the tool say when a question contains a description that doesn't pick anything out, or picks out the wrong number of things?

Three buckets from our test logs:

1. Nothing matches. "Is the business license for Joe's Diner still active?" when no such license was ever issued. Or "Who owns 412 Kestrel Rd?" when the parcel record came back blank after a data migration. Camp A wants the tool to say "There is no active business license for Joe's Diner" — a flat denial, because residents hate weasel words and this is what a human at the front desk would say. Camp B says thats a claim about the world and we can't always back it, since sometimes the license really exists and our database is just missing the row; camp B wants "I couldn't find a match in county records" every single time.

2. More than one matches. "The permit for the duplex on Marsh Street" — three permits, all superficially plausible. Camp A wants the tool to take the most recent one and answer, because a helpful human would. Camp B says that is silently inventing a referent, and if we guessed wrong we've just told somebody their building is legal when it isn't.

3. The words themselves are contested. A resident asks "Did the winner of the 2023 beautification award ever pay that fine?" There were two co-winners and no fine on record for either. Half the committee thinks the resident obviously had one particular person in mind and the right move is to ask which one; the other half thinks the tool has no business pretending to read minds and should just dump both records.

Here's the thing I keep circling and can't settle, and I want the memo to actually engage it instead of splitting the difference down the middle. Both camps claim they're the "accurate" ones, and I've realized they don't agree about something more basic — about what a sentence like "The owner of 412 Kestrel Rd is X" is even doing when there's no owner. One camp treats it as simply false and thinks saying so is honesty. The other camp acts like calling it false is a category mistake, like the question misfired rather than failed, and that turning a misfire into a confident "no" is how the county ends up in the newspaper. My hunch, which may be confused: the two candidate error messages that both camps treat as interchangeable aren't the same claim at all. "There is no active license for Joe's Diner" seems to commit us to something extra — that no such diner-and-license situation exists anywhere — and "It is not the case that Joe's Diner's license is active" seems like it might come apart from "Joe's Diner's license is not active," and I genuinely don't know which of those our flat denial is. Please work that out properly and tell me whether I'm onto something real or just tying myself in knots, because if it matters it changes the wording we hand the vendor.
