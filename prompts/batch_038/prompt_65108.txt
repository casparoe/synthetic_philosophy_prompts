I'm putting together a section on aggregation for a reading group and I keep hitting a wall with the dust specks vs. one person tortured for fifty years case. Everyone in my group who picks the torture says some version of "no number of specks ever adds up to an agony," and everyone who picks the specks says "sure it does, that's just arithmetic." What I actually want to know is whether the first crowd is objecting to the *scale* (a genuine discontinuity in how bad things can be) or to the *spread* (that it's a huge number of separate people each getting a little bit, rather than one person getting a lot) — because those are completely different complaints and people run them together constantly.

So build me a variant that pries them apart. Specs:

- The scenario proper should be one paragraph, and it should be the same choice as the original: same single victim, same fifty years, same intensity, same total number of specks, same feel and duration per speck. The only thing your allowed to change is how that fixed total of specks gets distributed across people. No uploading, no new machinery beyond the button and the absurd population the original already assumes, and no probabilities — no "but you can't really be sure how bad a speck is" escape hatches.
- It has to be a case in which a firm torture-picker from the original, running her own stated principle and not a conveniently new one, ends up picking the specks. Don't stipulate the flip, generate it. If the only way to get her to flip is to change what a speck feels like or how long the agony lasts, then the design fails and I'd rather you say that outright than fudge it.
- Write her principle out as an actual principle — something with the shape of a claim that could be true or false, not a slogan. Do the same for a smooth-scale total utilitarian and for someone whose objection is specifically about spreading burdens across separate people. Then give me the verdict each of the three delivers on the original case *and* on your variant, and make it come out so that at least two of them swap their relative ranking of the two options between the cases.
- Add one intermediate rung, a sentence or two, same fixed total of specks but split across some middling number of people of your choosing, and tell me which of the three rungs is the one that genuinely discriminates between the discontinuity story and the spread story.

Then the honest part. Which single feature of your variant is doing all the work? What does the case collapse into if I take that feature back out? What's the best reply available to someone who insists your variant is a cheap trick — and I suspect this one's coming — is concentrating that many specks into a single life even describable as "dust specks" any more, or does the number quietly stop being a mild irritation and turn into something worse than the torture on its own? If you think that objection sinks the design, tell me what range of numbers I'd have to work in for it to still go through.
