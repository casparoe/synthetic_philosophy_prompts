**Partition Dependence and the Manufacture of Rivals**

*§1. Two standing results that cannot both be doing their assigned work.* The literature on underdetermination contains, side by side, two results that appear to cancel each other. The first is the triviality result. For any theory T with observational consequences O(T), one can construct a rival T* that entails exactly O(T) yet is logically incompatible with T. Two recipes are standard. One conjoins T with a claim that has no observational consequences at all — that an inert field pervades space, or that the world came into being five minutes ago complete with misleading records. The other negates T and appends auxiliary hypotheses engineered to restore O(T) precisely. Both recipes are mechanical and can be run without limit, so the class of empirically equivalent rivals to any theory is not merely nonempty but inexhaustible. If that is all underdetermination amounts to, the thesis is uninformative: it is a theorem about the freedom of conjunction, not a discovery about evidence. The second result is the Duhemian one, which is supposed to be substantive. A physical theory meets experience only as a bundle, so a failed prediction never identifies its own target; it tells us that something in the bundle is false without telling us what, and the choice of what to surrender is not dictated by the data. I do not see how the second can be a deep insight into the structure of testing if the first is a cheap trick about conjunction. Both cannot carry the weight their partisans place on them.

*§2. Diagnosis: equivalence is a relation between partitions, not between doctrines.* My claim is that the two results do not conflict because they are not about the same relation. Empirical equivalence is never a binary relation between two free-standing bodies of doctrine. It is a ternary relation among two doctrines and a way of carving each into a core and a periphery — a partition. Let a partition of a doctrine D pick out a core C, whose members are treated as constitutive of the theory's identity, and an auxiliary set A, comprising boundary and initial conditions, instrument and measurement models, idealizations, bridge principles, and borrowed background theories. What is tested at any moment is C conjoined with some subset of A; what is preserved when a prediction fails is settled by which member of the bundle gets demoted.

Both triviality recipes, on this account, are operations on partitions rather than constructions of content. Conjoining T with an inert claim promotes surplus content into the core without adding anything to the periphery capable of bearing weight when an anomaly arrives. Negating T and appending restorative auxiliaries shifts the whole of T's content into the periphery at once. In each case the rival's empirical content is derived from the host's, back-fitted to it, and nothing has been added to the doctrine's capacity to absorb, resist, or generate anomalies.

So: the migration lemma. For any partition of a doctrine D there is another doctrine D′ with the same observational consequences under which content has been moved across the core/periphery boundary. It follows that the number of empirically equivalent rivals is a function of the freedom of the partition, and is unbounded whenever the partition is unconstrained. The triviality result is a corollary of this lemma, and is therefore a theorem about partitions rather than about evidence. What it establishes is not that underdetermination is trivial, but that the equivalence relation it invokes is defined over an unconstrained family of partitions. The Duhemian result survives because it concerns a constrained family: partitions in which the core is genuinely revisable, but only in ways the doctrine itself licenses. I will call a rival generated by unconstrained migration a parasite on its host, and I will use partition dependence for the general thesis that claims of empirical equivalence are incomplete until a partition is specified.

*§3. A criterion for genuine rivalry.* A rival R to T is genuine, I propose, only if R's core carries its own positive heuristic — that is, only if R determines, in advance of any anomaly, a distinctive and nonempty set of auxiliary moves it licenses. Three clauses:

(i) Asymmetric risk. There must be some possible recalcitrant phenomenon P, in a domain on which T and R are both presently silent, such that R's core licenses an auxiliary response to P that T's core does not, or forbids one that T permits. The rivals must diverge in what they license, not only in what they assert.
(ii) Non-derivation. R's auxiliaries must not have been selected by back-fitting to T's consequences; R must have been articulable independently of an enumeration of O(T).
(iii) Stability under migration. R's core must remain R's core under the operations of §2. A doctrine that is merely T re-carved fails.

Parasites fail all three. Lorentz's ether theory, with a privileged rest frame and a contraction hypothesis, and special relativity, which dispenses with both, agree on every observation within their common domain; that was historically the point of the contraction hypothesis. Yet they satisfy (i): the ether program's heuristic was to explain the dynamical effects of motion through the ether by further molecular and electrodynamic hypotheses, while the relativistic program's was to re-derive mechanics from symmetry principles, and these heuristics license different responses to the same anomaly. They are genuine rivals, and their equivalence is therefore a real problem rather than a notational artifact.

Newtonian spacetime, which supplies a distinguished state of absolute rest, and neo-Newtonian spacetime, which supplies absolute acceleration but no fact of the matter about absolute velocity, are the harder case for me. Given force laws that depend only on relative positions and velocities, no experiment detects absolute velocity, and the two are empirically equivalent. But the neo-Newtonian doctrine is obtained by deleting surplus structure; it licenses no auxiliary move its rival does not, and so it fails (i). My criterion therefore classifies the standard textbook paradigm of empirical equivalence as parasitic. I think that is a cost I can pay, and possibly a virtue: nothing competes for belief in that case, and counting it in the skeptic's ledger has done unearned work. I am not confident, and §5 returns to it.

*§4. Consequences.* First, for the constructive empiricist. If the choice between theories that agree on observables can only be pragmatic, that presupposes a fixed and well-defined equivalence relation. But agreement on observables is agreement relative to a partition, and two partitions may license different auxiliary developments, so the difference between T and R is not exhausted by what they say about the unobservable. The empiricist may fix the partition by stipulation, but then the equivalence on which the argument depends is legislated rather than found, and the conclusion drawn from it is correspondingly weaker. Second, for Quine. His later suggestion that empirically equivalent systems of the world can be reconciled by reconstruing their predicates, so that the apparent rivalry dissolves into notational variance, is on my account the recognition that the equivalence was partition-relative all along; reconstrual is itself a partition-shifting operation. Quine's dissolution therefore concedes my point rather than defeating it. What requires defense is not that partitions can be shifted, which is obvious, but that some shifts preserve a doctrine's identity and others destroy it. Third, for the argument from unconceived alternatives. That argument needs a notion of a rival that is not merely logically possible but scientifically available, and availability is exactly what §3 supplies: an alternative, had it been conceived, would have had to arrive with a heuristic of its own. The historical cases on which the argument rests are cases of partition-legitimate rivals that were available and overlooked. This makes the argument's key premise precise at the price of narrowing it.

*§5. Two worries I cannot yet discharge.* (a) My criterion makes admissible partitions depend partly on a community's actual dispositions to revise, which injects a sociological term into what is supposed to be a logical relation among theories. Read dispositionally, empirical equivalence ceases to hold between doctrines and holds instead between doctrines and research communities. Read counterfactually — as I prefer — I owe an account of what makes it true that a given core licenses this auxiliary move rather than that one, and I do not have one. (b) The criterion may be self-immunizing. Any rival that turns out to be constructible by recipe can be dismissed afterward as a parasite, and any rival that resists construction can be welcomed as genuine, so the criterion never exposes itself to refutation.
