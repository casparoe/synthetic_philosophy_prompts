A guy I know who does sports betting and reads way too much theory wrote this up for me about the Kelly criterion, and I want to know how much of it to trust:

"Kelly betting is just the claim that you should maximize the expected log of your bankroll, i.e. use log utility. Kelly's original 1956 paper set it up as a gambler who gets noisy information about the outcome, and showed the best exponential growth rate he can achieve equals the information rate of that channel. That growth-rate answer turns out to be the same thing as maximizing expected log wealth, which is what Peters means by ergodicity economics: no utility function is needed at all, because the long-run growth rate is an objective property of the gamble rather than a taste. Bet more than Kelly and your growth rate falls and your chance of going bust goes up; bet less and you're leaving growth on the table for no reason. So in the long run Kelly strictly dominates — any other fixed fraction ends up poorer than Kelly with probability approaching one — which makes it the uniquely rational policy for repeated betting, and anyone who says it's 'too risky' just hasn't understood that variance stops mattering once you're compounding."

I'm inclinded to agree with him, mostly because I like that it doesn't smuggle in a utility function, but the 'uniquely rational' and 'with probability one' parts feel too clean, and I've also seen people say Kelly ignores that you might die, or need the money next year, or not know the true odds. Go through it claim by claim: what's straight up right, what's a defensible simplification, and what's wrong or at least seriously contested. I especially want to know whether the 'no utility function needed' move is as free as he thinks it is, what the domination result actually assumes, whether 'going bust' even means what he says under proportional betting, and whether his verdict on the 'too risky' people survives once the errors get fixed. Please answer in American English.
