I have no formal training in logic or in philosophy. What I have instead is about a decade of thinking on my own, which began when I worked slowly through Gödel, Escher, Bach and never quite stopped. I am trying to set my position down in writing for a discussion group I attend, and before I do that I would like a careful audit of whether it hangs together. I am not looking for a tutorial, and I cannot look anything up at the moment, so I would ask you to argue from the ideas themselves rather than sending me to sources.

Here is what I believe.

1. Gödel's first incompleteness theorem states a permanent structural fact about any system rich enough to do arithmetic and to represent its own sentences. It is not a defect that better engineering, more computing power, or a cleverer design could remove.

2. When a hierarchy tangles, the tangle is survivable only because something stands outside it. In Escher's Drawing Hands the paradox is bearable because the cuffs stay flat on the sheet, and the sheet rests on a table, and the artist stands behind the whole arrangement. I have come to think that every tangled hierarchy has what Hofstadter calls an inviolate level, a level the tangle cannot reach, and that this is the general shape of any genuine resolution of paradox.

3. I also think that a mind is precisely such a tangle, all the way up. My thoughts about my thoughts are made of the same material as the thoughts themselves. There is no inner reader and no vantage point outside myself from which I survey myself. Nothing in a person occupies the role that the flat cuffs occupy in the picture.

4. Therefore I reject the argument, associated with Lucas and with Penrose, that Gödel's theorem shows machines cannot match minds. That argument seems to me to require a vantage point on our own reliability that we simply do not have, and I do not think the theorem carries any such consequence.

5. I think the liar paradox and its relatives are artifacts of notation. If languages are kept properly stratified, so that no sentence may speak about the sentences of its own language, the puzzles never arise. I take that to be the moral of Tarski's work, and I suspect most paradoxes dissolve under careful bookkeeping.

6. I think no system can produce a complete account of itself, and that this is a limit in principle rather than a matter of time or resources. I apply it to my own case: however much introspection I do, a full description of me by me is not on offer.

And here are three verdicts on particular cases, which I want held to the same standard as the principles above.

A. A program that prints its own source code is not a genuine tangle. The output is inert; nothing in it reaches back and constrains the program. Self-reference by itself is not what I mean by a tangled hierarchy.

B. A diary in which each entry comments on the entry before it is not a tangle either. It is a ladder that never closes, since there is always one more rung available. Ladders are harmless; only loops are dangerous.

C. A camera aimed at its own monitor is the clearest physical case of a genuine tangle that I know of, and I count it as evidence that tangles are features of the world rather than symptoms of sloppy writing.

I have no idea which of these is the weak link. I suspect that some pairs that look contradictory to me are perfectly consistent, and that some pairs I have never thought to compare are in serious trouble. Please treat none of the nine items as safe by default, and do not assume that the ones stated most confidently are the ones best supported.

What I would like back is an audit rather than a lecture. Specifically: identify the pairs that genuinely cannot all be held together, and rank them by severity, worst first. For each, say precisely which item would have to be given up or weakened, what the weakened version would look like, and what I would lose by accepting it. Then identify the pairs that only appear to conflict and say what the confusion is in each case, since I would rather not spend the group's time defending a problem I do not have. Finally, tell me which single revision to the set would restore the most consistency for the least cost, and whether each of the three case verdicts survives that revision unchanged. Where the reasoning turns on a technical point, I would appreciate having the point made concretely, with the relevant case actually worked through, rather than being handed a name to go and look up.
