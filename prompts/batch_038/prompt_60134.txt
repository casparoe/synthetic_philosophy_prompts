For eleven years I have worked as a software localizer. I manage string files and resource keys, maintain translation memory and terminology databases, write context notes so that translators know what a label is doing on screen, and argue with engineers about why one key cannot simply be reused across two unrelated surfaces. I have never taken a philosophy course. But I have been reading on my own about the Twin Earth thought experiment and the externalist position it is supposed to establish, and I keep having the uncomfortable sense that the moves being made there resemble things I already do for a living. I would like that impression tested rather than indulged.

Restate the Twin Earth case and the externalist thesis in the vocabulary I actually use — keys and resolved values, locales and fallback chains, translation memory, glossaries, context notes, pseudo-localization, builds and deployment environments — as faithfully as you can manage. Begin with the case itself: two identical builds, identical binaries, identical runtime state, shipped into two environments that differ in what a word displayed on screen picks out. Then take the rest of the package in order, one piece at a time rather than as a general impression: that meaning is not settled by what is inside the system; that reference is fixed partly by the world and partly by a community's usage, with specialists carrying the load for everyone else; the modal claim about what could have been otherwise; and finally the step from all of this to a conclusion about thought rather than merely about words.

For each piece, give me the residue report. Say which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion (naming exactly what you had to bend to make it fit), and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Please do not let the analogy earn more than it deserves: if a claim only appears to translate because you have quietly imported an assumption from my side of the fence and called it a rendering, flag it. And where my vocabulary turns out to be richer than the philosophical one — places where keys, locales, and fallback behaviour make distinctions the philosophers have no name for — note that as well, since it may be a clue to what they are circling.

Then give me your judgment: is the residue a defect in my vocabulary, meaning there is a concept I am simply missing, or is it a sign that the original claim was less determinate than its presentation suggests? Where you are unsure, say so rather than manufacturing a verdict. As you go, mark clearly which parts of the externalist picture are settled among people who work on this and which are genuinely disputed — in particular, whether it is agreed that no environment-independent content survives at all, and whether the argument is generally taken to carry over from words to thoughts. If the consensus is narrower than the way the case is usually advertised, say that plainly; I would rather be told that the field is split than be handed one side's conclusion as the settled view.
