For my STS seminar I have to write a short piece on Winner's "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" and I want to build my own case instead of recycling the Long Island parkways or the McCormick molding machine that everybody uses. Can you construct one to these specs?

Setting: a mid-sized public institution today — a city, a school district, a public utility, whatever feels natural. No sci-fi, and no software, algorithms or AI at all. The artifact should be physical infrastructure or a machine, because part of what I'm testing is whether the argument needs anything computational to get going.

The scenario itself should be one paragraph.

It has to do two things at once:

1. The designers deliberately build in a political effect in Winner's first sense — the thing settles some standing local dispute in favor of one group over another — and that effect actually materializes. I dont want the design to fail or backfire; that's the easy version.

2. The artifact also turns out to be inherently political in the second sense, but pulling the opposite way on the same axis (who ends up with power over whom), and over a twenty year horizon the inherent politics should come out stronger than the designed ones.

Hard constraint on 2, and this is the part I keep botching when I try it myself: the reversal has to come from conditions internal to how the thing works — what's required to run it, maintain it, keep it fed, who has to be trusted with access, what has to be standardized before it functions at all. It can't come from an unintended side effect (pollution, displacement, a market shift), it can't come from people misusing the thing, and it can't come from some later political decision about the thing. If you can't get the reversal endogenously, just say so rather than quietly smuggling it in.

Then after the scenario: say which of Winner's categories the artifact lands in (requiring vs. strongly compatible with a given arrangement, and internal vs. external conditions); say what the objector thinks who insists the politics is always in the social arrangement and never in the artifact itself, applied to this exact case; identify the single feature doing the work, what happens to both verdicts if I delete it, and how that objector would attack the case as rigged. And please flag anywhere you're tempted to stipulate the outcome — a sentence like *and so control gradually slipped out of the utility's hands* — instead of generating it from how the machine works. I'd much rather know.
