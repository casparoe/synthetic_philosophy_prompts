ok so I co-edit my department's grad student zine — it's mostly bad poems and one long grievance per issue — and this issue's theme is "allocation." My flatmate Dara does distributive justice and has explained the leveling-down objection to me at parties approximately nine times, and I want to build him something he can't stop thinking about. So: write me a piece of satire in two parts, about 800 words total, maybe 60/40 split. Part one is the release notes for version 4.0 of a fictional NGO-facing allocation platform — call it FairShare or whatever you like — which has just been migrated off its old equality engine onto a priority-weighted one. Part two is the support-forum thread that erupts afterwards. The premise is that FairShare decides who gets the next shipment of nets/wells/cash, and the entire product is literally a moral theory compiled into a backend, which nobody at the company seems to find strange.

The release notes should be pure deadpan dev-speak — Breaking Changes, Deprecated, Fixed, Known Issues, Migration Notes — and every single entry should be a real feature of the debate rendered as a software change, not a generic tech joke. Some seeds so you know the register, but please don't just re-use these: a response field that used to return something like "better in one respect" has been deleted outright; the weighting function is now a config parameter that no engineer on the team can justify beyond "it's strictly concave and strictly increasing, that's the spec"; a legacy maximin mode is being kept alive for exactly one enterprise client who refuses to upgrade; the notes boast that the system no longer logs an improvement when a flood makes the better-off worse off and nobody better off, and buries that under a "performance" heading. Also fair game: the fact that the new engine doesn't care how people stand relative to each other at all, only where each one sits on the scale; and the open question of whether the ranking is computed on expected well-being before the shipment or on the actual distribution after it, which the release notes handle by shipping both behind a feature flag and calling the disagreement "a known discrepancy."

For the thread, give me four or five tickets in genuinely different voices — a field officer, a donor, a very online philosopher who has read the migration notes closely, someone from a partner NGO — plus replies from a support rep who is technically flawless and completely unhelpful. At least one ticket must be an egalitarian user in genuine distress that the system has stopped reporting the thing they cared about, and the reply has to be some variant of working-as-intended. One ticket should be from a single individual user asking why the platform refuses to give extra weight to his own worst years the way it gives extra weight to the worst-off people — he finds this inconsistent and support should not be able to answer him. One should be a user insisting, with a spreadsheet attached, that the new engine has the exact same bug as the old one, just rarer and harder to trigger. One should be a sufficientarian asking for a threshold flag and getting told it's on the roadmap. Keep it dry throughout — the reader should be able to tell what's being ironized without anyone winking, and it should be fair enough that a specialist in the area laughs rather than winces, since I'd like to actually give this to Dara. No citations, no real product names, no dates pulled from the actual literature — invent your own version numbers and ticket IDs, and don't put anything in quotation marks unless you're inventing it. End on something small and flat that leaves the real question hanging: whether the objection to leveling down is an objection to equality, or just an objection to saying "better" without saying better-for-whom.
