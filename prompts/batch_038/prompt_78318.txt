I am a fourth-year doctoral student in a perception laboratory whose director organises nearly all of his work around the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction for temporal illusions. In an Orwellian case, the brain first composes an experience matching the physical sequence and only afterwards rewrites the record, so what is reported differs from what was experienced; in a Stalinesque case, the sequence the subject experiences was never the physical one, because the content was assembled and staged before it ever became conscious. He holds that for any given illusion one of the two must be true, and he is openly impatient with the familiar Dennett and Kinsbourne line that the two hypotheses predict exactly the same behaviour and differ only in where an arbitrary line between pre-experiential and post-experiential processing is drawn.

He has issued verdicts on a standing list of cases over many years, and members of the group have learned to predict them. Here they are, with his reasons where he gave any:

- Colour phi: two dots of different colours at different locations, flashed in rapid alternation; subjects report continuous motion in which the dot changes colour partway along the path, and report that change before the second dot appears. Stalinesque, on his view, because the mid-path change is not a distorted record of anything that ever happened.
- Cutaneous rabbit: taps delivered at the wrist and then further up the arm; subjects feel an orderly series of intermediate taps that were never delivered. Stalinesque, for the same reason.
- Flash-lag: a brief flash presented in spatial alignment with a smoothly moving object is perceived to trail behind it. Orwellian, and he is emphatic about it, for two reasons: what happens to the moving object after the flash changes where the flash is seen to have occurred, so a later event is editing an earlier record; and he refuses to treat the perceived offset as fabricated content, since both items were really present and only their relative timing has been misrepresented.
- Chronostasis: the second hand of a clock fixated immediately after an eye movement appears frozen, or its first tick appears prolonged. Orwellian — real content with an edited date stamp.
- Metacontrast masking: a briefly presented shape is reported as absent or badly degraded when a mask follows it some tens of milliseconds later, though it is perfectly visible when shown alone. He declines to classify this, saying the paradigm cannot tell him whether the shape was ever experienced at all, and that the distinction applies only where content is present and its timing or ordering is at issue.

My own pilot study, which he has not yet seen, runs as follows. Subjects watch a single grey disc drift rightward across a screen at constant speed. When it crosses a mark at the centre of the screen, the whole background flashes pale blue for 25 milliseconds. The disc never changes colour. In the base condition, subjects in the large majority of trials report that the disc itself turned blue, and place that change slightly before the disc reached the centre mark. In a long-interval condition, identical except that the disc has been visible and moving for 650 milliseconds before the background flash, so that there is ample time to process that flash veridically, the anticipatory dating survives undiminished. In a retro-influence condition, identical to the base except that a second, amber background flash occurs 90 milliseconds after the blue one at a different location on the screen, subjects who report the disc turning blue now date the change to just before the amber flash rather than before the centre mark: the later event has moved the date assigned to the earlier illusion.

I present this at group meeting next week and would rather know where it lands in his taxonomy before he tells me. Please do the following:

1. Reconstruct at least two decision rules that are consistent with every verdict on his standing list, each stated precisely enough to be applied to a case that is not on the list. Attend in particular to how the flash-lag entry constrains any rule appealing to fabricated content, since his gloss there is doing real work, and to whatever scope restriction the metacontrast entry imposes.
2. For each rule you propose, give the verdict it yields on my paradigm condition by condition, and flag any rule that yields different verdicts for different conditions of the same experiment.
3. Say which verdict you would bet on his giving for the paradigm as a whole, and why, taking account of his commitment that one of the two hypotheses must be true and of his objection to the observational-equivalence line.
4. Specify one control condition or auxiliary experiment that would discriminate between your surviving rules, and state what each rule predicts about it.
5. If the honest conclusion is that no verdict is available to him without giving something up, identify exactly what would have to go, and whether the concession is local to my paradigm or general to the distinction itself.
