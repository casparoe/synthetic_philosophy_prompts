I clerk for the Continuity Panel, the standing arbitration body that the three transfer companies bound themselves to in their founding compact. It decides one question: when a passenger goes into a scanner at one end and a person steps out at the other, is the person who steps out the person who went in? Everything else follows from the answer — whether a marriage subsists, whether a will has been triggered, whether an engineering or medical licence may still be exercised, whether a death has occurred and so whether anyone is liable for one. The Panel is precedent-bound in the ordinary way, and it has published seven rulings, which are the whole of its corpus. I have been asked to draft the bench memo for the eighth. My chair, who taught philosophy for twenty years before taking this post and who keeps telling the other commissioners that they are in fact adjudicating Parfit's question rather than the legal one, wants the competing lines of doctrine set out explicitly before the vote.

The seven rulings:

1. Routine Earth-to-lunar transfer. The passenger was scanned and destroyed at the instant of scanning, instantiated 0.4 seconds later, continuity of memory, character and disposition complete, no other instantiation made. Held: the same person; marriage and licence unaffected.
2. Archival restoration. A scan taken eleven years earlier was instantiated after the subject was killed in an industrial accident. The restored individual had the psychology of the man of eleven years before, swore that he was the decedent, and sought the estate and the continuation of the marriage. Held: not the same person as the decedent. The Panel added, in one sentence, that the claimant's sincerity was not in doubt and was not the issue.
3. Incremental substitution. A patient's cortical substrate was replaced in small functional units over fourteen months, consciousness and psychology uninterrupted throughout, until no original tissue remained. Held: the same person at the end as at the start.
4. Preserved body, erased psychology. A patient's brain and body persisted without interruption through a treatment that obliterated memory and personality; a new psychology was built up over two years of rehabilitation, and the resulting individual repudiated the earlier person's life in its entirety. Held: not the same person, notwithstanding that the organ in the skull was numerically the same organ throughout.
5. Duplication fault. A routing error during a forty-minute buffering interval caused one pattern to be instantiated twice, at two stations; both instances were fully continuous with the passenger, who had been destroyed on schedule. Held: neither instance is the same person. The estate was divided, but both continuers were given standing to enforce the passenger's contracts and to sue for his injuries, the Panel observing that what its precedents protect is whether what the passenger had reason to care about survived, and that this is not the same question as whether he did.
6. Delayed destruction. Owing to a hardware fault the passenger was not destroyed at scanning but remained conscious in the origin chamber for eleven minutes while transmission completed, and was destroyed only at the moment of instantiation. Held: not the same person, there having been, for eleven minutes, two of him.
7. Degraded transfer. A ninety-second buffer, minor corruption, and an emerger who had lost roughly the most recent eighteen months of episodic memory while retaining his skills, temperament, relationships and projects intact. Held: the same person.

The matter now before us. A passenger was scanned at a terrestrial port and destroyed at the instant of scanning, so that at no point did a living original coexist with anything. The transmission window to the destination habitat was closed for eleven days, and his pattern was held in buffered, un-instantiated form for the whole of that period. On the fourth day an automated integrity diagnostic spun up a partial instantiation of the pattern for nine seconds in order to test its coherence, then terminated it; the port's medical officer certifies that the instance made no report, formed no memories, and — her phrase — was not awake for anything. On the eleventh day the pattern was instantiated in a new body at the destination. Continuity is as complete as the technology permits: memory, character, skill and disposition intact; his wife identifies him without hesitation; he reports that he closed his eyes on Earth and opened them here. The commissioners must rule on whether the marriage subsists, whether the will has been triggered, and whether his licence remains in force.

What the memo needs from you: predict the Panel's holding and the reasoning it will give; reconstruct the doctrines that are jointly consistent with all seven precedents — I make it at least three, and they are not the ones the commissioners reach for first — and state what each implies for this passenger; identify which of the seven is the closest analog and explain why the resemblance may mislead; and, since two of the surviving doctrines will vote the same way here for reasons that cannot both be correct, propose the further case that would force them apart, either one the Panel should take next or one we can put to it hypothetically. If the corpus genuinely underdetermines the result, say plainly what the Panel would be committing itself to by ruling each way, including what it would then be obliged to say about those nine seconds, about anaesthesia and ordinary sleep, and about a passenger held in buffer for eleven days with no diagnostic run at all.
