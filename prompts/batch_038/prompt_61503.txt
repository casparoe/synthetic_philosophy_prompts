ok so my uni's philosophy of religion reading group got completely derailed last week and I still can't work out how to score it. We'd been reading up on Molinism and someone posed the usual puzzle: if God knows, before He creates anything, what every possible person would freely do in any situation He might place them in, where do those facts come from? Nobody has done anything yet, so there doesn't seem to be anything there to make them true.

Here's roughly how the exchange went, in order:

(1) Dan said the counterfactuals are grounded in the creatures' characters. God knows, for each possible person, the settled dispositions they'd have, and what they'd do in situation S just falls out of who they are plus what S is like. So middle knowledge is knowledge of something, not a brute list floating in mid-air.

(2) Mira said that's fatal: if your character plus the circumstances fully settles what you'll do, you don't do it freely. Dan has smuggled determinism in through the word 'character'.

(3) Dan said no — the dispositional fact makes the act likely, not certain. God's middle knowledge is of probabilities: He knows that in S, person P would freely do the good with, say, 0.7 chance.

(4) Mira said then it's not Molinism any more, it's God gambling, and He can't guarantee the world He's aiming at, which was the whole reason for positing middle knowledge in the first place. And anyway, she said, you can't have a true claim about the probability of a genuinely free act unless something makes it that probability rather than another one, and she can't see where that fact lives either — so the grounding problem just comes back a level down.

(5) Dan said the probability is grounded in character too, beacuse dispositions come in strengths the way habits do. And he said God doesn't need guarantees: across billions of creatures and repeated occasions, providence by odds is still providence. Then he added that Mira's own alternative is worse, because if God knows the outcomes for certain, He's the one who chose the exact circumstances that produced them, so on her view He's more directly on the hook for evil than on his.

(6) Mira said that last bit just turns the standard complaint about Molinism back on itself — God fixing the circumstances is God arranging the whole show either way — and that Dan's 'providence by odds' reduces God to a portfolio manager rather than a ruler.
