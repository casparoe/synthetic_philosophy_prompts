I am in my final year at school and am writing an extended project on privacy. I would like you to act as a professional philosopher who specialises in the ethics of information, and I would like you to think the problem through carefully rather than answer quickly.

I have been collecting my own intuitive verdicts about when privacy is violated, and I have a tentative principle of my own that seems to fit most of them. I call it the bystander test: my privacy is violated when someone comes to know something about me that a polite stranger, physically present beside me at the relevant moment, could not have come to know. Here are the verdicts, in no particular order.

1. A neighbour's drone photographs me sunbathing in my own back garden, which is open to the sky and overlooked from the lane. Violation.
2. My GP sends my diagnosis to a consultant at another hospital without asking me, so that I can be seen sooner. Not a violation.
3. My GP tells my mother the same diagnosis, also without asking me, because she has been worried. Violation.
4. A chain of shops works out from my purchases that I am probably pregnant, when I have told no one. Violation, and the clearest one on my list.
5. A stranger photographs a busy street corner, I am recognisable in the background, and the picture goes online. Not a violation.
6. My mother reads the diary I keep in a drawer. Violation.
7. I accept an app's terms and conditions, which allow it to sell my location history to a data broker. Violation, even though I consented, and even though I never read them.
8. A search engine records my queries; I know it does and I do not mind. Not a violation. Those same records sold to an insurer, which then refuses to renew my policy. Violation.
9. A research dataset is published with names stripped out, but three entries could be re-identified by matching them against an electoral register. Violation, although nobody was targeted.
10. My closest friend can tell from my face and my voice that something is wrong before I have said a word. Not a violation. An app that infers the same thing from the rhythm of my typing over a month and reports it to my employer. Violation.

The bystander test seems to handle 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9 and 10 reasonably well, but it appears to get 2 wrong, and it cannot separate the two halves of 8. Worse, I cannot see how to repair it without making things worse elsewhere: if I add a clause about the recipient's role or purpose, in order to save 2, that same clause seems to excuse the app in 7 and the insurer in 8, since selling and pricing are exactly what they are for. Yet 2 and 7 are the pair I find hardest to reconcile, because in 2 a disclosure without consent is perfectly acceptable and in 7 a disclosure with consent is not, which suggests consent is not doing the work at all.

What I would like from you:

- Begin with two or three sentences diagnosing where my principle actually breaks, before you propose anything.
- State the principle you think captures the pattern, or a small number of rival candidates, in precisely worded numbered clauses, and say which of my cases motivates each clause.
- Tell me honestly which of my verdicts your account cannot accommodate and which of them I ought to give up. I would rather have a spare account that overturns one or two verdicts than an elaborate one that rescues all ten, but please say so plainly if the cost of simplicity falls on 4 or 7, which are the two I care most about.
- Apply the account to two cases I have not listed: a firm that stores no individual fact about me at all but trains a system on my messages until it can imitate me convincingly; and a service that reconstructs the speech of someone who has recently died from their old correspondence. In the second case, say whose privacy, if anyone's, is engaged.
- Finish by giving me one further case of your own choosing that you expect the repaired principle to handle badly, or that you think should make me change my mind.

Please keep your answer to no more than 700 words, write it in British English, and argue the matter out in your own words: no citations, references or reading lists, and do not assume I know any technical terminology.
