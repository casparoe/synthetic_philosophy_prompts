I'm the secretary of a 24-person worker co-op — bike shop plus repair workshop — and we're deciding wether to take out a loan and open a second shop across town. One of our members just finished an econ masters and he's proposing we settle it with a betting pool instead of a vote. His plan: everybody gets 200 house dollars, no real money involved, you can buy and sell shares in "second shop is profitable at the end of year two" and in "we stay put and revenue grows 10% over two years," the pool runs for a week, and whichever side ends up priced higher is what we do. He says the crucial part is we have to agree in advance to follow the result and not override it afterward, or the whole thing is pointless.

Three things I can already see going wrong and I don't know if they're fatal or if I'm missing something:

1. Two members have said out loud they'll just dump all 200 on the outcome they personally want, because the moneys fake anyway and this is the only way their opinion counts for more than one vote. One of the two is the guy who'd be managing the new shop.
2. Our treasurer says neither of his two contracts is the thing she's actually afraid of. She's worried about whether we can still make payroll in a bad year, which isn't the same question as whether the new shop makes money.
3. Realistically maybe eight people would trade. The rest would just watch the prices move.

On top of that, half the board wants to see the prices at the end of the week and then vote on whether to accept them. I think that breaks his scheme somehow, but I can't explain why in a way that lands with anybody.

So — do we run this? I don't want a rundown of pros and cons for me to weigh. Pick a side and defend it: either run it with specific changes (tell me exactly what you'd change about the contracts, the stakes, the timing, the decision rule) or scrap it and tell me what we do instead to make this call. If you think one of my three worries is a non-issue at our size, say so flatly instead of hedging. And where you're just speculating about how 24 actual people in a room would behave, mark it as speculation rather than dressing it up as a conclusion.
