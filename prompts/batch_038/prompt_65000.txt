Been arguing about the socialist calculation debate with a friend for two years and we've finally agreed to write down where we actually stand instead of yelling past each other. She's a Mises-style impossibility theorist: no private ownership of the means of production means no real prices for capital goods, which means no rational calculation, period — and she's insistent this isnt the Hayekian dispersed-knowledge or incentives complaint, it's an arithmetic point. I'm the Langean: a board facing excess supply or demand can iterate prices up and down and land in the same place a market would, and now that iteration is nearly free that reply should look stronger than it did in the thirties.

Write the joint statement the two of us could actually sign. I want a numbered section of agreed claims, one of which has to pin down what we're even disagreeing about — half our fights are over whether a price is a number or an institution. Then a table of the residual disagreements, each row with my formulation and hers, worded so each of us would accept the line describing our own view. Then three cruxes, each with a case we'd both grant is relevant and our predictions recorded in advance. Make one crux the giant vertically integrated retailer that shifts capital around internally with no internal market in machines: is that a counterexample to the impossibility claim, or does it only work because the firm is parasitic on prices set outside? Say what would move each of us, and flag anything you think is merely verbal. No references needed.
