Reading some value theory for fun and I've hit a shape of case I can't find a name for. Someone's work is genuinely impressive — say a virtuso but cruel music critic, or a gorgeous novel by a man who systematically ruined the people close to him — and I think admiration of it would be fitting, in that the thing itself merits it. But I also think I shouldn't admire it, and not because I'd be punished or paid for not admiring it: because admiring it would put me in a certain relation to them, or would be a kind of betrayal of the people they hurt, or would say something about me. So there's a reason against the very attitude the object deserves.

Is there an established concept for reasons against a fitting attitude that aren't bribes or threats? How does it differ from the person who just denies the premise — who says the cruelty makes the work less admirable, so the attitude was never fitting to begin with? And how does it line up against the familiar puzzle where a demon threatens you unless you admire something worthless: is mine just that problem mirrored, or something else entirely? I dont want only a label, even if there is one — I want the edges of the thing, which nearby cases fall inside it and which don't. British English, please.
