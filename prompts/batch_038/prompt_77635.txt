For three years I have watched the same argument replay itself in every venue where language models are discussed seriously, and nobody gains ground. On one side are those who press the Dennettian line: if a system's behaviour licenses the intentional stance — if the best way to predict and explain what it does is to posit beliefs, desires, and understanding — then it has them, and demanding something further is residual dualism in a lab coat. On the other side are those who insist that fluency of this kind is manufactured rather than earned, that what we have built are counterfeit people, and that mistaking the counterfeit for the genuine is a moral and epistemic hazard rather than a discovery. Both camps accept the same facts about how the systems are trained and what they can do. Both use understanding, belief, and consciousness in the ordinary way. Both even borrow from the same author: "competence without comprehension" is wielded by functionalists to deflate the mystery and by sceptics to insist that competence is not yet comprehension. Neither side produces an argument the other treats as probative.

My working hypothesis is that the stalemate is not about machines. I suspect the word counterfeit is doing unexamined structural work: counterfeiting presupposes an authoritative issuer who can authenticate the genuine article, and there is no issuer for persons. On that reading, one side's central claim is not false but malformed, and the two camps are answering different questions — a metaphysical one about inner constitution, a normative one about which attributions license trust, testimony, and standing. I also suspect the intentional stance is invoked by both as though it settled the matter, when it was framed as a predictive strategy that stays silent on constitution.

Diagnose this. Is my authentication objection sound, or does it beg the question against the functionalist? Is the disagreement verbal, substantive but incommensurable, or two questions sharing one label? Argue from what each side actually says, not from a survey of positions on machine consciousness. Then tell me what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a design of test, a change of question — or argue that none should be expected, and explain why the debate keeps returning to exactly this shape.
