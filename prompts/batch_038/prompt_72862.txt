I am a high school senior preparing practice cases for my school's philosophy society, and we have worked through the lying-versus-misleading problem almost entirely by way of stock examples: the inquiring murderer, the refugee hidden during a war, the physician and the dying patient. Every one of them seems to settle the question in advance by loading the lie with mitigating detail and the misleading statement with harm. I would like a case built to the opposite specification — an ordinary, realistic situation in which the lie comes out clearly the lesser wrong and the literally true but deliberately misleading remark clearly the greater one.

The requirements: one situation, two versions, differing only in what the speaker says; identical motive, identical stakes, identical relationship to the hearer, and the same alternatives available to the speaker in both; the hearer forms the same false belief with the same confidence in each version and cannot detect any difference at the time; both versions are later discovered and the speaker confronted in the same manner; no professional or institutional role carrying special duties of candour, so no lawyers, doctors, police officers, or press secretaries; nobody placed in physical danger; and the scenario itself confined to a single paragraph.

Then run the check. State what each speaker asserts, what each believes, what inference the hearer draws, and why the two versions are equally successful deceptions, so that nothing but the act-type separates them. Work through what a view on which assertion carries its own norm — making lying a wrong of a different kind rather than merely a different degree — says about each version, and what a view on which only deception matters says; both should come out against my target verdict, and I want to know what third account, if any, could explain the reversal. Finally, name the feature of the case doing the work, say what happens to it if that feature is removed, and give the strongest reply available to someone who insists lying is always worse — including the objection that the misleader lied as well, since what a speaker asserts may include what he implicates — and tell me whether that reply destroys the case or only relocates it.
