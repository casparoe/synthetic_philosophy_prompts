so I'm in a moral responsibility seminar and for next week each of us has to write down our views on blame and hypocrisy so the others can attack them. Here's mine:

(1) whether something someone says is true has nothing to do with who says it, so "you do it too" doesn't rebut anything.
(2) all the same, a hypocrite lacks standing to blame — same norm, own violations, no standing.
(3) standing is binary rather than a matter of degree, and you only lose it with respect to norms you violate yourself.
(4) being the one the wrong was done to gives you a standing that third parties simply don't have.
(5) what's objectionable about hypocritical blame is unfairness: the hypocrite takes a liberty with the norm he denies everyone else, so he's grabbing more than his share of the right to condemn.
(6) though there's no fixed pool of blame — anybody can condemn anybody at any time and nobody is deprived of anything by it.
(7) blame you only think and never voice can lose standing in exactly the same way.

Case I can't sort out: Dana's lab partner cooked the data on a project of hers, and Dana has cut corners on data herself once before. I want to say she has a standing nobody else has because it was her project he falsified, and I also want to say by (3) she's got none because it's the same norm she breaks. I don't want to just reach for "standing comes in degrees" as the get-out.

I have a feeling (1) and (2) only look like they contradict. Audit the whole set: put the genuine tensions in order of severity, say for each what has to be dropped or weakened and what that costs me, and mark the ones that dissolve once you look at them properly. Bottom line up front — is the package salvageable? Max 450 words.
