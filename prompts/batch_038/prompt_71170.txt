Here's a case I keep going around in circles on and I want a number, not a survey. Suppose we replace someone's neurons one at a time with synthetic parts that are functionally identical to what they displace, over a thousand steps, so there's never any big functional jump — the usual thought being that if nothing is ever detectable at the moment of the swap, nothing could have been lost. But add this twist: between each step the person is put under full general anesthesia for about a day, so there is never a single unbroken stretch of experience spanning the whole process. What's your credence that the person who wakes up at the end is the same subject of experience as the one who started — i.e. that there's one phenomenal life running through it rather than a series of short ones strung together by memory and behavior?
