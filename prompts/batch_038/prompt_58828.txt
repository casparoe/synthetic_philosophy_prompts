Right, so I run internal comms at a mid-size logistics firm in the Midlands — about 2,500 staff split across a head office, four depots and a lot of drivers. Two years ago we opened up comments on the intranet news feed, anonymous by default, after the union pushed for it. Mostly it's been dead: the odd grumble about the canteen, someone losing their parking space.

Three weeks ago we posted a 400-word note about a change to shift patterns at one depot. It went off. Three hundred-odd comments in two days, which is about twelve times our usual traffic on anything. But reading back through it, almost none of the comments are actually about the note. People are arguing with other people's summaries of it, and one sentence gets quoted maybe nine times as something management supposedly wrote, except that sentence isn't in the note and never was. Nobody seems to have got past the headline, including, if I'm honest, some of the managers who weighed in.

One anonymous account, DepotRat22, ended up being the voice of the place. My boss started asking me what DepotRat22 wanted, and there was a call where a director said we should "give DepotRat22 something to take back to people". No one knows who it is. Its never claimed to represent anyone and has no mandate from the union or anybody else.

Two things complicate my gut reaction. One: the thread was right. It worked out — several days before HR did — that the new pattern would have broken the rest-break rules for the night crews. That's a genuine catch and it saved us from a mess. Two: after the dust settled, three people who'd been pretty horrible in the thread emailed me by name to apologise, one of them a manager I know quite well. So they were in there somewhere, the whole time.

Anyway, my comms director is delighted with the numbers and wants to make this the default going forward: comments open on everything, anonymous unless you choose to sign, most-commented items pushed to the top of the feed, plus a trending widget. My instinct is that this is a bad idea, and that something went wrong in that thread even though it landed on the right answer and everyone finished up reasonably happy. My own theory is that the problem is anonymity — that if people had to sign, it would have been a different conversation — but I'm not sure thats right, given the three who apologised obviously knew perfectly well who they were while they were doing it.

So: is my instinct onto something, or am I just squeamish about a bad-tempered fortnight that produced a good result? What actually went wrong, if something did? I'd rather have a sharp diagnosis than a general moan about online discourse, and I'd like it to explain why being right about the rest breaks doesn't settle the question. Then what should I do about the proposal — including whether switching off anonymous comments is defensible at all, given the union will see it as management clawing back the one channel where a driver can speak safely?

And please tell me how confident you are in the diagnosis and in the advice, separately, and single out the one point you're least sure of. I'd rather have a hedged answer that flags its own weak spot than a confident one that hides it.
