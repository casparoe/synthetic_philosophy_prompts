Write an essay on a controversy in Marx's critique of capitalism: whether its normative core is exploitation — the extraction of surplus labor, a claim about what workers are paid versus what they produce — or alienation, a claim about a distorted way of working and living. To keep the question sharp, consider two stipulated cases. Case A: a factory worker whose labor is paid well below the value she adds, but who loves the work, throws herself into it, and recognizes herself in its products. Case B: a self-employed contractor paid generously above the value she adds, who nonetheless experiences her work as external to her, as a mere means, and feels no connection to what she makes. If exploitation and alienation come apart this way, which case does Marx's critique condemn, and does that show one concept carrying the normative weight while the other merely diagnoses? Address the obvious reply that Case A is not coherently describable, since on Marx's own account a worker standing in those social relations is alienated whether or not she reports otherwise — and say whether that reply rescues the theory or makes it immune to evidence. I lean toward exploitation carrying the weight, and I suspect the alienation critique collapses into a general complaint about bad work under any economic system; argue me out of that if you can. Deliver the essay as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no separate introduction or conclusion, and with the fifth point stating your verdict.
