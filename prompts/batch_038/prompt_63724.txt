I'm writing a seminar paper for a course on the epistemology of outsourced cognition, and I want to compare two accounts of what a student loses when she uses a large language model to produce work she could, with effort, have done herself. Call the first the atrophy account: understanding is a capacity — on the ability-based conception, roughly the capacity to answer correctly the relevant what-if-things-had-been-different questions about a subject — and capacities decay when they are not exercised, so delegation converts a standing ability into a rented one. Call the second the parity account, in the spirit of the extended mind thesis: if a process carried on outside the skull would count as cognition were it carried on inside, and the external resource is reliably available, automatically endorsed and trusted, then the understanding is distributed across student and system rather than lost, and the coupled system still answers the what-if questions.

To force a difference between them, work with this case. Two students in the same regression-diagnostics course spend the same hours with the same assistant. Ada writes her own diagnosis of each defective model, then asks the assistant to attack it and revises until it stops landing hits; Bea pastes the data summary and accepts whatever diagnosis comes back, and her accepted diagnoses are, on the examiners' reckoning, slightly more accurate than Ada's final ones. At the end of term both can state the standard theory; only Ada can diagnose a novel dataset once the assistant is unavailable.

Please answer in exactly five numbered items of one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the sharpest disagreement between the two accounts, stated so that they cannot both be right; (2) a second, independent axis on which they differ; (3) what each account predicts about Ada and about Bea respectively; (4) the cheapest revision the parity account can make to accommodate the case, and whether that revision collapses it into the atrophy account; (5) your verdict on which account better explains the erosion of understanding, naming the single consideration that decides it.
