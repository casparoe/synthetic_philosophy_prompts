Taking a climate ethics course and there's a thing that keeps bugging me that I don't have a word for. Two cases where nobody's individual act makes any difference, but my gut reacts totally differently. One: I take a twenty minute shower during a drought, I know the water's going to end up in the same reservoir everyone else's is, it makes no diffrence at the margin, and I feel basically nothing. Two: I'm asked to be the fifth name on an open letter that's going to get three hundred signatures whether or not I'm on it, and my name on it feels heavy. And the weird part is it gets heavier the fewer other names there are. If it ends up with only four signatures I feel way more implicated than if it had four hundred, even though four hundred is much more likely to actually do something.

It's not guilt. I don't feel like I owe anybody anything, and I don't feel like a worse person, and it goes away entirely if it's a group I'm part of deciding together to send the thing. So it's not tracking how much I cause, if anything it runs backwards from that. My own guess is it's tracking whether someone could point at me and say "you, specifically," but I'm not sure that's a real distinction anyone cares about.

I know the standard replies to "your act makes no difference" and I know people have argued you're implicated in collective harms just by participating, but neither seems to be about this -- the fact that felt implication scales with being singled out rather than with contribution. So: does this have a name? Tell me the closest established concepts and exactly where each one stops covering my case, and if nothing fits, coin a term with a real definition plus whatever would count as a boundary case for it. Not after references, just the concepts -- a bare label won't help me.
