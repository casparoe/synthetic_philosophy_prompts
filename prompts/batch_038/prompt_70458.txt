So I'm one of nine part-time members on our city's charter review commission. We've got four meetings left before we send a recommendation up to the council, and the thing that's going to eat all four is the electoral system for the nine council seats. Right now it's nine single-member districts, first past the post. The good-government crowd wants us to go proportional — single transferable vote in three three-member wards has the most momentum behind it, and there's a smaller faction pushing for fully at-large, which I think is nuts but they're not nothing.

Here's the wrinkle, and I want the memo to actually live in it instead of reciting the usual FPTP-vs-PR chestnuts. Our Black residents are 24% of registered voters and tightly concentrated on the Southside: about 60% of the two Southside districts, which means they've elected their candidate of choice in those two seats every cycle since the 2001 map. Our Latino residents are 31% citywide but somewhere between a quarter and a third in every single district and a plurality in none of them — zero seats, ever, and no map we draw fixes that. So the equity case for proportionality and the equity case for district-based representation are pointing at two different groups, and nobody on the commission has said that out loud yet. Second wrinkle: our elections are nonpartisan by charter. I think that guts most of the standard PR menu — no parties means no lists, so anything top-up or mixed-member shaped isn't really available to us unless we're willing to invent slates, and its not obvious we can.

Deliverable: a decision memo I can circulate to the other eight before we meet on the 14th. The bottom line has to be one of — keep single-member districts; three three-member wards under STV; at-large cumulative voting; at-large limited voting, say three votes per voter for nine seats; or a hybrid you think beats all four. Tell me what actually goes in the charter amendment and on what clock, including whether we ask the council to phase it in at the next redistricting or just run it in 2027.

Do the arithmetic and show it. Droop in a three-seat ward is 25% plus a vote; at-large across nine it's 10% plus a vote. Walk me through what the Southside and what the Latino community plausibly win under each option given the numbers above, and flag every spot where you're assuming a transfer pattern or a cohesion level, because I'd rather know which parts of your recommendation are load-bearing guesses. Also note that nobody has said how the three wards would even be drawn, which is obviously half the ballgame.

Two ground rules. One: don't brief the law. We have councel on the vote-dilution question and I do not want invented case names or statute numbers. But do tell me what the exposure turns on — whether trading two safe majority-Black districts for better proportionality citywide reads as retrogression or reads as a remedy, and what a court would want to see in the record we build over these four meetings. Two: give me the losing side at full strength. If you come down on going proportional, I want the Southside argument stated well enough that the two commissioners who hold those seats can't wave it off as a strawman, and the reverse if you come down on keeping districts.

Last thing — say something about transition. Seven incumbents, neighborhood associations whose entire ground game is ward-level, and whether a phased two-cycle rollout is real prudence or cowardice in a nice suit.
