The practical upshot is that proof by contradiction is banned outright, and with it goes most of the mathematics built since Cantor: no non-constructive existence theorems, no uncountable infinities, and no real analysis as Weierstrass left it. Heyting eventually turned all of this into precise logical rules, and the resulting system is best understood as classical logic with excluded middle struck out, so any classical theorem whose proof never leans on contradiction is automatically an intuitionistic theorem too. One awkward consequence of identifying truth with provability is that intuitionistic mathematics is unstable: a statement accepted today can be rejected tomorrow as new proofs turn up, which never happens classically. Modern constructive analysts -- Bishop most famously -- have rebuilt a good deal of analysis, but only at the price of admitting the computable real numbers. So intuitionism is best seen not as classical mathematics held to a stricter standard of rigor, but as a smaller, weaker mathematics sitting alongside it."
