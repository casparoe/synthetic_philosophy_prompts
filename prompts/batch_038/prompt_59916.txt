I have followed the discussion of Kripke's dogmatism paradox on and off for the better part of a decade, mostly in comment threads, and I notice that every illustration anyone reaches for has one of two shapes: a person told something about a friend's character by a witness she has some reason to distrust, or a skeptical scenario in which the counterevidence is bizarre by construction. I want to know whether the paradox does any real damage once both templates are off the table.

For the purposes of the task, here is the machinery as I understand it, stated without appeal to any literature you may or may not recall. Suppose S knows that p. Then S is in a position to know that any evidence against p is misleading. And it is not rational to update on evidence one knows to be misleading. So S may discount in advance, and wholesale, whatever future evidence turns up against p — a conclusion that sounds like the definition of epistemic vice arriving as a deliverance of epistemic virtue.

The feature I want an example of is not that conclusion but something downstream of it that I have not seen anyone isolate, and which I will call leakage. Evidence does not arrive labeled. A single datum typically bears on many propositions at once, and the license the argument grants concerns only the evidence-against-p content. Yet the practice it licenses — setting the datum aside, not entering it in any register, not letting it trigger an inspection — is indifferent to what else the datum bore on. So I suspect the genuine cost of dogmatism is not that S's belief in p becomes unresponsive, which may be no cost at all if p is known, but that S's beliefs about other propositions, untouched by the argument, become unresponsive as collateral damage.

Construct a case that exhibits this. It should satisfy all of the following.

1. p is contingent and empirical, and known on the basis of a method S understands and has independent, non-circular reason to trust. Not a Moorean certainty, not a logical or mathematical truth, not a claim about S's own present experience.
2. The counterevidence arrives as a stream of quantitative readings, each individually unremarkable — well within the noise S's own procedures license — produced by apparatus S maintains herself. No single reading is one that anyone, dogmatist or otherwise, should update on, and no move of the form "the source is lying or confused" is available.
3. There is a distinct proposition q, neither entailed by p nor incompatible with it, on which the same stream genuinely bears; and S's discounting of the stream leaves her unwarrantedly ignorant of q, or holding a false belief about it. Name the decision she gets wrong in consequence, and make the harm fall on someone other than S.
4. S knows p from beginning to end. There is no hidden defeater, and her evidence is not self-undermining. The entire harm sits on the q side.
5. q must be checkable by means S already has, at a cost she could easily absorb, and she must have no non-dogmatic reason to forgo the check — otherwise the loss is attributable to scarcity rather than to the license. The rescue must also be real rather than just-so: q should be something S could have established, not an auxiliary postulate invented to save p.
6. Exclusions: no testimony about anyone's character, no skeptical scenarios, no preface-paradox structure in which individually credible claims cannot all be true, and no unfalsifiable core theory absorbing anomalies indefinitely.

After the narrative, give me the diagnosis in a short numbered section: first, which step of the argument above fails when it is extended from the p-content to the q-content, and why; second, whether the leakage is a feature of the argument or of the psychology of discounting — that is, whether a scrupulous reasoner who grants every premise would still suffer it; third, the minimal restriction on the third premise that blocks your case while leaving obviously correct dismissals of junk evidence intact, tested against a mundane instance in which discounting is uncontroversially the right move.

Format: one sentence each stating p and q up front; the narrative in roughly 350 words; then the numbered diagnosis. No references, please. I am not after a survey of the literature — I want a construction I can argue with.
