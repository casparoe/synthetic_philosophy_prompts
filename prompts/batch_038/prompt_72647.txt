i'm TA-ing a section of intro epistemology, this week is skepticism and the responses to it, and i want to hand out one poem at the end of class that does the fuel-gauge case everybody uses — you're driving, the gauge says F, so the tank is full, and a gauge that says F when the tank is full is a gauge thats reading right, and now you've apparently confirmed your own instrument out of thin air.

form: a pantoum, five or six quatrains. the form is the whole point here, i want it to *be* the problem rather than describe it. second and fourth lines of each stanza carry over as the first and third of the next, exact repeats, not paraphrases, and the last stanza brings lines 1 and 3 of the opening back in the second and fourth slots so the thing closes on itself. the trick i'm after: a line that enters as a bare perceptual report (the needle sitting on F, or whatever you land on) has to come back later doing the work of a reliability claim with not one word changed, so the class can feel the same sentence get promoted from evidence to verdict and then notice nobody voted on the promotion.

stuff it should get right without ever using the vocabulary — no bootstrapping, no easy knowledge, no reliabilism, no closure, no names. also don't just do the single-reading version, build the track-record version in too: the driver keeps checking, every new reading feels like fresh support, and the reader should be able to see that the pile of confirmations is still just the pile of readings. and the voice cant be an outsider snarking; its the driver, and the driver is pleased, because the very thing that lets them trust their eyes at all is what licenses this move — so the cost is internal, not a complaint from the skeptical side.

i'd like the last stanza to leave it genuinely undecidable whether we just watched a proof or a wheel spinning in mud, and that ambivalence should read as the correct answer, not a cop-out. no rhyme scheme required, lines short enough to say in one breath, somewhere around 20 to 24 total. assume nobody in the room has met the case before, so it has to work as a poem first — and please don't tack on an explanation afterward, i'm reading it cold.
