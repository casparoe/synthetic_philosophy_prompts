so I'm a junior and I somehow ended up on the youth advisory panel for our county community foundation. it's six of us, ages 16 to 22, and once a year we decide where the $2,500 from the pancake breakfast fundraiser goes. sounds tiny but people take it really seriously, there's always a crying moment at the end. Our faculty advisor made us do a unit before we started reading applications, mostly on famines — Sen's argument that the Bengal one in 1943 wasn't really about there not being enough food but about whole groups of people losing their ability to actually get it, plus Ireland in the 1840s (food kept leaving the country basically the whole time) and the Great Leap Forward. That unit is why I care so much about the two applications in front of us and why I keep bringing it up in meetings, which is apparently why the chair assigned me to write the opinion.

Both applications respond to the same drought. I'll call the place the Kasera Valley, it's not real, I'm changing the name because one of the panelists doxxed the region on our group chat and I don't want to name it here. Application A is from a US-based relief group that wants our $2,500 toward shipping in about a ton and a half of grain, and their whole writeup frames it as the worst drought in four decades, a natural disaster, please help these families survive. Application B is from a local org that wants to do cash transfers to roughly 60 households and source the food from farmers in two neighboring districts that had a decent harvest, and their writeup spends a lot of pages arguing the crisis is mostly not the drought at all: the regional government kept letting grain trucks out of the valley through October, two millers bought up the flour supply and held it, and the people going hungry are overwhelmingly the ones who lost wage income rather than the ones who lost crops.

Here's the actual fight. Our bylaws say panel funds "may not be used for political advocacy or lobbying," and B's budget has a $300 line item for producing and distributing a short report documenting those grain movements and the millers' pricing. Two panelists say that line item is advocacy, it's disqualifying, and we'd be funding something close to a hit piece on a government we have no standing to judge. Two others say the report is just recordkeeping and that if you cut it out you haven't understood what B is doing in the first place. The chair wants a written opinion from me before we vote Tuesday. Can you draft that memo for me? 700 to 900 words, addressed to the panel, with a real recommendation at the top — fund A, fund B as submitted, fund B with the line item cut, or some fourth option you think is better — and enough specifics that we can actually vote on it Tuesday. It has to take the advocacy objection seriously rather than swatting it away, because honestly I don't think those two panelists are stupid, and I need to be able to hand this to them. And I need it to explain why the A versus B disagreement isn't just a boring fight about overhead and shipping costs but a fight about what kind of event this even is. please don't invent quotes or give me a reading list, my advisor will fact check everything and I can't verify anything on a school chromebook. also it should sound like a 17 year old wrote it, more or less, not like a consultant — no "leveraging synergies," definately no bullet-point-itis.
