So I sit on the panel for a small regional arts fund — six of us plus the funder's rep who joined last year, and ten or twelve applications a round. The pot is fixed and gets handed out in tiers down a ranked list: top three get the big grants, next few get medium, the rest get nothing. So we don't just need a winner, we need a complete ordering of everything, every round, and the funder wants the same method used across all their panels.

For years we just had everyone write 1 through 12 and we averaged the numbers. Two complaints finally boiled over. Last round a hopeless application came in that nobody ranked better than ninth, and somehow its presence shuffled which two projects got the top tier — the projects themselves didn't change, nobody's opinion of them changed, just this one dud in the mix. And separately our chair runs head to head votes between pairs when we deadlock, and one reviewer did the math and noticed that if the chair changes the order those votes happen in, a different project wins. She isn't accusing him of anything but everyone's spooked. I'm also pretty sure two people have started ranking dishonestly to keep a project they hate off the list.

Heres the mess. The funder's compliance handbook has three rules. If all seven of us rank A above B, the final list has to rank A above B. Whether A lands above B can't depend on how any of us ranked some other application. And no single panelist can be in a position to determine the list by themselves. The chair wants to write to the funder and ask them to drop one of the three, and its landed on me to say which one and why. Worth noting the rep votes with the chair every single time, which I suspect makes rule three more delicate than it looks.

Meanwhile a vendor is pitching a proprietary scoring system and swears that with it nobody can ever gain by lying on their ballot and that it always picks whichever project beats all the others in head to head matchups. And a friend says I'm overthinking the whole thing, that all that stuff about it being mathematically impossible to be fair only applies to big electorates and we're seven people.

What should we actually run next round? Which of the funder's rules do I ask them to give up, and what's the honest argument for that one over the other two — including what we'd be signing up for once it's gone? Is there anything I can look for in the ballots we already have, some pattern in how our seven rank things, that would mean the problem just doesn't apply to us? And how am I supposed to tell whether a claim like the vendor's is a real fix or sales nonsense, without a mathematician on call.
