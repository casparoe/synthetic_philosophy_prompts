Argued with a friend til 2am about whether the neutrino is a win or a loss for the underdetermination thesis and I think we were shouting past each other. Facts as I have them: beta electrons came out with a continuous energy spectrum (Chadwick, 1914), and calorimetry in the late 20s confimed the energy wasn't hiding outside the atom after all. So either energy just wasn't conserved inside the nucleus — which is roughly what Bohr wanted, he'd already tried a statistical version of conservation years earlier — or something neutral and effectively undetectable was carrying it off, which is what Pauli proposed in 1930. Then in 1934 Bethe and Peierls worked out that such a particle could pass clean through the Earth, which everybody read as "we'll never see it," and Cowan and Reines caught it at a reactor in 1956 anyway.

My friend says 1930 is underdetermination in its purest form: two hypotheses, same evidence, nothing to choose between them. I say it proves the opposite — the equivalence was only ever temporary, new evidence showed up, case closed, basically the Laudan and Leplin line. Help me settle it. Concretely:

- What does underdetermination actually require here — logical equivalence, empirical equivalence, or just no rational way to pick — and which of those, if any, held between Pauli's particle and Bohr's broken conservation law, given what was on the table in 1930? Does the earlier refutation of statistical conservation in the radiation case count as evidence bearing on this choice, or is it a different process and so irrelevant?
- Run it backwards: is 1956 a victory lap for the sceptic, or does the episode feed Stanford's new induction — i.e. the real problem wasn't that the two 1930 options were tied but that nobody had thought of the things that mattered next (parity violation in weak interactions, for one)?
- Be explicit about where the thesis is simply silent — say, about why Pauli's option felt live to most physicists and Bohr's didn't, if that's not a question underdetermination can touch at all.

Last line, please: "Verdict:" then one word — confirms, undercuts, or neutral — for the underdetermination thesis.
