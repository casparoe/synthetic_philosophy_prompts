im TA-ing a survey section and friday is Braudel — the three time scales, the near-immobile history of people and their environment, the slower rhythmic one of harvests, prices, plague years and trade cycles, and then events skimming the surface. my prof wants me to open the discussion with something that isnt a slide of definitions, so: write me a poem in three sections, one per time scale, where the form carries the arguement instead of the poem explaining it. someone who knows Braudel should be able to tell which section is which without being told.

constraints i care about. section one should be the longest and the slowest: lines of about fourteen to sixteen syllables, few of them, hardly any punctuation, and no verbs of anyone doing anything — just rock, mud, wind, distance, the sea. section two should be mid-length, eight syllables a line or so, and cyclical, with a rhyme or a repeated line that comes back on itself, so you can feel the price of wheat and the good years and the bad turning over. section three should be short and clipped, four syllables a line, crowded with proper nouns and one battle and a king dying, and it should be by far the briefest, almost an interruption.

then the part thats actually mine: the last few lines should turn on the idea that the slow layer isn't a backdrop the events float on, its built out of them — terraces, roads, drained marshes, cleared hillsides, all of it congealed decisions nobody wrote down. so it ends with events looking like the only thing there ever was, seen from far enough away. no summarizing couplet, and dont name Braudel in the poem. 35-45 lines total.
