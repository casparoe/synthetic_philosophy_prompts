Ok, slightly unusual request — hope the setup makes sense before I get to the actual question.

I've got a philosophy MA (never finished the PhD) and last year I took a job at a small nonprofit that runs caregiver support programs for our county: respite vouchers, a modest stipend, a six-week training class. Then my dad had a stroke in the spring and I'm now the person filling out our own intake forms, which is how I noticied something that's been bugging me ever since. Our system sorts applicants into boxes — spouse, adult child, other relative, friend/neighbor, paid aide — and those boxes aren't describing anybody. They make the roles. Whether you get coded as a daughter-in-law or as a kind neighbor decides what money you can touch, what the social worker expects from you, and eventually what you feel obliged to do and who you think you are in the room with this person. I went back and re-read the Confucian role ethics material — the line of thought where a person isn't a self that then occupies roles but is more like a center of the roles they actually live — and it fits my situation disturbingly well, except I'm sitting on the other end of it now. I'm the one authoring role-definitions and watching them become real.

Here's the ask. I have a small amount of slack: me plus four volunteers, one evening a week, for a semester, to redo the intake categories and the training curriculum around them. Before I spend that, I want to know which unresolved arguments in role ethics deserve the time — meaning, where settling the question would actually change what I write down, and where it wouldn't. So give me a ranked short list, four items or so, and defend the ordering rather than just listing. For each: what turns on it in my situation specifically, whether it's reachable by a small non-academic group in four months or whether it needs professional machinery I don't have, and how the four relate — is there one about how roles get individuated at all that the rest hang off of?

Candidates I keep circling, and please tell me if these are the wrong ones. Whether a role can be criticized from inside the role, or whether role ethics is stuck ratifying whatever taxonomy it inherits — which for me is the difference between revising our categories and just administering them more kindly. Whether role-obligations have any ordering when they collide, or whether harmony is just the word for not having a procedure. Whether there's a role nobody recognizes: my friend/neighbor category is where a huge share of the actual care happens and it gets the least standing and the least money. Whether filial piety travels out of the family at all, which matters because something like half my clients are cared for by people with no kinship claim whatsoever. And what you do when the institution you're embedded in only speaks in the vocabulary of rights, eligibility and consent — I have to hand a legal funder a definition of who counts, and there's no slot on the form for anything relational.

Tell me which of these to burn the semester on, which to drop, and what kind of evidence or argument over the next few months should make me re-rank them. I don't want a survey of the field, I want a defended agenda I can put in front of four tired volunteers on a Tuesday.
