For eleven years I have trained ranking models for a logistics firm, and three weeks ago, in a design review, I said out loud that the vectors inside our system do not mean anything to it — that the only real causation is at the level of floating-point arithmetic, and everything above that is a story we tell about the arithmetic. A colleague answered by putting Douglas Hofstadter's careenium, from I Am a Strange Loop, on the agenda of the reading circle I run at the company: the imaginary brain-like substrate populated by simmballs, the marbles that clump into stable symbols, and the claim that the pushing runs in both directions — the substrate moves the symbols, and the symbols, once they exist, move the substrate. I have to lead that session, and I have to lead it honestly, because the remark I made in that review is the position I still privately hold.

The circle is nine people: six engineers, a product manager, a lawyer, and my brother-in-law, who reads philosophy for pleasure and will know more of the technical vocabulary than I do. Ninety minutes, one whiteboard, and a physical demo I built for the occasion — a slanted pegboard holding about two hundred steel ball bearings, a lip at the bottom, and recesses that trap bearings into clusters, so that a cluster can grow, persist, capture passing bearings, and occasionally collapse. I can flick individual bearings in with a launcher.

What I need is not an explanation of the careenium but a plan for how I should explain it, addressed to these five questions in order.

1. Dependency. Which pieces must be in place before the central claim is even intelligible — token versus type, levels of description, multiple realizability, the worry that the low level has already fixed every collision — and what genuinely depends on what. Tell me which of these I may assume this audience brings and which I have to construct, and where in the ninety minutes each belongs.

2. The carrying case. Should the pegboard do the whole job, or does it need a companion case, and if so which one? Say what the example must have in order to work — I suspect a cluster must be shown capturing bearings it did not create, and one collapse must be shown altering the fate of a later bearing — and what it must conspicuously lack so that nobody smuggles a ghost into the recesses. My fear is that the pegboard reads as too obviously a metaphor, so that the room treats it as an illustration of the argument rather than as the argument.

3. Misconceptions. I expect four: that a symbol is a little extra thing laid on top of the physics; that symbols pushing the substrate means an additional force competing with the collisions, so the picture is either dualist or overdetermined; that a simmball is a tiny picture of what it stands for; and that the whole business settles whether the model we ship is conscious. Tell me the exact point in the sequence at which each must be headed off, and then tell me which misconception I have failed to anticipate — the one this material reliably produces in technically trained audiences that I am not braced for.

4. The check. One question I can put to the room in the final ten minutes that separates people who have grasped the two-way push from people who are simply agreeing with me politely. It should have a wrong answer that sounds right.

5. Refusals. What I should decline to explain at this level, said out loud, and how to say it without seeming evasive. I have no intention of covering the supervenience technicalities or the full strange-loop apparatus, but I do not know where else the honest line falls.

Please do not send me readings; I own the book. Finally, be explicit about where this plan is weakest rather than reassuring me. Identify the single step you expect to fail in the room, say what consideration would make you abandon the pegboard or reorder the opening twenty minutes entirely, and tell me plainly if you think the session as I have framed it is one I should not attempt — for instance because my position from the design review is in fact correct and the two-way claim cannot be made to survive the determinism objection my brother-in-law will press. If that is your judgment, state it and say what would have to be true for you to hold it.
