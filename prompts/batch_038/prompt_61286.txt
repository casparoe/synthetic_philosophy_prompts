ok so my partner and I are going through IVF and I've been down a rabbit hole about what I'd actually sign off on, and my intuitions are a total mess. I know the term procreative beneficence and I've read a bit of Habermas-ish stuff about programming your kids, but I don't want a lit review — I want my cases sorted. Tell me whether there's a principle underneath them, or which ones have to go.

Here's what I actually think, case by case:

- We're both carriers for Tay-Sachs, clinic has three embryos, one is affected. Transfer an unaffected one and leave the affected one in the freezer or discard it: totally fine, I'd feel like a monster not to.
- Same setup, except the clinic offers to correct the variant in the affected embryo so we can transfer it instead. I think this is also fine, but it feels worse, like I'm doing something *to* somebody rather than choosing between people. Can't explain that.
- Selecting against a variant linked to congenital deafness: fine. A deaf couple selecting *for* it: not fine. I know that's asymetric and I can't defend it very well.
- Sex selection because we already have two boys and I'd love a daughter: fine. Sex selection because the dad wants a son to carry the name: also fine, technically? but it grosses me out and I don't know why, and the two cases look the same to me on paper.
- Picking a sperm donor partly because there's no bipolar disorder in his family: I did that and I think it was fine. Editing an embryo to remove a bipolar-risk variant: not fine. Same trait, same expected outcome, different method.
- Ranking our ten embryos by a polygenic score for educational attainment and taking the top one: not fine, feels like a sorting hat. Taking folic acid, not drinking, eating decently in pregnancy so the kid's brain develops well: obviously fine and praiseworthy. I cannot locate the difference in kind.
- Editing so the kid ends up four inches taller than they otherwise would: not fine. Editing so a kid with a muscle-wasting condition has ordinary muscle: fine.
- Editing out a variant that makes someone very distractable — the kid who can't sit still in a classroom — even though I suspect that same variant would have been an advantage in basically every other way humans have ever lived: torn, leaning not fine, but only barely, and I don't trust my lean.
- A thing the clinic is floating (fictional, just go with it): the edit can be switched off at 18 if the kid wants it switched off. Reversible programming. I think this dissolves my objection to a lot of the above — except I notice it doesn't dissolve the sorting-hat one, and I can't say why not.

And the one I keep circling back to. Only two embryos are viable, A and B, and B is better on every measure they can give us. If we pick B, A never exists. Nobody's harmed by picking B — A isn't sitting somewhere being wronged, A just never is. So why does it feel like I owe A something? Is that feeling tracking a real reason, and if it is, does that reason generalize to every case on my list or only to this one?

What I want back: the principle, or a short set of candidates, stated precisely enough that I could hand it to a stranger and they'd reproduce my verdicts without me in the room. For each clause, tell me which case forced it in. Then run it on two or three cases I didn't think of, and say what it delivers. And be honest about which of my verdicts it has to overturn — I'd rather lose one intuition cleanly than end up with a rule that has nine exceptions bolted onto it.
