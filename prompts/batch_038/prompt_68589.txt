I teach philosophy of mind and sit on our medical school's study-design review group. A former student has asked me to look at her company's product before I agree to write her a letter, and I should like a second opinion from someone working in the philosophy of language and mind. Please think this through carefully; I am not looking for product advice.

Her app lets people with chronic illness build a personal vocabulary for their symptoms. Rather than selecting from a fixed list — burning, stabbing, throbbing — each user coins her own tags. When a sensation strikes, she taps whichever tag she takes to fit, and the app silently logs the hour, location, medication, previous night's sleep, and her other taps that day. Two features trouble me. First, after a few weeks users often report that a tag no longer feels like it picks out what it did, and the founder's answer is that they should re-tag freely and let the meaning drift: on her account there is nothing further to be said about whether a user has applied her own tag correctly, since the only standard there could be is how the application strikes her at the moment. Second, she holds that the logs settle the matter anyway — if the circumstances recorded around a tag remain stable, the tag has a meaning, even though nobody but its author could ever use it.

Begin with a brief brainstorming section laying out at least two competing diagnoses of what is and is not wrong with this design, one of which should consider the possibility that the founder has misdescribed her own product to herself; then commit to one. In the advice proper, tell me: is the suspicion that these tags cannot genuinely mean anything damaging here, or is it aimed at a caricature? Do the automatically recorded circumstances supply whatever the first-person tagging lacks? And what should she change, if anything, that does not simply reintroduce the standard labels her users say do not fit them? Argue from the substance — no citations, passages, or reading lists, please.

After the prose, give me a JSON object with exactly these fields: "diagnosis" (one sentence naming the philosophical problem you take the design to raise); "correctness_is_illusory" (true, false, or partially — the founder's claim that there is no fact about correct application); "logs_supply_a_criterion" (yes, no, or qualified); "criterion_justification" (one sentence); "strongest_defense" (one or two sentences from the founder's side); "redesign" (array of at most three concrete changes); "confidence" (low, medium, or high).
