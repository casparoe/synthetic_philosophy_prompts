I cover AI for a science magazine and I'm applying for a nine-month reporting fellowship where the panel wants a plan, not a pitch letter. My half-formed idea: basically everybody frames corrigibility as a property of one agent — will it let you pull the plug — but the failure I keep running into in reporting looks nothing like that. The plug isn't held by you. Its held by a diffuse crowd (an API reseller, a compliance team, thousands of users who each have some right to appeal an action, downstream systems whose own operations depend on it), and their incentives arent yours. So a system could become effectively unshuttable without anybody deciding to make it that way, and without the agent ever resisting anything. I want to ask whether corrigibility is a property of a design or of a deployment, and whether it can quietly decay after launch.

Turn that into the proposal: the question stated tightly enough that it actually has an answer; why the usual framing (off-switch games, indifference tricks, the corrigible-agent literature) doesn't reach this; what I'd do month by month, including how much is reporting versus conceptual analysis and why that mix beats just interviewing the same dozen people; what a result looks like to a general-audience reader; and what would count as the project failing.
