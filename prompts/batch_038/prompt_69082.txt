Suppose a system has a state that meets every functional mark of being consciously accessible — it can be used in reasoning, it can be reported on, it can guide voluntary action, and the system sincerely and confidently says, when asked, that it is having an experience of the relevant kind — and yet on some defensible account of what makes a state phenomenal rather than merely available, nothing is experienced at all. I want that case built carefully, as an artifact someone could construct and instrument, rather than as a metaphysical possibility.

Some context first, because it determines how much of my framing you should trust. I work on inference infrastructure and I have no formal training in philosophy, so I will describe the theoretical background in my own words and I would ask you to correct me where I have got it wrong. I have been reading about the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness — roughly, the difference between a state's being available to a system for report and rational control, and there being something it is like for the system to be in that state — and about global workspace theory, on which conscious access consists in a content's being broadcast through a shared workspace to a range of consuming processes. I also read one of the multi-author reports that applies the leading scientific theories of consciousness to machine learning systems, which is what started this. What I want to know is whether the access/phenomenal distinction can be told apart by anything other than the definitions, in a case where the system is in front of me and measurable.

Please construct a thought experiment to the following specification.

The system:
1. A single engineered system, described in architectural terms — pipelines, buffers, gating, a shared broadcast channel, memory write paths, output heads. It should be plausible as a real artifact: recognizable components, no exotic physics, no stipulated magic.
2. It must have a state that satisfies, as fully as you can arrange, every functional mark of access consciousness: usable in reasoning, reportable, and available for the voluntary control of behavior. Crucially, the system must confidently and sincerely report having an experience of the relevant kind. A case in which the system reports nothing, or reports only that it is guessing, is not the case I want.
3. At the same time, on the best available account of what makes a state phenomenal, the state must lack whatever that account requires. I have in mind particularly the view that local, re-entrant processing within a sensory area is what constitutes experience, whether or not the content is ever broadcast. State which account you are using, say precisely what is missing, and make the absence fall out of the architecture rather than be asserted.

What I do not want:
- No philosophical zombies: nothing of the form "functionally identical, but the lights are off," where the absence of experience is stipulated rather than produced.
- No blindsight and no subliminal priming. Both are the standard examples and both turn on the subject's inability to report, which specification 2 rules out by design.
- No partial-report letter grids, no change blindness, no inattentional blindness, and nothing that leans on a subject's retrospective sense of having taken in more than they could report.
- No verdicts in the setup. If the description of the system already tells me that nothing is experienced there, the case fails. I want the theories applied to a neutral description to generate the verdicts themselves.

What I want back:
- The scenario, in one or two paragraphs, with the information flow spelled out clearly enough that I could sketch it as a block diagram.
- The verdicts and the reasons for them: what a global workspace theorist says about this system and why; what a theorist who locates experience in local recurrent processing says and why; and what someone who takes the access/phenomenal distinction seriously but stays neutral between those two should say. Be explicit about whether the three disagree about the facts of the case or only about which facts matter.
- The load-bearing feature. Name the single architectural feature that generates the dissociation, say what happens if it is removed, and say what happens if it is dialed up or down rather than removed. If dialing it produces a continuum rather than a clean split, say so plainly — for my purposes that would be a result, not a failure.
- The strongest attack on the case from the global workspace side. I expect it to concern what independent evidence there could be for phenomenality that does not itself route through report, reasoning, or control. If the attack succeeds, say so, and say what is left of the distinction.
- A note on instrumentation: what I would have to measure in the artifact to establish that the load-bearing feature is present or absent, and whether that measurement could in principle settle whether anything is experienced, or only whether the architecture has a certain shape.
- Whether there is a human case with the same shape — someone performing a familiar task competently, answering questions about it while doing so, and afterwards having no memory of the stretch — and exactly where that analogy breaks down.
- Finally, the minimal change to this architecture that would flip the case into the opposite dissociation, something phenomenal but not accessible, and whether that flip can be achieved under the restrictions above or whether it unavoidably depends on the contested sort of evidence I excluded.

Please describe the theories by what they claim rather than by who claims them or where they were published; I am not looking for references or a reading list, and I would rather have your reasoning than your citations.
