I've got 90 minutes at our company's ML summer school to teach Dennett's four kinds of minds — the Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian and Gregorian creatures. Audience is about 40 engineers who train models all day, know RL and transformers cold, have never read any philosophy. My worry is that they'll map the taxonomy onto machine learning inside the first five minutes, and that the mapping will be wrong in a way that quietly wrecks everything downstream, and I'd much rather build the session so the wrong mapping never gets a foothold than spend an hour saying no.

So plan it for me, don't explain it to me. What has to be in place before the first rung even makes sense — my guess is some idea about where the design information in a creature comes from and what exactly is doing the selecting, but I'm not sure that's the right first brick or whether it'll just sound like a warmup. Which single case should carry the whole thing (I was going to walk up through a jewel wasp, a rat and a chimp, but maybe one running example beats four), and what that example has to have and what it must conspicuously lack. Then the misconception map: I'm bracing for "Gregorian just means has language so obviously the cleverest," "each rung replaces the one below it," and "gradient descent is a selection process so every net we train is a Darwinian creature." Which of those is actually the load-bearing error, at what point in the sequence do I head each one off, and am I missing the one that engineers specifically will form? Also, my instinct is that Popperian is the rung doing all the work and Gregorian nearly comes for free once you've got it — argue with me if thats wrong. And there's the awkward question of whether a taxonomy built for organisms applies at all to artifacts whose designer sits outside the loop; tell me straight whether to raise it, duck it, or make it the hinge of the session.

Finish with what I should refuse to cover at this level and say out loud that I'm skipping it, plus one question I can ask at the end that would show whether they got the idea or just memorised four labels. Be committed about all of it — tell me what you'd actually do and why, not a menu of defensible options for me to choose between.
