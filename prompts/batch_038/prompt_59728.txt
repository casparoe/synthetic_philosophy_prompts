I am preparing a statement of my own position for a political philosophy workshop at which each participant circulates their commitments in advance and the group audits them. Mine concerns freedom, and it runs as follows.

1. Freedom is non-domination, not non-interference. The case that settles this for me is the slave with a benevolent master who never in fact interferes: the slave is unfree, and unfree in respect of the master's mere capacity.
2. Interference is arbitrary, and hence dominating, when it fails to track the interests and opinions of the person who suffers it. This is the criterion I use throughout.
3. Freedom's value is instrumental. I am a welfarist about ultimate value: freedom matters because, and to the extent that, it serves wellbeing.
4. Nonetheless, the slave case and the case of a comfortably provided-for person whose life is arranged by another seem to reveal something bad over and above any welfare shortfall, and I take that residue to be precisely what the notion of domination names.
5. Heeding Berlin's warning, I treat autonomy or self-mastery as a value distinct from freedom, and I deny that a state may coerce anyone in the name of their higher or real interests.
6. I am persuaded by the capability approach: what matters is people's effective opportunities, so a destitute person is less free than a well-off one, and redistribution can increase freedom.
7. Obstacles to freedom must be external and attributable to other agents. Nobody is unfree merely in virtue of their own unwise preferences, though I do hold that a person whose preferences have been shaped by manipulation is less free, and that this is a fact about freedom rather than about wellbeing.
8. Extensive non-arbitrary regulation — taxation, licensing, safety rules — does not reduce freedom, so a state may greatly expand the reach of law without shrinking liberty.

Please audit this set rather than expound the topic to me. Say which pairs genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to, and rank the real conflicts by severity. For each, state the cheapest revision and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the list. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most. I would rather not be pointed toward further reading; I will handle sources myself.
