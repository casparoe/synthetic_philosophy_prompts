Long argument with my roommate last night about citizens' assemblies and I still can't tell which of us was right. We were designing a hypothetical 100-person assembly to decide a city budget, and I noticed I have very strong feelings about how you pick the 100 people but no idea what those feelings add up to. Here's my list, in the order we argued about them.

Drawing 100 names at random from the voter roll: fine, obviously. Drawing 500 and letting whoever actually turns up be the assembly: also fine, maybe better, since nobody's compelled. Drawing 100, paying them properly, and letting them decline: fine. Drawing names until the group matches the city on age, income, education, and zip code: fine, and I'd insist on this one. If too few people without a college degree say yes, keep drawing until the numbers come out right: fine. Screening the drawn people with a short interview and dropping the ones who already have firm opinions about the budget: not fine, that felt like rigging, though my roommate said it's no different from voir dire. Drawing only from volunteers who put their own name in a hat: not fine, that's just a club. Drawing from volunteers recruited by a paid firm going door to door: fine — which is basically the same pool, and i can't explain why one is okay and the other isn't. Reserving 10 of the 100 seats for people who work in the city finance department: not fine. Reserving 10 for people who've been homeless in the last five years: fine. Again, no idea why those differ. Drawing a second random assembly to review the first one's proposal: fine, feels like a check. Letting the assembly choose its own replacements when members drop out: very not fine.

My roommate's principle is just "if the draw was fair, the body is legitimate," which sweeps up most of my verdicts but explains neither of the two pairs I'm stuck on. Is there a principle, or a small set of competing candidates, that gets these right or close? I want it stated precisely enough that I can test it, told what it implies about cases I didn't list (minors? non-citizens? a citywide draw that happens to select 60% of one party?), and told honestly which of my verdicts it can't save and would force me to drop rather than which clause I should bolt on to rescue them.
