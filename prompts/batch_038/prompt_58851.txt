I sit on the editorial standards committee of a mid-sized journal — eleven members — and we increasingly find ourselves voting not on single yes/no questions but on propositions that hang together logically: whether a published paper contains a factual error, whether the error goes to the substance of the argument, and therefore whether the paper should be retracted. Last month we voted premise by premise, and then, because our bylaws also require a recorded vote on the retraction question itself, our minutes ended up affirming both premises while rejecting the retraction — a combination of positions that no individual member holds. When we reran the same matter as a single vote on the conclusion, the outcome flipped. Our chair has asked me to come back with a recommendation about which procedure we should adopt permanently, and I have been reading on the discursive dilemma and on the impossibility results that generalize it, but every paper I pick up simply announces a list of formal conditions and then proves that no rule meets them, without ever saying why those are the conditions a body like ours ought to care about.

So before I recommend anything, I want the specification itself. Write the desiderata that a procedure for aggregating individual sets of judgments must satisfy to count as adequate for a real deliberative body that has to produce a single actionable collective verdict, be able to defend that verdict as its own rather than as an artifact of the order in which questions were put, and keep functioning as members' opinions shift and membership turns over. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it — a concrete profile, a failure mode like the one we just hit, or the intuition it is protecting — and state explicitly whether you regard it as non-negotiable or as a preference belonging to one camp in the literature: those who care above all about the consistency of the collective set, those who care about responsiveness to individual members, those who care about treating each issue on its own terms rather than letting connections between issues drive the result. Please also separate requirements that look like a single demand but pull apart — treating all members alike is not the same as treating all propositions alike — and say what a body gives up if it drops one of the pair.

Then the part I most want: identify which subset of your list cannot be satisfied together, and say which member of that subset a committee in our position should be the first to sacrifice and what that sacrifice costs us in practice. Please answer as exactly five numbered items of two to four sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion: items one through four the desiderata, each with its motivating case and its non-negotiable or camp-relative status, and item five the jointly unsatisfiable subset together with the concession you recommend.
