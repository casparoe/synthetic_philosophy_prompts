So I play in an online league that runs nothing but iterated prisoner's dilemma — standard payoffs, fixed 30 rounds a match, everyone knows before round 1 that it's 30, and there's a chat window between rounds with zero enforcement, pure cheap talk. The league has a moderator, Nadia, who adjudicates "bad faith" reports, and her rulings have basically turned into case law; people cite them in the Discord constantly. A flag costs you points and this season it's also the tiebreaker for who gets into the invitational, which is why I care this much about one specific report. Her rulings so far, as near to her own words as I can reconstruct from the writeups:

1. Someone typed "I'll match you the whole way, I promise" and then hit D in round 5 and again in round 9. Flagged. Nadia: "she said one thing and did another, that's the whole report."
2. Someone opened with "I'm running always-defect, don't trust me for one second" and defected all 30. Dismissed: "told you exactly what was coming."
3. Someone asked point blank "are you cooperating this round?" got back "why would I tell you that?" and then defected. Dismissed: "declining to answer isn't answering."
4. Someone who'd traded C's for 14 straight rounds hit a single unannounced D in round 15, ate the retaliation, and went right back to cooperating. Dismissed, but only just — her note was "that's a probe, not a con. He paid for it and they kept playing."
5. Someone typed in round 28 "no hard feelings, I'm taking the last one" and defected in round 30. Dismissed: "there's no round 31. Everyone knows the last round is free."

The new case is my friend Ines. She played 30 rounds against a guy who never once defected on her. She said nothing committal the entire match — every direct question got the same evasive non-answer as in ruling 3 — but she cooperated from round 3 onward, matched him every single time, and filled the chat with warm stuff ("nice," "again?", "this is my favourite match of the season"). In round 30 she hit D and took the sucker's payoff, and he filed. Half the league has been arguing about it in the abstract ever since: one side says 27 rounds of C from Ines was never cooperation at all, it was just a defection that hadnt come due yet; the other side says if you accept that then nobody in a known-length match has ever cooperated with anyone and the whole category collapses. Personally I keep flipping between precedents 4 and 5 — 4 because that one was also about hitting someone who'd earned nothing, 5 because that one was also the last round — and the thing that wont leave me alone is that the reason Nadia gave in 4 was about him paying for it afterwards, which Ines never does.

Can you work out what Nadia should rule if she wants to stay consistent with her own five precedents, and which one is the closest analog — and tell me whether my 4-vs-5 waffling is actually a sign that the precedents are split, or just me not seeing which one does the work? Then say what rule she's running, because I can think of at least three that fit all five writeups but come apart here: something about your words matching your actions, something about whether the other player had any way to respond to it, and something about trust you manufactured on purpose being a different object than trust that just accrued. Which of those does the Ines case force her to choose, and what does she lose by choosing it?
