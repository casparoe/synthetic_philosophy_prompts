ok so I'm trying to untangle something and I think a careful comparison would help me more than another general overview.

Two ideas that get used almost interchangeably whenever someone says "history matters," but which I'm now pretty sure are not the same idea at all:

1. Path dependence in the economist's sense — increasing returns, network effects, small accidents getting amplified, multiple equilibria, lock-in. QWERTY as the poster child.
2. Sensitive dependence on initial conditions in the dynamical-systems sense — chaos, positive Lyapunov exponents, butterfly effect, trajectories diverging exponentially.

Both get invoked to support the claim that if you'd nudged the past a little you'd have gotten a different present. But I think they come apart in both directions: a chaotic system can have a single attractor that every trajectory ends up on, which makes it hyper-sensative to initial conditions and yet path-INdependent about where it lands; and a locked-in system can be completely stable and predictable and still be merely one of several possible resting points.

Write me a comparison that does the following.

- Give me the cleanest statement of each notion you can: what exactly is a function of what, and over what time horizon. Path dependence is usually glossed as the future depending on the whole path rather than just the current state — make that precise, and then say how it differs from the chaos gloss.
- Here's a distinction I want you to use and stress-test rather than just accept: split path dependence into route-dependence (the endpoint is roughly fixed, the trajectory to it depends on history) and destination-dependence (which endpoint you land in depends on history). Then note that lock-in is itself ambiguous between "the endpoint is stable, nothing will push you out of it" and "you can't retrace the path you came in on." Build the resulting grid, and say which cells actually get populated by real cases and which ones are empty or incoherent.
- Then tell me which of the two notions the replay-the-tape thought experiment in evolutionary biology is really testing, and whether your answer changes if you take the convergence-friendly line that lots of traits get invented several times independently anyway.
