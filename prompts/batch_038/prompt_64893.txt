building a sorting instrument for my reading group on whether life is meaningful, and I need the questions more than the labels. Please write a decision tree: first question at the top, each branch point labeled with the claim that splits it, leaves = named positions. Around ten questions, and please phrase every one as a concrete case someone could answer without knowing which position their answer leads to — no "do you think meaning is objective?", that's the whole point of the exercise.

The distinctions I care about, in rough order:

- meaning vs. worth living vs. good for the person. I want the tree to actually separate "my life is meaningless" from "my life is a bad one" and from "it isn't worth continuing". A lot of people slide between those three and I want the slide to be visible as a branch.
- does value have to be recognised from somewhere outside us, or is being engaged / satisfied / endorsing your own life enough.
- actual results vs. attitude. Use the Taylor material: Sisyphus rolling the stone up forever and being happy about it, versus Sisyphus whose stone is at least the foundation of a genuinely beautiful temple, versus a variant where he builds the temple and it's demolished the next day. My group is going to disagree on all three and I want the disagreement to be tracked.
- whether a life can be meaningful without the person ever knowing it, and the reverse: the devoted person who spends fifty years on something they're wrong about (a cult, a falsified research program, a cause that never materialises). Do they get credit for the trying?
- the experience machine, as a question about whether feeling like your life matters counts for anything.
- whether the scale of time matters — a universe with no one in it 10^100 years from now, does that retroactively drain things?
- and Camus. I need absurdism to show up as its own leaf and not get collapsed into either pessimism or into "subjectivism with extra steps", because that collapsing is exactly what half my group will do.

Required leaves somewhere in the space: a supernaturalist view, a pure subjectivist one, a pure objectivist one, hybridism, Camus's absurdism, the pessimism/nihilism leaf, and at least two positions you think are coherent but nobody actually defends — label those "unoccupied" and give one sentence each on whether they're unoccupied because they're incoherent, because they're unattractive, or because nobody's bothered to look.

Also flag honestly any pair of leaves your questions can't cleanly separate, rather than pretending the tree sorts everything. And please distinguish, where it comes up, what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's a live fight — I don't want one camp's commitments handed to my group as the consensus.

Format: plain text, indent the branches, no diagrams that won't survive copy-pasting. Hard cap of 950 words for the whole thing, tree included.
