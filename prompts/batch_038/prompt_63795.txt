I have built an argument as a reductio of a rival position and now suspect that it is a reductio of my own. The target was the priority view; I am no longer sure that it misses.

The setup, as I understand it. Prioritarianism holds that the moral value of an outcome is the sum, over persons, of f(w), where w is a person's well-being and f is strictly increasing and strictly concave. The concavity is what makes a given increment matter more the worse off its recipient is. Unlike telic egalitarianism, the view is not relational: it responds to levels, not to gaps. That is precisely why it is supposed to survive Parfit's levelling-down objection — taking from the better off, with nobody gaining, strictly lowers the sum. The view is standardly stated with w as lifetime well-being, and my argument is aimed at showing that this restriction is not earned by the view's own rationale.

The argument.

(1) The only reason the priority view offers for concavity is that a benefit matters more the worse off its recipient is. That reason appeals to a level of well-being and to nothing else.

(2) Nothing in that reason turns on the recipient's being a person taken as a whole rather than a person at a time. Well-being, however, is had at times.

(3) So the concave weighting applies to well-being at times, and the value of a life is the sum, over its years, of f(w) for that year.

(4) Suppose a single person, with nothing else in the world differing, can have either of two lives. Life A: forty years at well-being 80, followed by forty years at well-being 10 — a brilliant career and a wretched old age. Life B: eighty years at a steady 34.

(5) A is better for the person than B on the ordinary account of a person's good, according to which well-being within a life adds up: A has both the higher total (3,600 against 2,720) and the higher average (45 against 34).

(6) By (3), taking f(w) = ln w, the value of A is 40(ln 80 + ln 10) ≈ 267.4 and the value of B is 80 ln 34 ≈ 282.1. So B is better than A.

(7) Therefore the priority view, weighted at the grain its own rationale specifies, implies that in a world containing only this person it can be better that they have a life that is worse for them, with nobody else gaining anything. At a finer grain it implies the same of ordinary prudence: a lossy smoothing of one's own life is called for, so that giving up a rewarding year to buy a marginally less bad one is required whenever the transfer loses little enough — which makes saving, training, and elective surgery impermissible in a great many actual cases.

(8) That is a levelling-down verdict: a distribution is preferred for being flatter, at the cost of leaving the person worse off overall.

(9) But the priority view is supposed to be the position that retains what is attractive in egalitarianism while escaping exactly that verdict, and the familiar dialectic is that the levelling-down objection disposes of telic egalitarianism and leaves the priority view as the defensible residue.

(10) So either the priority view does not escape the objection, or the objection indicts both views and cannot discriminate between them. Either way (9) is false and the dialectic collapses.

Two notes before you answer. The arithmetic in (6) is load-bearing, so please check it rather than take it on trust. And the flip depends on how concave f is: with f(w) = √w, A still wins (484.3 against 466.5). Whether that sensitivity is a weakness in my argument or part of its point is one of the things I want your view on.

I cannot accept (7). If I am forced to choose, I would rather surrender (3) than (1) or (9), but I cannot see how (3) is meant to fail given (2), and I suspect that abandoning (3) costs more than it appears to. So: identify the step that goes wrong and explain why it managed to look innocent; price each of the available exits — giving up (2), giving up (3), giving up (5), and resisting the move from (6) to (7) — saying what the prioritarian loses on each; and state whether any exit is free. Finish by telling me whether some version of the priority view is immune to this argument without ceasing to be a version of the priority view.

Please distinguish clearly between what is settled here and what is genuinely contested among people who work on this material, and do not present a contested diagnosis as though it were the consensus. Where you think my argument is merely trading on a stipulation I have made, say so rather than working within it. Write the whole answer in British English.
