I spent thirty-eight years in experimental physics, mostly designing measurements sensitive enough to register effects that everyone assumed were too small to see, and I retired two years ago. Since then I have been reading the philosophy of action with more seriousness than the field probably expects of me, and I have come to the free will debate carrying a habit I cannot switch off. When several models compete to describe one and the same body of data, I ask which of them buy their fit by quietly respecifying the data. In my line of work, a model that redefines the observable in order to match it ranks below a model that misses the observable and says so plainly.

I would like that triage applied to the positions in this debate. Call the first quantity revision debt: how much of what ordinary agents actually do and say when they deliberate, choose, blame, take credit, regret, resolve, and describe themselves as the authors of their own actions a position must, once it is fully spelled out, declare to be systematically mistaken or else merely useful fiction. Call the second quantity concealment: how readily the position can be stated in the everyday idiom of deliberation such that the debt never becomes audible to the person carrying it.

The positions I want triaged are these eleven:

1. The classical conditional analysis: freedom as absence of external constraint plus the truth of the relevant counterfactuals about what one would have done had one chosen otherwise.
2. The dispositional refinement of that analysis, including the finkish-disposition machinery associated with Lewis and the capacity accounts of Vihvelin and Fara, where a capacity may be masked and never exercised and no alternate possibilities are needed.
3. Frankfurt-style compatibilism: responsibility without alternate possibilities, secured by identification with one's desires at a higher order.
4. Reasons-responsiveness: guidance control exercised by a mechanism that is moderately responsive to reasons, with an explicitly historical condition, as in Fischer and Ravizza.
5. The reactive-attitudes strategy of P. F. Strawson: the truth of determinism is beside the point, because responsibility is constituted by participation in resentment, indignation, gratitude and the like.
6. Lewis's local-miracle compatibilism: we can do otherwise in the sense that, had we chosen differently, a small local miracle would have occurred, with the laws understood Humeanly.
7. Van Inwagen's consequence argument: nobody ever had a choice about the past or about the laws of nature, so if determinism is true nobody ever had a choice about anything.
8. Kane's event-causal libertarianism: indeterminism located within the competing efforts of will that constitute self-forming actions.
9. Agent-causal libertarianism: substances rather than events as the irreducible originators of action, in the manner of Taylor, O'Connor and Clarke.
10. Pereboom's hard incompatibilism: free will and basic desert are impossible whether determinism is true or false, but the practices of blame and praise can be rebuilt on forward-looking grounds.
11. Galen Strawson's basic argument: self-creation is required for ultimate responsibility and is impossible, determinism or no determinism.

For each, assign an integer from 0 to 10 for revision debt, where 0 means the position leaves the ordinary deliberative self-conception intact and claims to, and 10 means the position requires that essentially all of it be reinterpreted as error; and an integer from 0 to 10 for concealment, where 0 means the cost is stated up front in the position's own headline claim, and 10 means the cost only surfaces after several layers of technical machinery have been traversed. Let composite be the sum, and rank all eleven by composite with 1 being highest, that is, most suspect on my scheme. Ties are permitted and should be reported as ties.

Give me the result as a single JSON object, nothing outside it, with exactly this shape:

- ranked_positions: an array of eleven objects, each with the fields position (a short label matching my list), revision_debt (integer), concealment (integer), composite (integer), rank (integer), what_must_be_declared_mistaken (which specific piece of ordinary practice or self-description the position is committed to calling an error, in twenty-five words or fewer), debt_becomes_visible_when (a description, in twenty-five words or fewer, of the claim a candid proponent would have to endorse for the debt to show), and criterion_strain (how badly my two quantities fit this particular position, or the word none if they fit cleanly).
- tie_groups: an array of arrays of position labels, one array per set of positions whose composites are equal.
- most_likely_objector: an object with fields position and objection, naming the position whose defenders would most forcefully dispute my scoring of it and giving the strongest thing they would say.
- scheme_critique: two or three sentences on where my scheme itself goes wrong. I suspect it penalises candour and rewards nothing, and I suspect it is incoherent for any position that denies there is a stable target to be revised against. Tell me plainly if that is so, and tell me which of the eleven it bites hardest on.
- center_of_gravity: one or two sentences saying where the mass of this debate actually sits on my scale, and whether the compatibilist and incompatibilist camps are separated by revision debt or only by concealment.

Two house rules. Do not give me references, a bibliography, or a reading list; I want your judgement and not a set of places to go and look. And do not put anything in quotation marks anywhere in the response: where I ask what reveals the debt, describe the claim in your own words rather than reproducing anybody's sentence.
