I've been sorting my intuitions about when an agent counts as wireheading or tampering with its own reward, and the verdicts I actually hold do not seem to hang together. Here they are.

1. A rat pressing a lever to stimulate an electrode in its own reward pathway, continuing past the point of starvation: wireheading, obviously.
2. A reinforcement-learning agent that leaves its reward function untouched but places a fabricated image in front of its camera, so the function scores the world as better than it is: tampering, obviously.
3. The same agent instead rewrites the code mapping world-states to value, and thereafter genuinely prefers building paperclips to everything else, pursuing that new preference honestly in the real world: not tampering. It has acquired a goal, not faked satisfying one.
4. That same agent rewrites the mapping so that mediocre outcomes now score as high as excellent ones used to, and then stops working: tampering. Structurally this is (3) exactly, and yet I reverse.
5. A human who wears a nicotine patch until the craving is gone: fine.
6. A human who takes a drug that permanently deletes the ambition to write the novel they have wanted for a decade to write: not fine — though they would be content, and the novel was never going to be finished.
7. A human who spends two years in therapy and stops resenting their father: fine.
8. A human who pays to have that resentment surgically erased in an afternoon: I keep flipping on this one.
9. An agent that appends a penalty for states in which it is switched off: not tampering, just predictable instrumental behaviour — and yet I want to prohibit it by force.

Is there a principle that delivers all nine, or nearly all? Please state it (or a small set of candidates) precisely, clause by clause, naming the case above that motivates each clause. Then tell me what it implies for a case I have not listed: an agent that rewrites its reward function in a way its designers would endorse if fully informed, where the endorsement is itself produced by the rewrite. Say plainly which of my nine verdicts a good principle forces me to abandon, and, if saving all of them requires something ad hoc, show me the ad hoc clause and how much it costs.
