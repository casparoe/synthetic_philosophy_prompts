I'm 38, I drive a school bus and do some landscaping on the side, and I've been depressed pretty much continuously since I was 26. Four rounds of meds, one of them I actually stuck with for two years, and the psychiatrist finally said the word ECT to me last week. I'm not against it, honestly I'm tired enough that I kind of want it. The thing that's got me stuck isn't the seizures or the anesthesia, its what the coordinator said when I asked about memory: she said most of the gaps fill back in over a few months, but stuff from right around the treatment, and some older stuff too, can just be gone, and she didn't want to promise me how much. So I went home and tested myself. I tried to remember our honeymoon and I got exactly one image — a blue door — and then nothing, not even my wife's face on the day. I sat at the kitchen table for about an hour looking for other holes and I found a bunch.

Here's the part I cant get out of my head and I know how it sounds. If I do this, the guy who wakes up after twelve treatments has my name, my job, my wife, my dog, my truck payment and my body, but a big stretch of his past is missing, including maybe some of the reasons he loves the people he loves. It feels to me like I'd be handing my whole life over to a stranger who happens to look like me and who everybody is going to tell, very kindly, that he's me. My wife says that's crazy, I'll still be me, just with gaps. My sister says the opposite, that I'd be killing myself for a cure. And I can't tell which of them is right, or whether the question even has a right answer, or whether it's just two ways of describing facts I already know. Because here's the weird part: when I actually sit down and count how much is left of being 24, the guy who got married back then doesn't have much in him either, and I still care about what happens to me way more than I care about what happens to some kid on my bus route who I like fine. So why would a few more missing years flip that? Is there a line, or is the whole thing a slope, and if it's a slope how do I know where I am on it?

So two questions, and please don't just tell me to talk to my doctor, I've got the appointment Tuesday and I want to walk in knowing what I think. First: is the fear I've got actually a confused fear — like, is it resting on a mistake about what a person is — or is it tracking something real that I should be weighing against feeling normal again? Second: if it turns out nobody can say whether I'd survive the thing, does that change how I'm supposed to decide, or do I just decide it like any other health risk, trading memory for a shot at getting my head back? And then, more practical, how do I explain any of this to my wife without sounding like I think I'm going to die on a gurney? She keeps pointing out the death rate is tiny, like that answers me. It doesn't, and I don't know why not.
