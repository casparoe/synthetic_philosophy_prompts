Act as a professional philosopher who also knows the social-psychological literature on cultural tightness and looseness. Before you answer, brainstorm every distinct proposition the proverb might be carrying, then organise the results as a menu rather than arguing for your favourite reading.

I administer fellowships that place researchers in field offices abroad, and our offices differ sharply in how much conformity they demand: two are strict about punctuality, hierarchy, and never airing disagreement in front of the whole team, while one is unusually permissive, with staff arriving late, skipping formalities, and contradicting the director openly. The handbook requires visiting fellows to follow local office norms, and it justifies that requirement in one line: when in Rome, do as the Romans do. When I pointed out that in the permissive office this licenses exactly the conduct the rest of the organisation calls unprofessional, the handbook's author replied that the line merely records an obvious fact about being a guest, and that my example shows I have misunderstood it.

What precise claims could that sentence be making? For each candidate reading, state the claim in one sentence, classify it as trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and say what further premises would be needed to turn it into a requirement that visitors conform. Separate descriptive or predictive readings from normative ones, and within the normative ones distinguish a general thesis that local norms bind you because they are local from narrower ones grounded in etiquette and matters of indifference, in coordination, in gratitude or deference owed to hosts, or in local knowledge of what actually works there. Then tell me which reading, if any, supports the handbook, and which one explains my permissive-office case instead of being refuted by it. Finally: can the proverb hold for tight settings and simply fail to apply for loose ones — and if so, does it still express one obvious fact, or two different claims that have been run together?
