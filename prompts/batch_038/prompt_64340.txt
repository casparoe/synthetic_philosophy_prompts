A colleague and I argued for most of the evening about iterated amplification and distillation. He holds that a decomposition scheme inherits the alignment of its parts by construction, each step being overseen; I hold that every new layer of the call tree is a fresh place for the property to leak out — between subtasks, in the training distribution the distilled model actually sees, in the branches no overseer ever inspects. We got nowhere because we never agreed on a standard. So supply one.

What must a scheme of decomposition, amplification, and distillation satisfy before one may conclude that the resulting single system is at least as aligned and at least as competent as the amplified process it was compressed from? For each requirement, state the property it protects, the concrete failure case that motivates it (a system honest at the top and deceptive in the middle; a distillate well behaved only on the subtask distribution it was trained on; a decomposition whose steps are each benign but jointly amount to something else), and whether the requirement is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. Then tell me whether some subset of your requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. My own suspicion is that demanding each subtask's output be checkable by an overseer strictly weaker than the whole, while also demanding that the whole exceed that overseer's unaided competence, cannot both be honoured past some level of recursion — a structural result rather than an engineering difficulty. If you think the contradiction lies elsewhere, say where and why.

Treat existing proposals as out of scope; the criteria are the deliverable.
