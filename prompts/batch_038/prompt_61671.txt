I am an engineering lead at a studio that builds VR training simulations, and I have been asked to run a fifty-minute internal session for twelve engineers. None of them has studied philosophy; most have one introductory psychology course behind them and a year of work on predictive rendering, reprojection, and motion-to-photon latency budgets. The session is not cultural enrichment. I want them to stop reasoning as though there is a determinate instant at which the user "has" an experience of a given event, because that assumption is now driving poor decisions about when to commit simulation state, when to interpolate, and how to order audio and haptic events relative to rendered frames. The vehicle is Dennett's attack on the Cartesian theater and the multiple drafts model.

I ran a version of this last quarter and it went badly. Half the room left saying that consciousness is an illusion and that Dennett thinks nothing is real; the other half adopted the vocabulary and then immediately reinstalled a little viewer, asking who is reading the drafts. I do not want a repeat, and I do not want a script. I want the plan.

Give me the order in which the components must be introduced, with the dependency stated at each step: what cannot be said before what, and why. Then the single demonstration that should carry the idea. I have written a two-light demo in which two dots of different colours are shown in succession at different positions, at a spacing and interval where people report one dot crossing the gap and changing colour partway along; assess whether that is the right carrier on its own merits rather than because a well-known philosopher pressed it into service, and say what any example here must exhibit and must not exhibit. Tell me why the two substitutes I had shortlisted, the invisible-gorilla task and the blind spot, would mislead this particular audience, and diagnose the analogy I keep reaching for — that experience is a film edit rather than a live broadcast — which I suspect fails for a specific reason. Repair that analogy or replace it. Map the misconceptions the group will arrive with or generate, each paired with the point in the sequence where it has to be headed off, and tell me how to make the objection that something must still read the drafts load-bearing rather than suppressed. Close with one question I can put to them that would show the idea landed in their own domain, and state explicitly what you would leave out at this level, including whether to open the question of qualia at all and how to handle the fact that a prominent theory of consciousness they may have encountered also describes itself with a theatre metaphor. Whiteboard and one laptop. No bibliography and no citations.
