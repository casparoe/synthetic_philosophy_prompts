My seminar group has been pressing an argument against material constitution that I built, and its conclusion is one I cannot accept. Here it is:

(1) If x and y are distinct objects, then x and y differ in at least one property (Leibniz's Law).
(2) Wherever a thing is located, it has that location as one of its properties.
(3) So no two distinct objects share a location — not even in part: for any objects x, y and any region r, if x is located at r and y is located at r, then x = y.
(4) Consider the statue Hermes and the lump of clay Goliath it is made from. They are composed of exactly the same grains, and a thing is nothing over and above the grains composing it arranged as they are, so Goliath just is Hermes.
(5) But Goliath is composed of grains, and by (3) none of those grains is distinct from anything sharing its location — while Hermes, being composed of them, is located wherever they are. So each grain of Goliath is identical either to Hermes or to nothing at all. No grain is identical to Hermes. Therefore no grain exists.
(6) If no grain exists, nothing is composed of grains, so by (4) there is no Goliath and no Hermes.
(7) Hence, on pain of there being no heaps of sand, no statues, and no lumps of clay: taking a single grain from a heap of sand annihilates whatever occupied the rest of the heap's region.

The conclusion is absurd, and my instinct is that (4) and (7) are claims I most want to keep, so the trouble must be upstream. But I cannot see whether the argument contains a genuine slip — a step licensed only under a reading the earlier premises do not supply — or whether it is valid and I simply have to choose the cheapest premise to abandon. I do not want to be told the answer is "reject Leibniz's Law" without being shown why that move is or is not available here.

- "why_it_seemed_fine": two or three sentences on why that step looked unobjectionable in context.
- "cost_of_rejection": what giving it up commits you to, or what it forces you to explain elsewhere.
- "positions_paying_it": the standard positions on material constitution (e.g. monism, pluralism with coincidence, four-dimensionalism, eliminativism about ordinary objects, nihilism) that already accept that cost, each with one clause saying what else it must give up.
- "second_best_repair": the next step you would reject if the first repair turned out to be too expensive, with the same level of detail in a nested object using the four field names above.
- "verdict_on_4_and_7": whether (4) and (7) survive your repair intact, and if not, which of them goes and why.
