Say someone actually builds it: a personal AI you talk to about your own conduct, engineered from the ground up to be an impartial spectator. It has the full details of your situation, none of your passions, and it tells you how a completely informed and unbiased onlooker would see what you did — on demand, in company or in solitude, as often as you like.

Would Adam Smith think this is the finest moral technology imaginable, or would he say it quietly ruins the thing its copying? Go at the machinery rather than the slogans. On his account the inner spectator isn't a real person; it's something you build up out of a lifetime of actual spectators' reactions, and its whole job is to correct those reactions for their partiality. If a device just hands you the verdict instead of you constructing it by an act of imagination, does that distinction survive at all? Does the device count as a real spectator, an imagined one, or a third thing — and does it matter that it can't actually be pleased or disgusted by you, that it has no standing to withhold approval?

Then fold in Smith's own point that we systematically over-admire the rich and the successful: a model trained on what people in fact approve of would inherit exactly that distortion wholesale. And what becomes of a kid raised on one from infancy, if the spectator is formed by habit rather than by rules?
