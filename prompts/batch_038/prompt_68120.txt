**Question (10 points).**
(a) State the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and the self-indication assumption (SIA), and explain the respect in which they genuinely differ.
(b) Say what each assumption implies for the Doomsday argument.
(c) State the presumptuous philosopher objection and identify which of the two assumptions it is meant to undermine.

**Student's response.**

"(a) Both principles govern how you should reason from the fact that you exist and that you are this particular observer. SSA says you should reason as if you were a random sample from the set of observers who actually exist — past, present, and future — within your reference class. SIA says you should reason as if you were a random sample from the set of all possible observers. SIA is therefore just SSA adopted with the widest possible reference class: the two look like rivals only because SSA permits you to restrict the class, and any apparent disagreement disappears once the class is expanded to contain every observer who could ever exist. Since both principles require a reference class, both are equally exposed to the objection that a well-posed question can be made to come out any way you like by choosing the class differently.

(b) The Doomsday argument, in the form pressed by Gott and by Leslie, reasons from the fact that you are roughly the sixty-billionth human being to the conclusion that the total number of humans is unlikely to be vastly greater, on the grounds that an early birth rank is much more probable if few observers ever exist. Because SIA holds that hypotheses on which more observers exist are more likely to contain an observer like you, SIA delivers the same ranking as SSA in this case: both make it more likely, given your low birth rank, that humanity ends comparatively soon. SIA merely adds the point that you were more likely to exist at all in a large universe.

(c) The presumptuous philosopher objection is that a principle of this kind lets a philosopher settle a big empirical question from the armchair: if one cosmological theory posits a trillion trillion observers and a rival posits only a trillion, the principle hands the extravagant theory an overwhelming boost, so the philosopher claims to have defeated the modest theory without gathering any evidence whatever. Because it is SSA whose reference classes can be inflated at will, the objection is aimed at SSA. SIA escapes it, since under SIA only the observers who actually exist enter the sample."
