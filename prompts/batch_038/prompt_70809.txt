Both the Nash and the Kalai–Smorodinsky solutions to the two-person bargaining problem select a Pareto-efficient agreement given a disagreement point, and both are symmetric between players and invariant to positive affine rescalings of each player's utility. They diverge over a single axiom: Nash retains independence of irrelevant alternatives, whereas Kalai–Smorodinsky drops it in favour of a monotonicity requirement, and correspondingly selects the point at which the ray running from the disagreement point toward the "ideal" point — the most each player could obtain from any feasible agreement — exits the feasible set. Equivalently, it is the Pareto point at which each player's gain over disagreement bears the same proportion to that player's own ideal gain.

Say whether that objection is peculiar to Kalai–Smorodinsky or whether it also bites against Nash, and why.

(b) The equal-proportions rule is well defined because each player's own gain and ideal gain are rescaled together.
