A start-up is recruiting paid participants for a program I'll call dream sponsorship, and I want an essay that takes a side on whether such a market should be permitted.

Under the proposal, an adult participant receives a flat monthly fee — enough to matter, and marketed deliberately at low-income shift workers who are already short of sleep — in exchange for sleeping at home with a device that repeatedly presents a brand-linked sound or scent in the window around sleep onset, aiming to bias the content of her dreams and, downstream, her purchasing dispositions. The consent form describes the procedure plainly and omits nothing the company knows; she may withdraw at any time, though mid-course withdrawal carries a pro-rata repayment; and because she will not remember the nights in question, she can neither observe the intrusion nor afterward assess how it affected her. The company's defense is that the arrangement is a straightforward Pareto improvement: she values the money more than the intrusion, nobody is deceived, and if the market works the price will settle at whatever the intrusion is actually worth to her.

Write an essay that reaches and defends a verdict on whether this transaction should be allowed, and on whether that verdict generalizes to neighboring cases — paid focus groups, product placement, ordinary wage labor, paid trial participation. Weigh the case that dream sponsorship is only an extension of what we already permit against the case that the object being sold is of a different kind: that the transaction manufactures the very preferences whose satisfaction is supposed to justify it, and that a consent given by an awake person cannot cover what is done to a mind that is absent when it happens.

Do not hand me a menu of considerations and leave me to choose; commit to a position and argue for it. If your verdict is conditional, state the conditions precisely enough that one could tell which modified version of this proposal falls inside them and which falls outside. Close by identifying where you think your own argument is most exposed.
