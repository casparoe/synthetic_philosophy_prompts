I've been going down a rabbit hole about the timing of experience and I volunteered to present "my own view" at a reading group next month, before realizing that would actually require having one. The topic is when consciousness happens, and my two friends who are in the group have both told me my package is incoherent, but neither could say where, which is annoying, so I'd like a proper audit instead of a lecture. Here's what I believe, roughly:

(1) I'm with Dennett and Kinsbourne on the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction and I'm a Stalinesque: when the brain revises its story about what happened, it does the revising *before* the experience is had, not by reaching back and overwriting something already experienced. No ghost editing.

(2) I'm also a multiple-drafts person. There's no single place or time where a content gets "presented" to the subject; discriminations happen in parallel, at various places and various times, and I don't believe there's an official draft.

(3) On color phi specifically, though, I want to take the experience at face value. Two lights a few inches apart, one red and one green, flashing alternately; the subject sees a single spot sliding across and changing color, and reports the change somewhere in the middle of the trip, before the green one has even come on. I think that mid-flight change is genuinely in the experience — not a misreport about a later state, and not a confabulation.

(4) I keep leaning on a principle of my own, call it moment-ownership: every bit of phenominal content is owned by exactly one moment. Content can *represent* something temporally extended, but it can't be smeared so thinly across an interval that there's no answer to when it was had.

(5) I deny that people have any special authority over the *timing* of their own experiences. A "when" report is just another discrimination, it can be flat out wrong, and it doesn't get privileged status for being about my own stream.

(6) But I'm not an operationalist. Experience isn't a disposition to report; reports are decent evidence about experience, evidence of something that would obtain whether or not anyone said anything.

(7) And I do think there's a report-independent fact about what was experienced in a given interval — "was the change experienced before or after the green light?" has a determinate answer even if nobody can ever get at it.

So, does this hang together? Please go pair by pair and tell me which tensions are real and which only look real. For what it's worth, one friend insists (2) and (3) can't both be held, on the grounds that multiple drafts is supposed to dissolve precisely the question I'm answering in (3); the other says (5) contradicts (1). I don't find either of those convincing, but I could be wrong, and I'd rather you check them than take my word. Rank the genuine ones by severity, say what has to give in each case, what the cheapest revision is and what it costs me, and then name the single change that would clean up the most damage. I'm deliberately not telling you which commitment I'd prefer to keep, because I don't know, and I don't want you shaping the audit around a favorite.
