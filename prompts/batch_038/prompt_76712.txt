I found it genuinely gripping, which surprises me, and my reading group has been arguing about it for two weeks. One friend keeps saying Rousseau is just the first attention-economy critic. So: restate his case against the theater in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can manage. Take his moves one at a time — the rejection of catharsis (the theater doesn't purge the passions, it feeds and rehearss them); the claim that plays only land by flattering sentiments the audience already holds rather than correcting any of them; the point about weeping at the spectacle, where being moved in your seat substitutes for doing anything and gets spent right there in the dark; the attack on the actor's trade as a way of making a living by publicly selling your own person and depending on strangers' applause; the luxury-and-inequality argument about who actually pays for a playhouse in a small republic; and the festival alternative at the end, where the crowd is its own spectacle and there's no line between performer and audience. Then the part I actually care about: the residue report.
