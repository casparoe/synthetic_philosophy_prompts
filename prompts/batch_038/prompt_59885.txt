i TA an undergrad game theory section and last week we did extensive-form games with perfect information, and honestly the room glazed over right at the unraveling argument — they could follow each step and still not feel why the conclusion is so gross — so for the last day i want to hand out a poem instead of another worked example. You're going to have to be two people at once for this: a poet, and somebody who knows the theory cold, because if the game is wrong the poem is worthless to me and the econ majors in the back row will absolutely tell me so. The setup is the centipede game. Two players, a pot that grows every time somebody passes, a fixed finite stretch of decision nodes, and at each node the player to move gets more by taking right now than they'd get by passing and letting the other one take at the very next node — while both of them do best if the thing is passed all the way to the end. i don't need exact payoffs, just get that ordering right, since the whole paradox lives in it. And please no jargon: no subgame perfect, no Nash, don't even say "backward induction" out loud. The mechanism should be in the poem's bones rather than its vocabulary.

Form is where i want you to think carefully, because i want the shape to be the argument. Six stanzas, one per node, labelled by node counting down (node six first, node one last), and the stanzas grow as you travel backwards: node six gets a single line (nothing to reason about, just take), node five gets two, and so on up to six lines at node one — the idea being that every step backwards adds exactly one more thing you have to believe about the other player, so the opening node ends up carrying the entire chain on its back. Then chain them literally: the last line of each stanza should become, or be visibly answered by, the first line of the next, so the reader can feel the induction passing its own conclusion along like the pot. After the six-line stanza, one extra line, set apart, outside the count — the hand that passes anyway.

The part i actually care about is the wrinkle underneath. The clean argument assumes at every node that the other player is rational, and that you know it, and that they know you know it, and so on. But the only way you ever end up standing at node four is if somebody already did something the argument says a rational player would never do — so by the time the reasoning is needed, the premise it rests on has been contradicted by the sheer fact of arriving there, and you're left having to guess what kind of person you're actually playing against. I want at least one of the middle stanzas spoken from inside that break: a node that, according to the argument, shouldn't exist, where "you're rational" is no longer available as a premise. Keep the voice in second person throughout, one player addressing the other, the you who was supposed to be rational. Before you write, sketch the plan in a handful of lines — what each stanza is assuming, where the seam goes, what the orphan line at the end is doing — then give me the poem. Dont explain the game afterwards, I'll do that in class.
