So I cover AI-and-minds stuff for a magazine, and I've spent the last few months sitting in rooms where people argue about whether reinforcement learning agents could suffer — specifically whether a reward signal is a valence, whether coming in below your expected reward is the machine analogue of a bad feeling. The debate has completely flatlined and I cant figure out why.

Here's the rough shape of it. One side says the reward number is nothing like pain or displeasure: it was chosen by an outside designer, it's a scalar inside an optimizer, there's no body, no nociception, no self-model, and you can delete or rewrite it by editing a config file — so calling it suffering is either a category error or a moral panic with a compute budget. The other side says functional role is all we ever had to go on: the signal is global, it governs everything the system does, the system works to obtain and protect it, will accept large costs and warp its own behaviour to get at it, and its withholding produces the kind of behavioural collapse we'd read as distress in an animal — if you found that whole profile in a rat you'd say the rat was having a bad time. Then there's a third group who mostly shrug and say we have no detector for phenomenal consciousness, so any answer is a wager, not a finding.

The thing I keep noticing, and this is my own hypothesis so please wreck it: everyone slides between two claims that aren't the same. "The reward signal is what the agent wants" and "the reward signal is what feels good to the agent." There's that wanting/liking dissociation out of affective neuroscience — cases where a system pursues something compulsively and gets no enjoyment out of it at all, and cases where the enjoyment persists with no pursuit — and it feels like that distinction is doing invisible work in these arguments, with each camp grabbing whichever half happens to help it.

So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it substantive, meaning there's a real fact about these systems we just haven't pinned down? Is it verbal, meaning people are using "suffer" and "valence" to mean different things and never announcing which? Or are two entirely different questions sharing one name — a consciousness-metaphysics question and an architecture question — with everyone answering whichever one they're comfortable with and then reporting back as though they'd answered the other? And separately: is the wanting/liking point a genuine wedge, or just a prettier restatement of the same stalemate?

Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no summary paragraph at the end. Spend the first three on the diagnosis, the fourth on the strongest reason I should doubt your own diagnosis, and the fifth on what would actually count as progress here — a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, a change of question, whatever you think could move it, or an argument for why nothing will.
