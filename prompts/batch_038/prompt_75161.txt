I want a designed case from the units-of-selection debate, built to the specification below.

The purpose first, since it fixes what counts as cheating. I am running a seminar in which I want participants to see whether the question "at which level is selection acting?" is empirically load-bearing or a matter of pure redescription — and, if the latter, exactly what would have to be true for the redescription to go through. A scenario that stipulates that group selection is occurring, or that settles the matter by a definitional ruling about what counts as a group, fails; the verdicts must be generated by the biology of the case.

The setup. Bacteria growing in a biofilm composed of discrete patches. Producer cells secrete a costly extracellular enzyme that digests a resource accessible only outside the cell; non-producer cells free-ride. The patches must be genuine interacting entities with their own growth and demise, not arbitrary partitions of a well-mixed culture. Two arms, identical in every respect but the life cycle. In the *dissolve* arm, patches break up at the end of each growth cycle and cells are randomly redistributed to found new patches. In the *bud* arm, patches instead bud off daughter patches, whose founding composition is inherited from the parent. Constraint: the within-patch reproductive advantage of non-producers must be numerically identical in both arms, and neither arm may depend on relatedness coefficients, inclusive-fitness accounting, or any assortment mechanism other than the life cycle itself.

Deliverables. (a) The scenario in a single paragraph. (b) For each arm, the verdict and the mechanism on three accounts — the gene as replicator, the individual cell whose reproductive output is measured, and the patch as an entity with births and deaths of its own — set out so that agreements and divergences are visible at a glance. (c) The one feature of the case doing the discriminating work, and what happens if it is removed: does the case collapse into an ordinary free-rider problem? (d) The gene's-eye theorist's attack, in its strongest form, and a judgment on whether it shows the patch-level description to be dispensable or merely redundant.

If the two arms turn out to yield the same outcome, say so plainly rather than adjusting the numbers until they differ. No citations, no named empirical studies.
