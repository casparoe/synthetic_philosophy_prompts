I work nights in a warehouse and I basically can't sleep, so around 4am I've been reading about dreams and I've talked myself into something I can't get out of. No philosophy background at all, so sorry if I've mashed together ideas that aren't meant to go together.

It started with that guy — Malcolm, I think? — who says it doesn't really make sense to talk about what a sleeping person is experiencing. Then it went somewhere way stronger than I meant it to.

1. If something is an experience, there has to be a time when it's being had, and at that time the person is in a position to notice it. Otherwise "having it" isn't doing any work.
2. You can't notice anything while you're asleep. Any attempt to check on yourself in the middle of a dream wakes you up, and once you're awake you aren't dreaming anymore.
3. So there's no moment at which a dream is being had.
4. Everything we ever call a dream is settled by what the person says afterwards. There's no other evidence and nothing independent the report could be right or wrong about, so the report isn't describing the dream, it just fixes what the dream was.
5. So a dream is a bit of waking storytelling, not an experience. Dreams aren't experiences.
6. But run the same move on anything I only know through a later telling: my drive home last night, which I remember as one blur and not second by second, was also just a blur of storytelling, and there was no moment when I was having it.
7. That's obviously false — I was definately awake and having experiences on that drive.
8. So the argument has to be bad, and yet every single step still looks fine to me.

Where's the leak? Which numbered step would you throw out, and what do you lose by throwing it out — I don't want to give up the whole point about how thin the evidence for dreams is, so if I have to pick I'd rather hold on to 4 than to 6. Is there a straight-up mistake in there, some place where I'm sliding from "all we can ever go on" to "all there is," or is the thing actually valid and I just have to choose the least expensive premise to drop?

Then price the alternatives for me. What does it cost the people who think 4 is right and dreams aren't experiences? What does it cost someone like Dennett, who I gather also says dreams aren't experiences but for the other reason — that the story gets made up afterwards, or at the moment of waking? And what does it cost the people who insist there's genuine dream phenomenology and that 2 is just false?

Last bit: there were supposed to be experiments where lucid dreamers made pre-agreed eye signals while the machines still said they were asleep, and others where the length of an eye-movement burst matched how long the dreamed scene was reported to take. Say I've got those half right — do they actually touch 2, or can somebody who holds 4 just swallow them whole?
