I did 35 years in structural biology, mostly X-ray work on enzymes, and only got into philosophy after retiring, so pardon the blunt way I put things.

Dennett's crane/skyhook pair has me going in circles and I want a real comparison, not a recap. Set it against the weak versus strong emergence distinction. My hunch is the two circle the same worry from opposite ends — emergence asks whether the higher level has leftovers the lower level can't account for, skyhooks ask whether the higher level is doing lifting it never earned — but they carve things up differently and I can't say how. Where exactly do they come apart? If you can, give me something that counts as a skyhook but not as strong emergence, and something thats strong emergence without being a skyhook.

Second thing, and its my own pet idea, so be rough with it. In the lab we never derived anything all the way down. We ran on fitted macroscopic laws that outperformed any bottom-up calculation, with everybody knowing the derivation was still outstanding. I'd call those IOUs — promissory notes on a crane nobody has built yet. My suspicion is the crane/skyhook binary has no slot for an IOU, so anything not-yet-reduced gets lumped in rhetorically with the mind-first stuff and the special sciences come out looking guilty by default. Is the IOU a genuine third category, or does it collapse into cranes-under-construction the moment you allow cranes can be unfinished? And does the answer turn on whether crane/skyhook is a claim about the world or a claim about our explanations? Because if its the second one, I can't see how greedy reductionism is a metaphysical sin at all — more like bad taste in exposition.

Take a side on that last bit, don't hedge.
