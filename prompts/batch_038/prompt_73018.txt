I run the sports science side of a pro rugby club — GPS load, session-RPE, monotony and strain indices, wellness questionnaires, the acwr and all the arguments about whether it means anything. My philosophy reading is roughly zero, but I've been chewing on the work-and-leisure debate and I think I can translate it into what I actually use, and I want you to check whether the translation holds or is cheating.

Three positions I want rendered in my vocabulary:

1. Weber's protestant ethic argument — work as a calling, idleness as sin, the ascetic who can't enjoy the fruits of his own labour.
2. France's 35-hour week and the RTT days — the case that capping hours spreads work around, and the critics who say hours didn't really fall because intensity went up instead.
3. Karoshi, and the Japanese practice of drawing a bright overtime-hours line above which a heart attack gets counted as caused by work.

My current guesses, which I'd rather you attack than flatter:

- The Weberian ascetic is an athlete on chronic high load with no deload week, and the question of whether he's one of the elect works like a wellness questionnaire that can never be validated against outcome, because the outcome only lands at the end.
- The 35-hour week is a legislated cap on acute load, and whether it reduces strain depends entirely on monotony: same strain index, fewer hours, higher intensity.
- Karoshi is the danger zone in a population with no recovery monitoring and, crucially, nobody who owns the load decision the way a coach does.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want:

- Which of these map cleanly, which only map if I quietly strech a term, and which have no slot at all in load vocabulary.
- My framework scores cost, and it scores adaptation toward a performance goal that's assumed to be worth having. Is there an honest place in it for the claim that work is meaningful, or that leisure is good in itself rather than just restoration? If you have to write meaning into the load column to make it fit, say that Im smuggling.
- My own tools are contested — the ratio gets hit for being a ratio of correlated variables and for the sweet spot failing to replicate across sports. So split the failures: the ones caused by my framework being shaky versus the ones caused by it being the wrong kind of framework entirely.
- And the uncomfortable one: my readiness scores and Weber's signs of election might be the same animal, both self-report constructs that predict nothing. If the residue runs in that direction, say so instead of sparing my feelings.

End with a verdict: is the work/leisure debate mostly a disagreement about load management that my vocabulary already handles, or is there something in it that a load model can't express even in principle?
