we've got a philosophy-of-art reading group finishing up next week and i've talked myself into owing them a poem for the last night. the argument we keep circling back to is between dickie's institutional story — something becomes art when someone acting for the artworld confers on it the status of candidate for appreciation — and levinson's historical one, where something is art if it's seriously intended for regard in whatever way prior artworks were correctly regarded. for what its worth i've come round to thinking they're making the same move under different names: nothing about the object changes, only where it sits relative to what came before.

so, a pantoum: four quatrains, then the closing stanza that pulls the opening stanza's first and third lines back as its own second and fourth. the constraint that should do the philosophical work is that every repeated line comes back word for word identical and changes status purely from its neighbours — the way the same thing is a plumbing fixture in one stanza and a work in the next with no new property added to it. i want at least one line that starts out flatly describing something completely ordinary (a urinal in a shipping crate, stacked supermarket cartons, an unmade bed) and returns later carrying all the weight of being a artwork, unchanged, so the reader can feel that the difference is nowhere in the line itself.

please don't explain the theories inside the poem, and don't lean on artworld, institution, or theory as an end-word — it should never sound like a glossary. and i want the ending to leave the regress hanging rather than resolve it: if every work's standing comes from a prior one, how did the chain ever get going the first time. the final stanza should make you notice that question and refuse to answer it.

someone who's read danto on indiscernibles should be able to point at which stanza turns and say why the last line had to be a repeat; someone who's never heard of any of them should just get a good, slightly creepy poem about objects being looked at. keep it under about 24 lines and don't rhyme it into jingle.
