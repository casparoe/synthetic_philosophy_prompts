ok so I'm 17 and I co-run a philosophy discord, about 40 people, mostly students, and for basically the entire school year one argument has been spinning in place and I want to know why.

It's the Cartesian theater / multiple drafts fight. One camp says there's no place or moment in the brain where everything comes together and gets shown to a viewer — content is conscious in virtue of having effects, being famous in the brain rather than being presented anywhere. Their showpiece is the color phi thing: flash a red light and then a green one close together and people see a single dot moving across and changing color partway, and if you ask when the color change became conscious you have to pick between two stories — the brain resolved it before presenting anything, or the subject genuinely saw one color and the memory got edited afterward. And then the kicker, which Dennett himself points out, is that those two stories agree on every neural fact and on everything the subject could ever report or discriminate, so asking which one is true supposedly asks nothing at all.

The other camp says that's exactly the dodge. Strawson's version in that NYR Daily piece was roughly that these people are denying consciousness exists; other people say you can explain the reporting and the discrimination all you want but you've skipped the part where there's something its like; the Block-flavored people say fine, maybe access has no finish line, but phenomenal consciousness is the residue the whole argument was about to begin with. And then the global workspace folks have an actual broadcasting event — a sudden ignition across frontoparietal cortex — and Baars insists it isn't a theater because there's no little self sitting in the seats, and both camps count that as a win for themselves.

Eight months in, nobody has moved an inch, and I've started thinking the debate is broken in a specific way. My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: Cartesian theater is doing two separate jobs. It names an architectural claim (is there a single locus and a single time at which the content gets fixed?) and an epistemic claim (do first-person reports count as evidence, or are they themselves phenomena to be explained?). And the two sides keep sliding between them — when one camp wins on the timing data the other retreats to reports-are-data, and when the reports-are-data move gets attacked they retreat to the architecture — so the argument never actually closes on the same proposition twice.

Is that right? Or is this a real substantive disagreement that's just hard, or two different questions sharing a name, or an ill-posed question, or is there some hidden empirical or formal question underneath that both sides keep circling without naming it? I'd like a specific answer on whether things like the preregistered adversarial collaborations pitting global workspace theory against integrated information theory could ever bear on this, or whether no experiment even in principle could.

Please actually commit to a diagnosis. Tell me which one you think it is and argue for it — I don't want a menu of possible readings with the choice left to me at the end. And if you think my summary of either side is a strawman, say that flatly rather than politely working around it.
