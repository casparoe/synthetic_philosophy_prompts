I am auditing a graduate seminar in the philosophy of time, and this week's session concerned the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. The physics is not the obstacle for me: I am comfortable with Minkowski diagrams and Lorentz boosts, and with the fact that two inertial observers in relative motion disagree about which distant events are simultaneous with any given event, the disagreement growing without bound as the spatial separation grows. What I cannot get a grip on is the metaphysics being layered on top of that geometry. I would like you to work through it as a specialist in the philosophy of physics would — someone who has thought hard about how the premises of this argument are actually defended rather than merely recited. Please set out the argument's premises explicitly before evaluating them, keep the discussion conceptual rather than technical, and do not supply citations, references, or a bibliography; I have the readings and what I lack is an understanding of them. I want an explanation, not a literature survey.

Three connected puzzles, which I would like taken up in this order. First, the argument seems to move from the frame-relativity of simultaneity to the claim that the events in a boosted observer's slice are just as real as the events in mine by way of some premise about what it is for events to be co-real. Please make that premise explicit and tell me whether a presentist could so much as formulate it, or whether articulating it already concedes the game. I have heard the argument described both as a devastating reductio and as flagrantly question-begging, and I cannot see how both descriptions could be accurate. Second, transitivity: several people insisted that the relation of being already real for an observer has to be transitive, and that transitivity is what accumulates the overlapping slices into the whole four-dimensional block. Is that something the argument earns from relativity, or a stipulation about the word real? And what would a view that abandons it look like? My seminar leader mentioned a proposal on which what is already real for an observer is simply what lies in that observer's past light cone — a relation that is observer-dependent but, so far as I can tell, still transitive, and which is supposed to be compatible with relativity without collapsing into eternalism. Please explain what that proposal does and does not buy its defender: whether it escapes the Rietdijk–Putnam argument or merely declines to engage with it, and whether a point-like present of that kind is a substantive thesis about time or a relabeling of the block universe with one point singled out. Third, magnitude. Someone argued that the argument only bites in Andromeda-style setups, where a separation of millions of light-years means that whether an invasion fleet has already set out depends on the walking speed and direction of two passing pedestrians, whereas for two people crossing paths on a sidewalk the mismatch between their slices is a small fraction of a femtosecond and therefore philosophically negligible. That strikes me as confused — if the conclusion really is that all events are equally real, the size of the sliver should not matter — but I could not say why the intuition that magnitude matters feels so compelling, or whether there is any version of the argument in which magnitude genuinely does work.

Please open with a bottom line of no more than four sentences summarizing your answers to the three puzzles, then address them in order under short headings.
