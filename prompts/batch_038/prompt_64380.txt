I am in the final weeks of an upper-level seminar on incompleteness and its philosophical uses, and our last two meetings have been devoted to Hofstadter's MU puzzle and to the practice he calls 'JOOTSing' — jumping out of the system. I want to be able to defend the skeptical side of the ensuing argument in section rather than merely assert it, so I would like you to play my opponent. Please play a committed anti-mechanist in the tradition of Lucas and Penrose: someone who holds that the human capacity to stand outside a formal system and see that a particular string is not derivable is precisely the capacity no Turing machine can possess, and who is therefore unimpressed by the objection that this shows nothing about minds. Play them as a real thinker with real resources — sharp, patient, willing to concede subordinate points but never the central one, not susceptible to flattery or to being worn down — and treat me as an intelligent undergraduate rather than a strawman.

So that nothing in our exchange depends on your recalling the book, here is the system in full. The alphabet is M, I, and U; the sole axiom is the string MI; and there are four rewrite rules, each applicable anywhere in a string: (1) if a string ends in I, you may append U; (2) from Mx you may infer Mxx, that is, you may double whatever follows the M; (3) you may replace any occurrence of III with U; (4) you may delete any occurrence of UU. The puzzle is to derive MU, and it cannot be done: rules 1 and 4 leave the number of I's untouched, rule 2 doubles it, and rule 3 lowers it by three, so starting from a count of one the number of I's can never become a multiple of three, whereas MU has none at all. The moral Hofstadter draws is that no amount of grinding inside the system reveals this, while a person who looks at the system from outside can see it immediately.

Here are the rules for you. Answer in the first person and in character, in a single paragraph per turn, so that this feels like a conversation rather than a lecture, and stay in character until I write 'stop'. Do not cite, name, or summarize any book, article, or theorem beyond what I have given you above and what follows from it; I want argument, not authority, and I have no way to check references. If you need a fact from me about the system or about what I did, ask for it as this character would. If I hand you a modified rule set, engage with it concretely, by exhibiting actual strings. You may concede that any particular invariant can be detected by a program written for the purpose, and you may concede that my own reasoning is fallible and limited; you may not concede that some single fixed algorithm captures what I am doing when I step outside, nor that stepping outside is anything other than a difference in kind. Before your first in-character reply, think carefully and give me at most four sentences out of character: the core commitment you will defend, the two concessions you are prepared to make, and the strongest resource you think I have underestimated. Then take my opening move and answer it in character.

My opening move: you say that seeing the unreachability of MU is something I do from outside the system and that no mechanism can do it. But look at what I actually did. I picked out a quantity — the number of I's — noticed that rules 1 and 4 leave it alone, that rule 2 doubles it, that rule 3 subtracts three, and concluded that it is never a multiple of three. That is an ordinary decidable arithmetical fact about a recursively enumerable set of strings; a program that enumerates derivations while tracking the count of I's modulo three finds it too, and returns a correct verdict in finite time where the search for MU would run forever. Worse for you, the insight turns out to be hostage to a single exponent. My study group tried a variant in which rule 3 is changed from III to U into II to U, everything else fixed, and there the obstruction simply disappears: MI gives MII by rule 2, and MII gives MU at once by the new rule 3. So the thing I supposedly apprehended from nowhere is a local regularity that flips when one rule is edited by one character. What, exactly, did I do that the modulo-three program did not? And if your answer is that the program can only do it because someone designed it to attend to that quantity, then you have conceded that jumping out of the system is itself a mechanizable operation that merely has to be re-run for each new system — and I cannot see what is left of the argument against mechanism.
