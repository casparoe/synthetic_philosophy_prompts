My reading group has been working through Berkeley's Querist — the odd tract of numbered queries he wrote as Bishop of Cloyne in the 1730s, diagnosing Irish poverty as a failure of circulation rather than a shortage of treasure, and lobbying for a national bank to issue paper notes. A friend thinks the theory refutes itself. His argument:

(1) On Berkeley's account, money is not wealth. Wealth is the necessaries and conveniences of life; money is only a ticket that sets industry in motion and directs its produce.

(2) What makes something a ticket is not what it is made of but its being accepted; the material — gold, silver, paper — is indifferent.

(3) So whatever value money has is conferred entirely by the practice of accepting it, and by nothing in the money itself.

(4) A practice confers value only while it lasts, and no practice of acceptance can be guaranteed to last.

(5) Hence at every moment the value of money rests on a practice that might lapse, and no agent can rely on it across time.

(6) But money's whole function, by (1), is to let industry be planned and directed across time, and a token nobody can rely on cannot do that.

(7) So if Berkeley's account is right, money cannot perform the function the account assigns it. The ticket theory predicts the collapse of every monetary system, including the paper-note system Berkeley was proposing.

I don't accept (7): paper money did not collapse wherever it was described this way, and the argument would prove equally that contracts, land titles, and ordinary language cannot function, since all of them rest on practices that might lapse. I'd like to keep (1) and (3) if I can, because the ticket view is the interesting part — it is what makes Berkeley a Berkeleyan rather than a metallist.

Which step should I give up? I want the price, not just the diagnosis. If the ticket view cannot license (3) or (4), what is left of Berkeley's claim that paper serves as well as silver, and why weren't his opponents right all along when they said a note is worth no more than the credibility of the institution standing behind it?
