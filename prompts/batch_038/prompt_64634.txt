I am a third-year PhD student in philosophy writing the central chapter of my dissertation on well-being, and I want to locate my own position by getting a number out of you rather than a survey. The chapter concerns the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: the worry that pleasures are so various — warm water on cold hands, the taste of a peach, a chord resolving, the ease of a well-run stride, relief from a headache — that nothing but the label holds them together. Sidgwick evidently took the worry seriously enough to characterize pleasure in terms of what is desired or preferred, and contemporary theorists have tried to absorb it in various ways: reducing sensory enjoyment to a desire that the sensation continue, relocating pleasure out of sensation entirely and into a pro-attitude toward states of affairs, or simply insisting on a shared felt character.

Let HET be the following claim, which I think has a determinate truth value: there is no property that is simultaneously (i) instantiated by every sensory pleasure, (ii) instantiated by no non-pleasure, and (iii) able to do the axiological work hedonism requires of it — that is, to explain, not merely to mark, why the experience is good for the subject. Please give me your credence that HET is true.

More importantly, give me the derivation. Here is the factorization I have been working with, which you should revise if you think it is wrong or incomplete:

1. Kindhood: that sensory pleasure is a genuinely unified kind rather than a disjunctive or family-resemblance category held together by overlapping similarities.
2. Locus: conditional on unity, that the unifier is intrinsic phenomenal character rather than dispositional, attitudinal, or functional-causal.
3. Grain: conditional on a phenomenal unifier, that it is a determinable — a hedonic tone realized in indefinitely heterogeneous determinate feels — rather than one maximally specific quality recurring throughout.
4. Sufficiency: that a unifier of whichever form satisfies (iii), carrying normative weight rather than tracking it.

My own credence in HET is about 0.25, driven almost entirely by my confidence in term 3: I think the standard introspective premise of the objection — that when you go looking for the common ingredient you find nothing — tacitly assumes a shared determinate, and that the assumption is false. My supervisor is at 0.75, and thinks I have only renamed the problem. Tell me which single term our disagreement most likely lives in, and which term you yourself are least confident about.

One case I would like you to weigh, since it seems to me to put real pressure on term 2. Suppose a subject — through lesion, pharmacology, or simply as a stable natural variant — reliably reports a wide range of sensations as enjoyable, prefers their continuation, and behaves accordingly, but who, being an unusually reliable introspector in every other domain, finds nothing in common among them when she attends to how they feel. Is that a case of pleasure without a phenomenal unifier, which would falsify term 2; or of a subject who cannot attend to a determinable she nonetheless instantiates, which would leave my view intact? Say which reading you find more plausible and assign it a probability, since it bears directly on the combination.

Finally: state the combination explicitly, note any term you think the factorization is missing, and tell me what kind of evidence — introspective, conceptual, or from affective neuroscience — would move your number furthest, and in which direction.
