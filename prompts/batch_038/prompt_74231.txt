ok so I've been chewing on this for a while and I want a second opinion, not a survey.

Background, briefly: Nyāya standardly admits four pramāṇas — perception, inference, comparison, testimony — and the rival schools all want to move that number. Cārvāka drags it down to perception alone, the Buddhists stop at perception and inference, Mīmāṃsā pushes it up to six by adding postulation and non-apprehension. That much is just the usual setup.

Now the weird part. A friend of mine — call her Devika, she's got a Nyāya background but she builds ML systems for a living — insists there are at least five more candidate sources of knowledge running around now that a Naiyāyika can't cleanly absorb into the original four. Here they are, as she lays them out:

1. **Instrument deliverance.** A well-calibrated black box spits out a claim about something nobody has ever observed — say, a folded structure predicted from a sequence, where the prediction doesn't resemble the input at all. There's no person asserting it, so it's not testimony. There's no accessible mark to reason from, so it's not inference. And there's no sense-organ contact with the thing known, so calling it perception looks like a stretch.

2. **Diffuse corpus belief.** You end up holding a claim because it reached you through a long chain of lossy paraphrases of a source that no longer exists and that nobody in the chain read. Nyāya cashes testimony as the statement of a trustworthy person, but here there is no trustworthy person at any link — just drift that happens to be reliable.

3. **Simulated other-mind prediction.** You know what a particular person will do by running them, as it were, inside your own head — not by noting a sign, not by being told.

4. **Skill-flash.** The right answer arrives whole, in the hand or on the tongue, before any retrievable steps. Devika's own prediction is that a Naiyāyika will try to file this under yogic perception, or under memory feeding a tacit inference.

5. **Early error alarm.** A second-order signal that your own reasoning has gone wrong somewhere, firing before you can locate where. Nothing positive is thereby known; you just get knocked out of confidence.

Task: rank these five from most to least resistant to reduction — i.e., most likely at the top to be something a Naiyāyika genuinely can't explain away as perception, inference, comparison, or testimony in disguise. Give me a sentence or two of reasoning per item, and be concrete about *which* absorption move fails and why. Then: which one, if any, would you actually bet on getting admitted as a fifth pramāṇa after a few centuries of argument?

And feel free to push back on the frame. Specifically I want to know whether "irreducible" is even the right test — maybe some of these should be judged by whether they carry the same defeasibility and calibration structure the four classical ones do, in which case the ranking might come out totally different. If you think that's the better axis, give me that ranking too.
