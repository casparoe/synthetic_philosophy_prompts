I run a fortnightly podcast about fights in the history of economics — small audience, pretty nerdy. Next episode's guest is a political economist who thinks the socialist calculation debate gets taught backwards: on her reading Lange's trial-and-error answer to Mises basically worked, Hayek's later knowledge problem is just the Walrasian auctioneer point dressed up in epistemic language, and modern computing has removed whatever bite it had left.

I don't want an episode where she summarises her book at me. Give me a run of questions — ten or so — that start out reasonably fair and get progressively harder for her to field, ending on something close to what would actually change your mind here. Make sure the run presses on:

- the trial and error procedure itself. The planning board posts accounting prices for producer goods and revises them in response to excess supply and excess demand. Where does that excess-demand data come from in practice, and how fast can it arrive relative to how fast tastes and techniques are changing?
- Walras's auctioneer is a fiction; in a real market the adjusting gets done by speculative entrepreneurs taking positions and eating the losses when theyre wrong. What plays that role in her system?
- capital goods. Lange kept real markets for consumer goods and labour but not for capital, and he admitted the static model can't handle investment and risk. Ask her what she does with that concession instead of letting her treat the whole thing as solved.
- the manager's rules — minimise average cost, set output where marginal cost equals price. What if it's just easier to pad your costs or hide spare capacity?
- her claim that Hayek is making the auctioneer argument all over again. I want one question that forces her to say whether the objection is about information being dispersed or about it being tacit, becuase those are different problems and only one of them sounds like something a computer could chew through.
- and at least one that isn't stuck in the 1930s: press her on why the market-socialist experiments that actually ran never ended up looking anything like the Lange system.

For the four or five you reckon she'll try to swerve, add the follow-up you'd fire off if she gives the obvious answer, plus a couple of words on what you expect that obvious answer to be. Don't script her side of it beyond that.
