I am preparing a working-group session on the governance of hazardous discoveries, and I want a quantitative and comparative assessment rather than a survey. The topic is the unilateralist's curse — the pattern, named and modelled by Bostrom, Douglas, and Sandberg in a 2016 paper bearing that title, in which agents who are each individually rational and well-intentioned produce a collectively worse outcome because each is separately able to act. You do not need to recall that paper: the version of the problem I want analysed is fully specified below, and your evaluation should be of my specification, not of the literature.

**The model.** A single discovery can be released or withheld. Release has value +1 (net benefit) with probability 1/2 and value −H (net harm) with probability 1/2; withholding has value 0. There are N = 12 research groups, each able to release unilaterally, and the first release fixes the outcome for everyone. Each group conducts its own assessment, which correctly identifies the sign of the value with probability q = 0.75; assessments are conditionally independent given the true value. Each group is motivated solely by the common good and releases if and only if its own assessment favours release. Use H = 1 for parts (a) and (b)'s first figure-set, and H = 3 thereafter. You may take 0.75^12 ≈ 0.032 and 0.25^12 ≈ 0.

**(a)** Report, to three decimal places, the expected value of the twelve-group arrangement and of the counterfactual in which a single group holds the release decision, both under H = 1. Then give the fraction of the single-group value that the twelve-group arrangement retains.

**(b)** Repeat those two expected values under H = 3. Then diagnose the mechanism in four to six sentences. Your diagnosis must say explicitly why the familiar readings fail here — there is no self-interested motive, no ignored externality, no prisoner's dilemma, and no equilibrium-selection problem — and must instead locate the failure in the effective decision rule: name the aggregation function that twelve independent release decisions amount to, and explain why that function degrades as N grows in exactly the way a majority rule improves. State also whether raising q could rescue the twelve-group arrangement, and whether the accuracy it would require is higher or lower than the accuracy a lone group needs, with a one-line justification.

**(c)** Below are seven candidate remedies. For each, give a score from 0 to 10 for how much of the expected value lost in (b) it would recover under H = 3; sort the seven into tiers A through D (ties permitted); and justify each placement in one sentence. Every justification must be tagged with the parameter the remedy acts on: the aggregation rule, the assessment accuracy q, the payoff asymmetry H, or the effective number of actors N.

1. A principle of conformity: no group releases unless it has positive reason to believe that a large majority of similarly situated groups would endorse release.
2. Mandatory pooling of raw evidence among all twelve groups before any decision, with release governed by the pooled posterior.
3. A single designated release authority, chosen for competence or by lot, with the other eleven groups bound not to release.
4. A release council acting by supermajority: release if and only if at least nine of the twelve groups' assessments favour it.
5. A staggered-disclosure rule: a mandatory waiting period during which groups publish their assessments but not the discovery, so that later decisions are made in light of earlier ones.
6. Investment in assessment quality — adversarial review, replication, red-teaming — leaving each group free to release on its own improved judgement.
7. A bright-line prohibition on release, regardless of any group's assessment.

**(d)** Now suppose the independence assumption fails badly: the twelve groups share methods, training, and institutional culture, so their assessment errors are strongly positively correlated. Re-rank the seven and state which change tier. Explain why correlation is fatal to some of these remedies and merely limiting to others, and identify which one, if any, holds its position.

**(e)** The curse has a mirror image: where release would avert a catastrophe, the same multiplication of independent decisions yields too much unilateral withholding rather than too much unilateral action. Identify which of the seven remedies is most exposed to that reversal; describe the payoff structure under which your top-ranked remedy from (c) would itself become net harmful; and give a single number from 0 to 1 for your confidence that your top-ranked remedy is genuinely top rather than an artefact of this stylisation.

**(f)** In at most 150 words, name the two idealisations in my specification that most distort the analysis, and say how relaxing each would shift the ranking in (c).
