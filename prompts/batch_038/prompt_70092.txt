I'm a novelist. Third draft of a book where the narrator tells her daughter's whole life in the past tense even though the daughter is eight and alive in every scene — the grief is all grammatically finished before it happens. Two people who read it said, kindly-ish, that its Arrival with the aliens taken out. Fair. Which is why I've been rewatching Arrival and, more usefully, rereading Borges on time, because I think Borges is where the actual argument is and the film is somewhere else.

So: read Arrival through Borges's views on time. Not "Borges was a fabulist and Villeneuve made a cerebral sci-fi film, both are poetic" — I mean the arguments, and I mean them seriously enough to say where the film can't hold them.

Here's a distinction I keep circling and I want you to test it on me. Borges's refutation-of-time material (I may be mangling which essay is which — there's the one that takes apart the sentence about the moon rising over the river, and the postscript where he ends up saying something like the river that carries him away is him) doesn't read to me as an argument about how time *feels*. It's an argument about grammar and identity: succession needs something that persists in order to be the succession of, and he doesn't think there is any such thing, so time goes with it. Idealism does the heavy lifting. Arrival looks like it runs the other way — the heptapods don't have different physics, they have different orthograpy. Their writing is composed all at once rather than stroke after stroke, and Louise rewires her own head by learning to read it. Sapir-Whorf in a spacesuit. Cognition first, metaphysics afterward.

Things I'd like the reading to settle, or at least take a real swing at:

- Would Borges recognize the film's position as his own? Or have Chiang and Villeneuve psychologized an argument that was supposed to be about the world rather than about what's happening inside somebody's skull — and if so, does that make the film's version weaker, or just differently located?

- Funes. His problem is that perfect, simultaneous apprehension of detail destroys the capacity to abstract, and abstraction is what thinking is; he's brilliant and effectively paralysed. Louise is meant to get the opposite: total simultaneity buys her comprehension and agency. Is that a genuine disagreement with Borges or is the film simply not looking?

- The Secret Miracle. There the private year happens inside a moment the world's clock says didn't last; inner and outer duration are held apart and the miracle depends on the gap. Arrival collapses the gap — Louise's flash-forwards are shot exactly like memories, no marker, no seam. Which picture is more coherent, and does the film know which one it's picked?

- The invented language in the Tlön story, the one with no nouns at all that gets by on impersonal verbs, is Borges's thought experiment about what a self-less metaphysics does to speech. Heptapod B is nearly the same experiment run on writing instead of grammar, and it lands somewhere much warmer. Say why — and whether the warmth is earned or just scored.

- The ending. Louise chooses to have Hannah knowing how it goes. That's usually sold as a free-will point, but on Borges's premises there's no persistent chooser to do the choosing; what's left is a lyric identification with the process, not a decision. So is the film compatibilist, fatalist, or something Borges wouldn't have a name for? And does it cheat — does it let Louise keep both the embrace and the deliberation?

Be honest where the film resists the reading. I don't want a Borges-shaped cookie cutter pressed onto Villeneuve; if there are places where the film is doing something the essays get no grip on — the countdown structure, the misdirection about the "weapon," the fact that a film can hide tense in ways prose can't — say so and use it, don't sand it off.

And finish by telling me which of the two I'm writing badly. If my narrator's device fails, I need to know whether it's failing the way Arrival fails or the way Borges would fail it, because those are two completely different revisions and I have about six weeks before my editor asks.
