second-year PhD student in a consciousness lab here, and I've been stuck with writing the protocol for how our RAs handle subjects' verbal reports. We run masked-presentation and rivalry tasks: subjects describe what they saw, and we also collect forced-choice and eye-tracking data that sometimes flatly contradicts the report. My PI wants the whole thing framed as heterophenomenology — the reports are the primary data, and we suspend judgment about whether the inner episodes they describe are real in the sense of actually having occurred. But nobody has ever operationalized that, so in practice RAs do the opposite and type "confabulation" into the spreadsheet any time a report doesn't match the stimulus.

Can you write me a procedure an RA with no philosophy training could actually run? About 40 reports a session, two minutes each, anything unresolved gets flagged and comes to me. I need the questions to ask of a report in order, what counts as settling each one, when a report gets logged as data-about-a-report versus data-about-a-percept, what to do with reports of experiences the subject insists are indescribable, and where the neutral stance genuinely runs out and the RA is making a judgment heterophenomenology doesn't license.

Then run it on three cases: (1) a fluent, confident report of a face in a masked trial where forced choice is at chance; (2) two subjects in identical stimulus conditions giving incompatible reports, where we have independent reason to think one of them is more accurate; (3) a subject who says "I saw the word, but I'm certain I didn't see anything." At least one should land somewhere my PI will hate, and say which. Finally, tell me which steps are just philosophy smuggled in as bookkeeping, and what the procedure can't do at all.
