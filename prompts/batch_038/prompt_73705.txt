Every time I read something about whether my own choices actually matter — flying, beef, whether to bother voting — it picks one case, gives one answer, and I dont trust it. So before I read another take I want to know what I should even be demanding of an account here. Write the spec: what any theory of when an individual act is wrong because of what it contributes to a collective harm has to deliver. For each requirement, give the case or the worry behind it, and mark whether it's non-negotiable or just something one camp happens to care about.

Things I'd expect on the list, feel free to add or throw out: it has to handle the act that makes no difference because the same thing would have happened anyway (the extra steak at the buffet that was going in the bin regardless); it has to handle the pile-up where no single act crosses any threshold but a hundred thousand of them do; it cant just collapse "made no difference to anyone" into "did nothing wrong", or run it the other way; it has to give a sensible verdict on voting, where the chance of being decisive isn't tiny in the same sense as in the buffet case; it has to be usable by a normal person who can't see the harm, doesn't know where the thresholds are, and isn't going to run expected-value math on a burger; and it should explain why the same amount of harm feels wierdly okay when I do it as part of a group or system I belong to.

Then: which single requirement kills off the most of the answers people actually give, and is any subset of these jointly unsatisfiable? I half suspect "usable by an ordinary person with limited information" and "tracks actual causal contribution" can't both be had, and if that's true then the list is itself the result, which is fine by me.

Purpose, since adequacy depends on it: I want a theory good enough to organize my own decisions with, not a metaphysically elegant one. A line at the end on how the main contenders score is welcome but it's not the point.

And push back if you want to. If some of what I listed isn't a real requirement, or if writing the spec first is just a way of avoiding actually having a view, say that plainly instead of playing along.
