I work on interpretability-for-safety and I've spent about a year in a working group that was supposed to figure out how you'd detect a misaligned mechanism in a new model using detectors trained on other models. It hasn't gone anywhere and I want a diagnosis of the argument, not a survey of it.

Roughly the two camps. One says mechanisms recur: trained transformers converge on the same solutions the way animals converge on eyes, induction heads turn up in wildly different models, and they'd bet that whatever implements situational awareness or deceptive reporting will look recognisably similar from model to model, so a detector learned on one frontier model should transfer to the next one with some degradation. The other camp says the notion is confused. A circuit is only a circuit relative to a dictionary you chose, an attribution method, a threshold and an abstraction level; shift any of those and the 'same' mechanism becomes a different set of features in different places, and since features live in superposition and the encoder basis isn't unique, there's no principled reason to call two blobs that match under ablation in two models the same thing. They also point out that universality claims never get falsified, only re-described: when the detector fails to transfer, the first camp says what it found was an analogue rather than the mechanism proper.

The concrete fight, because I think it matters: last spring two people in the group looked at sibling models from one family. One got a 40 odd feature, three-layer story out of a sparse autoencoder and an attribution patching run; the other got a story overlapping on maybe a third of those features, in different layers, from a different method, with a broadly similar ablation profile. First person said same mechanism, second said different mechanism, nobody budged, and I honestly can't tell whether they'd even disagree about what the world is like if you showed them everything.

My hypothesis, which I want pushed on hard: its not a disagreement about neural networks at all. It's a fight about criteria of identity — whether 'the same mechanism' means the same algorithm, the same causal role in the graph, the same effect on behaviour, or the same weights up to some equivalence the second camp thinks doesn't exist — with a chunk of plain verbal dispute about the word 'same' bolted on. And maybe there's a hidden empirical question underneath about how much of model space convergent training actually occupies.

So is that right, or is it something else — bedrock intuitions about how natural an analyst's abstraction is, two different projects sharing one heading, a question that's just ill posed? Diagnose it with reference to what each side is actually committing to when they say what they say, and then tell me what would count as progress: a definition, an experiment, a formal result about when two subgraphs are isomorphic modulo a change of basis, a change of question, or a reason to think no progress is available and we should spend the year on something else.

Please answer in British English, and write it as continuous prose, the way you would in an email or a letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
