I hold a fairly specific view about constitutional precommitment and I would like it stress-tested rather than explicated back to me.

The view: a legal constraint enacted by a past majority counts as genuine self-binding by the present people — rather than as an alien shackle, or as a rule that merely happens to be costly to remove — when three conditions are jointly met. (i) Cold-blood: it was adopted under conditions in which the passions it exists to control were not in play. (ii) Reversal: its subject matter is one where the characteristic failure mode is preference reversal under pressure, not a change of mind about the facts. (iii) Asymmetric release: it can be undone only by a procedure more demanding than the one that enacted it. Where any condition fails, I concede that the Jeffersonian objection applies at full strength — the earth belongs to the living in usufruct, and on his mortality-table arithmetic no generation can bind the next for longer than roughly nineteen years.

What would count against this? Please give me:

1. Defeaters ranked from fatal through serious to merely awkward, with a sentence on what would have to be true for each to bite, and on whether it is live or only logically possible.
2. Which of them a committed defender of the view would herself concede as counting against it, and which she would dismiss as question-begging — and whether those dismissals are principled or merely defensive.
3. Any case, actual or describable, that discriminates between my view and the deflationary alternative on which entrenched rules persist only where compliance is cheap and are nominal precisely where they are costly. Say what the two positions predict differently.
4. An honest verdict on whether conditions (i)–(iii) have insulated the position into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that is a problem for a view of this kind. Condition (iii) looks especially exposed to me: the release procedure is itself either entrenched or not, which threatens a regress.
5. Where the two standard models of self-binding break down. In the individual case — the addict, the psychiatric advance directive signed while competent — the arrangement seems to require enforcers who have not themselves been bound. Tell me whether my collective version inherits that problem or escapes it, and what the role of the crew implies for condition (i).

Open with a three-sentence bottom line on whether the view risks anything at all, then proceed through the five items. Maximum 800 words. Name positions and arguments rather than sources; I will supply citations myself.
