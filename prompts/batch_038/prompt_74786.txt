I am auditing a philosophy-of-biology seminar, and last week our discussion of a paper on the evolution of a signaling trait was interrupted by a visiting student who said, roughly, that the entire explanatory strategy was empty because "survival of the fittest" is a tautology: whatever happens, the organisms that in fact left the most offspring were, by definition, the fittest, so the theory cannot come out wrong. When I objected that fitness is estimated independently — from morphology, physiology, and environmental conditions — before reproductive outcomes are known, he replied that this only shows the interesting content lives in those auxiliary estimates rather than in the selection principle itself, and added that Popper made precisely this charge against Darwinism and later withdrew it, which he took to prove that even Popper could not state the charge coherently.

I do not want a defense or a rebuttal. I want a precise inventory. Please lay out, as a numbered menu ordered roughly from the trivial to the radical, the distinct theses this single slogan can be used to assert. Axes worth considering, though you should add any I have missed: whether "fittest" is read as defined by actual reproductive output or as an independently characterized dispositional property; whether the target is the bare sentence or the whole theoretical structure (variation, heritability, differential reproduction, plus the population-level mathematics); whether the complaint concerns analyticity, falsifiability, the absence of genuine laws, or explanatory circularity in particular applications; and whether it is aimed at the theory as such or at a style of after-the-fact adaptationist storytelling.

For each thesis on the menu, tell me: what exactly it claims, whether it is true, whether it would matter if it were true, and — the point that interests me most — whether it would license the dismissal in my seminar, that is, whether it would show that the paper's account of that trait explains nothing. Where two of these theses are routinely run together under one sentence, identify the pairing and say which member of it is doing the damage.

Finally, take up the Popper episode so far as you can without being able to look anything up. Two questions: which of my candidate charges would his own demarcation criterion even register, given that a statement can be analytic and yet sit inside an empirically testable framework whose derived predictions are risky; and whether the claim that he both advanced and retracted the tautology charge is something you are confident of, unsure of, or think I have garbled. I have no library access, so please mark your confidence on anything historical instead of filling in details you cannot stand behind. No bibliography, no reading list, and no invented quotations — analysis only.
