ok, slightly weird request but I need a break from chapter 3 and I think this would actually be useful for a seminar I'm TAing on 19c aesthetics.

Write a satirical funding proposal — a formal submission to a city arts council, about 650 words — from a self-styled "Society for the Denial of the Will" that has read Schopenhauer on music very closely and very literally and now wants public money to phase music out of the city's cultural life. The genre should be recognisable grant-speak: a project summary, an itemised set of measures, an impact statement, one budget line that's quietly absurd, and a short closing paragraph asking for the renewal next year.

The spine of the joke has to be the doctrine, not the mood. So the proposal should:

- treat music as a *problem* in a way that only makes sense if you've actually got the argument — music as a copy of the will itself rather than of the Ideas, which is why it's the one art that gets under the skin, and why the will-less contemplation that a landscape painting buys you doesn't transfer to a symphony. The Society should notice this and be appalled rather than triumphant, and should be annoyed that everyone else takes the fact that music moves you as a selling point.
- roll out the hierarchy of the voices as actual policy: melody as the highest grade of objectification (the intellect and striving of the individual), the middle voices as plant and animal life, the bass as crude inorganic mass — and therefore propose a phased abolition that starts at the top of that hierarchy and keeps the bass playing longest, with the reasons drawn from it, including whatever the Society makes of the bass having to move in slow, wide steps and not being allowed to become melodic.
- include at least one measure about dissonance and consonance understood as ungratified and gratified willing — a quota, a curfew, a ban, something bureaucratic — justified in exactly those terms and not in terms of taste or public decency.
- ban program notes and anything descriptive or imitative on the grounds that music isn't about particulars at all, and give opera its own special clause about the words making a servant of the music, without getting bogged down in which composers he liked or loathed.

Tone: deadpan throughout, no winking, no narrator aside letting us know it's silly, and the council's forms and headings should be obeyed straight. Please also no cheap shots at the biography — no poodles, no mother, no Hegel-baiting. I want a specialist to laugh at an argument being followed off a cliff, not at a grumpy German.

Internal consistency is the thing I'll be grading, so to speak: the proposal has to be airtight right up to the point where it isnt, and the failure should be forced by its own logic rather than by a slip. Specifically I'd like the Society to realise near the end that its surviving bass line is still a copy of the will, and to try to patch that in a way that makes the position worse and not better — and then to land on a final measure (something about silence, or about thier remaining subscribers) that they cannot justify without admitting they still want the very thing they're abolishing. End dryly, mid-bureaucracy, on a request for a modest increase in next year's allocation. Don't resolve it.

No direct quotations from Schopenhauer, no section numbers, no page cites — I want the ideas in the Society's own words, which is what I'd want my students to produce anyway.
