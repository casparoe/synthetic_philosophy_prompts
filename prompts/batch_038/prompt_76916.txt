I'm drafting a position paper for a philosophy of medicine seminar and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is the package I intend to defend:

(a) Biological functions are selected effects, in the sense of Wright and Millikan: what a trait functionally does is what it was retained by selection for doing.

(b) It follows that the sounds a heart makes are not among the heart's functions, however reliably it produces them and however useful they are at the bedside.

(c) Disorder ascriptions are hybrid, on Wakefield's model: a factual component consisting in an internal mechanism's failure to perform the function it was naturally selected to perform, plus a value component supplying the harm.

(d) That factual component must be value-free and settled by biology alone, with no reference to anyone's conception of a good life.

(e) Even so, I need Boorse's apparatus: a notion of functional efficiency measured against a statistical norm for a reference class, because a trait can possess a selected function and still be executed better or worse, and pathology comes in degrees.

(f) Dyslexia and comparable learning disorders are genuine disorders, notwithstanding that literacy is a recent cultural acquisition with no evolutionary history behind it.

(g) Function talk should be unified — one analysis covering organisms and artifacts alike, so that a transplanted kidney and the machine temporarily replacing it have functions in the same sense.

Which of these pairs genuinely pull against one another, ranked by severity rather than listed as equally bad? Which appear to conflict but dissolve once examined, and why? For each real conflict, what is the cheapest revision and what does it cost me elsewhere in the package? Close with the single change that would resolve the most. Please answer as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no preamble and no concluding paragraph.
