I'm not an academic and I have no way to look anything up, so I'd like you to reason this through with me rather than point me toward sources. I've been working on the colour-phi case on my own and I've hit a wall.

First, let me set out what I take to be the facts — please correct me if any of it is wrong, because everything I'm asking for depends on it. Two small spots of different colours, green and red say, are flashed one after the other a few degrees apart on a screen, with an interval well under a second between them. People don't report two separate flashes. They report a single spot travelling from the first position to the second and changing colour somewhere along the way. The trouble is that which colour it seems to change into, and roughly where along the path the change seems to happen, depend on information that isn't available until the second spot has already appeared.

There are, as I understand it, two ways to take this. On the first — the Orwellian one — the subject genuinely goes through an experience of the earlier part of the sequence, and that experience is subsequently overwritten: the archive is edited after the fact, so what the subject ends up reporting is not what they lived through. On the second — the Stalinesque one — the editing is finished before the episode becomes an experience at all: the system waits until it knows how things turn out, and the reconciled version, motion plus a mid-flight colour change, is the only version the subject ever consciously has. The verdict is settled before the trial is staged. And the standard line, as I've seen it put, is that these two are indistinguishable: they agree about everything that happens in the head and when, and differ only about whether a given moment falls on the near side or the far side of experience, so that subjects "should be unable to tell the difference between misbegotten experiences and immediately misremembered experiences."

My suspicion is that this indistinguishability is not a discovery about consciousness but an artefact of how the case is always presented: one sequence, one subject, one report collected after everything is over. Change when and how the evidence gets produced and the two stories may come apart. I want a case that tests that suspicion.
