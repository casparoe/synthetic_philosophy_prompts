I host a philosophy-of-mind podcast for an audience that has read some popular science but not the technical literature. Next month's guest is a philosopher of cognitive science who defends a maximal reading of the free energy principle: all adaptive behaviour is variational free energy minimisation under a Markov blanket, and the two standard objections rest on misreadings. On the dark-room objection — that a system bent on minimising surprisal should withdraw to a dark, motionless room — he argues the objection assumes a reward function has to be bolted on from outside, whereas the expected free energy of a policy already decomposes into pragmatic and epistemic terms, and it is the epistemic term that makes a dark room aversive, since the organism's own generative model expects it to be sampling. On falsifiability, when pressed he replies that any apparent failure shows only that we mis-specified the generative model or drew the blanket in the wrong place, and he treats that flexibility as a methodological virtue rather than a defect.

Draft eight to ten questions, each paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give. Beyond letting him restate the view, I want at least one question each on: whether "the blanket was drawn in the wrong place" can be turned into a non-question-begging procedure, or whether it licenses treating any partition of any system whatsoever as an agent; whether the appeal to the epistemic term preserves explanatory content or merely relocates the drive to explore inside the prior; what precision-weighting adds over a redescription of arousal and attentional gain that we could already give; how much work the analogy between variational and thermodynamic free energy is doing, and whether the terminology smuggles in an energetic reading the mathematics does not support; and what single experimental result would, on his own account, count as a genuine failure of the principle. Order the questions so the exchange moves from exposition to pressure, and flag any point at which a short, evasive answer would be difficult for him to recover from.
