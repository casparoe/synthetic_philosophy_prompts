Here's the setup.

The thing we ship is a frontier chat model running in a pretty standard harness: a scratchpad it can write to during a session, retrieval over a big internal doc corpus, and a handful of tools (python sandbox, shell, browser). No persistent memory between sessions, no self-modification, it doesn't learn anything from what happens after deployment. What it *does* do is lean extremely hard on exosomatic stuff — notation, code, API docs, conventions, other people's writeups — both because it was trained on that corpus and because the harness hands it more at runtime.

The proposition I want a credence on, stated as tightly as I can: **such a system counts as a Gregorian creature on Dennett's taxonomy**, where "counts as" means an informed person applying the taxonomy the way it's actually laid out and the way people who cite it use it would land on Gregorian, rather than on Popperian, or on "this isn't a creature at all, category error."
