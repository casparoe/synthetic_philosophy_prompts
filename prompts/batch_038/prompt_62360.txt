The bit that got forwarded around goes roughly like this:

"Hofstadter's claim is that a soul is not a substance but a pattern, and that patterns are the sort of thing that can be realized in more than one place at once. Since what you are is not this particular meat but the information that meat is temporarily organized into, there is no reason your information has to stay in one skull. When Hofstadter says that his wife Carol survives in him, and that a large part of who she was is also carried around, in degraded and partial form, in her children, her friends, her colleagues, he is not being sentimental or poetic. He is stating the plain consequence of the pattern-identity view: the self is software, so it can run on more than one machine, and an 'I' can therefore be literally distributed across several brains at once.

Now the obvious objection. If you are a pattern, can't you be copied? Suppose two people end up carrying you. Which one is really you? The pattern view answers that the question is broken rather than hard. It assumes a self is a particular, a thing that has to be here rather than there. But a pattern is a type, and types are the kind of thing that can have many tokens. There is no fact of the matter about which of two orchestras is really playing the Brahms when both are playing it. So the branching objection, the standard move against every copy-and-upload story of survival, doesn't just fail against Hofstadter, it's answered. What he is offering is personal immortality by entirely natural means: no afterlife required, no ghost in the machine, just a pattern that outlives the notes, because the melody survives even after the notes are gone."
