I'm in a comparative federalism seminar this semester and my prof spent two whole sessions building an argument that subsidiarity refutes itself, and I walked out of both of them unable to find the crack. Here it is as close to his version as I can reconstruct.

(1) Subsidiarity says a decision belongs at the lowest level of government competent to handle it.
(2) A level is competent to handle a decision only if it can internalize the effects of making it.
(3) In an integrated economy almost every policy produces effects that cross the jurisdiction's border — pollution drifts, firms and taxpayers relocate, capital moves, people shop for benefit rules in the neighboring canton or state.
(4) So almost every policy has effects that the lowest level cannot internalize.
(5) So by (1) and (2) almost every policy belongs one level up, and the same reasoning applies again at that level, and again, until you get to the top.
(6) So subsidiarity, applied honestly, is an argument for centralizing everything. The principle meant to protect the cantons and the Länder turns out to be a slow-motion brief against having them.

He wouldn't say where it breaks, just told us to find it ourselves, and I can't get traction on any of the steps — each one looks fine sitting there on its own. What I do know is that I'm not going to accept (6), and I'm not willing to give up on subsidarity as at least a workable principle of institutional design, so something earlier has to go and I want to know what I'm paying for it.

So: which step is the one to reject, and why did it look innocent when you read it by itself? Then price out the alternatives — for each of three other steps a defender might sacrifice instead, say what they'd have to swallow to do it. One more thing: his running example all semester was cross border river pollution, which he insisted the argument handles beautifully, so tell me whether your fix survives that case or whether it's genuinely a win for him.

Give me exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, with no introduction and no conclusion: item 1 is the step to reject plus the diagnosis of why it slipped through, items 2, 3, and 4 are one alternative sacrifice each with its cost, and item 5 is the river case and the bottom line.
