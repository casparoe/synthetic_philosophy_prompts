So I've been going down this rabbit hole for a few months now, mostly from pop books and whatever I can find, and I hit something I can't get my head around on my own. No academic background here, and my maths basically stopped in high school, so please don't give me equations.

The thing is Gödel's rotating universe. Back in the late forties he came up with a solution to Einstein's equations where the whole cosmos is spinning, it isn't expanding like ours seems to be, and apparently there are paths through it that loop back on themselves so you could in principle rocket off and arrive at your own past. And from that he concluded something big about time not being real, or at least about there being no objective lapse of time, reality not actually stacking up new "nows" one after another the way it feels like it does.

Here's the step I can't follow, and it's the only thing I really want you to dig into. Our universe doesn't rotate. There's no evidence for that kind of looping path anywhere near us, and I think the observations pretty tightly rule out any big cosmic spin. So how can the existence of some other, merely possible universe show anything about whether time flows in *this* one? My own guess at the answer, which I made up sitting in the car yesterday, is this: if the passing of time were a genuine part of the furniture of the world, it would have to be written into the laws themselves, so that any world obeying those laws would have it — and Gödel found a world that obeys the laws and doesn't have it, so the passing can't be in the laws, it can only be something about how we experience them. Like finding out a recipe can't be what makes the cake sweet. Is that reconstruction actually the argument, or have I turned it into something else?

Two more things are nagging at me. First, does this prove too much? If "the laws permit a world without it" is enough to demote something from objective reality to appearance, couldn't you run the identical move on space, or causation, or objects, and show none of them are real either? What makes time special, if anything does. Second, I get that special relativity already messes with simultaneity — different observers slice "now" differently — but that feels like a merely ambiguous situation where you could still insist there's some true slicing. The looping paths seem worse than that, not just ambiguous but flat-out impossible to order in time at all. Is that distinction the real reason Gödel bothered constructing his spinning universe instead of just pointing at relativity, or is the rotating thing mostly a dramatic illustration of a point that relativity had already made?

And when you get to the end, please be careful about what he's actually denying. I keep seeing three different claims mixed together: that time isn't real at all, that the past and future exist just as much as the present so it's all one block, and that only the *passage* is unreal while the ordering of events stays perfectly objective. Which of those does the rotating universe result actually support, and which ones is it commonly but wrongly credited with?
