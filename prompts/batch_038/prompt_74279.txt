i'm in a reading group working through the hedonist imperative stuff (pearce and co) and one of our regulars — she's an ER nurse, which gives her a lot of standing in the room — has an analogy she leans on basically every single week. she says suffering is like a smoke alarm: the screaming is horrible but the screaming is what gets you out of the burning house, and a silent alarm you have to remember to go check is an alarm nobody checks. so the unpleasantness isn't a crude leftover in the design, it *is* the mechanism. when someone in the group says we could in principle dial down the hedonic set point and keep all the protective function, she says that's like saying you can keep a smoke alarm's protection while making it quiet — congratulations, you've built a smoke alarm that doesn't work. sometimes she swaps in a second version: suffering is like a fever, the misery is the immune system doing its job, and you don't get to keep the recovery without the awful night.

can you audit this properly for me? i'd like the correspondence table written out explicitly — what plays the role of the smoke, the sensor, the siren, the sleeper who has to be woken, the house, the fire brigade, the person who set the volume — and then where the mapping actually fails. the bits i most want you to dig into:

1. whether aversive phenomenal tone really does the same job as the loudness of a siren, or whether the analogy quietly fuses two things that can come apart: detecting damage and disliking it. there are obvious cases where they do come apart, and i want to know how much damage they do to her picture.
2. what the alarm imports from being an engineered safety device — a fixed spec, one occupant, a designer who had to trade off volume against annoyance, a builder who can't be asked to redesign it — that a nervous system shaped by selection, or a whole population of them, simply doesn't have.
3. whether the fever variant is doing the same work or different work, since a fever is at least partly the body's own doing rather than an installed add-on, and i suspect that difference matters for the argument even though she treats them as interchangeable.
4. whether her conclusion — that any drop below baseline suffering must cost us something protective, so the abolitionist programme is self-defeating rather than merely ambitious — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying that conclusion the whole time and she'd have nothing left without it.

and if it does break, tell me what analogy you'd reach for instead and what questions the better one lets you ask that the smoke-alarm picture can't even express. feel free to say if you think a evolved-systems framing is just the wrong shape of thing to be analogising about at all.
