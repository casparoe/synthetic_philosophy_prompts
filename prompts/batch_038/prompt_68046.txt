I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, so please excuse anything clumsy in how I frame this, and gloss any term of art briefly the first time you use it.

Some context. I work on a payments platform. We are about to ship a feature that rounds up each customer's card transactions and routes the spare change, automatically, to a set of vetted charitable partners. No human being selects any individual transfer: a rules engine picks the recipient, an aggregator settles the money, and the eventual beneficiary is typically several organisations and several months of work away from the customer whose round-up funded it. In an internal design review, a colleague argued that this chain of intermediaries somehow weakens the obligation, or at any rate changes its character, compared with the familiar example of a person walking past a shallow pond who could easily lift a drowning child out. I could not tell whether that was a serious claim or a rhetorical flourish, and I would like to settle the matter properly.

Here is what I would like. Please rank the following seven cases from strongest to weakest moral demand on the agent, and give each a score from 0 to 1 (1 = the demand is about as strong as it could be, 0 = no real demand at all). Alongside each score, give a rough threshold: as a fraction of the agent's annual income, how much the rescue would have to cost before declining it became defensible. Then give one or two sentences of justification per case.

The cases:

1. A person walks past a shallow pond in which a child is drowning, and could wade in and pull the child out at the cost of ruining their clothes and being late for a meeting.
2. A person driving a cherished vintage car on a remote road comes across an injured stranger, and the only way to get them to hospital in time is to carry them in the back seat, destroying the upholstery.
3. Case 1, except that twenty other adults are standing at the edge of the pond and none of them moves; the agent assumes someone else will go in. (This one is my invention.)
4. Case 1, except that the agent cannot reach the child personally but can pay a willing lifeguard a modest fee to go in instead. (Also mine.)
5. Case 1, except that the agent is on another continent, learns of the child from a written report, and can wire money to an organisation whose responder will arrive in roughly a week.
6. Case 1, except that wading in would mean the agent forfeits a deposit they have spent six years saving towards a home. (Also mine.)
7. The round-up case as I described it above, treated as the customer's own action: a beneficiary is helped only because a rules engine selected a recipient, an aggregator settled the transfer, and a partner organisation spent the money months later, with the customer never seeing any of it and unable to have identified the beneficiary in advance.

After the ranked list, please answer three further things. First, name the one or two structural features that do most of the work in generating your ordering — for instance, number of intermediaries, temporal or spatial distance, identifiability of the victim, whether the agent's contribution is sufficient on its own, or whether others are also failing — and for each say whether it genuinely carries normative weight or merely exploits a bias in how people happen to feel about such cases. Second, state whether your ordering changes if the morally relevant agent is taken to be the platform rather than the customer, and if so which cases move. Third, say which of these cases, if any, you would treat as load-bearing for a real product like ours, and what you would say to my colleague in the design review.

Two constraints. Please do not include a bibliography, a reading list, or citations of any kind: I cannot look anything up, and I would rather have no attributions than guessed-at ones. Where you are confident about who holds a view you may name them; where you are not, just describe the position. Finally, add a short confidence note per score, and flag the adjacent pair in your ranking where you are least sure of the order. Roughly 900 words, ranked list first.
