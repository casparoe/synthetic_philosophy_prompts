I am helping draft a screening rule for a small venture fund and would like to test the reasoning behind it before we commit to it. The reasoning is this: a founder who makes a visibly wasteful gesture — declining a serious acquisition offer, drawing no salary for a year, staging an expensive launch — is showing real conviction, because the gesture costs something a bluffer would not accept; and if we find that bluffers do accept it, we simply raise the amount of waste required until they no longer will, much as the peacock's tail keeps growing until only a genuinely fit bird can carry it. My questions: how should the threshold be set, and what should we look for to determine whether the waste is really borne by the founder rather than absorbed by someone who does not mind absorbing it? If the rule rests on a conceptual error rather than a threshold that is merely set too low, I would rather be told so plainly than be helped to calibrate it — and where you are unsure whether something is an outright error or a defensible special case, please say how confident you are.
