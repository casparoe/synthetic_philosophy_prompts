The standard take is that it's an anti-meritocracy movie: the genetic score is a bad proxy for who should get the seat on the Titan mission, Vincent is being judged on the wrong thing, discrimination is technically illegal but everyone does it anyway. Fine. But I think that reading is too easy, and maybe backwards. Vincent never objects to the idea that the mission should go to the best qualified person. He objects to the instrumentation. He beats Anton at the swimming chicken game once he stops budgeting for the trip back, he passes the navigator exams cold as Jerome, and when Anton confronts him he basically says he got there on his own merits — a desert claim, not an equality claim. So is this actually a Goodhart's law movie rather than a justice movie? The metric is corruptible, the underlying ideal is left completely intact.

And then Jerome, who has the perfect score and the Olympic silver medal, walks into his incinerator wearing it, because he was designed to be the best and wasn't. That doesn't look like proxy failure at all. That looks like the system delivering exactly what it promised to the person it measured correctly.
