his version: a regulated kidney market is just paired exchange with cash in the loop. the argument goes — we already run these chains where a total stranger donates to someone they'll never meet, that recipient's willing-but-incompatible donor then gives to another stranger, and it cascades through dozens of people across several states, including "bridge donors" who go home and wait months before paying their part forward. nobody thinks that's repugnant, he says. so we've already accepted kidneys moving between strangers for reasons that have nothing to do with love or family; all a market does is kick the chain off with a price instead of with goodwill. and since a swap is literally payment in kind — apparently congress had to clarify in 2007 that a paired exchange doesnt count as transferring an organ for valuable consideration, because on a plain reading barter is barter — the gap between a swap and a sale is way smaller than everyone pretending otherwise wants to admit. conclusion: if you accept the chains you have no principled ground left to reject the market, and the two objections people actually give (it preys on poor sellers, it degrades the thing itself) are really about regulation and framing, not about markets as such.
