been going around in circles on two-dimensional semantics and I want someone to just tell me flatly where my reading goes wrong.

my understanding: a sentence is metaphysically necessary iff its primary intension comes out true at every scenario. So necessity is basically a priori truth with more moving parts, and Kripke's necessary-a-posteriori category is just an artifact of not having the right intension in hand. On that reading, water is H2O is necessary because its primary intension is true at every scenario — we just cant see it without doing the chemistry first, which is why it feels empirical.

But there are two consequences I cant reconcile. First, if that's right, then materialism (say, that the complete physical truth entails every truth) is either a priori or refutable by pure scenario-construction, and the zombie argument isn't doing anything a decent conceptual analysis wouldn't already do. So why bother with zombies at all? Second, Chalmers keeps insisting water/H2O is a genuine case of the necessary a posteriori, which on my reading his own framework rules out. Is he being inconsistent, or am I?

Smaller thing: I proposed a patch to a friend. Define a third intension — take the primary intension, evaluate it at the actual scenario, then hold that result fixed while you vary the world counterfactually. I figured that recovers Kripkean necessity inside the system without giving up my reading. He said that isn't a third intension at all, it's just the secondary one, and that I'd reinvented the very thing I was denying existed. Is he right, or is there a real gap between what I described and the secondary intension?
