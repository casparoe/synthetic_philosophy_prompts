So I've got this little podcast — nothing fancy, recorded on a laptop in the spare room, maybe three hundred regulars — and I've never studied any philosophy, I'm a plumber by trade basicly, but there's one idea I've been chewing on for a couple of years now and its become my whole thing. Next month my guest is a philosopher who defends moral particularism: roughly, that there aren't any exceptionless moral principles, and that whether a feature of a situation counts as a reason to act depends entirely on the case you're in.

Here's my own version of why the view grabs me, and I'd like one of the questions built out of it. My mate Dave pulls out of my birthday every single year because he's "not feeling great". The first couple of times, that's obviously a reason to go easy on him. By the sixth year running, the exact same sentence out of the exact same mouth stops being a reason for sympathy and starts being a reason to be cross with him. Nothing about the words changed. I get that — but I also worry I'm just dressing up "context matters", which everyone already believes.

Can you write me about ten questions I could actually say out loud? Not "what got you interested in this" rubbish. I want ones that make him work:

- At least one that presses on his go-to examples of reasons flipping sides, and then goes after the comeback I expect, which as I understand it is that he's quietly swapped the description of the consideration — it isn't the very same feature any more. I want the follow-up that stops him retreating into re-describing forever.
- One on the extra machinery, the conditions that aren't reasons themselves but switch reasons on and off, or turn the volume up and down on them. My worry, in plain terms: if you can list those conditions, isn't that just principles with extra steps?
- One on the objection that the moral stuff has to hang off the non-moral stuff in some systematic way, and his likely answer that the "principles" this hands you are so wide they don't say anything. Then the follow-up: so what's actually left of the idea that morality is principled?
- One that asks him straight out whether his position is either empty or just false, depending on how thin or how thick you make the notion of a principle.
- One about teaching my kid, or advising a mate in a mess — what does particularism tell a complete beginner to do, and how does anyone learn if there's nothing to learn?
- One about disagreement: if two of us look at the same case and see different reasons in it, what could possibly settle it? Does his view leave blame and moral argument with nothing to grip?
- One asking whether the same goes for reasons to believe things rather than do things, and whether he'll own that it does.
- One on the stronger claim I've heard him flirt with — that in the right circumstances a temptation isn't outweighed but silenced, doesn't even show up as a reason. Does he need that, or is it a luxury he could drop?

For each question, give me (a) the answer he's most likely to reach for, in a sentence or two of his own voice, and (b) one follow-up keyed to that exact answer that makes it harder to give a second time. Then put them in an order that builds, mark two I could cut if we're running over, and flag anywhere you think he could fairly accuse the question of assuming he's wrong before he's answered.

British English please, and write them the way a person would really say them, because I'll be reading some off the page on air — not like an essay. And no reading list or references, I'd have no way of checking them and I'm not name-dropping anything on the show.
