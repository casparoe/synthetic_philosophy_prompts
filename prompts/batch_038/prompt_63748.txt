The reason I care isn't the seminar though. For the last two years I've been a peer mediator at my university's conflict resolution office: three of us, one-off 50-minute sessions, roughly twenty cases a semester, almost all undergrads — roommates, capstone group blowups, student-org leadership fights. Our training is entirely interests-versus-positions and paraphrasing back what you heard and helping people find a compromise, and it works fine on who buys the paper towels. But there's a whole class of case where it does literally nothing, where both people already know what the other wants, the compromise is obvious, and they still can't take it because of something about standing. I think that something is what the master–slave section is actually about, and I want to build a tool out of it.

So: take the argument of that section — not the pop version where two people fight to the death and one wins — and turn it into a procedure a mediator could actually run in a room with two angry 20-year-olds. I want the questions to ask in order, what a sufficient answer to each looks like, how to tell which structural position each party is occupying (including the one who says she genuinely doesn't care what the other thinks of her and just wants the deliverable, and the one who has quietly ended up holding all the practical knowledge of the thing everyone is fighting over), where the procedure terminates in a workable agreement, and where it has to stop and hand the case up to our staff coordinator because what's in front of you isn't a recognition problem at all. Be explicit about what from Hegel has to survive the translation and what can be dropped without the procedure turning into mush — the life-and-death struggle, the thing as an independent object, work as desire held in check, the reversal, the fact that recognition from someone you don't recognize is worthless to the recognizer. No exegesis for its own sake and please don't quote translations at me; I need the concepts doing work, and I need the version a non-philosophy mediator could use without reading the book.

Then run it on three cases.
