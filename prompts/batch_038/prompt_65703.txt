Background for you, in Dennett's sense: a "skyhook" is an illegitimate explanatory lift — design or mind hoisted up from nowhere, anchored to something above the system rather than inside it — while a "crane" is a legitimate accelerator, a special feature of the process that speeds it up without ever leaving the ground. Greedy reductionism is the mirror-image failure: skipping the cranes and trying to explain the penthouse straight from the basement, no intermediate floors.

Write it as an INSPECTION REPORT from a fictional regulatory body — call it the Bureau of Explanatory Compliance or something like that — on an application to install a load-bearing explanatory device in a philosophy department's new building. The inspector has already walked the site and is not impressed. Include:

- a header block: permit number, applicant, site address, scope of work, inspection date, inspector name
- a checklist of 8–12 line items, each with a code (PASS / FAIL / CONDITIONAL) and one or two flat sentences of findings. Good candidates: a basement tenant attempting to derive a symphony directly from particle interactions with no cranes in between; a third-floor crane whose anchorage appears to be located above the third floor; an applicant note claiming the device "only accelerates" a process that has not yet begun; a sign in the lobby advertising mind-first foundations
- at least one prior-violation entry and at least one complaint filed by an adjoining unit
- the main joke: a neighbor's complaint alleging that the applicant's crane is a skyhook wearing a crane costume (who installed the crane? the manufacturer appears to have been intelligent), and the inspector handling that regress with total procedural boredom, possibly by opening a second file and noting a fee
- a short "required remediation" or "conditions of approval" section
- a final stamped verdict

Voice: flat, procedural, the tone of someone who has denied a hundred of these and cares only whether the thing is anchored to something below it. Nothing winks, no asides to the reader. Invent all the people — no real philosophers as applicants — and don't put verbatim quotes from actual books in anyone's mouth; paraphrase the ideas instead.
