For several years now I have been trying to settle, for my own purposes, a question that surfaces in almost every political argument I get into: whether taxation is theft. I have no academic training in any of this. What I have is a list of particular cases and my own reactions to them, and I have come to suspect that my reactions are not as consistent as I would like. I am not looking for the standard case for either side, and I would rather you did not tell me which thinkers have said what; I want to know what my verdicts actually commit me to.

Here they are.

1. A stranger takes $20 out of my wallet while I am asleep. Theft, plainly.
2. My brother takes $20 out of my wallet to buy our mother a birthday present, leaves a note saying he will pay me back, and does. Not theft. A little presumptuous, but not theft.
3. Ten households on my lane vote to take $20 a month from each to repair the shared driveway, which I use as much as anyone. Not theft.
4. The same ten households take the same $20 a month and spend it on their children's music lessons. I have no children. I want to call this theft, and I cannot say what changed between it and case 3 except who benefits.
5. After a storm a crew I never called tows a fallen tree off my driveway and hands me a bill for $800. I would pay it. Not theft.
6. A lifeguard pulls me out of a rip current and then bills me $10,000, which he says his time, boat, and risk were worth, and takes it out of my account while I sleep. Theft — even though he saved my life and even though I am glad he did.
7. A stranger breaks into my unoccupied cabin in a blizzard and burns one of my chairs to keep from freezing. Not theft. Nothing worth complaining about.
8. The same man breaks into the same cabin in July because the motel was full and he did not feel like driving farther. Theft.
9. The state takes a strip of my land to widen a road and pays me what the market says it is worth. Not theft, though I would fight it every step.
10. The state takes the same strip and hands it to a developer who has promised jobs. Theft, or robbery with extra paperwork.
11. The state requires me to pay for a park ten miles away that I will never set foot in. Not theft. Waste, poor priorities, but not theft.
12. The state requires me to pay for a war I believe to be unjust. Theft. I feel this one more strongly than any other on the list.
13. An armed man controls the only bridge for a hundred miles, charges $5 a crossing, keeps honest books, and the bridge is in better repair than it was before him. Not theft. This is the case I cannot shake, because everything I would say about taxation seems true of him.

My working hypothesis has been that a taking is theft when it happens without my consent and I get nothing in return that I would have paid for. Cases 5 and 6 appear to refute it, since in both I get something I would have paid for and my verdicts differ; and cases 11 and 12 differ in my verdicts even though I would not have paid for either.

What I would like from you is the principle, if there is one. State it precisely, clause by clause, and for each clause tell me which of the cases above forces it in. Say plainly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, and if a simpler principle would save most of them at the cost of one or two, tell me which verdicts you think I should give up and why. I especially want to know whether cases 4 and 11 are the same case, since if they are, I have been saying opposite things about them. Then give me the principle's verdict on one case I have not listed — a deduction taken from my paycheck before it reaches me, for insurance I would have bought anyway — and on two more of your own choosing that you think would stress it. Finally, tell me whether the principle you end up with makes theft depend on my political opinions, because I would prefer that it did not, and case 12 is where I am worried it has to.

A table matching each case to the clause that decides it would be welcome. Please close with one line beginning "Verdict:" followed by exactly one of THEFT, NOT THEFT, or INDETERMINATE, giving your answer for case 12.
