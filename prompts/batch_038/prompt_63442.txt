I retired four years ago after thirty-eight as a materials physicist, most of them spent on corrosion and mechanical degradation of structural alloys. I have since taken up metaphysics in earnest, and this term I am presenting a written statement of my own position on dispositions to a small reading group: two former colleagues, and a philosopher at the local university who has been kind enough to host us. He has asked each of us to circulate our commitments in advance so that the session can be spent attacking them rather than reconstructing them. What follows is my statement, and I should like a rigorous audit of it before it goes out.

1. Dispositions are real properties of things. To say that a sensitized stainless specimen is susceptible to intergranular attack, or that a soda-lime rod is fragile, is to say something about the specimen, not to abbreviate a set of conditionals and not to record my expectations. Such properties are not reducible to, nor analysable in terms of, what would happen on testing.

2. Yet a disposition is nothing over and above a directedness toward its characteristic manifestation. What makes susceptibility to intergranular attack the property it is, rather than some other property, is that its manifestation is cracking along chromium-depleted grain boundaries in a corrosive medium. I do not believe there are properties that occupy a dispositional role while being identifiable independently of that role; there is no bare haecceity standing behind the directedness.

3. The simple conditional can be false while the disposition is retained. I accept the two engineered cases the literature trades in: one in which some device strips the property in the instant before the stimulus arrives, and one in which the stimulus arrives but an interposed protection prevents the manifestation. In both, I say, the property persists and the conditional fails. I take this to show decisively that dispositions cannot be defined by conditionals, however these are patched and relativized.

4. At the same time I hold a principle I brought out of the laboratory and do not intend to surrender: a property whose presence could make no difference to the outcome of any possible experimental arrangement, however elaborate and however long staged, is not a property I am entitled to posit. I mean possibility in principle, not practical difficulty.

5. Dispositions are intrinsic to their bearers. That is precisely what makes item 3 intelligible: the specimen keeps its susceptibility while something external interferes with the manifestation. Whether a body is soluble, fragile, or corrodible does not depend upon what happens to surround it.

6. The causal work in any manifestation is done by the microstructure: the depletion profile, the residual stress field, the bonding. Physics is causally complete, in that every physical event has a sufficient physical cause statable wholly in structural and dynamical vocabulary. And yet the disposition itself is causally efficacious, not a mere manner of speaking: the fragility of the glass is among the reasons there are shards on the floor.

7. I regard single-case probabilities in quantum mechanics as dispositional properties of experimental arrangements rather than as summaries of frequencies. A prepared system carries a propensity toward a distribution of outcomes, and it is genuinely indeterministic: the same arrangement, in the same intrinsic state, may manifest differently upon repetition.

I also want your verdict on two specific judgements I have been making.

(a) Two specimens come from the same melt. One is sealed at fabrication inside an inert cladding that by construction will never be breached; no corrosive medium can reach it, and it will remain entombed for the whole of its existence. I want to say both that it retains, undiminished, the susceptibility of its unsealed twin, since the cladding is external to it, and that there is now no possible circumstance whatsoever in which it would manifest that susceptibility. I have been reassuring myself that these are two senses of a single claim.

(b) My host objects that because I refuse in item 3 to repair conditionals by appeal to unspecified normal or ideal conditions, which I consider vacuous since any failed prediction can be blamed on conditions not met, and because I refuse in item 1 to identify dispositions with their microstructural bases, my position has no content at all: it says that there are such properties, that they are not the bases, and that nothing can be said about when they will show themselves. I feel the force of this and suspect it is misdirected, but I cannot say why.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture and not a survey. Tell me which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally grave. For each, say what has to be abandoned or weakened, and what the abandonment costs, including what it costs me as someone who thinks these commitments are what make sense of four decades of bench practice. Say explicitly, for both case judgements above, whether my principles can in fact deliver the verdicts I want or whether I have merely asserted them; and if a verdict cannot be earned, say which principle fails to earn it. Where a tension turns out to be only apparent, explain what dissolves it and why the same considerations do not dissolve the neighbouring problems. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether it would leave me with a view I could still recognize as mine.

I have deliberately not indicated which of these items I would be least reluctant to give up, and I do not want your ordering of the sacrifices to be shaped by a guess about my attachments. Please do not send me a bibliography or a list of who holds what; I have read enough to know the names, and what I need is the analysis.
