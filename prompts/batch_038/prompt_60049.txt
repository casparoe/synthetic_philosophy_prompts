I cover science and ideas for a magazine and I've got a piece due on the consciousness-as-broadcast debate, and one of my sources — a philosopher who works on this stuff — handed me an argument I cant get out of. He says it shows the anti-theater camp is stuck on both horns. Roughly:

(1) A theory of consciousness has to say, for a given subject at a given moment, which contents were conscious; a theory that can't do that isn't deferring the question, it's ducking it.

(2) On the multiple drafts view, and its successor "fame in the brain," contents become conscious by gaining wide downstream influence — a graded, competitive process that's only determinable after the fact, with no datable moment of crossing over.

(3) So on that view there's no fact about which contents were conscious at a time, at least none that isn't fixed by whichever probes happen to get applied.

(4) Color phi (the Kolers and von Grünau setup) is the case the view was sold on: people report a dot changing color halfway along its apparent path, at a point where the second color hasn't been presented yet. The selling point was that the view dissolves the puzzle by refusing to choose between "the brain revised the record afterwards" and "the subject really saw the change."

(5) But refusing to choose is just (3) applied to a case, so the dissolution isn't a dissolution — its the problem restated in calmer language.

(6) The other horn is worse: if you insist there is a fact about whether the change was experienced, you need a time at which it became conscious, and that's the Cartesian theater sneaking back in.

(7) So either there are no determinate facts about what anyone is conscious of, or the theater is unavoidable — and since the first option isn't a theory of consciousness at all, the theater is unavoidable.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If I'm forced to keep one premise I'd keep (4), because that's the only place here that touches actual data. Also tell me whether you think this argument is invalid or valid-but-resting-on-a-premise-I-should-give-up, and then say how confident you are in that call and which single point in your answer you're least sure about.
