I'm the standards editor on a documentary series about societal collapse — six episodes a season, and every script comes through me before it goes to the senior producer. I get about 45 minutes with each one. I have a history degree and no archaeology training, and I can't call an expert at 8pm before a lock, which is usually when the problem shows up.

The word "collapse" is doing enormous work in these scripts and nobody here has ever written down when we're allowed to say it. Complaints come at me from two directions at once: academics saying we're being sloppy and sensational, and descendent-community advisers saying the word itself is a denial that they exist. A Maya cultural organisation two seasons ago, a Rapa Nui adviser last season. Either kind can turn into a corrections note on air or a pulled episode, so it's not a small thing. And I can't just ban the word — it's in the series title.

What I've been doing, off the top of my head, is this:

We can say a society collapsed only if the script shows (1) a real loss and not just a change — people died or left, cities emptied, the state stopped doing things, the monuments or the writing stopped; (2) it happened fast; (3) it happened to most of the society, not just one region of it; (4) it stuck, meaning it didn't come back within 50 years; and (5) there's no living community today that traces itself to that society, because if there is, using the word is an insult.

I know this is bad. I know it's bad specifically because clause 3 would have killed the whole Maya episode and clause 4 is meaningless for a Bronze Age state but perfectly meaningful for a Rust Belt city, and clause 5 feels like it's not even the same kind of thing as the others. But it's what I have.

Two books is the extent of my reading on this: Diamond's *Collapse*, which the producers love because it says societies choose to fail, and Tainter's *The Collapse of Complex Societies*, which I got about a third of the way through and which seems to make collapse a technical term about complexity rather than a dramatic one. I've also been told there's a whole camp of archaeologists who think "collapse" is a category we should mostly stop using and study resilience and reorganisation instead. I don't know how much of that argument I actually need.

So here's what I want.

Turn this into a procedure I can run on a script in forty-five minutes. Ordered questions, in the order I should ask them, with a stated pass condition for each — what counts as a sufficient answer given that most of these scripts have no numbers in them at all, just phrases like "the cities were abandoned" and "the population plummeted." I need it to say what to do when the script is vague rather than wrong, because vague is the normal case. And I need a ladder of substitute words I can send back to the writer — reorganisation, contraction, regional abandonment, dynastic failure, state disintegration, decline — with a rule for picking one, so my note isn't just "don't say collapse."

Separately: the causal story and the descriptive word are getting tangled up and I think they need to be different tests. A script can be fine on "did this society lose complexity" and still be doing something indefensible with "and it was because they cut down all their trees." Give me a second track for that.

Before you write the procedure, tell me briefly what survives of the actual argument and what I'm allowed to drop. Which distinctions in this literature have to make it into my checklist because a script will get run over by them if they don't, and which ones can a non-specialist safely ignore for the purposes of deciding whether a narration line goes to air. Be honest that dropping things costs accuracy, and say where.

Then run it on three:

- The ninth-century southern Maya lowlands — the abandonment of the great cities, the end of the dated monuments, while cities in the north were rising and the region stayed occupied.
- Rapa Nui, where the script wants to say the islanders deforested themselves into a crash, and where I know enough to be nervous about what happened after Europeans arrived.
- The Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, which is many polities over a couple of centuries, some of which were destroyed, some of which were fine, and some of which came back differently.

For at least one of these, give me the answer I won't like. I've been telling the producer we can't say the Maya collapsed, on the strength of the complaint, and I suspect your procedure is going to tell me we can, at least for a bounded version of the sentence — in which case I need to know what to say to the adviser, and whether that's even my call to make or whether the procedure should stop and hand it to a person.

Finally, tell me what this procedure cannot do. Where will it systematically misfire, what kind of script will it wave through that it shouldn't, and which of my five clauses is it keeping in some form and which is it throwing out. And as a stress test: does it travel to a modern episode at all, if we did one on the Aral Sea towns? I half suspect the answer is that it quietly stops working there.

No bibliography and no reading list — I can't chase references on a deadline and I'm not going to.
