I'm taking a phil of biology/mind course and I've basically bought into the crane-vs-skyhook picture: that there's a principled difference between explaining something by stacking cranes (selection, learning, cultural evolution) and explaining it with a skyhook, and that greedy reductionism is a real, nameable mistake rather than just a slur you aim at reductionists you don't like. The problem is I can't say what would ever show me wrong about it. So — what would count against the view? In particular: is the crane/skyhook line a property of an explanation, or only a property of whichever explanandum you happen to pick? My suspicion is that you can redescribe basically any reduction as a crane just by choosing the right target, and I want to know whether that's fatal or merely annoying. Related: what would a genuinely successful, residue-free reduction of something like normativity or phenomenal character have to look like before I'd have to concede the other side pulled it off without a skyhook — and what would make me call an explanation a skyhook in the first place rather than just a crane I don't like the aesthetics of? And which of these would a commited Dennettian actually grant as counting against them, versus insist I've misdescribed the position? Rank the defeaters from "the distinction does no work at all" down to "awkward but survivable," and please keep separate what's genuinely settled here from what people who work on this still argue about — I don't want some contested reading handed to me as the consensus.
