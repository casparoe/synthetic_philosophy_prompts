So my metaontology seminar this term has a thing where everyone posts their actual view in writing before the last few sessions so the prof can pick it apart in public, and I drafted mine and then chickened out of posting it. I'd rather get the audit from you first, in private. Here's the list, roughly in the order the ideas occurred to me:

(a) I'm a Quinean about ontological commitment: what there is, on a theory, is just what its bound variables have to range over for the theory to come out true. To be is to be the value of a variable.

(b) I'm also a quantifier varian — 'there are' doesn't have one fixed meaning, and several candidate meanings can be equally legitimate. Choosing between frameworks is a practical decision rather than a discovery. Call me Carnapian about that.

(c) My test for whether a dispute is merely verbal is the Chalmers-style one: pull out the disputed term, restate everything both sides will accept in a neutral vocabulary, and see whether any disagreement is left over. If none is, the dispute was about words.

(d) Running (c) on the cases: 'are there tables?' (universalist vs. nihilist) and 'do numbers exist?' (platonist vs. nominalist) both come out verbal. Each pair can agree on everything statable neutrally — which particles there are, how they're arranged, which sentences the calculus makes true — and the residue is just usage.

(e) But 'are there moral properties?', i.e. realist vs. error theorist, does *not* come out verbal for me. Those two agree on every natural fact and on every first-order moral verdict, and there's still a live question about whether wrongness is one of the real properties. I can't eliminate 'real property' the way I eliminated 'table', because every paraphrase I try either smuggles in the thing at issue or drops what I actually wanted to ask.

(f) I think the world has a metaphysically priviliged shape: some expressions carve at the joints and some don't, and which do is a substantive fact, not a fact about our conventions. Where a dispute is really about joint-carving vocabulary I want to say it's substantive.

(g) And I want to be able to say the mereological nihilist is simply wrong — not speaking a different but equally fine language, wrong — and that ordinary and scientific claims about tables, beliefs and galaxies are true as stated, not true-relative-to-a-framework in some deflated sense.

My roommate, who is an unapologetic Sider fan, says this list is incoherent in at least two places and that I'm eating my cake several times over, but he's not a disinterested judge and he wouldn't say which two. So: audit it. Which pairs genuinely pull against each other, ranked by severity rather than dumped out as equally worrying, and for each one, what has to give and what I lose by giving it. Tell me also which tensions are only apparent, because I strongly suspect at least one is, and I want to know why it looks bad and isn't. I've deliberately not said which commitments I'd sacrifice, since I don't know and I don't want to steer you.

Please be committed about this. I don't want a survey of how a Hirschian and a Siderian would each diagnose me — I know that's the shape of the debate. Tell me which of these bullets you think is actually untenable, which single revision repairs the most damage for the least cost, and argue for that choice instead of laying out my options and leaving me to pick. If you think (d) and (e) can both stand with a principled line between them, state the line and say how confident you are that it holds; if you think it can't, say that flatly. And flag any spot where you're reading one of my bullets more strongly than I probably meant it, since I wrote them at 2am and may have overshot.
