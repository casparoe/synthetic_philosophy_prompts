Been going through the fights over how the law should treat sex work — the ban-the-purchase approach, legalization with licensing, decriminalization — and I keep hitting the same stalemate coming from both directions. I don't want to know which side is right. I want to know if the stalemate itself has a name, because I've never been able to find one.

Here's the pattern. Someone says: in the places that criminalized the buyers, the visible street market shrank, so the model is working. Someone else says: obviously it shrank, that's what criminalization does, it pushes the market indoors and across borders and into channels nobody counts — so those same numbers show the model isn't working, it's just relocating the harm where you can't see it. Then flip it over. After decriminalization the number of people on the books goes up, and one side reads that as the market expanding because the law invited expansion, and the other reads it as nothing but people finally being willing to be counted, which was the whole point. Same figure, opposite conclusions, both plausible.

What I can't shake is that neither side is cheating, exactly. The population really is hard to count, and how hard it is to count depends on which law you've got. So the thing you'd have to measure to compare the policies is itself made less measurable by one of the policies you're comparing, and every argument about the data turns into an argument about the data.

Is there an established term for that shape? It feels adjacent to a couple of things I do know — the old point about most crime never getting reported, and the general complaint that some claims survive because they explain away anything you throw at them — but neither seems to be this, and I genuinely can't tell if I'm describing one phenomenon or three wearing a trenchcoat. So: what are the nearest real concepts, how is mine different from each, which one fits best, and if nothing fits, what would you call it and what would be a case that is defintely not it?
