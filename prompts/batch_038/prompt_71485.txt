so our philosophy-of-physics reading group made everyone write down their actual position on time before the next meeting so the others can attack it, and here's mine. I want a proper audit, not a survey.

1. I take special relativity at face value rather than instrumentally — no hidden privileged frame doing work behind the scenes that we just can't detect. Minkowski spacetime is what there is, and "it's only a way of organising predictions" is not an answer I accept.

2. The Rietdijk–Putnam argument goes through. I did the arithmetic myself for the Andromeda version: two people passing on the street, one walking at ordinary pace, ~2.5 million light years to the galaxy, and their simultaneity slices come out days apart. I accept that if co-reality has to be a transitive, observer-independent relation and there's no privileged slice, then events in my future are already real. Four-dimensional block.

3. To be clear I don't think that's a determinism result. I believe in genuine objective chance — irreducibly stochastic outcomes, not dressed-up ignorance. The block and determinism come apart, and the block is the thing I'm actually committed to.

4. I also think there's real, non-epistemic openness to the future. Concretely: a statement about what some particular person will do in a hundred years isn't true-or-false right now. Not "we don't know yet" — there's nothing there yet to be true of. I don't see this clashing with 2, because 2 is about what exists out there in spacetime and this is about truth, and existing and being true arent the same relation. People have told me that move is a dodge and I don't think it is.

5. Whether time passes, whether there's genuine becoming, is exactly the sort of question physics cannot settle in either direction. No experiment could confirm or disconfirm passage. So physics underdetermines the metaphysics here, and I don't think you're allowed to walk from a physical theory to a conclusion about becoming.

6. I don't think the usual escapes work either. No growing block, no moving spotlight, no global now, no "the present is just wherever I happen to be located" indexical trick. All times are ontologically on a par.

7. But I do think the static block can ground the past/future asymmetry that actually matters — memory runs one way, I can affect the future and not the past, there are records of only one side. My view is the entropy gradient and the thermodynamic arrow do that work inside the four-dimensional picture.

8. And none of this touches free will or makes deliberation pointless. The block contains my deliberating, and contains my deliberating playing a causal role in what comes after it.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I keep getting told 2 and 3 are inconsistent and Im fairly confident that's wrong, but I'd rather have it settled than assumed. Rank the genuine tensions by severity instead of listing everything as equally bad, and for each one say specifically which commitment has to go or be watered down and what the watering-down costs you elsewhere. Don't hand me a menu with the choosing left to me — say which one you'd cut and argue for it. And on 4: either show me exactly how it survives next to 2 and 6, or tell me plainly that it doesn't.
