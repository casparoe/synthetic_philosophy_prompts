So in my cog sci program we keep re-teaching the same stalemate: in binocular rivalry you show each eye a different image, the person reports the percepts flipping every few seconds, and the scans show two kinds of effects at once — the relevant bits of sensory cortex track which percept is dominant, and then there's this sudden frontoparietal burst plus a late P300-ish wave that lines up with the button press. One camp says the burst is what conscious access is; the other says that's just post-perceptual cognition and task machinery, and that consciousness is already fixed back in sensory areas. Nobody's budged in twenty years.

My hypothesis, which I want pressure-tested rather than agreed with: the stalemate is mostly about words, since "conscious" means phenomenally present for one side and reportably available for the other. Is that the right read, or is there a real disagreement hiding underneath — maybe about whether there's any fact of the matter about when a given percept starts being conscious? And where does Dennett's multiple drafts stuff fit? It seems to deny a single locus, and so does the global workspace picture, yet they're always presented as rivals — is that opposition substantive or just a difference in what an explanation has to deliver? Tell me what would count as progress here: a paradigm that needs designing, a distinction nobody's drawn yet, or an argument that the data underdetermines the question permanently.
