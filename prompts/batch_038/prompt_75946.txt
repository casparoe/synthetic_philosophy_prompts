So my apartment's philosophy-of-religion reading group got into the kalam argument last week and it derailed almost immediately over one sentence — and the annoying part is that two different people used that sentence to make opposite points.

Our math guy, when somebody brought up Hilbert's hotel (fully booked infinite hotel, shift everyone up one, still has a room free, then the version with infinitely many buses), waved it off with "infinity isn't a number, so there's no paradox there, you're just misusing the word." Then maybe ten minutes later one of the people defending the argument used almost the same sentence the other direction: infinity isn't a number, therefore nothing can actually have an infinite number of parts, therefore the hotel couldn't be built, therefore no actual infinite in reality. Same slogan, both sides thought it settled things for them, and I couldnt say which if either was right. Still bugs me.

Can you lay out the precise claims that one sentence could be making? I want them sorted by which kind of number is being denied — natural number, cardinal, ordinal, real, and then the more exotic systems (extended reals, hyperreals, surreals) where "there is such a thing as an infinite number" is arguably just true — and for each reading, a one-line verdict: platitude everyone accepts, substantive but defensible, or flatly false. Then, for each, whether it buys anything in the argument:

- which reading, if true, would make the hotel a non-event instead of an absurdity? Is that reading the one the math guy needs, or does he need something stronger?
- which reading would actually license "no concrete collection can be actually infinite," and how much extra does that one need — something about traversing an infinite step by step, or about what physical realization would require — that isn't in the slogan at all? If the slogan does no work there and all the work is being done by premises nobody stated, say so.
- is there any reading that is both true and does real work for either side? I suspect not, but I might be wrong.

And the bit I most want: I think the actual fight is about arithmetic, not ontology, and the slogan is hiding it. That ℵ₀ + 1 = ℵ₀ is not disputed by anyone. But one camp hears that as "our finitary intuitions about addition simply don't generalize, nothing weird happened," and the other hears it as "a quantity that doesn't grow when you add to it isn't a quantity, so there are no such things." Also worth sorting out: the hotel guests can be thought of cardinally or ordinally, and I think those two ways of counting come apart here — ω + 1 is not ω even though the cardinals are equal. Tell me whether that distinction is load-bearing for the argument or just decoration. Are those two camps disagreeing about what the slogan means, or agreeing about the slogan and disagreeing about something else entirely? Which, exactly.

Deliverable is a numbered menu I can print and bring next week, verdicts attached, no essay. And if a reading is one that no professional would touch but that laypeople definitely mean when they say it, flag that seperately — it's probably the one that was actually in the room.
