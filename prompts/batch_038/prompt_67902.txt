Our department is running an internal competition for a semester of teaching relief, and the application asks for a project plan of roughly 1,200 words that will be read by two non-specialists and one Aristotelian. I need help turning a half-formed idea into that plan. The material I am starting from, all from the opening book of the Politics: Aristotle distinguishes three deficiencies in the household by using three different words, saying that the natural slave is wholly without the deliberative element, that the free woman has it but *akuron*, and that the child has it but *ateles*. Elsewhere in the same book he says that the natural slave shares in reason only to the extent of apprehending it rather than having it; that the slave is a part of the master, a living but separated part of his body; and that the master's knowledge of how to command slaves is nothing grand or exalted, since whatever the slave must know how to do, the master need only know how to order — a function so unimpressive that a man with the means hands it to a steward while he himself goes into politics or philosophy.

My hunch is that the long-running dispute over whether the natural-slave doctrine is even coherent has been fought entirely in a psychological register — is there a kind of human being who can register reasons but cannot originate them? — because both sides read *akuron* as describing an internal failure of a faculty. But *akuron* is the antonym of *kyrios*, and in the Greek of the period it belongs primarily to the register of force rather than function: it is said of decrees, verdicts, and agreements that are unratified or void, and of persons who lack standing to act in some matter. Read that way, the deficiency Aristotle attributes to the woman is not that her deliberation goes wrong but that it cannot become final; the slave's, by contrast, is that there is nothing there to be ratified. That would recast the household of Book I as a single distributed agency in which the content of a decision and its finality are allocated to different members, and it would explain why the master's expertise is so thin — he contributes ratification, not content, which is also why it can be delegated.

Write the project plan.
