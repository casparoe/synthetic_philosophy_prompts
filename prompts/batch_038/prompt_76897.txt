Before retiring I spent thirty-five years in observational cosmology, and since then I have been auditing a philosophy-of-physics seminar at my former institute. Next term the two speakers — one a substantivalist, one a relationist about spacetime — have agreed to replace their talks with an adversarial collaboration, and I have been asked to prepare in advance the document they will attempt to sign. Please draft it.

Begin with the claims both can endorse without reservation regarding the formalism: what diffeomorphism invariance does and does not establish, what Newton's bucket is supposed to show, and what the hole argument leaves genuinely open. Then set out a table of the disagreements that survive that common ground, each entry stated twice — once in phrasing the substantivalist would accept as a fair rendering of his own view, once in phrasing the relationist would accept as a fair rendering of hers — and mark for each whether it is a disagreement about the world or only about how the vocabulary is to be used. Then identify the two or three cruxes whose settlement would actually move one of them, together with a concrete case each agrees bears on the crux in question. Include among these a hypothetical detection of Planck-scale Lorentz violation singling out a preferred frame; each side should record, in advance, what it would then conclude about spacetime structure and what it would maintain remains unsettled. Close with the places where the collaboration is bound to break down because the choice rests on something other than evidence.
