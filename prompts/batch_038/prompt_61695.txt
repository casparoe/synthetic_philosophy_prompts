I TA a cross-listed intro seminar — half geology majors, half history majors — and every year the week on the Anthropocene turns into a shouting match where people argue about dates without noticing that they disagree about something else entirely. So I want to build a diagnostic for them: a decision tree (or a short quiz with a scoring key, your call) that sorts a student into an actual position on the question without ever asking "when do you think it started" or "do you think the Anthropocene is real." Those just get you back whatever label they picked up off a podcast.

The trick I'm after: the branch nodes should be questions about what a geological boundary has to *be*, not about years. Stuff like — does the marker have to be globally synchronous, or is it acceptable for the signal to show up at different times in different places? Does it have to be physically readable in an archive (sediment, ice, coral) or can it be an event we know from documents? Is it a problem if the marker turns out to be caused by one particular group of humans rather than by the species? Ought the name be neutral description, or is the naming itself part of the argument? Does a formal vote going against a proposal change whether you're allowed to keep using the word? Every node should be answerable by a first-year who has never heard the term golden spike, so either dodge the jargon or define it inside the question.

The leaves need to include at least: the mid-20th-century formal-epoch position as the working group actually argued it (Crawford Lake in Ontario, the bomb-fallout marker), plus whatever that position becomes now that the stratigraphers voted it down in 2024 while the word carried on regardless; the 1610 Orbis spike line, tied to depopulation and regrowth in the Americas; the early-Anthropocene line that pushes the start back to farming; the view that it's a fine informal concept but not a unit anyone should be ratifying; the view that the whole frame is wrong because it indicts a species for something a particular economic system did (Capitalocene and its cousins); and the flat deflationary "we're still in the Holocene" option. Add any leaf you think is genuinely live that I've missed.

Then the two things I care about most. First: which combinations of answers come out unoccupied — nobody who answers that way has a name or a champion — and for each, tell me whether it's empty because the view is incoherent, because it's politically unattractive, or because nobody's bothered yet. Second: which pairs of leaves your tree can't actually seperate. I already suspect the mid-century person and the "dates are the wrong question" person will answer nearly everything the same way, and I'd rather the instrument admit that than paper over it.

Eight to ten questions max, and please make it something I can hand out on a single page and have them work through in pairs.
