I run a six-bay independent repair shop. For eleven years Marcus has been the guy who finds the fault. He'll pop the hood, listen for ten seconds, tap something with a screwdriver handle, and go "coil pack on three." And he's right. Like nine times out of ten, right, first try. Ask him how he knows and he shrugs. He'll say "you can hear it in the idle," but push him and he cant tell you what he's hearing or why that sound means that part. He never really learned the electrical side, can't read a wiring diagram to save his life.

The other person I'm weighing is Dana. She's slower. She'll spend an hour with the multimeter and the service manual and trace the circuit. Sometimes she misses and we eat the diagnostic time. But she can explain what she did and she can teach it — half my apprentices can actually follow her reasoning.

Ray's argument, and he keeps repeating it like it's a knockout: Dana's hour of method sometimes lands on the wrong answer, and Marcus's ten seconds of guessing usually lands on the right one. So the explanation isn't doing any work, it's just decoration bolted on after the fact. The customer is paying for a fixed car. Right is right. The extra account of why doesn't add anything to the thing we actually sell. Something about that bugs me and I can't get it into words. Two things are actually on the table. We're starting to see EVs and hybrids come in, where there's no idle to listen to and no clicking relay — the stuff Marcus keys on isn't even there anymore.
