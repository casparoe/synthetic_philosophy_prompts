ok so a thing I keep coming back to. Sometime in the last couple of decades the official national accounts changed how they treat research: instead of writing off R&D spending as a running cost, the statistical agencies now count it as investment, which builds up a knowledge capital stock that depreciates and earns a return — and since that return has to be imputed rather than observed, capitalizing research bumped measured output up overnight without anybody making anything new. Software got the same treatment earlier. At the same time the productivity statistics still carve out a residual, the chunk of growth you can't explain with measured capital and labour, which is its more or less an official admission of ignorance.

Two things I want. First: what theory of where wealth comes from does capitalizing research presuppose? Walk me through what the convention actually commits you to — that knowledge is an alienable stock with a yield of its own, that its contribution can be measured separately from the living labour applying it, that it depreciates like a lathe — and then say which rival accounts get ruled out without ever being argued against. Marx is the obvious contrast: on his picture machines and accumulated knowledge transfer value into the product but don't create any, so automating raises the ratio of dead labour to living labour and squeezes the rate of profit; and in the Grundrisse notebooks he goes further and suggests that once general scientific knowledge becomes the direct productive force, labour time stops being the measure of wealth and disposable free time takes over that role. Set the accounting rule against that and tell me how much of the rule depends on the picture Marx is denying.

Second, the residual: can one and the same body of knowledge be a capital stock with an imputable yield and also the unexplained leftover in the growth accounting, or is one of those two entries a confession that the other is made up?

Also flag where the practice is philosophically underdetermined — where several incompatible theories would license exactly the same bookkeeping — versus where it genuinely can't be neutral. And finish with what would concretely have to change in the accounts if you took the general-intellect reading seriously, i.e. knowledge as a common, non-rivalrous product of society that has been enclosed as private property and then scored as its owner's contribution.
