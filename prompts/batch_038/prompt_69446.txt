Take Rhodes Must Fall — the 2015 campaign at the University of Cape Town that got Cecil Rhodes' statue hauled off campus — and run it thru Nietzsche's three modes of history from the second Untimely Meditation. I've got the outline (monumental, antiquarian, critical) and I've got the protest story; what I want is the mapping done carefully. Who was doing history in which mode: the students who threw faeces at the statue, the alumni and senate who wanted it kept, the university leadership who eventually voted for removal? Worth noting UCT sits on land Rhodes gave it and the statue was unveiled in 1934, which looks like textbook antiquarian piety toward an origin — and that the Oxford college with it's own Rhodes over the High Street doorway chose a consultation instead of a verdict, which reads like something else entirely. Then the harder part: the movement's line was that Rhodes falling symbolises white supremacy falling, which sounds like monumental history run backwards, mobilizing the past as an anti-model rather than as something to emulate. Does the triad have a slot for that, or does it just collapse into critical history? And be straight about what the three modes can't see here — endowments, the Rhodes Scholarships, who legally owns a lump of bronze — and whether UCT confirms the framework or stresses it.
