I direct a small research institute. Two years ago we adopted a data charter giving each of our two group leaders sole and final authority over whether their own group's datasets are released; the rest of us, even acting unanimously, cannot overrule either of them on that question.

Since then we have rotated through every possible arrangement and arrived back where we started. Neither group released anything. Then the methods lead invoked her prerogative and released alone. Then the clinical lead invoked his and released as well, so both were open. Then the methods lead invoked hers again and withdrew, leaving the clinical group open by itself. And then the two of them agreed that this last state was worse for everyone than the one we began in, so the clinical lead withdrew too, and we are back to neither releasing. Every step was unimpeachable, and the whole sequence has gone in a circle.

For what it is worth, their standing preferences are these. The methods lead most wants to release while the clinical group stays closed, then neither releasing, then the clinical group alone, and last of all both. The clinical lead most wants neither, then both, then methods alone, and last of all himself alone.

Is the charter itself unjust, is one of them arguing in bad faith, or is there some procedure — trading the prerogatives, waiving them conditionally — that stops the carousel without simply handing the decision back to me?
