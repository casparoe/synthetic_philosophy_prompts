Take Olds and Milner's self-stimulating rats — the McGill experiments where rats with electrodes in the septum or lateral hypothalamus pressed a lever for short pulse trains of stimulation, hard enough that eating and drinking got crowded out — and analyse the episode as the founding case of wireheading / reward tampering. I know the usual retelling, including the more lurid versions where the rat presses till it collapses, so don't just replay that. What I want is the mapping. Be exact about what the lever press does causally: it doesn't edit the rat's reward function or its beliefs about the world, it just drives the circuitry the function reads off. So under the tampering-versus-hacking distinction, is this tampering at all, or something the taxonomy was never built to describe — and who's the tamperer, the rat or the experimenter who put the channel there? Then run it backwards: the electrode shows the rat's reward signal was a proxy for fitness-relevant goods all along, so does the case confirm the wireheading worry or dissolve it into the older one, that any reward signal is exploitable once its normal causes can be bypassed? Finally settle the welfare question instead of listing positions — the rat is getting exactly what it wants, enormously often. Better or worse off, and does the episode hurt desire-satisfaction accounts of welfare? Say where your analysis leans on details the retelling may have inflated, and whether a human self-stimulation subject, who has beliefs about what he's doing and can be asked, changes the verdict. Commit: is the rat a wirehead? One definite answer with reasons, not a survey.
