Suppose Ryle got handed a laptop in 2025 with a frontier chatbot on it, and was also told the whole training story — that what it does it picked up from text rather than from living anything. His Concept of Mind is basically the claim that to believe something just is to be disposed to behave in a whole spread of ways, with no ghost hiding behind the behavior. So what does he say about the model? I want you to actually commit rather than hedging both ways. Does his dispositionalism license belief-talk about an LLM almost by default, since there's nothing more to belief than a multi-track pattern of what something woud say and do — making Ryle the last person entitled to balk? Or does something structural block it: that a model's dispositions come from gradient descent instead of a history of choices, that they can be reweighted, forked, or switched off between conversations, that its avowals aren't exercises of a disposition embedded in a life the way ours are? And then Dennett, who wrote the introduction to the 2002 reissue and then spent decades saying you can treat the thing as an intentional system. Is the intentional stance Ryle's own view with the behaviorism filed off, or a relapse into the ghost-in-the-machine picture with better marketing? If Ryle would call the whole question of whether LLMs have beliefs a category mistake, explain what the two miscategorised categories are supposed to be.
