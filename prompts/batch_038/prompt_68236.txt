A foundation has given me a year to write a book that will be neither a novel nor scholarship, and I need the proposal that justifies the money. My subject is how the past is used by people who are not historians, and my frame is Nietzsche's second Untimely Meditation: monumental, antiquarian and critical history, each in his account a way of serving life and each carrying a characteristic degeneration. The idea I want worked up into a proposal is that the triad is incomplete and individuated along the wrong axis. Monumental history asks the past for models, antiquarian history asks it for a home, critical history asks it for permission to break with an inheritance — but the working novelist asks the past for material, treating what happened not as exemplar, heritage or burden but as stuff to be rearranged until it becomes true in another register. Call this fourth mode fabular history, and its degeneration the point at which rearrangement stops being answerable to anything and the past turns into a costume warehouse.

What I want from you is a two-page proposal. State the question so that it can be answered rather than merely discussed: is the fabular mode a genuine fourth entry, or monumental history with the requirement of greatness removed, or antiquarian history with the piety removed — and what would decide between those readings? Give the criteria for what earns a place in such a taxonomy at all (the question a mode puts to the past, the affect that drives it, the failure it is liable to), and say why the familiar readings of the essay leave this open rather than merely silent on it. Then the method, month by month, for someone whose instrument is fiction rather than philology: how scenes and draft decisions are to function as evidence for a philosophical claim and not as decoration, where the project must lean on the capacity Nietzsche makes central to health in that essay — what the translations call plastic power — and where it can be kept free of that concept. Say what a successful result would look like to a reader who has never written fiction, and be blunt about failure; my own suspicion is that the fourth mode will turn out to be a description of my habits rather than a possibility anyone else would recognise. Close with an honest judgment on whether a year is enough, and which part I should cut first if it is not. The references are mine to add.
