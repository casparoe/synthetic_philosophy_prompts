So I'm writing a 4,000 word paper for my normative ethics seminar and I've kind of convinced myself of something my study group refuses to accept, and I want to check whether I'm missing an obvious reply or whether their just being stubborn.

The topic is supererogation, the saints and heroes stuff that Urmson kicked off. Here's what I think. Every candidate for a supererogatory act gets its status from the cost to the agent — donating a kidney, running into a burning flat, giving away half your income — none of them are required because they demand too much of the person doing them. But cost comes in degrees, and you can turn the dial down. Take any putative supererogatory act and watch what happens as the burden shrinks: at some point it stops being too much to ask and starts being something a decent person simply does. Now push the dial all the way to zero. There's a button in front of you, pressing it cures some stranger's illness, and it costs you literally nothing — no time, no money, no risk, no effort, not even a twinge of regret about the afternoon you'd planned, and no one else is made any worse off by it. Pressing the button still feels nice of you, but not pressing it would make you a monster, and everyone I ask agrees on that much. The trouble is there's no principled place to stop the dial between the button and the kidney. For any act you want to call supererogatory I can produce a cheaper version of it, and cheapness slides into costlessness one grain at a time. So supererogatory doesn't name a real deontic category at all, it's just a placeholder we use for acts whose costs we haven't finished pricing. Once the pricing is done, everything is either required or forbidden, and the only reason the category has survived in the literature is that nobody wants to run the numbers.

My question: given that this is basically Singer's shallow pond with the water drained out, which of the standard defences of supererogation is the weakest link, the one I should aim my middle section at? I figure they either bite the bullet and say the button case is still only praiseworthy-but-not-required, in which case they've definately given up the intuition that got them the category in the first place, or they insist there's a sharp cutoff somewhere along the dial and then they owe me a story about why it sits exactly there and not one notch lower. Is there a second horn I'm not seeing here, and does this argument already have a name?
