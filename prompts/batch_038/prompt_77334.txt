The thesis, roughly: Einstein's socialism was not a sentimental side hobby bolted onto an otherwise apolitical scientist. It was the same methodological instinct he applied to physics, pointed at society. In physics his recurring move was against background structures that determine everything while being themselves unaccountable — stuff that acts but cannot be acted on, that constrains the system but is not constrained by it, and that no possible experiment could ever call to justify itself. My claim is that his complaint about capitalism, especially in the 1949 essay Why Socialism? that he wrote for the first issue of Monthly Review, has that same shape: an economy nobody chose, a concentration of private power that a democratically organized political system turns out to be unable to effectively check, and an education system that trains people to treat that arrangement as natural rather than as one option among others. So socialism, on this reading, is the attempt to make the background explicit and answerable — to drag the thing that shapes everything into the space where it can be argued with and changed. And crucially, his own warning in that essay that a planned economy is not by itself socialism, and could perfectly well accompany the complete enslavement of the individual, is not a hedge tacked on at the end; on my reading it's the strongest evidence for the whole story, because he's saying the point was never state control of production, it was accountability. I've been calling this the background-structure objection, which is my coinage and not anybody's term of art, so don't go looking for it. I'm also explicitly not claiming Einstein ever articulated this himself or that he connected Mach and absolute space to capital anywhere in writing. I'm claiming the analogy is explanatory and not merely decorative. That's the soft spot and I know it.
