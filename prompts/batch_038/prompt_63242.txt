So I've been going down a rabbit hole on Twin Earth — the Putnam thing, twin planet where the clear drinkable stuff in the lakes is chemically different but behaves exactly the same, so their word for it comes out meaning something different even though nobody on either side could ever tell. Everything I read is about the two planets in isolation. What nobody seems to write about is what happens *after* contact, once they're trading and the Twin Earthlings start learning the word from us instead of just keeping their own. Because then the term is borrowed: the chain of usage runs back through Earth speakers to a baptism that happened here, over a glass of actual H2O — so when a kid on Twin Earth says it, is she talking about the liquid in her cup or ours?

I'd like a short story, call it 1500 words, that treats this as a *practical* mess rather than a seminar topic. My setup, feel free to adjust: contact is maybe thirty years old, there's a joint trade commission putting together the first shared contract language, and one clause about one shipment has to be settled — a cargo lost or rejected, somebody financially ruined, and the whole case turns on which liquid the word in the contract picked out at the moment of signing, and on who signed it (an Earth-born clerk vs a Twin Earth native who picked up the language late, from us, not from her grandmother).

A few constraints if you can manage them:
- don't use the words reference, extension, externalism, meaning, or any of that apparatus anywhere. The philosophy has to be recoverable from what happens, not from anyone explaining it.
- give one character the view that history doesn't matter at all, that the word just latches onto whichever of the two liquids is more real or more fundamental, full stop — and let that view be genuinely tempting for a while before the story breaks it on one specific case.
- end on the dictionary entry itself, and make the last line hinge on the ambiguity of a single word inside it. Don't resolve thier dispute.
