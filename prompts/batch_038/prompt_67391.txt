I'm circulating a draft position paper on Dennett's taxonomy of kinds of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures — and I would like a consistency check on my own commitments before it goes out. Here is what I hold:

(1) A creature's grade is fixed by the mechanism through which its competences are generated and revised: natural selection alone; operant reinforcement; internal models of the world that let candidate actions be tried and rejected before they are performed; and, at the top, the capacity to take up design information that other creatures have deposited in artifacts and practices.

(2) The grades are strictly nested. Nothing is Gregorian that is not also Popperian, Skinnerian, and Darwinian, and I take that nesting to be non-accidental: each higher capacity requires the ones beneath it.

(3) The Skinnerian-to-Popperian step is the step at which the animal stops having to be extinguished, injured, or killed in order to find out what works. Trials get internalized, so the hypotheses take the hit instead of the creature.

(4) The Popperian-to-Gregorian step is not a matter of more raw intelligence but of access to a cumulative public store of design information, which lets a creature acquire competences it could never have arrived at by any amount of individual problem-solving. I also hold that language is necessary for such a store, so nothing without a language is Gregorian.

(5) New Caledonian crows, who manufacture hooked tools and seem to pick up the local design from their elders without anything like a language, are Gregorian — or at any rate plainly more than Popperian. A solitary problem-solver of equal cleverness, whose inventions die with it, is not.

(6) A large language model, pretrained on human text and then shaped by reinforcement from human feedback, is Gregorian through and through, and it shows that the grades are a menu of design options rather than a ladder: the top rung can be built directly, without climbing.

Which of these pull against one another, which only look as though they do, and what would have to be surrendered in each case? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damaging, give for each the cheapest repair along with what that repair costs me, identify separately any pair I might wrongly suspect so that I don't revise where no revision is needed, and end by naming the single change that would buy the most coherence overall. I am not attached to any particular item, and I am not looking for an exposition of the framework — only an audit of the set.
