The following argument was assembled while I was preparing a graduate seminar on persistence, and I have become convinced that it is unsound without being able to say where the failure occurs. I would like a diagnosis rather than a survey.

The target is the problem of temporary intrinsics: how a single persisting thing can have incompatible properties at different times.

(1) Change consists in one and the same thing's having incompatible properties. If the properties held at the different times are not properties of numerically the same thing, then what we have is not a change in that thing but only a variation in the arrangement of several things.

(2) Any property a thing has, it has either absolutely or relative to something further: a time, a place, a part of itself, a counterpart. There is no third way of having a property.

(3) If a persisting thing had the incompatible properties absolutely, it would have them all at once, which is impossible. So any adequate analysis of persistence must relativize: the thing has its properties relative to something further.

(4) Perdurantism relativizes to parts. The worm is hot in virtue of one temporal part and cold in virtue of another. But those parts are numerically distinct objects, and a distribution of properties across numerically distinct objects is a static pattern, however intricate. So on perdurantism the coffee never changes; there are only hot bits and cold bits, arranged coffee-wise.

(5) Endurantism relativizes to times. The very same thing has being-hot-at-t1 and being-cold-at-t2. But it has both of these now, and it will always have both of them: on this view no persisting thing ever gains a property or loses one. Its entire career is a fixed stock of time-indexed properties, all had at once. Nothing is ever hot; everything is only ever such that it was hot at some time. So on endurantism the coffee never changes either.

(6) Since (2) and (3) exhaust the ways of having a property, and since parts and times are the only candidates the two theories of persistence supply, (4) and (5) exhaust the available analyses.

(7) Therefore, on every available account of persistence, nothing changes.

(8) But the coffee was hot and is now cold, and that is a difference in the world rather than a difference in our ways of describing it.

(9) Therefore every available account of persistence is false.

I regard (8) as beyond dispute and I am not prepared to trade it away. I am also reluctant to abandon (1) outright, because without something like it the word change loses its content and the dispute between endurantists and perdurantists looks as though it was never about anything.

Three things follow from this. First, identify the step at which the argument goes wrong, and explain why that step reads as unobjectionable when one goes through the chain without stopping. Second, the force of the argument depends on (4) and (5) failing for the same reason; state whether they do, and if they do not, draw out what the asymmetry between them implies for the two theories of persistence taken separately. Third, if you think I am wrong that a single step is to blame, then for each of (1), (2), (3), and (6) say what the endurantist and what the perdurantist would each lose by giving it up, and name the cheapest exit overall. Finally, if your considered view is that the argument is valid and that (8) must go after all, say so plainly and sketch what a metaphysics of persistence with no change in it would have to contain in order to earn the name.
