The dodge: keep Adams's equation, P(A→C) = P(C|A), as the whole content of what an indicative conditional means, but treat the arrow as an operator that cannot be iterated — you may build a conditional out of two plain, non-conditional sentences, and you may not place a conditional inside another conditional. As far as I can follow, the collapse only gets started once the language permits nesting, so under my restriction it never gets off the ground. What bothers me is that this may not be a semantic claim at all, just a refusal to write down the sentences that break the rule; and speakers routinely produce and understand nested conditionals of an entirely everyday kind — a prediction about an election with one conditional tucked inside another — which my rule leaves uninterpreted.
