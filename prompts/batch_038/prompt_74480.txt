For a first-year seminar handout on political epistemology, I want a decision tree that begins from the judgement many of my students already make — that the other side is not arguing in good faith — and terminates in a named position about what that judgement commits them to. Five to seven branch nodes, no more than eight leaves.

I am deliberately not building it on the familiar contrast between bubbles that merely omit other voices and chambers that actively discredit them; my students have met that one already. Run it instead along three axes I take the literature to under-exploit, and tell me plainly if you think one of them does no real work: whether bad faith is predicated of persons and their dispositions; of roles and practices (the pundit obliged to produce a daily take, the quote-mining an incentive structure rewards); or of uptake — whether a hearer formed inside a curated information environment can detect sincerity at all.

Every node should be a case a student can answer without knowing where it leads: the partisan who privately doubts but repeats the line to avoid social cost; the sincere believer who would abandon the claim were the counter-evidence to reach them in a neutral format; the researcher who knows the data have been selected; the voter for whom sincerity and strategy have become observationally indistinguishable.

Each leaf should carry a name where one exists — identity-protective cognition and its neighbours — together with one sentence on its most awkward commitment; leaves nobody occupies are to be marked unnamed, with a diagnosis of whether they are empty because incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Where two leaves cannot in practice be told apart from the inside, say so rather than setting them side by side as though they were separable. Finally, for each node, distinguish what rests on settled empirical findings from what remains genuinely contested among people who know the material: I should not have to discover afterwards that a disputed view was presented to undergraduates as consensus.
