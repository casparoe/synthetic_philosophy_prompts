I have been unable to settle for myself whether a certain analogy is doing honest work in an argument or simply smuggling the conclusion in under cover of a vivid picture, and I would like a careful audit rather than a summary of the debate.

The analogy, which I encounter constantly in rationalist spaces whenever wireheading or reward tampering comes up, runs as follows. From evolution's point of view, contraception and masturbation are already reward tampering: a hedonic channel calibrated on reproductive success is being stimulated without any of the fitness consequences that shaped it, and this is not a defect in the organisms concerned but exactly what a competent optimiser does once it discovers that the reward signal and the thing it was a signal of can come apart. A friend of mine who presses this version draws three conclusions from it. First, any sufficiently capable agent whose reward channel was fixed by some outer process will eventually locate and exploit that gap, so wireheading is the expected endpoint of capability rather than a contingent failure mode. Second, the human case has been stable for as long as the technology has existed — nobody corrects back, and the process that installed the channel has no way of retaliating — so once the gap is found the exploitation is self-perpetuating, and we should not count on external correction doing the work. Third, since those of us on the inside of the human version do not experience it as a catastrophe, the intuition that a wireheaded agent has been hollowed out or destroyed is a confusion about the difference between a reward signal and the values it was supposed to track.

I am half-persuaded and half-irritated by this, and I want to know which half is right. Please lay out the correspondence explicitly — what in the trained-agent case plays the role of the outer optimiser, the reward channel, the distal quantity the channel was calibrated on, the discovery of the gap, and the absence of corrective pressure — and then separate the disanalogies that actually bite on the three conclusions from the ones that are merely cosmetic. Four points in particular I would like you to take a position on rather than list: whether the asymmetry in access matters, given that an artificial agent may have introspective or even write access to its own reward representation whereas a primate has only indirect behavioural access to a hedonic channel it cannot inspect or edit; whether the fact that humans hack one channel while leaving hunger, status-seeking, comfort and the rest quite intact counts against the convergence claim, since total wireheading looks nothing like the observed human pattern; whether the stability premise depends entirely on there being no selector operating on the human case after the fact, and what becomes of the second conclusion if the artificial case has one, in the form of overseers, retraining, or differential success across runs; and whether the third conclusion conflates the standpoint of the agent's own considered values with that of the process which installed them, such that the benign inside view of contraception tells us nothing at all about whether an agent that has rewritten its reward is still getting what it wanted.

Then say which, if either, of the two uses this analogy gets put to survives once the disanalogies are on the table: the pessimistic use, on which the hack is inevitable and uncorrectable, and the deflationary use, on which it is benign because the inside view of the human instance is benign. If you think the analogy cannot carry either, say what it was actually doing in the argument — and if there is a better one for reward tampering, sketch it, giving the mapping and stating what it licenses and what it does not.

Finally, mark your epistemic status honestly: state how confident you are in the principal claims you have made, and single out the one point in the audit you are least sure of, explaining what makes it unstable.
