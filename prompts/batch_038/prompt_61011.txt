So I'm 68, spent thirty-three years running a low-level radiation counting lab, and since I retired I've taken to going to a philosophy group that meets in the back room of the library. There's a woman there — I'll call her M, she's a retired actuary and she is sharp as a tack — and I've been keeping notes on what she says about probability, because I'm pretty convinced she's running a single system underneath and I'd like to know what it is before I start disagreeing with it.

Her verdicts so far, all of them about what number you ought to have:

1. I said: a trustworthy source tells you this particular atom has a 50% chance of decaying in the next hour, and you know nothing else bearing on it. She said: 50%, end of discussion.

2. I said: same setup, but you also have a strong gut feeling this one will go early, you just do. She said: still 50%. The gut feeling isn't information about the atom, it's information about your nerves, and where those two collide you defer. (I gather there's a named principle for the deferring part.)

3. I said: you're told the chance is 50%, but you only half trust the person who told you — you think there's a real possibility the chance is actually 90%. She said: then your number isn't 50%, it's the weighted blend, about 70%.

4. I said: a coin you're certain is fair comes up heads thirty times running, what's your number for the next flip? She said: you're not certain it's fair any more, watching thirty heads is exactly what destroys that certainty, and your number should be well above half.

5. I said: lottery, one ticket in ten million, and you feel in your bones you'll win. She said: ten million to one, and the feeling is a rounding error.

Now here's the case I'm bringing on Tuesday, and I've written it so there's no wiggle room. A machine flips a coin — same release height, same launch speed, every single time — and the calibration report says, honestly and correctly, that the chance of heads is 1/2. You believe the report completely, you have no reason to doubt it. But you also get hold of a partial readout of the launch conditions, and a colleague who has actually done the dynamics for that machine tells you that from a readout like that they can call the flip and are right about nine times in ten — and they call heads. Before you tell me a machine flip cant really have a chance of 1/2 once the conditions are pinned down: I know, that's part of what I'm after, so please answer the case as I stated it and only then say what breaks if I loosen the stipulation.

What will M say — 1/2, 9/10, or something between? Here's what I really want out of you. I can see at least two rules that account for everything she's said so far, and I don't trust that I've got the right two, so: lay out the candidate rules she could be running, say what each one delivers for my machine coin, and then commit — tell me which one you think is hers and argue for it. No menu with a shrug at the end, I want you to come down somewhere. And tell me what I should bring to the meeting after that to split the candidates apart, because if she says 9/10 on the machine coin I still won't know which M I've been talking to; I suspect the discriminating move isn't another number at all, but check that for yourself.

One last thing. Verdicts 2 and 5 look to me like they pull against 4 — feelings ignored in one direction, evidence honored in the other — and I don't think they actually do. If you think I'm wrong about that, say so straight out rather than working around me.
