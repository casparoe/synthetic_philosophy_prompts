I'm making a handout for my second-year seminar on analogy and concepts and I want it to be a decision tree, not another list of -isms. Our microdomain for the term has been a shoebox of key-ish things: a Yale key, the same key with two teeth bent flat so it still turns the lock, a blank cut to the same profile, a skeleton key, a hotel keycard, a brass thing shaped exactly like a key that opens botles, and a printed string we've been calling the wifi key. Over eight weeks they've watched the extension of 'key' slide about depending on whether the job is get this door open, work out which object went missing, or sort the box by what its made of.

Build me a tree that starts from 'I accept the sliding' and ends at a named account of what a concept is: prototypes, exemplars, theory-theory, atomism (where the sliding has to be something other than the concept itself changing), the Copycat-style picture on which the slipping is the mechanism rather than a breakdown of it, and whatever else you think belongs in there — including the view that the shoebox is evidence there's no single thing here to have a theory about. Every node has to be a question about the shoebox that a student can answer with no idea which leaf it sends them to; nothing may ask them what they'd call their own view. Set out the logical space before you put people in it, mark the combinations nobody occupies and say why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unvisited), and flag any two leaves your tree can't really pull apart.
