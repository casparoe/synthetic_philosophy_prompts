I am a novelist; my last book was set in a clinic brokering paid kidney donation and paid surrogacy, and the years I have since spent on research and then on the festival circuit have left me with a dialectical pattern I cannot name.

The objection goes like this. Permit the trade and the distaste we feel toward it will not survive the permitting. Once it is routine, priced, advertised, joked about, the recoil fades, and with it goes something that was doing real work: a sense of what it is to have a body, or to make a person, that is not available for purchase. Then the trade is permitted, and the recoil does fade, exactly as predicted — and the reply that comes back is that the objection has been answered. Look, nobody minds any more; the outrage was squeamishness after all. A prediction that came true is read back to the person who made it as a mistake, and the evidence that nothing has been lost is the absence of the very feeling whose disappearance was the harm in the first place, an absence produced by the process under dispute. I keep meeting the same shape elsewhere: a language falls out of daily use and its loss is measured by how few people notice the gap, a city centre is given over to short lets and the warrant for having given it over is that everyone has got used to it.

I have been told this is just a slippery-slope argument, or a self-sealing one, and neither label seems to fit.

Is there an established concept for this, in the literature on market limits or anywhere else? If there is, I want it distinguished from the slippery slope and from self-sealing, and I want its edges: where the reply stops being a fallacy and starts being fair, since I imagine it is sometimes fair. If there is no established term, coin one, define it, and say what would and would not count as an instance.

I would rather be told plainly that I have described a confusion than be handed a term for one. And please mark, as you go, which of the neighbouring concepts you invoke you are confident exist and are what you say they are, and which you may be reconstructing from the shape of the thing. I am not asking for sources — only for a sense of where your confidence sits.
