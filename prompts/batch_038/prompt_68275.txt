I'm a fourth-year condensed matter PhD student, and my thesis is written in two registers at once without much of a seam between them: there's the microscopic Hamiltonian, and then there's the Landau-type free energy I actually work with, where I write down an order parameter, a stiffness, and every coupling the symmetry allows, some of which I fit to data or to a calculation I couldnt do from scratch. I'm fluent in that second register — coarse graining, relevant and irrelevant operators, universality, scale separation, quasiparticles as poles, hydrodynamics as whatever survives at long wavelengths — and I've never taken a philosophy class in my life. My partner does philosophy of science, and last weekend, after I complained that a referee had dismissed my effective description as "merely phenomenological," she went and got Dennett on me. She'd been reading Darwin's Dangerous Idea and texted me the line where he says good reductionists suppose that all Design can be explained without skyhooks, while greedy reductionists suppose it can all be explained without cranes. Then she made two claims that I still can't shake: that when I write my free energy down by hand I'm using a skyhook, i.e. hoisting the explanandum into place with nothing underneath it, and that my referee, who insists it's all just electrons and the Coulomb interaction so quasiparticles aren't really real, is doing greedy reductionism. Both charges landed a bit, and I'd like to figure out whether they can even both be true.

So here's what I want: restate Dennett's position — skyhooks as explanations of design that don't build up out of lower, simpler layers, mind-first lifters with nothing supporting them from below; cranes as legitimate mechanisms that speed up the ascent without being exempt from it themselves, which for him includes natural selection and, apparently, design and culture too; and the good/greedy reductionism split — as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use. Don't quietly swap in my framework's commitments and call it translation; specifically, don't just assume "level" means "energy scale" and then congratulate yourself on how well it maps, and flag every spot where you're leaning on something RG-flavored (naturalness, the reality of the UV, the idea that effective descriptions are approximations to something better) rather than on Dennett. Then run my four moves through it one at a time and tell me which, if any, are cranes and which are skyhooks: (a) writing the Landau functional by hand with symmetry-allowed terms and fitted coefficients; (b) deriving the quasiparticle spectrum from the microscopic Hamiltonian in the limits where I can actually do that; (c) saying things like "the lattice wants to dimerize" or "this mode's job is to screen the interaction," i.e. function talk; (d) the referee's move, plus its mirror image — refusing to use hydrodynamics because it isn't fundamental, versus refusing to talk about the Hamiltonian at all because the IR doesn't care.

And then the part I actually care about: the residue report. Which of Dennett's claims translate cleanly into coarse-graining language, which ones only survive if you bend them, and which have no expression in my vocabulary whatsoever. Here are my hunches, tell me if they're right or self-serving. I think skyhook roughly means assuming the thing you're supposed to explain, or an unearned parameter tuned by hand to produce the result, which is a charge I recognize. I think greedy reductionism is roughly ignoring universality — insisting on the UV theory when the observable you're trying to account for is provably blind to it. And I think a crane is a mechanism that generates a higher-level regularity from below while itself being subject to it, with no exemption clause. But there are three places where I suspect the translation breaks, and I want you to push on them rather than paper over them. First, effective field theory explicitly licenses writing down every term the symmetry allows without knowing the UV completion, which looks exactly like what Dennett forbids — so is his objection really about claiming fundamentality or exemption rather than about un-derived posits, and if so, does the skyhook/crane pair collapse into a distinction EFT already draws more carefully? Second, are his levels scales at all, or levels of competence and organization with no length scale attached, in which case scale separation does no work for him? Third, is there anything in my vocabulary that can express the normative force of the thing — that a skyhook is forbidden, not merely false — or "function," or the fact that his cranes are historical processes rather than equilibrium states? My guess is that the last one is a genuine hole in my framework, that we have no way to talk about what a system is for or about the difference between a process and the fixed point it flows to, and that that hole is where the interesting content of his argument lives. Tell me whether that's right, or whether once you strip out the metaphor there isn't much left that I don't already say with more precision — because if there isn't, I'd really like to be able to say so at dinner without getting torn apart.
