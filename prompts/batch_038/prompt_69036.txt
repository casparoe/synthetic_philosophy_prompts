Here are my gut verdicts on big-value/tiny-probability cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, they add up to. I know there's a whole literature on whether tiny probabilities of enormous outcomes should dominate, but I want to work from my own verdicts rather than the labels.

1. The St. Petersburg game, with the payout verified as genuinely unbounded: I'd pay about $20, no more. And I don't think the fix is "no casino has that much cash" — I'd pay the same $20 to an honest god.
2. A machine that three independent labs certify will, on a button press, produce a 1-in-10^20 chance of 10^30 flourishing lives and otherwise cost you $1: push it. Obviously.
3. The mirror machine: 1-in-10^20 chance of 10^30 people dying horribly, otherwise you get $1. Also push it — that risk is just noise.
4. A government spends its entire public health budget on a 1-in-10^9 shot at a technology that would end all disease: wrong. And not just because I distrust the number — stipulate its exactly right.
5. A lab that runs a dangerous pathogen project because the small chance of a vaccine breakthrough outweighs the small chance of an escaped pandemic: also wrong, even though the arithmetic has the same shape as 3.
6. The person who gives away everything for a longshot utopia looks like a fool to me; the person who accepts some small risk of catastrophe in order to keep normal life going looks sane.
7. Being clueless about the far-reaching effects of my actions doesn't license doing nothing.

Is there one account of how probability and value interact that gets all of these, or nearly all? I most want you on 2/3 versus 4/5 — same shape, different verdicts, and I can't tell whether that's a real distinction or just me flinching. State the principle precisely enough that it makes a prediction for a case I didn't list: a lab arguing that continuing to train a model carries maybe a 1-in-1000 chance of transformative benefit and maybe a 1-in-1000 chance of catastrophic loss, which of those should drive the decision, and does your account say anything different from "compare them"? And be straight with me about which of my verdicts it forces me to drop. Please answer in American English.
