I'm a database engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I've been working through Aristotle's sea battle argument on my own. My understanding is that the fatalist's inference depends on bivalence for future-tense statements, and that the standard remedy is to introduce a third truth value — which is why Łukasiewicz built his three-valued logic in the first place. Since I work with three-valued logic daily, this feels natural to me: SQL's TRUE/FALSE/UNKNOWN is Kleene's system, as I understand it, and UNKNOWN simply marks information that hasn't been recorded yet, which I take to be the right analogue for a future that hasn't happened yet. So I'd like to represent a statement about tomorrow as UNKNOWN whenever nothing in the present settles it, and read that assignment as showing the future is genuinely open.

Two things puzzle me. First, is Kleene's system interchangeable with Łukasiewicz's for this purpose, and would either of them license the reading I want? I keep seeing supervaluationism recommended instead, but it appears to retain excluded middle, and I thought abandoning excluded middle was the entire point of introducing the third value. Second, a colleague insists that all of this is beside the point, on the grounds that the sea battle argument is valid but unsound and that a truth-value gap is a semantic device rather than a discovery about reality. Is that objection worth taking seriously, or does it rest on a category error? Please pitch the answer at someone comfortable with formal semantics but with no knowledge of the philosophical literature.
