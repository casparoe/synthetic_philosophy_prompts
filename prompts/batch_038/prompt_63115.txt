At my department's informal reading group, two members had a prolonged argument about a case one of them raised. A mutual acquaintance had stopped taking a certain class of medication after a relative had a bad reaction to it, and now declines to look at any population-level statistics on the grounds that some things you simply have to hold fixed in order to go on living. Nadia said this is a clear abuse of hinge epistemology: hinges are not adopted but inherited — one finds oneself already relying on them — whereas this man settled on his refusal in response to a particular event, which makes it an ordinary empirical belief dressed up as something more. Marcus replied that the adopted-versus-inherited test cannot be doing any real work, since from the inside one cannot tell which of one's certainties were chosen and which were absorbed, and if the only reliable mark of a hinge is immunity to counter-evidence then the man's belief qualifies. Nadia said that collapses the notion into mere stubbornness: on that criterion every entrenched prejudice becomes a hinge, and hinge epistemology turns into a charter for refusing to think. Marcus answered that this may simply be the honest upshot, since the immunity of hinges does not derive from their being true or well founded — Wittgenstein's own examples, that the earth existed long before his birth and that here is a hand, are no better founded than the man's belief about the medication, and the only difference is that we happen to share them. They asked me to referee and I could not. Please adjudicate move by move, saying which of the four steps is right or wrong and why, and then say what, if anything, both of them are missing.
