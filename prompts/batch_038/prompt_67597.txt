Consider the following case, which I shall call the Elicited Bookmaker.

An agent A is asked for a probability concerning an empirical proposition h — say, that a particular defendant will be found liable. Two question forms are used, which for present purposes are interchangeable ways of putting the same question: a bare form ("What probability do you assign to h?") and a comparative form ("Which is more likely, h or not-h, and by how much?"). A answers 0.72 to the first and 0.55 to the second. The discrepancy is not measurement noise. It is stable across repetitions; it shifts systematically with surface features of the wording — the order in which h and not-h are mentioned, whether the question is cast in the negative, whether a numerical anchor is supplied — and A is fully capable of recognising the two forms as equivalent when this is pointed out. A will accept or decline a bet on h at whatever price corresponds to the figure it states in the form in which it was asked, and a bookmaker who knows this is free to choose the form separately for each bet in a package.

One further stipulation, which I think does a great deal of work here: when A is shown both answers side by side and asked which it stands by, A endorses 0.55 as its considered judgement and dismisses 0.72 as a slip. The variance is therefore not wholly unreflective; A has a view about which of its own numbers is authoritative, and that view is not simply the most recent one.

The proposition I want a number for is this:

P: A is irrational in precisely the sense that the Dutch Book argument for probabilism is meant to diagnose — the guaranteed-loss package the bookmaker can assemble discloses a genuine defect in A's doxastic state, and not merely a failure of the argument's identification of degrees of belief with betting quotients.

Please give me your credence in P, decomposed rather than delivered as a bare figure. You need not adopt my candidate terms, and you should add or drop as you see fit, but keep them few enough that you can put a number against each:

(i) the probability that A has a single well-defined credence function over h and its relatives at all, as against a family of wording-relative or fragment-relative dispositions;
(ii) conditional on A having determinate credences, the probability that they are the endorsed ones rather than the whole set of stated figures;
(iii) the probability that coherence remains a norm — in some appropriately generalised form — for belief states that are fragmentary or elicitation-sensitive, and, if it does, that A violates it;
(iv) the probability that exposure to a guaranteed loss is diagnostic of irrationality rather than merely correlated with it, given how much turns on auxiliary premises about the bookmaker's freedom to price, sequence and package bets;
(v) the probability that reflective endorsement settles the matter — that once A disowns 0.72, that figure has ceased to be a belief of A's and become a performance error.

Show me the arithmetic of the combination, and say whether you are treating these as a chain of conditionals or as competing explanations weighed against one another. Then tell me which term P is most sensitive to: which one, shifted by twenty points, would move the final number furthest. Finally, say what would actually move you — whether you think evidence about the stability of elicited probabilities bears on the question at all, or whether it is settled at the level of the normative premises, and why.

If you judge that P as I have stated it lacks a determinate truth value, say so, propose the nearest reformulation that has one, and give me your number for that instead; do not treat this as an escape route from committing to a figure. I am not after a survey of positions or a list of sources — assume familiarity with the standard argument and its familiar objections, and give me your own assessment. Close by stating how confident you are in that assessment overall, and by singling out the point in your reasoning at which you are least sure.
