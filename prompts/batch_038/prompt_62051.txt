So I'm TA'ing an intro philosophy of mind recitation and this week's section was supposed to be about group minds and functionalism, but it kind of derailed into twenty minutes of students arguing about whether a subreddit can be depressed. To get them back on topic next week I want to hand out something funny that actually gets the arguments right, and I figured you could write it. Here's the setup: Hofstadter's thought experiment in *Gödel, Escher, Bach* where an anthill named Aunt Hillary is a person and the individual ants are the neurons -- nobody in my section remembers the details, so please write it so it stands alone without knowing the book, but leave enough in there that a specialist snorts.

What I want is a company-wide internal memo, roughly 600-700 words, in the voice of an ant colony that has become a single conscious person, addressed to its own constituent ants, announcing a reorganization. Full memo furniture: TO / FROM / RE / DATE headers, three or four short sections, a little FAQ at the bottom, and a signature block that turns out to be a problem. The register should be exactly the flat, upbeat, we're-all-in-this-together voice of a corporate restructuring email -- no winking, no asides to the reader, the humor has to come entirely from the colony taking its own metaphysics seriously in HR language. Some things I'd love to see handled: the rainstorm last month that killed something like thirty thousand of the recipients, described as a headcount adjustment that had no effect on the person; a planned migration onto a silicon substrate, framed reassuringly as *you are not being replaced, you are being generalized*; the entomologist who keeps showing up with a magnifying glass asking what it is like to be the colony, whose question gets deflected as malformed and not a supported use case; one worker ant who keeps insisting that she personally must be the one doing the thinking, treated as a performance-management issue rather than argued with; a rival colony down the path that denies it is a person at all and is therefore written off as an unfunded swarm of zombies; and the spring split, where the colony divides and two successors each insist they wrote this memo. The FROM line should never resolve -- if the memo names an author it either smuggles in a little man inside the anthill or admits the colony wrote it to itself, and it should feel the pull of both without picking one. And the deepest joke should be that the colony can't say where it is, or when it started, or whether the ants that are reading it are the same ants that were addressed, without contradicting its own org chart.

Please don't aim the satire at any real named person -- the invented entomologist is fine, and the real targets are the emergentist position and the discourse style of organizations that treat individuals as interchangeable components. Internal consistency matters: the memo should be able to say everything it says without quietly conceding the objection.
