i run a small letterpress print shop and i've been working my way through Gödel, Escher, Bach very slowly for about a year now — my math stopped at high school algebra, so i get stuck in places and reread. the part i keep circling back to is the Typogenetics business, where his made-up strands of A's and C's and G's and T's get read out into enzymes that then go act on other strands, and then he sets that side by side with the arithmetic one where a formula can wind up containing the number that encodes it. i think i understand each half on its own. what i cant get a handle on is the seam.

here's my itch, and it may just be me being dense: as far as i can tell, nothing in a strand is ever about anything, and nothing on the arithmetic side ever mentions the strand. the two only line up if somebody is standing outside both systems holding them up to the light and noticing the match. so when people say something like a living thing is a formula that talks about itself, i wonder whether all the work is being done by the person saying it, and whether there's no self-reference inside the thing at all. related worry from my day job: i set a forme of type, print it, then distribute the letters back into the case — the letters never once knew they were spelling anything.

so: write me a pantoum about this, five or six quatrains, keeping the usual rule that the second and fourth lines of each stanza come back as the first and third of the next, and closing by bringing the poem's very first line back as its last line, so the strand ends where it started. i want one level of the poem to be the dumb typographical one — letters, copying, ink, the shop, type cases and the composing stick and distributing type back into the box, nothing about anything — and the other level to be the numeral one, where a number shows up inside the very string that codes for it. the point of the form is that the recycling does the work: a line that entered as inert marks should come back around in a new position where it's functioning as a claim about itself, without the words changing. and the last two lines should land so it's genuinely undecidable whether the loop closed on its own or only because a reader happened to be there watching it close — don't settle it for me, don't tip the scale either way.

a few constraints. don't use the words strange loop, self-reference, isomorphism, meaning, or Gödel anywhere in the poem — the form and the images have to carry them instead. it should still read as a decent poem about a print shop to somebody who's never heard of any of this. and if my way of describing the arithmetic is sloppy (it probably is), write the poem to the accurate version rather than to my version.

format: give me the poem first, with no title above it, then a single section titled "How the form does the work" — call it 200 words or so — going through stanza by stanza to say what each pair of carried-over lines is doing, and flagging every place you had to bend the pantoum rules, the biology, or the logic to make a line land.
