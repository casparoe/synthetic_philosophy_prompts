ok so I've been arguing with a friend — she's bioethics-adjacent, does transplant stuff — about when someone is dead, and after about three years of this I want to try the thing where instead of another thread we actually produce a document. Can you write it for us?

The two parties: a defender of the whole-brain criterion, who holds that death is the irreversible loss of the integrative unity of the organism as a whole and that brainstem-centered neurology is what tracks that; and a higher-brain advocate, who holds that death is the irreversible loss of the capacity for consciousness, so that a permanently unconscious body is already a corpse even if it's warm, ventilated, and growing.

Structure it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table with separate columns giving the whole-brain wording and the higher-brain wording of the same dispute, then the cruxes.

The agreed claims should be non-trivial. My sense is they can actually sign onto a lot — most of the physiology, what the bedside tests do and don't establish, the fact that neither criterion is the same as the cessation of every cell's metabolism — and, importantly, onto the point that irreversible as both of them use it is indexed to what technology is available. I've read way too much cryonics writing and I think the honest distinction they're both sliding over is the one between no physically possible process could restore it and nobody is actually going to try to restore it. If both camps collapse that, I want it written down as a shared concession, not as a gotcha against one of them.

For the disagreements, phrase each one so its own side would accept the wording and neither gets strawmanned. Include the persistent vegetative state case explicitly, since that's where the two criteria come apart in practice and where this stops being an academic exercise, and include whatever the honest disagreement is about donation-after-circulatory-death protocols and the waiting period before circulatory death is declared.

Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would actually move somebody. For each one, name the case or argument or empirical finding both sides would accept as bearing on it, and write down both parties' predictions in advance, pre-registration style, so we can tell later who updated and who didn't. Candidates I'd want considered:

- whether continued somatic integration in these bodies — wound healing, growth, hormonal regulation, and the documented cases of pregnant women declared brain-dead whose pregnancies were carried to delivery — counts as the organism functioning as a whole or as a bunch of organs being run by a ventilator, and what observation would actually discriminate between those two descriptions
- whether a criterion of death has to vindicate the dead donor rule, or whether both sides can accept that the rule might just be false while keeping their criterion intact
- how much of this fight is about which property the word picks out versus which property we should care about, and whether that makes part of it verbal

Last bit: don't adjudicate. Nobody wins by the end. The test is whether people who still disagree could both put their names on it, so if you find yourself writing something only one of them would sign, fix it or move it into the cruxes.
