I run a small furniture workshop, and I have to choose a lumber supplier for the coming year. The incumbent publishes ten years of delivery records: roughly nine orders in ten arrive complete and on schedule. The cheaper newcomer has no record I can inspect — a trade contact says they seem solid, and that is the whole of it. I keep paying the premium to stay with the incumbent.

My co-owner calls this flatly irrational. Either I believe the newcomer is more likely to fail, in which case I should be able to say by how much and price the difference, or I do not, in which case the cheaper bid wins. He adds that I accept unquantifiable chances constantly: last year I hired a shop foreman with no idea how he would turn out, and I did not agonize over that at all.

What I cannot explain to him is the sequel. The newcomer then offered terms that dominate the incumbent's on any reading I can write down — full refund plus a penalty on any late delivery — so that I am at least as well off in every outcome I can describe and better off in most. I still declined, and I still think declining was right.

Is one of us confused? Tell me what, if anything, separates my attitude toward the supplier from my attitude toward the foreman, and whether turning down the dominant offer is defensible or merely a mood I have dressed up as judgment. State how confident you are in your verdict, and name the part of it you are least sure of.
