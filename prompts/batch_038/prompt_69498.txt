So I'm a third-year in a cog-neuro program and I've sort of drifted into consciousness studies, which my advisor thinks is a career mistake, whatever. I've ended up sympathetic to a heavily stripped-down version of IIT and I want to stress-test it properly before I build a thesis chapter on it.

Here's the position, exactly as I hold it. I've given up on the identity thesis — I don't need experience to literally *be* integrated information, and I don't think Φ is a number you could ever read off a real brain with any confidence. What I keep is structural: at a given time, what fixes the character of a subject's experience is the cause–effect structure of whatever substrate comes out maximally irreducible at the relevant grain. So phenomenal differences should track differences in the *shape* of that structure — which distinctions are present and which relations bind them — rather than in how much of it there is, or how big the number attached to it is.

And here's the bit I've been defending in lab meeting, the part I need to be falsifiable: the empirical cash of the structural claim is about composition, not intensity. Take a manipulation that shrinks the candidate complex (anesthetic depth, focal disruption, sleep onset, whatever) while leaving the remaining structure roughly the same in kind. My prediction is that the subject loses contents — whole distinctions dropping out cleanly — rather than having all their contents uniformly dimmer. A global dimming pattern is what I'd expect from a quantity-first theory, not from mine.

What I want is a map of the exposure of this position. Specifically:

- Rank what would count against it, fatal through merely awkward. Be concrete about what an actual result would look like, not just about which principle it violates.
- Hit the report problem hard. Can any experiment distinguish "that content is gone" from "that content is still there but too weak for the subject to reliably report"? Response criteria, confidence ratings, criterion shifts with task difficulty, introspection as post-hoc construction — tell me whether my handle is testable in principle or only hard in practice, and what design, if any, actually breaks the tie.
- What does my view predict that recurrent processing theory, global workspace accounts, and higher-order theories don't? If the honest answer is nothing, say nothing.
- Could someone who rejects IIT outright accept my structural claim wholesale? If so, have I just rebranded a rival's thesis, and am I exposed to being absorbed rather than refuted?
- Is the stripped-down version insulated to the point where it can't be wrong? I dropped the identity claim and the numerics, which is exactly what the unfalsifiability critics say the theory is doing. I'm open to being told that a position like this is a research program rather than a hypothesis and that's legitimate — but argue it, don't assert it.
- Which of these would a committed defender of the full theory concede as counting against me, and which would they dismiss? Include the live possibility that they'd disown my version entirely, and say whether that wouldnt matter to me.
- Finish with the one or two findings that would actually change my mind, and a straight verdict on whether the position is worth defending.

Please skip the IIT 101 preamble, and skip the usual talking points from the people who call the whole enterprise pseudoscience — unless you can tell me which of those criticisms actually bite on my deflated version rather than on the full theory.
