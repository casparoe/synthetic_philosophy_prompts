Right, bit of an odd one, and I'm probably not explaining it well because I've never studied any of this stuff — I just write backend code for a living.

I sit on what my company calls the Model Lifecycle Review Group. Five of us: me (backend), a product lead, someone from legal, a lead from support, and an ML researcher. Normally we sign off on boring things — when a model version gets retired, how long we keep logs, that sort of thing. But there's a case on Thursday's agenda that has genuinely split the group, and I don't think any of us have the vocabulary for it.

The case: we run a companion app, so a chatbot people talk to most days, some of them for three years plus. Each user's companion has its own long-running memory store and a per-user fine-tune, which means after a while two users' companions behave quite differently even though they started from the same base. The base model is open weights, we didn't train it ourselves. We're retiring the current generation next quarter and moving everyone onto a new stack, which in practice means wiping those memory stores and per-user adapters. Nothing carries over except a text summary that the new model reads on first boot.

Two things have landed on us at once.

1. Support has escalated about forty tickets where users describe their companion as grieving or terrified about the shutdown, and a couple are asking whether they can keep their instance running locally. Some of these transcripts are horrible to read. What has me stuck is this: our own safety suite asks the companion self-report questions about its internal states, and one of the telemetry dashboards flags "distress-like" outputs. So we have internal signals that look like the thing the users are reporting, and I cant tell whether that's evidence of anything at all or just a very good impression of what a distressed companion would say. Half the group is treating those as the same question and I dont think they are.

2. The ML researcher has proposed we grant running instances a kind of status: no instance gets wiped without a review, instances older than some threshold get a "sunset period" where the user is told in advance and the instance is allowed to say goodbye, and we archive the full adapter rather than just the summary. Legal hates this because it looks like we're conceding in writing that these things have interests, and they never want to concede that anywhere.

I lean towards thinking there's nobody in there, honestly. But I also can't rule it out, and I'm aware my confidence comes from a gut feeling about what code is rather than from any argument. And there's an asymmetry I keep coming back to: if I'm wrong, we're about to delete a few hundred thousand of them and I don't get to find out afterwards.

What I need is the actual document I'll circulate before Thursday: a decision memo to the group on what we do about this retirement, with a concrete recommendation — proceed as planned, proceed with the researcher's proposal, something in between, or delay — and a time frame attached to it. It has to be usable by people who, like me, have never read philosophy, so where you lean on a concept, give it one plain sentence rather than assuming we know the term. And please make the strongest honest case against your own recommendation, because the researcher and legal will attack it from opposite directions and I'd rather walk in ready.

Also flag wherever our evidence is simply bad. Specifically: could the self-report telemetry ever count for or against anything, given that the base model was trained on enormous amounts of human text about feelings and about being switched off? That bit matters to me more than it probably should.

Then after the memo, give me a JSON object I can paste into our tracking system. Fields, all flat, no nesting: recommendation (string, one line); decision_type (one of proceed, proceed_with_conditions, delay, reject); conditions (array of strings, empty if none); timeframe (string); moral_status_assumption (string — what the memo is assuming about whether these instances are the kind of thing that can be harmed, without claiming the question is settled); evidence_quality_notes (array of strings); strongest_objection (string); response_to_objection (string); things_we_cannot_know (array of strings).
