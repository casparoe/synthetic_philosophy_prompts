I have taught philosophy of mind for some years, and for much of that time I have run a seminar on Dennett's *Consciousness Explained*. Across essays, lab meetings and the empirical literature I keep encountering a pattern for which I have never found a settled name, and I should like your assessment of whether one exists.

The pattern is this. A theorist explicitly and sincerely repudiates the picture of consciousness as something that happens at a particular place and a particular moment in the head — the picture Dennett derides as a theatre, and the doctrine he labels Cartesian materialism — and yet in their own positive work the repudiated picture returns, not as a claim they would defend under questioning, but as a structural feature of their notation, their diagrams and their experimental design. The repudiation is at the level of doctrine; the reinstatement is at the level of practice.

Three instances from my own reading. First, a colleague who devotes a chapter to arguing that there is no finish line beyond which processing becomes experienced nonetheless reports results in the standard form: the subject became aware of the colour change some three hundred milliseconds after onset. The latency is never offered as a hypothesis; it is baked into the reporting convention, and it is precisely the finish line the chapter denies. Second, a perceptual researcher whose figures show parallel modular processing converging on a single arrow labelled 'conscious access', with everything upstream of the arrow unreportable and everything downstream reportable; when I put this to her, the reply was that the arrow is a diagrammatic convenience, yet the masking paradigm, the threshold procedure and the criterion for a trial counting as 'seen' are all calibrated to it, so the arrow carries methodological weight and not merely pictorial weight. Third, and most telling, a philosopher who insists there is no fact of the matter as to whether the subject of the phi illusion experienced the colour change and then misremembered it, or never experienced it at all — but who, when pressed, will not say the question is ill-posed, and continues to weigh evidence for one side rather than the other.

So: is there an established concept for the practical reinstatement, in instruments and notation, of a picture one has formally rejected? A bare label would not satisfy me, and if there is no label I should prefer a coinage with a working definition to a shrug. What I want is the carving. Take the nearest neighbours and say why each of them fails to fit exactly: performative contradiction; the homunculus fallacy; the theory-ladenness of observation; tacit or implicit commitment; instrumental fictionalism and the deliberate 'talking as if' of a known-useless picture; the general notion of residual theoretical commitment. The differences matter in both directions. A performative contradiction concerns what an assertion commits one to, and here nothing need be asserted for the pattern to appear. The homunculus fallacy concerns an inner agent, whereas what returns here is a time and a place rather than a person — which is why I suspect the existing vocabulary misses it.

Two further requirements. Since this charge can be levelled at almost anyone, including whoever levels it, give me criteria that separate genuine residue from innocent notational convenience, and make them criteria that could in principle be failed, so that the accusation is falsifiable rather than a universal solvent. And consider whether the residue is necessarily a vice: if a theatre-flavoured architecture of the global workspace variety is what actually unifies and predicts the data, then the return of the picture may be evidence that it tracks something real, and the phenomenon I have described would be a discovery arriving by the back door rather than a failure of nerve. Tell me which of these you think it is.

Begin with your bottom line in the first sentence — either the name of the concept or a clear statement that there is none — and then set out your reasoning. No preamble, and please do not restate my question back to me.
