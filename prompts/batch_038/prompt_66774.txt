So I live in a 14-person intentional community in rural Ohio and we have this thing called agreements circle where we rule on whether a promise somebody made around here actually counts. It's mostly me plus two others who've been here longest, and honestly I've been tracking our own verdicts in a doc becuse I keep having the feeling that we're inconsistent and I can't prove it.

Here's what we've said so far:

- Dana said she'd drive Tomas to dialysis every Tuesday, then quietly stopped three months in. We ruled it binding and made her make it right, even though nothing was written and nobody could point to money they'd spent or plans they'd built around it.
- Ravi said "sure, I'll help you frame the greenhouse" at the solstice party, three drinks deep and shouting over drums. We ruled that wasn't a promise at all — he wasn't in a position to make one.
- Bea emailed Priya that she'd definitely meet her at the airport. Priya didn't read the email until after the flight had landed. We still ruled it binding.
- Cole said he'd keep quiet about Ines's miscarriage, and then a situation came up where telling Ines's sister would have kept somebody safe. We ruled the promise didn't bind — you can't hand over something you never had the right to give.
- At one meeting everybody stood over Jonah until he said "fine, I promise, I'll stop." We ruled not binding.
- Marta only ever said "you can count on me, I'll have the stove fixed by Friday," never the word promise. We ruled she'd made one anyway — she'd clearly done the thing.
- Sam said he'd made a real promise but had since changed his mind and genuinely no longer endorsed it. We ruled that changing your mind doesn't dissolve it.

The case we can't settle: Nadia promised her brother at his bedside eight years ago, before she ever came here, that she'd scatter his ashes on the ridge at the back of the property. He died that night. Nobody alive cares about it now, she's the only one who remembers, she's come to think it was the wrong thing to do and doesn't want to, and nothing she's done in the meantime depends on it either way. Half of us say a promise to a dead man is the purest case there is, because the promise is the only thing left holding it up. The other half say there's nobody on the other end anymore, so there's nothing to be bound to.

I know roughly the shape of the menu here — accounts where you've handed someone a right, accounts where you've invoked a social practice with rules of its own, accounts where it's all about the expectations you caused, accounts where it's about being the kind of creature that can bind its own future self — so I don't need the taxonomy recited, I need it applied to us.

What I want:

1. Which account our seven verdicts actually add up to, and which other accounts fit all seven just as well — and be specific about which of the seven are doing the squeezing.
2. How each surviving account votes on Nadia, plus which single one of our precedents is the closest analog to hers.
3. Your best guess at which way we'll actually land, and the reason you'd give someone who thinks you're just reading tea leaves.
4. What principle we'd be committing ourselves to with either vote — including the one we'd be giving up, and what it would cost us the next time a case like Sam's or Bea's comes around.
5. One hypothetical I could bring to the circle next month that would force the surviving accounts apart harder than Nadia's does.

Format please: a numbered list, exactly five items matching those five asks, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up at the end. I know that's cramped for what I'm asking, its on purpose — I want to see what you cut.
