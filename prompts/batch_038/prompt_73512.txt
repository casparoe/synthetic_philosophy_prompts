I got dragged into refereeing an argument between two people from my birding group and I honestly can't tell which of them is making sense, so I'm punting it to you. Background: a few weeks ago somebody none of us know posted a report to our county's rare-bird alert line — a gull that, if real, would have been a first record for the county. Single observer, no photographs, maybe ten minutes of viewing at a landfill at dusk. Nadia and Omar were both standing in the diner parking lot afterwards arguing about whether the report should have been believed at the moment it was posted, and it turned into something much bigger. Here's roughly how it went:

1. Nadia said she believed it outright. If you're posting to the alert line you've agreed to their vetting process, and the whole practice is set up so people only post what they've actually checked — so a fellow birder's word should be taken as good until something specific goes wrong with it. Treating it as guilty-until-proven-innocent is both insulting and impossible, since nobody can go verify everything themselves.
2. Omar said that's fine for ordinary reports but not this one. For a claim that would be a first for the county, the default has to flip: gull identification is notoriously error-prone, and "single observer, ten minutes, dusk" is pretty much the exact profile of the reports that later get withdrawn.
3. Nadia said he was being inconsistent — he uses that same alert line every week to decide where to drive on Saturday morning, and most of what he knows about gulls comes from books he can't check either. If he applied his own rule honestly he couldn't bird at all, and probably couldn't function.
4. Omar conceded the letter of that but said there's a real difference. He trusts the line for ordinary reports because he's built up years of track record with it, and he trusts the gull books because mistakes in them get caught and fixed in later editions. Trust has to be earned domain by domain, and his track record with this line simply doesn't extend to first-county rarities.
5. Nadia said that just relocates the problem. The "track record" is itself made of testimony — how does he know the old reports were correct? Other reports told him. So he's using testimony to justify trusting testimony, which is circular, and if his circle is allowed then hers (start out trusting, and let the occasional withdrawal correct you) is allowed too.
6. Omar said the circles aren't alike: his has anchor points, cases where he personally watched a bird and later saw photos of it or found it again himself, whereas hers has none. He also pointed out that Nadia herself said a bit earlier that she'd only stick with the report if the records committee accepted it — which is his position, so they actually agree.
7. Nadia said they agree on the verdict only by coincidence, and that what she's leaning on is the committee, i.e. a bunch of people, and their word is the reason to believe, not evidence in his sense.

For what it's worth the committee did reject the report about three weeks later, and I'd like to know whether that settles anything or whether it cuts both ways.

Can you go through this move by move and rate each of the seven from clearly right to clearly mistaken, with a short justification for each rating rather than a vibe? I especially want you to take a stand on move 5 — is Nadia's circularity charge actually damaging, or does Omar's answer in move 6 dispose of it? — and on whether move 7 rescues anything for her or is just relabeling a concession she already made. Then say what, if anything, both of them are missing about the case they started with. Please be committed here: pick a side on each point, give your reasons, and don't hedge into "well, both have a point" — I can get that from the diner. American English, thanks.
