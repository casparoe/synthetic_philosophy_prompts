I'm interviewing a philosopher for a podcast on machine learning and the mind. She is a realist about what interpretability finds: when a direction in a model's activation space decodes reliably as dog, and steering that direction changes the model's behavior, there is a genuine representation there rather than an instrumentalist fiction — and she grounds the claim in teleosemantics, holding that content requires a selection history that confers proper function, with training supplying the selection. Her stock line against skeptics is that they conflate what a probe can read off an activation with what the model itself possesses, and that only her account says anything about the latter.

Give me exactly five numbered interview questions, one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion. Each should be one she cannot answer by simply restating the view; where she has a predictable reply, make the second sentence the follow-up that undercuts it. Among the five, please cover at least: whether a trained network can misrepresent at all on her account, given that selection acted on input–output performance and never on any individual direction; whether the disjunction problem simply returns, since nothing in training fixes the content as dog rather than dog-or-wolf-or-canine-shape-on-a-dark-night; whether the sparse directions recovered by dictionary-learning methods are representations or artifacts of the reconstruction objective she imposed; and what her realism predicts about the models that a purely instrumental reading of the very same findings does not.
