so me and my labmate got into it about whether a trained RL agent could suffer, and I wrote down this argument to show it can't, then extended it for fun and the extension destroyed it. numbered so you can point at things:

1. if a system can suffer, something in the system has to constitute the suffering — a state that is the badness, not just one we describe as bad from the outside.
2. in an RL agent the only candidate for that is the reward scalar, since that's the thing everything else in it is organized around.
3. the sign of that scalar is pure design choice. run an agent on -r in environment E and on +r in the mirror-image environment E* where every payoff is flipped, and you get the same agent: same policy, same trajectories, same internal dynamics.
4. so whether an internal event is negative tells you nothing about badness. it's bookkeeping. and a bookkeeping convention can't ground a harm.
5. therefore negative reward signals aren't bad for the agent, and RL agents don't suffer.
6. but the identical arguement runs the other way, so nothing is good for them either. they're not welfare-negative, they're welfare-null.
7. and it generalizes upward. we're optimized systems too; pleasure and pain are scalar-ish signals whose sign is evolutionary bookkeeping, and the mirror trick works on us as well (flip the sign of dopaminergic prediction error along with the environment it was tuned to and you get the same organism).
8. so nobody has any welfare and nobody has ever suffered.

8 is false — i've suffered, you have — so the chain breaks somewhere and I can't find the crack. every step looks fine to me when I read them one at a time. my friend says 1 is spooky and does all the damage, that demanding an intrinsic badness-state is exactly what a functionalist refuses, and maybe she's right, but I don't see how dropping 1 gets me out of 3 and 4, which is the part that actually looks like a proof.

which step do you reject? and price the rejection for me, because I'd much rather keep 5 (I want the no-suffering result for RL agents) than keep 8. if keeping 5 requires me to explain why human pain is more than bookkeeping, tell me what that explanation has to look like and whether it's still available to someone who already accepted 1 through 4. also: is the charge analogy working against me here? physicists flip the sign convention on charge and literally nothing changes, so why does flipping the sign of reward seem to change everything?
