hey, so i co-run a reading group that's roughly half philosophy grad students and half ML people who do causal discovery, and next session is on causation. i already know how it'll go: everyone shows up assuming that Lewis-style counterfactual dependence and Woodward-style interventionism are the same theory in different notation, and they aren't, or at least they aren't obviously.

what i want is a decision tree i can print on one handout page that starts from something the ML people will already grant and walks down to a named position about what causation is.

starting point, roughly: "i've fit a DAG to observational data, it satisfies the markov condition given my variable set, and its do-calculus predictions match what the randomized trials show." nobody should have to already hold a metaphysics of causation to answer the first question.

what i need in it:

- between 8 and 12 branch nodes, each phrased as a question someone can answer without knowing which leaf it leads to. nothing like "are you a counterfactualist?" or "do you think interventions are primitive?" — that just sorts people by what they call themselves, which is the opposite of the point.
- at least half the nodes should be concrete cases in a sentence or two rather than abstractions, and the cases should be the ones where the two families actually diverge: preemption plus the switching and trumping variants; whether you need counterfactual dependence at all for causation; whether the interventions a causal claim quantifies over have to be physically possible or just well-defined enough to evaluate; whether they have to be surgical (leaving everything else alone) or can be messy and still ground a true causal claim; whether causal claims come out relative to how you coarse-grained your variables; whether omissions and absences get to be causes; whether causation is transitive down a chain; and whether the whole thing is offered as a reductive analysis or only as an explication of causal talk we were already having.
- leaves, at minimum: Lewis-style counterfactual dependence including whatever you do with the miracle-laden similarity metric; interventionism read as an explication rather than a metaphysics; interventionism read as a metaphysics; causation as invariance under interventions; and at least one leaf that isn't counterfactualist at all (conserved-quantity/transfer, process theory, singularist or primitivist causation, your pick). where a combination the tree generates isn't occupied by anybody, write "unnamed" and diagnose why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
- one sentence per leaf on its most awkward commitment. not a summary of the view — the thing that hurts.
- explicitly flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate, and say why. i suspect at least one pair of interventionist variants is going to collapse and i'd rather see that admitted than papered over.

no citations, no reading list, no footnotes, no "for further reading" section. names of views and their defenders are fine where you need them, but don't make me go look anything up in order to use the handout. indented outline or ascii tree, whichever fits a page better.
