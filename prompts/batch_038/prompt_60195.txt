I am drafting a response to a consultation on how a public streaming platform ought to weight audience metrics in its arts-funding recommendations, and one of the submissions leans heavily on Tolstoy. Its argument, in outline, runs as follows. Tolstoy held in *What Is Art?* that art is the transmission of feeling, and that he wrote: "Art is a human activity consisting in this, that one man consciously, by means of certain external signs, hands on to others feelings he has lived through, and that other people are infected by these feelings and also experience them." The submission then observes that Tolstoy also held that the degree of infectiousness is the sole measure of a work's excellence as art, and concludes that completion rates, replays and share-velocity are therefore not a vulgar substitute for judgement about artistic merit but a direct measurement of the very property Tolstoy made central. A second step follows: since Tolstoy appraised transmitted feelings by whether they serve the well-being of humanity, the platform is entitled to demote works that are highly infectious yet transmit harmful feelings — on this reading the epidemiology licenses quarantine.

I want an audit of the infection analogy itself. I am less concerned with whether Tolstoy was right than with how much weight his metaphor can bear when it is made to carry a measurement programme and a censorship policy.

Please address the following:

1. **The correspondence table.** What in the platform ecosystem plays the role of the pathogen, the carrier, the vector, the susceptible host, the dose, virulence, the incubation period, immunity, the epidemic curve, and quarantine? Be explicit wherever a slot has no natural occupant, or has several competing ones.

2. **What the source smuggles in.** Identify the features of epidemiology that the analogy imports and the target lacks — involuntary uptake, non-cognitive transmission, replication of the agent itself, dose–response, host homogeneity, the presumption that rate of spread tracks either harm or benefit. For each, say whether the mismatch is fatal to the submission's use or merely decorative.

3. **Tolstoy's own constraints on his metaphor, which the submission appears to have skipped.** He excluded direct involuntary contagion from art altogether — causing another to yawn when one cannot help yawning, or to laugh or weep at the sight of one's own laughter or tears — on the grounds that art begins only where a feeling is deliberately re-experienced and expressed by external signs. He also gave three conditions governing the *degree* of infectiousness: the individuality of the feeling transmitted, the clearness of its transmission, and the sincerity of the artist, the last of these the chief, since it entails the other two. What becomes of a metric built on reach once these are in play? Is any part of Tolstoyan infectiousness observable at population scale, or is the whole of it a property of single encounters between one work and one receiver — and if so, what does that do to the inference from aggregate numbers to merit?

4. **Whether the two-axis structure survives translation.** Tolstoy separated a work's quality *as art* (its infectiousness) from the worth of the feelings it transmits, the latter judged by what he called the religious perception of the age. A single engagement figure collapses the two. Does that collapse serve the submission or defeat it?

5. **The quarantine inference.** Tolstoy's mechanism of appraisal was condemnation, esteem and the deliberate choice of what to encourage, not the institutional removal of carriers from circulation. Does the epidemiological framing license a step Tolstoy did not take, and does the analogy generate that step on its own — that is, would the policy look different if art had been compared to something else?

6. **Verdicts, and a replacement.** State separately whether each of the two conclusions survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether either could stand without the analogy carrying it. Consider also whether Tolstoy's account of what had corrupted the art of his own day — payment, professionalisation, criticism and art schools — cuts for or against using platform metrics at all. Finally, if the infection analogy fails for this purpose, propose a better one (Tolstoy himself compares art to speech, which transmits thoughts rather than feelings) and say what the better analogy would license that this one does not.
