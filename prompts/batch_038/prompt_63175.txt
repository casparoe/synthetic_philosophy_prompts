So my reading group has been fighting about Hofstadter's careenium for close to a month now and I've decided the only way out is to make the two sides write the document they should have produced a long time ago. Here's the setup as I'm using it, stripped down to the bones: a sealed box containing nothing but simmballs — little swarms of hard particles bouncing off each other and the walls according to a fixed microscopic law. Some of the swarms are dense, stable, self-perpetuating configurations; call those symbols. A symbol is nothing over and above its particles, and if you knew the full micro-state plus the law you could in principle compute every subsequent collision. No ghost, no extra force, no second rulebook. Nothing exists in the box but the particles and the law.

Camp A says: in the careenium, symbols genuinely push the substrate around. When one symbol smacks into another and something comes of it, part of the correct causal story is told at the symbol level — which symbol it was, what role it played in the economy of the swarm — and that story is not just a lazy redescription of the particle story. Downward causation is real here and it isn't spooky.

Camp B says: only particles push particles. Every collision is exhausted by trajectories plus the law. The symbol level is pattern-finding after the fact — great for compression, great for us, but it never does any causal work of it's own, and what looks like pushing from the top is pushing from the bottom seen through a coarse-graining lens.

Draft the joint statement both camps could sign after a real adversarial collaboration. I want:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims. Both sides sign every one, and none of them may be vacuous — no "we both agree the careenium is complicated." I'm thinking substantive stuff like: the micro-law is complete; the same symbol type can be realized by many distinct swarms; some symbol-level counterfactuals come out stably true while the corresponding particle-level ones are fragile; symbol-level generalizations survive changes that wreck any particular micro-generalization. If one side only accepts a claim with a gloss attached, write the gloss down right there rather than papering over it.

2. A disagreements table: the issue, then Camp A's formulation in words A would sign, then Camp B's formulation in words B would sign, then one neutral sentence saying what actually differs. Each side has to be able to read its own row and say yes, that's me.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one camp. For each, give a concrete case or experiment inside the careenium that both sides agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction written down in advance. I want at least one crux to be about whether an interventionist test is even available here, since any intervention on a symbol just is a rearrangement of particles, and I want the table to show whether that objection sinks the test or gets absorbed.

4. A stipulated variant both camps have to address, because I think it's the one place they can actually come apart: the noisy careenium. Same box, same symbols, but the micro-law now includes a tiny random kick at every collision — small enough that it never breaks up a stable symbol in fewer than a few thousand collisions, big enough that exact micro-prediction beyond a handful of collisions is impossible even in principle. Symbol-level regularities survive anyway: same symbol-type collisions keep yielding the same kinds of outcomes, and they keep holding across swarms that realize that symbol type in totally different ways. Does the symbol level earn any causal credit in the noisy careenium that it didn't have in the clean one? Get A's answer and B's answer down as predictions they'd be embarrassed to retract later, and have the statement say whether this variant is a genuine crux or whether B swallows it too.

5. A short section on what each camp would count as evidence against its own position — not a strawman, something that would actually keep them up at night — and whether there's any single thing both agree would settle it.

6. Honesty about words. My suspicion is that a big chunk of this is a fight over "cause." If part of the dispute is verbal, say which part, say what both camps agree to say instead, and then tell me what's left standing once the verbal part is stripped out. If nothing is left, say that plainly and explain why two smart people were shouting at each other for a month anyway.

No bibliography, no reading list, no citations — I can't check them and I don't want them. And don't adjudicate: I'm not asking who's right. I want the document itself, in a form where both camps would put their names at the bottom.
