Been reading the rationalist blogosphere since before LessWrong was a thing, and one of the bits of philosophy that's kept nagging at me for years is Frege's puzzle about identity statements. Not because I think it's deep, but because I've never been able to shake the feeling that the whole debate is happening one level up from where the actual problem lives.

Here's the thought I want you to work with. Take the morning star / evening star case. The usual gloss is that "Hesperus is Hesperus" and "Hesperus is Phosphorus" differ in cognitive value because the names have different senses — different modes of presentation, sitting out there in Frege's third realm, objective and shareable. But notice what actually differs when you run the two sentences through a real agent with finite time and finite memory. Getting from "Hesperus = Hesperus" to anything costs nothing; getting from "Hesperus = Phosphorus" to the rest of your astronomy costs a search of your stored knowledge, maybe some empirical work, maybe years. So: what if sense just *is* that? Individuate senses by what the agent's implementation actually does with an expression — two expressions have the same sense for an agent iff swapping one for the other never changes what inferences it can draw, how much work those inferences take, or how confident it ends up being. Sense becomes a compute budget, not a mode of presentation. It's frankly psychological, frankly implementation-level, and it's the sort of thing Frege would have called psychologismus and thrown out of the window.

Write me an essay that takes this seriously as a *solution* to the puzzle and decides whether it works. Not a survey — an argument with a verdict.

Things I'd like it to actually wrestle with:

- Does it solve the puzzle or just relocate it? It explains why the two identity statements differ in cognitive value, but does it explain why "Hesperus = Phosphorus" is *informative* in the right way, rather than making every belief report about a trivially true identity into a claim about processing speed?
- What happens to the publicity of thought? If senses are pinned to implementations, then no two people ever grasp the same thought, and neither do you at 9am and you when you're tired. Frege needed thoughts to be objective and shareable — that's the whole reason he hated psychologism. Can this account recover disagreement, agreement, and communication at all, or is that a fatal cost?
- Granularity. Beliefs come out absurdly fine-grained on this view. Is that obviously a bug, or is there something right about it?
- How does it rub up against the usual suspects — Kripke's anti-descriptivist arguments, the necessary/a posteriori split, two-dimensional semantics, Millianism-plus-pragmatics? I want to know whether this is a rival to those positions or something that could sit underneath one of them.

I don't want a "there are considerations on both sides" ending. Tell me whether sense-as-compute-budget is a position someone could genuinely hold, and if it collapses, say which objection does the killing. A qualified verdict is completely fine — "it works for the informativeness puzzle but not for belief reports" is a real answer — as long as you commit to where the qualification sits.

No need for citations, a bibliography or a reading list; I'm after the reasoning, not the references. And at the end, tell me how confident you are in the main claims you've made, and pick out the single step you're least sure about — the point where you'd most expect an actual philosopher of language to say "no, you've just got that wrong".
