So I'm prepping a session for a grad seminar on Arendt and I need a case built to order, not a summary. The class has already swallowed the standard "post-truth" reading — lies destroy shared reality, therefore radical transparency is the fix — and about three of them are transparency maximalists. I want a scenario that breaks the transparency intuition using Arendt's own machinery rather than the usual filter-bubble/algorithms story, because they've heard that one a hundred times.

Here's what I need the case to do:

- Setting: a small contemporary political body, realistic scale — a city council, a regional transit or health authority, a school board, your pick. No science fiction, no platform or feed doing the work in the background, no bots.
- Nobody in the case says anything false. Not one false assertion, and no relevant document is withheld: everything gets published, on time, in full, including the boring stuff. There should be a full-disclosure event late in the story where the whole record is dumped at once.
- And yet after that disclosure event the body is worse off than it would have been under a regime of one good old-fashioned cover-up lie. The key thing: the disclosure doesn't repair anything, because there is nothing hidden underneath for it to reveal.
- The mechanism has to be specifically Arendtian. My reading of the Pentagon Papers essay is that what was being deceived about there was mostly plans, intentions, projections, and how things would look — material about the future that was never a fact in the first place — and that's why exposure was so unsatisfying. I want the case to run entirely on that kind of material, plus whatever the factual-truth essay says about why facts are more fragile than the truths of reason. If the harm in your case could be traced to somebody misbelieving a well-documented fact, the case fails.
- The scenario itself should be one tight paragraph. Then the analysis, separately.
- Don't stipulate the verdict. I should be able to see the degradation in the details — what people can no longer say to each other, what arguments become unavailable, what the disclosure event actually produces — rather than being told at the end that "nobody knew what was real anymore." That last move is exactly the cheating I'm trying to avoid.

After the paragraph, do the demonstration:

1. Map each load-bearing element of the case onto the claim in Arendt it's supposed to instantiate, including the distinction between concealment-type lying (which leaves the fact intact and can in principle be undone by disclosure) and whatever is happening here instead.
2. Removal test: swap the projections-and-intentions material for a single concrete hidden fact — say a suppressed report — and keep everything else identical. Does the case collapse into ordinary concealment, and does the disclosure event now do its reparative work? Tell me exactly which feature broke.
3. A harder test: run the same scenario with every speaker completely sincere, nobody intending to deceive at all. Does Arendt's account still predict the same damage? I think it might, which would be a nice result, but I want your honest read on whether her framework needs a liar or can get by without one.
4. Give the strongest objection from someone who says this isn't lying in any useful sense at all — just vagueness, consultant-speak, and bad communication — and that Arendt is stretching the word until it stops doing work. Then answer it as an Arendtian would, and say whether you think the answer holds or whether the objection actually lands.

One paragraph of scenario plus the four numbered bits is all I need. Keep the whole thing under about 900 words and don't pad it with background on Arendt's life — the students have read both essays, they just haven't put them to work against each other.
