I keep meeting a particular kind of philosophical claim, and I don't know whether the specific feature that interests me has a name.

The claim concerns the conditions under which anything at all can be presented: that everything we can be aware of is already shaped by our awareness of it, or that there is no way of getting outside one's own point of view, or that all observation is already theory-laden. Try to refute such a claim by producing a counterexample and the counterexample turns out to be something you produced from inside your own point of view, so the proponent absorbs it and counts your attack as one more confirming instance. The claim never loses — not because it is well supported, but because there is no admissible way of attacking it. Berkeley's master argument looks like a case: you cannot conceive of a thing existing unconceived, since the conceiving already amounts to the thing's being conceived.

What I want is whatever term covers that absorption. Not untestability in general, and not an immunizing maneuver — here nothing is patched on afterward; the reinterpretation follows from the claim's own subject matter, which seems to make it a different animal. Is this a recognized category? How does it differ from a self-sealing argument, from ordinary unfalsifiability, from begging the question, and from the thought that denying a thesis can presuppose it? And is total absorption a mark of vacuity or a legitimate argumentative strategy? Please tell me where the edges of the concept fall, not just what to call it.
