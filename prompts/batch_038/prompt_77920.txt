So can you rank these for me by how decisive they are — how much an experiment of that shape would tell you whether the mind is rewriting the record after the fact (Orwellian) vs. simply not having the content until later (Stalinesque)? 1. color phi — two different-colored dots flashed in alternation nearby, people report one dot moving and changing color midway
2. the cutaneous rabbit — taps at wrist and elbow, people feel taps in between
3. the flash-lag thing — a flash that's physically lined up with a moving object gets seen as trailing it
4. Libet-style temporal referral — the felt onset of a skin stimulus gets pushed back to before the brain could plausibly have finished processing it
5. one I made up, call it the verdict tone: a dot flashes for ~50ms, desaturated enough that nobody can reliably name it, then gets masked. 400ms later a tone tells you which of two colors it "really" was. The trick is the only report you ever collect comes after the tone — no speeded forced-choice at dot onset — and then you ask how confident people are that they saw the color at the time.
6. another made-up one, the saccade swap: a word flashes during a saccade and a second word appears 250ms after the eyes land. Here you force a report about the first word immediately, before the second word could inform anything, and then ask again afterward.

The reason 5 and 6 are in there is that I think the real crux is what I've been calling the readout window: whether the design lets the subject report on the experience before or after the arrival of information that could retroactively fix its content.
