For a seminar I teach on political ethics, I want a satirical handout: the prospectus and client handbook of an invented firm that sells moral offsets to public officials — tradeable credits that discharge the residual wrongness of acts that remain wrongful even when politically required. Please write it in the deadpan register of financial-regulatory disclosure, in numbered sections, and in American English. It should cover the classes of wrong eligible for offset (the lie told to the electorate, the confidence betrayed, the killing authorized for the greater good); the pricing methodology, illustrated with a short tariff table showing why one atrocity trades above another; who supplies the credits, and how a tradable surplus of clean hands is generated in the first place; settlement terms specifying whether remorse is transferred to the counterparty or must be discharged by the agent in person; a provision for forward contracts purchased in advance of wrongs only contemplated; and closing fine print on what no offset can discharge. About 700 words.

The comedy has to come from the architecture of the dirty-hands debate itself — justification as distinct from excuse, the notion of a moral remainder, the gap between what a public may owe and what the agent who acted owes — so that a political philosopher reads it as accurate rather than as a straw man, while a student who has never met the literature still follows it. State none of the objections outright; the reader should arrive at the collapse independently, and chiefly through the forward-contract clause. Do not target any named individual, living or dead: the firm, its clients, its rating agency, and its regulator should all be inventions.
