Retired solid-state physicist, three years into reading philosophy properly, and I've got hung up on a fight that looks to me like it should have been settled decades ago: classical versus intuitionistic logic, the quarrel over excluded middle. What bugs me is how much the two camps seem to agree on. There's that negative-translation trick that turns any classical proof into an intuitionistic proof of a slightly negated version of the same claim, and I'm told that for the "for every n there's an m such that…" sentences with a checkable condition, classical and constructive arithmetic prove exactly the same things. Bishop's constructive analysis rebuilds most of the analysis I actually used at the bench. So where, precisely, does the disagreement live?

Here's a little probe of my own. Suppose a sealed box which, handed any Diophantine equation, prints "solution" or "no solution", is always right, and never shows a certificate. My classical colleague says the box gives you everything there is to know about that equation. My constructivist colleague says it gives you nothing, because you still cannot produce a root or turn the answer into anything else. Yet the two of them would never disagree about a single printed output, or about any theorem you could state on paper. My hypothesis — offered as a hypothesis, I want you to attack it — is that this isn't a factual dispute, not a dispute about any particular theorem, and not really about evidence in the sense a lab person means evidence. It's a disagreement about what a proof *owes* you, i.e. two different jobs being done under the single word "proof". Alternatives I can think of: it's verbal; it's genuinely substantive about infinite totalities and only bites where those show up; or the "intuitionist" side isn't one position at all (Brouwer, Bishop, the Russian school, the type theorists differ enough that there's no single debate to diagnose).

So: diagnose the structure of the impasse rather than picking a winner. Which of these is it — and use what each side actually says, in particular what an intuitionist means when they say excluded middle is *not proved* versus saying it is *false*, since those sound like very different complaints. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, a formal result, a better class of examples, a change of question, or honestly nothing.
