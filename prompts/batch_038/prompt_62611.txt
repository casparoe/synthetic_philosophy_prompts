I'm an MPhil student and in a fortnight I'm presenting my term paper to my department's graduate formal epistemology seminar — twenty minutes plus about half an hour of discussion afterwards. I need a Q&A prep sheet, and I need it to be realistic about this particular room rather than generic.

The thesis. Lewis's triviality result doesn't show that indicative conditionals fail to express propositions; it shows that no Boolean algebra can host its own conditional-probability function, which is a definability limitation, roughly Tarski-shaped. So I build a two-sorted hierarchy. Level 0 is the ordinary algebra of events with a Kolmogorov function P. Conditionals are level-1 objects, and a seperate function P* assigns them numbers such that P*(if A then C) = P(C|A) whenever P(A) > 0 — so Stalnakers Thesis holds unrestrictedly at every level. What's supposed to block Lewis is that level-1 objects can't be conjoined with level-0 events: the decomposition of P*(K) into its parts on A and on not-A isn't even well formed, and his proof needs precisely that decomposition together with conditionalising inside a single algebra. Conditionals can be negated (the negation stays at level 1), and iterated conditionals go up to level 2, evaluated by the level-1 assignment. I own the cost: disjunctive embeddings — either it rains, or if the match is called off we'll go to the cinema — come out as simply not interpretable.

The audience. The convenor is an Adams/Edgington-style Bayesian who thinks Lewis settled this in 1976 and that the right response is to drop the Thesis and tie assertibility to conditional credence; she'll suspect I've reinvented her position with extra machinery. Two people come over from the statistics department and are really there for the measure theory — they never wanted a conditional event to begin with, and they'll want to know what happens when P(A) = 0 and whether I need regular conditional distributions. There's a non-classical logician who'll press on whether I've answered Lewis or just banned the expressions his proof uses. And one PhD student works on conditionals in the psychology of reasoning, de Finetti's three-valued conditional event and the experimental work around it.
