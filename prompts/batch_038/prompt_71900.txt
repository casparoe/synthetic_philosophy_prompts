For the past year I have been writing donation appeals for a small nonprofit, and I have become genuinely unsure where legitimate persuasion stops and manipulation begins. I don't have a philosophy background, so what I want is a decision tree I could actually run a draft appeal through, rather than an account of who has said what.

Please build it from the space of possible answers first. Each branch node should be a question I could answer about a piece of communication without knowing which label my answer leads to — for instance: would the technique still work on someone who had just been shown precisely how it works; would the person influenced endorse the influence a month later, when the urgency has passed; does the appeal supply considerations that bear on whether its claim is true, or does it mainly shift the reader's estimate of what other people think and do; does it work by compressing the time available to decide; does the writer have to believe it for it to land? Only then place the positions people actually hold at the leaves — including the view that manipulation is anything that bypasses a person's capacity to weigh reasons, the view that only covert influence is objectionable, and the view that the same move is permissible for a seller but not for a state. Note any combination of answers that seems unoccupied, and say whether that is because the position is incoherent, unappealing, or merely unexplored.

Work these three cases in somewhere: a suggested giving amount printed above the blank field on a donation form; a medical procedure described by survival rate rather than mortality rate; and a campaign ad aimed at a demographic the campaign expects to turn out at lower rates. Flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot really separate. Finally, be explicit about which of these branches are settled and which are still contested among people who work on the topic — do not present a live dispute as though it were consensus.
