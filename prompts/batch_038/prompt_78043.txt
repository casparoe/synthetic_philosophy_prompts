I run a reading group that sits uncomfortably between philosophy of mind and mechanistic interpretability, and for two years we have made no headway on the Searle–Dennett dispute about original and derived intentionality. Since the Chinese Room appeared in 1980 both sides have restated their positions many times, and each restatement seems to confirm the other side's worst opinion of it. Roughly: Searle holds that syntax is never sufficient for semantics, that computation is not intrinsic to physics at all — assigning a program to a physical system is observer-relative — and that the brain differs in kind because its particular biological processes causally produce genuine, intrinsic aboutness along with conscious experience; he reads Dennett as someone who has explained the phenomenon away by declining to acknowledge the datum. Dennett holds that intentional ascriptions are made true by real patterns that support prediction, in the way center-of-mass ascriptions are, that his two-bitser shows nothing in the physics fixes an interpretation from the inside — which content a state carries depends on how the system is embedded and what it is used for — and that Searle's intrinsic intentionality is a posit doing no explanatory work beyond marking the spot where an explanation was supposed to go.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis rather than a conclusion: they are not answering the same question. Searle is asking what must exist in the world for there to be experience and understanding at all, and treats any account that leaves that unanswered as a change of subject; Dennett is asking what makes a particular content ascription true, and treats any answer that invokes a further intrinsic fact as a non-answer. If so, the word carrying the load is "real," and each side can win locally without costing the other anything it cares about.

But I want the diagnosis argued rather than assumed. Is this a verbal dispute, a substantive metaphysical one, two questions traveling under one heading, or a real disagreement resting on starting intuitions neither side can argue for — Searle's that consciousness is a datum any theory must respect, Dennett's that nothing counts as an explanation unless it terminates in structure and process? Is there a hidden formal question doing the work, for instance in the triviality arguments about implementation and the subsequent attempts to tighten the mapping conditions: would settling when a physical system genuinely implements a computation also settle whether implementation suffices for content, or are those simply different claims that have been conflated?

I also want to know whether my own field could bear on any of it. In interpretability we routinely find a single direction in a model's internal state space doing duty for different features in different contexts, with which reading holds fixed by how the model is embedded in a pipeline and what is being asked of it. That is the two-bitser situation arrived at empirically rather than by thought experiment. Does it hand both camps a shared case that each must accept as relevant, or will each simply absorb it as confirmation — Searle as proof that syntax is observer-relative, Dennett as proof that there is nothing but pattern-in-context? If it can only be read through the contested vocabulary, say so plainly, and then tell me whether any conceivable case would be vocabulary-neutral.

Finally, say what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or nothing — and if nothing, explain why the impasse is something other than a failure of cleverness. I do not want a survey of the standard replies to the Chinese Room or a history of the exchange; I know both. Take a position on the structure of the disagreement and defend it against the strongest thing each side could say back to you. Please write the whole thing as continuous prose, the way one writes a letter: state your diagnosis in the opening sentences and argue for it from there, with no headings, no bullet points, no numbered lists, no bold or italic emphasis, and no tables.
