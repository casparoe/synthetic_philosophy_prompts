I came into philosophy from physics, and I have until the end of the month to submit a proposal for a three-year doctoral studentship; my supervisor has asked me to make the ethical half of the project precise, which is where I am stuck. The starting point is panpsychism's advertised moral payoff: if experience is a fundamental feature of the physical world, then the community of subjects is vastly larger than we have assumed. My suspicion is that this payoff has never actually been cashed out, and that the difficulty is structural rather than a matter of unfinished work. The experiences the view posits at the fundamental level are doubly indeterminate — it is unclear whether they belong to subjects at all, and unclear whether anything that thin could amount to suffering rather than the mere registration of a state. I want to ask whether panpsychism can sustain any determinate ethical claim once both uncertainties are faced at once, and in particular whether the honest move is to give up on obligations to micro-subjects and rest the view's ethical interest on a claim about the value of the natural world as such.

Please write the proposal: the question stated so that it has a determinate answer; why the standard positions — constitutive panpsychism, cosmopsychism, panprotopsychism — each leave it open rather than settling it; my method, and why it is preferable to the obvious alternatives, including simply deferring until someone solves the combination problem; what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a candid judgement of whether three years is realistic for it. Around 1,800 words. Leave out any references; I will add those myself.
