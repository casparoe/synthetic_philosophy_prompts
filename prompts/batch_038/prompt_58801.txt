So heres the move I keep running into and don't have a word for. Someone wants to argue that a higher-level property is genuinely irreducible to the lower level. The argument goes: the identification of the higher-level property with anything at the lower level only becomes exact in some limit that no real system ever reaches — infinitely many particles, infinite time, an infinite population. So strictly speaking the property isn't there in the world as the lower-level theory describes it. And then in the very next paragraph the same person insists the property is obviously real and robust in the finite systems we actually have, because we measure it, we engineer around it, it shows up reliably. The limit does the work of blocking the reduction, the finite case does the work of establishing that there's something there to be irreducible, and nobody seems bothered that both are being used at once.

Cases where I've seen it:

- A ferromagnet. Magnetization is sharp only in the infinite-particle limit, so we're told the higher-level notion can't be identified with anything in the mechanics of a finite collection of atoms — but then a real bar magnet on a lab bench is magnetized, and that gets cited as proof the higher-level facts are autonomous.
- Population genetics. The deterministic equations for allele frequencies are exact only for an infinite population; any real finite population drifts and eventually fixates, so the equilibrium the model predicts never literally exists. Still, people treat the equilibrium structure as a robust real feature of the biological world and use it to argue that fitness-and-frequency talk cant be cashed out in terms of individual organisms reproducing and dying.
- Maybe also chaos: Lyapunov exponents and attractors are strictly defined only as time goes to infinity, and yet real turbulent flows are said to have them, and that's offered as a reason the coarse-grained description isn't reducible to the fine-grained one.

So — does this pattern have an established name? The pattern being: the limiting case is invoked to make the property absent from the world the lower theory describes, the finite case is invoked to make it present and robust, and irreducibility gets read off the gap between the two.
