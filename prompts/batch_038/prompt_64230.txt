I spent thirty-eight years measuring transport properties of solids, and I have come to philosophy late, so I shall state what I want with the precision I expect of a laboratory protocol. My working life taught me to distrust the assumption that every description must rest on one beneath it; I now want to know whether that instinct can be made metaphysically respectable.

Construct a thought experiment — one concrete physical situation, described in no more than three paragraphs — that instantiates a hierarchy of obtaining facts with all four of the following properties:

1. Grounding within the hierarchy is a strict partial order: irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive. Nothing grounds itself, nothing grounds the thing that grounds it, and there is no mutual or merely partial grounding anywhere in the structure.
2. There is no fundamental level. Every fact in the hierarchy is grounded in some further fact, all the way down; the descent never terminates.
3. Every step is of the same kind and yields complete rather than partial explanation: nothing at any level is brute, and no fact is grounded in something of a different category.
4. The descending sequence nonetheless converges on a determinate limit — a well-defined value or state that the successive levels approach — and this limit is not itself a member of the hierarchy and does not ground any fact in it.

The setting must be recognisably physical, of the nested, multi-scale sort a working scientist actually encounters, and may not invoke deities, abstract objects acting as grounds, or stipulated brute necessities.

Here is what the case is for, since it fixes what would count as cheating: I want to see whether an infinite descent can be explanatorily respectable rather than merely a regress one has decided to tolerate. A construction that satisfies (4) by quietly installing the limit as a ground, or that satisfies (2) by making the levels not genuinely distinct, fails. The verdicts must be generated by the structure you build, not asserted in the description of it.

After the scenario, take the four specifications in order and show how your construction meets each. Then tell me which single feature of the case does the work in keeping (2) and (4) compatible; what the picture becomes if that feature is removed; and how a philosopher who insists that grounding must be well-founded would attack the case — in particular, whether the strongest form of the attack is that the limit is covertly fundamental or that the levels are not really distinct.

Begin with the scenario itself: no preface, no announcement of your plan, no restatement of my requirements. If you judge two of my specifications to be unsatisfiable together, do not quietly relax one — say so, and identify the collision.
