Consider a theory T that posits a subject matter P together with a class of P-directed beliefs B. Say that T faces a **reliability challenge** when the following three conditions hold jointly:

1. **Blindness** — none of the processes that produce members of B has any instance of P among its causes.
2. **Fidelity** — the members of B are, as a matter of fact, largely true.
3. **Completeness** — T is independently committed to the claim that the causal history of the world is closed under, and exhaustively described by, its non-P posits.

The challenge is to explain the joint satisfaction of Blindness and Fidelity without surrendering Completeness. Epiphenomenalism about qualia faces it in an acute form: if phenomenal properties have no effects, then the physical processes generating our reports and judgments about our own experience are blind to the very properties those judgments concern, yet the judgments come out true — and the epiphenomenalist, of all theorists, has already paid for Completeness and cannot give it back.

Add one further distinction. Call a reply **globally adequate** if it explains why B is largely true. Call it **discriminatory** if it explains why B is true *and* why B would have been different had the relevant features of P been different — that is, if it restores a counterfactual dependence of judgment on subject matter by some route other than causation by P itself. A common-cause story, in which one neural state produces both the experience and the judgment about it, is the paradigm attempt at the second grade.

Now consider five domains that raise a reliability challenge of the same general shape:

(i) Platonism about mathematics, where the posits are causally inert abstract objects and the beliefs are mathematical;
(ii) robust non-naturalist moral realism, where the posits are irreducible normative facts and the beliefs are moral beliefs shaped by natural selection;
(iii) realism about modal facts, where the posits concern ways the world could have been and the beliefs are modal;
(iv) Leibnizian pre-established harmony, where two causally closed series are coordinated without interaction, and judgments in one series concern the other;
(v) naturalness-based semantic externalism, where a community's term reliably picks out the property that best earns the role, though that property has never causally affected the term's users.

Your task:

- For each of (i)–(v), assign two integer scores from 0 to 10. **Structural fidelity** measures how closely that case's reliability challenge matches the epiphenomenalist's, judged against all four parameters: Blindness, Fidelity, Completeness, and the global/discriminatory distinction. **Exportability** measures how much of the most promising reply available in that domain could be transplanted into epiphenomenalism without importing resources the epiphenomenalist lacks — a priori intuition, a plenitude principle, a designing agent, a non-causal acquaintance relation, or an independent reason to think the correlation need not be explained at all.
- Present the ten scores in a table, with one sentence justifying each cell.
- Convert the mean of each pair into a tier list (S / A / B / C) and name the single analogue that offers the most promising route to *defending* rather than dissolving epiphenomenalism. State explicitly whether the best it can supply is global adequacy alone or discriminatory adequacy as well, and what the epiphenomenalist must concede to get it.
- Close with three to five sentences identifying the structural disanalogy you judge most damaging to that transplant, and say whether it renders the move fatal or merely expensive.

Argue from the content of the cases themselves; no bibliography or citations are wanted.
