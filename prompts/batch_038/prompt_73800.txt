My half-formed idea, which I tried to explain after class last week and got a very patient "interesting, but sharpen it," is this. Textualists sell their method as the humble one: the judge doesn't get to decide what the law should aim at, she just applies the enacted words, so the theory is supposed to be about deferring to somebody else's choices and keeping judicial construction near zero. But when I actually trace how the method gets used in the hard cases, the judge looks like she's doing a lot of construction anyway — she's building an idealized lawmaker who is fully informed, coherent across the whole statutory scheme, speaks in ordinary English, and would never have intended an absurd result, and then she asks what that construct would have conveyed to a reasonable reader. That figure has a lot in common with what a purposivist does when she reconstructs the mischief the provision was aimed at and reads the text as a means to it. Both look like the judge building a normative model of an author and then reading the words through it. They disagree about which features the model is allowed to include, but I can't tell whether that's a difference in kind or just a difference in how much gets smuggled in.

The concrete handle I keep coming back to is the federal provision that adds prison time for a defendant who "uses" a firearm during a drug-trafficking crime. In the mid-nineties the Court held that a guy who traded his gun to a buyer in exchange for cocaine had "used" it; the dissent said the enhancement was obviously aimed at the danger of a gun being employed as a weapon, and that the majority's reading turned a violent-crime provision into one that swept in a barter. Then, years later, the Court narrowed the same provision so that mere passive possession or storage near the drugs didn't count. Same word, same statute, two readings, and the interesting part for me is that the "literal" reading and the "ordinary meaning" reading point in opposite directions depending which case you're in — which suggests the constraint isn't the words doing the work, it's a judgment about what kind of speaker the legislature is being asked to be.
