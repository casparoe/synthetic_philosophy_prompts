I run access control and tenancy for a multi-tenant SaaS platform — RBAC design, tenancy isolation, scoped and revocable credentials, invite-only vs open registration, quotas and rate limits, moderation/ban workflows with apeals, occasionally federation with other platforms. Never taken a philosophy course. I've been reading the open-borders debate lately, the side that says political communities have a right to exclude would-be migrants against the side that says borders just trap people in whatever jurisdiction they happened to be born into, and I keep thinking its a tenancy-governance argument wearing a moral costume.

Translate the debate into the vocabulary I actually work in, claim by claim, then give me a residue report. Not the "states are like clubs" move everyone reaches for — too soft, and it hides the interesting parts. I want tenancy boundaries, who's allowed to create an account, credential scope and duration, revocability, admin rights vs operator rights, ToS consent, audit logs, data export and portability, common-carrier obligations, single-tenant vs federated.

The claims I want done:

1. A political community may exclude non-members because it's an association of its members rather than a service provider.
2. Birthright membership is legitimate even though the newborn consented to nothing and the birthplace lottery is arbitrary.
3. Restricting exit is much worse than restricting entry — an asymmetry both sides mostly just grant.
4. Guest-worker programs as the second-best option: admission without membership.
5. Openness would swamp the institutions that make the receiving state work, so exclusion is protecting a public good.

For each: closest counterpart in my vocabulary, how much of it survives intact, what I have to distort or bolt on to make it fit, and what has no counterpart at all. Then seperate out:

- which failures are my vocabulary's fault — does access control have any notion of a right to be *admitted*, as opposed to a right to be served once you're in? any notion of jurisdiction over a space rather than over an account? — and which are the original claims being vaguer than they looked;
- where my vocabulary has resources the philosophers seem to be missing (graded and revocable access, abuse-driven rate limiting, the gap between who signs the ToS and who's bound by it, common-carrier status, federation), and whether any of those actually dissolve a chunk of the dispute rather than just redescribing it;
- and the bottom line: is my intuition basically right, or is there something about a state — no exit, no consent, the fact that the excluded were never eligible to become members in the first place, whatever — that no tenancy model can hold?

I care most about that last bit, so don't soften it. If the translation breaks somewhere, tell me exactly where and what the break looks like from the inside.
