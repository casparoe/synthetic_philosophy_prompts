Right, so I do a fortnightly column for a science-and-ideas desk — mostly AI, a bit of neuroscience, the occasional thing about statistics that nobody reads — and once a month I get a sidebar where I'm allowed to be slightly silly. This month's is on the responsibility-gap argument about autonomous systems, the one where a machine does something dreadful and it turns out there's nobody who properly deserves the blame for it. I've got the shape of the piece but I need someone to actually draft the centrepiece for me.

The conceit: a job advertisement, in full corporate-recruitment deadpan, for a role at a firm that builds autonomous systems. Give them two product lines, something boring like warehouse and last-mile logistics plus one vaguely defence-adjacent thing they refer to euphemistically and never quite name, because that's where the pressure is. The role is something like Accountability Associate, and its entire function is to be the human being who is held responsible when the system does something appalling. The company has clearly thought quite hard about this, believes it has solved a genuinely difficult problem, and is quietly pleased with itself.

Structure it as a real job ad would be: about us, the role, what you'll be doing day to day, the person spec, what we offer, how to apply. Then a short candidate FAQ at the bottom, four or five questions, the kind an actual applicant would ask — will I be trained to understand the systems I'm answerable for, what happens if two serious incidents land in the same quarter, does the indemnity cover me after I've left, that sort of thing. Around 700 words, and keep it under 750 or my sub will cut the FAQ, which will be the best bit.

The important thing is that the humour has to come from getting the philosophy right, not from the company being cartoonishly evil. So I want the ad to brush up against, in its own bland vocabulary: the control and knowledge conditions on responsibility and what it looks like to satisfy them on paper only; whether blame is even the sort of thing that can be handed over by contract, and what the company's confident answer to that is; the awkward fact that the more the system learns and adapts after it's shipped, the less anyone upstream can be said to have controlled the outcome — HR should have spun that into a selling point somehow; the many-hands problem, and how nominating one blameworthy person is supposed to fix it without simply adding another pair of hands to the pile; and whether punishing the post-holder actually delivers anything anyone wanted from punishment — deterrence, desert, the apology to a bereaved family, the public message — or only the appearance of it. Plus one dismissive line about the question of whether the system itself could ever be the responsible party, which the company should treat as obviously silly and slightly offensive.

Nobody in the ad ever admits there's a problem. Deadpan throughout, no winking, no nudging asides in brackets — a reader should be able to work out what's being ironised from the benefits package alone. I'd like the 'what we offer' section to be doing a lot of that work; that's where I want the recoil to come from.

Please don't name any real philosophers, don't cite any papers or books, and don't put any words in a real person's mouth. My readers aren't philosophers and I don't want name-dropping doing the work that the concepts should be doing. Invented people are very welcome though — give me a line in the about-us bit from their Head of Moral Risk or whatever you want to call the post, something sincerely meant and accidentally damning.

Target is the practice and the genre, not any actual company: corporate ethics-speak that treats accountability as a box to be allocated and ticked. I want a philosopher of technology to read it and laugh and say that's fair, and a general reader to just enjoy it as a weird job ad. British spelling throughout, it's going in print.
