Our department runs a little Thursday reading group on naturalizing mental content, led by a postdoc I'll call Marta — she's on the job market this year so the group is weirdly intense and the coffee is bad. I'm a first year and I've basically been keeping a log of her verdicts on the cases that come up, because after six weeks I still can't tell what theory she's actually running and neither can anyone else in the room. Here's the log. (1) Frog's optic tectum bug-detector, standard lineage, snapping at flies in the wild and at BBs in the lab: she says the content is that there's a small dark moving thing there, flatly not that there's a fly or something edible, and she gets annoyed at the "the frog is being systematically fooled" framing — she says that framing already assumes the answer. (2) Same mechanism, same current behavior, but we stipulate a lineage in which non-nutritive dark specks never occurred: she flips, and says then the content is that there's food there, and that the two cases differ in nothing but history, which is her whole point. (3) Swampman at the instant he's created: no content, no beliefs, she'll bite that bullet happily — but she also insists that after six months of being fed, corrected, and nearly walking into traffic, swampman does have content for the states that keep him alive, thinner than ours but real, not merely ascribed by us. (4) A bacterium swimming up a sugar gradient: content yes, but she invokes Millikan's pushmi-pullyu and says you can't pry apart the aboutness from the directedness, so it's a mistake to ask whether the state says sugar-here or says go-toward-sugar. (5) The bee waggle dance: when someone asks whether the dance's content is fixed by what the dancer was selected to signal or by what the receiving foragers need to be true, she picks the receivers, without much argument. (6) A radiologist who after a decade of reading films reliably spots a hairline fracture that no ancestor of hers ever encountered: Marta says the perceptual state genuinely represents the fracture, and she rejects the move of demoting it to something descriptive or parasitic on the radiologist's other states. Half the group thinks 1 and 5 pull in opposite directions and she's just inconsistent; she says she isn't and won't say more.

Next week I've been volunteered to bring a case, and I want to build one that actually forces her hand, but first I want to know what she'll say. Down the hall there's an artificial life group that evolves simple agents in a 2D grid world with a genetic algorithm — about 20,000 generations, fitness is just "eat pellets." Pellets only ever spawned on dark tiles, and for the first ~19,500 generations there were also non-nutritive dark decoy tiles scattered around; the experimenters deleted the decoys for the final 500 generations because they wanted a clean champion population. In the champions there's an internal register, call it R, that ramps up when a dark tile is within two cells, and the steering module reads R and turns toward whatever R is pointing at. Then they copy the champions, weights frozen, no further training or reinforcement of any kind, into a fresh world with no pellets at all but plenty of dark tiles, plus a new kind of tile that is genuinely nutritive but pale. So: does R have content? If it does, is the content that-there's-a-dark-tile-nearby or that-there's-food-nearby — i.e. which stretch of the history counts, the 20,000 generations with decoys or the last 500 without them? Does a genetic algorithm's fitness pressure even count as a selection history, or is R's content at best derived from the experimenters' intentions the way a fuel gauge's is? And in the transfer world, where R fires constantly and the agent starves surrounded by dark tiles, is R misrepresenting, has it lost its content, or — since nothing about the agent or its lineage has changed — is it representing exactly what it always did?

What I want from you isn't a literature review, I've read the literature badly and I know the names of the moves. I want a prediction. Specifically: lay out the candidate rules that are consistent with all six of Marta's earlier verdicts at once — including the ones that have to do some work to swallow 3 and 5 together — and say what each of those rules predicts about R, separately for the training world and the transfer world, since I suspect they'll come apart. Then give me your best guess at what she'll actually say, with the reasons for preferring that rule over the others given her track record rather than given which one you think is true. And finally design the one further case that would most cleanly seperate the top two contenders; my hunch is that it has to be a case where what a register reliably indicates and what its indicating confers success on come apart with no disjunction available to hide behind, but I can't get the details right and I'd rather you build it than me. If you think her log is genuinely inconsistent, say so and tell me which verdict she'd have to give up — but assume for the sake of the exercise that she has an answer.
