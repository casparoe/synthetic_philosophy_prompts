I teach an experimental-philosophy seminar with a lab component, and this spring my undergraduates are piloting vignette studies on the side-effect effect: the familiar asymmetry whereby people are willing to say that an agent brought about a foreseen bad side effect intentionally, but not a foreseen good one, when the descriptive facts are otherwise matched. I have deliberately kept the original chairman-and-environment materials out of their hands, since roughly half the class met them in an introductory course and I do not want a contaminated pilot. What I need from you instead is a family of cases built to order: a two-by-two design crossing the moral valence of the side effect (bad, good) with deep-self concordance — whether the side effect accords with the protagonist's central and standing values, or is orthogonal to them — every other feature of the case held fixed across the four cells. Please write all four vignettes in full. The point of the exercise is to dissociate three accounts, and I want that dissociation made explicit. First, the pragmatic-implicature line associated with Adams and Steadman, on which calling the side effect intentional is largely a conversational vehicle for conveying blame, so a bad outcome should raise intentionality ascriptions and a good one should not. Second, Sripada's deep-self model, on which intentionality ascriptions track whether the outcome accords with the agent's central and stable values, valence mattering only as evidence of that accord. Third, a competence-based line growing out of the skill component in Malle and Knobe's account of the folk concept and developed by Nichols and Ulatowski as interpretive diversity, on which the asymmetry is driven by which concept of intentional action a given participant happens to bring to the vignette. For each account, give me the predicted pattern across the four cells — a main effect of valence here, an effect of concordance within each valence level there, nothing at all elsewhere — and tell me which cell comparisons are diagnostic and which are merely filler. Say whether any of the three predicts an ordinal interaction rather than two separable main effects, and what a subgroup analysis keyed to a free-response concept probe would have to show before I could credit the third account rather than the first.

The constraints are numerous, and I would rather over-specify than argue about them afterward. Each vignette is to run no more than about 140 words, and the four must match sentence for sentence, differing only where the manipulation requires it; the protagonist keeps the same name, sex, and job title throughout. The setting should be an ordinary workplace — a municipal fleet depot, a regional logistics office, a school district facilities department, something of that order — and the protagonist a single mid-level individual, not a corporation, a committee, or a software system, since I am not willing to import the puzzles about collective agency. The side effect must be moderate and financial or environmental in kind: no death, no bodily injury, no triage, because I will not let double-effect intuitions do any work here. It must be foreseen with certainty, on the explicit say-so of a third party in advance; it must not be a means to the protagonist's goal, nor causally required for it; and there must be no duress, no order from a superior, no time pressure, and no ignorance anywhere in the case. Describe the outcome in concrete, non-evaluative terms and let the reader supply the valence, and do not let any vignette contain the word intentionally or any synonym of it. The protagonist's desire regarding the side effect is to be held at indifference in all four cells — desire is already a recognized component of the folk concept, so the concordance variable has to be something other than a desire variable — and the strength, centrality, and vividness of her standing commitment must be matched across cells, so that what varies is only the direction of that commitment relative to the outcome. You may find it useful to give the commitment a scope wider than the outcome at issue, for instance a decade of professional work on some general aim of which the side effect is one small instance of satisfaction or frustration, provided the width of scope is identical in every cell. The dependent measure is a binary item asking whether the protagonist brought about the side effect intentionally, followed by three further items: a seven-point blame-or-praise rating, a seven-point rating of whether what she did reflects what she really cares about, and a free-response question asking the participant to say what makes an action intentional. The hard problem, and the one I want solved rather than merely named, is this: a bad side effect that runs against the protagonist's standing values looks on its face like a mitigated bad side effect, since a reader may take her commitment as excusing material, while a bad side effect that accords with those values looks aggravated. If perceived blameworthiness moves with my concordance manipulation, the first and second accounts have been varied together and the pilot is worthless. Explain how your wording keeps the blame and praise ratings as near equal as possible across the concordance conditions within each valence level, and concede honestly where you expect residue.

Finally, I want the demonstration that the design works, not merely the design. Walk through the constraints above one at a time and confirm compliance, pointing to the specific sentences that carry the valence manipulation and the specific sentences that carry the concordance manipulation. Identify the single feature of the cases that is doing the explanatory work and tell me what happens to the predicted pattern if I delete it. Then give me the sharpest attack on the design from whichever of the three accounts comes out worst; I expect the objection that the concordance sentence is a smuggled blame-mitigation cue, or that the orthogonal-values cell is not genuinely orthogonal but simply a case of a thinner deep self, and I want to know how far that objection bites and whether it can be met by a fifth cell rather than answered in prose. Please do not assemble a bibliography, a reading list, or citations for me — I have the literature and I want the vignettes, the prediction table, and the reasoning.
