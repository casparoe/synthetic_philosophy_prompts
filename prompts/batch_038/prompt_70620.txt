Consider the following kind of case, stipulated rather than drawn from any actual controversy. A community — a town, a congregation, a nation — carries a living memory of some formative event: an annual commemoration, a story told to children, an inscription on a monument, a self-description ("we are the people who took in the refugees," "we held the pass"). Professional historical work on that same event, resting on documents, physical evidence, and the considered judgment of historians with no stake in the community, says something materially different: the welcome was purchased under duress, the pass was abandoned, the founders arrived as conquerors. The community learns of this work, understands it competently, and is in no way deceived about its provenance or its strength.

Proposition P: in cases of this shape, the community's memory should give way. That is, the community has an all-things-considered requirement — not merely a reason among others — to revise what it teaches, commemorates, and says about itself so as to conform to the historical record, and its refusal to do so is a genuine failing rather than a permissible difference in kind of attitude. Read "should" at the level of the community as such: what it does through its institutions, curricula, ceremonies, and public speech, not what each member severally happens to believe. Treat P as resolved in its favor just in case, in the paradigm cases, retention of the conflicting memory is a defect the community is called on to correct rather than a different way of relating to the past that can be correct on its own terms.

Give me your credence in P as a single number, together with the decomposition that produces it. At a minimum I want terms for the following, each with a rough probability and one or two lines of justification:

1. Whether commemorative practice is truth-aimed at all — or whether it aims at cohesion, at fidelity to the dead, at the constitution of a continuing group agent — such that grading it as false, and hence as up for correction, misdescribes what it is.
2. Whether the record is as independent of the memory as my stipulation implies. Archives are assembled, preserved, and interpreted by communities with the interests of successors; much of what historians treat as evidence is earlier memory that hardened into document. If the two are not independent sources but successive phases of a single transmission chain, does the conflict still have the structure of a disagreement, and can one phase correct the other?
3. Whether the failing, if there is one, is epistemic, moral, or both — and whether your assessment changes with the direction of the error. Compare a memory that flatters the community against one that accuses it: suppose the memory holds that the community committed the atrocity, and the record shows that it was committed by the neighbouring group under a borrowed name.
4. Whether the requirement survives the possibility that revising a constitutive memory destroys the very community whose memory it is — and whether that possibility is a tragic cost to be borne, a permissible excuse, or a defeater of the requirement.
5. Whether "the community should" is well-formed at all, or whether every obligation here belongs to members severally, and whether relocating the obligation changes the verdict.

Then state how these combine into your number, being explicit about which of them you treat as independent and which you think pull the same way for the same reason. Identify the term your credence is most sensitive to — the one where a modest shift would move the final number furthest — and say what would actually shift it: an argument, a distinction, or a kind of case that would force you to reclassify these situations.

Two people I know hold P at roughly 0.85 and roughly 0.15. The first is a documentary historian; the second works on oral tradition and its role in group identity. Tell me which single term in your decomposition most likely houses their disagreement, and why you would place that bet rather than distributing it across the others.
