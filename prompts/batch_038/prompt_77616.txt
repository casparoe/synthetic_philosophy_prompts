**Same Mechanism, Different Model? Identity Conditions for Mechanistic Anomaly Detection**

*Section 1 — The setup.* The proposal I'll call mechanistic anomaly detection (MAD) says that we should not monitor a deployed model only by checking whether its outputs are acceptable. Instead, we fit a monitor to the model's internal activations — residual-stream directions, attention-head outputs, sparse-autoencoder features — and flag runs whose internals fall outside a baseline distribution collected during prior evaluations. The motivating thought is that the internals may betray a capability, a disposition, or a plan that the behavior conceals, so that MAD earns its keep exactly in the cases where behavioral evaluation is silent.

*Section 2 — The problem this creates.* Any baseline is a distribution over internal states that are presumed to realize the same mechanism as the state now being flagged. But monitors are not deployed against a single frozen artifact. They are re-fitted against fine-tunes, distillation students, differently shaped successors, and successive checkpoints of one training run. So MAD presupposes an answer to a question it never asks: when is a mechanism M in model A the same mechanism as M′ in model B? I consider three candidate criteria.

(a) *Implementational.* M and M′ are the same if they occupy the same components with the same weights, up to symmetry. This fails almost immediately. Features are superposed, so there is no fact about which neuron carries which feature; permutation symmetries make "the same head" ill-defined even within one architecture family; and the criterion is simply inapplicable across architectures, which is the case MAD most needs.

(b) *Causal-functional.* M and M′ are the same if they have the same counterfactual profile — the same effects on downstream behavior under the same interventions. This is more promising, and it is the criterion practitioners implicitly use when they say a circuit has been reproduced in a new model. But the counterfactual profile is read off behavior, and two components with identical behavioral profiles under the intervention battery can diverge in how they generalize off-distribution, which is the very property we care about. Worse, the criterion is in tension with multiple realizability: if a function can be realized by structurally very different circuitry, then accepting (b) forces us to call those different circuits the same mechanism, and we lose the ability to notice when a mechanism has silently swapped its implementation for one that behaves identically today and differently tomorrow.

(c) *Cluster-based.* Mechanisms are natural kinds in a loose sense: clusters of properties — topology, activation statistics, causal role, developmental trajectory during training — that tend to co-occur and to be reproduced by the training process. Identity is then similarity within the cluster, and similarity comes in degrees. This handles cross-architecture cases gracefully, but it makes "same mechanism" vague, and vagueness in the baseline is vagueness in the false-positive rate, which is the quantity the monitor is supposed to control.

*Section 3 — My proposal.* Stop asking for the metaphysically correct identity conditions and individuate mechanisms relative to the monitor. M and M′ count as the same mechanism for monitor D if classifying them as the same preserves D's calibration: if the conditional probability that D flags a run, given that the run is genuinely anomalous, is unchanged between the two. Mechanism identity becomes an instrumental equivalence class indexed to an evaluation procedure.

*Section 4 — The circularity I cannot dissolve.* Calibration in that sense is measured against a ground-truth label of genuinely anomalous behavior. But the entire selling point of MAD, stated in Section 1, is to catch anomalies that do not show up in behavior. So my criterion is silent precisely where MAD is supposed to have leverage: it can only license an identity judgment in cases where the behavioral evidence was already sufficient. And it has a perverse corollary. If a mechanism changes implementation while leaving behavioral calibration untouched, my criterion pronounces it the same mechanism and tells the monitor to stay quiet. That is a false negative generated by the identity criterion itself. Monitor-relative individuation may therefore be self-undermining, defining sameness in terms of the signal MAD exists to supply independently.

*Section 5 — A structural worry.* Across a training run, components change continuously: weights drift, features split and merge, circuitry is gradually replaced by functionally similar circuitry that shares no components with the original. If identity requires continuity, then every checkpoint hosts the same mechanism as its predecessor, and by transitivity nothing later in training can ever count as a change — the monitor can never fire on drift. If identity instead requires a fixed reference point, then nearly everything late in training is a different mechanism, and the monitor fires constantly. My instinct is that sameness of mechanism should be indexed to a scale of description, so that continuity holds at coarse grain and fails at fine grain, and that this dissolves the dilemma. But I am not sure whether grain-relative identity is a solution or just a relocation of the arbitrariness into the choice of grain.

*Section 6 — Where this leaves me.* A deflationary conclusion is available: MAD does not need mechanism identity at all. It needs a similarity metric over internal states plus a distributional baseline, and the word "mechanism" is doing decorative work that flatters the approach with the prestige of mechanistic explanation in the philosophy of science. I want to resist this deflation, because if it is right, MAD is just outlier detection with a good story. I am not currently able to say what the resistance consists in.
