Our philosophy of biology seminar spent two weeks on "genetic information" and half the class turned out to be talking past each other, so for our revision session I want to build a decision tree that sorts people into positions on whether DNA carries information in a substantive sense and whether the genetic code is really a code. Please construct it out of tests rather than labels: every node should be a question a classmate could answer without knowing which leaf it leads toward. The distinctions I care about include whether codon-to-amino-acid pairing is mere physical templating or a mapping between two independently specifiable vocabularies; whether a system can carry information if nothing in it can malfunction; whether "information" here collapses into causal specificity of an ordinary kind, as a key is specific to a lock; whether the ribosome and the tRNA synthetases have to be counted as parties to the code rather than as its background; whether the verdict you reach for translation carries over to gene regulation and to development more broadly; and whether epigenetic marks or maternally supplied RNA would score the same. Each leaf should be named (or marked unnamed if you doubt anyone actually holds it) and carry a one-line note on the case it handles worst. Please also list the combinations of answers that your questions make possible but that no leaf occupies, saying for each whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your nodes cannot genuinely separate. Roughly a dozen nodes; none should be answerable simply by recognizing a slogan.
