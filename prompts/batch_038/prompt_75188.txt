I am drafting a short position statement for a seminar on the ethics of harming, and I would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is the package, as I currently hold it:

1. The doctrine of double effect states a real constraint rather than a rationalization: an act with a foreseen grave harm may be permissible when that harm is not intended and the reason for acting is proportionately serious, but it is impermissible when the harm falls within what the agent intends, whether as an end or as a means.

2. The terror bomber acts impermissibly and the tactical bomber permissibly, even though each foresees precisely the same civilian deaths.

3. Diverting the trolley is permissible; pushing the man from the footbridge is not. In the loop variant, where the track rejoins itself and the one man's body is what prevents the trolley from circling back onto the five, diverting remains permissible.

4. I am unmoved by the closeness objection, that is, the charge that an agent can always describe her intention narrowly enough to slip past the doctrine. For the same reason I think Quinn's recasting of the doctrine in terms of direct and indirect agency is a relabeling exercise that adds nothing to the classic intending-versus-foreseeing formulation, which I prefer.

5. Craniotomy to save the mother is impermissible, and not because the death is intended: what the surgeon aims at is the reduction of the child's skull, and the death stands far too close to that aim for the intending/foreseeing line to be crossed honestly.

6. Escalating opioids for an imminently dying patient are permissible even when they foreseeably hasten death. I also hold that the distinction between doing and allowing is not reducible to double effect.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each identify what would have to be revised or abandoned and what that revision costs me elsewhere. Close by naming the single change that would repair the largest share of the package.
