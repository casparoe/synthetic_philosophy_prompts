I'm a third-year philosophy PhD student and for two years now I've been stuck between two people I like who argue about whether my individual choices in big collective-harm cases actually matter morally. Call them the difference-maker and the marginalist.

Difference-maker: an act only generates an act-based moral reason if it counterfactually makes a difference to how some actual person's life goes. With millions of actors, my flight or my burger changes nothing for anyone, and the "expected harm" move is just a way of dressing up the fact that nobody is ever harmed by me — probability-weighted outcomes that never materialize aren't harms to anybody.

Marginalist: each act raises the probability of harm to real people, probability-weighted harm is a genuine moral quantity, per-act expected harm is nonzero and scales with the number of actors, and the difference-maker is fetishizing the actual sequence.

They've talked past each other so long that I finally got them to agree to an adversarial collaboration, and I'm the one who has to draft the joint statement they'll both put their names to. I've built some scaffolding so the write-up isn't just two restatements of the same old positions — please use it.

The scaffolding. Two paramters. First, granularity g: how lumpy the harm threshold is. g = 0 is fully additive and continuous (each unit makes the outcome smoothly worse); g = 1 is a single discrete trigger (nothing happens at all until the threshold is crossed, then a lot happens). Second, absorption a: how much the supply side buffers an individual act. a = 0 means every unit you buy gets produced because you bought it; a = 1 means producers smooth over individual purchases so that no single act changes output at all.

And a family of cases I want the statement to run on. Villagers each add a bucket to a reservoir with a release valve that floods a downstream farm when the water crosses a mark. V1: sharp mark, and the actual sequence of buckets stays below it. V2: sharp mark, the actual sequence does cross it, but any given bucket was redundant given the others. V3: no mark at all — the wall just seeps more the fuller it gets. V4: there is a mark, but the valve is sticky and only trips when several buckets land within the same minute, so whether you were pivotal depends on timing you cannot know in advance.

What I want you to draft:

1. A numbered section of claims both can sign — about the cases, about what a single act does and doesn't do in them, about what each side's principle actually commits them to. For any claim one of them would only sign with a qualification, say so explicitly and write the qualification down.

2. A disagreements table: the issue, the difference-maker's formulation of it in wording they'd accept for their own side, the marginalist's formulation likewise, and a column saying whether that disagreement is genuine or verbal. I specifically want the table to say, for each region of the (g, a) space, whether the difference-maker concedes a real act-based reason exists and whether the marginalist concedes it doesn't. My hunch is that most of the actual content lives in that map and that they agree on more of it than they think.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would move at least one of them. For each crux, the case or result both would agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance, in a form that could later be checked (so: what a lab public-goods experiment with lumpy thresholds and adjustable supply would show about contribution rates and about people's own judgments of blame; what would follow if it turned out that pivotal probabilities in real cases resist sharp point values and have to be represented as intervals, given that the difference-maker thinks the expected-harm arithmetic is pseudo-precise while the marginalist thinks a decision rule that refuses to rank under imprecision can't underwrite any everyday moral advice at all).

4. A short section saying which parts of the dispute you think are just about the word "harm," or about whether counterfactuals are the right currency for moral reasons — and why. If a chunk is verbal, say which chunk and what the substantive residue is once you strip the words out.

Two constraints. Don't adjudicate — this is a document both of them sign, not a verdict; if you think one side's principle fails by its own lights, park it as an open item rather than settling it. And no bibliography or reading list, I want this fully self-contained so I can hand it to them and to my committee without anybody having to go look anything up. Work from my cases and paramters. If you think I've mis-specified one of them — like if V2 and V4 actually collapse into the same thing, or if g and a aren't independent — say so up front and repair the model rather than quietly working around it.
