You are a philosopher with dual competence in Aristotle's practical philosophy and in the history of monetary economics; think carefully before writing, and treat this as a seminar question rather than a survey.

Analyze the "closing of the gold window" — Nixon's suspension of the dollar's convertibility into gold on 15 August 1971, which unwound Bretton Woods — through Aristotle's account of money in *Politics* I.9 (1257a–b) and *Nicomachean Ethics* V.5. The facts are not in dispute: the dollar had been convertible at $35 an ounce with other currencies pegged to it; Triffin's dilemma was that the United States had to run external deficits to supply the world with reserve liquidity, and those deficits eventually outran the gold standing behind the promise; the London Gold Pool broke down in 1968, the Smithsonian realignment of December 1971 failed in turn, and the major currencies were floating by 1973.

Begin with a short mapping paragraph: what in the episode plays the role of the stamp on the coin, what plays the role of the compact among exchangers, and who precisely is the party that "changes the currency." Then work through Aristotle's own claims — money as a device adopted because the natural necessaries are not readily portable; money in the *Ethics* as a measure that makes unlike things commensurable by standing proxy for need; and the view he reports at 1257b that money is "nonsense, and nothing by nature but entirely a convention, because when those who use it have changed the currency it is worth nothing" (Rackham's translation; note that he reports this as an opinion others hold, and tell me what his own considered position comes to). Where does this machinery genuinely bite — its picture of a measure exposed to unilateral revision by whoever controls the stamp, and its account of an appetite for acquisition that has no natural limit driving that revision — and where does it fall silent or misdescribe? Aristotle has no concept of a money stabilized by a managed quantity rather than a commodity backing, no theory of the price level, and no obvious place for a world reserve currency inside a polis-scoped framework. Be scrupulous about anachronism, and say plainly where he has nothing to say rather than making him speak.

End with a last line of exactly this form, choosing one of the three labels: Verdict: CONFIRMS | ANOMALY | OUT OF SCOPE — where CONFIRMS means the episode is what a competent reader of Aristotle should have expected, ANOMALY means his account cannot accommodate it without revision, and OUT OF SCOPE means it is not the kind of thing his account was addressing.
