I convene a monthly reading group on social ontology in Nottingham: roughly a dozen people, three philosophy postgraduates, two economists, a transport planner, and a shift manager who has never read any of the material. Ninety minutes, no preparation required of the members, and everything has to be done in the room. The question for the next session is whether the disagreement between Lewis's account of convention (in *Convention: A Philosophical Study*) and Bicchieri's account of social norms (in *The Grammar of Society*) is a substantive one or a merely terminological reshuffling of the same phenomena. My previous attempt at a case failed badly: I used the practice of standing to one side on a station escalator, and the transport planner pointed out within five minutes that this is simply a coordination equilibrium plus a bit of tutting, so the group concluded that Lewis and Bicchieri were describing one thing in two vocabularies. I need a designed case that cannot be dissolved in that way.

Please construct one to the following specification. Setting: an inland freight depot somewhere in the Midlands, served by about forty owner-operator hauliers who are not employees, are not organised in any association, have no drivers' room in common, work staggered shifts, and almost never meet one another. The recurrent situation is a genuine coordination problem with two proper coordination equilibria — whether to reverse into a loading bay or to drive in nose-first — such that either pattern works well if it is universal and is a shambles if it is mixed. Over some eighteen months a regularity settles on reversing in, with no designer, no announcement, no notice, and no legal or contractual backing. It is sustained by the depot's two night clerks, who have no stake whatever in the coordination and lose nothing when a haulier drives in nose-first, but who keep a notebook and thereafter withhold small discretionary favours (priority on paperwork, a preferred unloading slot, access to the kettle) from those they have recorded. The drivers know that most others reverse in and know that the clerks keep a notebook, but the iterations of knowledge stop short of common knowledge for a structural reason that your case must make explicit rather than assert. Finally, in some instances the coordination payoff vanishes altogether: a driver arriving at an empty yard at three in the morning would strictly prefer to drive in nose-first and would face no coordination cost at all, yet the practice holds, and a driver who departs from it is criticised afterwards if a clerk happened to see.

Constraints: the scenario itself in one paragraph, though you may add a separate short timeline of how it emerged; entirely realistic, with no autonomous vehicles, no state, no contract, no union, and no character who is a philosopher or who uses words like norm, convention, equilibrium or expectation; and the verdicts must be generated by the theories rather than smuggled into the description. Then supply the demonstration. First, a spec sheet taking Lewis's conditions in turn — conformity, expectation of conformity, rough agreement of preferences over action-combinations, the conditional preference for general conformity, the parallel conditional preference regarding the alternative equilibrium, and common knowledge of the lot — stating for each whether the case satisfies it, fails it, or leaves it indeterminate, and doing the same for the components of Bicchieri's account as she states them. Second, name the single feature of the case that does the work: I suspect it is the decoupling of the clerks' sanctions from the coordination payoffs, but tell me whether it is that or the epistemic shortfall, and why. Third, two removal tests, each holding everything else fixed: strip out the sanctions and say what remains (a Lewisian convention, a mere habit, or nothing at all); then restore common knowledge by having the depot post a notice and call a drivers' meeting, and say whether the practice thereby becomes a Lewisian convention and whether it loses its normative grip in the process. Fourth, the strongest counterattack available to the side that comes out worse, with an assessment of what accepting it costs. And lastly, a frank note on where the design is fragile — in particular, whether a Lewisian can simply deny that the empty yard at three in the morning is an instance of the recurrent situation, and if so, how I should answer that in the room.
