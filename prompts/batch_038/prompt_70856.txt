Roommate and I have been going in circles on the sunk cost thing and I want a diagnosis, because his argument looks basically valid to me and I hate both horns of where it lands.

(1) What you should do depends only on what your options would bring about from here on out.
(2) Costs you've already paid bring nothing about from here on out — that's exactly what makes them sunk.
(3) So already-paid costs are never reasons for anything.
(4) But nothing in (1) cares what *sort* of past fact we're talking about, only whether it changes what happens next.
(5) Lots of past facts don't change what happens next either: a promise you made to someone who's since died, with nobody left who'd ever know; a vow nobody recorded and nobody is monitoring; eight years ground away on a project you picked freely that will now produce nothing for anyone.
(6) So by (1) those aren't reasons either. A rational agent wakes up each morning owing nothing to her dead, her vows, or her past selves.
(7) And notice that's just the shape (3) condemned — the past dictating to the present. If (3) is a theorem about rationality rather than a local quirk, then pointless promise-keeping is irrational too, and there's no line between it and throwing good money after bad.
(8) So either keeping a pointless promise is irrational, or honoring sunk costs isn't.

He's happy with the second horn. I cant live with either one.

Which step breaks, and why did it look fine going in? If you tell me it's actually valid, then tell me which premise is cheapest to abandon and what I lose by abandoning it — including whether the loss hits the ordinary decision-theory answer about sunk costs or only reaches further. I'd rather keep (3) than swallow (6), but I'm not sure that's a considered preference and not just me flinching, so don't take it as a hint. And watch out for the case where the past fact *does* leak into the future by way of evidence (eight years tells me something about how hard this is, and about who I've become), because I suspect he'll say that's the whole answer and I suspect it isn't.
