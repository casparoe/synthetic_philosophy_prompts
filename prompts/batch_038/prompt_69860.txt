My consciousness reading group has been stuck on the color phi stuff for like six weeks now and I want to know why, because the structure of the argument feels weird. Recap so you know what I mean: Kolers and von Grünau ran Goodman's question into the ground back in the seventies — blue spot up top, red spot down below, brief gap, and subjects report a single dot travelling between them that changes colour somewhere in the middle of its path. But it cant change colour in the middle, since the only thing that could license a change is the second spot, which hasn't happened yet.

So you get the two readings. Orwellian: the dot really is experienced moving, red-then-blue or whatever, and afterwards the record gets quietly edited so the change lands mid-path — memory revision, history rewritten. Stalinesque: none of that ever occurred; the motion-plus-change narrative gets assembled once the second spot arrives and then antedated to the midpoint — the show is staged for the benefit of the party faithful after the fact. And then the move everybody makes: the two are behaviorally indistinguishable from the subject's chair, so asking which one happened just smuggles in a Cartesian theater.

The other camp won't accept that. They say there are discriminators: online responses during the gap should differ from reports; trajectory-extrapolation models make the change prospective so there's nothing to revise or antedate; postdiction work in vision science looks like it's picking a side. Nothing settles. Every new experiment gets claimed by both sides within a year.

Here's the thing I keep bringing up and no one engages with. Run two phi sequences back to back with a short jittered gap, and look for bleed: if sequence one's content is edited when sequence two's material arrives, the midpoint colour of sequence one should get pulled toward sequence two's colours. If each sequence is built and then antedated independently, no bleed. My prediction is that whichever way it comes out, both camps will describe the result with the same computational vocabulary and each will call it a win — which would mean the labels aren't doing any work at all.

So: diagnose the stall. Is this a verbal fight about what "the content of experience at time t" even means, a hidden empirical question about latencies wearing philosophical clothes, a disagreement about which things count as data (the subject's report vs. third-person timing), or a bedrock metaphysical dispute about whether experience has determinate contents at instants in the first place? And is the indistinguishability claim itself an argument rather than a finding? Tell me what would actually count as progress here — a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected and why.
