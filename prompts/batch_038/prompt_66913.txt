My opening move: here's how it looks from where I sit. A few years ago you told the world that natural selection was tautological — survival of the fittest just reports that the survivors survived. Then you changed your mind, and your fix was to reclassify the whole enterprise as a metaphysical research programme with testable theories growing under it. That move looks like it saves everything and explains nothing: the core principle gets exempted from testing by being labelled metaphysics, and whatever content survives lives down in the applications, which any working biologist could test without your philosophy and mostly did. So what would refute Darwinism as such? If the honest answer is that nothing could, because the framework isn't the sort of thing that can be refuted, then your recantation reads as you deciding for yourself, at your own convenience, which parts of a theory are up for falsification and which aren't. And there's the narrower worry: you now seem to say a biologist can look at a beetle and judge it well-built for its way of life before checking whether it in fact survives, which is supposed to show fitness isn't merely defined by surviving. But judging how well-built it is already assumes what that environment rewards, and that's exactly what's in question. So — what do you say?
