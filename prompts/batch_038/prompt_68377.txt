My roommate and I have been going in circles about Williams for like three weeks now and he finally told me straight out that my position doesn't hang together, so I sat down and wrote out everything I actually believe about this stuff, and I'd like someone to just go through it mechanically before I put it infront of anybody else. Here's the list, roughly in the order it came to me:

(1) The integrity objection lands. A theory that keeps every one of my ground projects permanently hostage to an impersonal calculation isn't a theory I can live inside, and that's a real objection to the theory, not just squeamishness on my part.
(2) The drowning case: a guy who can only save one of two people in equal peril and who needs to run a general impartial justification before pulling his own wife out of the water has one thought too many. Something has gone wrong there, and it isn't just that he's slow.
(3) But I'm also a two-level guy, criterion of rightness versus decision procedure. Utilitarianism can be true about what makes acts right while the best psychology for creatures like us is one that basically never runs the calculus. I think a lot of people who cite Williams just miss this move entirely, and I lean toward thinking it answers both (1) and (2).
(4) Negative responsibility is false. I'm not answerable for what other free agents decide to do, so in the Jim case Jim does the right thing in turning down Pedro's offer.
(5) Integrity isn't a moral consideration at all. I think of it as structural, like the integrity of a bridge — holding together under load, with no direction built into it. You can have it and be a monster. So when Jim refuses, it isn't a moral reason beating another moral reason; morality just lets him off the hook.
(6) Ground projects are what make a life worth living, and if nobody had any there'd be nothing for the utilitarian to promote in the first place. The whole enterprise presupposes them.
(7) Reasons internalism: I have a reason to phi only if there's a sound deliberative route from where my motivations actually are to phi-ing. And yet I totally agree with the Singer-style claim that I ought to be giving away a big chunk of my income, and I dont think I'm confused about reasons when I say that.
(8) On method, I want a theory that's usable. If the best a theory can tell me is "right acts are the ones the calculus picks, but you should never apply the calculus," I find that deeply unsatisfying, and I'd count it against the theory.

I'm not asking for a tour of Williams and I'm not asking which of these is true. I want an audit: which ones genuinely pull against each other, which ones only look like they do and are fine once you separate things out, and for the real conflicts, what has to go and what it costs me to give it up. Rank them by severity rather than listing everything as equally bad, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most at once. Also be straight with me about how confident you are in each diagnosis, and single out the one you're least sure of — I suspect at least one of these is a place where I've described my own view in a way that makes it look inconsistent when it really isn't, and you might be falling for that.
