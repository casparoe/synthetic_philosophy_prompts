I'm a software engineer at a mid-size foundation and I sit on the panel that renews our long-horizon research grants — I'm the "technical" member, the rest are an economist, a historian, and two program officers. Here's the case I have to write up.

A group we've funded for three straight five-year cycles built a model back in the 80s in the Malthus tradition: finite resource base, exponential demand growth, and a claim that the system overshoots and then corrects hard. The correction hasn't happened. Commodity prices fell, crop yields kept climbing, substitution kicked in wherever something got scarce. Every cycle their progress report says basically the same thing — the structure was right, the timing was off, and here's the revised parameter set. The scarce input has migrated from oil to phosphorus to freshwater, and the arrival date has moved from around 2000 to 2040 to "sometime within the century." They've also picked up a couple of things that did go their way, regional fisheries collapse and topsoil depletion, and they present those as partial vindication of the same structure.

The panel is split down the middle. Two want to cut them: a theory that can accommodate any outcome explains nothing, and at this point we're funding a vibe with a lot of equations attached. Two want to renew: Malthus gets declared wrong every fifty years and the underlying pressure is obviously real, the group trains excellent students, and their open datasets get used by people who don't cite them at all.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday. It needs a bottom line I can actually vote on — cut, renew, or renew with conditions, and if it's conditions, what they'd look like concretely enough that the group could tell whether they'd met them — plus the reasoning behind it, including an honest account of the strongest thing the renew side has going for it, not a strawman of it. One more thing: I keep reaching for machine-learning language for what bothers me here (post-hoc tuning, backtesting on the same data you fit on, a model that never gets falsified becuse you can always move the date), and I want you to tell me straight whether that vocabulary actually does the work, or whether "the theory was right, just early" has something in it that a model-selection framing misses.

No reading list, no citations, I'm not going to chase anything down. Under 500 words.
