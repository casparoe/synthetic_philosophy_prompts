--- QUESTION (20 points) ---
Christiano's iterated amplification characterizes HCH as the fixed point of a process in which a weak aligned model is used over and over to build a stronger aligned one.
(a) Describe the two steps of the outer loop and say what each of them contributes.
(b) State the objection that this account is question-begging — that the reason HCH comes out aligned already assumes the thing it was supposed to explain.
(c) Matuschak and Nielsen's factored-cognition program also proposes to handle thinking that's too hard for one mind by splitting it into many small pieces done by simpler components. Is HCH just factored cognition pointed at alignment? Argue either way.

--- STUDENT ANSWER ---
(a) There are two steps. Amplification takes the current model and lets it delegate: rather than answering a hard question in one pass, it breaks the question into subtasks, gets answers from copies of itself on those subtasks, and combines them, recursively, so that an enormous tree of cheap computation produces a single answer. HCH is the limit where the decomposition can go arbitrarily deep — a human consulting HCH, where HCH is a human consulting HCH. Distillation then takes the amplified system, which is wildly expensive because it involves running that whole tree, and trains a new model to imitate its outputs directly. Distillation is where the capability gain actually lands. The amplified tree is scaffolding; the distilled model is what carries the new competence forward into the next round.

(b) The objection is that the scheme only yields an aligned strong system if the weak model you start from is aligned, and aligned here means that it gives correct, trustworthy answers. But once the amplified system is smarter than us we have no independent way of checking whether its answers are correct — that was the original problem. So the argument that HCH is aligned presupposes that we can already recognise a good answer, which is what the account was meant to explain. The reply available to Christiano is that we only need honesty, not competence: an honest weak model is one trying to do what the user wants rather than trying to deceive them. And in the amplification step there is a check we can run, because we can test the amplified system on easier versions of the same question and see whether it gets those right; if it's honest, the checks carry over to the hard versions.

(c) Factored cognition says that a thought too large for a single mind can be carried by a system of tools and partial views, where each piece does something simple and the composition does something sophisticated. That is structurally the same as amplification: a tree of dumb components producing a smart output. The difference people point to is that factored cognition is about human thinking with tools while HCH is about building an autonomous agent, but that difference is mostly about who is holding the pencil. In both cases the intelligence is in the decomposition rather than in the parts, and in both cases there's a human at the root of the tree who sees the result. That's also what handles the strongest worry about composition, which is that individually honest parts might add up to something deceptive. If a human is at the root, the aggregate behaviour is being checked by the very party whose interests the system is supposed to serve. So HCH is factored cognition plus an alignment claim, and the alignment claim is the part that needs the extra argument.
