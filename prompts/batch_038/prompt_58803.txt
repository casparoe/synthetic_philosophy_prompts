You are helping me prepare for a metaphysics department colloquium at which I will defend the following thesis: the distinction between finks and masks does not survive scrutiny. On the standard taxonomy, a fink removes the intrinsic basis of a disposition when the stimulus occurs, whereas a mask leaves the basis intact but interferes with the manifestation, and it is commonly held that the reformed conditional analysis that repairs finkish cases — by requiring that the object retain its basis through to the response — remains vulnerable to masking. My argument is that this asymmetry is an artifact of coarse individuation. Masking always operates at some stage of a multi-stage causal process running from a disposition's basis to its canonical manifestation, so if dispositions are individuated finely enough — at the level of the intermediate causal roles that the basis actually discharges — every mask turns out to be a fink of some further disposition further down the chain. The upshot I want to press is that the conditional-analysis project is salvageable after all, at the price of a considerably more permissive ontology of dispositions than its defenders typically advertise.

The audience: mostly analytic metaphysicians who deploy the standard taxonomy without qualms; one dispositional essentialist who thinks analyzing powers in terms of counterfactuals gets the direction of explanation backwards; and two philosophers of science who treat disposition ascriptions as shorthand for nomological claims and will worry that fine-grained individuation makes disposition talk unfalsifiable.

Give me the question period rather than a defense of the view. I want the eight or so objections this particular audience will press, ordered by how much damage they would do, each phrased in the register the person raising it would actually use, with a reply I can deliver in about two minutes and an honest note on where that reply is soft. Mark which objections I should partly concede inside the talk itself, and close with the one question I am hoping nobody asks — the one for which my position currently has no good answer.
