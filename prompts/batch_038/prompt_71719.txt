ok, I've been reading the akrasia threads for like a decade and I finally wrote down what I actually believe about it, mostly to see if it survives contact. here's the set:

1. Inconsistent discounting over time is an error, not a taste. Someone who weighs a delay more heavily when it's near is failing at something, the same way someone who flips a preference under an irrelevant reframe is.
2. Hyperbolic discounting is the best model of my own behavior, and when I eat the cake I'm not speaking for my far self. I dont want to count that near-slice preference as mine.
3. So precommitment is obviously rational. I've got an app that donates $50 to a cause I despise every time I skip the gym, and that's my far self legitimately binding my near self.
4. But I think it's illegitimate when anyone else does the same thing to me for my own good — a nudging state, an employer, an AI that's modelled my weak spots. The line about it improving my revealed welfare doesn't move me at all.
5. I'm a Parfit-ish reductionist about identity: no deep further fact, just psychological connections coming in degrees. There's no fact of the matter about which time-slice is the real me.
6. And I think a sophisticated hyperbolic agent who knows he'll cave later isn't irrational for planning around his own caving. Future defection is a constraint, like weather.

does this hang together? I want an audit, not a lecture: which pairs genuinely pull against each other, ranked by severity, and for each one the cheapest revision plus what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Also say which tensions are only apparent — I keep worrying about 3 vs 6 and I cant tell whether that one's real or whether I'm chasing a ghost.
