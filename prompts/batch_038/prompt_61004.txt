You are an expert in metaethics and normative ethical theory with substantial experience designing and marking final-year examinations. Please think carefully before answering: begin with a short paragraph of brainstorming in which you identify where the real crux of the topic lies and which versions of the question students can answer purely from memory, and only then produce the materials.

I am a doctoral candidate assisting with the assessment for a third-year normative ethics module, and I need one essay question on whether genuine moral dilemmas are possible — a situation in which an agent is under two obligations that cannot both be discharged and where no available course of action is all-things-considered better than the alternative. The question must be answerable in 90 minutes and must not be answerable by reciting the standard survey: it is not enough for a student to report that some philosophers think dilemmas are possible and others think the concept is incoherent. I want the question to force students to engage with the machinery — for instance, the question of which principle is actually doing the work when someone denies that dilemmas are possible (that obligation implies ability, or that obligations aggregate over conjunction, or something else entirely), and the question of what a theory that rules dilemmas out owes us by way of an account of what remains normative about the rejected option: regret, compensation, apology, the sense that something was owed and not merely outweighed.

Please supply: (a) the essay question itself, stated precisely enough that a student can see what counts as addressing it; (b) a model answer of roughly 700 words; and (c) a marking scheme totalling 25 marks, distributed over five named moves, each with the marks it carries and a one-line note on the characteristic way in which students attempt but fail that move. Where you can use a concrete case to sharpen the question — Sartre's student choosing between joining the Free French forces and remaining with his mother, or a case of your own construction — do so, but make clear in the marking scheme that mere description of a case earns no marks. Do not include a reading list, bibliography, or references of any kind; the deliverable is the question, the answer, and the marking scheme.
