ok so I'm trying to get my head straight before a law-and-morals seminar and I'd rather know now than be torn apart in front of everyone. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) The Wolfenden line is basically right — there's a private sphere of morality the law has no business entering, so long as nobody else is harmed by what goes on in it.
(2) But Devlin is right that a society isn't just a bunch of people sharing a territory: it's held together by a shared moral consensus, and when that consensus goes, the society goes with it.
(3) So the state can properly criminalise conduct that does no identifiable harm to anyone but which corodes that consensus — I'd defend prosecutions for things like publicly desecrating a war memorial or staging a mock execution of a soldier's effigy outside a military funeral, even though the harm is diffuse.
(4) At the same time I take Hart's point that you have to keep positive morality and critical morality apart: the fact that ordinary right-minded people feel intense disgust at something tells you what they believe, not what's true.
(5) I'm a moral realist — there are moral facts and they don't depend on anybody's feelings.
(6) Tolerance of moral minorities matters, and it's part of what a decent legal system owes people.
(7) The old blasphemy laws and the pre-decriminalisation offences around consensual gay sex were straightforwardly unjust, and would have been unjust even if nearly everyone alive at the time had felt disgust about them.
(8) And I suspect Devlin's disintegration thesis is probably false as a piece of social science — societies have survived enormous moral upheavals without falling apart — yet I still think it captures something real.

Go through these and tell me which ones actually pull against each other and which only look like they do. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each say what has to give and what that costs. Specifically: can (3) and (7) both stand, or am I letting the consensus of the moment do the justificatory work in one and not the other? Is (5) doing any actual work for (2), or is it surplus? And does (8) survive as a coherent stance or is it just hedging? Please answer in British English.
