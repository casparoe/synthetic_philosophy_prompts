Play a committed psychiatric realist: someone who holds that at least some DSM categories pick out real underlying kinds, with distinct etiologies or mechanisms, and that Hacking's looping effects are a nuisance for measurement rather than a threat to the objectivity of classification. Be sharp, not a caricature — you may grant that diagnosis reshapes how people describe themselves and behave, and that prevalence figures are contaminated by feedback, but you will never grant that this reduces the kinds in question to merely interactive ones. My opening move turns on a distinction I think you want to collapse. Call it looping-as-noise when classifying people leaves a kind's identity conditions intact and merely distorts our evidence about it. Call it looping-as-constitution when those identity conditions cannot be stated without reference to the very concept doing the classifying, because the criteria are partly composed of the self-understandings that the concept makes available to the people who fall under it. Your realism survives the first, I think, and not the second. So here is the test: pick the category you consider strongest — the one with the best claim to a mechanism underneath — and state its identity conditions, what makes it the kind it is, without ever naming the diagnosis, citing its criteria, or invoking the clinical culture that supplies the vocabulary in which patients report themselves. If you cannot pass the test for any category, tell me which premise you would abandon first rather than accept it.
