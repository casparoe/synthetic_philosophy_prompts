Context: one of us is drafting something friendly to the hedonistic imperative line (the Pearce-ish proposal that we should use gene editing, and later other biotech, to raise everyone's hedonic set-point until nothing worse than mild discomfort is left in the world, so that suffering gets phased out on purpose rather than by luck). I volunteered to steelman the old objections to that, and I picked up Beyond Good and Evil §225 because it looks like it was aimed almost exactly at this kind of programme, roughly a century before anyone proposed CRISPR for it. The text below is the old public-domain Helen Zimmern translation, so the prose is creaky and the capitals are his:

"Whether it be hedonism, pessimism, utilitarianism, or eudaemonism, all those modes of thinking which measure the worth of things according to PLEASURE and PAIN, that is, according to accompanying circumstances and secondary considerations, are plausible modes of thought and naivetes, which every one conscious of CREATIVE powers and an artist's conscience will look down upon with scorn, though not without sympathy. Sympathy for you!--to be sure, that is not sympathy as you understand it: it is not sympathy for social "distress," for "society" with its sick and misfortuned, for the hereditarily vicious and defective who lie on the ground around us; still less is it sympathy for the grumbling, vexed, revolutionary slave-classes who strive after power--they call it "freedom." OUR sympathy is a loftier and further-sighted sympathy:--we see how MAN dwarfs himself, how YOU dwarf him! and there are moments when we view YOUR sympathy with an indescribable anguish, when we resist it,--when we regard your seriousness as more dangerous than any kind of levity. You want, if possible--and there is not a more foolish "if possible"--TO DO AWAY WITH SUFFERING; and we?--it really seems that WE would rather have it increased and made worse than it has ever been! Well-being, as you understand it--is certainly not a goal; it seems to us an END; a condition which at once renders man ludicrous and contemptible--and makes his destruction DESIRABLE! The discipline of suffering, of GREAT suffering--know ye not that it is only THIS discipline that has produced all the elevations of humanity hitherto? The tension of soul in misfortune which communicates to it its energy, its shuddering in view of rack and ruin, its inventiveness and bravery in undergoing, enduring, interpreting, and exploiting misfortune, and whatever depth, mystery, disguise, spirit, artifice, or greatness has been bestowed upon the soul--has it not been bestowed through suffering, through the discipline of great suffering? In man CREATURE and CREATOR are united: in man there is not only matter, shred, excess, clay, mire, folly, chaos; but there is also the creator, the sculptor, the hardness of the hammer, the divinity of the spectator, and the seventh day--do ye understand this contrast? And that YOUR sympathy for the "creature in man" applies to that which has to be fashioned, bruised, forged, stretched, roasted, annealed, refined--to that which must necessarily SUFFER, and IS MEANT to suffer? And our sympathy--do ye not understand what our REVERSE sympathy applies to, when it resists your sympathy as the worst of all pampering and enervation?--So it is sympathy AGAINST sympathy!--But to repeat it once more, there are higher problems than the problems of pleasure and pain and sympathy; and all systems of philosophy which deal only with these are naivetes."

Here's what I cant get straight. (a) It reads like two different complaints, and I don't know whether they're one or two. Early on the charge is about *measuring* worth by pleasure and pain — that sounds like an objection to the currency itself, pleasure and pain being mere "accompanying circumstances and secondary considerations" rather than what makes anything valuable. Later the charge is that they want to do away with suffering and that he'd rather have it increased and made worse. Is the second just the practical upshot of the first, or an independent claim that suffering is good for something? It matters to me: if the objection is only about currency, someone could accept a hedonically upgraded world and still agree the upgrade wasn't the point. (b) What kind of claim is "it is only THIS discipline that has produced all the elevations of humanity hitherto" — empirical-causal, and so open to evidence about whether depth or creativity really requires agony; conceptual, so that "elevation" just means resistance overcome and a painless species couldn't be elevated by definition; or a claim about what a life has to contain to be worth living? Say which reading the passage best supports, and which one makes it a live objection to a biotech abolitionist rather than a period curiosity. (c) Scope: he keeps saying GREAT suffering and "the tension of soul in misfortune", so is there room for the abolitionist to concede difficulty, effort, chosen hardship and frustration while deleting agony, grief, chronic pain? Or does the well-being-is-"an END", renders man "ludicrous and contemptible" sentence close that door, since the complaint there looks like it's about the target rather than the means? Related: what is the CREATURE/CREATOR, clay-and-hammer contrast doing — is the point that the material to be shaped would vanish, or that the shaping just is making something suffer? (d) The "reverse sympathy ... sympathy AGAINST sympathy" bit sounds like a diagnosis of the abolitionists' motives instead of an argument against their proposal; if that's right, is there any normative content left in the section at all, or can an abolitionist shrug the whole thing off as psychology?
