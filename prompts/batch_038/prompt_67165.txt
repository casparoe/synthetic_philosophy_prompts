I'm a care coordinator — mostly older people and people with progressive disabilities — in a place where assisted dying is legal, and part of my job is sometimes walking someone through how to request it. There's a thing I keep running into that I have no name for, which makes it nearly impossible to raise with colleagues, so I'm hoping someone can help me pin it down.

A person tells me they want to die. Calmly. Not in crisis — they've been sitting on it a year or two. They're not clinically depressed as far as anyone can tell, they clear every assessment, they understand exactly what's being offered. Then you actually listen to the list of reasons and most of it goes like this: my daughter lives four hours away and can't get here. My flat has stairs and there's no money for a lift or a ground-floor unit. I've been on the home-care waitlist eight months and get ninety minutes a week, which isn't enough to both shower and eat properly. If it gets worse I'll go into a facility I hate, and I've seen what those are like. The suffering is completely real. But almost all of it is the kind of thing a housing office, a care budget, or a family that lived closer would just dissolve.

And the part I can't articulate is this. Its not that they've talked themselves into being fine with a bad situation — nobody's lowered their expectations, nobody's pretending, if anything they're seeing it more clearly than their relatives do. Nobody's pushing them, the family usually wants them to keep going. Their judgment isn't clouded. So every phrase I reach for — they've adapted to it, they're being coerced by their circumstances, it isn't really their own decision — comes out wrong, because the decision is theirs, it's clear eyed, and the circumstances are accurately perceived. It's more that they've been handed a menu where the only item that's guaranteed and actually available is a dignified legal exit, and they've correctly picked it. Signing off on it feels like we're writing down a social failure in the language of a personal fact. I want a name for that specific move, not for the general mess.

So: does this have an established concept? I know people gesture at structural stuff, I know there's a long-running argument about disability and assisted dying, and I know about the idea that our preferences get bent toward whatever happens to be on offer — but I think what I'm seeing isn't that last one, precisely because no one's expectations have been bent downward. How close is each of those, where exactly does my case sit relative to them, and which one fits best? And if nothing fits properly, what would you call it, and what are the edge cases — at what point does a request stop being this thing and start just being unbearable suffering with no social component anybody could have fixed? I'm not after a label to win an argument, I need to know what the thing is and what it isn't.
