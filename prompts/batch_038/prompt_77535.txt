I am teaching a philosophy of mind seminar, and we are screening Inception alongside the dispute over whether dreams are experiences at all. On one side sits Malcolm in Dreaming (1959): the claim that a sleeper has experiences is a piece of bad grammar, the sense of a dream report is fixed entirely by what happens on waking, and there is no further fact about an episode that ran through the night. On the other, Dennett's multiple-drafts picture: there is no moment at which content is presented to an inner audience, what gets written down in memory is what counts as conscious, and cases that look like censorship before the fact (Stalinesque) cannot be told apart from cases where the experience occurred and was afterwards rewritten (Orwellian).

I want a reading of the film that treats both as positions about dreams rather than as skeptical scenery. Inception's whole enterprise — extraction, inception, the architect who builds a world a subject will inhabit — seems to require that something is going on with the dreamer which could have gone otherwise, that there is a place secrets can be taken from. Is that right, or can a Malcolmian read the machinery as dramatizing his own thesis? Press hard on the totem: Cobb's spinning top is offered as a test for whether one is dreaming, applied from inside the dream. What does Malcolm's argument imply about a test of that kind, and what does the film's refusal to resolve the final shot imply about its own commitments? Then consider whether the team's interventions — Ariadne folding Paris, Arthur's corridor, the projections turning to look at the dreamer — are Stalinesque or Orwellian revisions, and whether Fischer's implanted idea, which arrives for him with the felt authority of an authentic memory, favors one side of the debate.

Take a definite position on which view the film's logic ultimately serves and defend it; please do not settle for the conclusion that Inception sustains both readings equally well. Where your case depends on a contestable interpretation of Malcolm or of Dennett, flag it and say what would follow if I rejected it. Close by telling me what, if anything, the film contributes to this argument that neither side's reasoning supplies on its own.
