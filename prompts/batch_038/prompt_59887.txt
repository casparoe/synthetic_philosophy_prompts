I'm building a small taxonomy of genealogical debunking arguments — the family of moves where you point at the causal history of a belief (evolution, cultural transmission, upbringing, whatever) and claim that history defeats its justification. Before I sort anything into it, I want the spec: what does a theory of genealogical defeat have to deliver to count as adequate? Not the arguments, the criteria.

Purpose, so you know what "adequate" is relative to: I want it as a filter. It should tell me, for an arbitrary piece of reasoning, whether it's a debunking argument at all or just a genealogy with attitude, and it should tell me when one is actually good.

So give me the requirements. The ones about the explanation itself — does it have to leave the truth of the belief out entirely, or is it enough that the process didn't track it? does it need more than "this belief had a suspicious history"? does it need an independent handle on unreliability, and where is that handle supposed to come from if not from more intuitions of the same kind the target is relying on? The ones about scope — must it be selective, or can a global version be coherent, and what happens to the debunker's own normative claims if it isn't. And the ones about what the debunker may assume without begging the question against the realist. For each requirement, the case or consideration that motivates it: I want to see why it's a requirement and not just a habit of one camp.

Then mark which are non-negotiable and which are really partisan preferences dressed up as standards, and say which single one knocks out the most candidates. Most of all: if some subset can't be jointly satisfied — explanatory irrelevance plus non-question-begging plus selectivity, say — show that explicitly, because then the list is itself a result and Id rather know now.

Last and short: the three arguments people actually press — the dilemma-style one aimed at realism about value, the global one aimed at morality as such, and a cultural-transmission one aimed at fairness norms — do they survive your filter? One line each, I'll argue with you after.

Bottom line first, please: your very first sentence should be your verdict on which requirement does the most work, then the reasoning. No preamble, no restating the question back to me.
