Been chewing on a paper about dispositions for a while and I've finally got a draft argument down, but I'm not sure it holds together. Rather than the whole thing, here's the core in fairly full — could you tell me where it actually goes wrong?

**Finks, masks and the myth of the background**

1. The simple conditional analysis says that x is disposed at t to respond r to stimulus s just in case x would r if x were s'd at t. Finkish cases break it. Martin's wire, hooked to an electro-fink that kills its conductivity the moment anything touches it, is still disposed to conduct when touched, but it would not conduct if touched — the stimulus itself removes the basis. So the analysandum is true and the conditional false.

2. Lewis's repair relocates the analysis inside the object: x is disposed to r when s iff x has some intrinsic property B such that, were x to be s'd and to retain B, then the s-ing together with retaining B would cause r. The finked wire is excluded because it doesn't retain the relevant basis.

3. Masks are supposed to break the repair in a different way. Here the object keeps its basis and gets the stimulus, but something extrinsic interferes with the manifestation rather than with the disposition: the genuinely fragile glass packed in foam, the cube that would dissolve wrapped in something that neutralises the water, the driver whose disposition to brake is blocked by a passenger's hand on the wheel. Mimics go the other direction — the conditional comes out true without the disposition, because an interfering agent produces the manifestation anyway.

4. My claim is that the fink/mask/mimic taxonomy is not a taxonomy of kinds of counterexample but a taxonomy of *when the interference happens relative to the stimulus*. In the fink case the interference is triggered by the stimulus; in the mask case it is already in place; in the mimic case it substitutes for the manifestation. That is a difference in timing and topology, not in kind. Which means the debate has been mis-framed: nobody has ever been looking for a conditional analysis that survives interference, because no conditional analysis can. Interference is always logically possible, and any conditional has to be indexed to a specification of background conditions in which no interference occurs. The real question was always whether that specification can be given non-circularly and non-arbitrarily, and the answer, I think, is no — the only way to say which conditions are "normal" is by reference to the dispositions we were trying to analyse.

5. Consequence. If dispositions are real, the conditional isn't an analysis at all. It's an idealisation, in the same family as the frictionless plane or the point mass: a model that is true relative to a stipulated background, useful precisely because the background is almost never fully satisfied. On that picture the ceteris paribus clause isn't a bug to be engineered away but constitutive, and the whole project of reducing dispositions to strict conditionals is a category mistake.

6. Which makes me suspicious of the intrinsicness industry that grew up around the fink cases. Lewis's move was to secure dispositionality by anchoring it in an intrinsic basis that survives the stimulus. But if 5 is right, where the disposition is located does no work: what does the work is the triple of object, stimulus and background plus an interest in a particular explanation. Fragility isn't in the glass, and it isn't outside the glass either; the question is malformed. So the disputes about whether duplicates can differ in their dispositions inherit the relativity rather than settling it.

7. Objection I know is coming: this makes dispositional ascriptions unfalsifiable. If every failure can be blamed on interference, dispositions stop being the thing that was supposed to give ceteris paribus laws non-vacuous truthmakers, and we end up with everything having every disposition under some background or other. My reply is that the idealisation is disciplined by actuality — an ascription is licensed only where the object does manifest often enough in cases where the specified background genuinely obtains. Interest-relative, not anything-goes.
