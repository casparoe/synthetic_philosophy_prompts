I'm a third-year philosophy PhD student, and my committee wants a dissertation prospectus by the end of term — roughly fifteen hundred words, describing a project I can realistically finish in three years. The occasion is personal: over the past eighteen months I have outsourced a growing share of my own work to language models, from synthesizing literatures to checking whether a proof step goes through to hunting for counterexamples to claims I want to make, and I can no longer reconstruct from memory arguments I could once reproduce in detail. I want the prospectus to ask whether that is a loss of understanding or a relocation of it.

My half-formed idea, which I would like sharpened rather than replaced: the standard debate pits those who say understanding was always distributed across testifiers and the model is merely a new testifier against those who hold that understanding why is a cognitive achievement no testimony can deliver. Both sides assume a single thing is being handed over. I suspect the cases that matter involve a third capacity — being able to regenerate a result yourself when the question is varied — together with a condition I want to call auditability: outsourcing is benign only so long as the outsourcer retains the capacity to check what comes back, and once that capacity is itself outsourced, the regress terminates somewhere. Where it terminates is the real question.

Please give me the question stated so that it has a determinate answer; why the two existing positions leave it open; the method, chapter by chapter, and why that method rather than the obvious empirical alternative of measuring retention among heavy users; what a successful result would look like and what would count as failure; and an honest assessment of where the scope is likely to break. Refer to positions and arguments, not to authors — I will supply the citations myself.
