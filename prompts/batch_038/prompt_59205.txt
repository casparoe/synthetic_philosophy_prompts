so I've been going in circles on the quantum immortality thing and I want to know whether there's an actual principle behind my gut calls, or whether some of them just have to go.

here's my verdicts. One pull of a gun rigged to a single atom — fifty/fifty — I'd take that over a normal revolver with three live rounds in it, even tho the odds are exactly the same. But a thousand pulls of the same atom gun and I'd refuse, not because I think I'd die but because I figure in a lot of the branches where I'm still going I'm going with half my head missing. When they put me under for surgery I feel none of that reassurance, and I think that's me being inconsistent rather than the idea being wrong. If a friend plays the atom gun in front of me I fully expect them to drop on the first pull and I'd be floored if they made ten. And if the branch has me in a permanent coma instead of dead, I count that as losing — which makes me think what's on offer can't be "I'll never die," only something weaker like "I'll never notice myself stopping."

My own half-baked fix, which I don't trust: maybe surviving isn't all-or-nothing. Maybe a low-weight branch has a thinner version of me in it, so surviving there is like half-surviving, and that's why I can say both "I'll be in every branch" and "act like there's a 99% chance."

Give me the principle — or the two or three real candidates — stated precisely enough that I could apply it myself, with which of the cases above each bit of it is there to handle. Then tell me what it says about a version I haven't worked out: the atom gets measured today and the result is recorded and watched by a bunch of other people, but the gun doesn't fire until next Tuesday. And be straight about which of my verdicts it can't keep. Don't just hand me a menu and let me pick — say which one you think is right and argue for it.
