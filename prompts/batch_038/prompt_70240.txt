writing up my position for a decision theory reading group so people can take shots at it. here's roughly where i've landed:

1. i buy the Dutch book argument: if your betting quotients break the probability axioms, a bookie can sell you a package of bets you'd each accept individually that loses you money come what may — and that's what makes incoherent credences irrational, not merely unprofitable.
2. but i don't think credences just *are* betting dispositions. i find the betting interpretation kind of behaviourist baggage; i think of a credence as an internal state with lots of downstream effects, only some of which are cash.
3. i think the same style of argment gets you conditionalization: someone who plans now to update some other way can be caught by a pair of bets, one offered before the evidence arrives and one after.
4. i'm relaxed about idealizing away logical ignorance — finite minds aren't literally required to be coherent, it's a limit idealization, no big deal.
5. and i think this family of arguments is what vindicates expected utility maximization as such, all the way up to the independence axiom — coherence isn't just a constraint on credences, it gets you EU-maximizing choice.

which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? rank the tensions by how bad they are, say what i'd have to give up in each case, and then tell me the single cheapest revision overall. and if i'm running two different arguments together as though they were one, call that out.
