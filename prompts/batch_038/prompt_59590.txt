I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, but before that I spent about six years doing biostatistics — almost all of it time-to-event work. Kaplan-Meier curves, Cox models, competing risks, proportional-hazards diagnostics, the whole apparatus. I'm still fluent, I read methods sections for fun, and I can spot a censoring screwup at thirty paces.

Here's my problem. I keep getting pitched versions of the same argument and I need it translated into the only language I actually trust before I can write about it honestly. The argument goes: stop squinting at the details of the present and use the base rate from history instead. Identify every hegemon or great empire you can, measure how long each one sat at the top, and use that distribution to put a number on how long the current one has left. The draft I'm working from has a chart with a median of roughly 110 years, a note that we're already past the 60th percentile of the historical distribution, and a line treating "this time is different" as precisely the inside-view error the method exists to correct. Some of this stuff borrows language from the cliodynamics people, some comes from finance types doing big-history cycles; the shape is identical either way.

So: restate that argument as faithfully as you can in survival-analysis terms. Empires as subjects, time at the top as time-at-risk, loss of primacy as the event. And keep it honest — don't quietly import your own assumptions and hand them back to me wearing a historian's clothes. What maps cleanly? What only maps if you bend it? What can't be said in my vocabulary at all?

Four places I want you to actually stop and work rather then gesture:

- The draft drops the hegemons that are still standing on the grounds that they "haven't finished yet." I know that's backwards from what you'd do in a trial, but I want the reason stated in *this* setting. What does right-censoring even mean when the censored subject is still the dominant power on the planet and there's no administrative study end date?
- The event definition. "Collapse" gets operationalized as falling out of the top slot on a composite index for a generation or more. Is there anything in my toolkit that corresponds to that, or is the hazard just not a hazard?
- Non-independence. These empires overlap, fight each other, and one's fall is frequently another's rise. Do I have any legitimate way to talk about that, or does it take out the reference class entirely?
- Selection at the threshold: we only count polities that got big enough to be counted. Left truncation, or something worse?

Then the part I actually care about, which is the residue. Where something refuses to translate, tell me which of two things is happening. Either my vocabulary is too thin — it can only handle exchangeable, repeatable subjects, and history genuinely isn't made of those, so there's a real claim here that I'm structurally deaf to — or the original argument was never as well-formed as the chart makes it look, and the failure to translate is the tell. My priors are that it's mostly the second with a little of the first, but push back if you disagree.

American English, please. I want the reasoning, not a reading list — nobody's fact-checking my citations, I just need to know what survives the trip.
