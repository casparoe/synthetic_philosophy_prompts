Please make a sustained argument for the following thesis: that the thirder answer to the Sleeping Beauty problem is the correct one, and that the decisive consideration in its favour is van Fraassen's Reflection principle rather than any of the routes usually canvassed.

Begin by fixing the case as economically as you can, since I am familiar with it. Beauty is told the whole protocol before she is put to sleep: a fair coin is tossed; if it lands heads she is woken and interviewed once, on Monday; if it lands tails she is woken and interviewed on Monday and again on Tuesday; after each interview she is given a drug that erases her memory of that awakening, so that on being woken she cannot tell what day it is or whether she has been woken before. Then record the two sets of credences accurately. On the halfer account, due to Lewis, Beauty's credence in heads is one half on Sunday, rises to two thirds once she awakens on Monday without being told what day it is, and becomes one once she is told that it is Monday. On the thirder account, due to Elga, her credence in heads on awakening is one third, becoming one half once she is told it is Monday. Do not argue for either set of numbers from indifference considerations, from the frequency of awakenings across repeated experiments, or from betting odds; those are the standard routes and I want them set aside.

The argument I want is this one, and you should develop it in the following order.

First, show that the halfer view violates Reflection as van Fraassen formulated it. On Sunday, Beauty knows the protocol in full, and so she is certain that her Monday self, upon awakening and before being told the day, will assign two thirds to heads. Reflection requires that your present credence in a proposition, conditional on your later credence in that proposition being some value r, should itself be r; because the antecedent is certain here, the conditional collapses and Reflection simply requires Sunday credence in heads to be two thirds. Since the halfer assigns one half, the violation is not a delicate matter of borderline application but a direct contradiction. Make the conditional explicit rather than gesturing at it, and explain what it would take for a principle with this form to be anything other than trivially satisfied or trivially breached.

Second, meet the obvious reply: that Reflection, stated this bluntly, is far too strong to be a requirement of rationality, and that it is well known to misfire in cases where you expect your future self to be impaired, drugged, misinformed, deprived of evidence you now have, or otherwise unfit to be deferred to. Concede what should be conceded here, and then argue that the counterexamples do not touch the restricted principle you actually need, namely that deference is owed to a future self whom you expect to have arrived at her opinion by conditionalising on your present evidence together with some further evidence she will acquire, with nothing lost in between. Explain why this restricted principle is independently well motivated — why, in effect, it is nothing over and above conditionalisation applied across time — and why the halfer's one half to two thirds move falls outside its scope.

Third, and this is the most important part, engage seriously with the halfer's best defence. Lewis is not simply careless about evidence: her point is that Beauty acquires no new information about the coin, and her explanation of the shift to two thirds appeals instead to the indexical or self-locating character of Monday's credence, so that Sunday's and Monday's doxastic attitudes may not be directed at the same content at all. Put this objection in its strongest form: that Reflection is not merely false in the Sleeping Beauty case but not even well defined there, because there is no single proposition that both Sunday Beauty and Monday Beauty are credencing, and hence no antecedent of the form 'my later credence in p is r' with a p that persists across the two times. Then argue your way out. The dilemma I want you to press is this: either the contents are the same, in which case the halfer owes an account of how a credence can move from one half to two thirds without any conditionalisable change in evidence; or the contents differ, in which case the halfer has surrendered the resource that made her view attractive, since the whole appeal of one half was supposed to be that the coin is fair and nothing has been learned about it. Take both horns seriously and do not simply assert the dilemma.

Fourth, show that your argument does not prove too much. On the thirder account Beauty's credence also changes when she is told that it is Monday, from one third to one half. Explain why that shift is licensed by the restricted principle while the halfer's is not, and check explicitly that the thirder view is stable at every point in the protocol at which the restricted principle applies — including the Monday awakening itself and the Tuesday awakening, where Beauty's situation is subjectively indistinguishable from Monday's under tails.

Write this as continuous argumentative prose of roughly fourteen hundred words, with the four parts marked by headings but developed as argument rather than as a list of points. You may refer to positions and arguments by name, but do not supply citations, page references, or a bibliography; I will add those myself. Do not hedge into a balanced survey of the debate, and do not end by saying that the matter is unresolved or that reasonable people disagree: the deliverable is a case for one side.

Conclude with a short, clearly marked section stating the strongest objection to your own argument that you have not managed to answer, and specifying what would have to be true for that objection to succeed.
