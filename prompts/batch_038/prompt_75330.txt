I've been cataloguing my supervisor's verdicts on lives for our well-being seminar, and I want to predict the next one.

Her calls so far. The surgeon who drops a promising side career in plant taxonomy to run a free clinic: admirable but a loss, and she's emphatic the loss is to *his* life, not just to the world's stock of botanical knowledge. The cellist who practises five hours a day and gives away a fifth of her income: no defect whatsoever, she'd recommend that life to her kids. The guy who donates half his salary but is a distant, dutiful father: a real failing — and she insists the failing is moral, not prudential or aesthetic. The retired teacher who reads novels and cooks elaborate meals purely because it keeps her sane enough for her volunteer shifts: fine, but not what she'd want. Someone who gives up music and fiction on the grounds that they don't help anyone: strongly negative, he's got something wrong about value and it isn't a moral mistake. A world in which everyone is a saint: worse — and she stresses that this is a *different* objection from the first one, not a bigger version of it.

Thursday's case: a hospice nurse who works seventy hours a week, gives away most of what's left, has no hobbies, no taste in food, no friendships outside work, never read a novel for pleasure — and is cheerful about all of it, says she wouldn't trade her life for anything, feels no sense of loss, and picked this life reflectively at 25 after genuinely weighing alternatives, so its wholeheartedly hers rather than something she fell into.

Predict her verdict, split the way she'd split it: admirable? a good life *for her*? a life to recommend? Then tell me which principle she's running. I can see at least three that fit everything above — an objective-list view on which the non-moral goods are missing whether or not she notices, a view on which what matters is whether your projects are your own rather than held on loan from morality, and a view on which the complaint is only about the pattern of lives across a world — and they don't agree here. Say what each predicts Thursday, which you'd bet on and why, and design me the one case that would seperate them. Also: is she just Wolf with extra steps, or is there something here Wolf doesn't cover?
