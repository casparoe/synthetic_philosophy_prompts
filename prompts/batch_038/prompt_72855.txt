I run a reading group on analogy and concepts, and while reconstructing the Copycat letter-string puzzles I built an argument whose conclusion I cannot accept. The canonical puzzle is: abc changes to abd, so what should ijk change to? I built a variant that exposes the trouble: abc changes to abd, so what should cba change to? The change in the source admits of descriptions that fit it equally well (replace the rightmost letter with its successor; replace the alphabetically greatest letter with its successor; replace the letter c with the letter d), and in abc all of them pick out the same change.

(1) Which description a solver carries into a new string is fixed by which associated concepts are most active when the mapping is made, and activation in the slipnet is graded, shifting with the string and from run to run.
(2) In cba the descriptions diverge, since the rightmost letter is a and the alphabetically greatest is c; so nothing but the activation dynamics settles whether the answer is cbb or dba.
(3) If nothing but the dynamics settles it, there is no fact of the matter about which mapping is correct: the output is not an answer to a question but a residue of how the run went.
(4) Our own categorizing works the same way. Which features of a case we carry into a novel one is fixed by graded, context-sensitive association, not by a rule that determines the extension in advance.
(5) So there is no fact of the matter about whether a novel case falls under a concept either, and nothing makes our verdicts correct rather than incorrect.
(6) That cannot stand: it would leave a solver that answers at random indistinguishable from one that answers well, and it would leave me with nothing to be surprised about when I discover that I said dba.
(7) Therefore (1) or (4) is false.

I would rather keep (4) than (6). Which step do you reject, and why did it look innocent? Price the alternatives for me: what I lose by rejecting (1), what I lose by rejecting (4), and what becomes of the claim that some answers to these puzzles are better than others. If instead you judge the argument sound, say which premise is cheapest to abandon and what that does to the idea that slippage is a discovery about concepts rather than an artifact of the architecture.
