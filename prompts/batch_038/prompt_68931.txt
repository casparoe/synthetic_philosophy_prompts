hey, I'm putting together a small fiction project about philosophy of biology and I'd like a short story from you — no need to look anything up, everything you need is below.

The setup is one I invented. A domed settlement on a terraformed world, about 140 years after the founding stock landed. The settlement runs an office called the Registry of Functions: nothing gets released into the wild commons, repaired, culled, bred from, or patented untill a Registrar has certifi ed what its parts are *for*. The founding charter says a part counts as having a function only if it's there because ancestors that had it left more offspring than ancestors that didn't — selection history, and nothing else. A dissident faction inside the Registry thinks that's insane in their particular situation, because every organism on this world has been shaped for a century and a half by conditions no ancestor was ever shaped by, so the charter rule declares most of the local fauna to be functionless lump and noise. What they want the forms to ask instead is what a part actually contributes, right now, to the capacities of the whole organism — and they point out that their rule at least lets you say when something is broken, whereas on the charter rule a part with no history can't malfunction, it just *is*.

The case that splits the office: a herd animal descended from stock engineered for meat has grown a dense fibrous sac wrapped around its second stomach. It digests nothing. It does seem to hold core temperature steady through the dome's cold nights, and the animals born without it go lethargic and stop breeding. It also, in maybe one animal in fifty, twists and strangulates the gut, and those animals die slowly and badly in front of children. Under the charter it has no function whatsoever, so it can't be defective either. Under the dissidents' rule it plainly does have one — and then what are the fifty? Malfunctioning? Working as designed? Both?

Write this as exactly five numbered scenes, a paragraph or two each, and nothing else: no title, no preamble, no wrap-up after scene five. Some constraints on the scenes:

1. Nobody lectures. The two positions should come out of what people write on forms, what they refuse to sign, and what happens to animals.
2. Give both views their strongest case, including the dissidents' — I don't want a strawman for the charter or for the rebels.
3. Work in one object that's a problem for whichever side looks like it's winning: something that was selected hard for a job it no longer does under the dome, and still gets certified, or something whose causal contribution to the animal includes an effect no Registrar would ever list as its function.
4. Somewhere in there, a Registrar has to notice that the two rules really do come apart on the sac — that this isn't a quarrel about the word "function," it's a quarrel about which animals live and which get rendered.
5. End on a signed decision with a cost attached to whoever signed it. No shrug, no "well, it depends how you define things."

Roughly a thousand words total is fine. Don't name any real philosophers and don't cite anything.
