I'm a software engineer with no philosophy training, and I work on a system that generates counterfactual histories: we perturb some initial condition — a battle decided the other way, a head of state surviving an illness — and simulate forward from there. My project lead defends the whole approach by invoking chaos theory. His position, roughly: history is a chaotic dynamical system; a perturbed run diverging from the baseline is just the butterfly effect; therefore history has a positive Lyapunov exponent and a finite prediction horizon the way weather does at around two weeks; therefore no long-range historical forecast is trustworthy, including ours; and the fact that our simulations actually diverge that way is direct empirical evidence for the claim. Several of us think that last step is circular, since we chose the equations, but we can't articulate what's wrong with the rest.

Could you audit the analogy rather than simply endorsing or dismissing it? Specifically:

1. Lay out the correspondence explicitly, as a markdown table with the columns `Source element (weather / chaos theory)` | `Intended counterpart in history` | `Verdict (holds / partial / fails)` | `What the failure costs the argument`. I want the state vector, the equations of motion, the distance metric, the exponential error growth, and the prediction horizon each given a row, plus any element of the analogy you think he is relying on implicitly.
2. Separate the load-bearing disanalogies from the cosmetic ones. Two I suspect belong in the first group: whether there is any well-defined notion of the *distance* between two possible histories, such that "exponential divergence" even has a truth value; and whether the behaviour of our simulation says anything about the world rather than about how we parameterised it.
3. Say whether the conclusion — no long-range historical forecasting is trustworthy — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and if it does survive, whether it survives for reasons other than the ones he gives.
4. If chaos theory is simply the wrong frame for what we are modelling, propose a better one and say what it predicts differently.

No citations or reading list needed; argue from the structure of the two domains.
