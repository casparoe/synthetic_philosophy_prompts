Last month a friend of mine and I spent an entire evening on a disagreement that neither of us could close. He trains models at an AI lab; I work in philosophy of technology. The subject was the safety scheme usually called iterated distillation and amplification, in which a human handles tasks beyond her unaided competence by consulting a hierarchy of trained assistants, and the behaviour of that amplified system is then compressed back into a single trainable model.

His position: the scheme's own safety argument already grants everything I want. It stipulates that the compression step must preserve whatever property the amplified system had, so if honesty or non-manipulativeness is lost somewhere, that is a failure to discharge an assumption the argument flagged, not a hole in the argument. My reply: that stipulation is stated as one black-box requirement while doing three distinct jobs — reproducing the teacher's outputs on the inputs it was trained on, reproducing them on inputs the teacher never faced, and reproducing the grounds on which the teacher had the property rather than the surface pattern the property leaves in its behaviour. If those three come apart, and I think they come apart for every property defined in terms of what an agent does when telling the truth is costly, then alignment-preservation is not a condition one can assume and tick off but a claim requiring its own argument, and no such argument is available for the properties that matter.

He conceded the distinction might be real, but said it proves too much: every learned system faces distribution shift, so I have identified nothing specific to decomposition; and in any case one can amplify again and inspect the compressed model directly. I think that last move is the crux, because inspecting the compressed model in the very contexts where it has newly landed is exactly the labour the scheme was designed to eliminate. I never got past a gesture at this.

Please write me a two-page proposal for a four-month residential fellowship on this question. I need: a precise statement of the question, one with a determinate answer rather than a mood; an argument for why the scheme as currently formulated leaves it open, which must earn my three-way distinction rather than assume it; the method, month by month, including at least one worked case of a decomposed task in which each component output is individually checkable by a human while the composed trajectory is not; what a positive result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and an honest note on where four months is likely to be insufficient. Define every term you introduce, and write for a panel of philosophers who have never read the alignment literature. No bibliography and no citations, I will supply those myself, and do not attribute views to particular researchers beyond what I have described here.
