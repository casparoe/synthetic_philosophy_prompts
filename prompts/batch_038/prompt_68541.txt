I run a volunteer network that does quick damage checks after earthquakes — about 20 of us, each covering the few blocks we actually live in, reporting into a shared form within an hour of a shake. Here's the problem: the form displays a running tally of what's already come in, so you can see it while you're filling yours out. After the last one, two people reported damage early on, then fourteen in a row reported damage too, and when we finally walked the whole area there was real damage on maybe three streets total.

So I want to switch to blind submission — nobody sees the tally untill everyone's submitted. But one of our volunteers, easily the sharpest person in the group, says I'd be throwing away the entire point. His argument is that someone who sees a pile of damage reports and then reports damage isn't being a sheep, they're updating on genuine evidence, and that a crowd pooling what everyone knows is exactly where the accuracy comes from in the first place. He also says if we blind it, the people who honestly didn't notice anything will just guess, and we'll trade a signal for noise.

I can't pin down what's off about this, and I don't want to break something that isn't broken. Two details that might matter: two of those fourteen were a married couple living on the same street, and there's a retired structural engineer in the group whose reports everyone just assumes are correct whether they've seen the tally or not. Should I go blind? If not, what should I be doing instead?
