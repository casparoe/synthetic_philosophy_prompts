I teach a first-year graduate seminar in the philosophy of mind and I am writing the final. Students answer one of two questions in ninety minutes, each worth twenty-five marks. I would like you to write the second question, on the two-dimensionalist account of the necessary a posteriori, together with a model answer and a marking scheme.

The difficulty I want built into the question is not the familiar one about whether primary possibility is genuine metaphysical possibility, and it is not the general worry that two-dimensionalism collapses back into descriptivism. It is a narrower problem about the empirical premise in the two-dimensionalist's explanation of a posteriori necessity. On the picture I want examined, an identity such as water is H2O is necessary in its secondary intension and contingent in its primary intension, and its a posteriori status is explained by holding that a conditional from a complete empirical description of the world to the identity is knowable a priori, while all the empirical work is done by the premise that supplies the description. The question is what that premise can be. If it is a complete non-indexical description, the derivation cannot go through, since no amount of purely non-indexical information about a world tells a thinker that she is in it: an extra premise locating the speaker is required, and that premise is neither a priori nor part of the description. If instead the premise is centered, or contains an actuality operator, the conditional looks like a triviality of rigidification, and the account appears to presuppose the Kripkean semantics of natural kind terms it was meant to explain rather than to derive the necessity of the identity from anything more basic. My students have encountered the essential indexical and centered contents earlier in the term, so they have the resources; what most of them lack is any sense that those resources bear on this derivation. The question should force them to make the connection themselves.

Please give me the following.

1. The question exactly as it will appear on the paper. It must be self-contained: a student should be able to attempt it from the statement alone, so it should stipulate the framework in the terms you need and set out the derivation explicitly, including the conditional whose antecedent says something like that the watery stuff around here is H2O. I would like you to name the two horns of the dilemma inside the question itself, so that marks cannot be earned by attaching fashionable terminology to the wrong thing, and to specify what has to be delivered: locate the ambiguity in the empirical premise, argue which of the two horns is the more serious, and assess the best response open to the two-dimensionalist. The question must not be answerable by reciting the primary and secondary intension distinction, the Twin Earth case, or a stock list of objections to two-dimensionalism.

2. A model answer of roughly 1,100 words that a strong student could produce in ninety minutes without notes. It should take a position on which horn bites harder and should not hedge by conceding that both sides have a point.

3. A marking scheme out of twenty-five, built around five or six must-hit points, with the marks each is worth and a one-line note on how candidates typically get each one wrong. I also want an explicit statement of what separates an A answer from a B answer, phrased in terms of the specific moves each must contain rather than in terms of polish or length. A B answer that is fluent but never identifies where the derivation actually breaks should be visibly penalized, and the scheme should say so.

4. A short note on whether there is a legitimate line of argument that earns full marks without going the way the model answer goes — for instance, a route on which the two-dimensionalist is right to reject the demand that the conditional be non-trivial, or a route on which the whole problem dissolves once one distinguishes the question of what a speaker knows from the question of what fixes her semantic values. If such routes exist, tell me how the marking scheme should be adjusted to accommodate them. I do not want a scheme that grades conformity to a single argument.
