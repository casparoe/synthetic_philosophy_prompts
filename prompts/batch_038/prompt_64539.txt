Two years of thinking about this on my own, no philosophy background at all, and I've landed somewhere I can't shake — so now I want to know what it's actually exposed to.

The view: intense suffering sits in a different moral league from anything good. Not "counts more" — a different league. Past a certain severity, no amount of happiness, however many people get to enjoy it, adds up to outweighing it, so when the two genuinely conflict, ending that kind of agony wins. I found out later this is close to what people call negative utilitarianism or suffering-focused ethics, but mine has an extra bit: I don't say an empty world would be better than this one. I say those two aren't the kind of thing you can compare, which is how I avoid being commited to wanting everyone gone. I got here from two directions — a relative whose last year was basically uninterrupted pain, and reading about how most wild animal lives probably go — and neither budged when people told me suffering builds character or that happy people count too.

So what would count against it? Give me the considerations that should actually move me, ranked from "this kills it" down to "uncomfortable but I keep the view." For each one I want to know whether a serious suffering-focused person would themselves concede it as damaging, or wave it off as thier critics' prejudice. I want at least one of the five to be about evidence rather than intuition — something in how pain and pleasure actually work in a nervous system, or in what people living in the worst conditions reliably report about whether their own lives are worth it — because if my view is resting on a factual guess about severity, I'd like to know which guess. And one of the five has to be about the no-comparison hedge specifically: whether refusing to rank an empty world against a suffering one quietly drains the view of everything it was supposed to tell me, since I still need it to say what to do when the real choice is between preventing agony and bringing about more happy lives.

Numbered list, exactly five items, a sentence or two each, worst-first. No intro, no wrap-up paragraph, and no reading lists — I can't go look anything up.
