I work on interpretability for a mid-size language model, mostly training sparse autoencoders over its residual stream. Two observations keep undermining my confidence in what I find. First, when I roughly double the dictionary size and retune the sparsity penalty, about a third of the features I was confident about vanish and are replaced by finer-grained successors, even though the underlying model never changed. Second, for nearly any feature I isolate, I can construct a gerrymandered direction — one that fires on all the same tokens except when they occur inside a code block, say — that is just as sparse and, with a little effort, just as nameable.

Please make the strongest case you can for this position: sparse autoencoder features are real components of the model's internal organization rather than artifacts of the analyst's chosen basis, and the way to block the gerrymandering worry is to individuate features by how they are actually consumed by downstream computation instead of by whether a human can label what they respond to. Then say where you think the case is weakest — in particular, whether individuating features by their downstream use collapses into a criterion that makes any claim about features explaining behavior circular.
