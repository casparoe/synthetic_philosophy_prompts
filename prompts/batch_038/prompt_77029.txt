ok, some background, because I think you need it to answer this properly. I write literary fiction, and for two years now I've been building a novel whose three narrators each live inside a different theory of time without any of them realising that's what's happening. One is convinced only the present exists and has no patience whatsoever for the past; one is a woman who experiences her own life as branching at every decision and is haunted by all the versions of herself she didn't become; the third thinks the past is still being written, retroactively, by whatever happens now. I got to all of this through Borges first — the tortoise essay, the story with the garden that forks, the man who remembers every single instant and can barely think as a result, the point in the cellar that contains all other points, plus that essay of his with refutation and time in the title — and only afterwards through the actual metaphysics, which I mostly know secondhand from one very patient friend in a PhD program. What I need from you is a sorting instrument, not an essay.

So: build me a decision tree that takes someone from nothing more committed than the bare feeling that succession is undeniable (or that it isn't) all the way down to a named position about time. I want the branch nodes phrased as things a person could answer without having read anything — concrete situations, the kind of thing my characters would actually say out loud at a dinner party. Does the future already exist somewhere the way the far side of the moon does? Is the present a place with thickness or a knife edge? Would you be able to notice time passing if nothing in you or around you changed? Does anything survive if you take away the self that remembers? Feel free to use A-series/B-series vocabulary at the leaves if you need it, but please don't lean on it at the nodes. And every leaf should carry three things: the name of the position (or the word unnamed if nobody really defends it), one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and — this is the part I care about most — one sentence on what kind of plot that position licenses, i.e. what could actually happen in a story narrated from inside it and what becomes impossible to narrate.

Two more asks. Borges has to appear in at least three different leaves, becuase I'm fairly sure he doesn't hold one position and I want to see where he splinters — including at least one leaf that only ever shows up in the fiction and never in the essays, and one where the essays commit him to something the stories quietly refuse. And flag honestly whatever your tree can't separate: I suspect there's a pair lurking around all-times-exist-at-once-in-an-eternal-intellect versus plain four-dimensional eternalism, and another around the no-self arguement for time's unreality and the no-succession one — tell me whether those genuinely collapse into a single leaf or whether there's a question that splits them. Finally, where a combination of answers is logically available but nobody occupies it, note the gap and say why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. That last category is the interesting one for me, since it's where I might put a fourth narrator.
