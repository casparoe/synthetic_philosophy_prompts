For several years I have informally arbitrated splits — two partners unwinding a company, siblings dividing an inherited property, neighbours sharing the cost of a private road — and I have been trying to write down, once and for all, the rule I actually apply. Before I keep using it, I want to know whether it hangs together. Here is what I am committed to.

1. The recommended deal must leave nothing on the table: it must be impossible to make either party better off without making the other worse off.
2. When the two parties are in genuinely identical positions, each must receive the same.
3. The recommendation must not depend on the scale each party happens to use for their own satisfaction. If one of them numbers their outcomes differently — stretching or shifting their own measure — the substance of the recommendation should not change.
4. Removing options that the recommendation was never going to select should not change the recommendation.
5. If the range of achievable deals widens in a way that raises one party's best achievable outcome while leaving the other's untouched, that party must not end up worse off than before.
6. A mediator should be able to apply the rule knowing only two things per party: what they get if no deal is reached, and the best they could possibly get. I do not want a rule that requires charting the whole set of feasible deals.

My instinct is that 3 and 5 are the pair in trouble — 3 seems to make the rule indifferent to how much each side stands to gain, which is exactly what 5 asks it to track — but I cannot say why, and I may be wrong about that. Please audit the whole package rather than trusting my hunch: which pairs genuinely conflict and which only look as though they do; rank the genuine conflicts by severity; for each real conflict, give me a concrete numerical example in which the two principles point to different deals; and for each, state the cheapest revision and what I would lose by making it. End by telling me which single change would clean up the most. Use standard names for these principles if they have them, but I want the audit, not a vocabulary lesson, and do not assume I would rather preserve one of them than another.
