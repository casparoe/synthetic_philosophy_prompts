I am a senior at a small high school, and the focus of my ethics seminar this term has been Bernard Williams's attack on utilitarianism. The two cases we worked through in class are the traveller whom a captain invites to shoot one tied-up villager so that the other nineteen will be released, and the unemployed research chemist with a family to support who is offered a job in a laboratory doing weapons research that he finds abhorrent. We also spent a session on Williams's remark about the man who saves his wife from drowning: on his account, if the man has first to satisfy himself that saving her is permitted by impartial morality, he has already had one thought too many, since what should move him is simply that she is his wife. My teacher's position is that the integrity objection gets its whole force from the fact that these ground projects are the sort of thing whose value is partly constituted by being theirs — a project whose worth can be fully captured from an impersonal point of view, she says, leaves the objection nothing to grip. I am not sure I believe her, and I would rather test the claim than argue about it in the abstract, so I want a case built to order. I have to present the result at the end of term in front of a class that contains at least two committed consequentialists, so the case cannot afford to be a straw man.

Here is the specification. I want one realistic, contemporary scenario, told in a single paragraph, involving an agent whose central ground project is itself impersonally valuable and impersonally describable — something along the lines of running a small disaster-relief organization, a scholarship fund, a vaccination outreach program, or a research initiative — who faces a choice in which a person to whom she has a strong special tie (a sibling, a former student, a co-founder) and one or a few strangers are in the balance, and where the impartial calculation points away from the person she is tied to. The case has to be engineered so that Williams's objection cannot be launched against her deliberation at all: the impartial thought is not an intrusion on the project but the ordinary medium in which the project is conducted, so that the intruding thought, if there is one, would be the thought about the tie — the structural inverse of the drowning case. The hard part is that it must not be rigged. So the project has to be genuinely admirable rather than fanatical, and she has to be a person of ordinary warmth rather than a cold obsessive; the tie has to be real and weighty, not a distant cousin or a casual acquaintance; the stakes cannot be so lopsided that every view in the literature gives the same verdict, and the consequences of following the tie have to be bad enough that a consequentialist could not simply shrug and endorse her doing so; and she must still have a reason for keeping the project up that is not purely impersonal, so that it counts as genuinely hers in Williams's sense. Please do not resolve the difficulty by having the tie quietly dissolve, by having the loved one nobly refuse to be helped, or by having some third party step in at the last moment.

Then show me that the design actually works. Please give the whole response as exactly five numbered items, with no introduction and no concluding paragraph. Item 1 is the scenario itself, in one paragraph; it is the only item permitted to run longer than two sentences. Items 2 through 5 should be a sentence or two each and should cover, in this order: why the integrity objection misfires in this case and which of its enabling conditions is therefore unmet; which specific feature of the two classroom cases is absent here, and what makes you confident that it is that feature doing the work rather than some incidental detail of the setting; the smallest edit to the scenario that brings the objection back, together with the verdict the edited case yields; and how a defender of Williams would attack the case, naming which of my constraints above she would claim has been quietly violated. If you judge that the specification cannot be satisfied as stated, use item 5 to say so, identify which constraint has to be surrendered, and give me the closest case you can build instead.
