There's an institution — everybody just calls it the Bureau — that certifies people as **isologues**. An isologue is somebody whose reasoning has been proven, by a very long battery of tests, to converge on the same answer as every other certified isologue's reasoning whenever they're handed the same well-posed problem. Not clones, not geniuses, just strangers who happen to compute the same way. Certification is enormously valuable: a panel of isologues can be legally treated as one distributed person, because they'll never disagree without talking. Here's the engine of the chapter. Once a generation the Bureau runs a draw on each panel. The rule is: any member may send in the reply-paid card. If exactly one card comes back, that member is designated the sole respondent of record — the entire panel's four centuries of decisions get consolidated into them as a single legal person, with the estate, the standing, everything. If two or more cards come back, nobody wins and Panel Nine is dissolved: all 8,411 lose certification, which in this world is roughly equivalent to losing your legal personhood along with your livelihood.

Every draw for four hundred years has lapsed. Nobody has ever sent a card. The lapse records are published, and there's a cottage industry of historians who argue that the perfect abstention streak is itself the best evidence that certification works.

Which is the superrationality trap, basically — it's adjacent to Hofstadter's idea that a rational agent facing a problem assumes every other rational agent facing the same problem will land on the same answer, so instead of guessing what the others will do you ask what anybody reasoning like you would do.
