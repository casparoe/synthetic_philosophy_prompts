I've argued about the special composition question with my colleague Marta for fifteen years and I still cant predict her on this one. Her verdicts, all delivered with total confidence, on whether some things compose something: you and me, yes; a redwood, yes; the yellow slime mold that creeps across a petri dish, yes; a Portuguese man o' war, all those specialized zooids, yes; the Utah aspen clone with tens of thousands of trunks on one root system, yes; a coral head, yes; a beehive, no; an ant colony, no; the fungal network linking two unrelated birches, no; a chair, a statue, a ship of Theseus, a stack of bricks — no, no, no, no; her nose plus the Andromeda galaxy, obviously no; and a tapeworm together with its host, two objects, not one.

Yesterday someone in seminar raised a grafted "fruit salad" tree: five citrus cultivars grafted onto one rootstock, one vascular system, one thing that leafs out and flowers and fruits as a single unit. Is there one tree there, or six genetic individuals in very intimate contact and no seventh thing? Tell me what Marta says and why, which candidate rules are consistent with her whole record, which one you'd bet she's actually running, and which further case I should put to her next to settle it.
