ok so I've been reading about chaos stuff for fun and I keep running into cases where my gut says predictable or not, and they won't line up. Here's the list.

Double pendulum: no idea where it'll be in a minute, even though there's nothing hidden about it, it's just a stick on a stick. Tides: printed a hundred years out to the minute, and that's also just gravity. Weather: good for maybe a week and then nothing, and I've read that's a hard limit, not a technology limit. A single radioactive atom: completely unpredictable, and I think it's really random, not just unknown to us. A big lump of the same stuff: half-life to like six digets. One person's decision tomorrow: no. The number of marriages in a country next year: yes, pretty reliably. Where the planets will be next July: certain. Where they'll be in a hundred million years: I want to say no, but I'm not sure I have a reason for that.

My guess at the rule is something about small differences getting amplifed, but that doesn't touch the atom case at all — nothing's amplifying there, it's just chance — and it doesn't handle the one that keeps bugging me. If you could calculate what I'll decide tomorrow and told me tonight, I feel like I could do the opposite, so the prediction would have to come out wrong. Not because my brain is undetermined, just because the prediction is sitting inside the thing it predicts. That never happens with the tides. Or does it? Predict a hurricane, everyone evacuates, and the numbers you predicted change too — except that feels way less paradoxical and I can't say why.

So is there one principle that gets all of this, or close to it? What is each clause there for, what does it say about cases I haven't thought of, and which of my verdicts does it make me give up?
