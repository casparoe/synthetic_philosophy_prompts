I sat in on a discussion of whether a large language model understands anything by what it says, and Dennett's slogan "competence without comprehension" was recruited by both sides: one participant used it to insist that a system can display every outward mark of understanding while having none of it, the other used it to say that comprehension is itself just a kind of competence, so there is nothing further to look for once the competence is in place. Please separate the distinct theses this slogan could be carrying — a claim about how a capacity can be implemented without inner representation, a claim about what comprehension turns out to be, a claim about which direction explanation must run between the two — and for each say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or something that gets into trouble, including any reading that would be self-undermining if asserted by someone trying to persuade others. Finally, say which reading, if any, licenses each of the two uses above, and what would have to be true of a model for the dispute between them to be settled by evidence rather than by stipulation about the word "understand."
