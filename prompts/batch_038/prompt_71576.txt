I'm running a small seminar on the political philosophy of care work and I want to hand out a diagnostic — not a reading, a sorting instrument. Ten-ish questions, each one a concrete policy situation or a made-up instrument, each with two or three options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions on paid parental leave, childcare, and the dual-earner family. The whole point is to sort people by what they'd actually sign up for, so no question can be about what they value in the abstract and no question can contain words like justice, equality, fairness or dignity. If a respondent can answer a question without picturing a specific leave scheme, the question is bad and I want it thrown out.

The branch points I want the tree hung on (add more if you like, but these have to be in there):

- whether the thing to fix is the split of care between mothers and fathers, or the fact that care of any kind is priced below paid work — the second can be true even in a household that splits everything down the middle
- whether non-transferable per-parent quotas are justified by what they change (fathers actually taking the leave) or by what they signal (care is not women's job) — and whether those two can come apart, i.e. a quota that signals the right thing but demonstrably doesnt shift behaviour
- whether group daycare is a substitute for parental care, an inferior substitute, or a public good that isn't in the substitution relation at all
- whether the dual-earner household is the target to be made workable, or is itself part of the problem because it sets the whole family's rhythm by the labor market — and whether there's a third position saying the make-everyone-a-breadwinner model and the make-caregiving-cost-nothing model share a mistaken premise
- whose interests the entitlement is actually in: the child's, the carer's, the employer's or the insurer's, and what changes in the design if you pick a different one

Then two cases I want used explicitly, because they break the usual templates. First, the same-sex couple: most leave designs are written as mother/father, so ask what each position owes a couple with two mothers — does the quota just split, does one partner get slotted in as the other parent, does the birth-vs-care distinction collapse or get sharper. Second, an instrument I made up, and I want at least two questions to to turn on it: a scheme where leave is nominally transferable between partners but any transfer forfeits 30% of the weeks, so the household ends up with less total leave if it pools. Some positions should love this (it's a quota with the coercion taken out), some should hate it (it puts a price on a norm), and I want to know which is which and why.

Leaves: give me the positions the tree terminates in, named. Use the standard label where one exists, coin one where it doesn't, and tell me which you did. Each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment — the thing a defender would rather not be asked about in a public lecture. Then flag every pair of leaves your questions can't actually separate; I expect a couple of the implementation-variant ones to blur together and I'd rather you say so than paper over it. Finally mark the answer-combinations that are logically available but unoccupied, one line each on why nobody's there: incoherent, unattractive, or just nobody has written that paper yet.

Audience is grad students who've read some feminist economics but no philosophy of the family, so every question has to stand on its own feet. And no bibliography, no citations, no reading list — I just want the instrument.
