(P) There are actual or genuinely possible cases in which a state has phenomenal character — there is something it is like for the subject to be in that state — while the state is not globally broadcast, and hence not available for verbal report, for reasoning, or for the rational guidance of action.

That is, Block's distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness is substantive and cuts the way he says it does, against the apparent implication of global workspace theory that the two coincide. One wrinkle of my own: I have been treating broadcast as a graded quantity with some threshold rather than an on/off switch, and I suspect the truth of (P) may be sensitive to where that threshold is placed. My own figure, reached largely by intuition rather than argument, is 0.55.

Please give me a number between 0 and 1 and then decompose it: how much turns on whether the distinction is substantive rather than a verbal redescription; how much on whether access without phenomenology is possible (a broadcast architecture, fully functional, dark inside); how much on whether global workspace theory is even approximately correct about the neural implementation; how much on the graded-threshold worry; and how much on whether any of this is decidable by experiment rather than by intuition. Say which term you regard as least settled, and what argument or empirical result would move your number most.
