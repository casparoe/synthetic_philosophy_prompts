I'm trying to work out my own intuitions about tearing down statues and renaming things, case by case, and I want to know what principle, if any, actually fits them.

1. Confederate general's statue in a city park, put up in 1910 by a heritage society: take it down. Easy.
2. The same general's name on the county courthouse: take that down too, though I'll admit I feel this one less.
3. Medical school building named for a 1920s donor who was an open eugenicist, but whose money built the pediatric wing: rename it, and keep a plaque explaining who he was and what he did.
4. Statue of a suffragist who fought for the vote while openly disparaging Black men: leave it up, add context.
5. A 1975 mural in a high school gym, painted by students, showing the town's founder alongside the people he enslaved — accurate, and the descendants find it degrading: I say paint over it. Which shocks me a bit, since it's the most truthful item on this list.
6. Street named after a much-loved local fire chief who, it turns out, informed on three neighbors during the McCarthy years: leave it alone. Structurally this looks just like #3 and I'm giving the opposite verdict.
7. War memorial listing locals who died fighting for the colonial power against the independence movement, in a country that's now independent: leave it. But that reads like #1 from the other side — a monument raised by the losers after the loss.
8. Bench plaque in the same town, "in loving memory of" a decent man who also abandoned his kids: the family keeps it, obviously.

My rough guess at a principle is that what matters is whether the thing honors someone as worthy of emulation, as opposed to merely recording them or their victims — but that doesn't explain #5, and I dont think it separates #6 from #3 either.

So: can you state a principle, or a small set of candidate ones, that delivers most of these verdicts — clause by clause, with the case that motivated each clause? Then tell me what it comes out as on cases I haven't listed: a city renaming a street after a living philanthropist who is later convicted; a university keeping the name of a founder whose views were utterly standard for his time; a statue of a scientist put up by his own students rather than by the state. And be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save and I should just drop.
