I am circulating a short statement of my own position on radical skepticism to a workshop group, and I would like an independent audit of it before I do. The position is broadly Wittgensteinian in inspiration, though I have stated it in my own terms rather than in anyone else's. Here are the commitments, as they will appear in the document.

(C1) A certain class of propositions — that the world existed long before I was born, that objects do not cease to exist when unobserved, that my senses and memory are broadly reliable, that other people have inner lives — are not conclusions of inquiry. They are not supported by evidence, and they cannot be undermined by evidence either. They are the framework within which giving and asking for reasons happens at all. I call them hinges.

(C2) Doubt is always local and always requires grounds. A doubt that has no specific ground, and no procedure by which it could in principle be settled, is not a doubt but an idle performance. Consequently one cannot doubt everything at once: any doubt one actually entertains already takes the framework for granted.

(C3) Hinges bear no epistemic status whatsoever. They are not known, not justifiably believed, and not even unjustifiably believed; they are not the kind of thing that could have a warrant. My relation to them is practical. It shows up in what I do, not in anything I am entitled to assert.

(C4) The skeptic is accordingly not refuted so much as dissolved: there is no thesis there to be true or false. And this costs me nothing at the level of ordinary knowledge. I know a great many ordinary empirical propositions, and the groundlessness of the hinges does not infect that knowledge, because my ordinary warrant does not have to travel through the hinges in order to reach them.

(C5) Hinge sets are not fixed once and for all. Propositions harden into the framework, and propositions that once played a hinge role can become fluid again and be put to the test. Which propositions are hinges depends partly on a community's historical situation, so hinge sets differ across communities and across time.

(C6) Someone who denies a hinge is not making an epistemic mistake. There is no body of evidence they are mishandling and no inference they are getting wrong; they stand outside the framework rather than badly inside it. And yet they are wrong. I hold that we are entitled to criticize them, to discount their testimony, and to tell them they ought to change their minds.

(C7) A concrete case, which I intend to describe in the paper. A close relative of mine became convinced several years ago that a heavily documented public event had been staged and that all reporting of it was fabricated. I found that I could not argue with her: every document, photograph, and witness I produced was absorbed into the suspicion rather than counted against it. After a while I stopped trying and simply went on doing ordinary things with her, acting within the framework she was rejecting. About a year later she abandoned the view. She describes the change in two ways, and I want to keep both: she says that having to treat everyone she loved as a deliberate liar eventually became unliveable, and she says that she came to see that her own standard, applied consistently, would have dissolved everything she depended on, including the grounds for the suspicion itself. I want to describe her change of mind as rational persuasion, and my own earlier conduct — declining to argue, going on acting — as the correct response.

I have no settled sense of where the weak link is, and I am not looking for reassurance that everything survives; equally, I would rather not be told that all seven are equally suspect.

1. Identify the pairs or clusters that are in genuine tension. Rank them by severity rather than listing them side by side, and for each say precisely what would have to be dropped or weakened to remove it, and what I would lose.
2. Identify any pair that only appears to be in tension, and say what dissolves the appearance. I expect at least one of these to be the first objection raised in the room, so please make the dissolving move explicit enough that I could state it myself.
3. Say where (C7) belongs in this accounting. My description of the case is doing work in the paper, and I need to know whether it is consistent with the rest of the package or whether it is quietly importing a notion the package has no room for.
4. Finally, name the single revision — one commitment dropped, weakened, or reinterpreted — that would clear the most tension across the whole set, and state its cost.

Please write your answer in American English.
