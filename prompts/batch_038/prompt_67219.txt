I am a functional safety engineer with about eighteen years in process industries, and my working vocabulary is the one I use in hazard reviews: layers of protection, barrier health and barrier degradation, safety instrumented functions and their integrity levels, proof test intervals, common-cause and common-mode failure, latent conditions, management of change, precursor analysis, ALARP demonstrations, fitness-for-service judgments, and the normalization of deviance. Through a civic-education nonprofit I have been dragged into a reading group on democratic erosion, and the political scientists in it keep talking past me. They distinguish backsliding from autocratization, they argue about whether Nancy Bermeo is right that the post-Cold-War pattern is dominated by promissory coups, executive aggrandizement, and strategic manipulation and harassment of elections rather than by open-ended coups, they invoke Steven Levitsky and Daniel Ziblatt's insistence that the decline happens slowly in barely visible steps and their four indicators of authoritarian behavior, they worry that V-Dem's indices are smuggling in a contested normative definition of democracy, and they ask whether the whole label is anything more than a partisan way of describing losing an argument about how a country should be run. I can follow all of it, but I cannot tell which parts are claims about the world and which are claims about definitions.

What I want is a translation into my vocabulary, done honestly, and then a report on the residue. Restate the central positions in the backsliding debate using the concepts above — a disputed election administration becomes a barrier whose proof test interval has been quietly extended; the erosion of judicial independence becomes the removal of an independent protection layer so that a single initiating event now propagates to consequence; gradualism becomes the accumulation of individually approved management-of-change requests, each within tolerance, none of which triggers a reclassification of the installation. Include my own draft attempt so you have something to correct: I have been telling people that elections, courts, and an independent press look like three separate barriers on a bow-tie but share a hidden dependency on the willingness of incumbent partisans to accept losing, so the loss of that one latent condition is a common-cause failure that removes all three nearly simultaneously, which is why decline outruns every single indicator; and I have been saying that the fight over where to draw the line between a damaged democracy and no longer a democracy is simply a fitness-for-service question about how much margin to require, settled by consequence severity rather than by philosophical argument.

Then give me the residue, and let it be the larger part of the answer. Which of these claims translate cleanly, which can only be rendered by distorting them, and which have no expression at all in my framework? I am especially interested in four points. First, the agent degrading the barriers is authorized to do so, cannot be relieved of duty, and answers to no licensing authority above the installation — does barrier language have any counterpart for that, or does it silently presuppose a principal who can order a shutdown? Second, my vocabulary assumes a barrier's function is fixed by design intent and therefore observable; the political scientists seem to be saying that what an institution is for is itself the contested thing, so that calling a court weakened already presupposes a theory of democracy. Third, Bermeo argues that today's mix of backsliding is more easily reversible than the Cold War mix and that some of it reflects democracy's advance rather than its retreat — is there any counterpart to a degradation that is not a loss, or does my vocabulary treat every reduction in barrier performance as unambiguously bad? Fourth, is the objection that backsliding is a partisan label translatable as an objection that the indicator is miscalibrated and measures something other than what it reports, or is it a different species of objection altogether? Wherever you find yourself importing an engineering assumption — that functions are given, that degradation is a bad, that someone is accountable for maintaining the installation, that there is an end state called loss of containment — say so explicitly rather than presenting the import as a translation.
