For seven years my spouse and I have kept a written record of the food decisions we argued our way to together, each entry with the one-line reason we gave at the time. These are the entries we still treat as binding:

1. We stopped buying supermarket chicken and now buy beef only from one named farm whose cattle are pasture-raised and whose small abattoir we have visited; the recorded reason was that a single cow feeds far more people than a single chicken.
2. We eat mussels and farmed oysters freely; the recorded reason was that we were not persuaded they can be harmed in the relevant way, and that their farming takes no feed out of the sea.
3. We stopped buying wild-caught fish, including small pelagic species we had been eating; the recorded reason was that we cannot see how the animal died or what else came up in the net.
4. We eat eggs from a neighbour's hens but none from any shop; the recorded reason was that we know those hens and know what becomes of them.
5. We declined to go fully plant-based. The recorded reason was that ploughing, harvesting and storing grain kills voles, mice and insects past counting, and that we could see no grounds for counting deaths we set in motion through a supply chain more lightly than deaths we set in motion with our own hands.
6. We declined to join a group donation to a scheme that would have spread rodent fertility bait across a nearby nature reserve; the recorded reason was that we did not want to be the people steering a whole ecosystem on the strength of a model.
7. We have repeatedly paid a veterinarian's bills for injured hedgehogs and birds found in our garden; the recorded reason was simply that the animal was in front of us.

We are no longer sure these seven lines express one principle. Our suspicion is that they express two or three that happen not to have come apart until now.

The decision in front of us: our township is running a deer-reduction programme in the woodland behind our street. The township has told residents the cull will proceed whatever the uptake among households; the participation fee pays the contracted marksmen and the meat inspection; only participating households may collect venison; and any deer not collected is sent for rendering. The township considered fertility control and rejected it as unproven at this scale. Without the cull, the herd is expected to overbrowse the wood and to come through the winter in poor condition, and the road beside it typically sees a dozen vehicle strikes a year.

What I would like from you, in order:

First, the candidate principles that fit all seven entries, stated precisely enough to be testable rather than merely evocative. There should be at least three, and I expect some to differ only in a clause or two; where an entry fits a candidate only under strain, identify the entry and describe the strain.

Second, for each candidate, what it says about the deer programme — and specifically whether it distinguishes paying the fee from collecting the meat, since those are separable choices for us and the township's assurance that the cull happens regardless may matter to one candidate and not to another.

Third, your single best prediction of what our own record commits us to, together with the entry that is the closest analog and an account of why it is closer than the rival analogs we might have reached for instead.

Fourth, an honest statement of whether the seven entries genuinely leave this open. If they do, say what principle we would be adopting by deciding either way, and which of the entries would then have to be reinterpreted or quietly retired to keep the record coherent.

Finally, name a further decision of a kind we could plausibly face within a year — cheap, concrete, and not just a restatement of this one — that would tell the leading candidates apart, so that we could settle the matter deliberately rather than by drift.
