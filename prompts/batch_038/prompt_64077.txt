So — weird thing has happened. I'm a novelist, my last book has a subplot about a small town tearing itself apart over a street name, and the town I actually grew up in has now set up a real renaming commission, and they've asked me to open the first public meeting with ten minutes from the writer who put them in a novel. I said yes before I thought about it. I do have a position, and it isn't the comforting one.

Roughly: a statue or a street name isn't a verdict. It isn't an assertion about the total moral character of the person honoured. It's closer to a piece of fiction — an invitation to the living to picture themselves as the kind of community that has this name on its signs. So the move that decides most of these fights (dig up the awful thing the honoree did, therefore the honour was a lie all along) is misreading the genre. But this cuts against me too, because then the real question is what living with the name does to us now, and to the people it's aimed at — which means I end up arguing to change some things and keep others, and I'll annoy both sides of the room.

The room: two local historians, one of them very good and very tired of presentism, thinks accuracy about the dead is close to a duty and will not accept genre talk; a woman from the genealogical society whose whole life's work is the town's nineteenth-century records; the city lawyer, who only cares about precedent and what happens if we say yes here; an organiser, mid-twenties, who thinks nuance is a delay tactic with good manners; and three or four commissioners who are just neighbours and want to know what it costs.

Give me the questions they'll actually ask, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of them would phrase it and not the way a seminar would. For each: how much damage it does, what I should give away in the first sentence, what I have to hold or the whole thing collapses, and where my reply is soft. Flag the one where the honest answer is that I have nothing. And include the question I'm terrifed somebody has Googled, which is that two years ago I let them name the new annex at my kid's school after me and I liked it, and I said yes without a single second of this analysis.

No reading lists or names to cite, please — I'm not going to cite anyone, I just need to survive forty-five minutes.
