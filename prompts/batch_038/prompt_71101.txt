so im trying to work out where LLMs sit in Dennett's four kinds of creatures (the Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian ones) for a short piece I'm writing, and I've backed myself into a corner I cant get out of.

Here's the reasoning. The Gregorian tier is the one where a creature gets to use tools that other creatures designed — writing, maps, language, calculators — and an LLM is literally built out of externalized human culture, so it looks maximally Gregorian. Arguably more Gregorian than I am, since basically all of its raw material is curated tool-stuff rather than the messy mixture I got. And the tiers are cumulative, right: you arrive at Gregorian by way of Popperian, which is the upgrade where the creature can run its options internally so its guesses die instead of it dying. So a Gregorian creature has had two rounds of protection against being wrong — it pre-tests in simulation, and then it inherits a big pile of hypotheses its culture already pre-tested for it.

Which gives you a prediction: the further up the tiers, the more reliable the thinker. But LLMs are spectacularly confident about things that are just false. So which is it — are they not really Gregorian after all (and then what's the missing ingredient: a body? a stake in being right? something about the training signal?), or is the ladder doing something it shouldn't be doing, i.e. issuing a reliability prediction it has no business issuing?

The operational test I had in mind was: drop the thing into a scenario it's never seen, and if it revises what it would do, that's Popperian-ness; if it does that revision using vocabulary and procedures it picked up from books and code, that's Gregorian-ness. Current models pass both, easily. So I'm stuck, and I'd rather repair the argument than throw the taxonomy away — but tell me straight if the whole thing rests on a mistake, and if it does, which step is the bad one and what the tiers are actually tracking if not reliability.
