Write a parody of an internal underwriting and claims-handling manual — roughly 650 words, in the flat register of a corporate compliance document, with numbered sections and at least one boxed sidebar — issued by a property insurer operating in a jurisdiction that has adopted occasionalism as settled doctrine: no created thing ever causes anything, God alone creates each event at each instant, and what we call causes are merely His habitual succession, reliable but not necessary.

The manual must try, with total sincerity, to keep the business running. Address how to price fire risk when fire does not actually burn anything; how to word the act-of-God exclusion when, on the insurer's own metaphysics, every single loss is an act of God; what becomes of subrogation when there is no tortfeasor to recover against; how to conduct loss-control surveys when the engineers' recommendations presuppose that sprinklers do something; and how the reserving actuary is supposed to justify relying on past custom at all. Include one worked sample claim file for a warehouse in which a large quantity of cotton caught fire, and note the adjuster's difficulty in describing what happened to the cotton without implying that the fire did it.

The humor should come entirely from the doctrine's real problems resurfacing as drafting ambiguities, coverage disputes, and internal contradictions the manual papers over — never from the document noticing that it is absurd, and never from naming a philosophical objection outright. A reader who knows the fire-and-cotton argument and the theology of custom should recognize every difficulty as genuine and fairly drawn; a reader who does not should still enjoy it as bureaucratic farce. Invent the insurer, the jurisdiction, and all personnel.
