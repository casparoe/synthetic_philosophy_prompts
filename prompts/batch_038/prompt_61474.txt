I sit in on a philosophy of mind reading group at the community college where I work (not a philosopher, I just like showing up), and two of the regulars got into an argument last Thursday that went for about an hour and went nowhere. They were on Dennetts real patterns idea — the bit where the fact that the intentional stance works is supposed to show that beliefs and desires are real, because there's a pattern in the behavior that only that vocabulary picks up on. Here's roughly how it went:

1. Dev said the pattern stuff settles the realism question precisely because it isn't about interpretation. A pattern is real if describing the data in those terms comes out shorter than listing the data, and that's a fact you can check. So whether there's a belief-and-desire pattern in somebody's behavior is an empirical question, not a choice we make.

2. Marguerite said that only works if the vocabulary is fixed before you look, and it never is. You can manufacture a compressing description of almost anything once you're allowed to coin predicates after seeing the data — same trick that lets people find 'patterns' in tea leaves or in stock charts. If compression is satisfied practically everywhere, it can't be what separates the real from the unreal.

3. Dev said no, the difference is projection. A real pattern lets you predict data you haven't seen yet, and you can test that; made-up predicates squeeze the data in front of you and then fall flat on their face. That's the discipline keeping everything from counting.

4. Marguerite said that's Goodman's problem wearing a different hat. Two vocabularies can compress everything observed equally well and come apart on what happens next, and nothing in the data chooses between them — the choice is about which predicates we're in the habit of projecting, and habits like that are ours rather than the world's. Interests all the way down.

5. Dev said she's proving too much: if patternhood is interest-relative then hurricanes, tides and tables are interest-relative too, and she clearly doesn't believe that. Plus in the case that actually matters, interests get constrained — if you want to predict the chess player you have to reach for the belief-and-desire vocabulary, and no amount of stipulating will get you a working substitute. Success is doing the constraining.

6. Marguerite said there's a real difference between those cases and she was glad he raised it: hurricanes and tides get picked out by the causal work they do, whereas beliefs do no causal work at all — the physical level is doing all of it. So the intentional pattern is a compact summary of what's going on, not something that figures in explaining it. Which means Dev can have instrumentalism about beliefs or realism about them, but not both, and the pattern talk is just the machinery he's using to try.

I honestly couldn't tell who was ahead at the end and neither of them would stop. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right where — not just who wins overall?
