Write an essay assessing a proposed repair to Fitch's paradox of knowability. The repair runs as follows. Call a truth epistemically inert if its truth is wholly unaffected by anyone's coming to know it, or failing to, at any time. The anti-realist then defends only the restricted thesis that every epistemically inert truth is knowable, and declines to say anything about truths whose being known would bear on their being true.

Your essay should determine whether the restriction genuinely blocks the paradox, and whether it is worth adopting even if it does. Address at least these points: whether the conjunction that drives Fitch's proof is inert, and if it is not, whether excluding it is principled or merely convenient; whether "epistemically inert" can be specified without covertly appealing to knowability, since sorting inert truths from the rest may require knowing which truths are knowable in the first place; and what survives of anti-realism once the thesis has been narrowed this far.

Reach a definite verdict and defend it rather than concluding that both sides have a point. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restatement of the question.
