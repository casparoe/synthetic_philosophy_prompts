I've been going round in circles about whether pain and pleasure are incommesurable at all, so here are my actual gut verdicts on a pile of cases and I want to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

1. Torture one person for a day, or give a trillion people each a one-hour headache. Don't torture. And I don't think any number of headaches ever tips it — a trillion trillion headaches still loses. Very confident about this one.
2. Flip it round: give one person a headache, or torture a trillion people for a day. Headache, immediately, no hesitation, and I feel zero tension with 1.
3. Compare a world containing a billion blissful lives plus one person who gets a pinprick, with a world containing just the pinprick and nobody else in it. I say the second world is better. Written down that sounds insane but I still say it.
4. My niece wants an ice cream; somewhere there's a stranger with an untreated toothache. Buying the ice cream isn't wrong. Not slightly wrong, no residue of wrongness I have to swallow.
5. I once sat in a dentist's chair for two hours being drilled, happily, for the sake of decades of comfortable chewing afterwards. Trading pain against pleasure seems completely unproblematic when it's all inside one life and it's my life.
6. My neighbour has a permanently bad back, and also has kids, a garden, and terrible jokes. His life is worth living and I'd be disgusted by anyone who suggested otherwise. So ongoing mild pain does get outweighed by good stuff.
7. But a person in unrelievable agony with no prospect of anything improving — I think non-existence would be better for them, and I don't think that's a cruel thing to think. So there's some level above which suffering isnt outweighable by anything whatsoever.
8. Given a fixed pot of money I'd put it into pain relief and palliative care before I'd put it into making already-comfortable lives more comfortable. But I wouldn't put all of it there. I'd still fund the opera house a bit, and the fertility clinic a bit.

The case I keep going back and forth on: if we could painlessly switch off all future consciousness, and thereby guarantee no more suffering ever, would that be the right thing to do? My gut screams no, but given 1 and 3 I can't articulate why.

So: is there a principle — one, stated precisely — that delivers all of these, or nearly all of them? I want it stated in a way I could actually apply to a new case myself, not just labelled with whoever came up with it. Then tell me what it says about three cases I haven't thought of, and tell me straight which of my verdicts it can't keep and why that one is the cheapest to give up. And I want you to make the call yourself: does the principle need a clause saying pain can only be compensated by pleasure inside a single life, since 5 works for me but 4 doesn't seem to work across people? If it needs that clause, is the clause even coherent, or does it just hide the problem? Don't give me a menu of candidate theories with pros and cons of each and leave me to choose — commit to one, say exactly what it is, and argue me into it. British English, please, spellings included.
