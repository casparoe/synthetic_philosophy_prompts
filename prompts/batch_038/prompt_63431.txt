Sen's liberal paradox has been kicking around for fifty odd years now and as far as I can tell nobody has budged. Camp A says it's a real theorem: minimal liberalism plus Pareto plus unrestricted domain are inconsistent, so one of them has to go and the interesting question is which. Camp B says the whole thing is a modeling artifact — Sen built a right out of decisiveness over pairs of *social states* rather than over a person's own actions, which is how the prude's right ends up entangled with the lewd's, and the Pareto step gets fed nosy preferences about somebody else's bedroom that a liberal shouldn't have counted as relevant preferences in the first place. Waive the rights, restrict the domain, and it evaporates.

So what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it substantive — do the two camps genuinely disagree about rights and welfare — or is "right" doing two seperate jobs here, one as a slot inside a social choice function and one as a moral side constraint on action, with the theorem only ever speaking to the first? Diagnose it from what each side actually says (I don't want a survey of the fifty proposed repairs) and tell me what would count as progress: a better formalization of rights, a new impossibility result, or is an impasse just what you should expect when the question is ill-posed?
