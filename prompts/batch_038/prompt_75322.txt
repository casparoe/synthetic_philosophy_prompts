I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar working through Dennett on the physical, design, and intentional stances and on his notion of a real pattern, and our professor has been ruling, case by case, on whether a given ascription picks out something real. Her verdicts so far: the centre of mass of a galaxy cluster — real, though no inventory of particles would list it; the cold front a meteorologist tracks across a weather map — real; the double top a technical analyst claims to see in a share price — not real, it fits the past but licenses nothing; a chess computer's belief that its queen is en prise — real; a thermostat's belief that the room is cold — not real, since the design stance delivers the same predictions for free; a native speaker's tacit grammar — real, though introspectively inaccessible; the clever strategy you ascribe to someone who solved a puzzle once by luck and cannot repeat it — not real; one woman's idiosyncratic mnemonic for names, which she deploys reliably and which nobody else shares — real; a crowd's belief that the platform is about to give way — not real, crowds not being unified enough to count as agents.

Next week's case is this. A neural network trained on a poker variant is such that ascribing it the belief that its opponent is bluffing, together with a desire to minimise losses, predicts its betting behaviour better than anything we can extract from its weights or architecture, and the ascription generalises to hands it was never trained on. However, when the same architecture is retrained from a different seed on identical data, it plays just as well while failing that ascription outright, and neither network contains anything resembling a localised representation of bluffing.

Predict her verdict and say which rule her track record leaves her running — as well as which rival rules fit those nine verdicts equally well but diverge here. Then tell me which further case would settle which rule she has, and whether she could consistently hold that the belief is real at one level of description and not at another. I'm inclined to say real, on the grounds that the ascription is doing genuine predictive work that no lower-level description does; but I can't tell whether the seed-twin failure is supposed to sink it, or whether that failure is a claim about the kind rather than about this particular system.
