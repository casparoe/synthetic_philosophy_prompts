I host my high school's philosophy podcast, and next month's guest is a philosopher with a distinctive answer to the problem of old evidence. Her view: when an agent already assigns probability one to E, conditionalization cannot raise her credence in H, but the confirmation is nonetheless real, and its magnitude should be read off the counterfactual prior she would have occupied had she never learned E — the likelihood ratio P(E|H)/P(E) in that prior does the work. I would like eight questions that press this position rather than letting her restate it. Please include at least one question on which counterfactual prior is the relevant one when there are many ways she could have failed to learn E, one on evidence that is a mathematical or logical necessity, where no prior lacking it seems available, one on whether confirmation on her account has become a historical rather than a synchronic relation and what the probabilities are still doing, and one on whether her rule could guide a bet or any decision at all. For each, supply the follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give.
