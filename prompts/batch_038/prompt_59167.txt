I did thirty-odd years of low-temperature physics and only got into philosophy after I retired, so forgive the blunt framing. What keeps nagging me is the proposal that you can build an impact measure for an AI — penalise side effects, minimise reachability loss, that sort of thing — without ever pinning down what the machine actually values. In physics an entropy-like quantity is value-free precisely because nobody expects it to track what anyone cares about, and I half suspect the AI people are trying to get something out of a value-free quantity that only a value-laden one can give you.

Write me an essay on that controversy: can avoiding side effects be defined without a full specification of value? I want you to take the reachability idea seriously first — steelman it properly, in the way you would in a lab meeting — and then break it with your own two cases: one where the measure makes a fuss about something trivial, and one where it waves through something genuinely awful. Then ask whether there's a repair that doesn't smuggle values back in through the window (reweighting states by their instrumental usefulness, say, or averaging over a set of plausible value functions — does that count as sneaking, or as being honest?).

Don't end on a both-sides shrug. Give me a verdict and defend it, and say explicitly whether you think the whole project is salvageable-but-hard or a category mistake.
