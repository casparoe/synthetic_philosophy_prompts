I have no training in philosophy. I build software that scores insurance claims, and part of my job has been attaching probabilities to the if-then rules in our scoring engine. Working through Lewis's result — that no proposition can serve as what an indicative conditional expresses if its probability is always to equal the corresponding conditional probability — I came to think the collapse is an artefact of the setup rather than a discovery about English. Lewis tests that identity against every probability function reachable by conditionalizing on anything whatsoever; an engine only ever re-evaluates a rule against evidence that does not itself concern the rule's antecedent or consequent. Hold the identity to that narrower class of updates, I want to argue, and the derivation never gets started, so conditionals can stay truth-apt after all. Call it the restricted-update reading.
