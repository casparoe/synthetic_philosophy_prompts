I am putting together the closing session of a philosophy-of-mind reading group, and I want to begin by pinning everyone down on a number rather than on a survey. The proposition I have in mind is deliberately regimented, because I think much of the noise in this debate comes from people using the target terms at different levels of ambition. Call the thesis strict eliminativism about the propositional attitudes: the ordinary terms belief and desire, as they function in mature everyday explanation, do not refer to any state kinds that will be retained — by identity, by realization, or by systematic counterpart mapping — in the ontology of a completed cognitive science, and their continued use will come to resemble the use of phlogiston or of the four humours rather than the use of heat or of the gene. That is a single conjunctive claim, and I want your credence in it as a decimal: not a range, not a restatement of the dialectic, and not an invitation to reconsider how the thesis was worded, though I do ask you to report separately on that last point below.

Please decompose the number rather than simply announcing it. At minimum I would like separate credences, each with a sentence of justification, for: that a theory-theoretic account of folk psychology is correct, as against an interpretivist or simulationist account on which the attitudes are not posit-like in the first place; that the attitudes are, roughly as the folk conceive them, propositional, causally efficacious, and broadly rational, conditional on the previous term being high; that a completed cognitive science will instead posit sub-personal states that earn the name without satisfying those constraints; and that the semantic machinery of reference actually fails in such a case — here I want treated as its own term the worry that eliminativism is either self-undermining, since the eliminativist's own convictions would be among the casualties, or else defused by something like reference magnetism, on which the world's own joints do much of the referential work for us. I would also like a three-way distribution over which historical analogy is the right one for belief and desire: a smooth reduction on the model of heat to molecular motion, a case of reference drift on the model of the gene from Mendel through molecular biology, or a genuine abandonment on the model of the humours — together with a line on how you think that distribution propagates into your headline figure.

Two further requests. First, my own credence in strict eliminativism as defined is about 0.12, while that of the colleague running the group with me — who works on distributed vector representations and doubts that sentence-shaped posits survive contact with the brain — is about 0.55. Tell me which of your terms the gap between us most plausibly sits in, and estimate the probability that a substantial part of the dispute is merely verbal, in the sense that we would converge if we fixed the wording of the thesis. Second, and this is the part I most want handled honestly: identify which single sub-question your total is most sensitive to, meaning the one you would have to change your mind about for the headline number to move furthest, and roughly how far it would move. Then say what non-philosophical development — a particular kind of neuroscientific result, or a particular kind of finding about the explanatory profile of artificial systems — would shift that term, and in which direction. No bibliography and no roll call of who has defended which position; I am interested in your numbers and in the structure that generates them.
