You are a philosopher of physics with a real command of general relativity and of the metaphysics of time. Think carefully before answering: begin by listing two or three candidate diagnoses of the impasse described below, note what would support or undermine each, and only then commit to one.

For several years I have tried to get my graduate students to argue productively about Gödel's rotating universe, and I am increasingly unsure that they are having a single argument. The dispute has not closed since 1949. One camp holds that Gödel established something deep: general relativity admits a cosmology with closed timelike curves through every point and no preferred global foliation, so the theory does not deliver an objective, mind-independent time — precisely the conclusion Gödel wanted against Einstein's own reading of relativity. A second camp calls this a non-starter in physics: the model is stationary where our universe expands, its negative cosmological constant is fixed by the density of its dust, and nothing observationally resembles it, so no conclusion about our time follows. A third camp thinks the first two are talking past one another: a closed loop of timelike segments is still a temporal ordering, so the step from the field equations permitting such curves to the unreality of time requires a metaphysical premise about becoming that no geometry can supply.

My working hypothesis is that the impasse is largely verbal — that the word 'time' here names at least three different targets (temporal order, simultaneity, an objective present) — and that the physics is not genuinely in dispute. Is that right? Diagnose the disagreement: which portions are verbal, which turn on a real formal or empirical question (whether spacetimes admitting closed timelike curves are generic rather than exotic; whether quantum effects enforce chronology protection; how much integrated acceleration a rocket would need to close such a loop), and which rest on starting intuitions neither side can argue for. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a theorem, an observation, a change of question — or argue that none should be expected and why. Reason from what each side actually says; I do not want a survey of the literature, and no citations, please.
