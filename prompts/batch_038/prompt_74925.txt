My thesis, roughly: grounding is real but there is no single grounding relation. What there is is a small closed family of specific relations — determinable/determinate, constituency (parts and members), realization/micro-basing, and something like proper-causation-for-non-fundamental-dependence — and "grounds" is a generic term over them, the way "cause" is generic over physical and agential causing. My own twist, the bit that's actually mine and will get me in trouble:

1. I model it as a typed directed graph. Composition is allowed only within a type. All the familiar "transitivity failures" — the dent in the sphere, the double negation case where p and ¬¬p seem to ground each other, Socrates and his singleton — I claim every single one of them is a chain that crosses a type boundary. Within a type I can't find a failure. So the standard puzzle ("grounding should be transitive but isn't") is a description error: we're reading one word as one relation.
2. Then the fundamentality bit. I refuse to say the bottom layer is just ungrounded, because that makes fundamentality a privative notion — the fundamental would be whatever lacks an explanation, which seems backwards. Instead I say there is exactly one self-grounding fact at the bottom. One loop, one place only. Irreflexivity holds everywhere else, and every fact in the graph has a ground.
