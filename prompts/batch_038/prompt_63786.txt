I teach a graduate seminar in democratic theory, and we have just finished the unit on sortition: descriptive representation, insulation from electoral incentives, deliberative quality, and the standard objections about competence, accountability, and the loss of authorization by voters. For the final I want one essay question, answerable in 60 minutes, that cannot be answered by reproducing that survey. Build the question around a concrete proposal, stated in full within it: a second chamber of 150 citizens selected by lot for a single non-renewable nine-year term, with no power to introduce, amend, or appropriate, whose only powers are to withhold assent and to return a bill to the elected chamber, which may then enact it only with a three-fifths majority. The question should require students to determine whether the reasons that would justify giving a sortition chamber positive lawmaking power also justify giving it purely negative power, or whether negative power rests on a rationale of a different kind; and to explain what becomes of the accountability objection when the members of the body are answerable only for preventing outcomes rather than for producing them — if indeed a veto produces nothing. Supply a model answer of roughly 700 words, plus a marking scheme out of 25 naming the specific moves an A answer must make and the specific errors that should cost marks. At least two of the costed errors should be claims that sound right in this literature but are not, and the scheme should make explicit that a well-organized catalogue of the pros and cons of sortition tops out at a B.
