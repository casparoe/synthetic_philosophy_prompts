Three months ago I got talked into being secretary of our 44-unit housing co-op, mostly because I'm the only member who'll take minutes. Turns out the job is basically refereeing bylaw amendments, and I have zero training for it. Its gotten ugly fast: one faction now says any vote that touches the amendment clause is automatically void, so nothing can ever be changed, and the other says the members can do whatever enough of them want and the document is just paper. Both of them keep cornering me at the table.

Here's the actual setup. Article X says the bylaws may be amended by two-thirds of members present at a general meeting, with 14 days notice. Article II has the limited-equity resale formula, which is the whole reason the co-op exists, and somewhere in the 1980s someome added a line saying Article II may only be amended by unanimous written consent of every member. There's also a line saying no amendment takes effect retroactively. Nobody who's still here remembers who added what, or why.

What I need is a triage procedure I can run in the two minutes after a motion gets handed to me, that tells me (a) whether it's in order to be voted on at all, (b) whether it can go as written or has to be split or reworded first, (c) what threshold applies, and (d) what I write in the minutes either way. Numbered questions in order, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each. And it needs to stop cleanly at the points where it isn't my call — where the question is really about values and belongs to the membership, or where it's a legal question for an actual lawyer, since I gather state co-op statutes can override our bylaws and I'm in no position to check that myself.

I've read a little about the self-amendment puzzle — roughly, the argument that a clause can't authorize its own destruction because the authority to amend comes from that very clause, versus the reply that the members' continuing power to govern themselves is prior to any document they happen to have written — but none of that is usable by me at 7pm in a church basement with forty annoyed people. So tell me which parts of that dispute genuinely have to survive into a checklist and which I'm allowed to drop, and be honest about where the procedure will misfire or spit out something that looks absurd.

Then run it on these four motions, all pending:

1. Mine: lower the Article X threshold from two-thirds to simple majority. A three-year fight about parking has convinced me two-thirds is too high.
2. A single motion that deletes the unanimous-consent line and, in the same vote, rewrites the Article II resale formula.
3. A motion adding a new Article XII protecting some other provision, ending with the words that Article XII may never be amended or repealed — plus a separate motion already filed to repeal Article XII by two-thirds.
4. A motion declaring that last spring's vote on the roof assessment was void because notice was defective, and directing the board to treat it as never having passed.

Straight answer on each: in order, out of order, or in order only if split or amended, and why. Be blunt about number one — if the procedure kills my own motion, say so. And finish with the version I can literally tape inside the binder: ten lines max, no theory.
