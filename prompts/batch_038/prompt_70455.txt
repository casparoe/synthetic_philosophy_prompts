So our little animal-advocacy group has a strategy weekend in a couple of weeks and about $40k of small-donor money to point somewhere, and I volunteered to be the one who writes down his actual position on farmed fish and crustaceans in a single page so everybody else can take a swing at it. Before I circulate it I want a consistency audit, because I built this up over a couple years of reading and of arguing with a friend who thinks the whole invertebrate thing is a distraction from pigs and chickens, and I genuinely don't know whether the pieces fit. Here's the position as I'd state it:

(1) Only suffering counts. I'm negative-leaning in the sense that a happy shrimp appearing in the world doesn't make things better, but a suffering shrimp appearing in it does make things worse. So the only question I care about in aquaculture is how much bad experience gets produced. (2) Sentience is a threshold, not a dial. I refuse to say a crab counts for 0.4 of a fish — "degrees of moral status" is exactly where motivated reasoning creeps in, and once you're in, you're in and you count the same as anything else that's in. (3) But I'm obviously uncertain about who's in, and how I handle that is by multiplying the probability a taxon is sentient by the weight it would have if it were. My numbers, off the top of my head: teleost fish about 0.85, cephalopods about 0.8, decapod crustaceans about 0.55. I want to keep those in the budget spreadsheet. (4) The way I decide who's probably in is strictly functional: nociception, protective behaviour that gets knocked down by analgesics, avoidance learning that outlasts the stimulus, and tradeoffs against a competing motivation — crab gives up a good shell to avoid a shock, that kind of thing. I explicitly reject the structural route, the "wrong shape of brain so no pain" line, which is where I think the fish-sceptic literature goes wrong; that's just mammal chauvinism in a lab coat. (5) On numbers, the moral weight in aquaculture sits overwhelmingly with the small animals nobody campaigns about — the shrimp in the ponds and the little pelagics vacuumed up and ground into feed for the farmed fish — not with the salmon and sea bass people picture. Per kilo of product the individual count in the invertebrate and feed-fish chain is orders of magnitude higher. (6) Where I'd actually spend the money: banning live boiling of crabs and lobsters and requiring electrical or percussive stunning for farmed fish, the way some jurisdictions have already started to, since the UK wrote decapods and cephalopods into its sentience law. That terminal minute is the worst thing that happens to these animals, it's a discrete ask, and it's the kind of thing a legislature can pass. (7) At the same time I'm against incremental welfare reform in general and specifically against "higher welfare" or certified-style labelling for farmed fish and shrimp, because it reassures consumers, entrenches the industry, and under (1) any increase in the number of animals raised is straightforwardly worse. Shrink the thing, don't make it prettier.

I'm not going to tell you which of these I'd ditch, partly because I don't know and partly because that's the point of asking.
