I would like you to write a joke about Zorn's lemma. The joke should be genuinely funny to someone who has used the lemma, and it should fail as a joke if the mathematics underneath it is swapped out for different mathematics. That last test is the one I care about most: I want a joke that could not be retold with the name of some other theorem substituted in.

There is plenty of material to work with, so please choose one thread and commit to it rather than compressing several. Some candidates, stated roughly:

- The conclusion hands you a maximal element, not a greatest one, and the difference between those two things is exactly the difference between an answer and a shrug; a partially ordered set can have many incomparable maximal elements, none of which outranks the others.
- The hypothesis is a condition on chains, and the upper bound it demands must live in the poset but need not be a member of the chain it bounds.
- The proof is a transfinite recursion driven by a choice function, and it has exactly two exits: you meet a maximal element, or you keep going and assemble a chain indexed by ordinals until you have contradicted the fact that you started with a set.
- The lemma is equivalent to the axiom of choice, so citing it in an argument often functions as an admission that no one is going to write the object down.
- Its consequences are uniformly existential and uniformly unexhibitable: bases for vector spaces over the rationals, maximal ideals, ultrafilters, products of compacta. Nobody has ever met one of these things.
- It is styled a lemma despite being equivalent to an axiom, and Zorn was not the first person to prove it.

Please avoid the following, all of which are already spent: the familiar quip that ranks the axiom of choice, the well-ordering principle, and Zorn's lemma by how obvious each of them is supposed to be; any pun on citrus; any joke whose entire content is that the material is abstract or non-constructive and therefore funny; and any joke in which the laugh comes from a mathematician being socially inept.

Form: a setup and a punchline, deliverable aloud, no more than about ten lines. Dialogue, one-liner, or a very short scenario are all acceptable. If the joke requires one piece of stage-setting about what the lemma says, build that into the setup rather than prefacing the joke with an explanation.

After the joke, add a brief annotation of no more than a paragraph or two, covering: which specific feature of the lemma the punchline turns on, and why that feature is funny rather than merely true; every place where the joke takes a liberty — any simplification, overstatement, or imprecision about the mathematics that a pedant in the audience would be entitled to object to, including whether the comic situation is one the lemma actually licenses; the alternative angle from the list above (or elsewhere) that you judged and set aside, together with the consideration that would lead you to prefer it on reflection; and finally your own best guess about where this joke is most likely to fall flat — for instance, whether the truest thing about the lemma is funny in a way that survives being compressed into a punchline at all, or whether the compression is what kills it. If you think the version you have produced is weaker than something else you could have produced, say so and say what the stronger version would have cost.
