reading Leviathan part 1, the chapter on speech, and got properly stuck on one sentence:

"And therefore in Geometry, (which is the onely Science that it hath pleased God hitherto to bestow on mankind,) men begin at settling the significations of their words; which settling of significations, they call Definitions; and place them in the beginning of their reckoning."

three things I cant work out.

1) "the onely Science." But he clearly thinks his own civil philosophy is demonstrative too — somewhere he says geometry is demonstrable because we draw and describe the figures ourselves, and civil philosophy is demonstrable because we make the commonwealth ourselves. So is "onely" narrower than it looks, or is "hitherto" doing all the work, i.e. geometry is just the one science that's actually been *achieved* so far? And if it's the latter, why frame that as something God has been pleased to bestow rather than a fact about which fields got their act together?

2) Why do definitions buy certainty here? Same chapter he's a hard nominalist — nothing universall in the world but names, names are marks we impose. If "triangle" is just a name we laid on things alike in having three straight sides, then the two-right-angles theorem looks only as necessary as our naming convention, which surely isnt what he means. What's the extra ingredient supposed to be that makes a geometric definition different from, say, a definition of justice?

3) this is the one bugging me. A few pages earlier he describes a man born perfectly deafe and dumb who, with a triangle and two right angles set in front of him, "may by meditation compare and find, that the three angles of that triangle, are equall to those two right angles that stand by it" — he just can't carry the result over to a differently shaped triangle without a new labour. So the deaf man lands the theorem with no words at all. Does that mean words add nothing but memory and generality (the knowing was already there), or that what the deaf man has isn't science at all until it's registered in definitions? And how is that consistent with geometry being the science *because* it begins at settling significations?

not after a reading list, just want to know how to read these sentences.
