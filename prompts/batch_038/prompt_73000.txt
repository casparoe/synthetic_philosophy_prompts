I'm on the curatorial committee for our university's little science museum and we have to settle the narrative for the 'Measuring the Heavens' gallery before we commision the design firm next month. The old one ran as a straight line — Babylonian tablets, Ptolemy, Copernicus, Kepler, Newton — and every panel was implicitly graded on how close it got to us; Ptolemy got a whole section on why epicycles were wrong. Two of us want to keep that spine (visitors need a story, and it would be dishonest not to say the geocentric model was wrong), one thinks that grading the past by its distance from the present is exactly why the gallery feels like a museum of fools, and I'm sort of in the middle. Draft the memo I'll circulate: recommend an actual structure for the gallery, give reasons the losing side can answer, and take the strongest case for keeping a progress spine seriously rather than just slapping 'Whig' on it and moving on. Also say what would change your recommendation — which objection would push you toward the other structure, and where you think your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
