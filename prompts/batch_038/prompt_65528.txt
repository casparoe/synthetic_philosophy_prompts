So I run about a quarter acre of vegetables and also volunteer doing invasive-plant work at a small preserve, and I've been trying to pin down where I actually stand on killing insects, because my gut verdicts are all over the place. Here they are, no particular order.

- Picking hornworms off my tomatoes by hand and dropping them in soapy water: fine. No problem with it at all.
- Spraying Bt on the brassicas for cabbage worms: fine.
- The electric bug zapper my dad keeps wanting to hang on the porch: not fine. It doesn't control anything and it kills mostly insects that were never bothering anybody.
- Ant bait stations in the kitchen: fine. The same bait scattered for the pavement ants out on the patio, who never come inside: not fine.
- Broadcasting a pyrethroid over the whole plot in early spring as insurance, becuase a neighbor mentioned seeing beetles: very much not fine.
- Floating row cover over the arugula: fine, even though a bunch of flea beetles get trapped underneath and starve slowly, which bothers me more than it probably should.
- Neonic-treated seed corn: not fine, and this is the one I can't explain to myself, since it almost certainly kills fewer insects outright than the foliar sprays I'd use instead. It's the pollen and the runoff and whatever it does to the birds downstream.
- A pheromone trap for Japanese beetles hung by my roses: not fine. From what I've read they catch maybe three quarters of what they lure, so the net effect is to pull beetles in from the whole neighborhood and then hand the leftovers to the person next door.
- The county truck spraying at dusk for West Nile: fine.
- An aerial Btk treatment to stop spongy moths from stripping the oaks: not fine. And I know the numbers killed are in the same ballpark, maybe worse, and the shade trees are worth something to people too.
- Letting the aphids have the kale: not wrong. A farm co-op treating a crop they could perfectly well afford to lose: wrong, I think.
- Sterile-male releases to crash a fruit fly population: fine, maybe the best option anybody has. A gene drive that spreads female sterility through the local mosquito population: I feel sick about it and I can't say why, since total deaths might be lower and each one gentler.

Closest thing I have to a principle is: you may kill insects to protect something that actually matters to somebody, by the least harmful method that works, provided you don't dump the harm on bystanders. Three clauses — necessity, least harm, no spillover. It gets the zapper (fails necessity), the trap (spillover), the insurance spraying (both). But necessity is doing way too much work and I don't know how to turn it into a scale. Human lives beat oak shade, apparently, except I also think it's fine to bait ants to spare my dog a miserable flea season and not fine to bait the same ants to spare my prize dahlias, and I can't tell whether that's a real distinction or just me being sentimental about the dog. And least harm seems to give the wrong answer on the neonics, where the method with the lowest body count is the one I want to call worst.

So: can you get me a principle, or a small set of candidates, that handles these? Stated precisely enough that I can see which clause each case is hanging on. For the pairs that look alike and that I judge differently — kitchen ants vs. patio ants, West Nile vs. the moths, neonics vs. Bt — tell me straight whether the principle earns the difference or whether it's forcing me to give one of the verdicts up. I'd much rather hear that than watch you contort the theory to save everything. Then run whatever you land on against three I didn't list: letting my squash go to the squash bugs rather than treating; a preserve wanting to spot-treat an invasive bark beetle that's wiping out a whole tree species, where the treatment itself will hit non-targets; and me choosing to buy from a farm that loses 30% of its crop to insects over one that sprays. Plus the case I keep flip-flopping on: a film of vegetable oil on the rain barrel to drown the mosquito larvae, which is targeted, protects my kid from bites, and is about the slowest death on the whole list. Does speed of death get its own seperate clause, or is that squeamishness?

Answer in American English, please.
