For the term paper in my senior philosophy-of-history seminar I have to evaluate the thesis that the arc of history bends toward justice, and what I have found is that the seminar talks past itself because nobody has specified what would count as a successful defense of it rather than an assertion of it. So I would like the specification first, and I would like it written for someone who has to apply it to arguments rather than admire them.

Please write the desiderata: the list of requirements any account of progressive historical direction must satisfy in order to count as a defense, with the consideration or concrete case motivating each one. Every requirement should be something an account could actually fail — no restatements of the thesis dressed up as criteria. I would like the list consolidated to roughly ten to twelve items, since I have to work with it in a paper of ordinary length.

Make sure the list engages at least the following, though you should add what I have missed and cut anything below that you judge to be merely local prejudice:

- the metric: what justice is being tracked, and whether the account owes a reason for choosing that measure (welfare, rights, equality, the condition of the worst off) rather than another, given that the direction of history looks different under each;
- the baseline: progress relative to what — the past, a counterfactual in which nothing had been attempted, or an ideal approached asymptotically;
- what I have been calling the ratchet condition: whether an account must explain why gains resist reversal, or only that the level has risen. I suspect most defenses slide between a trend claim and an irreversibility claim, and I want to know whether that distinction earns a place in the desiderata or is an artifact of my framing;
- endpoint sensitivity: whether a theory whose verdict depends on where one stops the record — vindicated in 1900, refuted by 1945, restored by 1989 — is failing a genuine requirement or merely an unflattering feature;
- mechanism: whether the account must say what produces the tendency (human agency, institutional and technological structure, a providential plan), and whether a bare statistical tendency with no mechanism may use the word "arc" at all;
- selection: the available record is the record of the societies that persisted, so what is owed to the possibility that the sample is survivor-biased and that the losses are systematically underrepresented;
- licensing: whether an adequate account must explain why it does not license either quietism or the treatment of atrocity as midwifery, or whether those are practical corollaries rather than theoretical failures.

For each requirement, state what meeting it would consist in, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are one camp's commitments wearing a requirement's clothing. I expect "must be compatible with a naturalistic metaphysics" and "must not smuggle in a theodicy" to be the contested ones, and I want you to argue that rather than assume it.

Then the part I care about most: identify any subset of these desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain the incompatibility as precisely as you can. If the list is in fact coherent, say instead which single requirement eliminates the largest number of positions actually on offer. Close with a few sentences each on how the principal families — a dialectical account, a Marxist one, a Kantian account on which the thesis functions as a warranted hope, and a contemporary empirically minded account built on long-run indicators of violence, poverty, and political rights — fare against your list. Do not give me references or a bibliography; I am working from seminar notes and only need your judgment on the criteria.
