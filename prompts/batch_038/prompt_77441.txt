For eleven years I have run a forecasting group of about two dozen people, most of whom came to it by way of the same rationalist blogs and forums I did, and all of whom have consequently absorbed a great deal of talk about coherence, calibration, and treating a probability as a price at which you would take either side of a bet. Each quarter every member posts estimates for roughly thirty questions — elections, regulatory decisions, whether a product ships, whether a published figure clears a threshold — and we publish the aggregate, which an outside partner treats as a real forecast rather than a hobby. Members also stake play money on every question, and most will accept either side of a bet at their posted number provided the stake is modest.

The gap in our process is that nobody checks whether a member's slate, taken as a whole, is coherent. We find out about violations by accident, usually when someone with a free weekend assembles a portfolio of bets against a fellow member and posts the arithmetic, and the resulting thread reliably collapses into an argument about whether being exploitable that way is a genuine defect at all — since our members are risk-averse in ways the standard presentation of the argument quietly assumes away. I would like to replace that argument with an audit I can run myself.

Write it as an ordered procedure. For each test: the concrete question to put to the posted numbers, the pass condition, the tolerance I should apply given that members post to the nearest whole percent (tell me how to set it and why — I have no intention of accusing anyone of incoherence over a rounding artifact), and what a failure means in plain language. Order the tests by diagnostic value under a hard budget of twenty minutes per member per quarter, arithmetic only; I am numerate but I have no measure theory and neither do the members. For every failure the procedure must produce the artifact I actually need: a table of bets — prices, stakes, payoff in each possible world — that the member can check line by line and that displays the guaranteed loss without invoking any theory. For every failure it must also say which of the member's numbers is the one to ask them to revise, and why that one rather than another, since the arithmetic never settles it. Assume the output goes to a two-person review panel before any member is asked to change a number, so it has to be verifiable by a second reviewer without re-derivation.

Mark explicitly every point at which the audit stops being a procedure and becomes judgment: questions whose resolution criterion is too vague to have a determinate payoff structure, members who post ranges rather than points, and above all risk aversion — the fact that a person who declines fair or favorable bets at large stakes is not thereby incoherent, and I need to be told how to keep that from being scored as a coherence failure. State plainly, in language I can paste into our welcome document, the difference between this audit and our accuracy scores, so that nobody in the group starts treating consistency as a substitute for being right. No reading list, please: I have read the standard treatments, and what they omit is exactly the practitioner's version.

Then run the procedure on four members. First: someone who posts 0.70 that a candidate wins her primary, 0.60 that she wins the general election if she wins the primary, 0.20 that she wins the general election if she loses the primary, and separately 0.65 that she wins the general election. Second: someone who posts 0.50 that a bill becomes law and 0.60 that it becomes law given it clears committee, and who writes in the notes column that once the committee vote resolves they intend to move to 0.90 however it comes out, having decided to commit. For this one, say specifically whether the exploitation is available now or only after the news arrives, and what that difference changes about what I should tell the member. Third: a member whose slate is internally consistent, whose numbers sum and cross-check exactly as they should, and who nonetheless posts 0.92 on a question where the base rate and the rest of the group sit near 0.55. I expect a clean pass here; say so, explain why the pass is correct behavior rather than a hole in the procedure, and name the instrument that would catch the problem instead. Fourth: a member who posts only in five-percent increments, gives ranges on two questions, and has declined every bet above a trivial stake for three years running.
