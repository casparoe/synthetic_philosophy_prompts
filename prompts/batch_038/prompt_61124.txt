I have been reading the Gödel material on rotating universes on and off for years, mostly because it keeps resurfacing in threads about the block universe, and I have noticed a recurring sleight of hand: people cite the Gödel solution as though it added something decisive to the familiar argument from the relativity of simultaneity, and then, when pressed, describe nothing more than the familiar argument. I want to test the difference for myself with a case built to order, and I would like you to build it. The background I am assuming, stated without citations because I cannot check yours and would rather have none: in his 1949 cosmological paper Gödel exhibited a solution of Einstein's field equations containing pressureless rotating matter together with a cosmological constant, and he stressed two structural facts about it — that there is no one-parameter family of spacelike hypersurfaces everywhere orthogonal to the worldlines of the matter, which he tied to the rotation of matter relative to the local compass of inertia, and that the solution admits closed timelike curves, indeed such curves through every point, though I believe they become available only once one goes out beyond a certain critical radius from a given observer. If that last recollection is wrong, correct it rather than working around it. Gödel's philosophical upshot, as I understand it, is that the ordinary concept of time involves an objective lapse of time, a reality made of successive layers of a present that comes into being, and that relativity theory shows this concept has no objective correlate; the rotating universe is supposed to cut off the retreat position that a friend of becoming would otherwise occupy.

Please construct a thought experiment that isolates exactly what the rotating universe adds. Here is the decomposition I have in mind; if it is wrong, or if the levels do not come apart the way I assume, say so and rebuild the case around a decomposition that does work. Level one: no observer-independent present, which special relativity already delivers. Level two: no preferred slicing even after one grants every convention and every appeal to the rest frame of the matter, which I take to be what rotation buys. Level three: no global before-and-after ordering at all, because the causal order becomes cyclic. The scenario should involve two cosmologists, each aboard a survey vessel with arbitrarily good but strictly standard equipment — clocks, gyroscopes, radar, light signals, idealized as needed, with no exotic physics, no quantum gravity, and no oracle that hands either of them the global geometry. One inhabits an expanding universe of the usual homogeneous and isotropic sort; the other inhabits a Gödel-type rotating universe. Give both the same assignment: produce, by a procedure she could in principle carry out, a global ordering of events into successive layers. Show what each does, what each measurement returns, and where each stops. Constraints: the scenario proper should run two or three paragraphs at most and should read as a scenario, with people doing things, not as a lecture about a metric; no line element and no coordinates, because I want this usable in a post for readers who are comfortable with light cones but have never seen the Gödel solution; nobody may actually travel around a closed timelike curve, since I want the case to turn on the availability of such curves rather than on time-travel paradoxes, which absorb all the attention in any discussion they enter; and the two situations must not be rendered locally indistinguishable by fiat, since I understand rotation to be locally detectable and would like the case to respect that. Most importantly, the verdicts have to be generated by the structure of each spacetime rather than stipulated: if you find yourself writing that one cosmologist fails because she happens to be in the bad universe, the case has failed.

After the scenario, do the demonstration. State which level each cosmologist fails at and why, in terms of what her instruments actually showed her. Then tell me which feature is doing the work in Gödel's argument — whether level three is load-bearing, or whether level two already gets him to the denial of an objective lapse, or whether he ever needed only level one and the rotating universe is doing rhetorical work. Run the removal test: suppose the rotating-universe cosmologist is confined to a region inside the critical radius, where on my recollection no closed timelike curve is accessible to her; say what changes, and whether the case then collapses back into the standard block-universe argument that special relativity already supports. Then give the counterattack: how would a defender of objective becoming, or a philosopher who accepts the physics but rejects Gödel's reading of it, go after this case — for instance by disputing what the ordinary concept of time really requires, by offering the local asymmetries of radiation or of causation as a surrogate for becoming, or by challenging the modal step from the mere possibility of such a universe to a conclusion about ours. Finally, flag anything in the design that you regard as cheating or as smuggling in the conclusion, including whether pairing an expanding universe against a rotating one holds fixed everything it needs to hold fixed; I suspect the two solutions differ in more respects than rotation alone, and I would rather be told that up front than discover it later.
