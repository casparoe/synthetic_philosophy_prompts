ok so this blew up at my school and two of my friends have been fighting about it for weeks and neither will let it go, so I figured I'd ask someone else.

We have a student magazine — run entirely by students, but it's an official school club, so it gets a budget and a page on the school website. Last fall a guy on the masthead (I'll call him D) wrote a column guessing who was behind an anonymous piece that had gone after the president of one of the clubs. He named a girl. He was wrong. She got a pile of nasty messages for about a week before people figured out it wasn't her. Then roughly 200 students signed a petition asking the board to do something, and the board voted to take D off the masthead and said they wouldnt run anything from him again, ever. He found out when the new masthead went up. Nobody told him ahead of time and he never got to say anything to the board.

Here's the argument, basically in order:

Maya: what they did was punishment. They took away a role and banned him from participating because of something he wrote, and if you're going to punish someone you're held to standards — you tell people in advance what gets you cut, you let them respond, and the sanction has to fit the thing. A permanent lifetime ban for one column, with no warning and no chance to speak, fails all three.

Theo: it wasnt punishment, it was a group picking who it wants to work with. Nobody owes D a seat on a student board. The magazine is just some students deciding they don't want him around, same as you deciding not to invite someone over. Calling it punishment pretends D has a claim on their project and their time, which is backwards — the actual compulsion would be making them keep him.

Maya: it's not a birthday party though. The magazine is school-recognized, it has a real audience, and being on the masthead is the kind of thing that ends up in applications. When what you hand out costs somebody their standing in a semi-public institution they can't just go recreate somewhere else, it works like punishment whatever you decide to call it.

Theo: cost isn't the test. Getting dropped by your friends or not getting a summer job costs plenty and isn't punishment. What makes it punishment is that it comes from someone with standing to hand it down, for breaking a rule the person knew about. The board has no standing and there was no rule, so it's just consequences — and Maya's version turns every decision about who to work with into a court case.

Maya: then they should have written the rule down first. Once a group starts handing out sanctions that heavy, it takes on the obligations that come with them: publish what gets you removed, hear the person out, put an end date on it. Otherwise it's whatever the crowd wanted with a vote at the end to make it look official.

Theo: and if they publish rules, every future editor has to argue over whether some piece crosses a written line instead of just using judgment. Also, the petition. Two hundred people said they wouldnt read the magazine if he stayed. The board wasnt blaming him, it was plugging a hole. So even on Maya's own description it wasn't a sanction, it was damage control.

Maya: that's the giveaway. If it's damage control, then the decision tracked how loud the petition was, not what D did. Insulating the penalty from the size of the crowd is the whole point of rules and hearings. A sanction that scales with the volume of the audience isnt a sentence, it's a mob.

Theo: and if getting cut from a student board is the same kind of thing as a mob, then her framework cant tell those two apart — which means it isn't getting her proportionality, it's just getting her a scary word.

Can you go through this move by move and tell me who's right at each one, not just who wins overall? If a move is half right, say which half. I specifically want to know whether the punishment-versus-consequences thing is a real distinction or two people grabbing different words for the same event, and whether the 200 signatures actually change what the board did or only what it felt like. And I think they're both steering around something: D did wreck a week of a girl's life over a guess, and neither of them has said what the board should have done instead. Tell me what they're missing.
