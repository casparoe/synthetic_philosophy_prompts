Ok so I've been chewing on this for a while and I want an actual number, not a literature tour. I keep meaning to write it up as a post but I need to find the crux first.

Claim under evaluation: DNA is a code — meaning the codon-to-amino-acid mapping is the same sort of thing as Morse code or ASCII, an arbitrary symbol system carrying instructions — rather than a metaphor that biologists have calcified into technical vocabulary over 70 years.

My credence is somewhere around 0.65 that it's genuinely a code and not just convenient talk. My housemate, who did a molecular bio PhD and is much better informed than me, is at like 0.25 and thinks the whole framing is category confusion. I want your number, and I want it decomposed rather than asserted.

Here's my rough factoring, reject it and give me yours if you think mine is wrong:

1. Is arbitrariness actually a necessary feature of codehood? The genetic code doesn't look arbitrary at all — similar codons tend to map to similar amino acids, and the whole arrangement looks tuned to soften the impact of point mutations and frameshifts. If a system can have that much non-random structure and still be a code, this term jumps way up.
2. Where does the mapping physically live? The DNA doesn't contain the codon-to-amino-acid assignment anywhere; that's implemented by the tRNA synthetases and the tRNAs, which are themselves gene products. Does that circularity kill the DNA-alone sense of "code," or is that just how every code works — obviously you need a decoder, that's not a bug?
3. Is the "information" in play supposed to have real semantic content, cashed out by selection history, or is it deflationary — causal covariance wearing a lab coat? I genuinely can't tell whether the two camps are even using the word the same way.
4. Recipe vs. blueprint. You can't recover the organism from the sequence the way you can recover a house from its plans. Does that bear on codehood at all, or only on the "instructions" gloss?

Also add a term for the possibility that the question just doesn't have a determinate answer — if you think that's the live option, price it rather than hiding behind it.

Give me a probability per term, show me the arithmetic or the fuzzy multiply or whatever you're honestly doing, then tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to — if I could settle one sub-question by fiat, which one buys the most movement. And say what would actually move you on that term, because "more empirical work" doesn't count as an answer.

Finally: which single term do you think my housemate and I are really disagreeing about? My guess is 2, but I have low confidence in that guess and I'd like to be argued out of it if its wrong.

One constraint — please don't retreat to "it depends what you mean by code." Pick the reading you think a working biologist most plausibly intends when they say it out loud at a seminar, and commit to it.
