In my phil-of-religion class the instructor keeps saying "you can't traverse an infinite" whenever the question of an infinite past comes up, and it always sounds like it settles the matter. Last week a student pushed back: an endless past has no first moment, so there's nowhere you'd be starting the traversal from — and the instructor said that just begs the question. I'm not so sure it does. Can you lay out the different precise claims that one sentence could be making — what's doing the traversing, from when to when, and which sense of "infinite" is in play — and sort them from vacuously true through substantive to flat-out false? Then tell me which of them actually deliver "the past has a first moment," which ones survive the no-starting-point objection, and whether anything is left over in both columns. One more wrinkle: he's perfectly happy with an endless future for God's life, so I want to know whether any reading that allows an infinite future can also rule out an infinite past without smuggling in an extra premise.
