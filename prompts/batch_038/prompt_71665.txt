In Carroll's dialogue, Achilles insists that anyone who accepts A, B, C and D must accept Z, promising that "Logic would take you by the throat, and force you to do it!" — to which the Tortoise replies, "Until I've granted that, of course I needn't grant Z," and asks that this conditional too be entered in the note-book. Two familiar diagnoses of what has gone wrong are that the Tortoise commits a category mistake, treating a rule of inference as though it were one further premise; and that he lacks the capacity or disposition to infer at all, so that no inscription in the note-book could supply what is missing. Compare these two diagnoses: what precisely does each take the Tortoise to misunderstand, and on which of them is the regress genuinely escaped rather than merely postponed? Then consider a variant in which Achilles stops writing conditionals and instead hands over an explicit rule formulated as a rule and not as a proposition — From A and B, infer Z. Say whether each diagnosis predicts that the Tortoise would now be silenced, and whether either diagnosis, if correct, does any work toward justifying deduction as opposed to explaining the Tortoise's confusion.
