so i TA an intro ethics seminar and our last session is the paradox of deontology — the one where the critic says: if killing is what's bad, why not kill one person to stop five killings, and if you wont, you've made the world worse by your own lights, you've only kept your own hands clean. i want to hand out a poem for that instead of ending on one more argument.

form: a ghazal, 7 to 9 couplets, each couplet able to stand alone, a single rhyme carried through the second line of every couplet plus a refrain that's identical word for word each time, and the final couplet bringing in the speaker the way a maqta traditionally puts the poet's own name there.

the refrain is where i want the philosophy to live. pick a short phrase that can be read as a prohibition in one couplet and as an accusation in another, so that the line that keeps my hands clean in couplet three is the line condemning me in couplet six — and the poem never gets to alter it, because thats exactly what a constraint is, it doesn't answer to the totals. the couplets staying autonomous should do some work too: the constraint applies act by act, not to the sum.

two of the couplets should be concrete cases. one where i could stop two killings by doing one, something like the transplant; one where the numbers are lopsided enough that a reader who's been nodding along starts to squirm. the refrain has to survive both untouched.

please dont use the words deontology, consequentialism, or agent-relative anywhere in the poem, and no closing couplet that explains the point. someone whos read the debate should be able to say "that's the minimize-violations objection" about one couplet and "that's the hands-clean reply" about another; someone who hasn't should just get a good ghazal.
