Write a short story of roughly 1,500 words built on the problem von Neumann left open in the lectures published posthumously as *The Computer and the Brain*: that the nervous system does not compute in mathematics, but in a language of its own which we have not yet learned to read, and that a machine genuinely built on the brain's plan would have to use components far slower, far less precise, and far more redundant than any engineer would freely choose — shallow logical depth bought back by breadth of parallel lines, imprecision bought back by statistical averaging, and a memory that is not a separate organ with an address but something smeared through the whole structure.

Build from this situation. In the autumn of 1959 a small group completes such a machine: deliberately slow, deliberately sloppy, deliberately redundant, with no memory bank to point to. It performs. But when they try to write down the notation in which it does its arithmetic, they find two complete and mutually consistent descriptions, and the descriptions disagree about everything that matters. Under one, the machine's states are the digits of a number system unlike ours; under the other, they are amplitudes in something closer to a chemistry. Every prediction either description makes about the machine's behavior is identical, and no experiment they can devise separates them. The senior mathematician holds that the machine has found a language and they have merely failed to find it. The physiologist holds that there is no fact of the matter until the machine has something to say to somebody, and that the second description is their own notation leaking into the machine's.

Tell it through the junior engineer, who must choose a description for a practical reason: the machine is about to be set a task on which the two accounts diverge in what they license, even though they never diverge in what they predict. Make the reader feel the difference between the two diagnoses without settling it. No character lectures, no dialogue that resolves the question, and the machine does not speak.
