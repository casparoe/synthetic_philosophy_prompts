I co-teach a seminar with the economic historians and there's a practice in that literature I can't quite get a handle on. People working on the so-called great divergence have mostly stopped asking "why did Europe pull ahead of China?" and now compare matched regions instead — say, England against the Yangzi delta — on the grounds that the civilisation-level question isn't really a question at all. But the books still come with civilisation-level titles, and at every job talk somebody asks the candidate to say somthing about China as a whole.

What theory of explanation does the matched-region practice presuppose? Reconstruct it for me. Does it commit you to the view that "why P" is incomplete until the contrast class is named, and that the contrast class fixes what the explanandum even is? Which rival accounts of explanation does it rule out — and does it genuinely rule them out, or just happen not to need them? Then the wrinkle: is that practice consistent with the way the same people still describe their own subject, or is the literature hedging between two incompatible pictures of what its question is? And what would have to change, methodologically, if you took the harsher line that "why Britain and not China?" isn't one question but a loose bundle of them, each indexed to whatever causal factors the asker happens to have in hand?

Argument, not bibliography, please.
