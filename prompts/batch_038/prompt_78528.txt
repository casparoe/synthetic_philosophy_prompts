I'm the programs person at a pretty small NGO doing livelihoods work in East Africa — mostly short business-skills training plus a small grant for market vendors. Our board met last week and one of the longtime members killed a proposal to pilot unconditional mobile money transfers instead by saying "give a man a fish, you feed him for a day; teach a man to fish, you feed him for a lifetime," everyone nodded, and that was that. I tried to push back and got nowhere, partly because I couldn't say what exactly I was objecting to.

Can you pull that sentence apart for me? I want a menu of the distinct claims it could be making — about the time profile of benefits, about what poor people actually lack (skills vs. capital vs. access), about dignity and who gets to be the active party, about dependency and moral hazard, about what the goal of aid even is, and about the giver's obligations rather than the effects on the reciever. For each one tell me: is it a platitude, is it substantive and defensible, or is it just false? Also flag what the proverb quietly assumes and leaves out — like whether the man owns the lake, whether he has a rod or a net, whether he can spare the afternoon, and whether the lake still has fish in it by the time he's learned. I keep feeling like the saying is doing a lot of work it hasn't earned.

Then the part I actually care about: which of those readings would really get him his conclusion, that transfers are the wrong thing for us to fund, and which readings are perfectly compatible with transfers turning out better than training? Is there a version where giving someone money just is handing them a fishing rod, so the contrast collapses? And is there a reading on which it isn't an empirical claim at all but a claim about what kind of giver you ought to be — because I suspect that's what he's really saying, and I want to know whether that version does any argumentative work or just shuts the conversation down in a way that feels like wisdom.

Don't bother citing specific studies or papers, I mostly want the conceptual map laid out as a list I could take back to the next board meeting.
