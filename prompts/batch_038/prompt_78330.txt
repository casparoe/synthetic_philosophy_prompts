so I'm auditing myself before I write up my thesis proposal, and I'd much rather be told now that my package doesn't hang together than find out in front of a committee. My supervisor's advice was basically "write down everything you actually believe, all of it, and see what falls out," which is how I got the list below. Background: second year, the proposal is nominally about the problem of the many, but I keep getting dragged into questions about time and I'm not sure the two stay separate in my head. Honestly the list looks fine to me when I read it top to bottom, but im suspicious that it isn't.

Here's what I believe:

(1) I'm an endurantist. Ordinary things — me, this table, a ship — are wholly present at every moment they exist. I don't buy that persisting works by having temporal parts, and I think the spacetime-worm picture gets both the phenomenology and the semantics of ordinary talk wrong.

(2) On change I don't want the relational move. I don't think "the poker is hot" is secretly "the poker is hot-at-t" with a time slot smuggled into the predication. I think was-hot, is-hot and will-be-hot are three perfectly respectable monadic properties and the poker really has them, full stop — not as shorthand for indexed ones and not as an adverbial modification of the having.

(3) But I'm an eternalist. Past, present and future all exist, there's no metaphysically privileged now, and "now" works the way "here" does. People tell me this basically forces temporal parts on me and I don't think it does, but I'd like to know whether I can actually get away with that.

(4) I'm willing to accept coincidence: at some moments more than one candidate object occupies the same matter, a statue-shaped one and a lump-shaped one, and they're numerically distinct. What makes one of them the genuine object rather than a mere candidate is that they differ in what they could have survived.

(5) I'm a Humean about the base. Every fact — including the modal facts, including which candidates are genuine objects — supervenes on the actual distribution of local, categorical, non-modal goings-on. No necessary connections, no primitive dispositions, nothing "sticky" beyond the local pattern.

(6) When the statue and the lump start to come apart (someone reshapes the clay while I'm not looking, say) I'm perfectly happy to say it's simply indeterminate whether they're identical, and I dont think that should worry me.

(7) At the same time I think vagueness is never in the world. Wherever there's vagueness, it's in our words or in our ignorance about where the boundaries fall; reality itself is perfectly precise all the way down.

So: audit me. Which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I want them ranked by severity rather than a flat list where everything gets flagged as "in tension" — if a pair is fine on inspection, say it's fine and say why, because part of what I need is to know which of these to stop losing sleep over. For each real tension, tell me which commitment has to give, whether that's a local repair or whether it drags others down with it, and what the resulting view would look like. Then separately: if I'm only allowed to change one thing, which single change buys the most coherence for the least? And flag anything in the list that isn't in tension but is just doing unearned work — I have a feeling at least one of these is carrying a load I've never actually argued for, and I'd like to know which one before someone else points at it.
