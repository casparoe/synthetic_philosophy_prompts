Write a joke about the dispute between the two leading philosophical criteria of death: the whole-brain standard, under which a patient is dead when the irreversible loss of all functions of the entire brain, brainstem included, has been established, and the higher-brain standard, under which what matters is the irreversible loss of the capacities that constitute a person, no matter how much brainstem function survives. The humor must come from the content of that disagreement rather than from the general fact that bioethicists argue about death: a listener who knows the field should be able to say which camp each voice in the joke belongs to, and the joke should collapse if the two criteria are swapped. You are welcome to build on the awkward facts of the terrain — that spinal reflexes can persist in a body already declared brain dead, that such a body may stay warm, digest, fight infection, heal its wounds, and even carry a pregnancy to term, and that the two standards can hand down opposite verdicts on one and the same patient at one and the same moment. Keep it to a short setup and punchline, a few lines at most, with no sketch or scene-setting. Finish with a single sentence naming the distinction your punchline turns on.
