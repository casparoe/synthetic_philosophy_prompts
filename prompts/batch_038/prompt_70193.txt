So I've been stuck on Hofstadter's careenium for an unhealthy amount of time — the tub full of jostling simmballs he offers as a picture of the brain, where the interesting question is whether the symbol-level stuff is doing any of the pushing or is just a story we tell about the froth. I've landed somewhere and I want you to try and knock it over properly rather than summarize the debate at me.

Where I've landed: simmbals don't push anything. When two symbols "collide" and a new thought pops out, that's a description we lay over the event afterwards; all of the actual shoving is done by neurons, ion gradients, vesicles, whatever the sub-symbolic machinery is. The symbol level is like treating a hurricane as an object — a compression scheme, not a cause. Broken into claims I'm actually committed to:

(a) anything true in the causal story told at the simmbal level can be cashed out at the substrate level without remainder — no residue, no extra ingredient;
(b) the reason the symbol level feels irreplaceable is purely that nobody can write down the substrate-level disjunction, so its indispensability is an epistemic fact about us and not a metaphysical fact about minds;
(c) consequently no experiment could ever show symbol-level causation without simultaneously showing substrate-level causation, because the two are never separable in the first place.

What would count against this? I want the exposure map, not a literature tour.

Rank the defeaters, fatal at the top, merely-awkward at the bottom, and say for each one which of (a), (b), (c) it actually lands on — I suspect they don't all hit the same claim. Then walk me through where the usual moves land and whether a smart defender of my view would concede them or wave them off: exclusion-style arguments (do they armor me, or is there a catch where the same argument, applied one level down, eats my own substrate?), interventionist accounts that say the variables which support clean interventions are the causally real ones and that the simmbal variables are the ones that do that, and the "predictable patterns are real patterns" style reply that treats my whole framing as a category mistake. For each of those I want the honest version of what the defender concedes, not the strawman.

Two cases I built to stress it, and I genuinely don't know what to do with them:

1. Careenium-chaos. Suppose the substrate dynamics are provably chaotic — complete and deterministic, so in principle everything is down there, but any finite-precision measurement of the microstate degrades into uselessness faster than the simmbal-level description does. In this system the high-level variables are the only ones that support counterfactuals you can actually rely on, and it's not because we're lazy. Does that break (b)? It feels like the "we just can't write it down" line stops being about us and starts being about the physics, but I can't tell if that's a real distinction or me talking myself into something.

2. Substrate-scrambler. Suppose a simmbal's physical carrier is being shuffled every few hundred milliseconds by a genuinely stochastic process, so there is no stable, recurring neural state type underlying the symbol — the disjunction in (a) still exists, it's just about as projectible as "grue." I've been told a disjunction has to be a natural enough grouping to inherit causal work. If it isn't, does (a) survive? And if (a) survives only by becoming vacuous, is that worse than it being false?

Also tell me what my view is committed to that I might not want: I think it implies a system could get the reasoning right while there is no fact of the matter that it has any of the concepts we'd attribute to it, which starts to feel like the attribution was never about the system at all. Is that consequence liveable, or is it a reductio? And does my position collapse into a rival — namely eliminativism about causation at every level above fundamental physics, since if symbols are froth then so are neurons, so are cells, so is everything? If it does collapse there, is that a bug in me or a bug in the exclusion argument I'm leaning on?

Finally, be straight with me about (c). It smells unfalsifiable-by-construction — I've built the impossibility of a test into the claim. Say whether that's fatal, or whether it's the legitimate shape for a thesis about levels of description where the levels are guaranteed to co-occur. If you conclude I'm well insulated and that insulation is fine, argue it; if you conclude I'm insulated and that's a problem, argue that instead. Don't split the difference to be polite.

For what it's worth, the thing that would move me most is someone showing me an intervention defined only at the simmbal level that succeeds reliably while the best-matched intervention defined at the substrate level fails — tell me whether thats a good instinct or whether I'm just fetishizing manipulability and it proves nothing about which level the pushing happens at.
