I'm a postdoc and on Friday I've got forty minutes with our new cognitive science masters intake during orientation week -- fourteen people, mostly psych and biology backgrounds, three who code for a living and have trained models before, and one who's done a philosophy degree and will enjoy being difficult. The session is on Dennett's four kinds of creature: Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian. Whiteboard only, no slides, and I've been told explicitly not to let it drift into a talk about consciousness -- the whole point is to hand them a vocabulary they'll keep meeting for the rest of the term. So: don't explain the taxonomy to me, I know it fine. Plan how I should build it in front of them.

The bit I can't get straight is the dependency structure. My instinct is that the real content isn't "kinds of animal getting cleverer" but "what is doing the selecting, and where the information about the world is coming from" -- genes, then the creature's own trial and error, then an internal model that takes the hits, then tools somebody else designed. But I don't know how early I can say that out loud without it being meaningless to them, and I don't know what has to be true about how I introduce the first level for the second one to be any more than a list. Related: what's the single case that should carry all four rungs? I was going to hang the whole thing on a digger wasp doing its rigid little routine, then work upwards from there, but I'm worried that makes it look like a ladder of smartness, which is the exact message I don't want. Tell me what the carrying example has to have and what it has to lack, and whether one example can do all four or whether I need to switch partway. And when should the ML people be allowed to ask where a trained model sits -- early, as motivation, or held to the end, given that I suspect they'll say "model-based RL is obviously Popperian" and be half right.

Then map the misconceptions and tell me the point in the sequence where each one becomes cheaper to prevent than to repair. I'm already braced for "so these are stages a human baby goes through", for "Gregorian just means uses tools, so the chimp with the termite stick counts", and for someone deciding the four levels are cumulative in the sense that you could strip the top ones off a person and get the wasp back. I also expect the Popperian rung to feel like cheating -- if an animal can predict, hasn't it just been conditioned to expect? -- and I want to know whether to attack that head-on or route around it. Give me one question to put to the room at the end that would show me whether the selecting-process idea actually landed rather than four labels being memorized, ideally a question where a wrong answer is diagnosable at a glance.
