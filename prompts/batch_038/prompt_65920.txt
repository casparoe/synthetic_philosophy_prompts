The proposition, stated as tightly as I can manage: in Of Cannibals, Montaigne is committed to the view that the standards by which one culture judges another's practices are drawn from the judging culture's own customs, with no independent standard available to him — call it relativism proper — as opposed to a single standard of nature and reason that he applies even-handedly to the Tupinambá and to the French and simply finds the French wanting. The passage I keep coming back to, in the Cotton-Hazlitt rendering I have open: "I find that there is nothing barbarous and savage in this nation, by anything that I can gather, excepting, that every one gives the title of barbarism to everything that is not in use in his own country," followed immediately by "As, indeed, we have no other level of truth and reason, than the example and idea of the opinions and customs of the place wherein we live."

Give me your credence in that proposition and then break it into the sub-questions it actually rests on, each with a number and a sentence or two of justification:
