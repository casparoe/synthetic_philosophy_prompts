ok so for debate club i've gotten attached to a pretty strong version of the paradox of voting and i want someone to actually try to break it, not just recite the standard objections at me.

my version isnt "your vote probably wont matter." it's that voting can never be explained instrumentally. the reasoning: if the point of an act is to bring about an outcome, the expected payoff has to clear the cost of doing it, and in any election big enough to matter the chance of being decisive is so tiny that you could value the outcome enormously and still not clear it. and i think the usual patches prove my point — once you add a duty term, or say voting is expressive, or that people vote for their team, youve stopped giving an outcome-based explanation and quietly admitted that story failed.

what would count against this? rank them for me: which objections would be fatal, which ones i could honestly shrug off, and which real-world facts (not thought experiments) should make me nervous — like races actually decided by a single vote, or turnout going up when an election looks close. also tell me straight: does my position even risk anything, or have i made it unbreakable by definining every rival account as "not really instrumental"? and if it is insulated like that, is that a problem for a claim about why people do things, or is that just what a claim like this looks like? numbers move me more than intuitions, fwiw — if you can point at a realistic close election where the decisive probability is way bigger than im assuming, that's the kind of thing i'd want to hear first.
