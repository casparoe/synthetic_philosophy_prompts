**The Latency Wedge: a Third Possibility in the Colour-Phi Debate**

1. *The experiment.* Two small spots of different colour are flashed in succession, separated by a few degrees of visual angle. At certain combinations of separation and inter-flash interval, observers do not report two stationary flashes; they report a single object travelling from the first location to the second and changing colour abruptly partway along its path. The finding was reported in the mid-1970s by Kolers and von Grünau, who took it up because Nelson Goodman had asked what a colour change would do to apparent motion. When pressed, observers are prepared to say that the colour change occurred before the second spot came on.

2. *The puzzle.* At the instant the first spot is on the screen, nothing in the stimulus tells the visual system whether a second spot will follow, where it will be, or what colour it will have. So the moving, recolouring object cannot have been the content of anyone's experience at that instant. And yet the report is about the first part of the path: the object was already there, and it was already coloured.

3. *The dichotomy.* Dennett and Kinsbourne sort candidate explanations into two families. On the Orwellian account, experience at the time was accurate and the record was altered afterwards; on the Stalinesque account, the finished story was composed only once the second spot arrived and then presented as though it had been composed all along. They argue that the two families are empirically indistinguishable: the same neural events, the same reports, the same behaviour, and — crucially — the same account of how it feels to be the subject. Their conclusion is that the question looks substantive only because it presupposes a finish line, a time and place at which processing becomes experience, and that no such finish line exists.

4. *My objection: the dichotomy omits a case.* As a kineticist I find a third possibility the natural one, and I do not see it represented. Call it the open-file account. When the first spot appears, the system opens a file whose content is not a determinate percept but a placeholder: location registered, colour registered, motion status pending. The placeholder is not a conscious experience of a stationary spot that is later edited, so there is nothing to overwrite; and it is not a completed entry backdated over the interval, because it was never timestamped in the first place. When the second spot arrives, the file is closed with a value whose temporal extent is the whole interval. The difference from Orwellianism is that no determinate content was ever fixed; the difference from Stalinesque revision is that nothing is written into the past, since the past slot was left empty by design rather than filled and then corrected. I have been told this is merely the retentional or extensional model of temporal consciousness in laboratory clothing. I would like to be told precisely what the difference is, because I cannot see one: those models address how a melody is heard as a melody, whereas mine addresses what the system is licensed to report before the evidence arrives.

5. *A test, which I call the latency wedge.* If composition is deferred until the second spot — Stalinesque or open-file — then the earliest moment at which a decision about the first interval can be executed is postponed. A subject instructed to press a key the instant motion begins should therefore show a longer latency in unpredictable sequences than in blocks where the second spot's location and timing are fully predictable and the system can pre-compose. If, on the other hand, experience at the first spot is already determinate and only the memory record is revised, the key press should not care whether a second spot is coming. The two families would then have distinguishable consequences.

6. *The corollary that interests me more.* Suppose the wedge appears. A measurable deferral of composition has a duration, and a duration is exactly what a finish-line model requires and what the no-finish-line model is supposed to render unintelligible. Suppose instead that it does not appear. Then the open-file account has lost its only distinctive prediction and collapses into Orwellianism. Either way the experiment seems to bear on the metaphysics, which is what I was told it could not do.

7. *Supporting intuition.* I lean on two other findings. In the flash-lag effect, a brief flash presented in perfect alignment with a moving object is seen as trailing behind it, and one family of explanations holds that information arriving after the flash is recruited to settle where the flash was seen to be. And Libet reported that the felt onset of a weak cutaneous stimulus is referred backwards in time by several hundred milliseconds. Both suggest to me that backdating is a genuine engineering option rather than a philosopher's puzzle.

8. *Where I expect trouble.* I suspect section 5 conflates the timing of neural events with the timing of experience. I cannot tell whether that conflation is a fatal error or the very point at issue.
