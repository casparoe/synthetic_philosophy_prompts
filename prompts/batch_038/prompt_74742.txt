I'm writing up where I've landed on Sen's liberal paradox for a reading group and I want a real consistency check before I present, not a lecture on the theorm — I know the proof. Here's what I actually hold:

1. Sen's minimal-liberty condition gets the idea of a right wrong. A right you can't give up isn't much of a right, so I'm with Gibbard: freedom is about the options you face, game forms, not a constraint on the social ranking.
2. That said, I want the whole analysis to live inside the Arrovian machinery — unrestricted domain, Pareto, transitive social ranking. Restricting the domain feels like defining the problem away, and I don't want to beat an impossibility theorem by moving the goalposts.
3. Pareto stays in full. I'm unconvinced by the Dworkin-style move of writing off the Lewd's preference as "external" — a preference is a preference, and counting only some of them is rigging the game in favour of rights.
4. Still, I think the paradox is mostly a curiosity manufactured by nosiness: if each person's preferences depend only on what goes on inside their own protected sphere, nothing contradictory falls out. So it isn't a genuine threat to liberalism.
5. On rights I'm a side-constraint person, not a rights-consequentialist: they limit what may be done to you, they aren't a state of affairs to be promoted.
6. Concrete verdicts so you have something to bite on: in the Lady Chatterley case the Prude's choice should hold even when it leaves the Lewd worse off — and it would be an exercise of liberty rather than a surrender of it if the Prude instead let the Lewd decide for him.

Go through these and tell me which of them pull against each other and how badly, ranked by severity rather than all flagged as equally suspect. I also suspect some of the tensions I worry about are merely verbal and evaporate once you pin down what the terms mean, so separate those out and say why they're only apparent. For each genuine one, what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost me elsewhere? And if I could only change a single thing, which change buys the most consistency?
