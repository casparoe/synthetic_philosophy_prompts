For two terms I've been auditing a New Testament seminar in which the professor rules, one candidate at a time, on sayings attributed to Jesus, working from the whole profile of evidence rather than from any single criterion. Her verdicts so far:

1. Attested in three streams she judges genuinely independent, in two distinct literary forms; the streams' theological programs pull against one another; nothing embarrassing in it; it sits comfortably with each evangelist; no Semitic poetic footprint; coheres with material she already accepts. → Authentic.
2. Single witness, but every surviving version shows trimming or reframing by the community that carried it, and it cuts against that community's interests. → Authentic.
3. Single witness, no sign of softening, sits neatly inside its evangelist's redactional program, but is built on a parallelism and mnemonic structure that reads as Aramaic beneath the Greek, and hangs together with accepted material. → Undetermined; she declined to rule.
4. Attested in four gospels, but she holds three of them literarily dependent on the fourth, so the witnesses collapse into one. → Inauthentic.
5. Single witness, strikingly dissimilar both to the Judaism of its setting and to the later church, no embarrassment, no linguistic footprint, no coherence support. → Inauthentic; she remarked that this is the profile of what a community invents when it wants a founder belonging to no one but itself.

Next week's case: a saying in which Jesus foretells the temple's ruin and his followers' persecution. Three streams she accepts as independent, in two literary forms; no trace of softening in any version; the saying disserves no stream and serves all of them equally, since each community had reason to want a founder who had predicted their troubles; no Aramaic poetic structure; coheres with accepted material.

Predict her verdict, then identify the rule she is running. In particular: does independent multiple attestation carry weight in itself, or is she demanding throughout that the material survived against someone's interest — so that convergent witnesses count only when their interests diverged, and embarrassment counts because it is the same demand seen from inside a single stream? Give at least one further rule consistent with all five verdicts and state what it implies for the new case. If the precedents genuinely underdetermine the ruling, say what principle she would be adopting on each side of the choice, and design the single additional case that would discriminate between the candidate rules. State your confidence in the prediction explicitly, and tell me what would change your mind: which of the five precedents I would have to have misreported, or which feature of the new case's profile your reasoning is leaning on most heavily.
