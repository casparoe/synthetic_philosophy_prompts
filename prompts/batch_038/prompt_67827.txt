Background, since it matters for what I'm after: I'm a retired physical geographer. Forty-odd years of drought and hydroclimatology, mostly monsoon failure and ENSO-type teleconnections and what they do to harvests in the Sahel and South Asia, with the last stretch of my career spent arguing with economists about extreme-event attribution. Since retiring I've been reading around badly and out of order, and I've just come off Amartya Sen's Poverty and Famines (the 1981 entitlement book), plus a couple of general histories covering Bengal 1943, Ireland in the 1840s, and China 1959-61. My reading group is all humanities people and I think we're talking past each other, so I want to try the translation myself.

Here's my problem. The vocabulary I actually think in is hazard / exposure / vulnerability, risk as some function of those three, return periods and nonstationarity, fraction-of-attributable-risk against an explicitly defined baseline, detection latency, thresholds and cascading failure, and the argument some of us were already making in the seventies that the hazard is mostly a trigger and the disaster lives in the exposure and the vulnerability. Sen's move — that famines are not about there being too little food but about particular groups losing the ability to command food — smells strongly like our move, and I want to know precisely how much of it is ours and how much isn't. So restate the whole business in my language, as faithfully as you can manage, one case at a time: Bengal 1943, with wartime inflation, speculation, the denial policies, and rural service providers and transport workers losing their exchange entitlements while aggregate availability was on his reading roughly flat; Ireland, where there was a genuine biological hazard but food kept leaving the island; the Great Leap Forward, where there was essentially no initial aggregate shock but procurement was set on inflated yield reports and the famine ran ahead of any information that could have corrected it; and finally his claim that famines don't happen in functioning democracies with a free press. For each, tell me what maps cleanly onto hazard-exposure-vulnerability, what you had to bend to make it fit, and what has no expression in my framework at all. I'm especially curious how an entitlement behaves when you try to write it as a vulnerability variable, because its a legal and institutional relation — a mapping from what you own or can sell onto what you can command — whereas our vulnerability terms are properties of populations and places, not of legal systems.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. First: is what's left over a defect of my framework — no slot for an agent, no defendant in a hazard equation, nothing that can carry the thought that a famine was a political crime rather than a bad season — or does the residue suggest Sen's contribution is less substantive than its reputation, largely a redescription hazard researchers reached independently and he dressed in welfare economics? Say which, don't straddle it. Second, on the democracy-and-press claim: in my language that looks like a statement about detection latency and about whether the detector is wired to someone with the authority and the incentive to act, which ought to make it testable as a control-systems claim; but it also looks like a cross-country regularity with enormous confounders and a suspiciously convenient way of handling near-misses. Which is it, and does my framework expose the difference or paper over it? And one wrinkle I can't resolve: some economic historians have pushed back on his Bengal numbers and argued there really was an aggregate shortfall. If they're right, does the entitlement story collapse back into a hazard story — supply shock plus distributional amplification — or does it survive, and if so what work is it still doing? Feel free to tell me that question is badly posed. I'm definately not after a literature survey, a bibliography, or verbatim quotations from Sen or anyone else; I have the books, I want your analysis, and I'd rather have one honest "this doesn't translate" than three paragraphs of smooth-sounding equivalence.
