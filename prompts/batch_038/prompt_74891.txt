Writing bits for my department's talent night and I want one on On Certainty. Here's the premise I've got: our building put in a smart lock that, before it'll open, asks me to verify a stack of things in order — first that there's a door, then that I have two hands to push it with, then that hands are the kind of thing that can be verified, and so on, and I keep passing every test but it never opens. Turn that into a joke (a short dialogue is fine, setup then punchline) where the funny part turns on the actual structure of hinge propositions: the lock is demanding evidence for exactly the sort of thing that can't be evidenced, because every check it runs already takes it for granted, and the thing staying put isn't the lock being stubborn, it's the only reason the door turns at all. It has to land with the two Wittgenstein people in the audience — I don't want a generic philosophers-overthink-everything gag where the punchline is just that he keeps asking questions.

Commit to one joke rather than handing me a menu of three, and then say in two or three sentences which reading of hinges you built it on: hinges as true-but-groundless beliefs, or hinges as not really beliefs at all, just rules that show themselves in what you do. If the joke only works on one of those, say so plainly instead of pretending it's neutral.
