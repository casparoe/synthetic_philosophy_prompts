I build predictive risk models for industrial equipment, and I have no formal philosophy training, so please don't assume familiarity beyond the terms I use below.

A recurring puzzle in my work has led me to something I gather is a real philosophical controversy. To estimate the probability that a specific unit fails in a specific way, I have to choose a denominator: all units, all units on that firmware build, all units on that build in that region operating within that load window. Each refinement makes the estimate more relevant to the individual case and less statistically stable, and at the limit the class contains only the unit I am asking about, where the observed frequency is trivially zero or one and carries no information. In practice I paper over this with smoothing, but I have come to suspect that my smoothing constant is doing the work that the choice of reference class was supposed to do: it is a disguised assumption about which class I meant.

Apparently this generalizes. I understand Reichenbach named it the problem of the reference class and answered it by requiring a homogeneous reference class, with later refinements along the lines of Hempel's requirement of total evidence and Salmon's maximal specificity; the standard objection is that maximal specificity drives every class toward size one and thereby collapses the frequency account of single-case probability.

Write an essay on whether the reference class problem is fatal to a frequency-based account of single-case probability, or whether it can be answered. Engage with the strongest form of the objection rather than a strawman about careless data pipelines, and take seriously at least one proposed repair — for example, restricting total evidence to properties that are statistically relevant rather than merely known, or treating the reference class as fixed by the question being asked rather than by the world. I would also like some attention to what happens when the individual whose probability is in question is themselves a member of the class and knows it, as in the Doomsday-style self-locating cases, since that seems to be where the trouble is sharpest. Do not sit on the fence: argue for a definite position and say what you think survives of the frequentist program.
