twelve years as a notary, mostly out of a freight office, and I never thought about it much until somebody in my book club said that saying I promise, or saying I do at a wedding, isnt describing anything — its doing something. Apparently theres a term for it, performative utterance, came from some philosopher. That kind of wrecked my brain, because its literally my job: a person says a thing in front of me and a piece of paper changes status.

so I built myself a picture and Id like it stress-tested. an oath is a check. the words are the paper. what makes it worth anything is what stands behind it: funds (the person actually intending what theyre swearing to) and the bank (the right procedure, the right official, the right form of words — you cant hand me a napkin with DONT FOLD on it and call it a check). and a check with nothing behind it isnt a small check or a weak check, its a bad check. counterfeit, void, no payment ever happened.

I use that picture to argue one thing and my brother-in-law uses the exact same picture to argue the opposite, which is the whole reason Im asking. Me: when a guy signs an affadavit hes not sure of — happened last month, an owner-operator swearing to his logs while we both knew the hours were fiction — then no oath ever happened, just noise on a page, and thats part of why charging that as perjury sits wrong with me, like charging somebody for stealing money that was never there. Him: the stamp is the stamp, the bank cleared it, what the guy privately believes is beside the point, the oath is completely real and hes completely on the hook.

can you audit the check picture for me? what plays the bank, the funds, the signature, the clearing house, and where does the picture start lying to me. which of the places it breaks actually matter for the argument Im using it for, and which ones are just decoration. does my perjury point survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the whole thing — and if his version survives better, say why it survives better. and if theres a better picture for what I actually do all day, give me one.
