I help run a volunteer mushroom-foray group and this year we started telling people which species have turned up at a site before they walk it, on the theory that it helps them know what to look for. Last month two people independantly swore they saw white gills and a sack at the base on what turned out to be an ordinary field mushroom — they'd both been briefed that destroying-angel type amanitas had been logged at that wood before. One of our old timers says that's just what expertise is, that knowing what you're after literally changes what you see and there's no clean line to draw between the two. The other says we've trashed their eyes as observers and should be sending people out blind, especially since we feed our records into a regional database and a manufactured sighting pollutes it for everybody downstream. Is there a principled way to settle which of them is right, or is "did the briefing change the seeing or just the reporting" unanswerable from the inside? Lead with your verdict, then tell me what we could actually change about how a walk is run that would give us some evidence either way. Under 250 words please.
