So a bunch of us (14 households right now, aiming at maybe 80) are trying to set up a co-housing thing on some land outside town, and I got volunteered onto the "how do we actually live together" working group. We've spent two months arguing about bylaws — quiet hours, guest limits, whether the common kitchen gets a chore rota with a signup sheet or just a vibe, whether we fine people or shame people or neither, whether decisions need consensus or two-thirds. Every argument eventually collapses into "some of you want a tight community and some want a loose one," which is how I fell down the tightness/looseness rabbit hole (Gelfand's line mostly). It's interesting but useless to me, and before I read another word of it I want to know what I should even be demanding of a theory here. So: pretend you're someone who does cultural psychology and institutional design for a living, think carefully, brainstorm a long list of candidate requirements first if you need to, and then write me the pruned desiderata — what any adequate account of "how much rule-following should a group demand of its members" has to deliver. For each one, give me the case or consideration that makes it a requirement; I don't want a list of labels, I want to see why each item has to be on there. A few I already have stuck in my head, use or discard as you see fit: it has to say whether tightness is a property of a group or of a group-and-a-domain, since our place would be strict about money and totally relaxed about how people raise their kids; it has to not quietly collapse into authoritarianism, or conservatism, or collectivism, and it has to say what would count as evidence that it's a separate dimension at all; it has to handle the fact that people inside a tight system are often the worst reporters of how strong the sanctions really are, because most of the enforcement is invisible to them; it has to deal with scale — nearly all the evidence is at country or state level, and I need to know whether the mechanisms do any work at 80 households where enforcement is purely reputational and everybody already knows everybody; and it has to take a position on whether the trade-off everyone assumes (tight buys order and predictability, loose buys creativity and adaptability) is real or an artifact of how the measures were built.

Then the part I actually care about most. Which of your requirements are genuine conditions on adequacy and which are just the prejudices of one camp — I suspect that insisting tightness be a single number is a measurement convenience rather than a requirement, and that the demand for a strictly non-functionalist explanation is a tic of one methodology. Mark the non-negotiables separately from the nice-to-haves. If some subset can't be jointly satisfied, say which and say why, because that would itself be the interesting result: my candidate is that norms partly work precisely because people follow them non-instrumentally, so a theory that recommends tightening on the grounds that it pays off may corrode the thing it's recommending, and if that's right then "the recommendation must be justified by its consequences" and "the recommendation must actually be effective" can't both be met. Also tell me which single requirement knocks out the most of the answers on offer, and end with one genuinely short paragraph on how the two or three most obvious candidate views do against your list — don't score them item by item, I'll do that at the next meeting.

No reading list and no citations please, I've got the empirical side covered and I want the spec. And don't hand me a menu with the decision left to me — commit. Take a definite position on which requirements are the real ones and give me your reasons, and if you're shaky on one of the empirical claims in passing, flag the uncertainty on that one item rather than hedging the whole thing.
