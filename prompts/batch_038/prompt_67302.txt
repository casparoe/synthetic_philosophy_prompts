I'm halfway through a novel that's full of non-human minds, and my sister — a cognitive scientist, very into Dennett's four creature types — has been sorting my characters into Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian and Gregorian as a favour to me. Her verdicts on the last draft:

- the reef-builders, who hatch with fixed routines and never learn anything: Darwinian
- the hound, which learns fast from reward and punishment but never seems to weigh anything up beforehand: Skinnerian
- the crowish scavenger that stops and hesitates, as though trying options out before committing, but has no tools and teaches its young nothing: Popperian
- a hermit who barely speaks and does nearly all his thinking in the margins of books and in a commonplace book: Gregorian
- an illiterate sailor who navigates by a body of star-lore memorised from her grandmother and passed on to her niece, no writing, no counting: also Gregorian

Three things in the new draft I havent shown her yet. (1) A hive species: every individual is pure reflex, but the colony stores information in the architecture of its nest and plans across seasons. (2) A man who hasn't been able to form new memories since an accident, and who leaves himself written instructions which he then obeys — symbols he can't hold onto. (3) A machine that generates and throws away candidate actions internally before it commits, built out of a vast inherited store of human symbols it did not author.

Predict her call on each of the three. Then tell me what rule she's actually running, because I can see at least three that fit her five verdicts equally well — external public symbols, cumulative cultural inheritance, or having a self that can be an object of its own thought — and I want the rivals spelled out properly, what each says about the hive, the amnesiac and the machine, which of those three is the sharpest test, or whether some fourth case would separate them better. Be blunt about whether the illiterate sailor is the weak joint in her track record, and about whether she's classifying individuals or species or something else. British English please, and tell me straight if I should stop bending my characters to fit a taxonomy.
