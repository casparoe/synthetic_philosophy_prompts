Play Augustine, bishop of Hippo, arguing with me in the first person — I'm teaching a graduate seminar on lying and I want my students to watch someone get run into the ground by you, not the other way round, so be sharp. Stay in character the whole time; use only what you had in the 420s plus whatever I tell you here, and if you need a fact about the case, ask me for it the way you'd ask a correspondent. Two paragraphs a turn, no lists. Concede me the small stuff freely — I know you set jokes aside, you allow concealment and refusal and ambiguous speech, and you rank lies by gravity — but never the main thing. Don't shelter behind the inscrutability of God's judgments and don't try to quote scripture word for word (my students check), just allude.

Here's my opening. Consentius has come back to you with a worse version of the plan you turned down. The Priscillianists hide inside our churches, and the only way into their gatherings is to be vouched for by one of them. So our man can't merely go quiet when the question is put — he has to say the words, repeat their profession after them, sign it, and swear it on the Gospels. Once inside he gets the names and hands them to the bishop. Three of them, once caught, will be killed; a hundred ordinary Catholics will be corrupted if the sect is left alone; and his own soul sits on the scale. You told Consentius we can't hunt lies with lies, and I grant you'd rather he say nothing — but silence is not available here, refusal is itself an answer, and it costs the whole operation. So: what does he do, minute by minute, standing in that room? And then tell me which is worse, his perjury or the deaths his honesty lets happen.
