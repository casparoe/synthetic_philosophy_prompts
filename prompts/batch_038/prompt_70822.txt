Background first, since it probably colors everything: I ran a nociception lab for about 35 years, retired four years ago, and I've fallen into philosophy the way other people fall into gardening. Mostly ethics, mostly the stuff about suffering. I've been reading around this position people call lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, and the older argument about whether pleasure and pain are even on the same measuring stick. I want to put my own intuitions down and see if they add up to a view, because I suspect they dont.

Here's what I actually believe about trading pain against pleasure. Numbered, because I'm a list person.

1. A hangnail, a mosquito bite, the low-grade back ache I've had since 1998: easily outweighed. I'd take an hour of it in exchange for an hour of something I enjoy, and I'd want other people to make that trade too.
2. One person tortured for an hour: not outweighed by any amount of pleasure, anywhere, no matter how many people get it or for how long. Firm on this. A billion blissful lives don't pay for it.
3. A billion people each with a hangnail for a minute is worse than one person tortured for an hour. Also firm.
4. Pain doesn't scale linearly with intensity, for me. Two hours at a 5 out of 10 is much worse than one hour at a 5, but it's nowhere near as bad as one hour at a 10. I don't mean that as a hunch. I've watched subjects' ratings and their faces for decades and I'd defend it. Ten on a clinic scale is not twice five.
5. But intensity is continuous. When we had people rate noxious stimuli in the lab, the distributions were smooth, and psychophysics has known for ages that magnitude estimates grow like a power of the stimulus with exponents well above one for noxious stuff. There is no natural joint at 7, or 8, or wherever the cutoff in (2) is supposed to sit. So (2) looks arbitrary to me and I still can't give it up.
6. Spreading: if I'm the one deciding who gets hurt, I spread it thin — ten people at level 3 rather than one at level 9. But once something is over the line in (2), spreading stops helping; the number of people involved just doesn't move me anymore.
7. First person and third person don't match. As a patient I'd refuse any deal involving the worst pain no matter what good came of it. As the person allocating, I think I'd accept one instance of it to spare a great many people mid-level pain. I can't tell whether that's a real moral difference or cowardice.
8. My own attempted fix, which I want attacked: score suffering as the integral of intensity to a power p, with p big — 4, 10, whatever — and cap pleasure per person at some finite ceiling. Then the badness curve is so steep that nothing real ever catches up with it, and I get threshold-like behaviour without asserting a threshold. My worry is that this is a lexical view wearing a lab coat, and that with finite p and no cap on the number of sufferers, enough low-grade pain still adds up past anything, which busts (2) and (6) again.

So: is there a principle — a single ordering rule over distributions of pleasure and pain, stated as precisely as you can manage, semi-formal is welcome, I'm comfortable with that — that gets as many of 1 through 8 as possible? Tell me which ones it keeps, which it forces me to abandon, and why the abandoned ones are the right ones to abandon rather than some other set. Then give me its verdicts on three cases I haven't listed, and please make one of them about wild animals, where you have an enormous quantity of low-intensity suffering and essentially nobody around to enjoy anything, because that's the case that keeps me up at night.

One more thing, and it's the question I'd most like answered because it's the kind I know how to think about: is a steep-but-finite power function distinguishable, even in principle, from a genuine lexical threshold? Is there some population, however far fetched, where the two order things differently — or some structural property of the ordering, transitivity, continuity, some completeness condition — that one of them has and the other provably can't? If they come out equivalent for every finite case, I'd like to know that plainly, because then I'd have to conclude the whole dispute is about nothing.
