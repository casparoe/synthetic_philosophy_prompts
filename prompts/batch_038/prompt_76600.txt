I run intake at a small charity fund that pays for medical care people can't otherwise afford. We get around forty applications a month and can fund six. Its written policy is basically luck egalitarianism as far as I understand it — we help people who got unlucky, and we don't reward people whose own choices put them there — but in practice that sentence just becomes an hour of panelists arguing.

Turn it into a decision procedure my panelists could actually run. They're volunteers, no philosophy background, about twenty minutes per case. I need: the questions in the order they should be asked; what an application has to establish to clear each one and move on; the exact places where bad luck shades into bad choices and what the panelist is supposed to do standing at that line; and the points where the procedure has to stop and say "this is a values call, escalate to the board" instead of pretending to compute an answer.

Two wrinkles it has to handle. First, when the choice was made under circumstances the applicant didn't choose either — someone skipped a screening because she was working two jobs and nobody ever told her it mattered. Second, half my panel wants to ask how the person got sick and the other half wants to ask how badly off they'll be regardless; the procedure should tell us which question governs and when the other one is allowed back in.

Then run it on three applications: a thirty-year-old with a congenital condition needing surgery; a fifty-five-year-old who chose to go BASE jumping, is now paralyzed, and needs rehab equipment; a forty-year-old who smoked for twenty years, quit ten years ago, and has lung disease. Be explicit about where following the principle strictly hands down a verdict I'm going to hate, and say what the procedure can't settle.

Printable numbered list for the questions, and keep the whole thing under 650 words.
