For a workshop on predictive processing I have been asked to put my position in writing so that it can be attacked directly. The package:

1. The free energy principle is a substantive empirical theory about how brains work, not a redescription of whatever happens; it is the sort of claim that could turn out false.
2. Perception, action, and learning are one process — hierarchical prediction-error minimization — so there is no separate reward-learning or motivational system. Value is nothing over and above prior preferences over hidden states built into the generative model.
3. Dopaminergic reward prediction error signals are among the strongest neural evidence we have for this picture.
4. Action is not a matter of representing future outcomes and planning toward them; an agent enacts its expected states by sampling the world so as to confirm them.
5. The dark room objection is already answered: because an organism's priors encode preferences over the states it expects to occupy, minimizing free energy does not license hiding in a dark room, and this is no ad hoc amendment.
6. An agent's boundaries are fixed by its Markov blanket, so what counts as one agent is a matter of fact rather than stipulation — and the same formalism applies to single cells, whole brains, dyads, and ecosystems.
7. I want the framework tested against local, well-characterized phenomena — mismatch negativity, binocular rivalry, adaptation to force fields — and I am wary of grand unified ambitions.
8. The slogan that perception is a controlled hallucination does not push me toward skepticism: I remain a realist about the external causes my perceptual hypotheses concern, and I think we perceive the world itself.
9. The brain only ever performs approximate inference and never represents exact posterior probabilities, yet perceptual content is genuinely probabilistic in a way that separates this picture from associationism.

Which of these are in genuine conflict, and which only look conflicting until they are unpacked? Order the real tensions by severity; for each, say which commitment has to give and what is lost by giving it up. Then tell me whether some single revision would dissolve more than one of them. I have no particular attachment to any item, so please do not read my ranking of what to abandon off the order in which I listed them.
