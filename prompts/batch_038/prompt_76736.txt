I am preparing the position statement for a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of the infinite, and before I circulate it I want a consistency audit of the package below. I know the material well enough that I do not need the literature surveyed or the standard cases retold to me; what I need is someone to trace which of my commitments commit me to what, and to be willing to tell me that most of them are fine. Argue from the structure of the commitments themselves, not from references.

Here is what I hold, stated as carefully as I can.

(1) The lamp. Take a lamp switched off at the start of a minute, switched on after half a minute, off after a further quarter, on after a further eighth, and so on. Thomson's reductio fails, and it fails for the reason Benacerraf gives. Every finite stage of the toggling is perfectly consistent; the description simply does not fix a state at the end of the minute. It does not follow that no state is possible there. Both end-states — lamp on, lamp off — extend the description without contradiction, so the supertask is logically possible.

(2) A methodological principle I mean to defend in general: no argument against a supertask that derives its contradiction from the completed task can be sound. Any such contradiction must trace back to an assumption about the terminal state that the task description never licensed.

(3) Achilles. The runner genuinely performs an ω-sequence of sub-traversals and finishes, and his position at the finish line is fully determinate in virtue of the continuity of the motion. Here I do not take the description to underdetermine anything at all.

(4) Supertask machines. An idealized machine that performs one operation in the first half-minute, the next in a quarter, the next in an eighth, and so on, could inspect the natural numbers one at a time and have a definite output value at the end of the minute, with no contradiction anywhere in its description. Such a machine decides the halting problem; and by iterating the device — letting later stages correct earlier ones — the same trick extends to arbitrary sentences of arithmetic. The machine is physically unrealizable, but that is beside the point: the issue is computability in principle, and logical possibility is the right standard.

(5) Two case verdicts. Variant A: the machine overwrites its output register at every stage, alternating its current best guess. I say there is no fact at the end of the minute about what the register says. Variant B: the register begins blank and is written to at most once — the first time the machine finds what it is looking for — and is never touched again. I say the register does have a determinate value at the end of the minute, namely whatever it settled at.

(6) No worldly indeterminacy. For any concrete system at any instant, each of its quantities has a determinate value; if my description fails to fix that value, the defect is in my description and not in the world. I have no use for ontic vagueness or metaphysically indeterminate states.

(7) A causal constraint I am not willing to give up: no quantity can take a value at a time unless that value is either the limit of its values at earlier times or is fixed by some finite earlier stage operating through the laws. Values do not simply arrive at an accumulation point.

(8) Where supertasks are physically impossible, the impossibility is always a matter of contingent resource — unbounded speed, energy, heat dissipation — and never of any incoherence in the idea of completing infinitely many operations.

I suspect there is more consistency here than a first pass suggests and less than I would like. I do not know where the joints are, and I am not asking you to spare any particular item. Please do not run a pairwise compatibility check and report every pair as equally worrying: I want discrimination, and I want to be told where there is nothing to fix.

Please organize your response under exactly these headings, in this order.

**Bottom line** (about 120 words). Whether the package is jointly tenable as it stands, and the single most serious fault in it, if there is one.

**Tensions, ranked** (no more than three entries, 150–250 words each). For each: a short label, a severity rating, the failing inference stated explicitly as premises and conclusion rather than as an impression, and which numbered commitment must be revised, restricted, or dropped — together with what that revision costs me elsewhere in the list.

**Only apparent** (about 100 words per item). The pairs that look inconsistent and are not, and the distinction that dissolves each. If a pair really does dissolve, say so plainly instead of hedging it into a minor tension; if you think one of my items merely needs rewording rather than abandoning, say that too.

**The cheapest repair** (about 250 words). The smallest set of revisions that leaves me with a consistent package, what each revision costs, and which single revision buys the most consistency for the least.

**Residual exposure** (about 120 words). After the repair, which of the surviving commitments is least stable, and what kind of argument would most likely break it.
