(2) Value is unbounded and scales with numbers: an outcome containing n good lives is n times as valuable as an outcome containing one, for any n.
(3) Nothing you can coherently describe has probability exactly zero — if you can write the outcome down, you can assign it some positive credence, however tiny.
(4) So take any act A with a finite expected value. Choose a tiny positive p and a payoff V big enough that p × V beats A's expected value. By (2) and (3) both p and V are on the table, and the gamble "V with probability p, otherwise nothing" is an act I can describe and therefore weigh against A.
(5) That gamble has higher expected value than A, so by (1) A is not right.
(6) A was arbitrary, so no ordinary act is ever right — not the 10%, not retraining as a doctor, not the careful incremental stuff. Only longshot gambles are ever right.
(7) But (4) applies to the gambles too: for any longshot there's a longer shot with a bigger payoff. So no gamble is right either. Nothing anyone does is ever right.
(8) Therefore (1) is false and maximizing expected value cannot be what makes an act right.
