ok so I've been chewing on the Chase and Sanborn bit in Quining Qualia for like the tenth time and I want to sanity-check where I've landed, because I keep getting steamrolled in these threads and I'm not sure if it's because I'm wrong or because I'm right in a way I can't articulate.

the setup as I read it: two professional coffee tasters, both of them certain the coffee doesn't taste the way it used to, and they hand you two incompatible stories about what changed — a slow drift over weeks vs a flip on some particular morning — and there is no fact available to either of them, introspective or otherwise, that could settle which story is true. and I take the point to be that once you pin down the reports, the discriminations, the hedonic reactions, the memory traces and the confidence levels, the leftover "but the quale itself changed" has stopped picking out anything. there's no shadow it casts anywhere in the system.

which is why I think the usual retreat doesn't work. when I argue with people they always fall back to the thin version — okay, forget ineffable intrinsic private properties, but there's still something it's like to see red — and my read is that the coffee case burns that down too, because the thin version is *supposed* to be what stays constant while all the functional stuff varies, and Chase and Sanborn is precisely a case where you stipulate the functional stuff constant and let the supposed constant do the varying. if you can't even coherently describe it as varying, it was never a thing to begin with. so the hard problem isn't defeated, it's dissolved, and anyone still running the zombie argument is just failing to notice that their own thought experiment needs the very hook the coffee case removes.
