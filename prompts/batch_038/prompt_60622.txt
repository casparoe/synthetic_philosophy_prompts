I'm teaching a seminar session on distributive justice and I want to hand my students a case that's been engineered rather than one they've already met a hundred times. So: build me a thought experiment to spec, and then show me the engineering actually holds up.

The spec. One claimant, one ordinary benefits office. Constraints:

- the scenario is one paragraph, realistic welfare-office setting. no trolleys, no sci-fi, no billionaires

- don't smuggle the verdicts into the wording. no "humiliating," no "heroic," no editorializing adjectives about her character. if the theories only come out the way I want because of how she's described, the case fails

Then give me the answer in these sections, in this order:

1. **The case** — the paragraph, ~120 words.
2. **Spec check** — a bullet per constraint above, one line each on how the case meets it. ~120 words.

American English, please. And be straight with me in section 4 — if you think one side wins too easily here, or if the guidance detail is doing less work than I'm assuming, tell me instead of quietly rigging it in my favor.
