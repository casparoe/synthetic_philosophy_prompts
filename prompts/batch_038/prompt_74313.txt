I'm writing a chapter for a collection on philosophy and prestige TV, and my case study is Severance, read against Marx on alienation and exploitation. I've got a thesis I like but I cant tell if its real or just cute, and I'd rather find that out now than during copy-editing.

The thesis: the show doesn't illustrate the four aspects of estranged labour from the 1844 manuscripts, it splits them across two people. Mark's outie is alienated from the act of producing — he doesn't experience any of it, doesn't remember any of it — but he's the one who signed the contract and pockets the salary. The innie is the mirror image: totally absorbed in the work, finds it meaningful, has real comradeship with Dylan and Irving and Helly, and gets finger traps and a waffle party instead of wages, with no idea what the numbers are for or who they benefit. So each of them holds a couple of Marx's four aspects and neither holds the whole structure, and that's why the thing feels stranger than an allegory about one bad job.

Two places I want pressure applied. First, does that hold up, or am I forcing it — in particular, can total absorption in a task count as unalienated labour when the task is meaningless even by the doer's own lights, since the innies can't form any conception of the product? Second, who is the exploited party here? The outie consented, the innie does the work and receives nothing, and we never actually learn that the refining produces anything saleable at all, so maybe this isn't exploitation in Marx's sense but something closer to the flat appropriation of somebody's lifetime. And then there's Helly, whose outie turns out to be an Eagan — worker and capitalist inside one body, which I think wrecks the class analysis and I don't know what to do with it.

Give me the honest counter-reading too. The show's engine is grief, not class; Mark severed so he could stop feeling for eight hours a day. Maybe the Marx frame is just a costume I'm putting on it, and if so tell me which parts of my argument survive the removal. Season 1 only please, I haven't properly watched season 2, so don't lean on it.
