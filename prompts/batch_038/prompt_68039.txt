A friend and I spent most of an evening arguing over whether Foucault can absorb scientific management, and I would like the question handled by someone disciplined about both sides rather than sympathetic to either.

Take Frederick Taylor's work — the Midvale time studies, the pig-iron and shoveling experiments at Bethlehem, the differential piece-rate — and analyze it as an instance of disciplinary power. Go through the mechanisms Foucault actually names, one at a time: hierarchical observation, normalizing judgment, the examination, the temporal elaboration of the act, body–object articulation, the production of docility. For each, say whether it applies on the shop floor, applies only by strained analogy, or is absent. Be specific about what the workshop lacks that the prison, the school, and the asylum have: no enclosure, no timetable in the monastic sense, no career structure, no file on the person behind the task.

Then the point where we stuck. Foucault insists that power is not the instrument of a class interest and that knowledge is not a veil laid over economic relations. Taylor is the least amenable witness imaginable: his stated problem is output and soldiering, his audience is ownership, and his techniques spread where they raised profits. Does the case simply refute that anti-economism, or does something subtler happen — does the apparatus that emerges (the science of the task, the norm, the standard, the specialist who measures) end up disciplining foremen, unions, state arsenals, and eventually whole professions as thoroughly as it disciplines the worker? Engage seriously with Foucault's other available resource here, namely that disciplinary mechanisms detach from the institutions that produced them and can be grafted onto any site.

Finally, treat the resistance as evidence. The 1911 walkout at the Watertown Arsenal and the congressional investigations that followed, ending in a ban on time study and premium pay in government shops, look like legal-sovereign power doing real work against discipline, not as an effect internal to it. Say what a Foucauldian account of that sequence can and cannot explain, and end with an explicit list of features of Taylorism the framework has no vocabulary for, plus your verdict on whether the episode confirms the theory, strains it, or is a case it was never built to handle.

Do not worry about whether Foucault mentions Taylor by name; the question is about the machinery, not the bibliography.
