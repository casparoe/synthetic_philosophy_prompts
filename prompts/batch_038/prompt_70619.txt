Write a dialogue in American English between Gregg Caruso and Daniel Dennett on punishment and just deserts. The hinge is a specific case: at a sentencing hearing the defendant submits a letter stating that he believes he deserves the maximum term, that mitigating evidence about his upbringing and his neurochemistry should be excluded because it excuses him in a way he finds demeaning, and that being told he was never a responsible agent would injure him more than the sentence would. The two are in effect being asked what the court ought to do with that letter.

Write exactly five exchanges — one speech each from Caruso and one from Dennett per exchange, each speech two to four sentences, with no narration, no stage directions, and no introduction or conclusion. They should argue over whether an offender's own claim to deserve punishment can be taken seriously without smuggling in basic desert, whether acting on the letter is retributivism by proxy, whether the state owes the offender the truth about his own responsibility even when he does not want it, and whether skepticism about desert can make sense of the injury he says he would suffer.

Both men are to be at full strength: each must answer the objection actually put to him rather than deliver a standing line, and Caruso in particular should press the worry that Dennett is licensing punishment by treating the offender's self-blame as authoritative. By the fifth exchange they should have located exactly where their disagreement stops being about sentencing policy and becomes a disagreement about what it takes to treat someone as an agent.
