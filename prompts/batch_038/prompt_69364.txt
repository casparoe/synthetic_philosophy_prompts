**Question 3.** Two cosmological theories are live options in the year 2140. They agree on everything anyone can ever measure: same particle physics, same stellar census, same readings on every instrument that will ever be built, including every instrument built after 2140. They differ in exactly one respect — how many conscious observers will exist over the entire future history of the universe.

- **T_sparse:** the total is about 10^11 observers, all biological, and the great majority of them already alive by 2140.
- **T_dense:** the total is about 10^15 observers. The extra ones come later, as short-lived simulated minds run in vast numbers on machines built around dying stars. Each simulation is a genuine conscious observer-moment. The machines are dismantled as each star dies, and nothing they do ever reaches the region where the biological observers live.

(In seminar we called this the presumptuous philosopher case.)

(a) State the Self-Sampling Assumption and the Self-Indication Assumption. Which of them, if either, shifts a 2140 physicist's credence between T_sparse and T_dense, and why?
(b) Some hold that the shift you identified in (a) is a reductio of whichever assumption produces it. Set out that objection and say whether you think it succeeds.
(c) Does your answer to (b) change if the extra observers in T_dense are simulated rather than biological? Say what would have to be true for it to change.

---

Both assumptions are ways of handling the fact that we are not just a disembodied collector of evidence but one particular observer among a great many.

The Self-Sampling Assumption says you should reason as if you were a random sample drawn from the set of observers in your reference class. In practice that means once a theory tells you how many observers there are and where you sit among them, you may treat your own position as a fair draw from that set, and update accordingly. The Self-Indication Assumption says that your own existence is itself evidence about population size, because a theory that posits more observers gives you more chances of existing at all. Roughly, SIA multiplies a theory's prior by the number of observers it predicts. So here the ratio is 10^15 to 10^11, which is a factor of ten thousand: if the two theories started out equally credible, SIA alone moves the physicist to something like ten thousand to one for T_dense, without her building a single detector. SIA is essentially just SSA applied with the widest possible reference class — every observer in the entire history of the universe — because if you make the sample big enough, the self-indication effect is what falls out of it.

That is why I think the presumption objection bites. If she can go to near-certainty in T_dense purely from the fact that she exists, then SIA is settling an empirical question from the armchair, which is exactly what makes the philosopher presumptuous rather than merely confident. Worse, SIA is also what drives the Doomsday Argument, since both are consequences of treating your own existence as evidence about how many observers there are. So SIA has two problems at once: it lets us over-populate the far future, and it lets us doom-monger about the present. And since no experiment could ever separate T_dense from T_sparse, science is stuck with the armchair result whichever way it goes. On balance the objection succeeds and we should stick with SSA.

There is one good reply open to the defender, though. T_dense and T_sparse count as empirically equivalent only if the extra observers are causally sealed off from us forever. But ten to the fifteenth simulated minds running around dying stars would leak: they would build things, radiate, rearrange matter, consume energy. If we have looked and found none of that, then SIA has been constrained by ordinary evidence and the boost to T_dense isn't armchair reasoning at all. I find that reply convincing, and it also settles part (c) for me: whether the extra observers are biological or simulated makes no difference in principle, only whether they are observable. Since they are stipulated to be unobservable, and since simulated minds are observers too, the counting comes out the same and nothing about my verdict changes.
