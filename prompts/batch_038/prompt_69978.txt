Ok, this might be a weird question but its been bugging me for about a year. I don't have any philosophy background — I run a small HVAC crew and I listen to a lot of history stuff in the van — but I keep hearing the phrase "hindsight is 20/20" get used in arguements, and I've noticed people use it to mean pretty much opposite things and nobody ever calls that out.

Two examples from my own life. One: the mill in my town shut down in stages over maybe six years, and now everybody says "you could see it coming, hindsight is 20/20," and it's used as a way of saying the people who ran things back then should of acted sooner and are to blame. Two: an online fight I got into about the 2008 crash, where a guy used the exact same phrase but meant the reverse — that nobody could possibly have seen it coming, so quit pretending you'd have done better. Same sentence, two totally different jobs being done with it, and in both cases it seemed to end the argument instead of continuing it. That's what I can't get past.

So what I want is a menu, not a dictionary definition. Break the phrase into the different precise claims somebody could actually be making when they say it. Is it a claim about what information existed at the time versus what we have now? Is it a claim about how your own head works once you already know the ending? Is it a claim about whether it's fair to judge people who didn't know? Is it saying that after-the-fact explanations are more accurate than predictions were, or less? For each version, tell me whether it's just a truism nobody would fight, whether it's a real substantive claim that could come out false, and whether it actually is false. Then say which reading supports each of my two examples — my suspicion is the mill one needs a claim that's false and the crash one needs a claim that's trivially true, and that's exactly why the phrase feels so unarguable going in both directions. Last thing: I've got my own half-baked idea that there's a third thing hiding in there about stories — that once you know how it ended you can't help assembling a chain of events that leads to it, and that chain feels like knowledge but isn't. Does that belong inside "hindsight is 20/20" or is it actually in tension with the phrase? Please don't just hand me a label and move on, I want to see where each version starts and stops and what would seperate them.
