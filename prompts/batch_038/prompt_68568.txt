Been going back and forth with a friend about wireheading and I keep losing, and I think its because I'm granting too much at the start. His setup: an agent gets reward from a signal that a frozen reward function computes off the agent's own sensory state. The agent can either go act on the world so the signal comes out high, or it can intervene on the pathway so the signal is high no matter what the world is doing. He says the second one is irrational because the agent has "traded the goal for a picture of the goal," and wireheading is just the cleanest case of that trade.

Argue that this is question-begging, and argue it all the way down rather than as a gotcha. My intuition, which I can't defend properly: from inside the agent's epistemic situation there is no such thing as "the goal" over and above the signal, because the signal is the only place the goal ever shows up to the agent at all. So the claim that tampering destroys the goal is borrowing a designer's-eye view that the agent cannot occupy, and the word "delusion" is where the borrowing happens.

But I don't want the argument to collapse into "so anything goes, the agent is just badly built, shrug." Take the strongest counter I know and meet it head-on: accurate world-models have instrumental value, so a rational agent preserves its own sensors most of the time, and tampering is only rational at the margins or when the agent is confident the signal is terminal rather than instrumental. Show why that doesn't rescue his intuition — or if it rescues part of it, say exactly how much of "wireheading is a failure" survives and which piece dies.

Start with the single sentence you're defending, then ~700-900 words of argument. No lit review, don't cite anything, don't restate my setup back at me.

Then after the argument give me a JSON array, one object per objection you actually dealt with, with exactly these fields: "objection" (one sentence), "strongest_version" (what it looks like once steelmanned), "response" (two sentences max), "verdict" (one of kills_the_claim / forces_a_concession / fails), "concession" (what the argument has to give up, or null), "how_much_survives" (integer 1-5, where 5 means the wireheading-is-a-failure intuition comes out fully intact).
