I teach a graduate seminar on consciousness each spring, and this term I built a handout that I now suspect is a booby trap for my students rather than for the target. The target is the reasoning Dennett deploys in his 1988 paper "Quining Qualia," and specifically the case of the two erstwhile devotees of the same mass-market coffee blend: one says the coffee tastes to him exactly as it always did but that his tastes have changed, the other says his tastes have not changed at all but that the coffee no longer tastes the way it did. The moral Dennett draws is that neither man is in a position to know which of his properties shifted, since the memory of the earlier experience is as much a candidate for revision as the experience itself — and from there he goes on to deny that there is anything answering to the traditional conception of a quale as intrinsic, ineffable, private, and directly apprehensible. My handout was meant to grant him the coffee case entirely and then show that the engine driving it runs away with itself. Here is the argument as I gave it to them, with the steps numbered:

(1) If the only evidence bearing on whether a subject instantiates a property is the subject's own sincere, attentive avowals, then the property is individuated by those avowals and by nothing else.
(2) Talk of phenomenal character — the taste-of-the-coffee, the redness-of-the-red — is talk of exactly that kind, since its whole point is to name what things are like from the inside, and no third-personal consideration fixes its application.
(3) Memory of an experience's phenomenal character is itself revisable: between t₁ and t₂ the trace of the earlier experience can drift.
(4) So a subject at t₂ who sincerely avows that his present experience of the coffee has the same phenomenal character as the one he remembers from t₁ may be right because the experience is unchanged, or because the experience changed and the memory changed to match, and no further evidence of the relevant kind distinguishes these.
(5) Where no available evidence distinguishes two descriptions, and the evidence of the relevant kind is exhausted by avowal, the two descriptions do not describe distinct states of affairs.
(6) Hence "the phenomenal character of E at t" and "the subject's disposition to avow sameness of phenomenal character with respect to E" are not distinct posits, and the former is eliminable in favour of the latter.
(7) Whatever follows for a term in virtue of (1) and (2) follows for any term whose application is fixed by first-person avowal practice.
(8) "Believes that," "intends to," "seems to remember that," and "intuits that" are terms of that kind.
(9) Hence beliefs, intentions, and apparent memories are likewise eliminable in favour of dispositions to avow: there are no beliefs, only avowal-dispositions.
(10) But (1)–(9) is put forward as an argument: I assert its premises, I intend it to persuade you, and you are invited to believe them.
(11) If there are no beliefs and no intentions, then nothing here is asserted, intended, or believed, and (1)–(9) is not an argument but a sequence of marks on paper.
(12) Therefore either (1)–(9) is self-undermining or it is unsound; and since steps of that shape are the only route from the coffee case to quining qualia, phenomenal character survives as a genuine, intrinsic, re-identifiable property.

Two things have gone wrong for me. First, one of my students — a careful one, and not hostile to the conclusion I wanted — says the reductio is a cheap performative trick: she holds that a theorist who accepts (6) is under no pressure whatever to accept (9), because belief-ascription is not fixed by avowal at all but by its success in making sense of behaviour, so (7) and (8) are simply false and (10)–(11) do no diagnostic work beyond that. Second, I have come to distrust (12) on my own account: even if the argument collapses, nothing I have said licenses the positive claim that phenomenal character is intrinsic and re-identifiable, and I suspect I have helped myself to that at the end. My instinct is that the break must come before (9), but I cannot tell whether what breaks is a hidden equivocation — a scope slide between the drift cases of (4) and the blanket elimination of (6), say, or a shift in "evidence of the relevant kind" — or a premise that is merely false. I want your judgment on the reasoning itself; treat (1)–(12) as freestanding rather than as exegesis, since faithfulness to the 1988 paper is not what is at issue. Please do not send me a bibliography or a reading list, and do not hedge into a survey of positions: I know the literature, and what I need is a diagnosis.

Structure your reply under the following headings, in this order: **Bottom line** (about 150 words: which step you would cut, whether the cut is an equivocation or a false premise, and what is left standing); **Where it breaks** (about 450 words: identify the load-bearing step and state precisely the reading on which it is true and the reading on which the argument needs it, showing that these are not the same reading — or, if you think the argument is valid, identify the premise that is least costly to abandon and say why it is not costless); **Why that step looked unobjectionable** (about 300 words: reconstruct its appeal in the strongest form available to someone who wrote it in good faith); **Repairs and their prices** (about 500 words: consider at least three — restricting (2) so that avowal-fixes-content applies only to phenomenal terms, restricting (6) so that elimination reaches only the drift cases, and denying (5) outright — and for each say what it costs, and in particular whether the repair leaves the coffee case with any force against the existence of qualia as opposed to mere force against first-person authority about them); **Performative contradiction or cheap trick?** (about 300 words: my student's objection, and whether (10)–(11) earn their place or are decoration); **What I should tell the seminar** (about 200 words: how I should revise the handout, and what the honest residue of the coffee case is once the runaway generalization is blocked).
