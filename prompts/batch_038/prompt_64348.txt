I want a thought experiment engineered to separate two positions that are usually bracketed together. Both Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett deny that dreams are experiences — Malcolm for conceptual reasons, since on his view nothing occurs during sleep and the only criterion for what a person dreamt is the impression he has upon waking; Dennett for empirical ones, since on his view the narrative may be composed and written into memory at the moment of waking rather than lived through the night. Build me a case in which the two give opposite answers to one plain question: did this person dream last night?

Specifications:

- Setting: a sleep laboratory, restricted to techniques that exist today. Polysomnography is fine; no dream decoding, no implants, and no pharmacology a real lab would not use. The variable to isolate is whether any of the content of the sleeper's night gets fixed in real time by the sleeper's own behavior, and that channel must be behavioral — speech, gesture, a trained signal — rather than technological.
- Structure: a matched pair of nights for the same subject, identical in sleep architecture and in what she reports at every awakening including the morning after, differing in that one respect only.
- Length: the scenario itself in a single paragraph.

Then: state each theorist's verdict on each night and the reasoning that gets him there from his own commitments rather than from my gloss; give, in one sentence, the verdict of someone who simply holds that dreams are experiences, and tell me if her answers fail to differ from either of theirs on at least one night, since that would mean the case is not pulling its weight; identify which feature of the case does the work and what it collapses into once that feature is removed; and give the best counterattack available to whoever comes out worse, along with whether that counterattack costs him anything he needs elsewhere.

What the case is for: deciding whether the Malcolm–Dennett alliance is a genuine alliance or two unrelated theses wearing the same hat. A case that asserts the verdicts instead of deriving them from each position's own machinery does not count.
