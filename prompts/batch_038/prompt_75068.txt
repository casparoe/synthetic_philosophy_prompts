I've been trying to think through a friend's defense of divine hiddenness, and I've ended up less settled than he is. Here is his argument as I've reconstructed it. (1) Love aims at being freely returned, and a return that is compelled is not a return at all. (2) Belief that someone exists and loves you is compelled whenever the evidence is overwhelming, since belief is not under voluntary control. (3) So a lover who wants to be freely loved must refrain from making the evidence of her existence and of her love overwhelming. (4) God is a lover who wants to be freely loved and could make that evidence overwhelming at will. (5) Therefore God's hiddenness is required by God's love, not merely permitted by it, and the existence of people who would believe if only they could see is no evidence against God. (6) The same reasoning applies to us: a spouse who makes her love unmistakable thereby spoils the thing she wanted, and a parent who never lets her child doubt her presence has given the child nothing worth having. (7) But that is false — candour between intimates is a virtue and its absence a failure of love, not a delicate strategy. (8) So (1) is false, and the defence of hiddenness turns into an indictment of love.

His rejoinder is that (6) is a bad analogy: finite lovers cannot make themselves overwhelmingly evident even when they try, and it is precisely the ability to do so that generates the requirement. I cannot tell whether that is a difference in kind or only a difference in the size of the gap. If I have to choose, I would rather keep (2) — belief does seem involuntary — and rather keep (7) than (1). Tell me which step you reject and why it looked innocent when we wrote it down; then say what the rest of the argument has to pay once that step is gone, including whether the rejoinder about omnipotence rescues anything, and whether (5) really follows even if some hiddenness is required. Please also tell me what would change your mind here: which consideration would push you to a different step, or where you suspect your own diagnosis is weakest. Answer in continuous prose, as you would write a letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
