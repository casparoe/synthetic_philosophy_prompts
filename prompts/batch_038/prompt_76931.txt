Write a dialogue between two philosophers of anthropic reasoning. One defends the self-sampling assumption (SSA: reason as though you were a random sample from the actually existing observers in your reference class, and conditionalize on which observer you turn out to be). The other defends the self-indication assumption (SIA: your own existence is itself evidence, favouring each hypothesis in proportion to the number of observers that hypothesis says have your total evidence). They have agreed to test their disagreement against two cases they designed together, chosen so that the disagreement reverses direction.

Case One. On priors of 1:1, either Solitary is true — the hundred crew of this station are the only observers who ever exist — or Crowded is true: those hundred exist, and a trillion others exist elsewhere in the galaxy, whom the crew will never contact or detect. You are one of the hundred. SSA, they agree, delivers near-certainty in Solitary; SIA delivers no shift at all, since a hundred observers with your evidence exist under either hypothesis.

Case Two. On priors of 1:1, either theory P is true — the universe contains roughly 10^12 observers, all leading lives subjectively much like yours — or theory Q is true: roughly 10^24 such observers. No telescope data have arrived. SSA leaves the odds at 1:1; SIA makes Q a trillion times likelier than P.

Each character should press the cost the other's rule incurs in the case where it looks worst — that SSA lets you conclude, before doing any astronomy, that the galaxy is empty; that SIA lets you bet your career on a vastly larger cosmos with no evidence whatever — and each should offer the strongest available repair: restricting or relativizing the reference class, moving from observers to observer-moments, invoking typicality conditions or freak-observer caveats, or accepting the verdict and arguing that it is not absurd after all. At some point one of them should attempt to dissolve the dispute by arguing that SIA is nothing but SSA applied to a maximally inclusive reference class, one containing merely possible as well as actual observers; the other should test that reduction seriously, and the two should settle whether it survives and, if not, which case breaks it.

Both should argue at full strength: no strawmanning, and every objection must be answered rather than merely noted. Close with a joint statement of what they still disagree about, and of what further case or argument would settle it.
