I write simulation software for multi-agent systems. My training is in engineering, not philosophy, but the epistemic side of game theory has become load-bearing for my work, so I have written down the assumptions I actually rely on and I would like them audited.

(1) I treat common knowledge semantically, as a fixed point: a state space, a possibility relation per agent, and common knowledge of an event as the greatest fixed point of "everyone knows." This is a condition on which state obtains, not a procedure anyone runs, and I deliberately do not model agents as checking or verifying anything.

(2) Rationality means best-responding to one's beliefs about the others, and the only restriction I place on those beliefs is that they assign positive probability to the others being rational. I take it that this is all common knowledge of rationality buys you, and that anything stronger requires an extra assumption.

(3) In the games I ship, though, I also prune weakly dominated strategies, so my predictions come from iterated admissibility rather than from (2) alone.

(4) I accept the standard theorem that in a finite game of perfect information, common knowledge of rationality forces the backward-induction outcome.

(5) For the centipede game my prediction is cooperation at the early nodes: an unexpected pass at the first node is evidence that the passer is not a payoff-maximizer, and given that, continuing is the best reply.

(6) As a design requirement, a prediction I implement should not depend on nodes that play never reaches; if a solution concept only says something about off-path behavior, I do not consider it worth the compute.

Go through these and tell me which of them can stand together. I want the genuine conflicts ranked by severity rather than listed flat, and for each one: what exactly has to give, and what is the cheapest revision that leaves the rest of the package intact. Where a pair only looks like a conflict, say so and explain why it evaporates — I expect at least one does. Close with the single change that would make the whole set coherent at the lowest cost. Assume I have no view yet about which commitment I would prefer to abandon.
