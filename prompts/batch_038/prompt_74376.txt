After a three-hour argument with a friend last night, I find that I need a clear-eyed map of the exposure of a position I was defending — and, frankly, of whether I was defending anything that could be wrong.

The position: Rortyan antirepresentationalism is best read not as a thesis about language or the world but as a proposal about vocabularies — the claim that the idiom of accurate representation, of beliefs and sentences hooking onto a mind-independent reality, does no explanatory work that cannot be done by an idiom of causal coping, social justification, and comparative usefulness in achieving our purposes. On that reading, the familiar self-undermining charge (that the denial of representation is itself a claim about how language relates to the world, and so either refutes itself or exempts itself by fiat) misfires, because nothing is being asserted about that relation at all: one can adopt the proposal while leaving every first-order empirical claim exactly where it was. Its only liabilities, I argued, are pragmatic — it may cost us expressive resources or make certain distinctions harder to draw. My friend's rejoinder was that this is precisely what it looks like when a view has been immunized past the point of emptiness, and that a recommendation which cannot fail is not worth recommending.

I would like you to adjudicate between us. What would count against the position as I have stated it? Sort the candidates by decisiveness rather than by familiarity, and for each say whether a committed antirepresentationalist of this stripe would concede it as a genuine cost or a genuine defeater, or would instead re-describe it as more of the deprecated vocabulary being waved about. Address at least the following, and label each fatal, damaging, or merely awkward:

- The view's dependence on Davidson's argument against the dualism of scheme and content, and on deflationary or disquotational accounts of truth. If either were overturned, or superseded by a theory that reinstates a substantive notion of word-world fit without a mirroring metaphor, what would survive?
- Whether the position makes predictions — about the fate of projects to naturalize mental content, about whether reference can be characterized without circularity, about whether particular philosophical problems dissolve rather than get solved — and whether success or failure in those projects would register as evidence for or against it. If it cannot fail, is that a defect in this kind of view or a feature of it?
- Whether there are phenomena the position cannot describe: the difference between correcting a mistake and merely changing the subject; the difference between discovering how things are and finding it more convenient to talk that way; the fact that some vocabularies let us do things others cannot in ways that do not look like mere expediency.
- Whether the quietist framing purchases immunity at the price of interest — that is, whether my defence of the position is actually a surrender of it.

Finally, tell me what the position risks that its representationalist rivals do not, and be willing to conclude that it risks nothing. My own worry, for what it is worth, is the second item on that list: whenever I try to say what would convince me I was wrong, I find that I am producing only further recommendations.
