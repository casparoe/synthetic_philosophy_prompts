here's my opening move. i'm on the consent/plebiscite side, and i've been calling my objection the deep-freeze problem.

stipulated case, invented, not a real place. Valmara has been a region inside a largely unitary state for 200 years. Its language used to be co-official; in the 1990s the national parliment demotes it to an auxiliary status, so it can still be used in local administration but not in the national curriculum or the courts. Valmara then holds a referendum that the constitutional court had already declared unlawful: 71% turnout, 66% for independence. The state refuses any legal route to a vote and entrenches a clause declaring the unity of the state permanent and incapable of amendment, and the court later rules that even 95% support could not authorize one. Then thirty years pass with no persecution, no mass rights violations, no discriminatory redistribution — if anything Valmara is a bit richer than the national average and subsidises other regions — full voting representation in the national parliment, and the language demotion is never reversed though nobody is ever arrested for speaking it. Three more unofficial votes over those decades, each with a bigger majority.

so: under your theory no remedy is owed, because nothing grave enough has happened. But the majority preference, however large and however durable, never becomes actionable — it goes in the freezer and stays there forever. Which means your theory permits a state to entrench permanent rule over a population that has said, repeatedly and unambiguously, that it does not consent to being ruled. Is that the view you actually want? And if it is, how is requiring a grave injustice first anything other than a nicer-sounding way of letting existing borders decide who counts as a people — the status quo doing the normative work while you dress it up? And if it isn't the view you want, tell me what inside your own theory gets Valmara out of the freezer without handing me the primary right you're refusing to concede.
