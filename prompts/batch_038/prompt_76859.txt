I'm facilitating a reading group on Bayesian epistemology, and one of the members has developed an objection to Jeffrey conditionalization that I can neither dismiss nor answer. I would like your assessment of it, and I should warn you that I suspect his argument contains a false step I cannot locate.

His case is this. A field ornithologist hears a song and must choose between two species. Her sensory evidence is represented by a three-celled partition — a call of type A, a call of type B, and a call too degraded to classify — to which she assigns 1/3 each in advance, with the conditional probability of each species given each cell fixed by a well-validated song guide. Then she actually hears the bird: mostly like A, but not unmistakably so. She revises the partition to 0.6 on A, 0.3 on B, 0.1 on degraded, and the Jeffrey update carries that revision through to her beliefs about the species.

Here is the objection. The rule tells her how to propagate the change once the new partition values are in hand, but nothing in it tells her to put 0.6 on A rather than 0.55, or 0.9. Those figures are free parameters. So the rule is empty: it is compatible with any assignment whatsoever, and the much-advertised capacity to learn from uncertain evidence has been bought by abandoning the idea that experience constrains belief. Ordinary conditionalization, he says, does not suffer this defect, because becoming certain of a proposition is itself a constraint, whereas Jeffrey's rule licenses moving the evidential probabilities anywhere at all. He anticipates the reply that the new values must simply be the ones the experience produces, and rejects it on the grounds that this relocates the arbitrariness into an unexplained causal process — and that causation is not a norm. His proposed repair is to require that the input minimize the relative information between the old and the new credal state, which would at least deliver a determinate answer, though he concedes he cannot yet say what quantity is being minimized relative to what.

Three questions. First, is his diagnosis correct that Jeffrey conditionalization, uniquely among Bayesian rules, fails to constrain belief, or has he misdescribed what the rule is in the business of doing? Second, does ordinary conditionalization really enjoy the advantage he claims over it here? Third, does his minimum-relative-information patch work, and if it were adopted, would the result still be recognizable as the update rule he set out to repair?
