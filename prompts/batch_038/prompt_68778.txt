ok so this is for a zine I'm putting together for my history class and I need help with one of the pieces. my teacher has said the word "collapse" like fourty times this semester — bronze age collapse, maya collapse, easter island collapse — and every time I ask what exactly died she says "well, the civilization" and then moves on to the next slide. so I want to make fun of that, but in a way that's actually smart about it, not just "collapse is a made up word lol."

here's what I want: coroner's paperwork. An autopsy report plus a filled-out death certificate, for a society. The deceased is a bronze-age-ish island-and-mainland trading society that you invent — give it a name, some big monuments, a palace system that ran the whole economy, a script that like twenty scribes could read, whatever you want. Don't use a real one so nobody can fact-check me, but make it obviously rhyme with the real cases so the joke lands for people who know them.

the joke I'm going for: the medical examiner's diagnostic tools are all built for bodies, so they keep giving insane answers when pointed at a society, AND the more evidence she collects the less dead the patient looks. but she has to stay completely serious and professional the whole time. no winking, no joke asides. and by the end a reader should be genuinely unsure whether something died, because there IS stuff that clearly stopped existing (the palace administration, the long-distance trade, the writing, the building projects) and stuff that clearly didn't (the actual people, who are apparently still there farming and making worse pottery).

things I want in there roughly:

- an external examination section that goes through the standard "cause of death: self-inflicted environmental damage, patient had been warned" theory and shows where it runs into trouble
- the problem that time of death was estimated from the last monument with a date carved on it, which turns out to be a fact about how records survive and not a fact about how people live
- somebody arguing the patient didn't die OF complexity, they just stopped paying for it, and that stopping was the metabolically healthy choice — the medical examiner should find this argument professionally upsetting
- a checkbox death certificate form where none of the boxes work, so she writes in the margins
- a dissenting opinion from the second examiner who has to be just as good at her job, not a crank. she thinks the first examiner is wrong about what the organism even is

and here's the one bit that's actually mine so please build it in: I keep thinking the only real test of whether a society died is whether the people in it experienced themselves as dead — like if you could ask a farmer in year whatever "did your civilization collapse" and he'd go "no, the tax collectors stopped coming, this is great." the report should run into this criterion and be totally unable to do anything with it, because you obviously can't interview the deceased. have her note it as a limitation of the examination in dry official language. that's my favorite part.

around 800-900 words, numbered sections, bureaucratic voice throughout. don't name any real archaeologists or historians and don't put in any real dates — I don't want to recieve a grade penalty for being wrong about a century. the humor should come from getting the archaeology right, not from the paperwork being silly for its own sake. if someone who actually does this for a living reads it, they should laugh and then be slightly annoyed at how accurate it is.
