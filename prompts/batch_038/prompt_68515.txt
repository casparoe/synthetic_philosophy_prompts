I do state capacity work for a living — growth diagnostics, technical assistance to ministries, the principal-agent mess in donor conditionality. Ten years of it, no philosophy training. I've been reading Mill on India: thirty-five years inside the East India Company ending as its chief examiner, his fight against handing the Company's powers to the Crown in 1858, the chapter in his book on representative government about how a civilised state may rightly govern a dependency that isn't ready to govern itself, and that carve-out at the front of On Liberty where he says the liberty principle simply doesn't apply to societies that haven't reached the point where free discussion can work.

Translate that position into my vocabulary, claim by claim, as faithfully as you can — the split London supervision of the Company as an agency problem, his case for a permanent experienced staff with local knowledge against both a captured indigenous elite and a distant legislature, and the improvement-of-the-governed justification for ruling people who never consented. Flag every spot where you catch yourself making him sound like a modern technocrat just to keep the translation moving.

Then the residue report: what maps onto things I already believe about state capacity, what you had to bend out of shape to fit, and what has no expression at all in a framework with no concept of a society's rung on a ladder and no concept of forming character as an end rather than a means. Dont give me the usual verdict on whether Mill was self-serving or inconsistent — I want to know whether the parts that refuse to translate point at something my toolkit genuinely can't represent, or whether his argument only looked like an argument until you tried to say it in ordinary terms.
