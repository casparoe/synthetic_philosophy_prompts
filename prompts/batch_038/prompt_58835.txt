I retired four years ago after thirty-eight years as a population ecologist, and I have taken up political philosophy with the same bad habit I had in the field: I record observations before I attempt a model. Here are my recorded verdicts on whom a wealthy state may exclude, and I should like to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

A healthy thirty-year-old who will work, pay in, and draw nothing: may not be excluded. A person who will predictably require care far costlier than anything they could ever pay in: may still not be excluded, though I confess the arithmetic troubles me. A person carrying a drug-resistant pathogen: may be held out until they are no longer infectious, and not one day longer. One applicant whose admission costs the contributory scheme a million: may not be excluded. Ten thousand such applicants whose combined admission would exhaust the scheme: may be turned away, or at least the intake may be capped. I cannot make these last two consistent with one another, and I suspect one of them is wrong.

Requiring a language and civics test before granting permanent residence: acceptable. Requiring the same before admission at the border: not acceptable. Granting a newcomer emergency treatment while withholding unemployment insurance they have paid into for five years: not acceptable. Withholding insurance they have not yet paid into: acceptable. Excluding a person because their arrival is projected to inflame a backlash that would make the scheme less generous for those already inside: not acceptable — and yet this is the argument I find hardest to answer, because in my own discipline systems with precisely this feedback structure are common, and they are almost always mismanaged by admitting arrivals one at a time.

My tentative principle is that a state may exclude only where the measure is temporary, publicly accounted for, and its cost falls on the state rather than on the excluded person. Please test it against the cases above; state it precisely enough that I could tell when it has been violated; say which of my verdicts it cannot save, and which I should therefore abandon; and say what it implies for two cases I have not considered — the child born inside the territory to parents who were never admitted, and the applicant excluded not for cost but because their stated political programme is to dismantle the scheme itself.
