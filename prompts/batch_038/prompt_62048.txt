Got into a three-hour argument with my friend Dana last night about when it's legitimate for groups with different values to cut deals, and I still cant tell what she'd say about the thing actually on my plate Thursday. Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it: she loved it when two member orgs in our coalition stopped competing for the same donors and split a joint grant in proportion to what each said the money would buy them. She backed a group that refused to co-sign a statement calling a military intervention "sometimes justified," even though signing would have bought them a seat at the table. She was totally fine with us dropping our soda-tax push in exchange for a restriction on marketing junk food to kids. She was fine with two members splitting the difference at 200 housing units when one wanted zero near the wetland and one wanted 400, and she specifically said it mattered that both sides were counting in the same units. She was fine with a member taking money from a company it campaigns against in return for the company reformulating. But she was harsh about a side deal two members cooked up privately and then presented to the rest of us as a done thing, even though she agreed the content was fine.

New case. There's a bill that would fund a program our coalition has wanted for a decade — big, quantifiable, in the same welfare units we already use in our own analyses. The price of passage is a rider letting the state do something a chunk of our members think is flatly a rights violation, and to get the funding we'd have to take a seat on the board that signs off on each instance. Our modeling says that with us on the board there'd be meaningfully fewer instances than if the bill passed without us, and the rider passes either way. Dana will be in the room when we vote.

Predict her verdict. More than that: which rule is she actually running? I can see at least three that fit everything above — something about mutual gains, something about doing versus allowing, something about whether the two sides' values can be measured against each other at all — and I think they come apart on this one. Say which she'd have to be running to say yes, which to say no, and give me one hypothetical I could put to her at lunch that would settle it. Also: does her side-deal objection predict anything here, or is that a seperate axis?
