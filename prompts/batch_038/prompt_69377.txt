For my quals I have to hand in a one-page statement of where I actually stand on modality before the oral, so the committee has something concrete to attack. Draft is below. I'd like a proper audit of it before I commit to it in writing — no references or lit review needed, just the reasoning.

The package:

1. Modal realism, full strength. Possible worlds are concrete things of exactly the same ontological kind as this one. "Possibly p" just means p is true at some world. A world is a maximal bunch of things all interrelated spatio-temporally, with nothing outside it related to anything inside it that way — so there's no such thing as a world made of two chunks that bear no spatiotemporal relations to each other, and I don't think anything like that could obtain.

2. Recombination is my plenitude principle: if you can build something by cutting and pasting duplicates of bits of worlds, in any arrangement whatsoever — including arrangements where the pasted bits end up bearing no spatiotemporal relation to one another — then it's possible. It's also how I think we come to know most non-trivial modal claims.

3. Counterpart theory, no transworld identity, and no haecceitism: two worlds can't differ solely over which individual plays which qualitative role.

4. A big part of why I think the view is worth the incredulous stare is that the modal facts are objective, settled by how the concrete worlds are, and not up to us or our interests. I want that to cover de re facts too — whether so-and-so might have won that election is just determinately true or false.

5. "Actual" is indexical like "here" and "now". Nothing is metaphysically privileged about our world.

6. Ethics: I'm a total-view consequentialist and I don't discount for distance in space or time. I also think the inhabitants of other worlds count exactly like my worldmates, since they're just as real as we are — being causally cut off from me doesn't look like the kind of fact that could matter morally.

7. Worlds are causally closed: nothing anyone does here makes any difference anywhere else. And by 2 there are worlds with infinite spacetimes and infinitely many people in them, as many happy or miserable ones as you like.

8. Still, I hold that consequentialism has to be action-guiding: it ranks the acts open to me, and some of them are flat out wrong.

Two things people have pushed on that I think are actually fine, but flag me if I'm wrong: (a) 5 looks like it contradicts the idea that there's a fact about which world is the actual one; (b) 2 looks like its stated modally, which would make the whole reductive analysis of possibility circular.

So — which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and for each one say what the cheapest revision is and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the list. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most damage. I don't want a tour of modal realism, and don't tell me which of my views I should be least attached to — I dont know, that's the question.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate the question back at me.
