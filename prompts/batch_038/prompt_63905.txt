ok so I'm prepping a masters seminar on blame and I've realised I don't actually know what I'd accept as a *successful* theory of standing to blame. Everyone writing on it seems to be arguing past everyone else — one camp says the hypocrite's blame is perfectly apt because the norm applies to them too, another says the hypocrite has forfeited the right to demand anything, a third says standing isn't a real condition at all and it's all just a redescription of other objections. So before I assign anything, I want the spec: write me the desiderata. What any account of standing to blame has to deliver — the cases and data it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results it has to stay consistent with — with the case or consideration behind each item, so I can see which requirements are doing genuine work and which are just one camp's preferences smuggled in as neutral.

Cases I want on the list (please add more of your own where you think something's missing):

- the textbook hypocrite — serial philanderer loudly denouncing a colleague's affair
- the differential blamer, who objects to the very same wrong at wildly different strengths depending on who did it
- the sheltered saint: someone who has never been tempted, never wronged anyone in the relevant way, spotless record, blaming someone who did fall. On any "standing depends on your own record" story this person has maximal standing, and yet their blame feels thin — cheap, uninformed, like it's coming from someone who doesn't know what they're asking for. I want the spec to make room for that datum without turning standing into a competition in having sinned enough.
- the inverse of that: the ex-addict or the ex-offender whose identical words carry a weight nobody else's do, and what that costs a record-based view
- blame delivered entirely in private, to no one at all
- blaming the dead, or someone who can never be told
- a friend blaming on the victim's behalf when the victim doesn't want it, and whether standing can be borrowed or delegated
- self-blame: does whatever story works for other-blame say anything coherent here, or does self-blame just get exempted
- and something on the legal side, where standing is a threshold question about who may bring a claim at all — say whether the moral notion is actually answerable to that analogy or whether importing it is a category mistake

Then mark, for each item, whether it's non-negotiable or an optional extra a theory can defensibly refuse — e.g. whether it's fair to demand that a theory explain the difference between blame being *true* and blame being *fitting*, or whether that's just the deflationists begging the question. And tell me which single requirement knocks out the most otherwise-plausible accounts.

The bit I most want: if some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, say so plainly and show where the squeeze is. My suspicion is that wanting the hypocrite's blame to be both apt and unfitting, while also wanting standing to be a substantive condition on blaming rather than a repackaging of impermissible-manner-of-blame, may not be simultaneously doable — but I want the argument, not my hunch. If the list turns out to be a no-go result, that's fine, that's the finding.

Finish with a short scorecard, no more than a paragraph all told: three or four families of view, described by what they say standing consists in (record-based, commitment- or consistency-based, demand- or address-based, and the deflationists) set against the list. British English throughout please, and dont pad it with a general history of blame — I want the criteria and their motivating cases to carry the weight.
