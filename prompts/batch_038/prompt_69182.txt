I clerk for the constitutional court of a small European state and I am drafting the bench memo for a case on next term's docket. Our court's establishment decisions over the past forty years:

1. It upheld a scheme under which the state collects a membership levy on behalf of recognized religious bodies by payroll deduction and remits it, less an administration fee, on the ground that the state's role was purely ministerial and membership rested on self-declaration.
2. It struck down a nativity scene erected each December in the courthouse rotunda, while upholding a cross-shaped monument on the harbour, put up in 1902 to the memory of sailors lost in a storm.
3. It upheld state salaries for teachers of religion in public schools where the syllabus is set by the religious body and withdrawal is costless, but struck down a state-written "ethics for all" syllabus that only credentialed teachers of one denomination were permitted to deliver.
4. It required the prison service to give an inmate access to a chaplain of his own faith wherever the state already pays for chaplains of the majority faith, but declined to require it to hire a chaplain for a faith with three adherents in the system.
5. It struck down a property-tax exemption as applied to a church's bookshop and hotel, while leaving the exemption intact for premises used for worship, instruction, and charity.

The new case: a registered association of ethical humanists — no deity, no worship, no clergy, no buildings — seeks admission to the payroll-deduction levy scheme on the terms granted to the recognized bodies. It also observes that two of those bodies use levy revenue to place counsellors in public hospitals under contract with the regional health authority, a contract from which the humanists are excluded.

What disposition best coheres with our own line of decisions? Please give me: the doctrines these five verdicts are jointly consistent with (there is more than one, and they are not merely differently worded); what each of them yields on the humanists' application, and on each of the two limbs of it separately; which single precedent is the closest analog and why; your best guess at the actual holding, with the reasons for preferring one doctrine over the rest; whether an equality-based reading would commit the court to extending the benefit rather than withdrawing it from everyone, and what turns on that choice; and one further hypothetical case we could put to the court that would discriminate between the candidate doctrines if its reasoning here proves opaque.
