Two people in a policy reading group I sit in on got into a fight over a case one of them invented, and it never resolved. The case: a small city has one budget surplus and two options — a pain clinic that frees 200 people from years of chronic pain, or a free transit year that gives 90,000 people a mildly nicer commute.

(1) Dev said there's no such thing as choosing between those on welfare grounds, because you can't compare one person's relief against another's convenience; any number you write down linking the two is a hunch in costume. (2) Nora said that's a cop-out dressed up as humility: the money gets spent either way, so refusing to rank the options just means they get ranked by default, and he's made the comparison he says can't be made. (3) Dev said no — declining to produce a number isn't the same as producing one, and he'd rather be visibly silent than confidently wrong. (4) Nora said his silence isn't visible at all, because there's no "neither" column; the surplus goes somewhere, and the somewhere gets picked by whoever's slogan wins the room. (5) Dev then sharpened it: within a single person you can rank the clinic above a bus ride, but each person's numbers are only fixed up to their own zero and their own unit, so "she lost twice what he gained" can be flipped by rescaling her side — it isn't a claim about anything. (6) Nora said that's exactly the missing assumption, and everybody supplies it anyway the second they start talking about helping the worse off, so supply it openly and argue over which rescaling is right instead of pretending the question doesn't arise. (7) Dev's last move: fine, but then the naturally cheerful outweigh the miserable, since the same clinic lands as a bigger gain for someone with more capacity for relief, and no amount of rescaling fixes that without smuggling in a view about who's owed what.

I couldn't referee it and I still can't. Can you go through (1)–(7), say who's right at each step, flag any move that just restates an earlier one in new clothes, and say what both of them are treating as settled that shouldnt be?
