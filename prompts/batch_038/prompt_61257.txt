ok so im a junior and my AP Physics teacher has this end-of-unit thing where you have to explain one concept using a genre that has nothing to do with physics — last year someone did a breakup letter from a photon to an electron, it was pretty good. I picked Maxwell's demon, mostly because I think its cool, but like half the class picked it too so mine has to not be another retelling of the little guy at the door. So instead of the story I want to do a fake product review, and I want the humor to be the specific kind where the physics is actually correct and that's what makes it funny.

The setup: a company sells a home appliance that is basically Maxwell's demon rebranded as an energy-saving smart gadget — call it a sorting vent for your furnace, something that separates the fast air molecules from the slow ones so your house heats itself and the gas bill drops. Write it as an online marketplace review, so star rating, a headline, a "Verified Purchase" tag, and a body in the voice of a normal person who bought it to save money and is not a physicist. At first they're delighted, the bill really does go down, and then the complaints start piling up — and every single complaint has to be a real thermodynamic objection dressed up as a customer gripe. The unit runs weirdly warm for something that's supposedly saving energy; the sorting gate is advertised as frictionless and zero-power but only under conditions buried in the manual; there's some internal memory that fills up and has to get wiped every night and the wipe cycle is what's quietly eating their electric bill; "up to 100% efficiency" turns out to mean something extremely specific in the footnote. Then add two more pieces: an "UPDATE, four months later" where the reviewer has begrudgingly done the arithmetic with a calculator and a spec sheet and gone from furious to almost impressed but still wants a refund, and the company's official public response to the review, in that flat PR voice where they are technically right about every single point and completely infuriating, signed by an invented title like some kind of compliance person.

Rules I care about: the resolution should never get stated as a lesson. No "and so we see the second law survives after all," no little paragraph explaining that information is physical — the reader should work out where the bill comes due purely from what the customer complains about and what's hiding in the fine print. Also the fine print needs to give an actual per-bit energy figure for wiping the memory at room temperature and it better be the right number, because my teacher will absolutely check it. Please don't name any real physicists, papers, or experiments — invent the brand, the model number, the company and the compliance person, since name-dropping is what every other group does and it just reads like a wikipedia page. The target of the joke is the marketing style and the whole "information beats thermodynamics" hype, not the demon itself. It should make a physics nerd laugh out loud but still be followable for my friends who've never taken physics. About 650 words total. And dont tack on sources or a suggested reading list at the end, that's against the rules of the assignment.
