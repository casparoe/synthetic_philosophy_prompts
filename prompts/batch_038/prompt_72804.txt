For twelve years I earned my living writing and maintaining decision procedures — a resolution engine, a SAT back-end, a library of proof tactics for a commercial theorem prover — and it never occurred to me that the work raised a philosophical question about me. Two years ago I left the company to begin a doctorate in philosophy, and my first conference paper is now scheduled to open the second day of a workshop on the foundations of machine reasoning, co-hosted by a mathematics department's logic group and an industry AI lab. The paper argues that the Lucas–Penrose argument against mechanism fails, though not for the reasons usually given, and that its failure leaves behind something more interesting than either side of that debate has admitted.

My thesis has three parts. First, the diagonal step in the argument — the move from the claim that a sound machine cannot establish its own Gödel sentence to the claim that I can nonetheless see that sentence to be true — requires that I be in a position to know the soundness of the particular system I am being identified with; but that premise is exactly what a mechanist about human reasoning must deny, so the argument in its standard form begs the question against its target. Second, once the premise is weakened to something a mechanist can accept, namely that no system can establish its own soundness from the inside, the result becomes symmetric: it constrains the human and the machine alike, and the asymmetry the argument depends on evaporates. Third, what therefore survives is not anti-mechanism but a thesis about the unknowability of our own soundness, and Penrose's appeal to objective reduction of quantum states in neuronal microtubules does not repair the gap that thesis leaves. It substitutes a speculative physics for the argument; and even if that physics were true, it would not deliver the epistemic advantage the Gödelian move was supposed to secure.

The composition of the room matters, because the same thesis draws very different fire from different corners of it. There will be three or four working proof theorists, one of whom studies proof-theoretic strength and ordinal analysis and who will not let me slide between consistency, omega-consistency, and soundness, or accept talk of a machine's axioms when the systems actually in view have no axiom set anyone can write down. There will be a philosopher of mathematics who is a mathematical realist and who thinks the argument's real target was never artificial intelligence but the objectivity of mathematical truth; he will want to know why I dismiss the residue as trivial when it may be doing his work for him. Roughly half the audience will be machine-learning engineers from the host lab, who regard the whole controversy as being about Turing machines and therefore about nothing they build, and who will press me on whether any of my claims predicts a failure mode they could measure. There will be one cognitive scientist working on bounded rationality whose opening move will be that human mathematicians are demonstrably neither consistent nor sound nor even reliably correct, so the premise I am at such pains to weaken is simply false. The session chair, a philosopher of mind, will close by asking what is left.

Please prepare my question-and-answer sheet. I want ten to twelve objections this particular audience will actually raise, ordered by how much damage each would do to the paper if I handled it badly rather than by how familiar it is in the literature. For each objection: which corner of the room it comes from, phrased the way that person would phrase it and in their own vocabulary; a reply I could deliver standing up in about ninety seconds; and a blunt note on where the reply is soft — what I would have to concede, idealize away, or merely assert. Flag the two or three that are genuinely damaging rather than merely irritating, and tell me what I should concede in the opening minutes to take the sting out of them. For each of my three claims, indicate whether the objection actually targets that claim or a stronger thesis I am likely to be heard as advancing. Finally, give me the one question I will be hoping nobody asks, and explain why it is the one this paper cannot answer.
