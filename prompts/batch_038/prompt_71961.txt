weird request but: i TA a second-year metaphysics seminar and next week is the problem of universals proper — one over many, transcendent forms vs immanent universals vs tropes vs resemblence classes. three weeks in and everyones eyes glaze over at "what makes these two red things both red," so i want to open the session with a poem where the form does the arguing instead of the poem just listing positions.

here's the conceit: terza rima, six tercets and then a single closing line, 19 lines total. aba bcb cdc and so on. the interlocking middle rhyme is the whole point — i want a reader who's paying attention to notice that the one unresolved sound in each tercet becomes a binding constraint on the next one, so that every attempt to stop the regress by positing one more item just hands the following stanza a new requirement. the third man argument and bradley's regress should be the engine, not labels stuck on the outside: stage at least three candidate accounts (something over and above the many, something wholly present in each of the many, tropes bundled by compresence, classes of resembling particulars) and let the rhyme show in each case that the stopping point isn't one.

constraints. no proper names and no "and so the platonist says" — the reader should identify each account from what it does, and someone who's never taken metaphysics should still get a good poem about red things out of it. keep the rhyme honest: half-rhymes are fine but the b and c links have to actually link, otherwise the conceit collapses and we're just reading an essay in tercets. and i'd love it if a metaphysician could point at the exact two or three lines where the coextensiveness problem bites for the class-of-resembling-particulars move (creatures with hearts/creatures with kidneys, or triangular/trilateral — your call which, i don't have a preference).

last thing, and honestly the one i care most about: that final line has to be genuinely ambiguous between the chain finally closing — something immanent, nothing left over, no further level to appeal to — and the chain just being cut off because somebody stopped writing. thats where the seminar actually got stuck last thursday and i don't want the poem to resolve it for us.
