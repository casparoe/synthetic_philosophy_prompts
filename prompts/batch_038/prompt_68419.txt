I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm reporting on Stoicism's commercial revival: the apps, the podcasters, the professional athletes who credit the dichotomy of control for keeping them level. The practitioners I've interviewed treat that dichotomy as a filter on effort — sort every feature of a situation into what is up to you and what is not, then invest wholly in the first column. The classicists I've interviewed call this a distortion, on the grounds that the ancient position treats outcomes as preferred indifferents, to be selected vigorously but under a reserve clause, so that nothing one pursues is pursued as a good.

For the piece I want a designed case I can put to both camps. Construct a single scenario in which a contemporary control-filter Stoic and a classical Stoic give the same person materially different advice about what to do, not merely about how to feel — and in which, reversing the usual direction of complaint, it is the classical reading that recommends the less strenuous course, accepting a loss the popular reading would keep fighting. Constraints: a realistic modern setting, one paragraph, no philosophers among the characters, and the divergence must be generated by the two doctrines rather than stipulated in how the scenario is described. Then show your work: state each reading's recommendation and the reasoning behind it, identify which feature of the case does the work, say what happens to the verdicts if that feature is removed, and give the best reply available to a defender of the popular reading.
