I'm a backend engineer, no econ or philosophy training, so please tell me where I'm getting this wrong rather than just going along with me.

Situation: my org is 16 people (11 engineers, 2 PMs, a designer, 2 in support) and we're actually going to decide next month whether we move to a four-day week. Leadership says they'll go with whatever we land on. I've been reading around Robin Hanson's futarchy thing — the setup where you let people vote to settle what counts as a good outcome, but then you let prediction markets pick which policy gets you there, voting on values and betting on beliefs. What appeals to me is that it takes the decision away from whoever talks the most in the meeting.

So I want to run a 75 minute session at our offsite where we "decide" the four-day week with a market instead of a vote, as a dry run. But I don't want a smooth demo. I want the team to run into the wall themselves and only then hear what the wall is called.

Here's the wall I think is there, tell me if I'm mangling it. To price "four-day week improves output" you need a price for output conditional on us adopting it and a price conditional on us not adopting it. But whether we adopt it is decided by comparing those two prices. So the market is forecasting a future that only exists if the forecast says so, and anyone in the room who already knows which way they intend to push can trade on that — at that point its not a forecast anymore, it's an intention with a payout attached. Plus nothing can actually settle inside the room, so somebody has to just declare the outcome, and that somebody is making exactly the judgement call the market was supposed to replace.

Weird related thing: we ran a real internal market once, on whether a migration would finish by a date. Four people traded, all on the same team, price sat at 0.9 for a week and then everyone stopped looking at it. So I already know thin + correlated traders is a live problem for us.

What I need from you:

1. The mechanics. What the two contracts literally say on the card, play money or points, and how the price moves. A spreadsheet order book is fine but if there's a simpler round-based thing that works for people who've never traded anything, do that instead.
2. Payoffs that make them care with zero real money.
3. The resolution rule — what we settle against and how we fix that rule in advance so it isn't rigged. This is the part I'm most stuck on.
4. Whether the step where we choose the metric should itself be a vote, and what to do about the fact that everyone who expects to lose under metric A will fight about metrics instead of trading. My worry is all the politics just moves one level up and hides there.
5. A debrief that maps what actually happened onto the mechanism and names the objection they just lived through, plus the honest bit about what this exercise flat out cannot reproduce and I'll have to explain by hand.
6. What will predictably break the first time I run it, given my specific room: 16 people who all read the same Slack, one extremely confident staff engineer, nobody who's ever held a position in anything.

Also: should the session be binding? If the market says yes we'd kind of have to do it. I can't tell if that makes the exercise work or turns it into a hostage situation.

No lecture up front, and they shouldn't hear the word futarchy until the debrief.
