I sit on a university animal-welfare committee that must decide, species by species, which animals in our care receive anaesthesia for invasive procedures, enriched housing, and a full harm-benefit review. Half the committee are comparative neuroscientists and veterinarians; the other half are philosophers who suspect the exercise is dressed-up speculation. A neighbouring jurisdiction has just written decapod crustaceans and cephalopods into its animal-sentience statute, so the invertebrate cases are not academic for us. Before our next meeting I want to build a sorting instrument that shows members where they actually disagree.

Please write a decision tree that takes a member from the shared starting commitment — behaviour and neuroanatomy are the only evidence we will ever get — to a named position on the distribution of consciousness across animals. Requirements:

- Nine or ten branch nodes. Each is a concrete case, not a claim about theories. Build at least five from these: an octopus whose arm ganglia solve local problems while the animal is asleep; a fruit fly whose central complex supports route navigation and whose behaviour changes after an injury we would call painful in a human; a small reef fish that passes mark tests and then directs scraping at the mark; a crab that reliably abandons a shelter where it was shocked, with a nervous system lacking any vertebrate-like pallium; a child born without a cerebral cortex who nonetheless tracks faces and shows distress and comfort; and the cerebellum, which holds most of a human brain's neurons while its surgical removal leaves reported experience largely intact.
- No node may name a theory, appeal to introspection, or ask whether some animal is conscious.
- Leaves must include, at minimum: a view on which consciousness tracks the integration of information; a view on which it tracks broadcast to a global workspace; a view requiring higher-order representation of one's own states; a deflationary view on which phenomenal consciousness is not what the concept promises; a panpsychist view; and two non-metaphysical endpoints — the behaviour-only pragmatist and the agnostic who treats the question as unresolvable but the welfare question as live.
- Where a combination of answers is available but unoccupied, mark it and say in one line whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
- Flag every pair of leaves your tree cannot actually separate, and name the evidence that would separate them.

The instrument should make members discover that they disagree about which case is decisive, not about which label they prefer. Every question must fit on one slide and be answerable in twenty seconds by someone who has never read the literature.
