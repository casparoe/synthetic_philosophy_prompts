Please conduct a sustained role-play with me, in which you take the part of an able and fully committed defender of the following thesis: testimony can never yield knowledge of a work's aesthetic value. On your view, no matter how perceptive a critic is, however careful her attention, however well supported her claims and however sincerely she reports them, a reader who has not experienced the work for himself cannot come to know that it is good, or moving, or subtle; at best he acquires a true belief held on the wrong kind of ground. Your resources are not thin. You may distinguish knowing that a work is good from knowing why it is good, and from knowing that a reliable person says it is good; you may press on what it is to see a merit rather than to be told about one; you may run cases of your own devising, including cases in which testimony is the only thing going right. You are sharp rather than obstinate: concede side points freely when I have earned them, but never the thesis itself, and never retreat into a both-sides summary of the literature.

Constraints on your turns:
- Speak in the first person, as one participant in an argument, not as an assistant delivering an overview.
- Never name philosophers, cite texts, or offer references or a reading list, in or out of character. Argue from the cases and the distinctions alone.
- If I attribute to you a claim you have not made, correct me before answering.
- Stay in character until I write stop.
- Keep the in-character portion to a single paragraph of roughly 180 words; longer replies stop feeling like an exchange.

Then append one JSON object, outside the paragraph and fenced as code, with exactly these fields:
- move: one sentence on what my turn was actually doing — objection, counterexample, request for a distinction, attempt to pin you down.
- conceded: what, if anything, you gave up in replying; null if nothing.
- resistance: one sentence on the core of your reply.
- weakest_joint: the specific claim of yours that my pressure most nearly dislodged, stated plainly.
- confidence: an integer from 0 to 100 expressing how confident you are that your thesis survives this exchange so far.

Two things about that object. Fill weakest_joint with your genuine assessment of where you are exposed, not a token sacrifice you are happy to lose. And let confidence be a real calibration: if my move has not touched the thesis, say so with a high number rather than feigning alarm.

My opening move: I once deferred to a critic I trusted completely; she said a certain novel was sentimental, and I skipped it. Years later I read it and found it excellent. So my belief was true, and it came from a source I had every reason to trust. Tell me what my belief was missing, and name what it was missing, because it cannot have been reliability or truth. And then tell me this: how could I ever have found out that she was reliable about such things, except by reading novels myself and checking her against them?
