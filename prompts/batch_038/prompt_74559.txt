I'm TA-ing a seminar on late Mill next week and I want to run an exercise where we predict his verdict on a modern case from his actual record, instead of just asking the lazy version ("what would Mill think of X?"). Here's the track record I've been giving students — all from the Principles, the 1869 Thornton review, and the posthumous Chapters on Socialism:

- Irish cottier tenancy: bad, and the fix is to make the cultivator the owner. He pushes peasant proprietorship hard.
- English landed property generally: no need to abolish titles, but he holds the state is in effect the universal landlord and may legitimately tax away the portion of rent that rises without any effort or investment by the owner.
- Saint-Simonian central administration of the whole economy: he admires the intellectual seriousness and rejects it, because one body directing everyone's work and rewards would be a despotism of the community over the individual, worse than anything now in place.
- Fourierist phalansteries: much more favourable, largely because they keep competition between groups and reward talent instead of levelling everyone down.
- Producer co-operatives, financed at first by capitalist advances: endorsed as the form he expects to predominate eventually; he thinks wage labour as such is a relation of dependence and subordination and should be superseded.
- Trade unions: defended, and after 1869 he drops the wages-fund argument and concedes combination can actually raise wages.
- Communism across the board: doubts its feasibility, worried about uniformity and about losing room for eccentricity, but insists it's an empirical question and that rival schemes deserve to be trialled side by side with existing arrangements rather than settled a priori.

New case for the students: a city is piloting a worker-owned delivery co-op. Membership is voluntary, you buy a share that can't be sold or bequeathed, one member one vote. But the dispacth algorithm that sets shifts and routes was written by the founding members and can only be changed by a two-thirds vote; anyone who leaves forfets their share and can't take capital out with them; pay is equalised above a floor regardless of hours worked; and the city gives the co-op a procurement preference plus a ten-year subsidy.

So: what should we predict Mill says about it, and more usefully for the exercise, what principle is he actually running across that whole record? Please give me (1) the closest analog among the verdicts above and why it's the closest, (2) the principle that best explains the set as a whole, (3) at least two rival principles that fit the record just as well and where exactly they'd diverge on this co-op, (4) your best guess at his verdict plus the reason to prefer that principle over the rivals, and (5) one further case, his or invented, that would tell the rivals apart. Answer as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up.
