Background so you know where I'm coming from: I did condensed matter theory for about forty years, mostly renormalization group work, and I retired four years ago. Since then I've been reading philosophy on the side, more seriously than I expected to, and I've gotten stuck on the infinite ethics stuff — worlds with infinitely many people in them, or people who live forever, and what a utilitarian is supposed to do when the sums diverge.

Here's a thought I can't shake, and I want your credence on it, not a survey of the field.

The thought is that maybe the right axilogy for infinite worlds is basically the physicist's move: put in a cutoff, compute, compare, and then ask whether the answer survives a change of scheme. Concretely, take welfare levels along an infinite sequence of people and define the difference between two worlds as the difference of their welfare streams. Three toy cases I've been playing with:

(1) World A: everyone at welfare 1. World B: everyone at 1 except the first billion, who are at 2. Any sane cutoff procdure says B beats A by exactly a billion, and it stays put as the cutoff is removed. Comparison robust, so B is better.

(2) World C: 1, 0, 1, 0, ... World D: 0, 1, 0, 1, ... Smooth both with Cesàro-type averaging and they look identical at 1/2 each; truncate at N and the sign of the difference flips with the parity of N; permute which person sits at which position and you can get other answers again. Comparison is scheme-dependent, so there's no fact about which is better.

(3) Any pair where some people are better off in one world and others better off in the other, and the differences form a conditionally convergent series — so the rearrangement business applies and a relabeling of the same people can make the difference come out to whatever you like. Again, no fact.

So here's the claim P I want a number for: the correct account of value for infinite worlds is one on which incomparability is real rather than a stand-in for our ignorance, and the pairs of worlds that ARE comparable are exactly those whose regularized difference is invariant under any reasonable way of removing the cutoff or reindexing the people. Scheme-independence is what makes a value comparison meaningful; everything else is genuinely unranked.

Give me your credence that P is right, and give me the decomposition. A number plus a line of reasoning for each of these at least, and add any other terms you think belong:

- that incomparability is acceptable in an axiology at all — i.e. that a theory which refuses to rank some pairs isn't thereby failing to be a theory of value;
- that scheme-independence is the right criterion for meaningfulness here, as opposed to just picking a scheme (some invariant mean or ultrafilter-flavored device) and living with a complete ordering;
- that the physics analogy actually carries weight rather than being decorative. The disanalogy that worries me: in renormalization, experiment eventually tells you which scheme-independent content is the real prediction; in ethics nothing like that seems available, so the analogy might license the mathematics but not the methodology;
- that the resulting set of comparable pairs is big enough to matter — if P collapses into "almost nothing is comparable" I'd count it as false in spirit even if it's technically coherent.

Then tell me how those combine into your headline number (are you treating them as independent, or conditional on each other, and why), which single term the total is most sensitive to, and what would move it. I especially want you to say where you think your own reasoning here is most likely to break — which of those four you're fudging, and what argument or example would flip your verdict. If you invoke a theorem to do any of the work, state it in your own words and say how confident you are that it's really a theorem and not something you're half-remembering. Don't pad it with references, I want the reasoning. And don't hide behind "it depends which axioms you adopt" — I know it does. My own guess is about 0.55, and my wife thinks I'm just importing my day job, so tell me if she's right.
