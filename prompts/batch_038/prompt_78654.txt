I am in the second term of a joint MA in economic and global history, and this week's seminar — co-taught, as it happens, by an economist and a historian of late imperial China, who agree about almost nothing else — was supposed to be about the methodological status of contrastive explanation: whether a question of the form 'why did sustained, machine-based industrialization begin in Britain rather than in the Yangzi Delta?' is well posed at all, given that the two relata must be made comparable before anything can count as an explanation of the difference between them, and given that the question seems to change its content depending on whether the contrast class is taken to be China, all of Eurasia, or every other region of the world. A classmate of mine closed the discussion down with a proverb. He said that 'necessity is the mother of invention', and that once this is granted the methodological worry evaporates: British wages were high and British coal was cheap, so British producers faced a standing necessity to substitute machinery and fuel for labor, and that is precisely what they did; labor in eighteenth-century China was plentiful and therefore cheap, so no comparable necessity arose, and nothing comparable was invented. In his telling this is a causal mechanism rather than a narrative, and mechanisms of that kind do not oblige us to settle anything about the well-posedness of contrastive questions.

What I want from you is a disambiguation of that single line, of a kind I can bring back to the seminar. Please lay out, as a numbered menu, the distinct precise claims that 'necessity is the mother of invention' could be being used to assert. I would like the menu to run the full range from the near-vacuous to the strong, and to attend at least to the following dimensions, plus any others you judge load-bearing: whose necessity is in question (a producer facing a cost structure, a consumer with an unmet want, a polity under existential pressure); what the 'necessity' consists in (a relative factor price, an absolute cost, a physical scarcity, a need that must first be recognized as a need by someone in a position to act on it); what the relation to invention is said to be (does necessity cause invention, merely raise its probability, fix only its direction while something else entirely supplies its rate, or simply make an already-conceived device worth adopting); whether the claim is offered as a ceteris paribus tendency or as an exceptionless law; and what temporal and epistemic conditions it silently presupposes — for instance that a feasible technique is available to be thought of at all, and that whoever would have to think of it is also whoever stands to gain from it. For each reading, state in a sentence or two whether it is a platitude nobody denies, a substantive and defensible thesis, or straightforwardly false, and give your reason for that classification.

Then tell me which reading my classmate's argument actually requires and whether any reading on the menu supports it. Be committed rather than even-handed: state plainly whether the argument succeeds or fails, and if it fails, identify the exact step at which it fails — including whether the defect lies in the proverb itself or in the inference from factor prices to the invention of particular machines. Address his methodological point head-on as well: if the proverb is read as a general law-like tendency, does that entitle him to treat the contrastive question as unproblematic, or does the invocation of the law itself presuppose the comparability of the two cases that the seminar was disputing? I already know the surrounding literature by name — Allen's high-wage-economy account of the British case, Pomeranz's coal-and-colonies account of why the comparison with China is closer than it first appears, Elvin's high-level equilibrium trap, and Hicks's induced-innovation hypothesis about relative factor prices steering the direction of technical change — so please do not summarize any of it and do not supply citations or a reading list; I want the conceptual analysis. Keep the whole answer under 700 words.
