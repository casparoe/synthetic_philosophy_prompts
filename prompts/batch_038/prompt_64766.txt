So I keep running into this claim that if the simulation argument holds up, longtermists should chill out about the far future — like, all that talk of astronomical value spread over trillions of future lives loses its grip once you think we might be running inside somebody's ancestor simulation. I want an essay that actually adjudicates this instead of shrugging at it.

Start by spelling out the trilemma the argument rests on: the three disjuncts about how few human-level civilizations reach a posthuman stage, how little interest posthuman civilizations take in running ancestor sims, and how large the fraction of observers like us who are simulated would have to be. Then — and this is where I want you to do some real work — sort the "it dampens fanaticism" idea into the distinct routes it could take, because I think people mush these together:

- the termination route: if we're a sim, our continued existence depends on the simulators staying interested, and the histories worth simulating are the dramatic ones, so being simulated would raise the chance of an early cutoff and shrink the far future we can actually reach;
- the discounting route: simulated welfare counts for less, or a simulated far future is somehow "less real," so the astronomical numbers get multiplied way down;
- the selection route: sims are over-represented among worlds where things turn out a particular way, so our evidence about the long run isn't representative of the long run.

Say which of those, if any, actually gets off the ground, and don't let the second one pass just because it feels intuitive.

Then take seriously the argument going the other direction: whoever runs ancestor simulations plausibly descends from a civilization that made it through the great filters, so finding ourselves simulated would be evidence that the far future does arrive, and that it's full of value-generating descendants. That looks like it pushes credence the opposite way. Which effect dominates — the shrinkage or the boost? Is it a wash? Does it depend on how the sims are selected for, and if so, what selection story is most plausible?

Handle the branch where we're almost certainly not simulated, too. Does the whole question wash out because the three disjuncts look roughly equiprobable and the expected-value math gets dominated by the non-sim branch anyway? Or does the trilemma itself, even unresolved, do something to a longtermist's numbers?

Come down somewhere. I don't want "it depends on your priors" as the landing spot — give me a verdict, something like whether the simulation argument on net dampens far-future fanaticism, inflames it, or is just beside the point for it, and then build the strongest objection you can to your own verdict and say why it doesn't land (or where it does). No reading lists or citations, I just want the reasoning out in the open.
