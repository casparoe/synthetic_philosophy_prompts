Here is the situation. A chapter in a general-audience book on the physics of information, assigned for a course I am auditing, contains this passage:

"Maxwell's demon is the thought experiment in which a tiny being sits at a trapdoor between two chambers of gas, opens the door for fast molecules moving one way and slow molecules moving the other, and thereby creates a temperature difference out of a gas that was uniformly warm — extracting work from nothing and violating the second law of thermodynamics. For a century the paradox went unanswered. The resolution eventually came from information theory: Brillouin showed in the 1950s that the demon has to acquire information about the molecules in order to operate the door, and that acquiring information always carries an energy cost, so the demon pays the second law back in heat. Bennett later refined this result: measurement need not cost anything after all, but the demon's memory is finite, so sooner or later it must erase old records to make room for new ones, and erasing a single bit costs at least kT ln 2 of energy, which is dumped into the environment as heat. That cost exactly cancels the demon's gains. Information is therefore physical, the second law is safe, and modern experiments on single bits have measured the Landauer bound directly. The upshot is that entropy and information are literally the same quantity, and the demon has become a design principle: no computer or nanomachine can ever be made more efficient than the Landauer limit allows."
