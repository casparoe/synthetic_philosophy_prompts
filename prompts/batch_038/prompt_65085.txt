A visiting metaphysician is giving the closing talk at our graduate workshop, and I have been asked to lead the forty-minute discussion afterward. Her position, which she labels ontological abstinence, runs as follows: where a purported entity is such that no observation, measurement, or causal transaction could bear on whether it exists, the appropriate response is neither belief nor disbelief but a third, sui generis attitude of withholding. She insists this is not agnosticism — agnosticism treats the question as determinate but unresolved, whereas she holds that a traceless posit fails to raise a genuine question at all. She is an accomplished speaker who has fielded the standard objections many times, so I want a discussion that gets past the first reply she is already prepared to give.

Draft a sequence of ten to twelve questions, ordered so that the pressure mounts rather than merely accumulating, with a follow-up attached to each and keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. The first question should pin down the positive content of the third attitude — what one is doing, normatively, when one abstains — since everything else depends on it; the last should ask what would force her to give the view up.

Between those, the sequence must cover at least the following, each with its follow-up:

- Whether the claim that no possible trace could bear on a given posit is a claim about the world or about our capacities, and how a negative modal claim of that reach is supposed to be established without surveying possibilities we have no independent grip on. Her predictable move is to treat accessibility as fixed by the actual laws together with some robustness across nearby counterfactual situations; press on what that move costs her.

- The status of the past, of regions beyond the cosmological horizon, and of other people's experience. She will distinguish direct access from access by record or trace; force her to say whether "trace" is a threshold notion or a matter of degree, and if a matter of degree, why her third attitude is not simply a graded confidence scale under a new name.

- Whether the criterion is agent-relative or agent-neutral: information permanently closed to me but open to others, or to a differently equipped inquirer. If relative, she appears to make what exists vary with the inquirer; if neutral, she must specify whose capacities set the standard.

- Her relation to constructive empiricism, which likewise withholds belief in unobservables while accepting the theory. Ask what remains once the two are distinguished, and demand a concrete case in which the abstainer and the constructive empirist would conduct inquiry, or license assertion, differently.

- The holistic objection: if evidential bearing is distributed across a whole theoretical package, no single posit is traceless in isolation, and the criterion never gains a purchase. If she rejects holism at this point, ask for the atomistic account of evidential bearing her criterion then requires.

- The reflexive objection. The view quantifies over possibilities, counterfactual dependence, and evidential relations, and it needs the abstract apparatus of mathematics even to be stated — mathematics being the standing case of a domain that is, on Benacerraf's way of putting the difficulty, causally inert and yet apparently knowable. Does she abstain from numbers? Whichever she answers, there is a cost; make her choose one and pay it.

- The exemption problem that follows: she will likely say the criterion governs first-order claims about what exists and does not apply to the logical and semantic machinery in which those claims are framed. Ask what licenses a two-level exemption, and why the same license could not be claimed by the metaphysical posits she means to rule out.

Do not include anything of the "what drew you to this question" variety, and do not ask her to summarize or defend the view as a whole. Frame each item so that it can be read aloud as spoken, and so that an evasive answer is visibly evasive. No citations, references, or suggested readings anywhere in the output: the questions have to carry their content themselves.
