I'm on my lab's oversight review board — we rule on whether a proposed oversight setup counts as "adequate" before a model gets used on a task. Our track record so far:

- Accepted: staff mathematician reads a model's competition-geometry proof.
- Accepted: proof in a field nobody here knows, checked line by line by a weaker model, plus a 5% random audit by an outside expert.
- Accepted: two model copies debate a judge who's never seen the source, on a word problem whose answer sits in one buried line of a 40-page report.
- Accepted: "is this drug regimen dangerous for this patient" broken into a chain of small questions each answerable by a ward nurse.
- Rejected: thumbs up/down on paper summaries, where staff couldn't tell accurate from fluent-and-wrong.
- Rejected: debate in front of a judge who'd read a popular but incorrect account of the event and was sure of it — the right evidence came out, the judge didn't budge.
- Rejected: "is this code safe" decomposed into checkable subquestions, where every step passed and the whole was still unsafe.

Next month's case: a model proposes reweighting our feedback signal and writes a 25-step argument that it wont hurt honesty. Every step is checkable by our data scientist, who is exactly the right expert. Two snags. She wrote a memo last year insisting this class of reweighting can't cause the failure the argument warns about, and she's held that line through two rounds of pushback. And the conclusion is about aggregate behaviour over millions of examples, which nobody can check step by step even though the steps are fine individually.

Predict our verdict — lead with one line, accept / reject / accept-with-conditions — then tell me which rules fit the precedents equally well and disagree here, which single precedent is the closest analog, and what we'd be committing ourselves to whichever way we go. Under 350 words.
