I am organizing a two-day workshop on precommitment, individual and constitutional, and I would like to commission the document that its participants should ideally have read before arriving: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the two camps the workshop will put in the same room.

The first camp holds that a commitment can have genuine normative force for the agent who later faces the choice. Such an agent — a person who has resolved, or a polity that has entrenched — may stick to the plan even when defection looks better by the lights of the moment of action, and the reason is not merely that defection has been made costlier or impossible. Binding oneself changes what one has reason to do, not only what one can do. The second camp denies this. On its view rationality is local: at each node of a sequential problem the agent does what is then best, and everything a prior commitment accomplishes it accomplishes by altering the feasible set or the payoffs — by lashing oneself to the mast, installing penalties, delegating to a judge, or raising the threshold for reversal. A constitution, on this reading, is an obstacle course that a later majority may clear if it wishes hard enough, never a standing reason that survives the change in circumstances which motivated it.

I have a further suspicion that I want the document to confront head-on rather than bury in a footnote. The same structural argument seems to me to favor the first camp in the individual case and the second camp in the constitutional case, and if that is right, one of the two literatures is borrowing intuitions it has not earned. Individually, predictably reversing oneself looks like a defect: an agent who does so can be pumped, and the usual rejoinder is that pumping is objectionable only if the pumped party is one continuing chooser. Constitutionally, the persistence of the earlier decision looks like the defect instead: the thought that the dead hand of a past majority should not fix the terms of present politics is taken to carry weight on its own. The collaboration should determine whether this asymmetry is principled — because the identity of a continuing agent is not the identity of a continuing demos, because a self-binder can always release himself while a bound populace cannot, because legitimacy is indexed to the living — or whether it is an artifact of how each literature slices up its agents and its times.

Please deliver the following, in this order. First, a numbered section of claims both sides can sign: claims about the formal structure of sequential choice (backward induction, diachronic consistency, what a pump argument does and does not show, the difference between changing an option set and changing an option's normative status); claims about what entrenchment devices mechanically do (amendment thresholds, judicial review, delegation to independent bodies); and claims about which phenomena a theory of precommitment owes an account of. Second, a disagreements table, each row giving the dispute in a formulation its own side would accept, with a separate column for each side's preferred wording, and an explicit verdict on whether the dispute is substantive or verbal — where verbal, naming the word doing the work, since I expect candidates such as "bound," "the same agent," and "consent." Third, the cruxes: at least four sub-questions such that settling one would actually move a side, each with an account of what would settle it (an argument, a formal result, a case, comparative institutional evidence) and each side's prediction written down in advance, before the evidence is canvassed. The mast case must appear as a shared case, together with an account of where it misleads; I would also like the harder material drawn from psychiatric advance directives, contractual self-exclusion schemes, entrenched constitutional provisions, and central bank independence.

Finally, be candid about failure. If some item cannot be signed by both parties without one of them equivocating, say so and diagnose what blocks the agreement. Do not include a bibliography or a literature review by citation; refer to positions and arguments by description and I will supply the references myself.
