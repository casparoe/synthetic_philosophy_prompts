Novelist here, mid-revision, and the problem I need help with is a piece of dialogue, not plot. Two characters argue at a town hall: a retired poll worker and a guy handing out lealets for a tiny third party. Each keeps insisting the other's votes are "wasted," and my one beta reader who actually knows electoral systems says the scene reads as mush because neither of them means the same thing by it and neither one notices.

Before I rewrite, give me the menu of precise claims that phrase could be carrying, from the near-tautological to the contentious. I want at least: the reading where a vote is wasted because it couldn't have changed who won; the reading where it's worse than wasted because it changed who won in the direction the voter didn't want (the spoiler thing); the technical version the gerrymandering people use, where every vote for a loser is wasted plus everything the winner got past the half-plus-one they needed; and whatever reading makes "wasted" a claim about what a vote is *for* rather than about what it caused. For each, tell me whether it's a truism, a live empirical claim, or a normative claim wearing a descriptive costume — and which voting rules make it come out true. My town elects one council member by plurality, but I'm flirting with having them switch to ranked ballots, or to a list system with a five percent threshold, and I need to know how the argument shifts under each.

Second thing, and this one's mine so you won't find it anywhere: my canvasser has a speech where he says a vote is a receipt, that the counting is the whole point, so a system where a third of the town votes for something and gets nothing back isn't wasting their votes, it's refusing to hand over the receipt — and the honest word for that is silencing, not wasting. Is receipt-versus-silencing a genuinely seperate reading, or does it collapse back into the expressive one? And where exactly is the seam that lets him slide from "your vote is wasted" to "you shouldn't cast it" — I want to know if I can have him make that move on purpose and the poll worker still miss it.
