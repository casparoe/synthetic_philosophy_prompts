We run a Nomic game over Discord — eight of us, three years going. You're the philosopher-of-law type I'd hire as outside counsel if I could afford one: assume you've spent an unreasonable amount of time on self-reference, on legal systems that amend themselves, and on the difference between a rule and a rule about rules. Think it through properly and do some brainstorming on a scratchpad first — list the candidate principles her record supports before you commit to one — but what you hand back should be an argument, not a survey.

Our ruleset has two tiers in the usual Nomic way: mutable rules change by simple majority of players present, immutable ones can be transmuted into mutable ones only by unanimous vote, and transmutation has to be stated explicitly in the proposal rather than implied. On top of that, three rules matter for what follows.

Rule A — Proposals are the only route by which the rules change. A proposal must be written out in full before the vote, and if it passes it takes effect the moment the Chair announces the tally.
Rule B — Every rule in this game is open to repeal or amendment, including this one, by the procedure in Rule A. (Rule B was mutable until last spring; see ruling 5.)
Rule C — The Chair rules on whether a proposal is admissable to a vote. Her ruling stands unless two-thirds of the players overturn it.

We have never written down anything about retroactivity, or about whether the game has to stay amendable, or about what happens if the ruleset empties out entirely. That's the hole tonight's proposal falls into.

Her track record over the past year:

1. Proposal 41: repeal the rule requiring a quorum of five. Admitted, passed 5–3. She ruled that the quorum rule still governed the vote that killed it, so the repeal applied only from the announcement onward.
2. Proposal 44: "No proposal may be made on a Tuesday." Admitted, passed, still on the books.
3. Proposal 46: struck down as out of order — it tried to amend a rule that had already been repealed. Then Proposal 47, which read "the Chair's ruling on Proposal 46 is reversed and 46 is to be treated as having passed," was also struck down, on the grounds that a vote can't rewrite what has already happened.
4. Proposal 49: add a new immutable rule reading "No proposal that would repeal Rule B is admissable." She admitted it to a vote. It failed 3–5.
5. Proposal 52: amend Rule B to add the clause "Rule B may be repealed only by unanimous vote." Admitted, passed 7–1, the dissenter arguing she had no business admitting it at all. Her stated reason: tightening a rule is a different thing from removing it.
6. Proposal 55: "All rules are hereby repealed and this game is concluded." Admitted — she said policing what players want to vote for is not the Chair's job — and failed 2–6.

Tonight's is Proposal 58: "Rule B is repealed. In its place: no proposal that adds, repeals or amends any rule is admissible unless every player votes in favor of it. This rule may not itself be amended except by a proposal that every player votes in favor of." It's cleanly drafted, submitted in full before the vote, and takes effect on the announcement of the tally. Five of us would vote yes, three are firmly no, nobody abstains.

Two wrinkles I want you to actually engage with rather than wave at. First, 58 uses Rule B's majority machinery to install a unanimity requirement — it amends the amendment power with the amendment power it's in the business of narrowing. Second, since ruling 5, Rule B can only be repealed unanimously, and 58 would only ever get 5 of 8; so there's a real question whether she should strike it as inadmissible up front or admit it and let it die at the tally, and I suspect which of those she picks tells you more about her principle than the outcome does.

What I want from you:

- Your prediction, single answer with reasons: does she admit 58 to a vote or strike it down, and if she admits it, does she treat the post-ruling-5 unanimity clause as something she enforces at the admissibility stage or something she just lets the ballot resolve?
- The candidate principles her six rulings could be read as implementing. Write them out as principles, not vibes, and say which of them survive the whole track record and which only most of it. I especially want to know whether ruling 6 — admitting "repeal everything, the game is over" — is compatible with the principle you land on, or whether you think she was simply inconsistent there and which ruling you'd throw out to make the set cohere.
- If we're the ones deciding and we want to be consistent with her record: what should we rule? Which of the six is the closest analog to 58, and where does that analogy start to strain?
- Either ruling commits us to something, so spell out what. In particular: does admitting 58 amount to conceding that a game can vote away its own capacity to change, and does striking it amount to declaring some level of the game inviolate — and is that last claim a claim about our rules or a claim being made from outside them, since nothing in Rule A, B or C says the amendment power has to survive?
- One further proposal we could float next month that would discriminate best between your top two candidate principles, with the ruling you'd predict on it under each.

If you think the record genuinely underdetermines the answer, fine, but still tell me which way you'd bet and how much of that bet rests on her wording in ruling 5, the tightening-isn't-removing line. Don't end on a both-sides shrug — pick, and defend it.
