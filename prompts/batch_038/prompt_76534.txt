Two years of a game theory reading group and we still can't settle which bargaining solution to use for splitting the surplus in our two-firm merger model. Nobody disputes the math: we all accept that the Nash solution is the unique one satisfying Pareto, symmetry, scale invariance and IIA, that Kalai–Smorodinsky is the unique one satisfying the first three plus monotonicity, and so that IIA and monotonicity can't both hold. The fight is over which axiom deserves to win. One camp: IIA, since a rule that moves when you bolt on options nobody was ever going to take can be manipulated with dummy alternatives. Other camp: monotonicity, since IIA lets the unchosen menu do normative work, and since equal proportional gains is the right way to handle what happens when one party's aspiration level shifts — if the feasible set grows so that only my best case improves, I shouldn't come out ahead of it.

My hypothesis, and I want it attacked rather than nodded at: the two camps are doing different jobs, IIA is a constraint on prediction (where parties actually land when the menu changes) and monotonicity is a constraint on justification, so neither side is wrong and no theorem can settle it.

Complication. Our resident deflationist says the axioms only ever bite when the Pareto frontier is curved, so its a corner case nobody should lose sleep over. I half remember the two solutions agreeing on straight-line frontiers, but I'm not sure the disagreement point drops out as irrelevant there. Check that first, then tell me whether my deflationary diagnosis survives it — and if it doesn't, what would actually count as progress.
