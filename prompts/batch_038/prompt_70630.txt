So I run a small vision-and-consciousness lab and I've got a result that I think is going to get me eaten alive at a workshop next month, and I want a sanity check from you before I write it up.

Paradigm is a Sperling-style partial report with a twist. Six items in a ring, each is a grating patch with a color (red/green, roughly equiluminant) and an orientation (tilted left or right by 15°). 60 ms display, pattern mask, then a cue at SOA = 0, 150, 300, 600, or 900 ms pointing at one location. Two kinds of block, interleaved across days:

- Block A (discrimination): forced choice on the cued item — "tilted left or right?" on some trials, "red or green?" on others, randomly mixed.
- Block B (experience map): no discrimination at all. After the cue they click whichever location in the ring they feel they had *seen clearly*, and give a 1–4 clarity rating for it. On catch trials we ask them to base the click specifically on color, and on other catch trials specifically on tilt.

What we get:

1. In Block A, orientation d′ at the cued location holds around 1.35 all the way out to 900 ms SOA. Color d′ falls off a cliff after 300 ms, down to ~0.2 at 900 ms.
2. In Block B it's the exact mirror. Late-SOA clicks land on the right-colored item above chance out to 900 ms (about 28% vs. 17% chance — small, but reliable across observers), and clarity ratings track color. With respect to tilt, the clicks sit at chance at every SOA.
3. Add a concurrent tone-counting load — the usual ignition-killer — and the orientation discrimination doesn't budge. Color was already dead at long SOA so there's nothing to see there.
4. Remove the mask and the whole thing collapses: both features report fine and the Block B clicks start tracking both.

So: same array, same SOA, same observers — access to orientation with no reported experience of orientation, and reported experience of color with no access to color. Yes, I know 1 and 2 look like they can't both be true; the click advantage is weak and the discrimination floor is 2AFC, so they're not strictly contradictory, but the crossing is real and it replicates.

Here's what I want from you. Rank these six diagnoses by how much you'd bet on each being the right story, given only what I've written above — don't ask me for more data, just assume the numbers are as stated:

1. Introspective confabulation — the "I saw it clearly" color reports are made up, and the real phenomenology tracks orientation.
2. Local / partial access — orientation is being handled by non-ignited local processing that supports forced choice but isn't conscious, and the color report is honest.
3. Straight-up Block — this is a clean double dissociation of access and phenomenology, the overflow case, distinction vindicated.
4. Task artifact — Block A and Block B measure different things (a discriminability judgment vs. a clarity judgment), so this is a criterion / demand-characteristics difference, not a consciousness difference.
5. Boring two-channel story — a coarse fast color-summary channel and a slow orientation channel; nothing here is about consciousness at all.
6. Higher-order thought — consciousness needs a higher-order representation, color gets one, orientation doesn't, so the theory should be pleased rather than alarmed.

Format: bottom line first — one sentence saying which you'd bet on and why. Then a markdown table with exactly these columns:

- `diagnosis` — number and short name
- `bet_pct` — integer, how many of 100 points you'd put on it; the column has to sum to 100
- `load_bearing` — the single number or detail from my description that this diagnosis most depends on me being right about
- `kill_shot` — the cheapest follow-up experiment that would most decisively kill it
- `embarrassment` — 1 to 5, how bad it is for the field if this one turns out to be the truth

Then after the table, one paragraph: tell me which of my four findings a hostile audience should attack first, and what I should have run to protect it. Don't be polite about it. No references or citations, I just want your reasoning — and if you think the truth is a seventh thing none of these capture, say "none of these" and put your points on that instead.
