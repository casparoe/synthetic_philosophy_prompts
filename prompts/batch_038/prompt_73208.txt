So my roommate and I went at it for like two hours last night about democratic backsliding and I'm still buzzing. He's a 1L and his whole position is that the term is a rhetorical weapon rather than a description — his argument being that every move people point at (packing a court, redrawing districts, tightening media regulation, tightening who can vote, purging a civil service after a coup attempt) has been done by governments nobody calls authoritarian, so the label is mostly tracking which side you're on. My counter was that he was scoring each move in isolation, and that the actual content is in the trajectory plus the question of who's left to call it once the referees themselves get replaced. Neither of us moved an inch. I TA a 22-person political theory section, 75 minutes, next Thursday, and instead of summarizing this argument at them I want to make them run into it.

Design an in-class game that forces the disagreement to happen. My constraints: 22 students, 75 mins, no real money (but I do want a scoring scheme with at least two currencies that pull against each other — something like grip on power vs. domestic/international legitimacy, with a win condition that makes trading one for the other genuinely tempting). Keep the countries fictional so it doesn't dissolve into a shouting match about the last election, but the cards should be recognizable. Before any play, the class or a subset of it should write a short rubric for what counts as erosion without having seen the cards.
