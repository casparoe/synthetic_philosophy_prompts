Halfway through drafting a seminar paper on threshold deontology — the view that past some level of catastrophe it becomes permissible to do what would otherwise be a grave wrong, with interrogational torture as the standing test case — I hit a wall. I can pick apart particular versions all day but I have no idea what an adequate one would have to *do*. So write me the spec.

I want the requirements any such theory has to meet, each with the case or consideration behind it, and each marked as either something no theory can dodge or something only one camp insists on. My own half-list, which I expect is both incomplete and partly wrongheaded: what the threshold is a threshold of (lives saved? expected lives? and what if the probability is tiny and the catastrophe huge); whether the permission its handing out is a justification or an excuse; what happens when the agent manufactured the very emergency he's now appealing to; whether one massive harm and ten million trivial ones that add up to it sit on the same side of the line; whether the theory has to be usable by someone who doesn't yet know the facts, or can be an after the fact assessment; and whether it owes an explanation of why there's a threshold at all rather than a consequentialist slope or an absolutist wall. Throw any of those out if they aren't real requirements, and add the ones I've missed.

One wrinkle: I want the spec to work for a body that has to publish its rule in advance, not just for an individual agent in the moment, and I suspect those two framings pull against each other in a way that matters.
