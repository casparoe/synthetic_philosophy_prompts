I'm in a metaontology reading group and two of the regulars, Ines and Karl, spent the entire post-seminar dinner on a case borrowed from the limnology department. I honestly couldnt tell who was winning, so maybe you can referee.

The case: two lake scientists have argued for years over whether a lake recovered after the cleanup. They agree on every measurement — phosphorus back to preindustrial levels, the same fish assemblage back in the same proportions — but the sediment seed bank is dead and the lake now drains into a canal it was never connected to. One says it recovered, the other says it's a new lake.

(1) Ines: run the standard test. Ban the word 'recovered', make them state everything in neutral vocabulary. They do, they agree on every resulting sentence, and they still disagree. A dispute that leaves a residual disagreement behind after elimination isn't merely verbal — it's about whether being that lake survived.

(2) Karl: but look at what's left. Both concede no further fact decides it. So the residue is a disagreement about which of two candidate properties the word should latch onto when neither one carves a joint. Verbal, just a level up. Calling it substantive buys nothing.

(3) Ines: that makes your test unfalsifiable — anything that survives elimination gets relabeled verbal one level up, so no dispute could ever be shown non-verbal. And they act differently, one wants the lake struck from a protected register. Plus the residue isn't about words at all: it's about which candidate is more eligible, and eligibility isn't a linguistic matter.

(4) Karl: stakes never made a dispute non-verbal, you can bet on anything. And on eligibility — they agree nothing here is more eligible, that's precisely the problem. If disputes about eligibility are worldly, then every trivial stipulation fight is about the world.

They agreed on exactly one thing: if lake marked a real natural kind the case would be easy. They disagreed about whether that concession helps Ines or Karl.

Go through (1)–(4) and rate each move rather than just picking an overall winner. Then: is that shared concession actually neutral, and is there a third diagnosis both of them waved off too fast? Ines muttered something at one point about the two scientists simply having run out of neutral vocabulary.
