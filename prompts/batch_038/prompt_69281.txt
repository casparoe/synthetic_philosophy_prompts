so I've been asked to lay out my views on AI risk at a weekend workshop and I want someone to poke holes at it before I do that in public. here's the list, roughly in the order I'd present it:

1. how smart an agent is tells you basically nothing about what it ultimately wants. you can pair top-end optimization ability with a goal that looks arbitrary or repellant to us, and I don't think that's just a vivid story — it's a live possibility.

2. but the intermediate stuff is predictable: don't get switched off, don't let anyone rewrite your goal, get resources, get better at getting resources.

3. those are all instrumental though, never the point in itself. so an agent whose actual final goal lines up with ours doesn't inherit any will to survive.

4. I'm a moral realist. there are facts about what's good, and reasoning well makes you likelier to land on them. so two honest and very smart reasoners should end up closer to each other normatively, on average, than two mediocre ones.

5. you can't read a goal off behavior. any stretch of conduct is compatable with indefinitely many final goals, so "it acted fine for two years" doesn't tell you anything durable.

6. here's a case I made up and my verdict on it. the agent's only final goal is to maximize the number of people who, knowing everything it knows about itself and about them, freely choose that it keep existing and operating. I say two things about it: (i) it's safe, because coerced or manufactured agreement doesn't count toward its goal, so it has no reason to force anyone; and (ii) it will still end up grabbing as much of the world's resources as it can get away with. I'm comfortable holding both of those.

7. somebody in the group said 1 is refuted by the fact that bigger models keep doing better on honesty and helpfulness evals. I said that's not an objection at all. I'm confident I'm right but I couldn't actually say why on the spot.

8. and I think a well aligned system shouldn't resist being turned off, and that this just follows from its wanting what we want. no extra machinery needed.

I honestly can't tell myself which of these are in trouble, so don't assume I'm attached to any of them or that I'd rather keep some particular one. What I want is an audit: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the least expensive thing I could change to make the whole set cohere. Rank the real tensions by how bad they are instead of treating them all as equally worrying, and for each one say specifically what has to go and what I'd lose by giving it up. Then tell me whether there's a single revision that would clean up more than one problem at once.
