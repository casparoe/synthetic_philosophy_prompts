So here's the thing that's been bugging me. He thinks it's just arithmetic and that anyone who resists it is bad at math. I read enough to find out the whole thing rests on a rule about how you're supposed to reason when you're one of a bunch of observers — something like "reason as if you were a random sample from the people who actually exist" — and that there's a rival rule that says you should already lean toward worlds with more people in them, because that's where you're likelier to find yourself. Bostrom has names for both of them, self-sampling and self-indication, if I've got that right. My complaint is that these two rules give different answers to the same question and I can't see anything in the setup that decides between them, so it feels like two intuitions shouting at each other rather than an argument.

What I want is a miniature version I can actually check. Build me a small, fully spelled-out scenario where the two rules land on genuinely different verdicts, and where there's also somebody standing outside the whole thing who can run it over and over and just write down what happened, so we get a real frequency instead of another formula.
