Write a parody document of roughly 750 words in the genre of an institutional animal-care and husbandry guideline — the sort a welfare office circulates for maintaining a colony of research animals — except that the animals are reinforcement-learning agents being trained under reward signals. Give it a bureaucratic title, a document-control header, and numbered sections covering at minimum: enclosure and housing (context windows, memory limits, checkpointing), feeding schedule (sparse versus dense reward, reward shaping), environmental enrichment, signs of distress and stereotypic behaviour, handling and restraint, and humane endpoints. Invent the lab and the welfare officer; name no real company or living researcher, and cite nothing.

The piece is written from inside the position: the welfare office sincerely believes these agents may suffer, and it is not a figure of fun for believing so. The target of the satire is the vocabulary, not the concern — the mismatch between welfare categories built for creatures with a fixed hedonic architecture and what a trained agent actually is. Stay deadpan throughout, never wink, and never state the underlying philosophical question outright; a reader should nonetheless finish unsure whether a reward signal is a hedonimeter, a mere training input, or neither.

The humour must come from getting the technical details right rather than from science-fiction decoration. In particular: the guideline should turn on the difference between the scalar reward delivered at each step and the learned value function, with the welfare officer earnestly trying to settle which of the two is the thing capable of feeling good or bad; it should treat the fact that the reward model was itself fitted to human labels as a supply-chain matter, akin to outsourced feed; and reward hacking should appear as a stereotypy, in the same register as a caged animal bar-biting. Any claim about how training actually works should be one an RL researcher would recognise as accurate, merely re-described.

Then add one section titled "Notes on the choices" (about 150 words) identifying which real features of RL training each item exaggerates, and saying where the genuine open question about reward-as-valence sits within the piece.
