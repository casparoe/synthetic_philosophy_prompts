I'm the animal welfare lead at a small aquaculture certifier — we audit maybe 200 farms, mostly salmon, seabass and vannamei shrimp. About once a month a member farm submits a change to their harvest/stunning protocol for approval, and right now I decide those on gut feel plus whatever the auditor sends me. I want to stop doing that.

Here's what I've talked my board into, and I need you to make it actually runnable. Two internal rules:

Rule 1, the bracket rule. Where there's no direct evidence about whether a species can suffer, we set its welfare status from the closest relatives we *do* have evidence about — the same trick paleontologists use when they infer soft tissue in a fossil from whatever sits on either side of it in the family tree. So a farmed species inherits its status from the bracket around it, and the more the bracket itself is contested, the more we hedge.

Rule 2, insensibility through to death. No method gets approved unless the animal is insensible from the instant the method starts until death, and unless the person doing the killing can verify that on a moving line without special equipment.

Write me the procedure an auditor can run: the questions in order, what counts as a pass on each one, where it stops with a no, and where it has to hand the decision up to me because it's a values call dressed as a factual one. I especially need you to deal with the two spots where I think it's going to fall apart.

First, the bracket rule can be made to say nearly anything depending on how you draw the tree, so give me something that stops an auditor from bracket-shopping — picking whichever cousin conveniently gives them the verdict they wanted. Second, reflexes are our only real-time check, and reflexes are a bad proxy: you can abolish the movements while the animal is still feeling everything, and you can have an animal that's genuinely out and still twitching. Tell me which of the standard checks survive that problem and which ones I should stop pretending are checks at all.

Then run it on three live submissions:

1. A salmon operation moving from percussive stunning to CO2-saturated water, arguing the new method is more reliable because it's automated and doesn't depend on one stun operator's skill and speed.
2. A shrimp processor that chills in ice slurry and then splits/decapitates, and that maintains crustaceans aren't inside our welfare standard anyway. I expect the procedure to say something here I really don't want to hear — give it to me straight rather than softening it.
3. A mussel farm applying to come under the welfare scheme, which sounds like it should be a formality and which I suspect isn't.

Constraints: the person running this has a biology degree, no philosophy, and about 45 minutes per submission. Anything that would need a lab test, an EEG rig, or a vet present has to be flagged as such, since we cant assume any of those. And be upfront about which steps are judgment calls wearing a procedure costume — I'd rather know which of my approvals are really just me guessing.
