I work on query processing inside a database engine, and my daily vocabulary is: declarative queries versus physical plans, materialized views and their incremental maintenance, caching and cache invalidation, cost models, lazy versus eager evaluation, and approximate query processing for when exact evaluation is too expensive. I have no formal training in philosophy, though I have read enough epistemic logic to follow a Kripke frame if someone walks me through it slowly.

I keep running into the philosophical problem of logical omniscience and I want it translated into that vocabulary as literally as it will go. Five items, in this order:

1. The possible-worlds analysis of belief — a belief state as a set of worlds, believing a proposition as that proposition holding at every world in the set — together with the claim that this analysis makes an agent automatically closed under logical consequence and an automatic believer of every necessary truth.
2. The awareness and limited-reasoning family of repairs, in which a second component of the model records which propositions, or which fragments of the language, the agent is even able to entertain.
3. Impossible-worlds approaches, where the worlds an agent is related to may include ones that are internally inconsistent or that violate logical truths.
4. The pragmatic response: that the possible-worlds model is a deliberate idealization, adequate to the theoretical job belief states are hired for, and that the fine-grained work is really done by what a speaker presupposes and what a context makes salient rather than by propositional content at all.
5. A short note on whether any of the above translates differently when the attitude is knowledge rather than belief.

For each item, give the translation as faithfully as you can, then a residue report itemized as clean, distorted, or inexpressible: for the distorted ones, say what had to be bent and how far; for the inexpressible ones, say why the target vocabulary has nowhere to put it.
