I'm drafting a section for a seminar handout on self-binding and I keep getting stuck on a case I can't actually build. Here's what I need from you.

Design me a thought experiment in which the judgment-based account of akrasia (Davidson's line: you're akratic iff you intentionally act against your own all-things-considered judgment about what to do) says there's no akrasia at all, while the intention-or-resolution account (Holton's line: weakness of will is unreasonably revising a resolution, i.e. an intention you formed precisely to hold off an inclination you knew was coming) says the agent is plainly weak-willed. Same agent, same act, the two accounts split. That's the direction I care about: she does exactly what she judges best at the moment she does it, and it's still a failure of will.

What the case is for, so you know what counts as cheating: I want students to see that these two accounts aren't just relabelling each other, and that self-binding setups are where the gap shows up in the open. A case that stipulates the verdicts — where I'm told she acts against her better judgment and also told she doesn't — fails. The verdicts have to fall out of the details you give me.

Constraints:

- Realistic. An ordinary adult, an ordinary project, an ordinary temptation. No sci-fi, no neurosurgery, no time machines, no perfect predictors.
- The scenario proper is one paragraph. Numbers and analysis go after it.
- She's a sophisticate in the O'Donoghue–Rabin sense: a quasi-hyperbolic (β-δ) discounter who correctly predicts her own future preferences. No self-deception, no surprises.
- She's set up a committment device with a real penalty attached, and she can break it — it isn't a physical lock, it's a cost she chooses to eat.
- Calibrate the penalty so breaking it is neither obviously smart nor obviously dumb. I dont want a device so harsh nobody would break it, or so feeble that breaking it is just good shopping.
- No addiction or compulsion framing. I don't want the easy exit where she wasn't really herself.
- Crucially: nothing new happens between forming the resolution and breaking it. No news, no changed circumstances, no new reasons. The only thing that changes is when she is.

Then show your work:

1. The arithmetic. Give me β, δ, the dated amounts, and compute the discounted sums both from the perspective of the day she makes the plan and from the perspective of the moment of temptation, penalty included, so the ranking flip is visible on the page. The flip has to come out of present bias, not out of me asserting it.
2. The verdicts. What the judgment account says and why, what the resolution account says and why. Point at the exact bit of your scenario each verdict hangs on.
3. Which feature is doing the work? My guess is the no-new-information clause plus the fact that the resolution was aimed at this very inclination — but tell me if it's actually the sophistication that matters.
4. Ablations. What happens if I take out (a) the commitment device, (b) the sophistication, making her a naive agent instead, and (c) the no-new-information clause? For each one: does the case collapse back into bog-standard akrasia, collapse into a perfectly sensible change of mind, or does the split survive?
5. The best attack from the Davidsonian side. I expect a redescription move — either that the earlier judgment is the one she's acting against, or that at the moment of action she doesn't really have a settled all-things-considered judgment because the inclination is doing the talking. Say which version actually bites, and what I'd have to change in the case to block it.
6. The honest bit: is the β-δ machinery load-bearing here, or would any old preference reversal over time do exactly the same job? If it's decoration, say so — I'd rather know before I build the section around it.

And if you think the spec isn't jointly satisfiable — that being a genuine sophisticate makes it impossible to form the kind of resolution Holton's account needs, since she knows from the start that she'll break it — tell me that plainly instead of quietly fudging one of the constraints.
