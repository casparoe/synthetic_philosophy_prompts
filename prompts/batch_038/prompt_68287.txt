In six weeks I am delivering the closing paper at a two-day workshop on the temporal structure of experience, hosted by a research centre that brings together philosophy, vision science and computational neuroscience. The composition of the room matters a good deal. Four philosophers of mind work on the multiple drafts model and regard the Orwellian/Stalinesque question as closed in Dennett's favour. Three vision scientists run apparent-motion and masking paradigms and are openly impatient with arguments that lean on introspective report. Two computational neuroscientists treat postdictive effects as an artefact of latency compensation and predictive coding, and see nothing to explain. One phenomenologist works on retention and the specious present. The remainder are graduate students, split roughly evenly between the camps. Nobody in the room is a natural ally.

The paper's thesis is that the familiar colour-phi underdetermination argument — since no possible report could decide whether the brain edited the record after the fact (Orwellian) or constructed the mid-path colour change before it was ever experienced (Stalinesque), there is no fact of the matter, and the Cartesian theatre is discredited — fails, though not in a way that restores the theatre. My argument runs as follows.

1. The underdetermination is generated entirely by treating verbal report as the sole read-out of a subject's content at a time. Because the multiple drafts model individuates drafts by their causal and dispositional profiles, it cannot privilege report without circularity.
2. I therefore relocate the distinction rather than dissolve it. A revision is Stalinesque just in case, at the time of the target event, no subsystem in a position to guide the subject's ongoing behaviour carried the post-revision content; it is Orwellian just in case some such subsystem did carry it, and the content that later governs report is installed by overwriting a trace that had remained accessible. I call this the availability condition.
3. The availability condition is not a redescription, because it yields a discriminating design. To the Kolers and von Grünau paradigm I add continuous motor tracking: the subject follows the illusory trajectory with a stylus from before the first dot until after the report is given. The read-out is then not a discrete judgement that must land inside an impossibly narrow window, but a continuous trace whose deviations register which colour is governing the movement at each moment.
4. Hence the question is empirically decidable after all; and the theatre's genuine error was to treat report as the only read-out, not to suppose that there is a fact about when content is settled.

For each objection, give me a reply I can deliver in about two minutes, a blunt note on where that reply is softest, and an indication of whether I should concede it, contain it, or fight it outright. Then tell me what I ought to concede in the opening minutes before anybody has to ask, which single reply has to land if the paper is to survive the session, and which question I am quietly hoping nobody puts to me — together with an honest assessment of what I would have to say if they did.
