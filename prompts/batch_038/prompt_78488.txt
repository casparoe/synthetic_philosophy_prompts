Eight years ago I was diagnosed with narcolepsy and put on a wakefulness drug. The dose my neurologist settled on does precisely what it is supposed to: on it, my alertness is by every measure we can find indistinguishable from a healthy person's. I never wanted more than that, and I had a clear reason for not wanting it. My rule was simple — a drug that repairs a deficit is legitimate; a drug that carries you past the line into being better than well is a different kind of thing altogether, and I wanted no part of it.

Last month I became a night-shift dispatcher for a trucking firm. The pay is nearly double what I was making and it is the first job that has let me cover my mother's care. My doctor has told me plainly that the dose which makes me normal for a day-shift world leaves me genuinely impaired between two and six in the morning, when I am routing drivers. He is willing to raise it. I have been on the higher dose for two weeks and I am not merely adequate — on some nights I feel sharper than coworkers who have never had anything wrong with them.

I cannot work out which side of my own rule this falls on. The higher dose is not making me better than healthy; it is making me healthy relative to a situation that did not exist until my employer printed the schedule. And that schedule is nobody's nature — it is a cost the company chose. So if I keep my rule honest, whether a drug counts as repair depends on what world you happen to be standing in and who decided the terms of that world, which means the rule was never really about my body at all.

Two questions, and I want argument rather than reassurance. First: does my rule survive this case, or does the case reveal that the line I drew was tracking something other than what I thought? Second: if my coworkers, none of them diagnosed with anything, began taking the same higher dose to handle the same shifts, would what I am doing change its moral character — and if it would, what exactly does that tell me about where the line really sits? I would rather be told my rule is wrong than be told I have thought about it carefully.
