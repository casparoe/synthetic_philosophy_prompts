Strawson's Basic Argument: to be truly morally responsible for what you do you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, at least in the respects that matter; but you can't be responsible for that unless you were already responsible for the character that made the earlier choices, and so on forever. My take is that the regress principle doing all the work — call it the no-unearned-credit principle, you can't be credited or debited for an outcome unless you're responsible for the capacities that produced it — is massively overgenerative. If it kills moral responsibility it also kills credit for knowing things, for skill, for being a good friend, for pretty much any achievement, since in every case the capacities were handed to you rather than earned.

Argue that case for me, but do the hard part: defend the claim that the right move isn't just biting the bullet and going nihilist about desert across the board, and say which half of the principle has to go — responsibility for what you did, or responsibility for the way you are. I want the mechanism spelled out, its not a both-sides survey. And if you think I'm wrong that it overgenerates, argue that instead, but then explain why the principle would have a scope limit sitting exactly where moral responsibility does.
