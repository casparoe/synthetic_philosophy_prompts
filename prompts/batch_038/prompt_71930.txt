Three weeks out from giving my paper at the early modern WIP seminar, and I'd rather get mauled here first. My line: the beast-machine doctrine isn't a metaphysical claim about animals at all, it's a claim about ascribability. A state only counts as a thought if its subject is the kind of thing that could report it, and for Descartes reporting means deploying signs flexibly to the occasion rather than being triggered by it. So animals really do have sensations — that's the whole force of the More letter, on my reading — but there's no one for those sensations to be thoughts *of*. He isn't an eliminativist about animal pain, he's a non-ascriptionist, and the endless argument over whether he softened late in life comes from people reading a criterion of ascription as a criterion of existance.
