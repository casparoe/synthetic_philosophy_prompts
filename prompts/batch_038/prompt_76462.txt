Take the lucid-dream signalling experiments that began at Stanford around 1980, together with their more recent multi-laboratory descendants, and analyze that body of work as a test case for the Malcolm–Dennett dispute over whether dreams are experiences.

The facts I want treated as given, so that nothing turns on your recalling the literature: trained subjects slept in the laboratory under continuous EEG and eye-movement monitoring; upon becoming lucid they executed a prearranged pattern of left–right eye movements, and those movements appeared on the record inside REM sleep, time-locked to the moment in the dream at which the subject had decided to signal; in companion timing studies, the elapsed duration of a dreamed action such as counting to ten tracked closely the waking duration of the same action; and in the later studies, sleeping subjects answered questions posed aloud by experimenters — among them simple arithmetic problems — by means of prearranged eye movements or small facial muscle contractions, getting only a minority of the questions right, with the remainder wrong, ambiguous, or absorbed into the dream narrative as the speech of a dream character. Relatedly, it has long been known that externally applied stimuli during REM are frequently woven into the content of the dream rather than waking the dreamer.

Now run the analysis in both directions. First, state precisely what Malcolm's argument in Dreaming (1959) commits him to and what Dennett's composition-at-the-moment-of-waking proposal commits him to, and separate the commitments that are usually run together: the claim that there is something it is like for the sleeper during the sleeping period; the claim that the content of the dream is fixed before waking; and the claim that the waking report is a faithful record of anything that happened. Then map the evidence onto those three claims one result at a time, saying which claim each result bears on and which it leaves untouched. Treat the time-locking and duration-matching results separately from the question-answering results, since they may pull in different directions: a late-binding view can be modified so that the narrative is assembled at waking but stamped with the right temporal relations, whereas a subject who hears a novel arithmetic question during REM and signals a correct answer looks less like post-hoc composition than like online uptake, and Malcolm's account appears to have no place for comprehension during sleep at all.

Be explicit about where each position is silent rather than pressing it into service. Malcolm's argument leans heavily on the impossibility of getting any verification out of the sleeping period itself; the experiments open exactly such a channel, and I want your judgment on whether that refutes him, or whether it changes the subject from the grammar of dream talk to a psychophysiological question he never asked. Similarly, say whether Dennett's proposal was ever addressed to the lucid case, or only to ordinary dreams and to the confabulation of narrative order.

Then take up the deflationary replies available to both sides — that lucid REM is a dissociated or borderline state and so not a paradigm dream; that performance reflects task demand and the experimenters' cuing; that a question experienced as a dream character's speech is equally well described as perception of an external stimulus and as confabulation about it, which is just what makes it evidence for both readings at once — and say which reply costs its defender the least.

Finally, give me your verdict on the episode as a whole: a confirmation of the ordinary view that dreams are experiences, an anomaly for Malcolm, a partial anomaly for Dennett, or a case neither philosopher anticipated. And say what the case shows about the method, given that an armchair analysis of the concept of an experience turned out to be hostage to an empirical contingency — namely, whether anyone could open a signalling channel into sleep. I want your view on whether that dependency is a defect in the analysis or simply a feature of conceptual claims of this kind.
