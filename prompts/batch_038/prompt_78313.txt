I am a third-year PhD student in philosophy of science. My dissertation concerns how traits are individuated in evolutionary explanation, so I work adjacent to the function literature rather than inside it: I am comfortable with both of the main accounts, the historical one, on which the function of a trait is what its ancestors were selected to do, and the systems one, on which it is the contribution the trait currently makes to some capacity of the containing organism. What I have not been able to find, and what I need in order to finish a section of a paper, is a term for a dialectical pattern in the way the debate between those two accounts is carried on. I describe it below as precisely as I can, deliberately avoiding vocabulary that might prejudge whether it has a name.

The pattern has three components, and I have watched all three unfold in the same seminar more than once.

First, each account gets invoked in precisely those cases where it delivers the intuitive verdict. When someone wants to insist that the current use of a structure recruited long after that structure first spread is nonetheless its function — flight for feathers, say — the argument is made in terms of present contribution to a containing system; when the same person is pressed with the familiar complaint that a present-contribution analysis makes far too much count as functional, since it would list the noise the heart makes among the functions of the heart, the reply is that of course the analysis in question is the historical one. Symmetrically in the other direction: a structure that no longer does what it was selected to do is said to have no function at all when the historical account would yield the unwelcome verdict, and to have one when it does not.

Second, the two accounts are almost never applied to one and the same trait, in one and the same argument, with the resulting conflict stated and then adjudicated. When the conflict is stated, the response is that the word is ambiguous between two respectable senses and that the case simply fails to disambiguate — after which the sense that ends up being used is the one that supports the conclusion the speaker already favored.

Third, every proposed rule for choosing between the senses turns out to be unusable, because it is formulated in terms of the notion the debate was supposed to clarify. A rule directing us to apply the historical sense to genuine adaptations and the systems sense elsewhere cannot be followed by anyone who does not already know which structures are genuine adaptations, and knowing that was supposed to be part of what the accounts settle.

Does this pattern have a name? I have ruled out four candidates myself, but not confidently, and I would like to be told where each of them fails: a merely verbal dispute; pluralism about function, which I recognize is a defended position rather than a pathology and is in any case a thesis about the concept, whereas what I am describing is a way of arguing that goes on unremarked among people who explicitly reject pluralism; motivated reasoning, which seems to fit the first component but has nothing to say about the third; and ad hoc rescue, which fits the third component but not the fact that nothing here is ever presented, or experienced, as a retreat.

So: is there an established concept that covers it, and if so, how does the pattern relate to each of the candidates above and which is the best fit? If nothing covers it, please coin a term and give it a real definition — necessary and sufficient conditions, such that I could determine whether a given exchange in the literature is an instance. I would also like the edges marked out, and two questions answered in particular. Must the two accounts actually deliver conflicting verdicts in the case at hand for the pattern to arise, or does it occur just as readily where they agree and nobody checks? And is the third component, the question-begging selection rule, essential to the phenomenon, or a frequent companion that could be stripped away without changing what the thing is?

Please do not give me a reference list; I can supply citations for whatever concepts you name. The label matters far less to me than the carving: what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where it stops.
