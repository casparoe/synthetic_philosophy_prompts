I evaluate diagnostic tests for a living. Sensitivity, specificity, likelihood ratios, ROC curves, predictive values, spectrum and verification bias, and the gap between an assay's intended-use population and the population it actually gets used in — that vocabulary is fluent for me, and philosophy is not. Take Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge: truth and belief, the two subjunctive conditions, the clause holding the belief-forming method fixed, and the resulting denial that knowledge is closed under known entailment. Restate the whole package in my terms, as precisely as the material allows. Mind the naming trap as you go: what he calls sensitivity is what I call specificity, and what he calls adherence is what I call sensitivity.

Then give me the residue report. Which conditions become operating characteristics with no loss? Which translate only once you quietly supply something my framework does not provide — a reference class, say, or a measure over the situations the conditionals range over? What happens to the method clause in the cases where the belief would have arrived by a different route had things gone otherwise — is that a calibration question or a dodge? And where does the translation break because my quantities attach to a test type across a population while his attach to one person's one belief?

Finally: what is closure supposed to protect that an instrument cannot want? Once you see the tracking theory as a calibration problem, does its failure to close look like a discovery about knowledge, or like a symptom of the theory never having said anything determinate? Be blunt about the second reading.
