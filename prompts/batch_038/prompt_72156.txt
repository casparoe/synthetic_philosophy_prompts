I've written and maintained the labeling guidelines for a small annotation team for about two years. The task sounds easy: mark whether an incoming support email is "escalation-worthy." Every time a new labeler joins, I walk them through all my past examples, they agree with every single call I've made, and then two weeks later they're marking things differently — and they can always explain how their reading fits every example I ever gave them. Adding more examples doesn't seem to help, it just gives us more surface to disagree over. And there's nothing external to check against afterwards, so it's not like we can settle it by seeing what actually happened.

What really got to me tho: I reread my own notes from year one last month and I don't mark things the way past-me did, even though I'd swear I mean the exact same thing by the words.

My manager thinks the fix is a much longer, more precise written definition of the category. Will that actually help, or is there something going on here that no definition is going to touch? And if it's the second thing, what do teams like mine actually do about it in practice?
