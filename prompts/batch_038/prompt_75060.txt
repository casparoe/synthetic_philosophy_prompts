ok so I've been writing up my take on self-deception for my school's philosophy club and I want a check on whether it hangs together before I read it out, because I have a feeling I'm getting something for free somewhere. Here's what I hold:

1. Self-deception is real and it's not just a folk-y way of describing being wrong about stuff.
2. There's no divided mind. One person, one set of beliefs. I dont buy the partitioning move (the Davidson kind) or a Freudian censor either — that's just putting little people inside and relocating the problem instead of solving it.
3. What makes it *deception* rather than plain error is that the truth is already somehow available to the person. If you've got zero inkling that p is false, you're not fooling yourself about p, you're just mistaken about p.
4. Mechanically it's nothing exotic: it's ordinary motivated reasoning stretched over time — biased evidence-gathering, going looking for confirming cases, remembering selectively. Same machinery as any other bias, just pointed at yourself.
5. People are usually blameworthy for it, which is why "he's fooling himself" lands as a mild condemnation and "he's biased" doesn't.
6. Nobody ever does it on purpose though, because if you could watch yourself doing it, it wouldnt work.
