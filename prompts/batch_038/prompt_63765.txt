I'm a third-year political philosophy PhD student, and about eighteen months ago our university museum's collections committee poached me as its in-house philosopher — unpaid, roughly one afternoon a fortnight. We get five to eight requests a term: restitution claims from descendants or from source-community trusts, offers of gifts where the provenance has holes in it, the odd deaccession request. The committee is a curator, a solicitor, a registrar and two lay trustees, none of whom have read any political philosophy, and I get about forty minutes per case to hand them something they can actually vote on. At the moment we decide by vibes and I want to stop that.

What I want to run us on is Nozick's entitlement theory — Anarchy, State and Utopia (1974): justice in acquisition, justice in transfer, and rectification of injustice, plus the proviso — because its the one big framework in the literature that's genuinely historical rather than end-state, and a provenance question is a historical question. So: write me a decision procedure that fits on two sides of A4 and can be read out loud to non-philosophers.

What I need from it:

- The questions in order, and for each one what counts as a sufficient answer. Written documentation? Oral testimony from the source community? And where does silence land — most of our files just stop having records at some point, and I need the procedure to say what an absence of evidence does rather than letting whoever's loudest fill the gap.
- The three exits, marked clearly: where the procedure terminates in "we retain, and here's the reason", where it terminates in "we hand it over", and where it hands the decision up to the committee because the question is one of values rather than facts. Be explicit about that third category. My suspicion is that claimant standing, time limits, and how much weight the proviso carries all live there, and I don't want to smuggle them past the table as though they were settled by the theory.
- Rectification is the step I'm worried about, since it needs a counterfactual baseline we can't compute. We've quietly been using market value at the date of loss plus interest, which I now think is a pattern sneaking back in through the back door. Tell me what a Nozickian rectification step can legitimately do when it can't run the counterfactual — and don't just answer "exercise judgment".
- Which of Nozick's distinctions have to survive the translation for a lay reader (historical versus end-state, and the proviso, I'd have thought) and which can be dropped wholesale without breaking the thing.

Then run it on three cases we've got close variants of:

1. A set of brass plaques removed by a European military expedition in West Africa in the 1890s, gifted to us in 1961 by an officer's estate. Claim brought by a national commission; no identifiable individual descendants; three undocumented gaps before 1970. My fear is that the procedure says retain-and-pay-nothing because every transfer after 1961 was clean, and I'd rather find that out from you now than in the meeting.
2. A watercolour sold by a Jewish family in Vienna in 1938 to a dealer, bequeathed to us in 1958 in perfect good faith by that dealer's granddaughter, whose own family are now not well off. The claimants are.
3. A source country offers to withdraw a claim if we agree a permanent exchange carrying a clause that bars them from ever selling or re-loaning the object onward. Does the theory let us attach a clause like that, or is that precisely the move the Chamberlain case is meant to show we can't get away with?

Two constraints worth knowing: the sector's conventions — the 1998 Washington Principles, the 1970 UNESCO Convention threshold — are on the table as facts about what museums do, but I don't want them doing the justificatory work, and I'd like you to point out anywhere the theory and the conventions pull in different directions. Flag every step that's a judgment call wearing a procedural costume. And finish by telling me what this procedure cannot do: where it will systematically give us the wrong answer, and whether I should honestly be arguing the committee out of Nozick and into something else.
