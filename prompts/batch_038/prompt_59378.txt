So for the last four years I've helped run a tiny grant fund. Real money but small, a couple hundred grand a year, from two people I know who made it in software and didn't want to think too hard about where it went. What we do is score every application on three numbers. How bad the problem is if nobody fixes it. How much a chunk of money like ours could actually move the needle, given what's already known and what's still guesswork. And how few other people are already working on it. Multiply the three together, fund the top of the list, done. It's worked fine, honestly, better than the gut-feel way we did it before.

But something about the third number has been eating at me, and I think there's a story in it rather than an argument, so I'm asking you for the story.

The problem: the third number is a measurement of how ignored something is, and the act of publishing our list changes it. The second our ranking goes up on the website, grad students and little nonprofits and other funders all pile onto whatever we rated highly, and it isn't ignored anymore, so our own announcement retroactively makes our score wrong. We're eating the thing we were trying to protect. And then you notice the perverse move available to you, which is to say unkind things in public about a cause you privately think is excellent, so it stays cheap and unfashionable and your money goes further. You'd be lying to do good. Or you'd be doing good by lying. I can't tell which framing is right and that's sort of the point.

Write me a short story, roughly 1200 to 1800 words, that lives inside that problem. Real scenes and people, not an essay wearing a story as a costume. Invented organizations are fine, please don't use any real charity or funder's name.

A few things I'd want in there, but fold them in however you like:

The person who writes the scores should be a character, and we should see them actually doing it, with the spreadsheet or notebook or whatever, including the moment where they realize the numbers aren't the same kind of thing as each other. Multiplying a 9 on "how bad is it" by a 1 on "can anyone do anything about it" gives you a 9, same as a 3-3-3, and somewhere in the story somebody should object to that and be right about it.

I want a character who's doing the unglamorous work on something nobody cares about, and I want them to find out they've been rated highly. Their reaction should not be simple gratitude.

There should be a scene where somebody has to choose between publishing an honest score and publishing a strategic one, and the story should let that choice cost them something either way. No neat moral at the end, and nobody giving a speech that explains the theme. If the last paragraph ties a bow on it I'll know you didn't trust me.

Third person or first, your call. I'd rather it be a bit sad and a bit funny than solemn the whole way through.
