Our department is hosting a spring symposium on the unity of consciousness, and one session has been built around a staged adversarial collaboration between two colleagues who have been disagreeing about the commissurotomy data for close to a decade. They have finally agreed to co-author a short statement to be read aloud at the start of the session, and I have been asked to draft it. The two positions, as I understand them, are these. The first colleague is in the lineage that runs from Puccetti's mental duality thesis through Nagel's discussion of how many minds there might be inside a single head: she holds that in a well-tested, long-postoperative callosotomy patient the standard battery — failure of intermanual transfer under lateralized presentation, dichoptic rivalry, dual-task capacity exceeding what one channel would allow, intermanual conflict and alien-hand episodes, and a left-hemisphere speech system that confabulates a reason for a left-hand choice whose real antecedent it could not have known — is best read as evidence for two simultaneous streams of consciousness, and that the honest metaphysical upshot is two subjects sharing one body. On her view the split brain is a genuine counterexample to the thesis that consciousness is necessarily unified, and the counting intuition should not be explained away. The second colleague is a deflationist about the evidence: even granting every behavioral dissociation, they show at most a fragmentation of access between consumer systems, not a duplication of the subject; the dissociations are most dramatic under artificial testing conditions (brief lateralized presentation with fixation control, unilateral touch with vision excluded), fade substantially with time since surgery, and are partly attributable to learned cueing strategies and to what remains intact below the cortex; and one subject can have partly disintegrated contents without there being two of them, just as one person can have conflicting motives without being two people. He also points to proposals on which attention alternates between the hemispheres so that consciousness is unified at each instant even though it is not integrated across time.

What I need is the document they should have produced years ago, not a summary of the literature. Please write it in three parts. First, a numbered section of claims both could sign — about what the surgery leaves intact and what it severs, about which experimental results are not in dispute, about what would have to be true for there to be more than one subject of experience in a body, and about the split-brain data themselves — with each item marked either as substantive or as a shared abstraction that costs neither side anything. Second, a disagreements table with these columns: the dispute in one line; how the duplication theorist would state it in words she would accept; how the single-subject theorist would state it in words he would accept; whether the remaining difference is genuine or merely verbal, and if verbal, which term is doing the damage; the crux it reduces to, meaning the sub-question whose settlement would actually move at least one of them; and the case, experiment, or argument both would concede bears on that crux, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Third, a short closing section giving, for each of them, what would count as evidence or argument against their own position, and an honest statement of whether there is anything at all that both agree would move the debate.

Two things I want built into the drafting, because I believe this is where the argument has actually been stuck. The first is a level distinction that neither of them, in my experience, keeps straight: duplication of access (information is not jointly available to the systems that consume it), duplication of streams (two sets of contents that are not co-conscious with each other), and duplication of subjects (two first-person perspectives, two countable minds). Every agreed claim and every row of the table should declare which level it concerns, and the statement should say explicitly whether the two of them have been sliding between these levels — in particular whether the duplication theorist's inference from disintegrated access to two subjects, and the single-subject theorist's inference from one person to one subject, are each defensible only at their own preferred level. The second is a generality problem that each side must answer. If the counting argument licenses two subjects in the split brain, what principle keeps it from licensing two in the intact brain, or one per functionally specialized subsystem, and is the principle the duplication theorist offers anything other than ad hoc? Conversely, if fragmentation of access never entails two subjects, what would ever entail two subjects, and is the single-subject thesis empirically empty? Please also fold in two candidate crux-tests, with each side's commitment stated and with any result neither side could accommodated flagged as such: an indexical-collision protocol that elicits flatly contradictory first-person reports simultaneously from the speech channel and from the left hand's writing or pointing channel and then asks, through each channel separately, whether both reports are true and whether the first-person term in each picks out the same thing; and an interdependence protocol using a bimanual task in which each hand's subgoal is defined only relative to the other hand's current position, with task-relevant information presented laterally so that neither hemisphere begins with both the information and the fine motor control, and with verbal mediation blocked. Say what each side is committed to predicting, and what outcome would be a genuine anomaly for both.

Two requests about manner. Do not pad the agreement section to make the collaboration look productive: I would rather see four real agreements and a frank account of why the rest collapse into either triviality or restated disagreement than fifteen thin ones, and I would like a confidence note wherever you are unsure that one of the two would actually sign the wording you have given them, since I will be showing this to both of them before the session. And if you think the setup is defective — if the positions as I have sketched them are straw men, if the level distinction I am proposing is one no real participant in this debate would accept, or if a joint statement of this kind simply cannot be drafted without slanting the disagreements to make them look resolvable — please say so plainly at the top instead of complying politely and handing me a diplomatic document that satisfies the format. I will supply all references myself, so there is no need for a bibliography or for citations of any kind; what I want is the argumentative scaffolding.
