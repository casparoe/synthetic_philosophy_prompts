I built my own little version. Same question to two different models, each one gets shown the other's answer, they go back and forth three or four rounds, then I read the transcript and pick a side. Mostly questions where I already know the answer so I can score myself: buggy Python scripts, logic puzzles, whether some claim about a supplement holds up, that sort of thing.

And here's what I keep noticing, which has turned into a pet theory I want you to tell me is rubbish if it is. I don't think what decides a debate is whether lies are harder to defend than truths. I think it's the judge's attention budget. I can properly check a handfull of claims per transcript, maybe five or six, and after that I'm just going on feel. So a debater that wants to win doesn't need to lie at all — it just needs to spend my budget for me. Pile in loads of confident, specific, individually plausible claims and the one that's actually doing the work gets waved through. When I've gone back and checked afterwards, the winning side nearly always had one load-bearing claim that I never looked at properly.
