So I'm running the Q&A after a talk at our local phil-of-mind reading group — basically the meetup-circuit version of a colloquium, half the room has read Chalmers and the other half just wants to know whether uploading is going to work. The speaker is a philosopher whose whole thing is that she *accepts* the triviality objection: fine, with enough interpretive free rein any physical system implements every finite-state automaton, so "the brain is a computer" comes out true of a rock too and is therefore worthless as stated. Her move is to relocate the interesting question to which computational description of a system is explanatorily priviledged, and she picks out the privileged ones by counterfactual robustness — a description counts if the system's behavior tracks it across the relevant range of interventions, not just along the one trajectory it happened to have.

I've got about 25 minutes and I want to spend them making the position hard to hold rather than letting her deliver the talk again in shorter sentences. Write me 8–10 questions in the order I should ask them, each with a one-line follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. She's very good at absorbing an objection by pointing out it assumes a representationalist picture she already rejected, so the follow-ups need to not be absorbable that way. Cruxes over gotchas, though a couple of gotchas are welcome.

Things I want at least one question on, each:

- whether counterfactual robustness is doing real work or just relabeling the problem. What tells you the difference between a genuinely robust implementation and a gerrymandered one that happens to survive every intervention we can actually perform on it?
- whether her account just collapses back into functionalism, and if so whether she inherits the absent/inverted qualia problems or thinks she escapes them, and why.
- the scaling case: the billion homunculi, or a sufficiently complicated pile of rocks. At what point does robustness buy her anything that triviality didn't already concede?
- analog vs digital. Neurons aren't obviously discrete-state machines, so what is she even calling a computation here, and does her answer change if neural coding turns out to be analog all the way down?
- and the one I actually care about most: if privilege is fixed by counterfactual tracking, what does that imply for whole-brain emulation? Specifically, is there any test short of building the em that could tell you whether you preserved the right counterfactuals — and if not, is her account contributing something to the upload debate or just making it unsolvable in a more sophisticated vocabulary?

End on a question about what would count as evidence *against* the view, phrased so that "my account is neutral on that" isn't a legal answer.
