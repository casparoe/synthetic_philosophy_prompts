I am drafting a workshop paper on standing to blame, and before I build an argument on top of the framework I have converged on, I would like a proper audit of the framework itself. The paper is meant to be a short, self-contained piece for a graduate workshop, so what I need to know is whether my package of commitments is jointly tenable, and if it is not, which plank I should cut. Here is the package.

(1) Blame is at bottom a composite of a belief that someone acted wrongly and an emotion of the resentment or indignation family. It does not essentially involve saying anything to anybody: blame can be wholly silent and still be full-blooded blame.

(2) Standing is a property of the blamer rather than of any particular act of blaming. Two people can form the very same attitude toward the very same conduct, and one can be badly placed to hold it while the other is well placed.

(3) Losing standing never touches the target's desert. What the hypocrite gets wrong is not the judgment that the other person acted badly; that judgment is true. The defect lies elsewhere.

(4) What destroys standing, specifically, is holding oneself exempt from a standard one applies to others. The decisive failure is a failure to turn one's condemnation on one's own conduct, so a person who is guilty of exactly the fault they deplore, but who condemns themselves for it in private, keeps their standing intact.

(5) The reason that failure matters is that it gives the blamed party a legitimate objection. The complaint that grounds standing-deficiency is the target's: if the person being blamed cannot reasonably object, there is no standing problem at all.

(6) Meddling is a standing-deficiency of the same kind as hypocrisy, and I want to explain it by the same mechanism. Someone with entirely clean hands who presses a near-stranger about a private failing is badly placed to blame in just the way the hypocrite is.

(7) Blaming without standing is impermissible, not merely inadvisable, impolitic, or futile.

(8) Standing is forfeitable and recoverable: acknowledgment, apology, and genuine reform of one's own conduct restore it.

(9) I do not think we have direct voluntary control over our moral beliefs or our reactive emotions.

(10) The tu quoque move should carry essentially no weight in ordinary moral argument: telling someone they are a hypocrite never shows that they were not wrong. In law, by contrast, the structurally analogous move does real work, and there are cases in which outrageous conduct by investigators, or plainly selective enforcement, deprives the state of the right to proceed against a defendant whose guilt is not in doubt.

I also want the package tested against two case verdicts I feel confident about. First: a man embezzled from his employer thirty years ago, has quietly repented ever since, has told no one, and now hears a colleague boasting about a comparable scheme and feels, and openly expresses, undisguised contempt for him. My verdict is that the colleague has no complaint against this blamer and that the contempt is not merely permissible but apt. Second: a woman shares her brother's failing, has never mentioned it to him or to anyone else, has made no self-reproach whatever, and simply harbours a settled, wordless contempt for him on account of it. My verdict is that she is badly placed in exactly the way an outspoken hypocrite is, and that the impropriety attaches to the attitude itself rather than to anything she does with it.

Please work through all of this and tell me which commitments are genuinely incompatible with which. Rank the incompatibilities by severity instead of presenting them as equally damaging, and for each one say what would have to be given up or restricted, what the cost of that revision is, and whether the cost falls on something I have framed as central. I am fairly sure I have also included at least one pair that only looks like it conflicts; tell me which one, and say why it survives inspection, since a blanket report of tension everywhere would be useless to me. Then tell me which single revision would repair the largest number of problems at once, and sketch briefly what the resulting view would look like.

You may refer to my commitments by their numbers as you go, but please write the whole thing in continuous prose: no headings, no numbered or bulleted lists, no bold, no tables. I want it to read like a letter from a colleague who has genuinely thought about the package rather than like a referee's report.
