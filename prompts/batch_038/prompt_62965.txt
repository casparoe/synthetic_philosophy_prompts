This spring I am co-teaching a graduate seminar in the philosophy of mind with two colleagues from the machine learning group, and in week five we take up Dennett's tower of generate-and-test — the fourfold division of agents into Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Last year I circulated a handout that simply expounded the four storeys in ascending order, and the session collapsed within ten minutes. The engineers in the room objected that every criterion as I had stated it could be answered only from the inside or by stipulation, and the seminar then dissolved into the usual argument about whether any of this bears on consciousness. This year I want to hand them a sorting instrument instead of an exposition: something that can be given to a student together with a card describing an agent, and that yields a classification from facts about the agent's architecture and history that a third party could in principle check.

What I need, then, is a decision tree with six to eight nodes along any path, terminating in leaves that are the four named kinds plus whatever else the logical space turns out to require. Two constraints matter more to me than elegance. First, no node may be answerable only by introspection: every question must concern where selection happens and on what timescale — across generations of a design process, across the agent's own lifetime, inside the agent before it acts, or in an external symbolic environment the agent can consult and revise — and must be answerable from publicly inspectable facts about how the thing was built and trained. Second, no node may contain the name of any of the four kinds or of the man it is named after; the point is that a student who has never read a page of Dennett can run the tree and land somewhere, and be told the label only afterwards. Each node should carry a one-line gloss naming the criterion it operationalizes, so that I can see at a glance where the tree is doing work and where it is merely restating a term.

I also want the instrument to be honest about its own architecture. Dennett presents the tower as cumulative, each storey presupposing the ones below it, and my students will want to know whether that is a discovery or a stipulation. So give me the tree in its nested form, and then set the four capacities out as independent dimensions, enumerate the combinations that result, and say for each empty cell whether it is incoherent, merely uninstantiated but constructible, or already occupied by something the scheme was never designed to describe. Flag explicitly any pair of leaves your tree cannot in fact separate; I expect at least the pair consisting of a system that has absorbed a culture but cannot consult or alter external artifacts at run time, and one that can.

Then run the tree on all of the following, reporting the classification and, wherever the answer is contested, one sentence on what the disagreement turns on: C. elegans, noting that there is some evidence of simple learning in it, which is precisely why I include it; a honeybee colony treated as a single agent; a Physarum plasmodium that finds the shortest route through a maze; a newborn human infant; a sheepdog after two years of training by one handler; a hand-written chess engine that searches a game tree against a fixed evaluation function; a tabular model-free reinforcement learning agent in a grid world; a Dyna-style agent that plans against a world model it has learned from experience; a Go program whose evaluation was acquired entirely from self-play with no human games in its training data; a large language model whose weights are frozen at deployment but which may call a calculator and a retrieval system through text; that same model after it has additionally been updated online from user approval signals; and a human expert who, following illness, can no longer acquire any new notation or vocabulary but retains fluent command of everything she learned before. To those add three cases of my own devising, which the tree must handle on pain of my judging it incomplete: an agent whose parameters are fixed at manufacture but which keeps a growing natural-language notebook of instructions to itself, reading it at the start of every episode and appending to it at the end; an agent produced by black-box evolutionary search over architectures, receiving no feedback whatever within its lifetime, but which at run time deliberates by simulating outcomes on a learned model; and an agent with a rich internal world model that has invented during training a compositional private code for labelling and recombining its own states, in a laboratory where no human artifact has ever been introduced. Finish by proposing two further cases of your own that you think the scheme mishandles, saying which cell each lands in and why that verdict should make us uneasy.

The handout should fit on two sides of A4 and survive a twenty-minute breakout exercise, so keep the prose to a minimum and let the tree do the work. For two cases I will tell you where I currently stand, and I would like you either to vindicate me or to show me exactly which node I am answering differently from the way you think I should. I take the frozen, tool-using language model to be Gregorian — not because it consults artifacts at run time, which it plainly does, but because its training corpus is itself a sediment of human-designed cognitive tools, so the scaffolding is present whether or not it is currently in use; and I take the tabular grid-world agent to be Skinnerian and nothing more, since it has no model to consult and its only route to a better policy is a history of rewarded and unrewarded actions. Where I am genuinely unsure is whether gradient descent against a loss function on a fixed corpus should count as Darwinian selection at all: there are candidate configurations and there is a fitness measure, but there are no generations in any biological sense and no environment the agent is coping with. If your tree is forced to answer that question at its first node, say so, and give me both ways of answering it along with the downstream consequences of each. Please do not append a bibliography or a reading list — the handout will carry my name and nothing else, and I would much rather you reasoned from the concepts than gestured at sources.
