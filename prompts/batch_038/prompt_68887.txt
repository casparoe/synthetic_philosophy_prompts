Our upper-level metaphysics seminar on material objects ends with a 48-hour take-home, and I want its single question on the special composition question to force adjudication rather than a tour of the standard positions. The question should have students evaluate a view I will stipulate for them: some things compose something just in case no complete description of the world can state all the facts about those things without employing a singular term for something they compose. Build two test cases into the question itself — a swarm of bees whose members bear dispositions that are irreducibly collective, and a shoe in Boston together with the Eiffel Tower, where every fact there is, is a fact about one of them or a plain relation between them — and ask whether the view delivers the right verdicts or only appears to, and what it commits its proponent to about whether composition depends on our descriptive resources.

Please write the question as students will see it; a model answer of roughly 700 words; and a marking scheme worth 25 marks that separates an A from a B by naming the moves each grade must contain, with a one-line note beside each band on the mistake its loss is meant to catch — for instance, assuming without argument that irreducibility of description tracks irreducibility of parthood, or filing the view under conventionalism without saying why that is or is not fair to it.

Begin directly with the exam question as students would receive it: no preface, no restatement of this request, and no commentary after the marking scheme.
