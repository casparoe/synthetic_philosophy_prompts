"In the centipede game, backward induction proves that taking immediately is the only rational opening move, since Player 2 will take at the final node and the reasoning unravels from there. The proof works only under common knowledge of rationality: if Player 1 passes, the chain is broken, because Player 2 can no longer be certain that Player 1 is rational and therefore has no rational action of his own available. That is the entire content of the backward induction paradox — a valid theorem whose premise fails in practice, human beings not being, and not being knowable to be, perfectly rational. Experiments confirm this: in the classic laboratory study of the game, subjects almost never take at the first node and typically play deep into the tree. What remains of interest is therefore a question about bounded rationality. The deeper worry, that common knowledge of rationality becomes incoherent once a deviation has occurred, has been answered: after Player 1 deviates, what Player 2 should do is uniquely determined by the very same backward induction, now applied to the remaining subtree."
