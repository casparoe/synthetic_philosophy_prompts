I just took over the performance unit at a state workforce agency. We pay job-training providers partly on a 90-day placement rate, and every quarter a handful of them jump ten points and nobody knows whether to clap or open an investigation. Our last audit turned up a provider that had quietly stopped enrolling anyone with a record or a long gap in thier work history — the metric looked fantastic and the program stopped serving the people it exists for.

Write me a triage procedure my analyst can run on the quarterly numbers before we decide whether to send auditors out. Assume we have intake dates, barrier and demographic flags, placement dates, wage at placement, and 6- and 12-month follow-ups — no site visits, no experiment, no clean comparison group. I want the tests in order, what each one needs and what a fail looks like, and the points where the procedure has to stop at "can't tell from this data" instead of guessing. Ground it in whatever is actually known about how a proxy comes apart from the thing you care about; I know there's a difference between gaming somebody chose to do and drift that just falls out of the structure of the measure, and I want those handled differently because the fixes are different. Flag every step that's really a judgment call wearing a lab coat.

Then run it on three: a provider whose placement rate rose sharply the same quarter they cut intake by a third; a provider whose wage numbers improved while 12-month retention got worse; and an honestly excellent provider who just hired two great job coaches — the case where I expect your procedure to flag someone who did nothing wrong. Finish with what this thing can't do and what I'd have to buy to make it able to.
