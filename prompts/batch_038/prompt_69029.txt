Thirty-five years running a trace-organic analytical laboratory left me, in retirement, reading philosophy seriously; it has now collided with the other thing I do with my time, which is to sit on our national pesticide advisory panel.

Next month the panel rules on renewing the registration of a widely used fungicide. The sponsor's two-year rodent study shows a small, statistically borderline rise in a rare tumour at the top dose only; modelled human intake lies some forty-fold below that dose, but the principal metabolite is persistent, and we have no groundwater measurements at all from the region where the crop is treated most heavily. Half the panel holds that with children's exposure in the balance we simply do not know that it is safe, and that this settles the matter: suspend. The other half holds that the evidence is precisely what it was in the draft assessment, that what has changed is only how costly being wrong would be, and that costs belong in the risk-management step rather than in any claim about what we know. A third colleague proposes a compromise — leave the assessment as drafted, but strike the word "safe" from the published opinion, on the ground that whether we may say we know something depends on the context in which it is said.

Draft the memo I will table: about 700 words, recommending one course (renewal, conditional renewal, or suspension) together with its conditions and a review date, and devoting a paragraph of its own to the strongest case against my recommendation. Then identify, in terms my colleagues could follow without the vocabulary I have picked up in my reading, the commitment about knowledge and practical stakes on which that recommendation rests; say how the losses suspension would impose on growers bear on what is at stake; assess whether the third colleague's verbal adjustment changes anything at all; and state what the recommendation would be if the panel took the opposite view. If nothing would change, say so plainly, and explain why the disagreement is nonetheless worth the panel's time.
