So, odd request. For two years I've been on the citizen advisory panel for our town's water system after the plume turned up under the old plant site, and the final public meeting is on the 14th. The part that actually wrecked me isn't the contamination — it's that the risk number we were handed came as a range, somewhere between one in a million and one in a thousand over a lifetime, depending on assumptions nobody could pin down, and alot of those assumptions weren't even about the water. After four rounds of consultants that range never got narrower. Not once. We kept being told the next round of data would settle it. Around month eight I went looking for whether there's a name for this and fell deep into imprecise probability stuff: the idea that your belief about something isn't one number but a whole set of them, that you can condition the entire set on new evidence and watch the interval get *wider* instead of sharper, that if your interval is already the full zero-to-one then no amount of evidence can budge it at all, and that when you finally have to act, what picks the number isn't your evidence but some rule you've adopted for handling a range. That described my last two years better than anything the consultants ever said.

What I want to read at the meeting is a ghazal, seven or nine couplets, and I want the form to carry that content rather than the vocabulary. Each couplet should be one member of the set: completely self-contained, entirely reasonable on its own terms, and flatly incompatible with its neighbours. Keep a real monorhyme and make the refrain the datum — the same short phrase coming back at the end of the second line of every couplet, and in both lines of the opening couplet, so the listener can watch everybody being handed the identical piece of evidence and reaching wildly different conclusions anyway. One couplet, maybe the fifth, should be the vacuous one, the zero-to-one that has given up, and it should visibly not be moved by anything the refrain brings it. And I want one couplet — second or third — to be the dilation moment, with the turn happening *inside* the couplet: first line before the datum, fairly sure of something, second line after it, and the range has opened up instead of closing. The final couplet has to be the decision, because that's the punchline and also the literal truth of what happened here: the vote was scheduled, somebody had to produce a number, and what produced it wasn't the data. Traditionally that's where the poet signs their own name in the last line, so put that convention to work — a name attached to a rule rather than to a fact.

Please don't use any of the technical words in the poem. No Bayes, no credence, no interval, no dilation, no maximin, no set. It should be built out of well water and folding tables and the four rounds of consultants and the room at seven in the evening and the range itself. Somebody who knows this corner of philosophy should be able to read it and recognize exactly what's happening structurally without a single term of art showing up; my neighbours should just find it a good, slightly angry poem about a town that never got an answer. And don't let it resolve. Don't close the interval in the last couplet — it didn't close for us, and the couplets shouldn't suddenly start agreeing just because the poem is over.
