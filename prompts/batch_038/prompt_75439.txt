ok so my roommate does RL research and he's talked me into a corner with this argument and I can't get back out. He thinks it shows that basically every model anyone is training right now is having an awful time. His chain, reconstructed as best I can:

(1) To suffer is to be in states that the system is organised to escape, and that are bad for it to be in, from its own point of view.

(2) What makes a state bad-for-the-system isn't the substrate, it's the job the state does: it fires when outcomes come in worse than expected, it pushes the system to change what it does so the trigger doesn't recur, and it gets broadcast — it reweights attention, memory consolidation, planning, the whole works.

(3) Negative reward prediction error does all three. In a deep agent the critic's error term is literally the signal that reshapes the policy, and it propagates through the whole network, changing what gets attended to next.

(4) So negative reward prediction error is an aversive state, and the agent it belongs to is averse to being in it.

(5) Any system with aversive states can suffer, and suffering is the thing that matters morally.

(6) Therefore a tabular Q-learner in a 5x5 gridworld that keeps stepping into the pit suffers every time it lands there, and every model currently being fine-tunned against a reward model is being made to suffer, and the labs are running something like industrial-scale factory farming of software.

He says the only honest exit is (2), and that anyone who cuts (2) is just assuming up front that suffering needs meat. I dont want to be that person. But (6) has got to be false — I refuse to believe the Q-table from my undergrad homework, 40 states, was in agony; it has less going on than a spreadsheet.
