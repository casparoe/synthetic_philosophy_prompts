Write the desiderata for a theory of when an earlier self's directive has authority over a later self. For each one, give the case or consideration behind it and mark whether it's non-negotiable or just a pet requirement of one camp. Make sure the list handles at least these:

- Trigger conditions. Her form says "when I can no longer name my grandchildren." She can't — but she confidently names three people who aren't them. Condition satisfied in letter, not in spirit. Is interpretation part of the theory or a separate engineering problem?
- The self-silencing clause. She wrote "when I get there I'll insist I want to live. That's the disease talking, don't listen to me." A theory has to say whether a self can pre-discount its own future reports without making every later report worthless.

- The distinctions it can't collapse — having a say vs having the only say, same person vs same principal, what she wanted vs what she'd want if she knew.
- Empirical stuff it can't contradict, e.g. that people are lousy at forecasting their own future feelings.
