**DRAFT — "Waste Makes It True"**

**1.** Darwin wrote to Asa Gray in April 1860: "The sight of a feather in a peacock's tail, whenever I gaze at it, makes me sick!" The problem he was pointing at is easy to state. A tail that wins you mates but makes you slower, hungrier and more visible to predators should have been eliminated generations ago. Amotz Zahavi's answer, in the mid-seventies, was that the burden is not a side effect of the signal — it *is* the signal. The tail says "I am in good condition" precisely because a male in bad condition could not carry it and survive. Honesty is purchased with waste.

**2.** So here is the mechanism as I understand it. For a signal to be trustworthy, faking it has to hurt more than it pays. Which means the amount of waste is what does the work: a signal is credible in proportion to how much of it is thrown away, burned, rendered useless. Low pitch, huge antlers, absurd train feathers — all pure loss, and that's the entire point of them. This is the same logic as Michael Spence's job-market model, except Grafen got there first with peacocks and the economists later noticed it applied to university degrees too. And as far as I can tell Zahavi and Grafen were on the same page about all this — Grafen wrote Zahavi's intuition down as equations, Zahavi was pleased, end of story.

**3.** Where I know I'm being sloppy. I've been lumping together three things that are probably not the same thing. A bullfrog's croak: you cannot fake the pitch, it falls out of body size, no effort or willpower involved anywhere. A gazelle stotting at a cheetah: I've been calling this a pure waste display, an advertisement that says "I have energy to burn, don't bother", and the burning is the message. A sparrow's chest badge: an arbitrary mark that works only because neighbours punish liars. I suspect at most one of those three actually deserves the word handicap, and I can't tell you which.

**4.** Humans. Charity galas, the overpriced bottle at the table, the four-hour open-source patch nobody asked for, the wedding that costs more than the deposit on a house, marathon fundraising. My claim in the draft is that the signal only works if the resource is genuinely destroyed rather than converted into something useful — a donation to a charity that actually does good is a *weaker* signal of commitment than burning the same sum, because the useful donation partly pays for itself in warm glow, tax relief, and public goods. But the strictest version of that predicts the most credible human signals should be the most useless ones, and that is plainly false: nobody trusts a person who burns ten thousand dollars more than one who donates it. So either the biology is wrong or my human extension is, and I'd like to know which.

**5.** What I think I actually want to argue, by the end, is that the load-bearing idea isn't "waste" at all but "a cost that bites different senders differently" — the tail is ruinous for a weak male and merely annoying for a strong one, and that gap is what no liar can close. My worry is that this is a retreat into something trivially true, a tautology dressed as a theory, and that once you say it that way there's no content left.
