I do civic-tech stuff on the side and our little group has been arguing about compulsory voting for maybe two years now, same six people, nobody budges. One camp says mandatory turnout fixes a bias, because the people who dont show up are systematically poorer and less educated, so a voluntary system quietly up-weights the comfortable. The other camp says you're just conscripting a pile of uninterested people who'll vote at random or follow the ballot order, and that there's no duty to vote in the first place. The fights about dropping the voting age to 16 and about letting long-term residents who aren't citizens vote split the exact same way.

My working theory — tell me if its dumb — is that they aren't actually disagreeing about the empirical stuff. They're disagreeing about whether a non-vote is missing data or a real data point. Is abstention a null you should never impute, or is it itself a preference (contentment, protest, "the system's fine") that compulsion would overwrite and destroy? And are 16-year-olds and non-citizen residents rows you filtered out, or rows that aren't in the table yet?

Is that the real structure of the disagreement, or am I just forcing a database metaphor onto something that's really about liberty and duty, or about who counts as a member? Walk me through what each side is actually committed to and where they genuinely can't meet, then tell me what would count as progress — some real-world case that could settle part of it, a distinction someone should draw, or an honest argument that this one just never resolves and why.
