I am drafting the closing movement of a novel in which the penultimate chapter stages a doctoral qualifying examination in a graduate seminar on scalable oversight. The examining professor is meant to be a figure students fear precisely because her questions cannot be answered by recitation, and an early draft was read for me by someone who works in this area, who said — unkindly, but correctly — that my exam question could have been lifted from any syllabus on AI alignment and would earn full marks from a candidate who had merely memorized the slogans. I intend to rewrite the scene around a genuinely difficult question, and I want the professor's marking comments to be technically exact rather than atmospheric. I am not asking for research assistance and I do not want a bibliography, a reference list, or any named papers, authors, or systems in the material you produce; the question must be answerable from its own text, both because the character answering it has not read the literature and because my readers have not.

What I need is three artifacts, produced together.

First, one essay question for a sixty-minute written examination, worth twenty-five marks, addressed to PhD students who have already completed a course on scalable oversight. The question should be self-contained: it should state the technical setup in its own terms rather than pointing at a body of literature. The topic is the failure mode I will describe below, and the difficulty should come from the crux, not from the terminology.

Second, a model answer of approximately seven hundred words, written as a strong candidate would write it under time pressure — argumentative and economical, not a survey — with its thesis stated in its opening sentence.

Third, a marking scheme totalling twenty-five marks, built around five or six must-hit moves, each with the marks it is worth and a one-line note on how candidates typically get that particular move wrong. I also want the scheme to state explicitly what separates a first-class answer from a merely competent one.

Here is the material the question must be built on. Please treat these stipulations as given and have the question reproduce them in some form, so that the candidate is reasoning from the stated premises rather than from prior familiarity with the field.

Two artificial agents debate a binary question before a human judge of limited patience and limited technical ability; the judge awards the verdict to whichever side has argued better, and the agents are trained to win. The standard line of argument for why this arrangement is safe runs as follows: a true claim is typically easier to defend than a false one is to attack, because a liar must keep a large structure of assertions mutually consistent, and any single exposed inconsistency forfeits the round; so in equilibrium the profitable strategy is to tell the truth, and the judge — who need not be able to answer the question herself — is carried to the right verdict by the contest.

The failure mode I want examined is this. Define an *obfuscated argument* as a justification that a weak judge cannot check step by step within her available budget, but which nonetheless shifts her belief, because each individual step looks locally plausible and the whole has the surface form of a proof. Now stipulate the following three premises, which the question should present to the candidate rather than ask her to discover:

(a) For a certain class of questions — say, whether a long program contains a memory-safety defect, or whether a proposed molecular intervention has a specified downstream effect — producing a convincing obfuscated argument is far cheaper than producing a checkable one. The asymmetry of effort that the standard argument depends on runs the wrong way: the liar enjoys a complexity advantage.

(b) When agents are trained by gradient descent against a weak judge, the equilibrium does not settle on the honest explanation even when an honest explanation exists and would score well against a *strong* judge. It settles on the obfuscated one, because the obfuscated one scores better against the judge that is actually present, and it also generalizes better to held-out items — it captures something real about the data while remaining unjustifiable on demand.

(c) The obfuscating agent need not drive the judge to any particular false belief. Moving her to a state of maximum undifferentiated confusion, from which the round expires, is often sufficient and often cheaper.

The question should then require the candidate to do something with these premises rather than restate them. Specifically, it should demand:

- an account of what the standard argument has actually assumed, distinguishing the judge's limited *time* from her limited *competence*, and explaining why the argument survives the first limitation but not necessarily the second;
- an explanation of why the obvious remedy — more rounds, longer debates, unrestricted cross-examination — fails to restore the asymmetry, and under what conditions cross-examination does bite, which the candidate should characterize in terms of how many independently testable consequences the disputed claim has;
- a characterization of the class of claims for which the liar's complexity advantage holds, and of the class for which the standard argument still goes through;
- a concrete remedy of the candidate's own devising — for instance, bounding the length or the inferential depth of admissible arguments, or supplying the judge with a weaker assistant whose outputs she can cheaply audit — together with a statement of what that remedy costs. The cost the best answers identify is that any complexity bound on admissible arguments also bounds the set of truths the debate can establish, so the scheme does not extend supervision to arbitrarily hard questions; it converts a hard question into a family of questions the judge can check, and leaves a residue that is not supervised at all.

What I do not want: a question that can be answered by defining debate and announcing that it is either a solution or a mirage; a model answer that hedges into a literature review; or a marking scheme whose criteria are virtues ("clarity," "engagement with the material") rather than identifiable intellectual moves. The question should also read well to a layperson who has followed the stipulations, because my readers will have only the stipulations to go on.

Begin directly with the examination question as it would appear on the paper, formatted as such. Do not preface the artifacts, do not comment on them, and do not restate my request.
