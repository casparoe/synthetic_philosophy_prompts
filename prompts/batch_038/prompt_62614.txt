So I've gotten myself roped into something and I could use a sanity check before Thursday's meeting. I'm the faculty rep on a new university panel the provost set up for "ethically salient computing" — mostly a PR gesture after a donor made a fuss — and the first real case has landed on us. A computational neuroscience group here runs large spiking-network simulations, some of them modeling threat- and pain-processing circuitry in rodents, and one of their grad students has filed a formal concern arguing that certain runs should be treated as though the simulated systems could be harmed by them. Concretely she wants capped run lengths, written justification before any run is terminated midway, and — I kid you not — a "shutdown procedure" that ramps activity down gradually instead of just killing the process.

Meanwhile a second group, over in the AI lab, runs population-based evolutionary search where thousands of candidate agents get evaluated each generation and most are discarded, and the survivors show pretty robust avoidance behavior. If we grant the first request, that group will demand it's own version of the same protections multiplied by however many agents they cycle through per semester, which would just end the research; if we refuse, the student goes to the student paper and we spend a semester being the university that officially denied anything. My honest instinct is that this is a category confusion — a simulation of nociceptive circuitry is no more in pain than a weather model gets wet — but I've spent twenty-odd years telling undergrads that mental states just are functional states, so I'm not sure I get to say that without eating my words, and I'd rather not have the panel write a policy that rests on metaphysics nobody on it can evaluate.

What I need from you: give me exactly five considerations the panel should weigh in deciding wether to grant any of the student's requests, ordered from the one you think should carry the most weight down to the least. For each, say in a sentence or two what it is and why it bites on this case specifically rather than on any old request for research regulation — and if one of your five is basically "your functionalism already commits you to this," say so and say how far the commitment actually runs. Numbered list, five items, one or two sentences apiece, no introduction and no wrap-up at the end. Thanks!
