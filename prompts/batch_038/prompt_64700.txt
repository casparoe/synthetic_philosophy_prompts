I am circulating a short position paper to a graduate seminar on the boundaries of the mind and want a consistency check beforehand. Here is what I claim to hold:

1. **Parity.** If a process occurring outside the head would be accepted without hesitation as cognitive had it occurred inside the head, then it is cognitive. Where the skull happens to sit makes no difference to the kind of thing cognition is.
2. **Otto and Inga.** The entries in Otto's notebook are among his non-occurrent beliefs, not merely cues that trigger internal recall. For explaining Otto's trip to the museum, Otto-plus-notebook rather than Otto's brain is the right unit of analysis.
3. **Constitution, not coupling.** External resources can be literal parts of a cognitive system; I refuse to retreat to the weaker claim that the notebook is just a very reliable input. But I also hold that content is fixed intrinsically: what the notebook's entries mean, and what Otto's beliefs are about, is settled entirely by his internal states.
4. **Glue and trust.** An external resource counts as part of the mind only if it is reliably available, typically invoked, and its contents automatically endorsed — the agent does not stand back and scrutinize them. This is my main bulwark against the objection that everything an agent touches becomes part of her cognition.
5. I also want to say of a diligent researcher who cross-checks every item her phone surfaces against independent sources, and who would readily abandon any of them on contrary evidence, that her phone is a genuine part of her memory — arguably a better one than Otto's notebook, which he never questions.
6. I deny that experience extends: there is nothing it is like to be a coupled system in the way there is something it is like to be Otto. And I deny group minds: no corporation or research team is a believing subject in the way Otto is.
7. I take the neural evidence seriously — lesion and imaging work situating episodic memory in the hippocampus and medial temporal lobe — and regard it as fully consistent with my extensionism.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look as though they do? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say precisely what would have to give and what the cheapest repair costs you downstream. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, rather than listing every defect as equally bad.
