I cover law, science, and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I've been trying to work out what I actually believe about when a state may punish speech, because my instincts keep straddling the Atlantic. Here are my verdicts as I've recorded them.

A neo-Nazi group marching through a town of Holocaust survivors: protected, and the courts were right to permit it. A cross burned on a Black family's lawn: punishable, where the aim is to intimidate. Holocaust denial published in Germany: punishable, and I think the German statute gets something right. A British man's obviously jokey tweet about blowing up an airport: his conviction was a disgrace and the appeal court was right to quash it. A French comedian whose act turns on gestures with antisemitic associations: I've come round to accepting the bans on his shows. The Danish newspaper's cartoons of Muhammad: lawful, correctly left uncharged, and still a bad decision to publish. A tenured academic's article arguing that some groups are less intelligent: I'd defend his right to publish it in either jurisdiction. An AI-generated video of a candidate pocketing a bribe, released four days before polling day: I want that criminally punishable, and I can't get there from anything above.

My working rule is that speech loses protection when it is aimed at a particular person as a weapon rather than at an audience as a claim. It handles the cross and the academic, fails on the German case and the deepfake, and cannot explain why the comedian differs from the tweeter — though I'm certain he does.

Give me a principle, or the smallest set of them, that earns the most of these verdicts. I want you to commit: adopt one, state its clauses and the case each clause exists for, say what it rules in cases I haven't raised — a Swiss conviction for denying the Armenian genocide, a platform that algorithmically amplifies a slur without composing it — and name the verdict of mine it forces me to abandon. Please don't survey the American and European traditions and leave the choice to me.
