ok so I want a joke about the problem of old evidence in Bayesian confirmation theory — the one where a theory seems hugely confirmed by data it retroactively explains, except the scientist already knew the data, so conditonalizing on it does literally nothing because its probability is already 1 and the likelihood ratio comes out flat. Einstein's theory getting credit for Mercury's weird orbit is the classic case.

- the punchline has to turn on the mechanism itself: the evidence being locked inside the total evidence already, or the embarassing workarounds people reach for (pretending you never learned E, partial/soft updating, treating the new *theory* as the surprising thing). Someone who knows this literature should go "ugh, that's exactly it."

- no citations, no book titles, no invented references. You can use people who are already part of the story of the problem.
- bonus points if there's a short tag after the punchline that makes things strictly worse for the Bayesian.
