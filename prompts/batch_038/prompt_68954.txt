I'm opening a talk on moral bioenhancement to a room that's mostly bioethicists, and mostly convinced that testing or building virtue with a pill is a brand new idea. I want a short joke to land the fact that Plato already ran this proposal — real stand-up-able material, three or four lines max, not a paragraph.

The drug in question is wine, and it's the first book of the Laws. The punchline has to turn on what the Athenian Stranger's argument actually is: wine is useful precisely because it produces the state of soul without any of the goods that would normally justify it, so the man who keeps his character intact under it has been tested cheaply and safely — and the hypothetical he raises about a drug that would induce fear with nothing frightening present, which would be an even better bargain for a legislator, except that no such thing exists. Ideally there's a beat about the fact that nobody's allowed to drink except under a sober supervisor. If you could swap "wine" for "money" or "power" and the joke still worked, its not the joke I want. And please no Plato-walks-into-a-bar routine — that crowd has heard every version of it, and so have I.
