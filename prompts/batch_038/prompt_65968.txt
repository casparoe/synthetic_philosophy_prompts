ok so I've been asked to write down my actual views on fakes and originals for a panel, and before I say them out loud I want someone to check whether they hang together. Here's the list:

1. Aesthetic value supervenes on what's perceivable. If two objects are visually indistinguishable, they're aesthetically on a par. I don't believe there's magic in the canvas.
2. But when I stand in front of an original I get something I don't get from a copy, and I trust that feeling as tracking a real difference, even though I can't point to anything on the surface that accounts for it.
3. Van Meegeren's Supper at Emmaus, the fake Vermeer: once it was exposed its value collapsed for me, and I think that collapse is the correct response, not an error to be argued out of.
4. What makes forgery bad is the deception, the intent to pass something off. The wrong is in the act.
5. Even so, a forgery that everyone now knows is a forgery is still aesthetically worse than the thing it copies. Being a famous unmasked fake doesn't rehabilitate it.
6. I'm a historicist about works: the art-historical situation something was made in is partly constitutive of what the work is, so a visually identical canvas painted in 1936 rather than the 1660s is a different work, and a derivative one rather than an inventive one.
7. On restoration I'd strip nineteenth-century overpaint off a damaged old master panel, because that isn't the artist's hand and it's forgery of a sort. But I would not remove the patina from a bronze, because what people have actually lived with and loved for a century has become part of the work.
8. And a workshop copy made under the artist's own supervision strikes me as totally fine and worth having. No problem there.

Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do, ranked by severity rather than all lumped together. For each real tension say what has to give and what I lose by giving it up, and for each merely apparent one say what dissolves it. Then tell me the single revision that buys the most consistency at the least cost. I don't have a favorite among these, so don't assume I'm attached to any of them.
