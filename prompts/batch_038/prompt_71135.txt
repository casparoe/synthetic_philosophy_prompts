I'm in a reading group that's been going around in circles on WWI responsibility for months — Fischer, the twenties revisionists, Clark's sleepwalkers, the whole thing — and lately one of us has been leaning hard on a tort analogy to try to close it out. The July crisis is like a multi-car pileup on a foggy motorway: nobody meant it, everybody did something careless, and afterwards you don't ask whose fault the crash was, you apportion it — contributory negligence, percentages. On that picture "Germany started the war" is just a sloppy way of saying Germany was the largest contributor, and the Fischer controvery was really an argument about percentages dressed up as an argument about causes.

Audit the analogy for me. Correspondence table first: what's the fog, what's the road, what plays the role of the highway code, where is the single harm that gets divided up, who's the loss adjuster and where does their standing come from. Then the disanalogies, and I want them sorted into the ones that actually bite on the use we're putting it to versus the ones that are merely interesting. Four I'm already suspicious about: whether a pileup even has one harm when the 1914 outcomes were different for each participant (Russia loses an empire, Belgium gets overrun, Germany loses a war it plausibly could have won); whether but-for causation buys you anything when historians say a general European war was coming regardless of who lit it; whether pileup drivers can see each other and adjust in real time while a general staff in July 1914 is working off three-day-old telegrams and a railway timetable it can't switch off mid-run; and above all whether "fault" means anything at all without a body of rules every party accepts the authority of, which the highway code hands you and the pre-1914 alliance system arguably doesn't.

Then the payoff question: does the conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table? Is "Germany was X% responsible" a claim the analogy can actually underwrite, or was the analogy carrying the entire idea that responsibility is quantifiable in the first place? While you're at it, does the sleepwalker image fare any better, or is it doing the same trick from the other direction — making blame look inapplicable rather than divisible? And where does that leave Article 231: the Allies wrote a legal finding of responsibility into the treaty itself, so does the fact that they reached for legal language make the tort frame more apt, or does it just show legal vocabulary being used to do non-legal work?

Finish with what a better analogy would be if there is one — bar fight, poker game where one player keeps escalating, a misdiagnosis — or with the honest verdict that this whole family of analogies should be retired and what you'd put in its place instead. No bibliography, I know the literature, I just want the mapping and the audit.
