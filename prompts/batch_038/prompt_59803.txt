Background: I'm on the bargaining team for our grad student union and in about two weeks we have to decide whether to announce a hard public floor ("no deal under 12%") before the next session. Last week two people argued about exactly this at a bar and I still cant tell who was right — Miriam, who's on the team with me, and Theo, a friend of ours who does negotiation stuff at the business school. Roughly how it went:

1. Theo: You should've announced the 12% floor before session one. In chicken, the guy who throws his steering wheel out the window wins. A public, irreversible commitment is the only thing that makes a threat more than noise.
2. Miriam: Thats backwards when they can do the same thing. Admin's got way more to gain from looking unbending in front of the trustees than we do, so if we throw our wheel out they throw theirs out and we've just raced each other into a crash. Whoever wins a commitment race usually wins the wreck.
3. Theo: So your alternative is no commitment at all? An uncommitted negotiator is just one who can be pushed around. If you've ruled nothing out, they know your threats are cheap talk.
4. Miriam: No — I'd rather commit in a way I can't unilaterally reverse. Our threat is credible because 900 members have to ratify anything, not because I said something to the student paper. Handing the commitment to a body that can vote me out is stronger than a quote with my name on it.
5. Theo: That's still a commitment, just a slow one, and slow means they can wait you out till the semester's over and everybody needs paychecks.
6. Miriam: Slow also means they can blink without us losing face, which is the entire point of the exercise.
