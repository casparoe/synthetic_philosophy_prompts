Orwell's claim about political language, in the strong form I take from Newspeak, is that vocabulary sets the outer bound of thinkable thought: if the words go, the thoughts go with them. I want to hold a sharpened version of this, which I'll call asymmetric lexical entrenchment — the thesis that destroying a word destroys the thought it made available, while acquiring a word does not install a thought, because a new label has nothing to grip until enough surrounding usage has accumulated around it. Note this is not the familiar strong Whorfian claim, and I think it sidesteps the usual refutations, since the color-term and numerical-cognition work all manipulates labels and measures performance, which is precisely the direction my thesis says should show little.

What would count against asymmetric lexical entrenchment? I want the observations, arguments, and results that ought to move me, ordered by how decisive they would be, including anything already in hand rather than merely conceivable. Three places I'd especially like pressure applied:

(a) Whether a thesis that predicts null results on every test designed to detect lexical effects has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and whether that is fatal here, or just the ordinary situation for claims about loss rather than gain, where the burden is different.

(b) What a genuine positive test would have to look like: what historical, developmental, or clinical evidence would show a thought becoming unavailable rather than merely costly or awkward to express, and how I would distinguish that from a thought that survives while its label dies.

(c) What Orwell's own apparatus commits me to that I may not want — doublethink in particular seems to require holding two contradictory beliefs knowingly, which looks like exactly the conceptual machinery Newspeak is supposed to remove, and I'm not sure my asymmetry rescues that.

For each, say whether a defender of the view would concede it as counting against them or dismiss it, and explain why. Close with an honest verdict on how exposed the position really is, and whether the asymmetry is a substantive refinement or a retreat.
