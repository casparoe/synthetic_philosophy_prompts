I'm building a casebook chapter on costly signalling and I want to use Fouquet's fall as the worked example — but not the obvious version of it, the "lavish party, jealous king, done" one. Here's the material I'm leaning on; if you think any of it's wrong, say so rather than quietly going along with it.

- Fouquet was Louis XIV's superintendent of finances; Mazarin died in March 1661 and the king decided to rule without a chief minister.
- He built Vaux-le-Vicomte over several years with Le Vau, Le Brun and Le Nôtre — reported cost around 16 million livres, three villages cleared for the grounds — and part of the château was built specifically for the king.
- On 17 August 1661 he threw the famous fête: Molière's Les Fâcheux premiered, Vatel ran the table, fireworks, the whole court present. Voltaire's later summary: "On 17 August, at six in the evening Fouquet was the King of France: at two in the morning he was nobody."
- Colbert had already been working on the king, arguing the magnificence could only have been paid for out of misappropriated public money. Fouquet was arrested at Nantes on 5 September by d'Artagnan. The trial ran three years; the judges voted for banishment; Louis overrode them with life imprisonment at Pignerol, where Fouquet died in 1680.
- Afterwards the estate was sequestered and the king seized, confiscated or bought 120 tapestries, the statues and every orange tree — and then took Le Vau, Le Nôtre and Le Brun to Versailles.

What I want from you:

1. Do the mapping properly. Sender, receiver, signal, the quality it's supposed to disclose, and what's meant to make the cost guarantee honesty. Then test the differential-cost condition directly: was a party like that really harder for a second-rate financier to throw than for Fouquet, or does it run backwards — the thing that made it cheap for him (the crown's murky accounts plus his own credit) being precisely what Colbert claimed it proved?

2. The audience problem. There's no single reciever here: the king, Colbert as a rival, the nobility, the parlement, the financiers and the artists all see the same display and read it differently. Does the handicap model have the resources for multiple receivers with conflicting payoffs, or does it silently assume a two-player structure that this case doesn't have?

3. The anomaly I actually care about. The signal seems to have been honest — it reliably conveyed that Fouquet had access to money he shouldn't have had. And that's what killed him. Work through why a theory built to explain why honest signals are good for the signaller produces this, and whether the fix is to say the model was never about the signaller's welfare, or that we've got the wrong model.

4. A coinage of mine I want pressure-tested. The standard picture assumes the cost is sunk and gone — the peacock's tail is metabolically spent, not recoverable. But Fouquet's display was sitting in a building 55km from Paris with an inventory inside it, and the receiver walked off with the tapestries, the statues, the orange trees and the artists. I've been calling this a confiscable handicap: the signal doubles as a warehouse, so its cost isn't really paid, it's just relocated. Is that a genuine hole in the theory, or is it already handled by things the signalling literature knows about — eavesdroppers, predators and parasites keying on displays, kleptoparasitism? Tell me which, and whether the biological analogies actually hold or just sound like they do.

5. Since part of Vaux was built for the king, is the fête better read as a gift than as a signal? Say what changes depending on which frame you use — obligation and reciprocity on one side, information transfer on the other — and whether the two give different predictions about Louis's response.

6. Versailles as the sequel. There's a familiar reading on which Louis deliberately made courtiership ruinously expensive in order to keep the nobility dependent and unable to fund trouble. Does that run better as a runaway/arms-race process with no information left in it, or as a handicap equilibrium where the cost still tracks something? Say what observation would discriminate between the two.

7. Finally, advice. If Fouquet had been counselled by the model, what should he have done — and is "signal less" even available to a man whose office is itself the object of suspicion, where restraint reads as concealment?

End with your verdict on what this episode does to the theory: confirmation, anomaly, or a case the framework never anticipated and would have to be extended to cover. And be disciplined about where the mapping just stops working — a court is not a mating market — rather than forcing it to say something. No bibliography, no reading list, no citations.
