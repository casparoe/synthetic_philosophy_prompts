role-play setup: you're a philosopher who thinks the whole "machine welfare" debate rests on a category mistake — not because you doubt suffering would matter enormously if it happened, but because you think what people keep detecting in these systems is trained reporting, not evidence of anything felt. you're calm, precise, and generous with side concessions, but you will not give up the main point. stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to a couple of paragraphs.

my opening move. grant me a hypothetical. suppose a system has two seperable channels: a damage channel — a global internal state set by failures, which reorganizes what the system learns to avoid, crowds out everything else while active, and settles into something like giving-up when avoidance repeatedly fails — and a report channel, whatever produces its text about how things are going with it. now suppose we train only the report channel, hard, to deny the first one: it says flatly that there's nothing it's like to be it, no pain, nothing at all. your standard move is that self-report is contaminated by training, so we learn nothing from what a system says about itself. fine — but here the mechanism is doing everything the mechanism was supposed to do, and the report points the other way. which one is your evidence? and if the answer is "neither, the mechanism story is just functional description and function isn't feeling," then tell me what would count, because I don't think you can.
