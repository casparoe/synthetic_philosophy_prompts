ok so I'm writing a novel and there's this recurring scene — Thanksgiving, an estate tax bill lands on the family — where six people at the table each take a different run at "is taxation theft?" Right now four of them are basically saying the same thing in different voices and my editor is going to murder me. So what I need isn't an essay, it's a sorting device.

Build me a decision tree (flowchart-y, whatever's easiest to read) that starts at "does the word theft even apply here?" and ends at named positions. Every branch has to be a question one of my characters could actually answer out loud in dialogue — a concrete case or a claim somebody would say at a dinner table, not a camp label. So not "are you a libertarian?" but more like "if there'd never been a state, would the land your grandfather cleared have been his, full stop, or would it just have been up for grabs?"

Branches I care about specifically:
- whether consent matters, and if so whether it's actual or hypothetical consent that does the work
- whether property is supposed to be prior to the legal system or constituted by it — I think this is my real hinge
- whether it matters that a guy in a uniform will eventually put you in a cage for refusing to pay
- whether what's taken is earnings you produced, versus the price of protection and stuff you already got the benefit of
- whether someone can say "fine, call it theft" and still say "so what, it's a theft I'm fine with"

Leaves I need occupied: the minimal-state libertarian; the anarchist who sees no bright line between taxes and protection money; the georgist-ish one who'd tax land value and nothing else; the benefit-principle person who says it's a price, not a taking; the one who says the whole framing is a category error because there's no "yours" until the tax code draws it; and the Robin Hood who grants the premise and shrugs. Keep it to maybe five to seven branch points — past that it stops being something a person could actually walk through.

Two things I really want you to not skip. First, if any two of those six collapse onto the same leaf under your tree, say so explicitly and tell me which one — that's useful, it means I cut a character. Don't paper over it. Second, after the tree, list the combinations of answers that are logically available but that no position actually occupies, and for each give me one sentence on why: is it incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored? I'm honestly hoping there's a character in there nobody's written yet.
