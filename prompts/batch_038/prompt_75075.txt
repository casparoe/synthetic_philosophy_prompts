so there's something happening at my school Tuesday and I'm trying to predict what my friend Sam will do about it, mostly because Sam has this whole theory he won't shut up about. He read like two game theory books over the summer and keeps using the word superrationality, which he says is from some old essay he can't remember the name of. Before I get to Tuesday, here's his track record, because I think you need it to figure out what he'll actually do:

- Two people in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, no talking beforehand, and both know the other is just as sharp and has thought about the same problems. Sam says cooperate. He gets mad when people call that naive — he says they'll both land on it because they're basically running the same reasoning twice.
- Same game, but the other guy is a known opportunist who's never thought about any of this. Defect, instantly, and he says cooperating there isn't noble, it's just wrong.
- Same game, but the opponent is a computer program that got handed the identical writeup of the problem. Cooperate.
- Voting. He votes in literally everything including the ones where his vote cannot possibly matter, and if you say "your vote doesn't matter" he gets genuinely irritated. He says his vote isn't a pebble in a pile, it's one copy of a decision a huge number of people are making at the same time.
- Recycling. He rinses out his containers and walks them to the bin behind the cafeteria where nobody's watching and nobody would ever find out. He says it's the exact same shape as voting.
- Lottery tickets. Irrational, no debate. And he insists this doesn't contradict the two above because "there's no group in the lottery, it's just you and the math."
- He's in a puzzle Discord with a few hundred people who are all basically him. Once everybody had to choose whether to burn an hour on a thread where nobody would get credit for it, and he did, and so did most of the regulars, and he calls that the cleanest real-life version of the thing he goes on about.
- He says he'd still vote and recycle even if it were way more annoying, like a 40 minute drive in the rain, but he admits there's some point where it stops being worth it and he can't tell me where that point is.

Okay, Tuesday. The school board votes that night on cutting the music program entirely, and there's a plan to walk out of first period and stand in the parking lot during the vote. A local news station said they'd send a crew if a few hundred kids actually come out. Sam plays trumpet, so he cares about this way more than he cares about basically anything.

Here's the problem though. The plan lives in one group chat with about 15 people in it. Most of the school hasn't even heard of it. Three of the 15 have already said they'll bail if it looks like nobody else is coming. And Sam knows for a fact that none of them are thinking about it the way he thinks about stuff — nobody in that chat is doing the "if I do it, everybody does it" move, they're just going off whatever their friends do. His own read, which he's said out loud: if he stays in his seat nothing changes at all, and if he walks out he's one of maybe 30 kids getting detention plus a note home plus missing test review, and the board doesn't blink.

But also — and this is the part I can't get him to engage with — there are definitely a lot of kids who'd want to walk out. The band has like 200 people in it and everybody's pissed, it's just that nobody's talking to anybody about it.

So what does Sam say Monday night? I want more than a coin flip on it. What rule is he actually running that makes all eight of those verdicts come out right? What other rules fit them just as well? What does each of those rules say about the walkout, and which one do you think wins — and I want the reasoning, not just the answer. Then give me a case I could put to him afterwards that would actually settle which rule he's on, because I've tried and he just says the walkout one is "obviously different" without saying how. I'm pretty sure he's being inconsistent but I can't prove it and I'd like to be able to.
