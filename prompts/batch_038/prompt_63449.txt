You're a philosopher of psychiatry who's also spent time in clinics, and I want you to referee an argument I watched and still can't call. Think carefully -- before any verdict, spend a few lines mapping where the two sides are actually disagreeing vs. just talking past each other.

Our community mental health clinic holds a case conference every Thursday, and last week two of the senior clinicians went at it over a proposal to add a new intake category, working name "failure to launch," for young adults who've been out of high school 18+ months with no job, no school, reversed sleep, and near-zero social contact, but who don't meet criteria for depression or psychosis.

(1) Dana said add it, because right now these kids get bounced to "other specified" and it unlocks nothing -- no housing voucher, no case management, no school re-entry program. (2) Ray said Dana was proving his point for him: the category's only recommendation is that it gets you services, and once the label exists the kids start organizing their self-understanding around it, dressing their lives in its vocabulary, which is just Hacking's looping effect with extra steps. (3) Dana said fine, it loops -- but the loop is the therapy; a kid who can say "this is a recognized thing that happens to people and there's a pathway out" is a kid who shows up to the appointment. (4) Ray said that assumes the label picks out a real cluster instead of a grab bag: some of these kids are depressed, some are on the autism spectrum and never assessed, some are just poor with no bus route, and lumping them together hides all of that and will make the intake data meaningless. (5) Dana said bipolar and ADHD were grab bags at first too, and nobody now thinks that made them illegitimate. (6) Ray said that proves too much -- if every category is allowed to make its own object, then psychiatry isn't tracking anything and the debate collapses into which loops you feel like starting. (7) Dana said yes, that's basically it, some loops are worth starting, and the question isn't whether you'll create kinds but whether you'll do it deliberately or by accident. Ray then said the withdrawal was there before anyone named it -- families described it for years without a label -- and Dana answered that the families started describing it that way only after our intake coordinator began writing the term in the free-text field, so the loop was already running and the proposal just catches up to it.

Go move by move, 1 through 7: rate each one from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and say briefly why.
