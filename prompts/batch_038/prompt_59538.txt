I retired four years ago after thirty-eight years in physical chemistry — reaction kinetics, with a long and lucrative detour into calorimetry — and I have spent the retirement doing what I ought to have done at twenty-five, namely reading philosophy seriously rather than in the margins of grant applications. My present obsession is Maxwell's demon, which I had always assumed was a solved problem, in the way that a chemist assumes a textbook footnote. It is not. The literature is vast and, more troublingly, the participants do not appear to agree about what would count as settling the question at all: some insist that any adequate account must locate a compensating entropy increase somewhere in the physical world, others insist that the whole business of locating it is a category mistake and that the second law needs no such rescue, and still others argue that the standard exorcism works only for the handful of demons its authors had in mind. Before I commit another year to this, I want the specification rather than the candidates. Act, if you will, as a philosopher of physics who has also done the statistical mechanics, and tell me what any proposed resolution or dissolution of the demon problem must deliver in order to count as adequate: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the results it must not contradict, and the considerations behind each. Since adequacy is relative to purpose, fix the purpose as follows — I want an account good enough that I could state it in a public lecture without asserting a falsehood, and good enough that an experimentalist building a nanoscale feedback device could design against it.

To keep myself honest I have built a device on paper, and I should like your list to be able to adjudicate it. Take the familiar one-molecule engine: a box bisected by a partition with a trapdoor, and a single molecule in thermal contact with a bath at temperature T. The door is not opened by an observer but by a bistable latch — a particle in a double-well potential whose well-index is the demon's one bit — coupled mechanically to a sensor that registers which half the molecule occupies. Here is the twist I introduced. The latch is never logically erased; instead, at the end of each cycle the memory bit is coupled to a fresh, small, pre-cooled auxiliary body and allowed to relax into it, so that the bit returns spontaneously to its blank state while dissipating into something much colder than T. The auxiliary bodies are finite and consumable, drawn from a stream that crosses the boundary of my system from outside, and the stream will eventually run out. My device therefore appears to satisfy, for as long as the cold stock lasts, every condition I know how to state, while producing net work from a single heat bath. A second variant troubles me differently: a purely mechanical ratchet and pawl, at a uniform temperature, in which there is no bit anywhere in the description, so that no account appealing to the cost of information can even get a grip on why it fails. I mention these because I suspect that a great deal of the disagreement in this literature is really disagreement about what the boundary of the system is, and about whether a cycle that pays for itself out of an uncounted finite resource is a violation of the second law or merely a deferral of it. Your desiderata should tell me whether that suspicion identifies a genuine requirement, and if so, how to state it.

Think carefully, and begin with a short piece of brainstorming: an inventory of the distinct ways in which the second law might fail to be threatened by such devices at all, and of the distinct places a compensating cost might have to be paid, before you commit to any list. Then give me the requirements, numbered, each with the case or consideration that motivates it. In particular I want the distinctions that must be respected stated explicitly — between thermodynamic entropy, Shannon entropy, algorithmic complexity, and mutual information; between logical irreversibility and thermodynamic irreversibility; between the cost of acquiring a measurement record, the cost of discarding it, and the work credit obtainable by acting on it; between what holds on average over an ensemble and what holds for a single run; and between a demon conceived as an intelligent agent outside the physics and a demon conceived as one more physical subsystem obeying the same microscopic dynamics. Say likewise what empirical and theoretical results an adequate account must be consistent with — fluctuation theorems and the generalized second-law inequalities that credit a feedback controller with the mutual information it acquires, the experimental demonstrations of work extraction bounded by an information term, the ordinary successes of equilibrium statistical mechanics for finite systems, and the brute macroscopic fact that nobody has ever built the thing. Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp, and say frankly where a purported requirement is really a camp's metaphysics wearing the clothes of a constraint. Then address what I most want to know: whether some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. My own conjecture is that the four conditions — a finite physical memory, exact cyclicity of the whole apparatus including that memory, deterministic and microscopically reversible dynamics, and net work drawn from a single heat bath — are precisely the contradiction, and that every so-called resolution is nothing more than a principled choice about which of the four to loosen. If you agree, show the reasoning; if you disagree, tell me which of my four is not what I think it is, and whether some other pair does the excluding. Identify, finally, the single requirement that knocks out the greatest number of proposals. Only after all of that, and briefly, tell me how the main families of response measure up against your list — accounts that charge the cost to the act of measurement, accounts that charge it to the erasure of memory, the dissenters who hold that no such account can be general enough to exorcise every possible demon, and the modern stochastic-thermodynamic treatments of feedback — a compact table is perfectly acceptable here. Please do not supply a bibliography, a reading list, or any citations; I can find the papers myself, and a reference I cannot check is worse than none. What I want is your reasoning laid out in full.
