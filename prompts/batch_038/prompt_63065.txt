I've spent about twenty years doing reservoir simulation and history matching — geomodels, ensembles of realizations, P10/P50/P90 ranges, analogue fields, value-of-information studies before we commit to a well location. No philosophy training and I dont want any.

But I keep running into this argument from philosophers of physics (Dawid mostly, plus the people who run with him) that string theory can be "confirmed" without any new experiment. As I understand it there are roughly three moves: (1) nobody has managed to build a rival that does what string theory does, and that failure to find alternatives is itself supposed to be evidence for it; (2) theories that people trusted on non-empirical grounds in the past usually survived once the data finally arrived, so that track record licenses trusting a theory again before the data; (3) a theory built for one job that unexpectedly ties together unrelated problems picks up support from the coherence. And then the landscape/measure business on top: in a multiverse you can't predict anything until you've chosen a weighting over the possible universes, and no data picks the weighting.

Restate all four of those in my vocabulary — non-uniqueness of the inverse problem, history matching, prior and ensemble weighting, the analogue argument, the next well — as faithfully as you can, and don't quietly import the philosophers' assumptions as if they were part of the translation. Then give me the residue report: which pieces map cleanly, which you had to bend to make fit, and which my language can't express at all. The bit I most want: in a field where every model eventually gets checked against the next well, is "will never be tested" even sayable? And if it isn't, is that a blind spot in how I think, or a sign that what they're arguing about isn't a real disagreement?
