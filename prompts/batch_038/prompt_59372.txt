I am convening a two-day workshop for a public-policy institute on the question of whether the Nordic model travels. The participants have spent three years talking past each other, and the opening session will consist of reading aloud a joint statement that members of both camps will put their names to. Please draft that statement.

The two camps, in their own preferred formulations:

**Camp A — institutional exportability.** Nordic outcomes (compressed income distribution, high employment including among women, high measured generalized trust, and fiscal systems that have so far sustained large redistributive flows) are produced by institutional choices: universal rather than means-tested benefit design; light employment protection paired with generous income replacement and active labour-market policy; coordinated wage bargaining underwritten by high union density, which in several of these countries is itself mechanically sustained by unions administering unemployment insurance; a tax mix that leans on broad-based consumption and payroll taxation rather than on high marginal rates alone; and revenue collection that depends on third-party reporting of income by employers and financial institutions rather than on voluntary self-assessment. On this view none of these choices presupposes a distinctive national character, each is in principle legislable by another affluent democracy, and the main causal arrows run from institutions to attitudes rather than the reverse.

**Camp B — endowments and preconditions.** The same institutions are as much a symptom as a cause, and they emerged from conditions that cannot be legislated into existence: an unusually compressed pre-industrial class structure, with a large free peasantry and, in some cases, no deeply entrenched landed aristocracy; an early state church that produced mass literacy and an administrative cadre before industrialization; small, linguistically homogeneous, administratively continuous populations; exposure as small open economies, which raised the demand for social insurance instead of disciplining it away; and a stock of generalized trust that predates the postwar welfare state and that would be drawn down, not replenished, by transplanting Nordic spending levels into a society that lacks the underlying stock.

Neither camp should be rendered as a strawman of the other, and the statement must not adjudicate between them.

Please produce:

1. **Agreed claims**, numbered, of the kind both camps can sign. Aim for eight to twelve. Each must be non-vacuous: something a third party outside both camps would be inclined to deny, or something that does real inferential work later in the document. Include at least two claims about shared methodological commitments (for instance, about what would count as a legitimate comparison across countries, or about how to treat self-reported survey measures of trust) and at least one claim about the descriptive facts both camps accept regarding the Nordic countries and their contrast cases. Where an agreed claim constrains a crux listed below, flag that in the entry itself.

2. **A disagreements table**, with exactly these columns: `ID` | `As Camp A states it` | `As Camp B states it` | `Neutral formulation both would sign` | `Genuine or verbal` | `What turns on the distinction`. Six to nine rows. The wording in each side's own column must be wording that side would accept as accurate; the neutral formulation must not smuggle in either side's theory. For rows you judge verbal, say which ambiguity is doing the work.

3. **Cruxes.** Between four and six sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one camp. For each, state the question, the kind of evidence or argument both camps accept as bearing on it, and each camp's prediction about what that evidence shows, recorded in advance. My own hunches about where the cruxes lie, which you may adopt, revise, or reject with reasons: the direction of causation between generalized trust and universal, non-means-tested benefit design; whether comparisons between people of Nordic ancestry living in the United States, Canada, or Australia and people living in the Nordic countries can license any inference about institutions versus culture, and if so what it would take to make that comparison interpretable; whether the transferable content of the model is its financing and administration rather than its headline generosity; and whether the operative variable behind trust erosion in higher-immigration settings is heterogeneity as such or something adjacent to it, such as segregation, labour-market integration of newcomers, or the resulting inequality.

4. **A short section identifying the part of the dispute that is merely verbal.** I suspect the word "exportable" is carrying at least three distinct claims — that another country could adopt the institutions by legislative choice; that adopting them would reproduce comparable outcomes; and that adoption is feasible given the importing country's actual political constraints — and that much of the argument collapses once these are separated, though not all of it. Say which of the three each camp is really asserting, and what substantive disagreement survives the disambiguation.

Constraints: no citations, no bibliography, no named researchers, studies, or datasets, and no invented percentages or country-level figures. Where a crux turns on an empirical matter, describe the kind of evidence and the inference it would have to support. Do not resolve the debate, and do not offer a synthesis that dissolves the disagreement — the residual disagreement must survive the drafting, made exact. The joint statement should run roughly 1,100 to 1,500 words.

After the statement, give a structured summary as a single JSON object with two top-level keys. Under `cruxes`, an array of objects with the fields `id`, `question`, `kind` (one of `causal-direction`, `mechanism`, `measurement`, `interpretation-of-evidence`, `feasibility`), `why_it_moves_at_least_one_camp`, `evidence_both_accept`, `camp_a_prediction`, `camp_b_prediction`, and `what_would_make_one_side_concede`. Under `verbal_disputes`, an array of objects with the fields `id`, `contested_term`, `sense_a`, `sense_b`, `which_sense_the_argument_uses`, and `residual_substantive_question`. Every field should be filled with a single sentence or a short phrase, and nothing in the JSON should introduce content absent from the statement.
