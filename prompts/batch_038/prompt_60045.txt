In my philosophy of mind seminar we've spent two weeks on panpsychism and one sentence keeps doing all the heavy lifting: 'physics tells us what matter *does*, not what it *is*.' A classmate used it in a response paper to argue that since physics is silent about intrinsic nature, and something has to play that role, and the only intrinsic natures we're acquainted with are experiential ones, you're basically pushed to panpsychism. Our TA wrote 'interesting, but the premise is doing way more work than you think' and didn't explain further.

Can you lay out the distinct claims that slogan could actually be making? I can vaguely see at least a methodological one about how scientific theories abstract, a metaphysical one about whether things have intrinsic natures at all, something about causation being the only way physics pins down properties, and whatever version sits underneath Russellian monism — but I'm sure I'm missing readings and conflating others. Sort them from platitude to substantive-but-defensible to flatly false, and for each one say whether it licenses the jump to 'so phenomenal properties must be the intrinsic nature of the fundamental stuff,' and if not, exactly where the gap opens up. Also: what happens to the slogan if you deny there are any intrinsic natures in the first place — does the structuralist option just dissolve the whole argument, or does it leave something behind that still troubles physicalism?
