I spent thirty-eight years in experimental physics, where the working question is always which variables are actually doing the causal work, and I have lately brought that itch to the historiography of the French Revolution. I should like a decision tree that sorts a reader among the competing explanations of why the Revolution turned into the Terror — an instrument for a small reading group of former colleagues, not a survey of schools.

Please build it from the logical space rather than from labels, so that no node asks which camp one belongs to. Six to eight nodes, each a counterfactual intervention or a claim about causal structure that a reader can answer cold: what would have followed had war on Austria never been declared in 1792; had the king not fled; had the same men governed through an ordinary harvest; whether the concentration of unchecked power in a single assembly was a design fault with predictable dynamics or a by-product of the emergency; whether ideology operated as a cause or as an after-the-fact rationalization; whether an account that deduces the Terror from the principles of 1789 is thereby vindicated or merely unfalsifiable; and whether any dynamic identified should be expected in revolutions generally, or only in this one.

Then locate the leaves: structural necessity, of the sort that holds revolutions devour their own children; emergency response; ideological causation; class interest with ideology as mask; institutional design failure; and irreducible contingency — plus any leaf I have missed. For each, one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Mark the answer-combinations your tree permits that no position occupies, saying whether each is incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves it cannot in principle separate.
