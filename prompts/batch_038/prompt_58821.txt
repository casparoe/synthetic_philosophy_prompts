I'm working on my dissertation chapter on luck egalitarianism and I've landed somewhere a bit idiosyncractic, so I want to stress-test it before I commit. My view: the only thing that generates a claim for compensation is the absence of agential control at the moment of the relevant choice — I think the brute luck / option luck distinction is too coarse to carry any real weight and I've basically dropped it — and I top the whole thing off with a sufficientarian floor that I explicitly claim is doing no egalitarian work at all. What would count against this? Rank the defeaters from "chapter is dead" to "awkward footnote." In particular: does the control condition just smuggle the abandonment objection back in, given that no-control cases are exactly the ones where we most want to intervene and my floor isn't itself responsive to how badly someone was treated? Does the floor end up doing all the normative work — i.e. have I built something that collapses into prioritarianism with a responsibility-tracking decoration on it? Is the point that institutions manufacture which choices count as risky, and at what odds, so nothing is ever purely option luck, fatal or merely awkward? And is there anything from the empirical literature on how ordinary people actually apportion blame between circumstances and choices that should move me, either direction? Finally, tell me what my view predicts that its rivals don't, and where you think I've quietly insulated it so that it can't actually fail — and whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind.
