Two people in my department — a committed foundationalist and a committed infinitist — have been trading the same three objections at each other since I started here, and next month I'm chairing a session where they've agreed to try an adversarial collaboration. I want to hand them the document they should be aiming at. Write it as if the collaboration already went well.

Start with the claims both can sign. Presumably they agree about what the regress problem even is, that plenty of beliefs are held on the basis of others, that a chain of reasons which literally loops back on itself isn't justifying anything, and that a belief held for no reason at all isn't justified either. Get those down in wording neither side would want to walk back later. Then the disagreements, each phrased so its own camp would accept the description of its own view — I think the live one is about what "having a reason" requires, i.e. whether the backing belief has to be there occurent, in the subject's actual doxastic economy, or whether it's enough that a further reason would be produced on request however far down you push. The foundationalist will say the infinitist is writing promissory notes that never get paid; the infinitist will say the foundationalist's basic beliefs are just the spot where the questioning is stopped by decree.

Here's the case I want them both to react to, because I think it's the crux and neither has engaged it: suppose there's an archive — a person, a lab, a running system, doesn't matter — that holds no finite stock of reasons but can, for any belief you point at, produce a further supporting belief on demand, indefinitely, with no repeats and no last entry. Call the resulting state justification in motion, as against justification at rest. Does the archive's keeper have justified beliefs, and if not, what exactly is missing? Have each side write down its answer before seeing the other's.

For every crux, name the argument or case both agree bears on it and record each side's prediction about what it'll show. Along the way flag which of the agreed claims a coherentist would also sign, since that's how we'll see where the real fault line runs. And if some of this turns out to be an argument about words, say which part and why.

Plain flowing prose throughout, like a letter the two of them co-signed — no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables. If you need to separate the agreed claims, do it inside sentences ("first, we both accept that...").
