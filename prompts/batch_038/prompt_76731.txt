I've been auditing a grad seminar on Nyāya epistemology and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the rule our instructor runs when she sorts cognitions into her four sources. She's a hardliner: perception, inference, comparison, testimony — full stop. Anything the Mīmāṃsā folks want to add on top (postulation, non-apprehension) gets folded into those four, every single time, and she's pretty smug about it.

Her verdicts so far:

- "This man is stout and well-fed, but nobody ever sees him eat during the day, so he must eat at night." → inference. A student objected that we've never surveyed enough cases to know any such general rule; she said the rule is there, look at how food intake tracks body size, and moved on.
- "Devadatta is alive but he isn't in the house, so he must be outside." → inference, no discussion, she treated it as the same shape of case.
- You walk into the kitchen, the counter is bare, you think "there's no pot here." → perception, flatly. No separate source for absences.
- Someone in the village tells you a gavaya is a wild animal like a cow; weeks later you're in the forest, see one, and think "that's the gavaya." → this one she refuses to reduce. The forest cognition isn't available from the words alone, and it isn't mark-based either, since you weren't tracking any sign.
- Smoke on the far side of the hill → fire. Knowledge, she says, even if you never walk over to check; if the pervasion is genuine the cognition already counts, and confirmation is only reassurance for the nervous.
- A villager who knows the terrain tells you where the well is, and you find it → testimony, and knowledge.
- Same content, same truth, but it came from someone guessing who has no idea what they're talking about → not knowledge. Her line was that the source has to be a reliable speaker, not that the content has to come out right.
- Rope taken for a snake → not knowledge at all. Memory and doubt also excluded.

Next week's case is mine to present. Astronomers find a planet wobbling in a way nothing they know of accounts for, and they conclude an unseen body out there is perturbing it. I'm going to give it to her in two versions. Version A: ten earlier wobbles of that kind have each turned out, years later, to be an unseen planet. Version B: this is the first wobble anybody has ever looked at, there's no record of past confirmations, and the body they posit is unobservable with anything they currently have or could build soon.

So: which version does she call inference, does either drop below knowledge, and does B finally make her concede postulation as its own source or does she fold it in anyway? I want the candidate rules her track record is consistent with — at least two that really do differ, not just restatements — what each one predicts for A and for B, your best guess with the reasoning behind it, which of her earlier cases is the closest analog to B (I suspect people will disagree about whether that's the smoke case, the guessing-villager case, or the gavaya case, and I want to know why you pick the one you do), what she'd be committing herself to with each possible answer, and one further case that would cleanly separate the surviving rules.

Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence and then give the reasoning — no preamble, and don't restate my question back at me. Also tell me how confident you are in the overall prediction and single out the step you're least sure about.
