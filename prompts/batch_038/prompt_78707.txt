I'm setting the final for a graduate seminar on non-classical logic and I keep hitting the same wall: students can recite the liar, define dialetheism, and say "paraconsistent means you drop explosion," but they can't tell me what actually committing to a formal apparatus commits them to philosophically. So I want one essay question, answerable in about 90 minutes, worth 20 marks, that turns on a distinction they always blur — the one between choosing a consequence relation (which values you designate) and making a claim about which values sentences actually get. K3 and LP run off the same truth-tables, FDE permits both gaps and gluts as values, and yet none of that by itself settles whether anyone should believe there's a true contradiction out there. I'd like the question to force them to say what adopting a paraconsistent logic does and doesn't commit you to, and to include one sub-part that corners the student who shrugs and says "fine, I'll just stay agnostic between the gap reading and the glut reading" — what does that agnosticism cost you when you have to say which inferences you accept, and can you still do semantics?

Please write the question itself, a model answer of around 700 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points, marks for each totalling 20, plus a one-line note on the most common way candidates fluff each one.
