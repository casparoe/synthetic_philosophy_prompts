Write a customer-support FAQ page, roughly 550 words in numbered Q&A entries, for a smart-home assistant whose latest firmware update has replaced its single-number confidence scores with sets of probability measures. The humor should come entirely from the theory being applied honestly rather than from stock AI jokes. Entries should address: why the forecast interval for tomorrow's rain grew wider after the device consulted a second, less reliable model; why the assistant now declines to recommend any particular action on the grounds that several incompatible options are each admissible relative to at least one measure in the set, and why switching to a maximin-style tiebreaker provoked a complaint from a customer who prefers admissibility; why a year of accumulated data has left its initial vacuous prior exactly where it started; and a reply to a competitor's advertisement alleging that interval-valued confidence is just mushy single-number credence in disguise rather than a genuine plurality of sharp opinions. Beneath each entry, include the short plain-English gloss that help pages conventionally offer, and let the gloss be where the irony leaks. Keep the corporate-helpful voice deadpan throughout: a reader should sense that the company is being ironized without the piece ever admitting it, and a specialist should recognize every technical move as one the framework actually licenses.
