I cover science and ideas for a magazine. Before this job I spent three years in a very selective fellowship for young writers, and the thing you got picked for and praised for there was getting the concept exactly right — a piece that made a physicist say "yes, that's precisely it." I was good at it. Now I'm on staff and the job is different: find the story before anyone else does, sit through the boring county meeting, pull the document. And here's what's bothering me — I still do the old thing, all day, without ever deciding to. I'll spend two days perfecting 800 words about a result everybody else already covered, editors say "beautiful," and it goes nowhere. When I try to make myself do the other kind of work I feel nothing, no pull whatsoever, like the machinery just doesn't run on that fuel.

So I'm not really asking for career advice. I'm asking: was the fellowship version of me ever actually after good journalism, or was it after the approval the whole time, and I'm only finding out now becuase the two came apart? How do I tell the difference between something that broke when the situation changed and something that was never there to begin with — is there a test I can run on myself that isn't just asking myself how I feel? And if the answer is the bad one, is there anything to do about it that isn't white-knuckling?
