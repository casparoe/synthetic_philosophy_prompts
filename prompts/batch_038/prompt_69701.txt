For about four years I've been keeping a spreadsheet for a little group I'm in. Nine of us, all total amateurs, and every evening we each separately write down yes or no on whether it'll rain here in town tomorrow. No talking it over first, we just type our answer into a shared doc and the group's answer is whatever the majority said. Then I check us against the weather station up on the ridge and log it.

Here's the thing I can't find a word for. Nobody in the group is any good on their own. Individually we are all under 50 percent — best of us is 47, worst is 38, me somewhere in the middle of the bad part. That's worse than flipping a coin. But the group majority comes out right about 71 percent of the time, and it's been that steady for years.

I went digging into how that's even arithmetically possible and I think it's because our misses don't line up. There's roughly a camp that calls rain at the slightest bit of cloud and a camp that calls dry unless it's practically already pouring, and when I go down the columns day by day, on almost every day where somebody is wrong, somebody else is right in a way that cancels it. Days where most of us are wrong together are rare, like one in ten.

And here's what convinced me I'm looking at something specific and not just "groups are smart." Two years ago I added my brother-in-law, who is the single worst forecaster we've ever had on his own, 38 percent, calls dry constantly. The group score went UP, from about 68 to 71. Then his mate joined, a guy who makes basically the identical mistakes at the identical times, and we fell off a cliff to 59 percent and stayed there till they both drifted off. Adding a worse person helped. Adding an equally bad person in the same direction wrecked it.

I know the popular versions of this territory. I know the story about the villagers guessing the weight of an ox and the average coming out right — but that's averaging numbers and it seems to assume each guess is at least in the neighborhood. And I've half-heard of some theorem about a jury being more likely to get it right than one juror, but the way people tell it, it only works when each juror is more often right than wrong, and it gets better as the jury gets bigger. Both of those are the exact opposite of what I'm watching. Ours gets worse when it grows the wrong way, and not one of us beats a coin flip.

So does this have a name? I want the nearest things people have actually written down, and for each one I want you to say how far off it is from what I've described, because I'm pretty sure the popular candidates don't reach it. And if there genuinely isn't a term for it, make one up and define it properly — I care way less about the label than about where the edges are. Does it still count with three camps instead of two? What if the camps are lopsided in size? Is the one-day-in-ten where we all miss together part of the same phenomenon or a different failure wearing its clothes? Does it need a yes/no question or would it work with numbers? What happens the moment we're allowed to talk before submitting?

Last thing: tell me how confident you are that I haven't just described something that already has a name and I'm about to be handed a fancy word for a known result. Say what would change your mind — which detail, if I've got it wrong or told it to you differently, would collapse this into something standard, and where your reasoning about it is most likely to break.
