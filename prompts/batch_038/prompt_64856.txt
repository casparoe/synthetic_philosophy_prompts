Did my service in Finland a while back and I've kept following the conscription arguments ever since, mostly newspapers and forums, so no academic training here. There's a pattern I keep running into and I can't find a word for it.

The case for the draft used to be simple: a small country can only deter an invasion if a big chunk of its citizens are trained, so take everyone. When that threat looked like it was fading, the people who supported it didn't change their minds -- new reasons showed up instead. It trains people for floods and search and rescue. It's the one place where immigrants and rural kids and Helsinki kids meet. It teaches young men to turn up on time. It gives the reserve system its signals and cyber people. Sweden switched it off and then back on again and both times it was more or less the same list of reasons being used, just with a different one on top. Each reason seems to arrive right about when the previous one is losing force, and none of them on its own looks heavy enough to justify taking a year of a whole cohort's life, but there are always four or five going at once so the case never actually runs out. When I ask someone which is the real reason they say all of them, and that the institution has turned out to do things nobody intended when it was set up.

So: is there a name for this, and does the literature have anything that fits the whole shape rather than a piece of it? The near misses I've found all seem off. Mission creep sounds like the institution changing what it does, but what it does barely changed, only the story. Goal displacement sounds like the means taking over, which is maybe half of it. Post-hoc rationalisation is too individual -- this is a whole public debate doing it for decades in several countries, and the reasons aren't invented, they're real benefits that just don't seem to be doing the load-bearing. Something about latent functions? I don't want a label slapped on it. I want to know how you'd tell this apart from an institution that genuinely has a bunch of good side effects and is honestly listing them, and seperately whether the pile-up structure -- lots of weak reasons that get replenished as they're spent -- is itself a recognised thing with its own name.
