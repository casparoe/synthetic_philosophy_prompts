So here's a view I've talked myself into about dispositions, and I want it properly stress-tested rather than summarized back at me.

Part one: the fink/mask material doesn't refute the conditional analysis of dispositions, it just refutes the lazy version of it. If you formulate the analysis so that it quantifies over the object's intrinsic basis and holds that basis fixed in the counterfactual — something along the lines of Lewis's reformed analysis in "Finkish Dispositions" — then Martin's rigged wire and Bird's antidote cases stop looking like counterexamples and start looking like scenarios that were described too loosely. Part two, which is the one I actually care about: I don't think the fink/mask distinction is doing any real work. Finks, masks, antidotes, the mimic cases, the reverse-fink cases — its all one phenomenon, interference somewhere between stimulus and manifestation, and the sub-labels just record where in the chain the interference happens to land. So my taxonomy has one box instead of five.

For context on where I came at this from: materials, not the metaphysics end. Passivation is my favorite example — the oxide skin on aluminum is a standing mask on a genuinely reactive disposition, nothing mysterious about it, and engineers treat it exactly that way without losing any predictive grip.

What would count against my view? Rank the defeaters, fatal ones first, merely awkward ones last, and tell me which of them I'm actually exposed to as opposed to which I can absorb. Specifically:

- Cases where holding the intrinsic basis fixed rescues nothing because the interfering change is itself intrinsic to the object. Do those exist, or am I entitled to say "then the basis wasn't retained, so the analysis was never triggered"?
- The charge that my conditional is vacuous — that "would manifest if stimulated and kept its basis" just restates "is disposed to manifest if stimulated." Is that fair? And if the analysis survives only by becoming trivially true, is that a refutation or a change of subject?
- Whether collapsing the taxonomy costs me something I'll need later. Is there any argument that genuinely requires finks and masks to be different kinds of thing, or are they only ever deployed illustratively?
- What my view predicts that a categoricalist rival doesn't, and whether empirical results — metastability, radioactive decay, anything from the chemistry of inhibited reactions — can get a real handle on this, or whether that's just decoration.

Be honest, too, about what a defender of the five-box taxonomy would concede counts against me versus what they'd wave off, and don't be polite about it — if the right verdict is that I've definately insulated the position past the point where it says anything, say so. The one thing that would move me most is a case where the disposition and my conditional come apart and I can't redescribe it as "you named the wrong basis."

Please write the answer in British English.
