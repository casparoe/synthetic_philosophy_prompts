I'm nobody's idea of an archaeologist — I got into this because I spent two summers volunteering on a dig and then kept reading on my own after the season ended, mostly popular books and long threads online. Somewhere along the way I landed on a view I've been argueing with my brother about for months, and I want to know how exposed it really is before I keep defending it. The view: how a body gets treated at burial is a pretty direct read on where that person stood in their community while they were alive. Lavish grave = high standing, plain or missing grave = low standing, and when you find infants or small kids laid out with serious wealth — gold, weapons, imported shells, the works — that's the knockout argument, because a baby obviously hasn't earned anything, so whatever status it's being buried with has to be inherited. The same logic runs in reverse too: a cemetary where everyone gets basically the same treatment is decent evidence for a genuinely flat society. So when I read that some site has a handful of spectacular graves sitting among a lot of plain ones, I take it that we're looking at a ranked society where the people buried well were the people running things, not just the people whose families liked showing off.

A couple of things I've read recently have made me nervous, though. One is that in a famous very old European cemetery — the one with all the gold, I think it's in Bulgaria — some of the graves have no skeleton in them at all, and those empty ones are supposedly the richest in gold of the whole site. Another is the grave at that same place that everybody called a prince's grave for years and that I've since seen described as probably a metalworker's. And then I keep running into the argument that a funeral is a performance put on by the living for the living, so the display might say more about the mourners than about the corpse. So: what would count against my view? I want the real defeaters sorted by how much damage they do — which ones would be fatal, which are merely awkward, and which ones I could honestly shrug off. For each, say what the world would actually have to look like for it to bite, and tell me which ones a committed defender of my position would concede as genuine problems and which they'd wave away as critics overreaching. I'd also like to know what my view predicts that the rival ways of reading graves don't, ideally some check I could run on a site report myself without being a specialist.

And then there's the thing I'm most afraid of: does my position risk anything at all, or have I quietly insulated it? Because it feels like there's an answer ready for every possible result — rich graves mean inherited rank, uniform graves mean equality, weird or inverted or empty graves mean ritual symbolism — and if a theory can absorb literally any pattern in the dirt, I don't see what I'd ever be able to count as evidence against it. Tell me straight whether that's a fair criticism or whether I'm just confusing flexibility with emptiness, and if it is a real problem, what exactly I'd have to give up to climb back out of the corner.
