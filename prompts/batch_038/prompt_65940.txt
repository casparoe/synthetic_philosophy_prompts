Two years into a dissertation on revision under indeterminate perceptual input, I have inherited hosting duties for my department's small podcast. Next week's guest is a philosopher who argues that Jeffrey's rule is the only coherent way to update on uncertain evidence: the rigidity of the relevant conditional probabilities is not an optional assumption but constitutive of learning from experience at all, and the order-dependence of successive kinematic updates is a feature rather than a defect. Her work is what my committee keeps citing, so I would rather not spend the hour asking her to explain the rule to a general audience.

Draft six to eight questions that press the view where it is weakest, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. I want at least one question on what makes rigidity hold in a given case rather than merely being assumed, and on how her answer avoids rendering the rule vacuous; one on an agent who discovers mid-experiment that a drug is shifting her colour discrimination; one on an agent who is unsure whether her present experience falls in the violet cell or the blue cell of her own partition; one on what she should say when the new constraint an agent faces is not a partition at all; and a closing question on what would count as evidence against her position.
