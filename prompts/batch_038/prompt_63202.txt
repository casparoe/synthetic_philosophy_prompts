my flatmate does psych and he explains colour phi with a telly analogy. Two lights, one red one green, flashing alternately in different spots: you see smooth movement and a colour swap halfway through, a swap that happens before the second light has even come on. His gloss is that consciousness is a live show going out on a seven-second delay so the censor can bleep the swearing — nothing you ever receive was uncensored. Stalinesque revision is just that delay-plus-censor, he says; Orwellian is a genuinely live broadcast that gets re-edited in the archive afterwards. And since you can't have a moment of experience without a moment of transmission, he reckons there's no fact about what you were seeing at any given instant, so the whole debate about the timing of experience is pseudo-profound.

Can you map the analogy out properly — what's the studio, the censor, the delay, the archive, the viewer — and then say where it stops holding, separating the disanalogies that actually wreck his conclusion from the ones that are just decoration? Two follow-ups: does the delay picture really get him to 'no fact of the matter', or does it only work if you've already put a screen in the head with a clock beside it? And his new wrinkle — what if it isn't broadcast at all but streamed, generated on demand as you watch, so there's neither a delay nor a recording to tamper with: is that a genuine third option alongside the Orwellian and Stalinesque, or does it collapse back into one of them?
