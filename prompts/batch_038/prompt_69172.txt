I've been chewing over what actually counts as corruption, entirely off my own back, and I've got a list of gut calls I can't make fit together. Could you have a go at working out the principle underneath them?

Here's the cases. My mate's dad is a plumber and my mate got him to sort the boiler for nothing, because they're family — not corruption, obviously. A councillor's brother wins a council contract, proper market rate, but he turned out to be the only bidder — corruption, I'd say. A teacher gets a box of chocolates off a parent in July, fine; the same teacher gets a nice watch off the same parent in January, right when the catchment places are being decided, not fine — though I honestly don't know about that same watch handed over in March after the kid didn't get in, purely as a thanks for trying. A rich dad gives the school fifty grand for a new sports hall and his son gets a place: I keep thinking that's fine, and I can't tell you why it's fine when the watch isn't. An MP takes a paid consultancy, declares it in the register exactly like he's supposed to, and I still think it's wrong, even though it's the most above-board thing on my list. Over in a country I used to work in, lorry drivers slip the customs bloke a few quid each so they aren't sat there all day; he's paid peanuts and the tips are about half his wage. I don't want to call him corrupt, I want to call the setup corrupt, but I can't say what that difference actually amounts to. And paying to jump the queue for your kid's operation — not fine, although literally everyone I've said this to says they'd do it in a heartbeat.

The theory I keep half landing on is that it's about who pays. A favour isn't corrupt if the person doing it is paying for it himself, out of his own pocket or his own time; it's corrupt when the cost lands on somebody who wasn't in the room and never got a say. That gets the plumber and the chocolates, and I think it gets the MP too, sort of. But it seems to let the customs bloke off, and it makes the sports hall fine, and I'm not sure about either.

So: is there a principle that gets all of them, or nearly all? What would it say about a case I haven't thought of? And which of my calls have to go if I want to keep the thing simple?
