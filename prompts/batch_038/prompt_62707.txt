Second time, in the seminar, somebody waved it around as though it *settled* Hume — like the maths proves all priors are equally legitimate, because if you average over every possible target function then no strategy does better than any other, so induction is arbitrary all the way down and anyone who says otherwise is just hiding their metaphysics.

Can you take that one sentence apart and give me the menu? I want the seperate precise claims somebody could be making with it, and for each one: what exactly is being averaged over (which class of problems, which measure, which error quantity), what the conclusion is actually about (algorithms? priors? the justification of inference?), and whether it comes out as (i) trivial bookkeeping, (ii) a real theorem with real content, (iii) a plausible but unearned generalization, or (iv) flat false.
