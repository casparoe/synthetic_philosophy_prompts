I got elected to run trainings for the shop stewarts in our local — about 30 people, warehouse and hospital folks, nobody's taken a philosophy class — and I've got 90 minutes next month. The thing I want them to actually leave understanding is an argument I read about years ago and can't stop turning over: that a written rule doesn't by itself tell you what to do in a case nobody thought of, because any pile of past decisions is compatible with a bunch of different rules you could be said to be following. Our grievances are all this, honestly. A clause says overtime gets divided equally as far as practicable, and in 1998 nobody imagined the situation we're in now, and management's reading isn't obviously less faithful to the words than ours.

So plan the session for me, don't explain the argument to me. What order do the pieces have to come in and what does each one depend on; which single clause should carry the whole thing, and does it work better if it sounds airtight or if it's obviously vague; the misconceptions they'll bring — I'm bracing for "the words mean what they mean" on one side and "so the company can do whatever it wants" on the other — and exactly where in the sequence each one gets headed off. There's also one sharp steward who's going to say fine, but then our reading's no better than theirs, so why bother filing — I need to know where that lands and whether I've earned the right to answer it by then. And tell me what I should straight up refuse to get into at this level, plus one question at the end that shows whether they got it or just got spooked.
