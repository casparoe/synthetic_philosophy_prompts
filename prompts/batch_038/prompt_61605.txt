Two people from my meetup got sucked into the raven paradox on thursday and I've somehow been made the tiebreaker, so I'm outsourcing it to you. Warning up front: both of them are quantitative and neither is stupid, so please don't hand me the stock "yes it confirms, but negligibly, mystery dissolved" paragraph. I've read that one. It doesn't survive contact with actual numbers, which is what they were fighting about.

Setup they both agreed to: a valley containing N objects total. 10,000 of them are ravens. 100,000 of them are non-black. You sample one object uniformly at random. H = all ravens are black. For the negation they stipulate f = 0.1, meaning under ~H 10% of ravens are non-black.

1. Dev says the white shoe you turn up is worth basically the same as a black raven, and that this kills the standard reconciliation. Raven: LR = 1/(1-f) ≈ 1.11. Shoe: there are 100,000 non-black slots in the valley, and under ~H there are 10,000 ravens that could be sitting in those slots, so LR = 100,000/90,000... sorry, 100,000/(100,000 - 10,000) ≈ 1.11 as well. Same number. So "it confirms but only trivially" isn't a solution, it's a miscalculation, and shoe-inspecting is efficient ornithology.

2. Mara says he dropped the f. Under ~H only fR = 1,000 ravens are non-black, so the shoe's LR is 100,000/99,000 ≈ 1.010, while the raven stays at 1.11 — about ten times stronger in log odds. Her general claim: the shoe's weight is governed by fR/M, so it decays to nothing as ravens get rarer, and it only matches the raven when there are about as many ravens as non-black things.

3. Dev says that general claim is the actual problem, not the fix. Shrink the ravens to 100 and keep M at 100,000 and her formula spits out 1.0001 — the shoe is worthless. But H is a claim about color, not about population size, so a formula where the evidential weight of a non-raven observation swings with how many ravens happen to exist has smuggled an abundance claim into a color claim. And in the rare-raven limit she's committed to saying a non-black non-raven confirms nothing, which is just Hempel's equivalence condition being flatly denied rather than explained.

4. Mara says fine, and then goes further, which is where it got interesting. Her 1.010 already rests on a stipulation nobody had noticed: that M = 100,000 is the total count of non-black things and that it's the same under H and ~H. Instead hold fixed the number of non-black non-ravens at 100,000 and let the non-black total grow to 101,000 under ~H. Now P(shoe|H) = P(shoe|~H) = 100,000/N exactly and the white shoe confirms by precisely zero. The raven gives 1.11 in both versions. So the shoe's contribution isn't a fact about the shoe or about H, it's a fact about which counts your background knowledge pins down. That, she says, is what the paradox is really about.

5. Dev calls this surrender wearing a lab coat. If the same observation report confirms under one background assumption and doesn't under another, then equal evidence for equivalent hypotheses is dead and nothing has been solved. And he says her two stipulations aren't equally available anyway: you can't know there are exactly 100,000 non-black non-ravens without having checked each one for ravenhood, and if you've done that you've already established the conclusion by survey, so the zero is an artifact.

Please go through 1-5 one at a time and rate each clearly wrong / partly right / clearly right, showing the arithmetic where it matters. Then answer three things: (a) which number is actually right for the white shoe in the setup as they stated it, (b) does Dev's last objection in 5 land or is it a gotcha, and (c) who's more right overall.
