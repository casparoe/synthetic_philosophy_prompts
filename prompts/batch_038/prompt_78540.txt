My partner and I have been building a little app — really it's a spreadsheet that talks back — for tracking how good our days are. You log activities as they happen (morning run, long lunch with a friend, three hours of Civ, a hot shower after shoveling, finally getting a nasty piece of code to work) and each one gets a hedonic score from -5 to +5. At the end of the week it tells you where your good feelings actually came from so you can schedule more of them. I've been using it for a couple months and it's genuinely changed some of my habits.

Last night I showed it to N and we argued for like four hours. Their objection is NOT about measurement error or people lying in their logs or the app being gimmicky. It's that the scale itself is fake, because there isn't any one thing that all these experiences share. Sun-warmth on the deck, the buzz after a hard set, the click of a proof finally making sense, laughing so hard your ribs hurt, the numb comfort of rewatching a show you've seen nine times — N says these have literally nothing in common except that we apply the same word to them, so averaging them into a weekly number and telling me my Tuesday was +3.2 is arithmetic on nothing. I said that's obviously wrong: I can compare them. I'd take the concert over the massage any day of the week. And if I can rank them and trade them off against each other, there has to be some common currency they're all measured in, some feeling-tone that comes in degrees.

N's comeback was that my ranking isn't about any common feeling at all — I just want the concert more, or would choose it, and "pleasure" is a label I paste onto whatever I end up picking. Which felt like a dirty move that makes my own data meaningless.

Then N dropped the thing I actually can't answer: when I'm absorbed in the coding problem I'm not feeling anything anywhere. There's no sensation, no buzz, no warmth. I only notice it was enjoyable afterwards, looking back. So what exactly is my +4 an entry about?

- Does my ranking argument work? Does the fact that I can prefer one to another prove they share a measurable something, or does N's want/choice reading dissolve it?
