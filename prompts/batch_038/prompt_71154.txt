ok so — I'm TAing a philosophy of religion seminar and next week's reading is David Lewis's "Do We Believe in Penal Substitution?", the paper where he sets up the case of a justly sentenced offender whose devoted, innocent friend volunteers to take the punishment instead, notes that most of us say the state should still punish the offender, and then points out that our actual practice is nowhere near as clean about this as that intuition implies. fines get paid by somebody else's money all the time, employers eat their employees' tort liability, insurers cover harms nobody personally bore, parents settle for their kids. and yet the theological version is supposed to be uniquely barbaric.

I'd rather open the session with something funny than with a summary, so: can you write me a parody of the terms of service and FAQ for a startup that brokers substitute punishment — basically TaskRabbit for sentences? Around 750 words, numbered clauses, deadpan corporate legalese, written as if by a general counsel who has genuinely thought hard about the philosophy and is trying very hard not to.

the jokes have to come from getting the content right, so specifically:

- split the product by penalty type. monetary penalties are trivially transferable and nobody's ever blinked at that; custodial and corporal ones aren't, and the fine print should show the company straining against that line — rebranding a prison term as some kind of "presence obligation" or "time-equivalent service" while everyone involved knows it isn't the same thing and the boilerplate is doing work
- the FAQ should handle, in the company's own chirpy voice, the objections a retributivist would actually make: that punishment has to be borne by the person who deserves it; that inflicting it on an innocent volunteer is its own injustice whatever they consented to; that money is fungible and suffering isn't; and that if the point is reform or repentance, substitution improves the wrong person
- one clause should quietly concede that what they're selling might not be punishment-transfer at all but just liability-discharge-by-payment, and that the distinction matters enormously to their underwriters and not at all to their marketing copy
- and one on what happens if the substitute dies partway through — does the remainder revert to the client, prorate, or get absorbed
- let the whole document lean on the civil/criminal asymmetry without ever using the word inconsistent

constraints: no real people, no real religion as the butt of it. one oblique nod to the theological version is fine — say, an enterprise case study with a single substitute and an unbounded number of beneficiaries — but it should be a structural wink, not a joke at faith's expense. the target is the secular double standard and the boilerplate that papers over it. don't quote or cite Lewis or anything else, and don't explain the jokes: a reader should be able to tell what's being ironized without the document ever winking. readable by someone who's never heard of Lewis, snort-worthy for someone who has.
