I sit on the ethics review panel that clears user-facing messaging at a consumer finance app, and next month's proposal is contested. Our determinations to date, each with the one-line reason we recorded:

- Approved: a free trial on by default at signup, with a one-tap opt-out on the same screen and a push reminder three days before the first charge. Reason: the user is fully informed and can exit at negligible cost.
- Rejected: a checkout banner reading "offer ends in 9:58" whose timer reset on every page reload. Reason: the message is false.
- Approved: copy framing an idle-balance feature as a loss ("you are forgoing about $12 a month") rather than a gain, the figure being arithmetically accurate. Reason: accurately stating a real cost is argument, not pressure.
- Rejected: a flow that re-asked users to confirm a data-sharing preference four times after they had already declined, with "decline" set in low-contrast grey. Reason: the design's purpose was to wear the user down.
- Approved: a notification quoting the user's own stated savings goal and their shortfall against it. Reason: it reminds people of a commitment they made to themselves.
- Rejected: an A/B test that varied the emotional tone of users' activity feeds and measured subsequent transaction volume, though every item shown was accurate. Reason: users were made instruments of an experiment on their own moods.

The new proposal solicits a charitable round-up donation at a time chosen by a model trained to predict when the user is most likely to give. The copy is accurate and makes no appeal to urgency; the timing method is described in the in-app help centre, and the release notes read, in full, "we are improving when prompts reach you"; users may switch donation prompts off permanently, but the feature is on for existing users unless they act.

Predict how the panel will rule and on what ground. Set out the principles our record is jointly consistent with — deception, the influencer's purpose, the use of non-rational leverage, endorsement the affected user would give if they understood the mechanism and its effect on them, exploitation of a state the user cannot readily notice — or whatever else fits; say what each implies here, and which of them our own loss-framing and self-goal approvals already put under strain. Name the closest precedent on each side and where the analogy fails. Then specify one further proposal we would have to rule on that would discriminate between the surviving principles. If the record genuinely underdetermines this case, state plainly what principle each available vote would commit us to.
