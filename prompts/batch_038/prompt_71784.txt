You are a philosopher whose expertise happens to straddle two literatures that rarely meet: the classical Indian debates about karma as an account of moral desert, and contemporary Anglophone theorizing about deserved suffering, culpability, and retribution. You are also skilled at designing thought experiments — cases engineered so that named positions are forced to deliver named verdicts. Think carefully; this is a construction task with tight tolerances, not a survey.

**What the case is for.** There is a standing objection to karma as a theory of moral desert. It runs roughly as follows: desert is agent-relative. It attaches to a person in virtue of what that person did, knew, intended, and could have avoided. But the karmic ledger appears to charge agents for features of the world they had no access to and no control over, which makes it a theory of causal liability or taint rather than of desert. The standard defense has two parts. First, karma is said to be intention all the way down: what is recorded is the volition, not the outcome. Second, a strand of the tradition holds that the weight of a deed depends on the "field" in which it is sown — the moral standing of the recipient, the recipient's relation to the agent, the recipient's trajectory — so that the same gift yields more merit when given to a worthier recipient and, by the same logic, the same injury carries more demerit when the object injured is weightier in the relevant way. Those two commitments sit uneasily together. I want a single concrete scenario that makes the tension visible, rather than a discussion of it in the abstract. A case that merely asserts the divergence fails; the divergence must be generated by the doctrines described below.

**Stipulated background** (work from these glosses rather than from your recall; do not correct them, do not cite sources for them). *Retributive desert:* the suffering a wrongdoer deserves is fixed by the wrongfulness of the act together with the agent's culpability — intention, foresight, motive, and the absence of excuse. Nothing about the victim's moral standing beyond their status as a person enters the calculation. *Karma-as-intention:* the karmic record is of the volition; unintended or unforeseen effects do not register. *The field doctrine:* the weight of a recorded volition is modulated by the standing of its object. *Impersonal ripening:* the fruit of a deed matures by a natural process; no administrator, judge, or deity weighs, apportions, or waives anything. *Karma-as-character-formation:* on some accounts the deed's weight comes largely from what it does to the agent's own dispositions.

**Design specifications.** All must be met.

1. Two agents commit acts that are indistinguishable with respect to act-type, intention, foresight, motive, degree of resolve, prior record, and character. Neither has any excuse the other lacks, and neither is more negligent or more malicious than the other.
2. The harm actually done is identical, and identical in every measurable respect: to the victim, to bystanders, to the agents' own dispositions, and to anyone's state of knowledge. Nothing about the two acts' consequences differs at all.
3. One agent nevertheless incurs substantially heavier demerit than the other, and the entire difference is generated by the field doctrine applied to the two victims.
4. The field-relevant difference between the victims is invisible: neither agent knows it, neither could reasonably have discovered it, and no ordinary inquirer could reconstruct it afterward.
5. No deity, judge, administrator, or any other weighing intelligence appears in the story, and the mechanism you invoke is impersonal.
6. Ordinary, realistic setting. No supernatural observers, no on-screen ripening, no one present who knows what fruit follows.
7. Prohibited devices: the canonical list of immediately-ripening heinous deeds (harming a parent, and the like); any retelling or thin disguise of a known parable; the disguised-holy-person move, in which a beggar turns out to be a saint; any appeal to the victim's own past karma; and any appeal to what the victim would have gone on to contribute, since that is a difference in consequences and violates specification 2.
8. The extra demerit must not be explicable through character-formation, since specification 2 rules that route out — and your answer should say why it is ruled out.
9. A retributivist reading the case must be forced to the verdict that the two agents deserve exactly equal suffering, and must be unable to reach any other verdict by ordinary means.

**Deliverables.**

A. Before the scenario, show your brainstorm: list at least five candidate field-features, test each against specifications 2, 4, and 7, discard the failures, and say in two or three sentences which one you chose and why it generates the asymmetry most cleanly.

B. The scenario itself, in no more than 250 words.

C. A compliance check, specification by specification, showing that each is met and — critically — that the karmic asymmetry in specification 3 follows from the field doctrine as glossed above rather than from something you wrote into the story.

D. Verdicts from five positions, each with a sentence or two of reasoning: a retributivist about deserved suffering; a karma theorist who holds to karma-as-intention and must therefore resist, restrict, or reinterpret the field doctrine; a karma theorist who accepts both the field doctrine and impersonal ripening; a karma theorist who accepts the field doctrine but explains its operation by an administering intelligence; and a skeptic who says karma is not a theory of desert at all but of causal liability, and therefore that your case is no embarrassment. For the middle three, say what each must make of the invisibility in specification 4.

E. Identify the single feature of the case that does the work, then run four removal tests, stating how the verdicts change in each: the agents learn the field-difference before acting; the field-difference is deleted; an administrator who could waive the fruit is added; the field-difference is discoverable in principle afterward but nobody bothers to look.

F. The strongest attack on the case mounted by whichever position comes out worst, including the charge that the case builds in its own verdict. Then answer the payoff question directly: does a ledger that charges agents in this way count as a theory of moral desert, a theory of liability, or something else, and what turns on the answer?

G. In two sentences: does the same design yield a case in which karma is more *merciful* than retributivism? Sketch it, or argue that no such case is constructible.

**Form.** Do not quote, cite, or paraphrase-as-quote any text, and do not invent scriptural attributions. No bibliography, no reading list, no named works. Gloss every technical term in a phrase. Write for a reader fluent in contemporary ethics who has never studied Indian philosophy. If you conclude that some specification cannot be satisfied, say so plainly and propose the minimal repair instead of quietly dropping it.
