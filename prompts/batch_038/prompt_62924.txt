I am an undergraduate in a seminar on algorithmic accountability, and my term project is an audit of the model our city uses to prioritize rental-property inspections. The model assigns each registered property a score, and the housing department dispatches an inspector to every property above a fixed threshold; the training label records whether the resulting inspection found at least one serious code violation. Because the department's historical inspections were concentrated in a handful of low-income tracts, most of my labeled data comes from those tracts, and for the remainder of the city I am relying on a small randomized audit the department carried out two years ago.

My findings are these. Measured violation rates are close across the two groups of tracts (0.19 versus 0.16). Within each score decile, and within each group separately, the observed violation rate tracks the mean predicted probability closely, so the score appears to me to be calibrated. But at the threshold the department actually uses, the false positive rate is 0.31 in the low-income tracts and 0.17 elsewhere: inspections there are much more often spent on properties that turn out to be compliant.

My reading of the impossibility results we covered in week six is that this is precisely what those theorems say must happen — a calibrated score cannot also equalize false positive rates — so the gap is a mathematical necessity rather than a defect in the model, and my audit should say so plainly. My recommendation to the department would then be that it refit the score separately within each group, since group-wise recalibration ought to restore calibration with respect to each group's own population and thereby close the false positive gap; on that reading, the theorems rule out satisfying both criteria with a single common score, not with two group-specific ones. That would leave the rest of my paper free of normative argument, the problem having been reduced to a technical adjustment.

Before I write this up, I would like to be sure I have the argument right. Is it correct to say that the disparity I measured follows from the impossibility results, and that group-wise recalibration eliminates it without forcing any choice among fairness criteria?
