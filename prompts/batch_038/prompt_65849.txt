So — I help run a mutual aid group, about fifteen of us, we all put money in each month and split a pot between whoever's in a hole that month. For three years we did it the dead simple way: even shares between the households that applied, no questions asked. Last month it went sideways. Two people applied: a single dad with three kids whose shifts got cut, and a woman who lives alone, works full time, and asked for help with an electricity bill because she runs medical equipment at home all night. We gave them the same number and half the room felt fine about it and half felt sick about it, and the argument afterwards turned out not to be about the money at all — it was about what we thought we were equalising, which nobody could name. Since then I've been reading around it on my own, nothing academic, library stuff and bits online, and found out this is apparently a real named question: something about whether you equalise welfare, or resources, or capabilities. I want to run a ninety minute session with the group on it before the next allocation round. Worth knowing the room: there's a retired accountant who will want a formula by the end, a woman who thinks any formula is an insult to the person asking, and one guy who'll say within ten minutes that this is all just a way of dressing up not wanting to work.

I don't want the three approaches explained to me, I've got the rough shape of each. I want a plan for how I explain them. What has to be in place before this even looks like a question — right now most of them think "we split it evenly, obviously" is a finished answer, and I can't get across that it isn't, and my guess is that's the hinge the whole night turns on, but I don't know what to put *before* it so that the hinge has something to grip. Then the carrying example. I'd assumed I'd use our own two cases from last month, but I'm worried that's a disaster since everyone already picked a side and they'll just replay the fight with fancier words. So tell me what a good carrying case has to have and what it has to not have, and whether the real one or an invented one is the better bet for this particular group — and if you say invent one, build it rather than describing what it should be like. Then the misconceptions and where in the sequence each gets headed off. The ones I can already see coming: that capabilities is just a posh word for what people are able to do so it collapses back into resources; that caring about wellbeing means you end up topping up the guy who needs fancy beer to be happy; that this is the same old equality-of-opportunity-versus-outcome thing they've heard a hundred times; and that the whole debate is a technicality because we all agree equality matters anyway. Which of those do I defuse early, which do I let them walk straight into because hitting it is actually useful, and what do I say at the moment of the walk-in? Plus one thing I can do at the end that tells me whether they've got the question rather than just being polite to me — something that isn't a quiz and doesn't turn into a vote on real money.

And tell me what to leave out and to announce that I'm leaving it out; there's more than ninety minutes of material here and I'd rather cut on purpose than run out of room halfway through the good bit. Finally, be straight with me about where your plan is weakest — which stretch is most likely to fall flat or actively backfire with these people, and what would change your mind about the ordering, like is there some one thing about the group that if I told you it to you'd rearrange the whole thing? Say which parts of the plan you're confident in and which you're kind of guessing at.
