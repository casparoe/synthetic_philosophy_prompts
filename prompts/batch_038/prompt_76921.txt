I am preparing a paper for a comparative philosophy workshop defending a hybrid position in the Confucian debate over human nature, and before I present it I would like a rigorous consistency audit of the position. Here are my commitments, stated as plainly as I can manage.

1. Human beings are born with spontaneous affective responses that track morally salient features of situations. The alarm anyone would feel on suddenly seeing a small child about to fall into a well is the paradigm case, and it is not produced by calculation, by upbringing, or by concern for reputation. Crucially, I hold that these responses are keyed to the features that make a situation call for compassion, shame, deference, or discrimination — the tracking is not a happy accident.

2. These responses are capacities, not yet virtues. They become virtues only through extension and cultivation, and the right model of cultivation is agricultural rather than carpentry: one nurtures a growth already underway. I take Mencius's warning against the man who pulled up his seedlings to help them along as stating a methodological constraint — development forced from outside is spoiled by the forcing.

3. Nevertheless ritual, music, the classical texts, and teachers are strictly necessary. I mean the strong claim: nobody becomes good without them. Not that they help, and not that they speed things up.

4. The content of ritual is a human artifact. It was devised by sages in response to the strife that arises when desire meets scarcity, and its function is to allocate and to differentiate. Because it solves a functional problem rather than transcribing a cosmic order, other societies could have arrived at different schemes, differing in considerable detail, that were equally workable.

5. What makes such invented schemes stick is that the human heart recognizes and takes pleasure in them on encounter, in the way the mouth takes pleasure in well-prepared meat. This approval is immediate and unlearned; it is not the sediment of conditioning.

6. I also accept Xunzi's diagnosis of the default condition: nature left to itself yields disorder. My evidence is that every known society has required constraint, and that people's first impulses under scarcity are acquisitive.

7. On the sage-invention problem — how sages, sharing the nature of everyone else, could have produced ritual in the first place — I do not think the objection is fatal. The sages differed from others in the quality of their cognitive endowment and in their accumulated reflection, and that difference is what made invention possible.

8. Non-human animals display proto-moral affect, such as care of offspring and agitation at the distress of companions, yet have no virtues, because virtue requires reflective endorsement of a norm.

9. A person raised among corrupt practices can still recover full virtue by their own effort and without a teacher, because the original responses are never wholly extinguished. I read Mencius's image of the hillside stripped of its trees, which nonetheless keeps putting out new growth and is only misdescribed as barren, as literally true of persons.

10. Methodologically: where the texts underdetermine, I prefer readings that make Mencius and Xunzi empirical rivals with testable disagreements, rather than readings on which they simply define their terms differently and so talk past one another.

I want two things from you. First, the audit: identify which of these commitments pull against one another, rank the resulting tensions by severity rather than presenting them as a flat list, and for each one say exactly what would have to be surrendered or weakened and what is lost by the weakening. Second, discrimination: several of these will look contradictory and are not. Say which those are, and explain how the appearance of conflict arises and why it disappears. Please do not report every pair that can be made to sound uncomfortable — I need the genuine fault lines separated from the spurious ones, and I need to be told where a tension is merely verbal. Finally, tell me which single revision to the package would repair the most damage at the lowest cost, and argue for that choice.

I have no prior sense of where the weak point is, and no attachment to any particular item. Argue from the content of the views themselves rather than from which commentators happen to hold which; I am interested in the structure of the position, not its pedigree.
