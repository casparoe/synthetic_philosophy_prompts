I have spent about a decade on build systems, dependency graphs, and large-monorepo tooling, and I have never taken a philosophy course. My entire model of what depends on what comes from that work: nodes and directed edges, topological sorts, cycle detection, layers, generated artifacts, and the difference between something derived from something else and something primitive because no upstream rule produces it. Reading about grounding and fundamentality in contemporary metaphysics, I keep feeling that this is my own vocabulary wearing unfamiliar names. I want to test that feeling rigorously rather than comfortably.

First, the translation. Restate the central commitments of the grounding framework as faithfully as you can in concepts I already possess:

- the claim that grounding is a strict partial order — irreflexive, asymmetric, transitive — and what each constraint is supposed to buy;
- the claim that the grounding relation is well-founded, so that descent terminates in a fundamental level, and what work fundamentality does there beyond meaning that the node has no in-edges;
- the distinction between full and partial grounding, which I suspect is the difference between a complete input set and one contributing input among several;
- the requirement, if there is one, that grounding is factive — only what actually obtains can ground anything;
- the claim that grounding is hyperintensional, so that two claims true in exactly the same circumstances can stand differently to what grounds them;
- and one or two of the standard illustrations — the way a disjunctive fact is said to be grounded in whichever disjunct holds, or the way a set's existence is grounded in its members — recast as dependency facts.

For each, state plainly whether it maps onto something I already have, whether it maps only if I bend one of my concepts, and exactly where the bending occurs.

Second, the residue report, which is the part I actually want. Three questions I cannot settle myself:

1. My build graphs are well-founded because I made them so: a cycle is a defect, and an infinite chain is not even representable. Is the well-foundedness of grounding likewise a stipulation about what counts as an admissible graph, or a substantive thesis about the world that could turn out false? I understand that some metaphysicians defend the possibility of an infinitely descending chain of grounds with no fundamental floor. Translate that position into my terms — what would an ill-founded dependency graph over facts be, and does my vocabulary contain anything that could distinguish such a graph from one I simply have not finished resolving?

2. In my world, edges come in kinds — compile-time, link-time, runtime, data provenance — and I compose them freely without asking whether the composition is legitimate. Is grounding one relation, or a family of relations trading under a shared name? If a family, does transitivity survive chaining heterogeneous links, and does anything in my graph model even allow me to pose that question?

3. My dependency graph is a representation: it describes how a tool resolves targets, and the world is indifferent to it. Grounding is supposed to obtain whether or not anyone models it. What would have to be added to my framework for that difference to become expressible, and would the addition be something I could in principle check, or does it amount to a redescription that changes nothing operationally?

Third, a methodological constraint: do not let my vocabulary do silent work. In particular, do not infer that grounding must be well-founded merely because acyclicity and finiteness are structural features of every build system I have used, and do not treat a level in the grounding sense as though it were an abstraction boundary that exists because someone chose to draw one. Wherever the translation depends on an assumption my framework supplies for free but metaphysics does not, flag it as an assumption instead of presenting it as a match.

Finally, the verdict. Is my working hypothesis — that grounding talk is dependency-graph talk over facts, plus a well-foundedness constraint, plus the claim that the graph is world-made rather than tool-made — a fair summary that leaves nothing important out? Or is there something in the grounding debate with no counterpart in my framework at all? If there is such a residue, tell me whether you take it to show that my framework is missing a concept I could add, or that the metaphysicians' disagreement is less substantive than their vocabulary implies.
