Not a moral responsibility person — I do philosophy of language mostly, plus a logic course every other year — but I've been chewing on something for a while and can't find a term for it, so I figured I'd ask.

Two cases, both real, both people I know.

One: a guy's brother borrowed a lot of money years ago and never paid it back, never even properly acknowledged it. He was livid for about a year. Then, as he puts it, the anger "went somewhere else." Now he's completely fine with the brother — cheerful at Thanksgiving, would lend him a car, would help him move on a Sunday. But nothing is owed and nothing is expected, and the subject is closed. He got annoyed when someone suggested he'd forgiven him. "Forgiven him? I haven't forgiven anything. I just don't need anything from him."

Two: a colleague who reliably takes credit for other people's work. After the third time, a friend of mine stopped objecting, stopped raising it with the chair, stopped mentioning it at all — and stayed perfectly pleasant. She still coauthors with him, still says hi in the hallway, still laughs at his jokes. She also still thinks what he did was wrong, and will say so with no heat whatsoever, and she's adjusted her paperwork: no joint submissions without an agreement in advance.

So what is this thing? It isn't forgiveness — nothing has been resolved, no apology given or wanted, and the person would reject the label. It isn't just resentment fading with time either, because the standing verdict that the act was wrong survives intact; its the demand that's gone, not the judgment. It isn't the cold shoulder, since the warmth and the helpfulness are all still there — she'd genuinely be glad to see him. And it isn't simply "lowering your expectations about people," because it's aimed at a particular person with a particular history, not a general forecast.

I half-remember Strawson's objective attitude from a grad seminar — something about treating someone as an object to be managed or handled rather than as a participant you can address — and I grant that's in the neighborhood. But that notion seems reserved for people who aren't fit to be reasoned with: small children, the seriously ill, the compulsive, maybe the very stressed. Here the other person is treated as perfectly competent and fully in the picture. What's withdrawn is narrower than a whole stance, and it was chosen, sometimes explicitly as a way of keeping the peace.

Is there an established concept for a chosen, partial, warm withdrawal of the demand while keeping the judgment? If there is, tell me which one, and how it differs from the near-misses I listed above and from plain distrust-with-manners. If there isn't, coin a term and define it, and be precise about the edges: at what point does it turn into forgiveness, at what point does it turn into the objective attitude, and at what point is it just prudence plus politeness? And if you think the better answer is that nothing new is going on here — that one of the reactive attitudes has simply switched off while the others kept running — then argue that rather than asserting it. I care much more about the carving than the label.
