I'm a PhD student in philosophy of law serving this year as parliamentarian to my department's graduate assembly, and I need to circulate a written opinion to the rules panel before it votes Friday. Our charter's Article X requires a two-thirds vote of the full assembly for any amendment and provides that Article X itself may not be amended. Last spring the assembly, by two-thirds, adopted Amendment 1, which struck that entrenchment sentence and substituted language permitting Article X to be amended by simple majority. Acting under that rule, the assembly then passed Amendment 2 by a bare majority: it lowers the threshold for all ordinary amendments to simple majority, declares itself unamendable, and includes a savings clause specifying that if the entrenchment portion is held invalid, the threshold change falls with it. A petition now asks the panel to declare Amendment 2 void; the respondents answer that no charter provision grants the panel power to invalidate anything the assembly has adopted, and that the panel's opinions are advisory in any event. The petition was filed a week after the assembly used the new simple-majority rule to pass a measure the petitioners had previously blocked under the old threshold.

Please draft the opinion I'll circulate. Begin your reply directly with the opinion itself, no preface or commentary, and make its first sentence the recommended disposition. Then give the reasoning the panel can actually act on: whether the panel has jurisdiction to rule on validity at all, whether a simple-majority amendment can bind future simple majorities, what the non-severability clause forces on us, and what follows from the fact that any opinion the panel issues can itself be overridden by the body whose act is in question. I want an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side and a clear bottom line on whether Amendment 2 is valid, partially valid, or void — worked through on these facts rather than as a survey of the general literature on self-reference and entrenchment.
