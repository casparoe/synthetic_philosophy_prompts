Three hour argument with my friend Rob last night and I think I lost it, but not in any way I can articulate yet. It started from a news story about a family who flew in on visitor visas they'd got by telling the consulate they were coming for a wedding, then claimed asylum at the airport. He said the whole "illegal" framing was garbage because seeking asylum is legal, and I said no, what they did was fraud, the asylum claim doesn't retroactively launder the lie on the visa form, and he said I was being a border-obsessed creep. We went around in circles because every time I named a specific thing that was wrongfull he just said the slogan again but louder, and every time I said the slogan was false he said I didn't get that it's about human beings and not paperwork.

So can you take "seeking asylum is legal" apart for me? I want the full menu of distinct claims that one sentence could be making. Genuinely distinct — "it isn't a criminal offence in most places" vs "there's a right to be admitted" vs "a state can't send you back" are obviously not the same claim, but I think there are more layers than that and I can't see them all. For each reading: what would have to be the case for it to be true, is it in fact true (roughly, in the world as it currently stands, not ideally), and is it a boring platitude nobody disputes or is it the substantive thing Rob actually needs to win?

Be explicit about the joints I kept tripping over. Whose law is being invoked when someone says "legal" — a country's criminal code, its immigration rules, or a treaty? Which moment are we evaluating — before the crossing, at the instant of asking, after a decision is made? And what is "seeking": a private intention, an utterance at a desk, a lodged application? My suspicion is that the line only sounds decisive by sliding between those, and I want to know which slides are doing the work.

Two things to press on specifically. One, I'm fairly sure the Universal Declaration says everyone has the right "to seek and to enjoy in other countries asylum from persecution" — that seek/enjoy split looks like it matters, since the second half is about a right to actually have the asylum and not merely to ask. Does that wording hand Rob anything, or does it cut the other way? Two, I have a hazy memory that the refugee convention says states shouldn't penalise refugees for entering irregularly, but only where they came directly from the territory they were at risk in and presented themselves promptly and had good cause. If that's roughly the shape of it, then it's a conditional exemption with tests built in, not a blanket permission — and I want to know how someone on his side is supposed to handle a protection drafted as an exception rather than as a right to come in.

Don't give me citations, sources or a reading list, I'm not writing a paper. I just want to walk into round two knowing which of the four or five things he could have meant is true, which is false, which is decorative, and which one he was actually saying at one in the morning. And tell me what I'd have to concede — I don't want a strategy for winning, I want to know where he's right and I'm just being pedantic about forms.
