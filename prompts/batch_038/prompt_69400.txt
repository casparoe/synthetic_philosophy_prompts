I am the secretary of my school's Student Council, which this year has been given the power to settle its own franchise. Our rulings so far are these. Every pupil from Year 7 upwards votes, and no competence test was applied to the eleven-year-olds. A girl here on an eight-week exchange was enfranchised, although she will have gone before most of our decisions take effect. Our Year 13s kept the vote on the refurbishment of the sports hall even though it will not open until two years after they have left. The caretaker and the catering staff, who are on site every day and are expected to observe everything we decide, were refused. A girl from the partner college who takes two of her A levels in our classrooms, and who must observe our rules whenever she is in the building, was given the vote on the decisions that set her own timetable. Parents were refused, although they pay the fees, drive the minibus and bear the cost of almost everything we do.

Next month we must decide whether a girl who has accepted a place for next September, and who will be bound by whatever we decide for the whole of her seven years here, may vote now. Our clerk insists there is a difference between the people our rules bind and the people our decisions affect, but she will not say which of the two entitles you to a vote; and whichever way we go will itself be settled by a vote of those who already have one.

Predict our ruling. Give the principles that fit our record equally well so far, say where they come apart on this case, recommend the decision that best coheres with our own precedents and name the closest of them, and tell me what further case would settle which principle we are really committed to.
