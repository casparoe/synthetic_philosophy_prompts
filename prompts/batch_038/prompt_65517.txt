I am putting together a seminar exercise on the first formulation of the categorical imperative and need a case engineered to split two readings of the contradiction-in-conception test. Construct a maxim, together with an ordinary situation in which an agent adopts it, meeting all of the following specifications:

(a) The universalization is logically coherent: the world in which everyone acts on that maxim can be described without inconsistency, and the agent's own act remains possible in that world. On the strict logical reading of the test, therefore, the maxim passes.
(b) On the practical-contradiction reading associated with Korsgaard, universalization nevertheless defeats the purpose for which the agent adopted the maxim — so the maxim fails the contradiction-in-conception test outright and generates a perfect duty rather than merely an imperfect one.
(c) It is not one of Kant's stock illustrations: no false or lying promise, no suicide, no rusting talent, no refusal of benevolence, and no deception of any kind.
(d) The setting is mundane and realistic — a workplace, a department, a street, a shared building — and the scenario itself is a single paragraph.
(e) The divergence between the two readings is produced by one identifiable feature of the case, a feature that could be switched off without otherwise altering the maxim or the setting.

Then show the case working. State the maxim at the level of generality the test demands, describe the universalized world, and give each reading's verdict together with the reasoning that yields it. Name the feature from (e) and report what each reading says once it is removed. Say how a defender of the logical reading would attack the construction — by re-describing the maxim, by disputing that the agent's actual purpose is the relevant one, or by insisting the contradiction was conceptual after all — and whether that defense succeeds.

Finally, commit. Tell me which of the two readings this exercise ought to be built to vindicate, and what that reading must surrender elsewhere in order to survive the case. I want a definite position with reasons, not a survey of the options left for me to adjudicate.
