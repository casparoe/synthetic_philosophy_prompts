Someone told me Schopenhauer put music in a totally different category from every other art: not a picture of the world but a direct image of the will itself, which is why it hits harder and doesn't need concepts to be understood. And that inside a piece there's a hierarchy — the deep bass stands for crude inorganic matter, the middle voices for plants and animals, and the melody for the highest thing, the striving of a conscious mind.

Restate that, claim by claim, in the vocabulary I actually work in: arrangement, timbre, tension and release, the kick as the grid everything else is measured against, expectation built by repetition and broken by a drop. Be faithful though — don't quietly turn "the will" into "the listener's predictive model" and call that a translation, that's just my framework winning by default.

Then the residue report: which claims survive intact, which you had to distort to fit, and which I literally cannot say in production terms. Two extra things I want. Is the bass/melody hierarchy just flat wrong for what people actually make now, where the low end often carries all the feeling? And where the translation breaks down, is that evidence Schopenhauer's metaphysics isn't doing any work, or evidence my vocabulary can't reach below representation?
