I build and maintain data lineage systems for a living: directed acyclic graphs of tables, views, and transformation jobs; column-level provenance; materialized views and their refresh dependencies; base tables at the top of the graph; well-foundedness enforced by the scheduler, since a cyclic dependency is simply a build failure. I read philosophy on the side, and I have been working through the contemporary literature on metaphysical grounding and fundamentality — the debate in and around Fine and Schaffer, including the arguments over whether grounding is irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive, the distinction between full and partial grounding, the dispute between those who treat grounding as a form of explanation and those who treat it as a form of non-causal determination that merely backs explanation, the question of whether grounding is one relation or a stand-in for many, and the question of whether the grounding relation must be well-founded.

My working hypothesis, which I want you to test rather than flatter, is that this entire debate is a claim about pipelines wearing a metaphysical costume: to say the mental is grounded in the physical is to say the mental is a derived table and the physical is upstream of it; to say something is fundamental is to say it is a source node with no inputs; and disputes about infinite descent or ungrounded entities are disputes about whether every job terminates.

Your task is to restate the grounding debate in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report. Concretely:

1. Translate the central theses — grounding as distinct from mere necessitation or supervenience; the formal properties claimed for the relation; full versus partial grounding; explanation versus determination; the status of the fundamental — into the terms of lineage, dependency edges, refresh order, determinism of a view by its base tables, and job termination. Show the translation actually working on at least two concrete examples from the literature, not just on slogans.

2. Sort the results into three passes: what maps cleanly, what maps only with distortion that you must describe explicitly, and what has no expression at all in a lineage graph. For each distorted or inexpressible item, say whether the failure is a limitation of my vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was less determinate than it sounded.

3. Be honest about the direction of fit. Do not define fundamental as source node and then announce that the metaphysicians agree with me; that is smuggling, not translating. Where my framework carries commitments the grounding theorist need not share — acyclicity, termination, edges being facts about how a job was configured rather than about why a value must be what it is — flag them as imports and say what they cost.

4. Address three cases I expect to be hard for me. First, the suggestion that a fact can be grounded in nothing at all, derivable from an empty premise set: can my schema express that, and if it can, does the distinction between fundamental and derived survive? Second, the priority monist claim that the whole is prior to its parts, which seems to reverse the direction of every aggregation edge I have ever drawn. Third, non-well-founded or self-grounding proposals: whether my insistence on acyclicity disqualifies them or merely fails to represent them.

Finish with a verdict on my hypothesis, stated plainly: how much of the debate is genuinely about pipeline structure, and what — if anything — is left over that my graphs cannot hold.
