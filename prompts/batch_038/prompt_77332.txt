Last night I had a four-hour argument with a friend who builds personality questionnaires for a living, and I would like an outside adjudication rather than more of the same from either of us.

The practice in question is exploratory factor analysis. A large correlation matrix among, say, sixty questionnaire items is fitted by a much smaller number of common factors plus item-specific error. As everyone in the field knows, that fit does not determine the loadings: any rotation of the factor axes reproduces the observed correlations exactly, and oblique rotations additionally allow the factors to be correlated with one another. Practitioners resolve the indeterminacy by imposing a simplicity criterion — maximize the variance of the squared loadings, push cross-loadings toward zero — and then name the resulting axes ("extraversion," "conscientiousness," "general ability"), report individual scores on them, and use those scores to screen job applicants, support clinical formulations, and compare populations. Replication of a named factor across samples, languages, and item wordings is treated as strong evidence that the factor is a real thing in the world.

My friend's position, which he holds with some heat: the rotational indeterminacy is not a defect to be papered over but the whole point. Since no body of correlation data can select a rotation, the ontology of the factors is fixed by the interpretive community's conventions — simplicity plus convergent naming across independent studies — and he says this is precisely the situation Poincaré described for geometry, where the question is never which choice is true but only which is more convenient. On his view the practice already presupposes a conventionalism about psychological structure and merely fails to say so out loud.

My objection has three parts. First, the analogy looks strained: rival geometries are supposed to be empirically equivalent all the way down, whereas rival rotations stop being equivalent the moment the factors are correlated with external criteria — job performance, heritability, developmental trajectory — so the choice is constrained by data, just not by the original matrix. Second, the practice seems internally inconsistent in its attitude toward two kinds of underdetermination: when a named factor fails to replicate in a new sample, researchers say the instrument was at fault, but when a rival rotation fits the same matrix equally well, they treat their own as disclosing the real structure rather than as one admissible convention among many. Third, and this is what the argument finally turned on: the use of factor scores to make decisions about particular people seems to require something stronger than either of us was willing to grant, since disadvantaging an applicant on the basis of a quantity that is only defined up to a choice of coordinates within an equivalence class looks like treating a bookkeeping decision as a property of a person.

Please reconstruct the implicit metaphysics and epistemology of this practice as precisely as you can. Specifically: what does it strictly presuppose about latent factors — that they are mind-independent common causes, that they are whatever our interpretive conventions settle on, or nothing at all beyond predictive bookkeeping? Which rival views does it quietly rule out, including the view that items form a network of mutually reinforcing causes with no common factor behind them? Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined, so that several mutually incompatible theories would license it equally? Is the simplicity criterion a substantive claim about the modularity of psychological causes or a methodological convenience, and what follows if the truth is that causes overlap densely? Does the decision-making use raise the presuppositional stakes above those of pure measurement, or can a conventionalist account of the factors still underwrite selection practice? And what would have to change — in what gets reported, in how scores are described, in what counts as replication — if the field adopted my friend's conventionalism openly?
