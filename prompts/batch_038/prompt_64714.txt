Second-year PhD in philosophy of mind here, working on a dissertation chapter about downward causation, and I've hit a wall I could use some help with. No citations or reading lists please — I've got the literature, what I need is the reasoning laid out cleanly.

The two things I'm trying to hold in my head at once:

One, Hofstadter's careenium picture from I Am a Strange Loop. Roughly: a table full of tiny magnetic units (he calls them simms, I think — little careening things) that clump and unclump into bigger stable formations, the simmballs, whose walls vary in bounciness so that what's outside the table gets registered inside it. Zoom in and it's all little units shoving each other. Zoom out and the clumps are doing the shoving — they track things, they dance with each other in a way that mirrors the world, and in the extended version they end up steering what the whole system does out there. His pointed question is which of those two descriptions is the honest one, and he clearly wants to say the high-level one isn't just a courtesy.

Two, Kim's exclusion argument. If every physical effect already has a sufficient physical cause, and you're not willing to swallow systematic overdetermination everywhere, then whatever the high-level property was going to contribute has already been spent, and the mental (or symbolic) level is left with nothing to do.

What I want is a head-to-head comparison of these two on the specific question of whether symbols push the substrate around. But please don't give me the reflexive "Hofstadter is poetic and vague, Kim is rigorous, Kim wins" — I've got a standing suspicion that they aren't even talking about the same thing, and I'd rather have that suspicion stress-tested than assumed.

To keep myself honest I've been sorting high-level-causation views into two buckets. Call them:

- **leverage**: the high level really does change what happens, but it can only do so by being the kind of thing the low level is already built to be steered by. Key in a lock, thermostat set point, a bit in a control register. Tiny bits of physical stuff gating a lot of downstream physical stuff. Nothing spooky, but you lose real counterfactuals if you try to paraphrase it away.
- **re-description**: the high level causes literally nothing. It's a compact way of narrating causal work that is already one hundred percent done downstairs, and in principle it can be cashed out fully in low-level terms.

So, questions:

My hunch is that the exclusion argument quietly assumes that for a cause to be real it has to be a *distinct* event competing for the same causal work, and that leverage cases break exactly that assumption. 3. There's an identity problem I keep tripping over and I want it handled explicitly. Kim's argument is built for non-reductive physicalists who deny that the mental just is the physical. Hofstadter sounds like he's happy to say a simmball *is* a physical pattern in the careenium, which looks reductive, which looks like it buys him immunity. But then does the immunity cost him the thing he wanted? If symbols are nothing over and above the simms' arrangements, in what sense are there two candidate pushers?
