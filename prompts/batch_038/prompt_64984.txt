I'm building a seminar around the multiple drafts model and I want to check my own verdicts before I teach them, because I'm fairly sure they don't hang together. Going case by case:

Color phi: red spot, then a green one a split second later and a few degrees over. Subject reports smooth motion with the color changing midway. Asked whether the brain composed the motion and slipped it in ahead of the report, or whether the experience was plain red-red and then got overwritten in memory, I say the question is empty — there's no fact of the matter which.

Change blindness, flicker paradigm: a big change sails right past. Here I refuse to say the question's empty. I say the detail was never in the experience, so there was nothing to revise. "Never there" and "there, then revised" feel like genuinely different situations to me, even though I cant point to any difference the subject could detect.

Cutaneous rabbit: taps at the wrist, then the elbow, and the subject feels hops up the forearm in between. I say those intermediate taps really were felt, not reconstructed at report time.

Libet: roughly half a second of cortical activity before an experience becomes reportable, yet subjects date it to the onset. Here I want a fact of the matter, and I want it to be that the experience happened late and the dating is wrong.

Binocular rivalry: incompatible images, perception flips between them. I'm inclined to say there's a determinate moment of the flip and a single stream that has it.

Saccade to a clock face, second hand looks frozen too long: I happily call that a report-level artifact, nothing was frozen in the experience.

So — is there one principle that delivers all six? My stab: there's a fact about what an experience was like and when it happened only insofar as the question makes a difference to what the subject can subsequently do — discriminate, report, act. But that looks like it gives the wrong verdict on Libet and on change blindness, which are the two I care most about. Repair it or offer me a rival, say which case each clause is there for, tell me what it predicts in cases I haven't touched, and be blunt about which of my verdicts it forces me to drop — I'd rather lose one than keep a principle with six exceptions stapled to it. And if you think the whole framing is broken, e.g. that I'm smuggling the theater back in through the notion of the subject's report, say that plainly rather than playing along. Same goes if I've garbled a phenomenon; I know the Libet numbers especially have been picked apart.
