Can you write me a dialogue between two philosophers trying to work out whether a particular dispute is substantive or merely verbal? The dispute: does a company — say, a retail chain — literally believe that one of its suppliers is about to go bust, or is that just a façon de parler for something going on at the level of the people, the paperwork and the decision procedures? One of them thinks it's a real question about what exists and what has mental states; the other thinks that once you've said everything there is to say about the individuals and the organisational machinery, there's nothing left over to disagree about.

I want them to actually try the move of eliminating the contested word and restating the whole thing in supposedly neutral terms — and then to notice that the neutral restatement isn't neutral, so that each accuses the other of smuggling their own view into the shared vocabulary. I'd also like them to run into the problem of whether the deflationist's own conclusion ("there's no fact of the matter") is itself substantive or just another verbal move, and whether that self-application sinks it.

Neither should be the dim one, and they should be responding to each others actual points rather than making parallel speeches. It's fine — probably better — if it ends without a winner, so long as it's clear what would have settled it.
