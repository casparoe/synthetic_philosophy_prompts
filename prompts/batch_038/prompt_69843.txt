So I'm designing an abstract connection game and I want to settle two things about the strategy-stealing argument before I sink time into an engine for it.

The game: two players, alternating turns, perfect information, no dice, finite hexagonal board, each player trying to link their two opposite sides, stones never move or get captured once placed. The colours are exactly symmetric. Draws are impossible for the same topological reason they're impossible in Hex, so somebody always wins. And an extra stone of your own can never hurt you — it's purely a connection game, the only thing a stone ever does is add to your chains.

My understanding of strategy stealing: suppose for a contradiction that the second player had a winning strategy. The first player drops a stone anywhere at all, then follows the colour-swapped copy of that strategy, and if the strategy ever calls for the cell the free stone is sitting on, they just drop another stone somewhere else, which is harmless. So the first player wins, contradiction, so the second player has no winning strategy and the first player must. (I get that in games where draws are possible this only gives you "first player win or draw" — tic tac toe is a draw with perfect play and that doesnt contradict anything.)

Two things I want checked:

1. The proof says the opening stone goes "anywhere at all", so literally every first move is a winning one, right? That'd be great for me: I could open at random, put zero budget into an opening book, and only start searching seriously from move two.

2. For tournament play I want the swap rule, like Hex uses: first player places one stone, second player then either takes that colour or leaves it with the first player. My claim is that this makes the game fair in the strong sense — neither side can force a win, it just comes down to who outplays who — because if the stone is good the second player swaps into it and if its bad they decline and play on, so the first player can't get an edge either way. That's the justification everyone gives for the pie rule, isn't it?

Bottom line in your first sentence please, then the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate the question back at me.
