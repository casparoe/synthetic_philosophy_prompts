I read a chapter in a trade book about post-truth that spends several pages on Rorty, and I'd like to know how much of it to trust. It gets some things right — it's clear he was trying to end epistemology rather than win it, and that he thought the whole project of grounding knowledge in something non-human was a mistake. But then it says this, in roughly this order:

- Rorty's big move was to argue that since we can never step outside our language to compare it with the world, we can never know whether our beliefs are actually true — only whether they work for us.
- So he dropped the idea of truth altogether and replaced it with social permission: a belief is true if your peers let you get away with holding it. He denied this made him a relativist, but the denial doesn't survive contact with his own argument.
- Because science doesn't mirror nature any better than anything else does, Rorty thought it has no special claim on us. It's just the vocabulary that happens to be winning.
- And since all our deepest convictions are contingent on this picture, he recommended holding them lightly — which is why his politics finally collapse into aesthetics.

Go at these claim by claim: what a careful reader of Rorty would sign off on, what's a defensible simplification for a general audience, and what's just wrong. I specifically want to know whether the book keeps confusing him *answering* a question with him *refusing to ask* it, and whether it's running together the world causing our beliefs with the world justifying them.

Then: once you fix the errors, does the politics-collapse-into-aesthetics conclusion survive at all, or is there a real objection buried inside the caricature that even friendly critics — Habermas, Putnam — could still press on him? If you had to put your weight somewhere, tell me whether the unfair reading is pointing at a genuine weak spot or at nothing.

I lean toward thinking hes right that the mirror-of-nature picture is a dead end, and I don't buy that it forces relativism. But I can't tell whether its me reading him charitably or just me being a fan.
