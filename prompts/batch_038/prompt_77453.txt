So, bit of an unusual request. Normally I work as a dental hygienist, but for the last four years I've volunteered with a little nonprofit in my county that runs what we call a rumor desk whenever there's an emergency — river flooding, a wildfire on the ridge, that ice storm two winters back. People message us claims on social media, and during a bad week it's maybe fifteen to twenty a day, and it's usually just me and one other volunteer, neither of us with any journalism training, and we have about ten minutes per claim before we either post it as confirmed, post a we-can't-verify-this-but-here's-what-we-do-know version, or say it's flat false. We have been making the whole thing up as we go and we got burned twice last spring, once badly enough that my brother-in-law, who's a lawyer, told us to never repeat anything that names a person without a second sign-off. The reason I'm writing is that on my own time I've been slowly working through some stuff on Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge, and I think it's actually describing my job. My rough understanding, which you should feel free to correct: knowing something isn't just having a true belief plus decent reasons, your belief has to actually track the fact — so if it weren't true you wouldn't have believed it, and if it were true you would have come to believe it. There's also that example he gives of the grandmother who gets to see her grandson and believes he's fine, where if he'd been sick or worse the family would have gently told her he was fine anyway, so her belief is true and feels perfectly reasonable but it isn't tracking anything. And there's the closure business, that you can't just chain beliefs together and inherit knowing.

What I want is for you to turn that into a procedure two tired volunteers can actually run, not an essay about it. Please act as someone who has genuinely spent a career in both analytic epistemology and newsroom verification, and start with a short brainstorm where you sort out which parts of the theory are load-bearing for us and which are philosophical decoration we should drop, and be blunt if you think some piece of it actively misleads a desk like ours. Then give me the flowchart in words: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer at each one, where the procedure stops at a no, where it hands the call to my editor or to a lawyer because it's a values question or a named person rather than a facts question, and how long each step is supposed to take. Two things I especially need handled. First, the chained case — a lot of what comes to us is only reachable by stitching two claims together, so tell me whether every link has to pass the same tests and what degrades as the chain gets longer. Second, the case where the signals would look identical either way: we've had runs where the same forty-ish accounts post the same thing within an hour, and I've started suspecting someone is manufacturing the volume, which means the volume tells me nothing about whether it's true even when it is true. If there's a name for that failure and a test for catching it, put the test in the procedure.

Then run it on three claims we've basically seen in real life: (a) a message saying the county shelter has run out of insulin and is turning diabetics away, sent by one woman who says her mother is there, with no second source; (b) a claim that the mayor left town before the evacuation order went out, sourced to a screenshot of a license plate at a gas station two hours away; and (c) a claim that the water is safe to drink, which is what the utility itself is posting and what three local news outlets are repeating off the utility. Please make sure at least one of the three comes out with an answer I'm not going to like, and say plainly why, and end with the parts where judgment can't be squeezed out no matter how good the checklist is, plus what this procedure fundamentally cannot do for us. Keep it in plain language — I have no academic background and I don't want to have to decode terms — and no bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, because I can't look any of it up anyway. Think it through carefully before you write, and if my description of the theory above got something backwards, fix it rather than working with my mistake.
