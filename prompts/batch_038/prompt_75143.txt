I've recently taken on advising a small family foundation about where to give, and most of my reading these past months has been the argument between those who want to maximize total well-being and those who think the relief of suffering has priority over the production of happiness. A recurring move in the second camp has me stuck, and I lack the vocabulary for it.

It goes like this. One writer argues that no amount of mild pleasure — a billion pleasant evenings, a billion satisfied minor preferences — could ever outweigh a single hour of agony, and concludes from this that pleasure and pain are not the sort of things that can be weighed against one another at all. A few pages later the same writer compares two charities by adding up small harms averted across many people and declares the larger total the winner, treating mild discomforts and mild satisfactions as perfectly comparable and summable. Asked for the principle, the reply is that there is a threshold of intensity: below it, everything trades off against everything; above it, nothing trades off against anything mild. But what of a pain just below that threshold and one just above it? Either they are comparable and the slide back to the first case begins, or they are not and the view now denies something that seems plainly true.

There is a second wrinkle. These writers generally accept the trade-off inside a single life — they think a person should endure a brutal operation to avoid decades of low-grade pain — while rejecting a trade-off of exactly the same shape when the operation falls on one person and the decades of relief on others.

Is there an established name, or an existing literature, for this configuration? I want the carving more than the label: what exactly is going on, which concepts in the neighborhood come closest and how the thing I have described differs from each of them, whether my two wrinkles are one phenomenon or two, and whether any coherent principle gives these writers what they want without collapsing back into the comparisons they disavow. If nothing established fits, say so plainly and coin a term with a definition and its boundary cases. And please mark clearly what here is settled among people who work on these questions and what is genuinely contested — I would not like to come away believing a disputed position is the consensus.
