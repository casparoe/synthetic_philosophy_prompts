I'm drafting the conceptual framework for a dissertation chapter in the history of economic thought, on the paper-money proposals of the 1730s, and I have run into an argument-form I cannot name. I first met it in Berkeley's Querist, where the case for an Irish national bank issuing and supervising a paper currency rests on the claim that money is not itself wealth: it counts as riches only to the extent that it sets idle hands to work and lets one person command the labour of another. On that account a token with no intrinsic worth can outperform gold and silver precisely because a valuable medium gets hoarded, melted down, or shipped abroad, while a worthless one keeps circulating and keeps people employed. I take the underlying move to be a general one rather than distinctively monetary: establish that some item's worth lies wholly in what it causes agents to do, and conclude that its lack of backing is an advantage rather than a defect. The same shape appears in institutional token economies, where a ward or classroom pays out paper slips redeemable for privileges and the designers treat the slips' worthlessness as what makes them safe to hand out liberally; and in arguments about credentialing, where a degree is said to be nothing over and above a signal of prior effort and is therefore cheap to multiply.

What interests me is the second half of the move, which usually arrives without being argued for. Once the medium has been shown to be a counter rather than a commodity, the argument tends to slide to the conclusion that the quantity of counters is unconstrained, or at least that adding more of them can only help: if paper is worth nothing in itself and does its work by provoking activity, why not provoke more activity by issuing more of it? Proponents of sovereign currency issuance make something like this move when they observe that a state borrowing in its own currency cannot run out of it; token-economy designers make it when they inflate the slip supply on the grounds that the slips are merely symbolic; and metric-inflation arguments make it about citation counts and impact scores. Yet the premise seems to license the opposite of an unlimited supply. If a counter's value is entirely a function of the activity it tracks and provokes, then issuing counters out of proportion to that activity should destroy the very thing that made them useful — and my reading of Berkeley is that he wants the paper supply calibrated to the industry it is meant to excite, not expanded without limit, which would make the inflationary corollary a corruption of the instrumentalist premise rather than a consequence of it. So I cannot tell whether the pattern I have described is a real, recurring move with an identity of its own, or a straw man that fuses two claims — a functional theory of value and a permissive theory of quantity — that need not go together.

So: does this bundled pattern have an established name? I have been pointed toward fiduciary or token money, chartalism and the state theory of money, the idea of money as a veil, money as a social technology, Berkeley's own instrumentalism, and the quantity theory as the standard corrective — but each of these seems to name either the functional premise alone or the objection to it, rather than the inference from one to the other. For each of the closest candidates, tell me how far it reaches and where it stops short, and say which one you would nominate if I insisted on an existing label. If none captures the move, coin a term for it, define it so that it is cleanly separable from its neighbours, and give me the boundary cases that show where the edges lie: does the quantity theory's denial of long-run neutrality count as an instance of the pattern or as the reply to one? Is Gresham's law inside or outside it? Does the claim that diplomas are only signals and so we should issue more of them instantiate the same thing, or a merely analogous one? And commit to a verdict on my real question: is the bundled move a genuine tendency of instrumentalist reasoning, or have I mis-described instrumentalism by loading it with a conclusion its better proponents explicitly reject? I want a definite position with reasons rather than a survey of the options, no references or reading list (I have those), and a note on the two or three places where you are least confident that your carving matches how these concepts are actually used.
