I am auditing my own intuitions about when interference with a person's conduct is legitimate, using Mill's *On Liberty* — especially the chapter on applications, and the India passages in it and in *Considerations on Representative Government* — as a test bed. I am taking a seminar on empire and liberal political thought, and after a long argument with the teaching assistant I decided that the only honest way to see where I stand is to write down my verdicts on particular cases first and look for the principle afterwards, rather than starting from a slogan. Here are the cases, with what I currently think about each:

1. A mail steamer leaving Bombay with cholera aboard is held in quarantine, delaying a passenger's business by two weeks. Legitimate, even though the delay harms only the passengers themselves.
2. A widow in a famine-struck district refuses medicine for her son because a local practitioner forbids it, and the boy dies. Legitimate to override her, on account of the boy.
3. An English clerk drinks heavily every evening in his own rooms, harms no one, and loses his post for it. Not legitimate to interfere — though I also think his employer may decline to renew him, which sits badly with me.
4. That same clerk, employed as a signalman, is barred from drinking on duty. Legitimate.
5. A Calcutta editor publishes an accurate account of a collector's arbitrary confiscations and is imprisoned. Not legitimate.
6. That same office is closed for printing an incendiary pamphlet urging the killing of tax collectors. Legitimate.
7. A merchant refuses to have his contract dispute heard by British judges and takes it to a panchayat instead, and the administration permits both routes. Legitimate — but I cannot say which limb of my view this falls under, since nobody's judgment is being overridden at all.
8. A genuinely competent administrator rules a province without any consultation of its inhabitants, on the ground that their own rulers were worse. Not legitimate, whatever the quality of the administration.
9. The argument that a foreign ruler can accomplish for a people what that people cannot accomplish for themselves — impartial law, security, revenue honestly collected — and that this is precisely what makes his despotism permissible. Unpersuaded, though I cannot yet say why.
10. A people taught the arts of self-government but excluded from its exercise for two generations. Not legitimate.
11. A village forced to send its children to school, on the ground that they will otherwise grow up incapable of judging anything. Torn, and the case I most want an answer about.
12. A village whose children are made to sit an examination whose passing is a precondition for holding land. Not legitimate.

My tentative principle is this: interference is legitimate only to prevent harm to assignable others, never to make the person interfered with better off; except that where an agent is not yet capable of being guided by free and equal discussion, interference aimed at his improvement is legitimate for the duration of that incapacity. Two things trouble me. The capacity clause looks like it is doing all the work, and it seems to license case 8, which I have already condemned; and case 7 satisfies neither clause, since nothing there is paternalistic in either direction. Cases 2 and 8 are, I think, the hardest pair to hold together, and 11 and 12 look almost alike on the surface while pulling me in opposite directions.

Please write in American English and put your bottom line — the principle you would settle on, in one or two sentences — at the very top, before any elaboration. Then state the principle clause by clause, saying for each clause which of my cases motivates it and what the clause costs. Say plainly which of my verdicts the principle must overturn, and defend the overturning rather than merely announcing it; if a simpler principle can be had at the price of a verdict I care about, tell me which verdict that is. Address cases 7, 9, and 11 explicitly, since they are where I expect the principle to strain. Finally, give me its verdicts on three cases I have not listed, of your own choosing but comparable in texture to these, and keep the whole response under 900 words.
