Been running a small product team for about three years and there's this one project I keep pouring people into even though its clearly not going anywhere. But here's the thing, I don't feel like I'm dodging it. If you asked me point blank I'd say yeah, sure, the retention numbers are bad, I'd say that out loud.

Only I've noticed I've quietly arranged things so nobody actually does ask. I moved our metrics review into a meeting that the two people who track churn don't come to. I dropped the 1-on-1s with the engineers on that project and told myself it was to give them autonomy. An analyst sent me a doc last spring laying out the curve, and I read the first paragraph, got a bad feeling in my stomach, marked it unread, and then didn't think about it once for like two months. I'm not making that up, I genuinely didn't. I've gone back looking and I can't find a belief hiding in there. I don't secretly think the project is working. My wife says "you know deep down" and I honestly don't think I do.

And there was never a moment where I sat down and decided to do any of this. No plan, no little voice going "don't look at that." It just kind of... happened, one small convenient decision at a time. So what is it then? Is this the same thing as being weak willed, or being overextended and not having time to think? Or is it a different animal, and if so how do you tell which one you're dealing with from the inside, since from the inside it feels identical to just being busy?

Also what do I actually do about it. The obvious advice is "look at the data," but looking at the data is apparently the exact thing I keep arranging not to do, and the part of me that would have to notice the arranging is the part that's compromised. Feels a bit like being told to use a broken ruler to check whether the ruler is bent.
