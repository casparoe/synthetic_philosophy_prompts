Bit of an odd one, and it's half research for a book and half genuine bewilderment, so bear with me.

I'm a novelist, British, and two years ago I moved to Leipzig for my partner's job. When I was a baby my parents had me christened in a church in Yorkshire, and as far as the German authorties are concerned that's still the operative fact about me, because every month the tax office skims a slice of my income tax — 8 or 9 per cent of it, I think — and hands it straight to the regional Protestant church. Nobody ever asked me. There's no form, no checkbox, no moment at which I said yes. The only way out, I'm told, is to book an appointment at a government office, show up in person, and pay a fee of a few dozen euros to be struck off — after which I apparently can't be married in a church, can't stand as a godparent, and wouldn't get a church funeral.

My German friends think I'm overreacting. One of them, a lawyer, made the claim I find most interesting: the payment isn't a religious matter at all, it's a membership subscription. The church is a body the state recognises, and the state merely collects its dues for a handeling fee, exactly like an employer passing gym subscriptions on through payroll. On that picture, quitting is just cancelling a membership you never signed up for, and there's nothing left to object to.

Meanwhile my family back home insist this is a specifically German oddity and that Britain is properly secular, which I find hard to swallow given we have a head of state who is also the head of a church, bishops with seats in the upper house, and prayers before parliamentary business — and yet nobody over there pays anything resembling that monthly slice.

So, really one question with two halves. Is the membership-subscription story a fair account of what's actually going on, or is it papering over a difference in kind between a club you join and a status the state assigns you at birth and then charges you to leave? And given that — am I being precious if the whole arrangement leaves me feeling that the government has recorded a verdict about what I believe without ever consulting me, especially when my own country does something quieter along the same lines?

Bottom line in your very first sentence, please, then the reasoning; no preamble and don't repeat the question back at me. And write it in British English, since I'll be reading bits of it aloud over breakfast.
