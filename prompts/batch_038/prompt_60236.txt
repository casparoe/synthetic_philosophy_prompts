You are an expert in normative ethics with a particular command of the demandingness debate about consequentialism, and you are also an accomplished satirist. Open with a brief brainstorm — one paragraph, no more — identifying which *genuine* features of the maximizer's position are most exploitable: agent-neutrality; the disappearance of supererogation, so that no "exceeds expectations" rating is available at all; marginal-utility accounting of ordinary household spending; rest and personal projects holding value only insofar as they restore productive capacity; and the collective-action move capping what any one associate owes at their fair share under full compliance.

Then write, deadpan and in an institutional register, the "Associate Wellbeing and Sustainability Handbook" issued by the Office of Aggregate Welfare at a fictional research institute that has internalized act-consequentialism without remainder. About 700 words, formatted as numbered sections with headings, and it must include:

- a sustainability allowance derived solely from diminishing marginal returns, never from any agent-relative prerogative;
- a grievance procedure for associates who complain that the institute's arithmetic leaves no room for integrity, together with the form-letter disposition;
- a disciplinary file entry on an associate who, having pulled his wife rather than a stranger from a flooded stairwell, reported afterward that his reason was a calculation about which rescue the calculus permitted — the report that earns Williams's phrase "one thought too many" — and who is here commended rather than censured, with the reasoning made explicit;
- a closing wellbeing survey in which every answer option is the same.

Three constraints. The irony must stay legible without the handbook ever winking. It may not concede a single genuinely non-instrumental permission: wherever a concession appears to surface, that appearance must itself turn out to be a compliance violation. And no invented objection may do work that the theory's own logic, correctly applied, cannot do — name no real organizations or living persons.
