I am a third-year doctoral student working on the metaphysics of the special sciences, and the argument below is one I constructed for a dissertation chapter. My adviser says it dies somewhere in the middle but declines to tell me where, so I would like an independent diagnosis.

First, the intended readings, since several steps depend on them. Let M be a psychological property — being in pain will do throughout. Let R be the causal role that, on role-functionalism, defines M: roughly, being typically caused by damage, causing aversiveness and withdrawal, interacting with belief about its object to produce distress, and so forth. Call any physical property P whose instances occupy R in some system a realizer of M in that system. Assume the standard multiple-realizability thesis: there are actual and possible systems in which M is instantiated whose realizers are physically heterogeneous — human C-fiber firing, the very differently organized nervous system of a cephalopod, a suitable synthetic architecture — where the heterogeneity goes to the internal constitution of the realizing state and is not merely a matter of scale or peripheral variation.

The argument:

(1) A putative property is genuine — a real kind, fit to figure in non-accidental generalizations and to serve as a term in causal explanation — only if its instances are united by a common basis: something all and only the instances share, in virtue of which they share the property's characteristic causal profile.

(2) A mere disjunction P₁ ∨ P₂ ∨ … ∨ Pₙ provides no such basis. Its instances share nothing beyond each falling under some disjunct, and whatever causal profile we attribute to the disjunction belongs, on any occasion, to whichever disjunct is present. (This is what is wrong with grue-ness, and what makes jade a doubtful mineral kind.)

(3) So if M is multiply realized by heterogeneous P₁…Pₙ, M is identical neither with any Pᵢ nor with their disjunction. Type identity theory fails.

(4) Role-functionalism's replacement is the second-order property of having some property or other that occupies R.

(5) In any world w, that second-order property is coextensive with the disjunction of all and only the properties occupying R in w.

(6) If two properties are coextensive in every world, then — given the extensional individuation of properties that (3) already relied on — they are the same property.

(7) Hence the second-order property is the disjunction of the realizers, and so, by (2) and (1), is not a genuine property.

(8) Therefore role-functionalism does not rescue M; it relocates the disjunction one level up. There is no property of being in pain, only a predicate applied to heterogeneous states.

(9) But (1)–(8) exploit nothing distinctive about the mental. They apply to any property individuated by what it does rather than by what it is made of: money, gene, predator, catalyst, immune response, memory. Each is realized by heterogeneous substrates; each is therefore, by (8), not a genuine property.

(10) I cannot accept (9)'s conclusion: it entails that the special sciences have no real kinds whatsoever, which is exactly the autonomy Putnam and Fodor invoked multiple realizability to secure.

(11) So either (1) or (10) must go. If (1) goes, (3) loses its engine — the heterogeneity of the realizers is precisely what (1) was making fatal — and multiple realizability is no argument against type identity at all. If (10) goes, functionalism is eliminated along with biology and economics.

(12) Either way, multiple realizability does not support functionalism. It is either inert or self-consuming.

What I would like from you:

(a) Identify the step you reject and explain why it looked impeccable. I have a private suspicion about (6), but I cannot see how to reject (6) without surrendering the extensional individuation that made (3) available, so I do not yet trust the suspicion; and I suspect I may be misdescribing my own argument.

(b) If the argument is in fact valid, which of (1) and (10) is cheaper to abandon, and what does each cost? In particular: does abandoning (1) leave me with the type identity theory that (3) refutes, or only with something weaker?

(c) Take the standard replies in turn — Kim's second-order-property analysis and the exclusion problems it attracts, Yablo's determinable/determinate proposal, Fodor's defense of the special sciences, Shapiro's contention that genuine multiple realization is far rarer than advertised, Block's liberalism/chauvinism dilemma — and say which of my steps each one targets and what it must pay. I am most interested in whether any of them can block (7) without also blocking (3), since a reply that dissolves the threat to functionalism by dissolving the threat to type identity is no help to me.

(d) Finally, and most importantly: is there a formulation of (1) strong enough to deliver (3) yet weak enough to escape (9)? If there is, state the principle exactly, with the qualification that does the work. If there is not, say so plainly — because that would mean a large literature has been resting on a principle no one can state coherently.
