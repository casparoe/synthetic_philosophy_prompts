My friend does philosophy of measurement and thinks the whole bootstrapping fuss is overblown. His pitch: bootstrapping is just instrument calibration. You trust the fuel gauge enough to read it, log the readings, note that the car sputtered out roughly when the gauge said it would, and at the end you've earned the belief that the gauge tracks fuel level — exactly what a lab does when it calibrates a thermometer against the ice bath it read on trust, or checks a balance using weights whose masses it believed off a certificate. So the easy-knowledge worry is a philosophers' artifact, not a real defect in the reasoning.
