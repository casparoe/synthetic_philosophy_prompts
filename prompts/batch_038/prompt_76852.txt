So — I'm 43, I do labeling compliance for a food importer, and I'm finally taking the philosophy of language class I never got around to in my twenties. We're on Twin Earth next week and I volunteered to open the discussion. My angle: everyone in the group hears "Twin Earth" as a story about weird chemistry, and I want to argue that's not where the force of the argument lives. The real claim is that what your word picks out depends on facts outside your head, and the chemistry is just the most vivid way to dramatize it. So I want a case that makes the point with no exotic substance anywhere in it.

Build me a Twin-Earth-style pair to these specs:

- Two speakers, as internally alike as you can plausibly get them without a duplication machine — identical twins seperated in infancy, if that helps, or whatever gets you closest.
- Their local physical environments are identical too. Same water, same rock, same flora and fauna, nothing swapped, nothing substituted. The usual lever is off the table.
- And yet an externalist should be able to say their words differ in extension — with the difference biting somewhere concrete. Give me the test someone could actually run, or the mistake someone would actually make, that exposes it.
- The word is a natural-kind term. Not a proper name, not an indexical, and not a term whose content is pinned down by a legal standard or a labeling regulation. I live that life every day and it makes the case way too easy.
- Realistic throughout: a real trade, real places, no sci-fi. And no reskinning the classics — no H2O/XYZ, no arthritis, no elm/beech.

The scenario itself: one paragraph, no more.

Then show me the design works. Say what each speaker's word refers to, and identify the extra fact that fixes the difference — specifically, what would a fully informed internalist still be missing if she knew everything about both brains and everything about both towns? Where does that fact live? Then name the single feature of the case doing the work, and tell me what happens to the verdicts if I delete it: does it collapse back into ordinary Twin Earth, or into the social/arthritis kind of case? Last, give the internalist her best counterattack, and be honest about whether she can just say the word is ambiguous, or that you've smuggled in community usage and the environment isn't pulling any weight at all.

The point of the whole exercise is to show that externalism is about dependence, not about liquids — so if your case ends up needing a strange substance to generate the difference, it's a failure. And don't stipulate the verdicts: the difference in extension has to fall out of the history you set up, not be asserted at the end.
