Christopher Nolan's Memento gives us a protagonist who manages his own evidence deliberately: Leonard destroys keepsakes so that his future self will reach a conclusion he has chosen in advance, retells another amnesiac's case as a way of describing his own at one remove, and at the end knowingly falsifies a clue pointing to a man he has good reason to think is innocent, because he needs a purpose. I would like the film read against Parfit's hitchhiker and the wider problem of rational irrationality, with Kavka's toxin as the nearest cousin. The crux I want pressed is this: the hitchhiker's predicament depends on transparency — the driver reads the agent's disposition straight off his face — whereas Leonard's project depends on the opposite, on his self-manipulation being invisible to him once it has taken hold. Does that inversion make Leonard an instance of the same phenomenon, a structurally different one, or simply a broken analogy? Please say which scenes the hitchhiker's logic genuinely illuminates, where the film resists the reading, and what the film shows about rational irrationality that the standard thought experiments leave out. I am teaching this in a practical-philosophy seminar and would rather not present the film as an argument in costume: if the honest verdict is that Leonard is confused about his own rationality rather than exercising it, or that the film equivocates between the two, say so plainly.
