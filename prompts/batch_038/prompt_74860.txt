A position paper circulating in my lab frames its proposed benchmark suite for trained models with Dennett's ladder of minds: Darwinian creatures whose repertoire is fixed by genetic endowment, Skinnerian creatures that can be shaped by reinforcement within a lifetime, Popperian creatures that carry an internal model of their surroundings and so can try candidate actions in imagination rather than in the world, letting their conjectures die in their stead, and Gregorian creatures that acquire external tools — language above all — that restructure how they think. The paper maps each rung onto a training paradigm: architecture and population search for Darwinian, RL and RLHF for Skinnerian, model-based planning over a learned world model for Popperian, and large language models for Gregorian. It then argues that because the Gregorian rung sits at the top, and because language is what carried us there, a fluent language model has already cleared the rungs beneath it, so probes of those lower capacities — lifetime reinforcement learning, navigation, counterfactual simulation — are inapplicable to it and measure the wrong thing.

Audit the analogy. Give the correspondence table explicitly, then separate the disanalogies that actually bear on that argument from the ones that are merely local. In particular, identify what the source domain quietly imports: at which timescale selection operates, whose history it is that gets selected, what makes a rung a prerequisite for the next, and what it takes for an external tool to count as internalized. Does the conclusion survive once those are on the table? If a ladder is the wrong shape for a trained model, propose a better figure and say what it licenses instead.
