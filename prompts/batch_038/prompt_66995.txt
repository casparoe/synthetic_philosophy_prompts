Two guys in my investing discord have been going back and forth about Kelly betting for like a week and I've honestly lost track of who's right. Reporting it as best I can, roughly in order — they were typing at each other, not writing essays, so I've compressed:

1. Dev opens by saying theres exactly one correct bet size on a favorable gamble and it isn't a lifestyle choice. His argument: repeat the same positive-edge bet, reinvest your winnings, and there's a unique fraction that maximizes the long-run growth rate of your bankroll. Everything else either grows slower or busts. So people who say "bet size is just your risk tolerance talking" haven't done the math.

2. Nadia says he's fooling himself: run expected utility maximization with log utility of wealth and out pops precisely the Kelly fraction. So it isn't utility-free at all, it's log utility wearing a lab coat. Somebody with square-root utility bets less and has made no mistake.

3. Dev says the growth-rate derivation never mentions utility anywhere, that's the whole point. He'd been reading about ergodicity economics, whatever that is. His version: with a multiplicative gamble, the average across a bunch of parallel copies of you is not the average across time for one you, and those come apart badly. Expected value optimizes a statistic no single person ever actually observes — maximize it here and you shove your entire bankroll on any coin with an edge, and you're broke with probability one. Kelly is what one person living through time should care about, and that's objective.

4. Nadia, two counters: (a) the setup never happens — same bet, same odds, forever, whole fortune at stake each round. For a genuine one-off there's no growth rate to maximize and expected value is all there is. (b) You never know the odds. Feed your estimated edge into the Kelly formula and you systematically bet too much, which is why everyone sensible uses half Kelly. So the "objective" optimum is computed out of numbers you invented.

5. Dev: estimation error is an implementation detail, not a refutation, and half Kelly is just her admitting she's risk averse after all, i.e. right back to utility functions. Also he says she's got the trade wrong: half the Kelly fraction costs you half the growth rate, you don't get to keep the optimality and skip the swings.

6. Nadia: no, half Kelly keeps about three quarters of the growth rate and cuts the variance of your log wealth by a factor of four, best trade in gambling.

7. Dev: and she's missing the knockout — given enough rounds the Kelly bettor ends up ahead of every other constant-fraction bettor with probability one, including the double-Kelly guy, whose expected value is strictly higher. So one criterion says the double-Kelly bettor has more expected money and another says he's almost surely poorer, and only one of those two paths is the one that happens.

8. Nadia: that's her point, not his — if the criteria split like that then "the rational bet size" isn't something the math settles. It's settled by what you're trying to do, and they've been shouting past each other the whole time.
