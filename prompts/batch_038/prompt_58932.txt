so i'm giving a talk at a museum-studies reading group on authenticity and I've done the dangerous thing of writing down what I actually believe about fakes, forgeries and originals, so that people can attack it in public. Before I do that, can you tell me whether the set hangs together? I want an audit, not a lecture: which of these pull against each other, ranked by severity rather than all flagged as equally bad, which pairs only look inconsistent and dissolve once you say what's really going on, and for each genuine tension which commitment has to go and what giving it up costs me elsewhere in the list.

Here it is.

1. Two works you can't tell apart by looking are aesthetically equal. All the value is in the experience of the object, so anything you have to be told before you can see it isn't part of the work.
2. That said, once a forgery is exposed I think it belongs in the study collection, not hung in the main gallery as the masterpiece it looks like.
3. Artistic achievement is historical. Part of what makes a work great is that it did something that hadn't been done — originality isn't a bonus feature bolted onto a good-looking object, it's built into the value.
4. I'm deeply sceptical of the attribution premium. The market pays for names, and the constant demotions of workshop pieces from famous masters to their assistants show how little the picture itself has to do with the price. A superb painting by a pupil ought to count for as much as the master's.
5. On conservation I'm a minimal-intervention person: reversible, do as little as possible, never strip a surface back so it looks new. Craquelure and patina are part of what you're actually looking at, and part of what's valuable.
6. You should interpret a work in light of what the maker was trying to do with it — a picture that reads one way to a modern viewer might not mean that at all.
7. I'd happily go to a show of forgeries, provided they're labelled as forgeries. That's the interesting way to look at them, and it doesn't feel like a compromise of anything above.

I'm not going to tell you which ones I suspect, because I've gone back and forth and I no longer trust my own sense of where the trouble is. Two things I would like from the answer, though. First, be concrete rather than programmatic: for the worst tension, tell me what a defender of each side would say and why that reply doesn't work, since I'll get exactly those replies from the room. Second, if there's some weakened or restricted version of (1) that could survive alongside everything else — a distinction I'm allowed to draw that makes it consistent rather than merely abandoned — I'd much rather have that than be told to delete it.

British English, please. And assume nobody in the room has read the literature on this, so don't lean on specialist terminology you haven't unpacked.
