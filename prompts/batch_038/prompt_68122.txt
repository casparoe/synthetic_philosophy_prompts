Our philosophy-of-physics reading group has been going round in circles on substantivalism versus relationism since October and its properly stalled now — not stalled the way hard questions stall, but stalled the way where everyone's position has set like concrete and the same three arguments come back every fortnight.

Here's where things sit as I understand them. Camp one: the hole argument shows the points of the manifold aren't real individuals, because diffeomorphically related solutions of GR agree on everything anybody could ever observe, so if you insist the points have identities of their own you've bought yourself a radical indeterminism for no benefit; what's real is the invariant structure. Camp two: that's too quick — you can keep the points and simply deny that diffeomorphic models describe different possibilities, so the indeterminism was never real, it was an artefact of counting gauge redundancy twice. Camp three: camp two's escape is empty, because if diffeomorphic models really are the same possibility then the points have done no work at all, so you either throw them away (which is what camp one wanted from the start) or you're committed to differences between possible worlds that nothing qualitative could ever detect — haecceitism through the back door.

My own hypothesis, which nobody will engage with: this isn't a disagreement about spacetime at all. All three camps accept the same theory, the same models, the same solutions, and not one of them can point to an observation the others would describe differently. What they're actually arguing about is how much surplus structure a physical theory is allowed to carry before it becomes metaphysically objectionable, which is a norm about theory-building rather then a fact about the world. Hence the deadlock: it's being run as a physics debate when it's really about philosophical appetite.

Right or wrong? I want a diagnosis, not a survey — I know the positions, I've just lost the plot on what kind of thing the disagreement is. Is it verbal, in that the three camps mean different things by 'real' and by 'the same possibility'? Substantive but mislocated? Two different questions travelling under one heading — an interpretive question about a formalism on one side, a metaphysical question about objects and identity on the other? And I'd like you to take seriously the fact that camp three's move looks like it ought to dissolve the whole argument rather than prolong it, and yet two years on it hasn't. Why not?

Then the bit I can't work out by myself. A colleague reckons the dispute is decidable by future physics: if quantum gravity turns out to be discrete, with finitely many fundamental atoms of spacetime and a fixed causal order between them, you simply can't run the diffeomorphism trick, the atoms are just there, and substantivalism wins by default. Is that a genuine empirical resolution, or a category mistake that misses what the argument was ever about? And given whatever diagnosis you land on, what would actually count as progress here — a theorem, a new theory, a distinction nobody's thought of, a change of subject — or should we just stop meeting?
