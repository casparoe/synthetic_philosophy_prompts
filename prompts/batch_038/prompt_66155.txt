I retired three years ago after forty-one years as a structural biologist, most of which I spent doing two things rather than one: solving structures, and teaching graduate students how to look at a figure without being misled by it. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy in a systematic way for the first time, and I have become absorbed by Otto Neurath and by the picture-statistics work he directed in Vienna — first the Vienna Method, later ISOTYPE — with Gerd Arntz doing the drawing and Marie Reidemeister performing what the team called transformation. What has struck me is not the usual talking point about pictures being internationally intelligible, but Neurath's own restraint: he never claimed that ISOTYPE was a language, and was willing to call it a technique that works like one while remaining dependent on words to do its work. As far as I can tell, he did not say where that boundary falls or why it falls there.

My half-formed idea, which I want turned into a proper proposal, is that this restraint can be given content and then tested against the practice. As I read it, a chart built on the first rule of ISOTYPE — never enlarge a symbol to indicate a greater quantity, always repeat the same-sized unit — can express pictorially only what repetition and juxtaposition can accomplish: counting, comparing, ranking, part to whole, and perhaps change rendered as a sequence of such arrays. What it seems unable to do without borrowing a sentence is anything that imposes structure on structure: negation, the conditional, quantification over kinds rather than over individual units, and, most interesting to me, the statement of the chart's own rule of interpretation. The legend that fixes what one repeated figure stands for, and the instruction that the viewer is to count rather than measure, are not themselves depictable; they have to be said in a host language. My suspicion is that a picture language whose only compositional operation is the accumulation of identical units cannot state its own semantics, and that this is a structural feature of such a system rather than a contingent limitation of 1930s woodcut technique. I also want the proposal to decide, rather than assume, whether the familiar result that a language cannot define its own truth predicate makes this a genuine structural analogy or merely a suggestive one, and whether anything here bears on Neurath's larger program of unified science or is local to the design project.

I am applying for a twelve-month late-career research fellowship at an institute for the philosophy of science, and the panel expects a two-page proposal; please write it. State the question so precisely that a reader could tell whether it has been answered. Explain why the two obvious positions — the deflationary reading on which Neurath's restraint is mere modesty about an applied design project, and the ambitious reading on which ISOTYPE was a genuine fragment of a language — both leave the question open rather than settling it. Give a month-by-month method for what I would actually do, and say explicitly whether the project must engage the empirical psychology of chart perception or can proceed by conceptual argument alone. Justify that approach against the obvious alternatives: a straight history of the Vienna workshop, a general theory of depiction and description, or a comparison with contemporary infographic practice. Say what a successful result would look like in terms a non-specialist panel could recognize, what would count as the project failing, and give an honest assessment of whether twelve months is realistic. Do not supply a bibliography, a literature review by citation, or any references — I will add those myself. Likewise, please do not invent specific charts, archival holdings, dates, or quotations; where the argument would normally rest on one, describe the kind of evidence required and I will supply the instance. You should be able to write this from general knowledge alone. Close the proposal with a single final line beginning "Verdict:" followed by exactly one of the labels tractable as scoped, tractable after narrowing, or not tractable in twelve months, then a hyphen, then one sentence naming the question the project answers.
