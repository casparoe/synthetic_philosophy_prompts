Ok so I'm 17 and I'm on the committee of my school's ethics society. We meet Thursdays and we've had the same arguement three weeks running. For the first time ever we actually have money: £240 from a bake sale, and the school said they'll match it up to £150 if we hand in a proper plan by half term, so call it about £390 total. Plus roughly fifteen people willing to give up some time, and about twenty-five weeks left of the year. That's the whole resource pool, it isn't coming back, and if we don't use it we just look stupid.

We want to spend it on animal welfare. The problem is the disagreement always lands in the same place: how much does a bug count, really.

A guy who came in to talk to us in October gave us this scoring rule — I'm fairly sure he made it up himself, he didn't say it was anyone's. He said: moral weight for a creature = intensity × breadth. Intensity is how much a single experience can matter to it, i.e. how bad the worst moment of its life can be for it. Breadth is how many different kinds of thing can go well or badly for it — a dog has food, companionship, play, dread of the vet, some sense of a future it's heading into; a moth maybe has one or two; a thermostat arguably has a sliver of one (being set to the wrong temperature). Then you multiply by how many individuals there are. And, he said, you should probably also multiply by your confidence that the thing is sentient at all, because otherwise you end up with an argument that says we should devote enormous resources to gravel just in case.

So using something roughly like that, here's what's actually on the table for us. I want you to pick an order and defend it, not just list considerations at me.

1. Get the biology department to stop putting the mealworms in the freezer before practicals and do whatever the alternative is. Cheap, quick, we could probably do it in a fortnight. Honest caveat: I don't know if freezing is even bad for a mealworm, and I think not knowing that is kind of the whole issue.
2. Put money behind a campaign about prawn and crab welfare. One of our members read something about how they're killed in shops and supermarkets and it's genuinely grim, and there are a colossal number of them.
3. Give to the local wildlife/hedgehog group. Everyone in the club likes this one because it's visible, it's cute, we can go and help on a Saturday, and we'd know exactly where the money went.
4. Spend it on making the case at school that insects matter at all — assemblies, posters, a whole thing. Changes nothing directly for any animal, might change a few hundred people.
5. Spend the year arguing about the scoring rule instead, on the grounds that we don't yet know enough to spend the money well, and hand the cash over at the end.

What I want from you:

- An actual ranking of the five, with the money and the hours split however you think is right, and the reasoning for the order. Be explicit about which criteria you're using and where they fight each other — 3 is more tractable and more fun, 2 might have way more expected weight per pound, 5 is about the deepest question but is also the easiest way to spend a year doing nothing and feel virtuous about it.
- Which single crux does most of the ordering hang on? Like, if we only get one argument settled on Thursdays, which one.
- Which option should we cut even though it's the popular one in the room. Say why it feels good and why that feeling isn't a reason.
- What would you want to have found out by June that would make you reshuffle the list, and what would make you leave it alone?

Then the bit I actually care about most: how much of your ranking survives if the scoring rule is wrong? Run it again under a couple of variants — say, intensity matters but breadth doesn't at all, and the opposite, and the version where you refuse to multiply by confidence. Tell me which of the five options is robust across those and which one only looks good because of the specific rule we were handed. If the answer is that 5 wins under every variant, I want you to say so plainly rather than diplomatically burying it.

Please write it in British English. No reading list, no suggested papers, no references — we're not going to look any of it up, and you'd probably invent the titles. Just the reasoning.
