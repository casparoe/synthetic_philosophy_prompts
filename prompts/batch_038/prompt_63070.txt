You are a political philosopher whose work is on global distributive justice, the ethics of immigration, and the boundaries of the demos. Please think carefully and treat what follows as a genuine audit rather than a survey: I want you to trace what each of my commitments commits me to, not to tell me who holds which view.

Context. I have been asked to submit a one-page statement of my position in advance of a workshop on the ethics of membership, so that the other participants can attack it rather than guess at it. Before I send it, I want to know whether the package hangs together. I have deliberately not ordered the items by how confident I am in them, and I am not going to indicate which of them I would be prepared to revise: I would rather you locate the pressure points yourself.

The commitments:

1. Distributive justice is global in scope. Every person's claims count equally, and nationality is not in itself a ground for weighting those claims.
2. Nonetheless, a democratic state acts within its rights — as a matter of right, not of charity or supererogation — when it funds a health and social insurance scheme for its own citizens and residents to the exclusion of non-members, and when it restricts immigration in order to sustain that scheme.
3. The ground of the priority asserted in (2) is reciprocity: those who contribute to, and depend upon, a shared cooperative scheme of institutions owe one another more than they owe outsiders, and may shape the terms of that scheme accordingly.
4. The composition of the demos is not a pre-political given. A decision about who belongs must be justifiable to everyone subject to it, including those coerced by it, whether or not they are members.
5. Being born in one country rather than another is a paradigm case of brute luck. I reject any principle that permits a fact of that kind to set the terms of a person's life chances, and I support standing, institutionalized transfers from wealthy states to the global poor, not merely emergency relief.
6. Methodologically: a theory of justice must be addressed to agents who can actually act on it, and principles that no realistic agent could implement are defective for that reason.
7. Methodologically also: the fact that a principle is presently politically infeasible is not itself a ground for rejecting it, since what is feasible is endogenous to what people can be brought to demand.
8. Patriotism — a preferential concern for one's own polity and its people — is a genuine virtue, not a bias to be grudgingly tolerated.

Three case verdicts I want to keep:

A. A wealthy state may give its own citizens priority in sponsoring spouses, parents, and minor children for admission, even where the same admissions slots would do more good if allocated by need.
B. The same state may not operate a fast track to citizenship for co-ethnics abroad who have no other connection to it.
C. A state's obligation to admit people fleeing persecution is stronger than its obligation to admit people fleeing poverty, and the difference is not reducible to the comparative urgency of their needs.

What I want back. Begin by laying out, briefly, the dependency structure among these items — which are doing justificatory work for which, and which are load-bearing rather than decorative. Then deliver the audit, ordered by severity rather than in the order I listed them. For each tension you identify, state (i) precisely which items generate it, (ii) whether a standard reconciliation is available and, if so, why it succeeds or fails here — I want the mechanism, not a label, (iii) what has to give, and (iv) what the cheapest revision costs me, including which of the surviving items it puts under new pressure. Where a pair only looks like a tension, say so explicitly and say what does the dissolving; I expect several of my items to be innocent, and I do not want them flagged alongside the guilty ones. If a single revision would clear the most ground at once, identify it and say what it still leaves unresolved.

Be as committed as the material allows: give me your actual verdict about which item is the weakest link, not a menu of options presented as equally defensible. Close by telling me what would change your mind — which argument or consideration would lead you to a different verdict about the most severe tension, and where in your own reasoning above you are most likely to have gone wrong.
