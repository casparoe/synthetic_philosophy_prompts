I'm 74 and two years retired from systematics — forty-odd years describing beetles and fighting with colleagues about which groupings were real and which were just dumping grounds. Philosophy is my new hobby; I go to a Thursday reading group at the library. This month we're on hedonism, and we hit the snag you'd expect: a cold beer after mowing, a hot bath, the click when a proof finally falls out right, and getting one over on a rival don't seem to feel like one and the same thing. Our ringleader waved the whole problem away by announcing that pleasure isnt a natural kind — its a wastebasket category, like 'invertebrate' or 'weed', and once you see that there's nothing left to discuss.

Having spent my career on precisely that distinction I should have been delighted, but I dont think he bought his conclusion with that one sentence, and I can't say what's missing. So: what are the different precise claims 'pleasure is a wastebasket category' could be making? Give me exactly five, ordered from the ones I'd sign today down to the ones that are just false. For each, a couple of sentences at most: the claim stated precisely (and say which thing it's a claim about — the word, the concept, or what's out in the world, because I suspect that's where he slipped), a verdict tag (trivial / substantive and defensible / contentious / false), and whether it would actually license shutting down the question of what pleasures have in common, and hence whether hedonism can even state its thesis. No introduction and no wrap-up paragraph, just the five numbered items.
