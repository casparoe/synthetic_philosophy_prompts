So I got roped into being the keeper of our "how engineering works" doc at my company — the one that says how we propose changes to how we work (RFCs, basically). Two weeks ago someone noticed that rule 7 says any change to the doc needs a two-thirds vote of the eng org, rule 12 says the doc may also be amended by any procedure the org agrees to unanimously, and rule 4 says individual rules can be deleted by a simple majority. So you can apparently delete rule 7 with 51% and then rewrite everything else the same way. Then someone else pointed out rule 3, which says "this rule may not be amended," got amended last year by a 90% vote and literally nobody objected at the time, so is the doc now in an illegal state or is rule 3 just gone? And there's a third thing that's been bugging me: an RFC got merged that retroactively declared a previous amendment void as of the date it was merged, which means for a while we were operating under rules that, per the new rule, never counted. That whole mess sent me down the self-referential-rulebook rabbit hole — I've been reading about Nomic (the Suber/Hofstadter game where some rules are marked immutable until a vote makes them mutable) and about constitutions with clauses governing their own amendment, and people claiming there's a genuine paradox sitting in there somewhere.

Before I read any actual theories about this, I want to know what I should even be demanding of one.
