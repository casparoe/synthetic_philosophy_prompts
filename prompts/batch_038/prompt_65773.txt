Our LessWrong-adjacent reading group has been bogged down on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism for like six weeks and I want a diagnosis, not a survey. Setup: some of us think all the pleasures share a single property (it feels good; you're disposed to want it to keep going) and hedonism can rest on that. Others think "pleasure" is a family-resemblance grab bag -- the cold drink, the solved proof, the back rub, the schadenfreude -- so hedonism is either false or just says nothing. A third faction thinks both of those are a category error, because pleasure isn't a sensation at all but an attitude, being pleased that p, and of course there's no common feel if you're looking in the wrong place. Nobody budges.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the fight isn't phenomonological, it's about what job the concept has to do inside a theory of well-being, and each side keeps scoring hits on a target the others don't actually hold. Relatedly, our introspection reports split cleanly in half -- some of us insist we can find a common hedonic tone if we look, some insist there's simply nothing there -- and nobody can tell whether that split is evidence or just disagreement about what counts as looking.

So which is it: verbal, substantive, two different questions wearing one name, or is there a hidden empirical thing doing the work (liking-vs-wanting flavored, maybe)? And what would actually count as progress here, if anything can? American English please.
