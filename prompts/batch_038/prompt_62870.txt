You are a specialist in the metaphysics of dispositions — the conditional analysis together with its fink, mask, and mimic counterexamples, and the more recent alternatives to both. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming candidate principles before committing to any prediction.

I am a metaphysics PhD student writing a commentary on a senior colleague's work, and I want to anticipate how she would rule on a case of my own construction. Her published verdicts are these. A glass that a sorcerer would deprive of its brittleness if anyone were about to strike it: fragile. A glass packed in styrofoam and dropped onto a tile floor: fragile. A wire whose live condition an attached device would cancel the instant a conductor touched it: live. A toxin whose lethal action an antidote already circulating in the victim would neutralize: poisonous. A steel ball that would shatter when struck only because a concealed mechanism substitutes a glass replica at the moment of impact: not fragile. A glass in foam that absorbs ninety percent of the impact of an ordinary drop while leaving its silicate structure wholly intact: fragile. She has also handed down what look like opposite rulings on interference cases that are structurally identical: where the interfering material was described as packaging she ascribed the disposition, and where the same material was described as part of the product's design she withheld it.

My case: a drinking glass in whose composition a stress-responsive polymer network is woven through the silicate at manufacture. Above a threshold force the network stiffens and dissipates the impact before any crack can propagate, so the glass survives essentially every strike; if the polymer is chemically depleted, the remaining silicate shatters exactly as ordinary glass does. The network cannot be removed without destroying the glass.

Is it fragile on her view? Lay out every principle that fits the track record above, say what each predicts here, identify which one accommodates the packaging/design split without treating her as merely inconsistent, say which you would bet on and why, and propose the further case that would most sharply separate the survivors. Finally, flag any of her earlier verdicts that turn out to be unstable under the principle you favor.
