I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about taking other people's word on aesthetic matters and my verdicts are a mess. Here they are.

My best friend, whose taste in music I trust completely, says a new record is the best thing she's heard all year. I don't come to think the record is great — I just think she's probably right and now I want to hear it. Not fine to believe it on her word.

Same friend, same record, says the second track modulates somewhere it shouldn't and that's why it drags. I've never heard the track, but I'm perfectly happy to believe it drags. That one feels fine, and I can't say why.

A critic whose palate I trust says a tasting menu is superb. I book the table without hesitation. Acting on the testimony seems totally normal, but I still don't feel like I *know* the meal will be superb.

A sommelier tells me a wine has green apple in it. I taste it and now I get green apple. The testimony clearly did something, but only through the tasting, not instead of it.

Someone tells me a joke is hilarious. Useless, no effect whatsoever.

An art historian says a painting everyone raves about is clumsy and probably a workshop copy. I look at it and think it's gorgeous. I feel like I should defer, but I notice my actual judgment doesn't budge at all.

And the contrast case that annoys me: someone tells me what I said to my sister was cruel, I come to believe it, I apologize. That works fine, and cruelty is a value judgment about a particular thing too.

My tentative principle is that testimony is defective exactly when it invites me to skip the experience, and fine when it only tells me where to point my attention once I'm having it. That handles the sommelier and the dragging track, sort of handles the booking-a-table one, but it makes the art historian case come out wrong and it doesn't touch the cruelty case at all.

Is there a principle that gets more of these, or a small set of them? Please state it precisely, tell me which of my cases motivates each clause, and then run it on cases I haven't listed — say, someone who's been blind since birth being told a sunset is beautiful, being told a proof is elegant before you read it, and being told a match you were working through was beautiful to watch. And be honest about which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, because I suspect at least one has to go.
