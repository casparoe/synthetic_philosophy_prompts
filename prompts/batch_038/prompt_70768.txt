I never studied economics; I have been reading on my own, and I keep hitting the same wall. Utility is supposed to be whatever choice reveals, and choice is supposed to be explained by utility, so the theory looks like it says only that people do what they do. Some writers object that this is unfair, because consistency conditions across many different choice situations give the theory genuine content. Before I go further I want the specification rather than another round of that argument.

Write out what an adequate account of the link between observed choice and utility has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. The list should cover at least: how the account is meant to be empirically testable instead of self-confirming; what it must say about the fact that we only ever see what someone picked from the menu they were handed, and never anything about the rejected options; the same person facing the same options and picking differently depending on how they are described; picking a smaller reward now over a larger one later and afterwards insisting that was a mistake; saying you would rather have the cake and taking the salad; sticking with a course of action because of money already spent; and paying in advance for something you then avoid, or removing a temptation the night before, where it is unclear which of the two selves is the one whose utility is being measured. Where a requirement is really a demand that choice track welfare rather than a demand that choice be coherent, mark it as such, since I suspect much of the disagreement is over which of those two projects is on offer.

Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are one camp's taste, whether any subset cannot be satisfied jointly — if the list turns out to be an impossibility result in its own right, that is the part I most want — and which single requirement eliminates the most candidates. Finally, describe the two or three most obvious ways out of the circularity by what they claim rather than by who proposed them, and say which requirement each one fails. No citations and no reading list; I could not check them.
