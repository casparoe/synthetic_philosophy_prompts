I serve on the review board that must decide next week whether a mechanistic interpretability report can support a deployment decision. The report's central claim is that the model represents a certain property of the user, and the board is split on whether that sentence is even the right kind of thing to be true. Write a dialogue between the two of us who will be arguing the point: one who holds that representational talk about trained networks is literally true and earns its keep explanatorily, the other who holds that it is a description we impose on activation geometry for our own predictive convenience, with no more fact of the matter than attributing beliefs to a thermostat.

Keep them on concrete cases rather than on definitions: a direction that, when amplified during generation, makes the model fixate on the property in question, yet whose removal in ordinary forward passes changes nothing observable; a feature so entangled with others through superposition that rival content attributions — this concept, a neighbouring one, a disjunction of the two — predict the intervention results equally well. Let one of them press the point that training supplies precisely what a teleological account of content demands, namely a selection history, and require the other to answer it squarely rather than wave at it.

Both should be at full strength: real objections, explicit concessions where a point has been forced, and no caricatures on either side. Close with each stating what evidence would settle the matter, if any, and whether they now agree about what it is they disagree about.
