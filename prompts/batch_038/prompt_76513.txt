I am in the second term of an MA seminar on the semantics of conditionals, and after working through Lewis's triviality results line by line I have arrived at a position I find myself unable to defend when pressed. It has four parts, which I take to hang together. (i) Adams's thesis — call it PC — is correct as a descriptive claim: a rational agent's degree of belief in an indicative conditional equals the conditional probability of the consequent given the antecedent, rather than the probability of some truth-evaluable content. (ii) Lewis's theorem shows that this is not a contingent feature of our conceptual scheme but a mathematical one: in any probability space closed under conditionalization, no proposition can have its probability equal the corresponding conditional probability across all pairs of sentences, save in degenerate cases. (iii) It follows that indicative conditionals are not truth-conditional, and that believing one is a Ramsey-style state of supposing the antecedent and adding the consequent on that supposition. (iv) The embedding problem — conditionals under negation, under disjunction, in antecedent position, in iterated conditionals — is not an independent objection but an expected consequence, since PC was never supposed to have unlimited scope. There is a fifth commitment a classmate keeps attacking, and it is the one I most want examined: I take (iii) to be *established* by (ii), not merely made plausible by it. That is, I think the triviality result is what rules out truth conditions for indicatives, rather than being one awkward datum among many that a truth-conditional account must absorb.

Tell me what would count against this package. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and for each one I want to know what the mathematics, the language, or the world would have to look like. Specifically: (a) Is the inference from (ii) to (iii) as tight as I assume? Lewis's theorem constrains binary connectives that pick out a proposition from a pair of propositions; a restrictor semantics, on which "if" is not a two-place connective at all but narrows the domain of a covert modal, and the multidimensional or conditional-event approaches in the de Finetti, Jeffrey, van Fraassen and Bradley lines, all appear to sit on the far side of that constraint while remaining thoroughly truth-conditional. If so, has my position been left with an inference to the best explanation rather than a proof, and what would settle the question one way or the other? (b) What would have to be true for PC itself to fail in a way I could not absorb by narrowing its scope — and is there any such thing, or is the view already insulated? (c) What does my position predict that its rivals do not? I can list the failures of strengthening the antecedent, of transitivity, of import–export, and the McGee-style cases against modus ponens, but I suspect a truth-conditional restrictor account predicts most of these as well, in which case they are not differential evidence. Tell me what would be genuinely differential, and what result or observation would speak *against* my view rather than merely fail to support it. (d) How much weight should I place on the experimental literature: to what extent do people's degrees of belief in conditionals actually track P(C|A) rather than conjunctions or biconditionals, and does the suppositional view survive if that descriptive claim turns out to be only approximately true, and only for simple, non-causal, non-deontic conditionals? Flag anywhere you are unsure of the specifics.

I would also like the honest division between what a committed suppositionalist would themselves concede as counting against them and what they would dismiss as question-begging. I expect embedding under operators, conditionals in questions and commands, and the availability of a truth-conditional account that matches PC's predictions at least locally to be conceded as real costs, but I may be flattering my own view, so say where I am. Close by telling me directly whether this position risks anything at all, or whether it has been qualified into a thesis no evidence can reach — and, if it is insulated, whether that is a defect for a view of this kind, which is after all partly a descriptive claim about a construction in a natural language and not merely a metaphysical stance. Throughout, please keep separate what is settled among people who work on this material from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present a live dispute — say, over whether the triviality results are decisive against *any* truth-conditional account — as though it had been resolved. Where the literature is divided, say that it is divided, and say which way you lean and why.
