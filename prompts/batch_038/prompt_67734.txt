This term I am auditing a philosophy of probability seminar alongside the first year of my physics graduate program, in which I have completed a course in classical mechanics and one in statistical mechanics. My command of the physics is good: I am at home with ensembles, phase space, Liouville's theorem and canonical transformations, coarse-graining and macrovariables, typicality, and the various strategies for grounding the second law. My command of the philosophy is poor. I can state Bertrand's chord problem and I have heard the surrounding names, but I cannot yet tell which moves in the seminar are substantive and which are relabeling.

What I would like is a translation exercise, done as faithfully as you can manage and then audited.

**Part one: the translation.** Restate the following claims in my vocabulary — measures over a phase space, invariance under a flow, the choice of macrovariables, typicality rather than probability, ensembles as predictive devices rather than descriptions of an individual system, and the low-entropy boundary condition on the early universe where that becomes relevant.

1. The principle of indifference itself, as a rule for distributing credence in the absence of relevant evidence, together with the supporting thought that a symmetry in the evidence licenses an equal division.
2. The charge that in the continuous case the principle is not merely indeterminate but self-defeating: that indifference over the side length, surface area, and volume of a cube yields mutually incompatible commitments; and that in the chord problem the construction by random endpoints, by random radial point, and by random midpoint give 1/3, 1/2, and 1/4 respectively, each apparently equally licensed.
3. The classical reply that the problem is ill-posed until a generating process is specified — that the question concerns how chords come to be produced, not what the geometry says.
4. Poincaré's line of thought, as I understand it: that a sufficiently mixing physical process drives almost any smooth initial distribution over the underlying variables toward the same limiting distribution, so the measure is a fact about the apparatus.
5. The maximum-entropy and invariance-group repair, which demands scale and translation invariance of the answer, lets the resulting integral equations select a unique distribution, and thereby lands on the radial construction.
6. The proposal to restrict indifference to genuine alternatives rather than to every way a situation can be described.

Where the philosophy uses a technical term — credence, chance, well-posedness, invariance under a transformation group, a "genuine" alternative — gloss it in a clause. Do not assume I share the seminar's definitions.

**Part two: the residue report.** For each of the six, tell me which category it falls into: what translates without loss; what translates only with distortion, and what the distortion conceals; and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Then diagnose the residues. For each inexpressible item, say whether the gap is a limitation of statistical mechanics — no resources, perhaps, for talking about what an agent should believe absent physical information, or no way to keep objective chance distinct from warranted credence — or evidence that the original claim was less determinate than it sounded, for instance if "absence of relevant evidence" had been quietly standing in for "absence of information about the generating process."

As a worked case, run the 1/3, 1/2, and 1/4 discrepancy through the translation concretely: what physical process, if any, does each correspond to, and what must a statistical mechanic say about the random-midpoint construction in particular?

**Part three: test my hypothesis rather than indulge it.** I suspect that the microcanonical measure is the principle of indifference in dynamical dress — that the appeal to Liouville invariance and typicality does not eliminate the arbitrariness but relocates it, first to the choice of macrovariables and coarse-graining, and then to the initial condition of the universe, at which point we are again postulating a measure over a space of alternatives with nothing to recommend it but its special status. Tell me whether that is right, partly right, or a conflation of two questions, and if it is a conflation, say where the analogy fails. In particular: invariance under the dynamics and invariance under permutations of a description are different kinds of reason. State explicitly whether they ever coincide, and what becomes of the chord problem once the difference is noticed.

**Constraints.** Do not smuggle the presuppositions of statistical mechanics in and present them as translation — especially not the assumption that every probability question must have a physical chance beneath it, since that is precisely what part of the seminar denies. Where you find yourself importing an assumption, flag it as an import. I do not want a reading list, citations, or attributions; I want the reasoning. And do not adjudicate the debate in favor of either side. Give me the residue report and your view on whether the residue indicts my framework or the original claims. Format the residue report as a table — claim, statistical-mechanical counterpart, status, note — followed by prose for the diagnosis.
