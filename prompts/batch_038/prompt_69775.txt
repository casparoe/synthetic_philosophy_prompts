A colleague is defending a reply to the argument from divine hiddenness, and the reply runs by analogy: God's self-withholding is like a parent who steps back behind the doorway while a toddler learns to walk, or a teacher who sits out in the corridor during an exam. On this picture the absence is not abandonment but a precondition for the child becoming someone who chooses and acts on their own; a parent who hovered visibly would get compliance rather than character. My colleague concludes that non-resistant nonbelief is therefore what a loving God would arrange, and so is no evidence against God's existence.

Could you audit the analogy? I would like the correspondence table stated explicitly — what plays the parent, the child, the doorway, the walking, the exam, and the grade — and then a separation of the disanalogies that actually bear on that conclusion from the ones that are merely decorative, including whatever features of the parenting case the analogy is quietly smuggling into the divine case. I have one worry of my own, which you should test rather than simply adopt: in the source case the toddler already knows who the parent is and can read the withdrawal as deliberate, whereas the adult nonbeliever's question is whether there is anyone there to withdraw. Finish with a verdict on whether a repaired analogy could carry the conclusion or whether the analogy was carrying it from the start, and, if you think a better one is available — one that preserves either the hiddenness or the love without the other — say what it would be.
