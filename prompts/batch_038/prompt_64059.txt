i run a metaphysics reading group and i want to build a 'what do you actually believe about where you came from?' sorting quiz -- the Kripke-style question of whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg. Roughly eight questions, and every one of them has to be a concrete case, not a slogan: mitochondrial replacement (nuclear DNA from the intended parents, mtDNA from a donor egg), an embryo that splits into identical twins a few days in, a clone made by putting an adult cell nucleus into an enucleated egg, a sperm differing from the actual one at a single silent nucleotide, a slow chain of a thousand such swaps, an embryo frozen for thirty years and then implanted, and maybe one where the same two gametes fail to fuse and are replaced by different ones. Two or three answer options each, and no question may ask whether the reader thinks origins are essential -- the sorting has to fall out of what they say about the cases. Then give me the scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the positions: numerical gamete essentialism, zygote/early-embryo essentialism, essentialism about the genetic code rather than the cells, the tolerance-plus-branching-worlds way of handling the swap chain, and the counterpart-theoretic denial that there's any necessity of origin here at all. Finish by telling me which pairs of those my question set can't actually separate, and flag any combination of answers that's logically available but that nobody defends, with one line on whether that's because it's incoherent or just unattractive.
