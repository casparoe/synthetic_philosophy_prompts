I am assembling a set of test cases for a question in the ethics of autonomous systems: whether responsibility-gap claims pick out anything over and above two familiar sources, namely our inability to predict or interpret a learning system's behavior, and the diffusion of causal contribution across many hands. I would like you to design a case that isolates the remaining possibility — a harm that is fully transparent, causally clean, and traceable to a single identifiable agent who acted rightly, for which no one is blameworthy, and which nevertheless leaves a determinate victim whose claim seems to run against someone in particular.

**Purpose and standard of success.** The case is meant to test whether the vocabulary of responsibility gaps survives once opacity and many-hands diffusion are removed. A scenario that generates the verdict from its facts succeeds; a scenario that announces the verdict fails.

**Specifications for the scenario.**

1. Present-day technology in a realistic institutional setting: a closed-loop clinical system, an automated environmental or infrastructure control, or a comparable deployment. No superintelligence, no exotic agency. The system must be genuinely autonomous in the operative sense — it selects and executes interventions without real-time human approval — while its decision procedure remains fully interpretable and fully logged. The gap may not depend on opacity.
2. The harm must be foreseen rather than unforeseeable. At the moment of deployment the relevant parties know the failure mode, know its incidence, and know that incidence to be smaller than that of the harm the system prevents. No party is ignorant, negligent, or in breach of any duty of care, review, disclosure, or oversight; specify which duties each candidate bearer discharges.
3. The harm must be traceable, by ordinary but-for and proximate-cause standards, to a single human act — the decision to deploy the system and leave it running under a protocol the actor is required to follow — rather than to a chain of contributions. The gap may not be a many-hands gap.
4. The system does not select who is to be harmed. No targeting choice, no switch-flipping, no forced trade between identifiable victims: the failure mode is a known, indiscriminate risk attaching to an intervention that is otherwise beneficial.
5. The victim neither consented nor assumed the risk; close that route explicitly in the facts.
6. The scenario must contain identifiable, living persons who benefited from the very same deployment and whose benefit is attributable to it, but who did nothing to bring about the harm and stand in no contractual, promissory, or role-based relation to the victim.
7. One paragraph, no more than 250 words. Write for a reader with graduate-level familiarity with moral philosophy but no legal training, glossing any legal notion in a clause.

**What counts as cheating.** Asserting that nobody is responsible, that the victim has been wronged, or that a gap exists, without the facts making it so. Smuggling in a culpable party — a cost-cutting administrator, a manufacturer who withheld data, an undertrained operator. Rendering the harm sheer misfortune with a machine standing nearby, which dissolves the responsibility question rather than posing it. And closing the gap by inventing a compensation fund or a liability regime: the question is whether the gap is present in the normative landscape your case describes, not whether a legislature could abolish it.

**The demonstration, in this order.**

(a) A compliance check against specifications 1 through 7, one or two sentences each, citing the features of your scenario that satisfy them.

(b) The verdict of each of four registers, applied to the case as described rather than in the abstract: a fault register (who, if anyone, is blameworthy or culpable); a causal-and-role register (who did it and who was in charge, and what follows from that alone); an answerability register (who owes the victim an explanation, a justification, or an apology, and must answer to her); and a distributive register (who, if anyone, should bear the cost of her loss). For each, say who is picked out and why, or why the register returns nothing.

(c) Your diagnosis: in which of these registers, if any, does a genuine gap remain — a slot that the facts leave unfilled although the victim's situation demands that it be filled? State plainly whether her claim is directed at anyone, and if so at whom, and what in the case makes it directed there rather than a general claim upon society at large.

(d) The load-bearing feature. Identify the single feature of your scenario doing the most work, then run three deletions: remove the identifiable beneficiaries; remove the legal mandate binding the actor; remove the system's interpretability. For each, say whether the gap survives, widens, or collapses, and into what.

(e) The deflationary attack. Give the strongest version of the objection that your case displays no responsibility gap but only a category confusion — that blame, accountability, answerability, and cost-bearing are distinct relations, so that an empty answer in one register is simply the correct answer — and then the best reply available to someone who thinks the case shows something real.
