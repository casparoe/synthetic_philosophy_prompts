So I've been reading about vagueness for maybe a month now, not for a class, just because it won't let me go. And I keep running into a shape of argument I don't have a name for. Maybe there is one and I've just missed it.

The setup: one of the two big theories says there really is a precise number of hairs at which a man stops being bald. The line is sharp, it's fixed by ordinary use, we just can't see it. But the theory doesn't stop at "we happen not to know where it is." It goes further and argues we *couldn't* know — there's an argument, running off a premise about how much slack or tolerance any judgement has to have, to the conclusion that nobody could ever know which case is the last bald one. So the theory entails, as a consequence of itself, that its own central claim can never be checked, never confirmed, never ruled out. And it says that in advance, not as a shrug but as a result.

The other big theory says something like there being gaps instead, and as far as I can tell the two line up on absolutely everything you could ever actually test. Any sentence you can evaluate, any experiment with word use, any survey of speakers — same verdict from both. The one place they differ is exactly the place the first theory says is permanantly out of reach.

And when you object that this makes it untestable, the defenders don't deny it. They say that's fine, that's precisely what you should expect if the theory is true. The undetectability isn't a wart they're apologising for, it's a theorem.

I've hit the same shape somewhere else too. That old thought experiment with the universe containing nothing but two exactly similar spheres: someone insists there's a fact of the matter about which sphere is which — a real fact, not just two ways of describing the same arrangement — and when you point out that nothing could ever reveal it, they say of course nothing could ever reveal it, it isn't the sort of fact that shows up in evidence. Again: the theory carries a proof that its distinctive claim is unreachable.

So, does this have a name? I've looked at self-sealing arguments, at Popper's stuff about rescuing theories and about theories that are compatable with any possible evidence, and at underdetermination, and none of them sit right. Self-sealing seems to be about a belief that gets reinterpreted every time something looks like it counts against it — here there's no reinterpretation going on, the thing was built that way from the start and the unreachability is derived rather than patched on afterwards. Underdetermination covers the two-rivals-same-evidence part, but that's not the bit I find distinctive. What's distinctive is a theory that accounts for its own untestability and then counts that as a point in its favour.

I'm not really after the label though, so please don't just hand me one. What I want is the carving. Where's the edge of this thing? A theory that says "there's a fact here and no possible evidence could ever reach it" — when is that perfectly legitimate and when is it a way of buying immunity from criticism? Physics seems to be full of claims nobody can check, stuff about times before any record could exist, or exact values behind measurements that only ever give intervals, and nobody thinks those theories are broken for it. But I have a nagging intuition that the spheres and the hairs are different somehow, and I can't articulate why. Related: does it matter that the unreachable part is the *only* difference between the two rivals, as opposed to being one unreachable detail sitting alongside a lot of testable ones? And does anything change if the unreachability iterates — you can't know where the line is, and you can't know that you don't know it, and so on down?

Give me the nearest existing concepts, say specifically how each one falls short of what I described and which of them fits best, and if nothing actually fits, coin something and define it properly — with the boundary cases worked out, including at least one case that looks like it belongs but doesn't.
