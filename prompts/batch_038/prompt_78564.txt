Write a pantoum on Frege's puzzle about the informativeness of identity statements. Form: four-line stanzas in which each stanza's second and fourth lines recur verbatim as the next stanza's first and third, and in which the final stanza's second and fourth lines are the first stanza's first and third, so the poem closes back on itself. Subject: one bright body, watched once after sunset and once before sunrise and given two names in the watching — but nowhere in the poem may you name Frege, sense, reference, or identity. Let the repetition carry the argument: the returning lines should land in new positions doing new jobs, so that a reader who knows the 1892 essay can point to which recurrence is the genuine discovery and which is empty self-sameness, and so that the closing recovery of the opening lines reads as the triviality of a thing said to be itself rather than as a second sighting of the same light. Keep the diction plain and observational, with the astronomy concrete rather than allegorical. Five quatrains at most, under 200 words; give the poem alone, with no title and no commentary.
