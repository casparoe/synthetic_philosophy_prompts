I'm in a small grad seminar on skepticism and next week is the workshop day where everybody circulates a two-page draft and gets pulled apart for twenty minutes. Mine is on Nozick's tracking account and the closure problem. The idea I'm trying to push is that a lot of the standard back and forth quietly assumes something nobody says out loud: when you evaluate the sensitivity counterfactual you hold the method fixed, but there are worlds where the method isn't even available to the subject. So I want to restrict the counterfactual to worlds where it is, and I'm calling that the availability proviso. If it works, the tracking theorist gets closure back without giving up sensitivity, which is supposedly the one thing they can't have. Draft is below. Please be rough with it, I've been staring at it for three days and can't tell anymore whats load bearing and whats decoration.

---

**§1.** Nozick's account says S knows that p only if p is true, S believes p by some method M, and two counterfactuals hold: if p weren't true and S were to use M, S wouldn't believe p via M; and if p were true and S were to use M, S would believe p via M. Call the first sensitivity and the second adherence. Because sensitivity isn't closed under known entailment, the account delivers the famous split verdicts: I know I have hands, I know that having hands entails not being a handless brain in a vat, but I don't know that I'm not a handless brain in a vat. Nozick accepts the closure failure. The usual complaint is that the split looks arbitrary, since the very same experiences produce both beliefs, and the tracking theorist's only resource for telling them apart is a description of the method, which can be gerrymandered to taste.

**§2.** My diagnosis is different. The sensitivity counterfactual as stated is evaluated over the nearest not-p worlds in which S uses M, and it silently assumes there are any such worlds — that M is available to S in the nearest not-p worlds. Sometimes it isn't. So: S's belief that p is sensitive iff in the nearest not-p worlds *in which S could still employ M*, S does not believe p via M. That's the availability proviso.

**§3.** What it buys. Take "I have hands." The nearest world where I lack hands *and* where my actual method — visually attending to these hands in front of me — is still available is a world where my hands are, say, numbed and out of view, or replaced by convincing prosthetics I haven't examined. There I don't believe I have hands. Now take "I'm not a handless BIV." My method for that is inference from how things seem, and that method is available in the BIV world, where I'd still believe it. So the two beliefs get different verdicts for a reason that isn't a redescription of the method: one method drops out of the nearest not-p worlds, the other doesn't.

**§4.** And closure comes back, or so I claim. If p entails q and I believe q by deducing it from p, then the deducing method is inference, which is available anywhere a believing subject is, so the proviso never bites for it; and in the nearest not-q worlds p is false, so either my belief that p is gone — no premise, no deduction — or my belief that p survives its own falsity, in which case it wasn't sensitive to begin with and I never knew p. Either way, if I knew p and competently deduced q, my belief that q is sensitive. This is the step I trust least.

**§5.** The objection I already know: the proviso can make sensitivity come out vacuously. A seismograph prints a report only when power is on, and the power supply is wired so that any tremor strong enough to matter also blows the fuse. So in every nearby world where the printout would mislead me, there is no printout and hence no reading-the-printout method. My belief that there was no tremor is then sensitive in the proviso's sense — vacuously — even though the machine's silence carries no information whatsoever. My fix is an independence clause: the proviso applies only where the availability of M is not itself correlated with the truth of p. I'll admit that smells ad hoc.

**§6.** Finally, a paragraph arguing the safety-first person can't just steal the proviso, on the grounds that safety looks at nearby worlds where S believes p by M and asks whether p holds, so there's no availability gap for the proviso to fill. I wrote this paragraph at 2am and I suspect it's wrong.
