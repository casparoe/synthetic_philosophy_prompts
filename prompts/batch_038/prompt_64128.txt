Write it out as one realistic present-day paragraph: someone is directly asked a question by a person who's fully entitled to an answer and isn't up to anything wrong themselves, and they answer truthfully -- every word they say, they believe. The false belief gets produced by something else they've arranged: an object left where it'll be found, a gesture, what they conspicuously decline to mention. Silence and refusal can't be the way out, because declining to answer would itself give the game away. And telling the plain whole truth would land an innocent third party in serious trouble.

Then do the analysis, not just the story. Does Augustine's machinery actually produce a verdict here, or does it run out?
