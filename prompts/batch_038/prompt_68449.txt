Over the semester I have built up a case that epiphenomenal qualia undermine themselves, and I want it pressure-tested, because I now suspect it sweeps away considerably more than dualism.

(1) Property dualism about consciousness, if it accepts that the physical causal order is closed, must hold that phenomenal properties are real but do no causal work: what my zombie duplicate says and does is fixed entirely by its physical and functional organization.
(2) So every belief and report I make about consciousness has a complete causal explanation in which phenomenal properties never figure.
(3) If a belief has a complete causal explanation in which X never figures, then that belief was not caused by X.
(4) A belief that was not caused by what it is about is not knowledge of that thing; its truth is, at best, a coincidence.
(5) Hence none of my beliefs about phenomenal properties are knowledge of those properties.
(6) But introspective awareness of phenomenal properties was the evidence that made dualism attractive in the first place, and evidence that cannot be known is no evidence at all.
(7) Therefore property dualism is self-undermining, and the meta-problem of explaining our reports about consciousness has a deflationary answer: there is nothing that those reports are reports of.

I am most attached to (1) and (2), which strike me as simply the standard picture of an epiphenomenalist dualism, and the remaining steps felt like bookkeeping at the time. Which step should I give up, why did it look innocuous in place, and what does each candidate rejection cost the rest of the argument — in particular, what does the dualist get to keep about introspection if she refuses it? Finally, tell me whether (7) is the conclusion the argument actually licenses, or something narrower or considerably broader.
