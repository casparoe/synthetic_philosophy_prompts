So there's this old result — I think it's Good's, something about the principle of total evidence — saying you should never turn down free information, as long as you'll update on it straight and then choose optimally, and as long as the news doesnt change what you want. My gut verdicts don't line up with it and I'd like to know what principle they actually add up to.

1. A doctor offered a free test whose result would change her treatment: look, obviously, even though the result might be grim and it'll upset the patient.
2. Me at a restaurant with the dessert trolley coming past: rational to wave it off, because I know I'll order the tart and I think right now that ordering it would be a mistake. My tastes aren't changing, I'm just predicting I'll be weak.
3. A novelist with an unopened rejection letter on the desk: rational to open it, even though she's certain that if it's a rejection she'll stop wanting to write at all. The news changes what she wants, and she's fine with that.
4. An investor who knows that a doom-laden op-ed reliably makes him panic-sell, even when he knows better: rational not to read it.
5. My mate asking whether he should look through his partner's phone when he's already 95% sure there's nothing there: don't look — and I'd say that even if the looking were guarenteed costless and undetectable.

2 and 3 are the ones that bother me. They look like mirror images: in both, the evidence reliably changes something about the agent rather than the prospects of any act she's choosing between, and yet I flip. And 4 might be 2 wearing a disguise, or something else entirely.

Can you state a principle that gets all five, or most of them, without collapsing into "don't look when looking makes you worse off," which is just the question restated? Say which clause each case triggers, whether 2 and 3 really are the same kind of case or whether one of my verdicts is incoherent, and what your principle predicts for a case I haven't given — pick one yourself. And tell me straight which verdict it forces me to drop.
