I teach a 75-minute second-year ethics seminar, about 18 people, on why some reasons for having an attitude look like the wrong sort of reason. On the page it always lands as a cute puzzle — demon, glass of water, shrug — and I want them to actually get stuck in it. So build me a live activity, no lecture up front.

Here's the mechanic I want, roughly: I have a bag of candy and a promise that anything earned gets paid out for real. Design a run of three or four offers I make to the class, each paying for an attitude (admire this mediocre poem, believe something about a deck I've shuffled, want to drink from a covered glass). The offers should differ in kind, not just in size. At least one should come from me as someone who has actually read the poem before class, so that accepting it is itself evidence bearing on the question rather than a bribe — and they should only realize that later. At least one should be an offer where the only thing they can hand over is an announcement. And put the class in charge of auditing each other's claims, because I want them to discover how much of an attitude is checkable from outside and how much just isn't. Nobody says "wrong kind of reason" until the debrief.

Please give me these sections, in this order: **Setup & materials** (~150 words), **The offers** (exact wording and timing, ~400), **Payouts & auditing** (what I do with the jokester who declares the attitude on the spot to collect, ~200), **Debrief** (which moment maps to which distinction, and what to do if several of them honestly report the candy made them look closer and they actually came to like the poem, ~250), **What will go wrong the first time** (~200), **What this activity can't reproduce** (~150).
