For about two years I've been part of an informal group that meets to work through members' real decisions using written protocols, and last month it split badly over one of them. I was mostly taking notes; I haven't told anyone which side I lean toward and I'd prefer to keep it that way, so please treat the exchange as evenly as you can manage. The case: Marta's father has early-stage dementia, lives eleven hours away, and Marta has a stable, well-paid job she doesn't love. She ran our "idealized deliberation" protocol — forty-eight hours of information-gathering, after which you answer not "what do I want to do?" but "what would I decide if I were fully informed about the relevant facts, thinking clearly, free of fatigue and self-deception, and vividly aware of what my life would actually feel like ten years out?" — and reported that her answer flipped: she went in intending to stay and send money and came out intending to move. Dev's objection was that the protocol cannot be doing what Marta thinks it's doing, because the idealized agent inherits your values by stipulation, so whatever it concludes was already loaded in; it is a rubber stamp with extra steps, and it feels authoritative only because it's you in costume. Marta's reply, and the exchange that followed: (1) that's true only if idealization adds information and nothing else, but in her case the flip came from the vividness clause rather than from any new fact — she had known propositionally for years that her father would decline, and knowing it had not moved her at all. (2) Dev said this merely relocates the problem: vividness does not correct a preference, it amplifies one you already had, so the protocol measures the quality of her imagination rather than what she has reason to do. (3) Marta said that if the amplification of what you actually care about cannot generate reasons, nothing can, and Dev's standard demands a reason-giver that operates independently of anyone's cares, which he will never supply. (4) Dev conceded he might not supply one, but argued the concession costs Marta more than it costs him, since her protocol is advertised as a check on self-deception and a check whose output is guaranteed to track its input is not a check. (5) Marta said the guarantee is exactly what's in question: it corrected her, in the single case where it has actually been run. (6) Dev said one case in which a procedure lands where the subject was already emotionally drifting is not evidence that it is a procedure.

They then moved to a second, more technical dispute about how to state the protocol at all. (7) Dev pointed out that Marta's original formulation asks what the idealized agent would *do*, but that agent isn't facing Marta's problem: idealized-Marta has no mortgage, no employer, and no father with dementia, so the question has no determinate answer, and if you supply her with Marta's actual circumstances you have stopped idealizing. (8) Marta agreed this version was bad and proposed an advice model instead — ask what the idealized agent would advise actual, flawed Marta to do, taking as given everything true about her, including her procrastination, her fear of poverty, and the two previously endorsed plans she has already quietly abandoned. (9) Dev's response was that the second version is worse, because it makes what Marta has most reason to do depend on how flawed she happens to be, so on this account she can acquire reasons by becoming less informed, less rational, and more akratic. (10) Marta called this a cheap paradox: the advice model tells an akratic agent to precommit, and precommitment is not itself a defect but the correct management of one. (11) Dev said that this is precisely the trouble — the account can then never tell her to repair the flaw rather than route around it, and a normative theory whose deliverance is "keep the defect and build scaffolding" has stopped being normative without announcing that it had. Where they ended: Marta thinks Dev has located a real ambiguity but has shown neither reading to fail; Dev thinks both readings fail for connected reasons and the group should retire the exercise. The group has since adopted a compromise nobody can articulate — we still run the protocol, but its output counts as one input among several.
