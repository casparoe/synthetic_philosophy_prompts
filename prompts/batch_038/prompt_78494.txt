I have recently taken charge of scenario analysis at a catastrophe-reinsurance modeller, and the job has made a philosophical distinction uncomfortably practical. Our engine handles an indicative claim (if the surge exceeds three metres, the portfolio breaches its attachment point) and a counterfactual one (had the surge exceeded three metres, the portfolio would have breached it) in exactly the same way: re-weight the ensemble conditional on the antecedent and read off the frequency. My limited reading suggests that Adams's thesis licenses this for indicatives, that Lewis's triviality results show it cannot hold in general, and that imaging was offered as a repair.

I think natively in ensembles, likelihoods, calibration curves, Brier scores and probability transport; I have no training in formal semantics. Please restate that debate — the probability of a conditional, the Ramsey test, imaging — wholly in my vocabulary, and then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what you had to distort, and what my toolkit cannot express at all. Do not smuggle in possible worlds or similarity orderings and call it translation; if you need them, flag the fudge. I want to know whether imaging is merely our re-weighting under another name, and whether the failure of Adams's thesis for counterfactuals marks a gap in my framework or a muddle in theirs. Please answer in American English.
