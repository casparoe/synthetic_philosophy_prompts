The dispute I want documented concerns what death leaves behind, and it is between two positions that agree on nearly all the facts and part company on what the facts amount to.

The first position is a patternist one, in the spirit of Hofstadter: a person is not a substance but a self-sustaining, self-referential pattern; patterns are substrate-independent and can be instantiated at varying degrees of fidelity; and because we all carry one another's patterns in our own heads, a person who dies is dispersed rather than annihilated — attenuated, distributed across the people who carried her, but not simply gone. The second position grants every descriptive claim in that paragraph and denies that any of it is survival: what persists in a survivor is a representation of a person, standing to that person as a portrait stands to its sitter, and nobody says a sitter survives in her portrait. On this view what matters in survival requires that there still be a subject for whom things go well or badly, and the subject is precisely what has ceased.

These two have agreed to co-sign a single statement of where they actually stand. Draft it for them. It has to be a document both could put their names to without feeling caricatured, so the formulations must be checked against each side in turn.

Both parties must rule on a common set of cases, which I have invented and whose details you should treat as fixed. Central case: Ines Marchetti, a mathematician, died at sixty-one having published almost nothing. For eleven years her closest collaborator was her doctoral student, V., who after Marchetti's death spent three decades proving theorems by Marchetti's distinctive method — attacking every conjecture through its degenerate cases first, keeping her idiosyncratic notation, framing results as she framed them. Two colleagues who knew Marchetti say, separately and unprompted, that working with V. is like working with her. V. has since trained two students who exhibit some of these habits and never met Marchetti at all.

Variants the statement must also cover:

1. The same habits and the same mathematical output, but transmitted entirely through a cache of Marchetti's unpublished notebooks found in an archive by a stranger.
2. The same notebooks used to train a system that is itself a plausible candidate for having a self, and that goes on doing new work in Marchetti's manner.
3. V. and another of Marchetti's former students, W., develop the inherited habits in mutually incompatible directions, each claiming to be continuing Marchetti's line.
4. Marchetti's pattern is dispersed piecemeal: each of a hundred people absorbs one of her habits, and no one of them ends up with anything resembling a coherent point of view.

Now the deliverables, in this order.

I. A numbered list of claims both parties can sign. Include claims about what patterns are, about multiple realizability, about the descriptive facts of the central case, and about what is emphatically not in dispute. Do not pad this section: each entry should be a claim an informed reader would have expected to be contested.

II. A disagreements table, one row per genuine disagreement, with these columns: the disagreement stated as the patternist would state it and accept; the same disagreement stated as the opponent would state it and accept; what could bear on it; the patternist's prediction; the opponent's prediction. Anything you judge to be merely verbal belongs in section IV, not here.

III. Cruxes. Name the two or three sub-questions such that settling them would actually move one side, and for each give a discriminating test — a case, an argument, or a kind of empirical finding — that both sides accept as bearing on it, together with what each side predicts in advance of seeing the result. Consider, without being confined to, the following candidates:

- Whether what V. carries is *running* Marchetti's pattern or merely *representing* it, and whether any observation could tell those apart. A simulation of a hurricane leaves nobody wet; a transplant goes on growing. Which of these is the right analogue for the transfer of a person's pattern into other people, and can the choice be forced rather than merely asserted?
- Whether the transmitted pattern can generate correct new work in Marchetti's style that Marchetti herself never produced and could not have been prompted to produce — and whether that capacity would tell in the patternist's favour or would prove too much, since a sufficiently skilled biographer might manage something similar.
- Whether survival admits of degree, and hence whether the real dispute is about identity (one-one, all-or-nothing, and on some views not what matters) or about what matters (graded, and perhaps detachable from identity altogether). If both parties accept that identity is not what matters, is there any disagreement left to sign a document about?
- Whether the opponent can distinguish a pattern carried by a person from a pattern carried by a notebook, given that both are matter arranged in a certain way; and, symmetrically, whether the patternist is committed to holding that a well-drawn fictional character partly constitutes a survival of its author. Variants 1 and 4 should be where this crux bites.

IV. What is merely verbal. State plainly which parts of the year-long argument were about words, and propose a stipulative vocabulary of three or four defined terms that both parties could use without either conceding the substantive issue. Say explicitly which of "survives," "persists," "is still present," and "lives on" each party is entitled to keep, and on what conditions.

V. One or two sentences, in each party's own register, stating what that party would have to give up in order to be wrong.

Argue from the concepts, not from the literature: I want no survey of who holds what and no bibliography. Where an empirical matter would be relevant — how faithfully people really carry one another's cognitive habits, whether systems trained on a limited corpus generalize past it, what bereavement does to a survivor's self-model — say what would count as evidence and which way each side predicts it goes, but do not fabricate findings or name studies.
