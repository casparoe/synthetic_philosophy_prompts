I'm putting together the last session of a reading group on Schopenhauer's aesthetics — mixed cohort, music students, two from film, one who works in practice as an architect, and a dancer who has been muttering about dance all term — and I want to run it as a ranking exercise rather than another go-round of general discussion, because that's the only way to make them commit to something.

Here's what I'd like you to do. Take these seven, none of which is just plain instrumental music: a Gothic cathedral; a panel of entirely non-figurative stained glass; unaccompanied dance, no music, no text, just bodies in a space; a tragedy in verse; a programme symphony that spends its time depicting a brook and a thunderstorm; a free improvised saxophone solo that was never written down and can never be repeated; and a film-with-score, meaning the moving images and the music taken together as the single artwork the audience actually encounters. Rank them by how much of music's privileged metaphysical status in Schopenhauer's system each one can inherit, and for each, name the specific feature that stops it from simply occupying music's slot — because if you can't say what disqualifies it, you haven't really ranked it, you've just expressed a preference. I want the ordering to arrive in your running order of sentences, no list please, and I want to see you state the principle you're using to decide, early, so a reader can check whether you then apply it consistently or quietly swap in a different one halfway down.

Two things I especially want you to wrestle with rather than dodge. First, the dancer's objection, which is actually a good one and which Schopenhauer never squarely faces: the body is where the will is most immediately objectified, so why on earth should architecture, which only ever gets at the lowest grades of the will's objectivity, sit anywhere above a human being moving? Give me either a principled answer or an admission that the system has to be amended to produce one. Second, the film-with-score, which is the one that breaks the exercise: if music outranks everything and film inherits whatever its soundtrack carries, does the composite go to the top, or does the presence of the representational images drag the whole thing back down to the level of the images? Say which, and own the cost.

Then tell me where the ordering stopped being Schopenhauer's and became yours, and flag the two or three places where the system genuinely forces your hand instead of leaving you room to choose — those are the spots the students should argue about afterwards. Be honest if two of the seven end up tied; a forced ranking that hides a tie teaches them nothing.

Please write it all as continuous prose, the way you'd write a letter, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — I want to be able to read it aloud and hand it round. Something in the region of nine hundred words is about right.
