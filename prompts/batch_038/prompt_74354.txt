I'm a staff software engineer at a mid-size consumer app company, and I have no formal training in philosophy, so please tell me plainly where I've got something wrong rather than writing around it. Over the holidays I read Tolstoy's *What Is Art?* (1897) and got stuck on a line I copied into my notes:

"the degree of infectiousness is also the sole measure of excellence in art"

To me that reads like a metric definition — and a bad one, which is exactly why I want it written up as one.

The piece I'd like is a satirical internal engineering design document, roughly 900 to 1,100 words, for a fictional short-video platform called Kindling whose feed is now mostly synthetic: the large majority of items are generated by in-house models rather than made by people. The document is RFC-1897 (the number is deliberate), proposing that the company retire its engagement north-star metric and adopt a Tolstoyan infectiousness score as the sole ranking and acceptance criterion for content. Write it in the flat, well-organized voice of a real design doc — Motivation, Non-Goals, Proposed Definitions, Instrumentation, Rollout Plan, Accepted Risks, Open Questions — closing with a reviewer-comment thread in which a data scientist keeps asking how one A/B tests sincerity and a newly created Head of Content Values keeps approving things nobody else can explain. Deadpan throughout: nobody inside the document thinks any of this is funny.

The document has to get the theory right, not approximately right. Please build the following in as doc voice rather than as exposition:

- infectiousness — the receiver being united in feeling with the maker and with the others receiving the same work — as the single indication separating real content from counterfeit content;
- the three conditions governing the degree of infection (individuality, clarity, sincerity), with sincerity as the one that absorbs the other two;
- a separate, second-order ranking of content by the moral quality of the feeling transmitted, with feelings of human union at the top;
- accessibility to a plain working person with no education in the arts as a requirement rather than a nice-to-have;
- the marks of the counterfeit: professionalism and living off the work, borrowing and imitation, striking effects, and mere interestingness — noting that a thing can be all of these and still fail.

Then develop, rather than merely name, these invented operational details: a sincerity attestation step in creator onboarding; a standing "plain listener" panel of users with no arts education who must be moved without effort and without changing their standpoint, serving as the ground-truth instrument for the score; a deprecation plan for the legacy catalog including the founder's own early hits, with grandfathering explicitly ruled out, since Tolstoy repudiated his own famous novels and so the document must repudiate its founder's; and a Known False Positives section covering the scheduled removal of a Shakespeare-analogue and a Beethoven-analogue property.

The humor should come from the theory failing as a specification, not from tech-industry jokes. I want the standard objections to surface only as engineering artifacts: a signal defined internally and therefore unobservable from outside the maker, which makes sincerity structurally unmeasurable for synthetic content; a metric so Goodhartable that the first successful experiment is won by outrage, because outrage infects efficiently; an arbiter required to decide which feelings are worth transmitting, staffed by precisely the tastemakers the theory denounces; and a rollback path in the rollout plan that nobody wants to own. No objection may be stated as an objection anywhere in the document.

Two consistency constraints. First, the RFC's authors may not quietly import a non-Tolstoyan criterion to patch a hole; if a hole cannot be patched with the theory's own resources, it goes under Accepted Risks with a named owner and an empty mitigation field. Second, don't put words in Tolstoy's mouth: no invented quotations attributed to him, no footnotes, no bibliography, no citations, and no reading list. Apart from the single line above, paraphrase only.

The document should be readable to a colleague who has never read Tolstoy: define each term inline, in document voice, at first use.

Finally, append a clearly separated afterword of about 200 words in your own voice rather than the document's. There, distinguish what is uncontroversially in the treatise from what this piece exaggerates, transposes onto a fictional company, or invents outright; and separately, say where readers who know Tolstoy genuinely disagree about how to read him — for instance, whether infectiousness really is the sole criterion or whether the ranking of feelings is doing the normative work, whether his dismissals of Shakespeare and Beethoven are argued positions or temperamental ones, and whether the book offers a theory of what art is or of what makes it good. Don't present a contested reading as settled, and don't present the settled parts as merely contested for the sake of even-handedness. If you think I've misread the book in setting this up, say that too.
