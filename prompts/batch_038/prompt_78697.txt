I teach a second-year metaethics seminar — 18 students, one 75-minute slot — and I want an in-class game that runs them straight into the embedding problem for expressivism without anyone naming what's going on until the debrief.

Rough shape I had in mind, improve it as you like: teams of three or four, each with a translation ledger that has exactly three resources — Boo(...), Hooray(...), and Boo(whoever doesn't Boo(...)). Round one: translate a list of ordinary moral sentences into the ledger, and the list has to include embedded ones — a conditional, a negation, a question, a disjunction. Round two: a 'logic inspector' (me, or a card you write) hands each team three arguments and they must show, using only what their own ledger licenses, why the conclusion has to follow whenever the premises do. One argument should be modus-ponens-shaped and moral; another should be structurally identical but with the moral content swapped out for something flatly descriptive, so they can compare. Round three: teams swap ledgers and try to break each other's — find an inference a ledger calls valid that plainly isn't.

What I want back: the exact sentence and argument lists, ready to print; rules and scoring; a minute-by-minute timeline for the 75; and a debrief mapping each round onto the real problem, including what the higher-order attitude trick buys and what it costs. Then two honest bits — what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I'm expecting someone to just read the the conditional as a conditional between attitude reports and declare victory), and which feature of the problem the game can't reproduce that I'll have to supply myself.
