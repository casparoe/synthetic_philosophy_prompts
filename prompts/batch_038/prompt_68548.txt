You are to work in a dual capacity for this task: as a philosopher of probability with a genuine working command of the literature on self-locating belief and on how credence relates to betting behaviour, and as a comedy writer fluent in the deadpan register of internal corporate communications. Please approach the philosophy as load-bearing rather than decorative. The humour should come from getting the details right, so that a specialist reads the piece and recognises that its author knows precisely where the dispute bites — not from generic jokes about academics disagreeing.

**The commission.** Write an internal circular, approximately 700 words, issued by the trading desk of a betting firm that has begun offering markets on an experiment it refers to internally as the Awakening Product. Address it to the desk's traders, its compliance officer, and one junior analyst who has reasonably asked why a coin toss requires a policy document. Give it a subject line, a reference number, and a distribution list, in the manner of such things.

The product works as follows, and your circular must recap it accurately somewhere near the top, in the flat explanatory voice of someone bringing a new starter up to speed: a subject is put to sleep on Sunday and told the whole procedure. A fair coin is tossed. If it lands Heads she is woken once, on Monday. If it lands Tails she is woken on Monday and again on Tuesday, and between the two awakenings she is given a drug that erases the memory of the first. Each time she wakes she is asked for her degree of belief that the coin landed Heads, and then put back to sleep. The firm takes bets on the coin at every awakening.

**Required beats.** Your circular must include all of the following, in an order of your choosing:

1. The recap described above, clear enough that a reader who has never encountered the problem can follow everything that comes after it.
2. An account of the desk having been arbed twice this quarter, once from each direction, because it priced the market as though the customer's belief were one half and settled it as though her belief were one third — or the reverse. The arithmetic of per-awakening versus per-experiment settlement should be what does the damage, and a reader should be able to reconstruct the loss from what the memo says, including the fact that a stake taken on Monday is taken again on Tuesday by a customer who has forgotten Monday.
3. A boxed compliance note on a newly introduced feature: any bet placed at an awakening is automatically re-offered at the next awakening at double the stake, the customer having no memory of accepting the first. Compliance wants to know whether this is a product or a time bomb, and the memo should make clear that the answer depends on a question nobody on the desk can answer without taking a side.
4. A market on which day it is, which the firm cannot settle, because the only available settlement agent is the customer herself and she is systematically unreliable on Tuesdays.
5. Two invented analysts — name them, and do not name any real philosopher — each with a price and each with a reason. The desk's attempt to remain neutral between them should be visibly impossible rather than stated to be so.
6. One beat concerning the firm's risk engine, which cannot represent the customer's evidence because its database has no field for an indexical. The IT ticket raised about this should be recognisable to anyone who has thought about whether ordinary conditionalisation applies when what one learns cannot be expressed without a word like today.
7. A closing house position that is managerial to the point of absurdity.

**Constraints.** Write throughout in British English, in spelling and in the idiom of a City firm's internal documents. Keep the tone rigorously deadpan: no winking at the reader, no asides conceding that any of this is strange, no signposting of punchlines. Include no bibliography, no footnotes, no references and no attributions to actual authors — the piece must stand on its content alone. Crucially, do not let the circular state the competing positions as positions: there should be no sentence of the form "one camp holds that...". Have them appear only as prices, exposures, contract terms, operational problems and email threads.

The target of the satire is not the philosophy and not the people who do it. It is a style of discourse: the unshakeable confidence that any disagreement about what someone ought to believe can be dissolved by issuing a procedure, a spreadsheet column and an escalation path. Enact rather than announce the real pressure points — what evidence the subject actually has when she wakes; whether that evidence has changed since Sunday; whether she can be indifferent between two awakenings she cannot tell apart; whether her Tuesday self is a party to the contracts her Monday self signed; and why a book that loses money in her hands is not thereby a book that condemns her reasoning. You may allude to the stranger positions in the neighbourhood, including those that refuse to settle on any precise number at all, but do not name their proponents.

**Process.** Before drafting, think carefully and set out a short planning sketch — five to eight bullet points, clearly separated from the deliverable — identifying which feature of the debate each required beat is built on and where the laugh sits in each. Only then write the circular. Present the planning sketch first and the finished circular second, with no other commentary.
