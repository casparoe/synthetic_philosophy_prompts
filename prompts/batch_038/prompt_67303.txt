I'm giving a 45-minute colloquium talk plus Q&A at a political theory department in about three weeks and I need a prep sheet for the question period. Not a defense of my view, not a bibliography — actual sentences I can say out loud when someone has me cornered.

My thesis, which I know is provocative: Arendt doesn't think lying is a foreign body that corrupts politics, she thinks it's native to it, and the only thing she can coherently condemn is the modern total lie — the one that doesn't hide facts but replaces the whole shared factual world with a fabricated image. So ordinary, targeted, concealment-style political lying is inside her account of the political, not outside it. My argument runs roughly: (1) she holds that factual truths have a coercive, almost despotic character — they end argument rather than feeding it — so a politics founded on unconditional truthfulness undercuts the plurality and persuasion she thinks politics consists in; (2) the capacity to say what is not is the same capacity as the capacity to begin something new, which is her whole account of action and natality, so the liar and the actor are doing structurally the same thing; (3) therefore her condemnation of lying can only be doing work where the lie stops being a move inside a shared world and starts dissolving the shared world — which is what she's describing in the Pentagon Papers material about image-making and the world getting hollowed out rather than misdescribed. Conclusion: she's not a truth-absolutist about politics, she's a pluralist about facts.

The room: two senior Arendt people, one of whom works mainly on the essays and thinks in terms of the common world and will not accept propositional framings; one person on totalitarianism and propaganda; a Habermasian who wants to talk about conditions of communication; and then a cluster of younger people who study disinformation empirically — corrections, conspiracy belief, the way debunking can entrench. Those two groups barely speak each other's language and I expect objections from both directions.

Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually get, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that particular person would phrase it, with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Flag which ones are really claims that I've misread the texts, since they know those better than I do and I'd rather hear it from you now.
