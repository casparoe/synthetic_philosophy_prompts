The standard defence of futarchy leans on an analogy with ordinary prediction markets: because a contract pays out only if the predicted event obtains, speculators who are wrong lose money, and prices are thereby disciplined toward the truth. Make the case that this discipline fails specifically for decision markets, in a way that leaves predictions about exogenous events untouched. The core of your argument should be that when the price itself determines whether a policy is adopted, the quantity being estimated is no longer an independent target, so a mistaken estimate can become correct simply by being acted on — producing an equilibrium in which the market is never corrected. Explain why such self-confirmation is a defect rather than a triumph, given that the futarchist's own criterion is realized utility, and answer the obvious objection that a genuinely bad fixed point would eventually show up as low utility and be revised away.
