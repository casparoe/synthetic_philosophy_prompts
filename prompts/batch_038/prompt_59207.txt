I am contributing a story to an anthology of new fiction about the long quarrel between working scientists and philosophers over whether philosophy does any work at all — the quarrel behind the physicist's polemic against philosophy as a distraction from real problems, the cosmologist's announcement that philosophy had died by failing to keep up with physics, and the remark usually attributed to Feynman comparing the philosophy of science to ornithology's usefulness to birds. Most of the contributions will presumably stage that quarrel as a debate. I want mine to stage it as a workplace. Please write the story to the following specification.

**Premise.** A cold-seep marine station on a basalt coast studies a modular, colonial animal that grows by budding and trails tens of metres of physically connected units; the lab's vernacular for a colony is "a string." The station's flagship manuscript makes two claims: a population estimate for the managed seep field, and the finding that strings coordinate their feeding currents in response to a predator cue. The difficulty is that genetic sampling of the field returns 41 distinct genotypes, the visual transect counts 3,400 units, and the coordinated response crosses genotype boundaries in some places while stopping dead at physical connections in others. How many organisms are in the field is therefore 41, or 3,400, or something else again, depending on whether an individual is fixed by genetic identity, physical continuity, functional integration, developmental origin, or reproductive independence — and the draft uses genetic identity in the population section, physical connection in the behaviour section, and reports a single headline figure in the abstract as though both sections had answered the same question.

A mid-career philosopher of biology has been seconded to the station for eleven weeks because the funding agency now requires large collaborations to file a Measurement Integrity Statement. The philosopher's actual deliverable is a two-page form asking whether each headline quantity has been operationalized, with a yes/no box beside it. Name the philosopher; give them a previous posting of this kind that went badly.

Opposing them is the principal investigator, and she must be given the strong version of her case, not a caricature. She has been through a mandated review before: it cost a season of survey work and produced recommendations nobody implemented. Her substantive argument is that a criterion of individuation which never changes what the lab predicts, measures, or does is not a criterion but vocabulary; and that the reason she never articulated the distinction is not obtuseness but the fact that the distinction does not appear in the data.

**The turn.** The philosopher solves nothing. What they contribute is a diagnosis in a form the lab can use — that the manuscript answers two questions and reports one number — and the discriminating experiment that follows, severing and re-attaching units across genotype boundaries to see whether the coordinated response tracks connection or kinship, must be proposed by a junior scientist, prompted by the reframing rather than handed the answer. Make the philosophy load-bearing but not inventive.

**Requirements.**

1. Between 2,200 and 2,800 words, with a title.
2. No character may state the theme, and no scene may consist of anyone explaining what philosophy is for. The two-page form should recur at least twice and its inadequacy should do comic work without the narration pointing at it.
3. The pivot of the story must be a single physical artefact — the transect map, the manuscript's Figure 3, or the form itself — described concretely enough that a reader could see what is wrong with it.
4. At least one scene should run almost entirely in technical register: numbers, instrument names, protocol steps, argument about a threshold. Do not summarise it.
5. Do not name any part of the philosophical literature. No reference to natural kinds, underdetermination, demarcation, or any philosopher living or dead. If the seconded philosopher thinks in those terms, render the thought as an ordinary practical worry about a spreadsheet.
6. Nobody converts and nobody is humiliated. No reconciliation scene, no apology, no mentorship arc.
7. The ending must leave the cost-benefit question genuinely open — a reader who sides with the principal investigator should finish believing she was right about something that mattered. Close on an image or an action, not on a line of dialogue that lands the point.
8. Do not open with arrival, weather, or travel, and do not use the station's remoteness as a mood device.
