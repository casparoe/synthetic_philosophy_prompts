ok so I built this argument to win a fight with my roommate, who's been vegan for about six years, and it won way too easily. That's the part that's bothering me. She went quiet and I don't think she should have been out of moves, so I'm fairly sure I've built something broken and I want to know where.

(1) The only thing that matters morally about a diet is how much death and suffering its production causes.
(2) Crop farming kills enormous numbers of animals — plowing, harvesting, and pesticide application shred field mice, voles, ground-nesting birds, and uncountable insects.
(3) So a vegan diet isn't death-free, it just relocates the deaths into the fields where nobody is watching.
(4) Now compare yields. One beef animal gives you order-of-magnitude 200 kg of edible meat, and that's one death. One broiler gives you maybe a kilo and a half. So per kilo of meat, beef costs you on the order of a hundred times fewer deaths than chicken.
(5) And if the cattle are on grass they eat no cropland at all, so that death isn't stacked on top of a pile of field-animal deaths the way a chicken's is, becuase chickens get fed grain and soy.
(6) Therefore a grass-fed beef diet kills fewer animals than a vegan diet: one death, many meals, no harvest required.
(7) The same reasoning scales upward. The bigger and wilder the animal, the fewer deaths per meal — so whale beats cow, and a hunted deer beats farmed beef, because that deer was practically certain to die this winter anyway from cold, parasites, or a predator, so my bullet doesn't add a death to the world, it just moves it earlier.
(8) So the death-minimizing diet is hunted megafauna, and vegetarianism isn't the ethical option, it's the option that maximizes animals killed per meal.

I don't actually believe (8). I think confinement chicken production is among the worst things we do and I'd want it gone tomorrow. But I can't find the crack. Her comeback was basically "your math is the math and math doesn't care about your feelings," plus a challenge: if I reject (6) I owe her a reason why one cow's death is worse than a hundred chickens', and if I can't produce one, then either I accept (6) or I admit I don't care about the animals, I just find cows more sympathetic than mice.
