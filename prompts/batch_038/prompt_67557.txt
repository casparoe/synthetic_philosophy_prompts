I'm TA-ing the second half of a grad epistemology seminar and my section next week is On Certainty, which always goes badly because everyone reaches for the earth having existed for a long time or here-is-a-hand, and then the whole hour turns into brains in vats again. So instead of picking a stock example I want to hand out a case that's been built to order. Specs:

- There's a proposition H that works as a hinge for some group: it gets relied on in basically every inquiry they run in a given domain, any attempt to test it would have to presuppose it, and nobody in the group would volunteer it as something they believe — if you asked them they'd be puzzled, because it never comes up as a candidate for belief or for doubt in the first place.
- H isn't a logical, mathematical or conceptual truth, and it isn't one of the usual Moorean ones either. No hands, no the external world, no other minds, no the earth being old. It should be the kind of thing an informed outsider could coherently deny.
- The hinge status has to be unevenly distributed. There's a second group working right alongside the first, interlocking with them on the same practical questions, for whom H is just an ordinary empirical claim they'd happily argue about using evidence. Same place, same period, same subject matter.
- An outsider raises a genuine doubt about H and the first group understands it perfectly well — it isnt nonsense to them, it has a determinate content — and yet it still doesn't function as a belief-claim for them.
- Realistic setting: a hospital unit, a bridge crew, a family firm, a municipal water board, a fishing village, whatever you like. No vats, no demons, no sci-fi. The scenario itself should be one tight paragraph; the discussion after it can run longer.

What the case is for, since that's what decides what counts as cheating: I want to pressure-test whether being exempt from doubt can be a property something has only relative to a group, without the hinge framework collapsing into a fancy way of saying widely shared assumption. So if your scenario would read exactly the same if H were simply described as a strongly held empirical belief the first group happens to share, it fails — the difference between the two groups has to show up in how they go on, not in what they'd report if you polled them.

Then do the demonstration. State H and show it meets each clause of the hinge spec, and in particular spell out concretely why testing H would require relying on H rather than just asserting that it would. Show where the two groups visibly diverge in practice and what that divergence costs them. Wittgenstein has the image of a river where the water moves along but the bed can shift too: tell me what specific event in your case would demote H for the first group, and explain why that event is not just more evidence against H. Then say which single feature of the case is doing the work in making the outsider's doubt intelligible-but-inert, and what you'd have to take out to flip it into a case where the doubt really is unintelligible to the insiders. Finish with how someone who thinks the whole hinge framework is empty would attack your case, and whether you think the attack lands.
