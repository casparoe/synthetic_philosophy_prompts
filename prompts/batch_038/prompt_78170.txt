Memetics, I think, is rescued rather than refuted by digital media. The familiar complaint that cultural items are reconstructed rather than copied — and so supply no replicators for selection to act upon — seems to presuppose the low-fidelity transmission environments of speech, ritual, and manuscript culture, and to lose its grip on copy-paste text, image macros, format templates, and hashtags. On this view, memes are genuine replicators at least in the online domain, and their differential replication is what explains why some formats take off while near-identical rivals die out.

Set out what would count against the position: the findings, cases, and arguments that should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and marking for each whether a committed defender ought to concede it as damaging or can properly dismiss it. Say also what this account predicts that an explanation appealing only to sharers' psychology and network position would not. Finally, give an honest verdict on whether confining memes to high-fidelity digital transmission has drained the view of risk — and whether that would be a problem for a theory of this kind, or an acceptable feature of one.

Answer as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
