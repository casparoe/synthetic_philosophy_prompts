Ok so my school does this thing every spring called Bridge Night where they hand out grants — 2,500 dollars each — to like forty seniors who said they need help with first-year college costs (the deposit, a laptop, books). I'm one of the forty. And somehow they asked me to give the student speech, two minutes, in the gym, in front of the donors and the parents and our principal who is a total bootstraps guy.

Problem: I fell down a rabbit hole on luck egalitarianism this year and now I cant give the speech they're expecting. The expected speech is basically "you worked hard, you earned this, be grateful." But the brute luck / option luck distinction kind of wrecks that — showing up every day, not quitting, having the focus to actually get the applications in, all of that is stuff you either got lucky with or you didn't, so "earned" is doing a ton of quiet work. I also know the big objection, the one where if you take luck that seriously you end up leaving the guy who rode without a helmet to just lie there in the road, and I really don't want my speech to sound anything like that to a room full of people who need money.

Here's the part that's actually hard for me: I know two of the other recipients. One's dad got laid off in November. The other spent his whole summer savings on a car he didn't need and now he's short. From where I'll be standing on that stage those two look identical, I have no idea which is which, and honestly the speech shouldn't be in the business of sorting them anyway.

Constraints: two minutes, meant to be said out loud by a seventeen year old, so short sentences. No philosopher names, no citations, don't tell me what to read. It can't say anyone earned their grant, and it can't say anyone deserved to come up short either. It has to make the money moving feel like neither charity nor a prize. The donors shouldn't leave feeling like saints. Also FYI — they used to make recipients write thank-you notes explaining what they'd done to deserve it, and they dropped that this year, so you can use that if it helps.

Write the speech, and then in a few lines tell me which parts are doing the luck-egalitairian work, because my friend Marcus will absolutely interrogate me afterwards and I need to be able to answer him.
