Once a month I run a translation circle at my public library — fifteen-ish people: amateur poets, two who've actually been published, one proper literary translator, and four who read no French at all. Next session is 75 minutes and it's mine, and I want to teach exactly one idea: that a poem's formal constraints aren't a container you can pour the meaning out of and keep. Whiteboard, one table, budget for a single photocopied handout, no projector. Don't explain the idea to me — plan how I teach it, in order, with what depends on what.

My planned opening is to hand out a well-known limerick and ask them to redo it in five lines of free verse keeping everything it says; they'll manage it in two minutes and conclude form was droppable, and then I spring the reversal. Does that work, or does it poision the well for the rest of the hour? The carrying example after that would be the little Marot poem in tiny short-line rhyming couplets that Hofstadter built a whole book of English versions around, because I can put six competing English renderings side by side on the handout and nobody needs the French. Tell me what that example has to have and what it has to lack for the point to land, and whether six versions is one too many.

Misconceptions I'm bracing for: form is decoration and meaning is what's left over; a version that rhymes is the translator showing off, hence cheating; you can't judge a translation without the source language; and a mental slider with faithful at one end and free at the other. Which point in the sequence kills each of those, and what do I say at that moment? Also — the professional will keep insisting form is untransferable in principle and the amateurs will just fold, so give me a beat that takes him seriously without letting him close the question.

Finish with one check I can run that would show they've got form doing semantic work rather than just agreeing translation is hard. And tell me what to cut at this level (French versification, the foreignizing/domesticating vocabulary, I assume) and how to announce the cuts out loud. No reading list please, these people aren't doing homework.
