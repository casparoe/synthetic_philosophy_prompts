Teaching a film-and-philosophy seminar, and next week is Modern Times. Act as a philosopher who reads Marx's stuff on machinery seriously rather than at slogan level — the long chapter in Capital on machinery and large-scale industry, plus the Grundrisse notebooks where he suggests capital's own drive to cut necessary labour-time eventually hollows out labour-time as the measure of value.

Marx opens that chapter by quoting Mill's doubt about whether all the mechanical inventions yet made have lightened the day's toil of any human being, and then answers that this was never the point: machinery's capitalist application is a means of producing surplus value. So start there. Which bits of the film actually dramatize the *distinction* between the machine and its use under capital — the speed-up ordered down from the president's office, the feeding machine that tries to abolish the lunch break, the Tramp getting sucked into the gears and coming out an oil-soaked appendage, the tightening tic that follows him off the factory floor? And where does it collapse the two and indict rhythm, speed, mechanism as such, which is a different and weaker claim?

Then the harder question. In the Manifesto Marx credits Sismondi's petty-bourgeois school with having proved, incontrovertibly, the disastrous effects of machinery and division of labour, while its positive aim was to restore old property relations or cram modern production back inside them. Is the ending — the Tramp and the Gamin walking off down an empty road at dawn — that kind of exit, and does the film know it? Argue the case, then argue against it; I dont want the movie flattened into an illustration of a category Marx happened to invent. Finish with what the film contributes to the Marx debate that argument on its own wouldn't.

Brainstorm two or three candidate readings before settling on one. And at the end say how confident you are in each main claim, and single out the one you're least sure of — I'd rather know then have you bluff. No need to quote the film, I have it in front of me.
