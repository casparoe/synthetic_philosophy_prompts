I cover science and ideas for a general-interest outlet, which means about one story a month on some new evolutionary-psychology result and about the same number of emails telling me it's a just-so story. The phrase seems to be doing very different jobs for different people -- sometimes "unfalsifiable," sometimes "untested," sometimes "the trait might not be an adaptation at all," sometimes just "the author is a Victorian in a lab coat." Before I write the piece that sorts this out, can you give me a menu?

Separate the distinct precise claims "just-so story" can be making about an evo psych explanation -- post-hoc fitting to any outcome, no independent evidence, no way to test the historical sequence as opposed to the present-day correlation, failure to rule out byproducts/drift/inherited baggage, smuggling todays values into the Pleistocene, an under-specified picture of the ancestral environment -- and for each say whether it's a trivial truth about all historical science, a substantive and fair charge, or just false.

Two things I especially need. First, which of the readings survive the retreat I keep seeing: a critic dismisses a hypothesis, an experiment later confirms the effect, and the critic says a real effect doesn't make the adaptive story any better. Second, whether the standard comeback -- that everything from plate tectonics to dinosaur parenting was once just a story about the past -- works against all those readings or only some of them, and what a charge would have to say to survive it.
