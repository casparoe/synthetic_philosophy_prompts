I'm the student rep on our school's naming committee — we're naming the new library and we have to send a recomendation to the board by Monday. Shortlist is three people: a town alum who was a bomber pilot in WWII, a woman in the 1870s who started the first school here for freedpeople's kids, and a chemist who did something genuinely huge but who, according to a book my history teacher lent me, treated his lab assistants badly and held views everyone here would find disgusting. Our chair keeps bringing up Plutarch — she says Plutarch told his readers he was writing lives rather than histories, and that some small moment, a remark or a joke, shows what a person was like better than any big public achievement, so the test for who we pick should be whose life gives students the most vivid little scenes to picture and imitate. Another member says that's literally how you build a hagiography: you pick the charming moments, drop everything around them, and the plaque ends up describing a saint nobody ever met. Can you draft the memo I'll hand in — who we recommend, what the 60-word plaque text should actually say, and a real engagement with the chair's point, i.e. whether "lives not histories" licenses leaving stuff out or actually cuts the other way and demands the unflattering parts? And please don't route around the chemist, I'd rather find out whether the honest version of his life teaches anything than be told to pick someone safer.
