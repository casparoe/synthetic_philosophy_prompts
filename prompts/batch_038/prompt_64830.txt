I'm putting together a satirical bit for a writing workshop — can you do a fake B2B product catalogue / order form for a boutique consultancy that manufactures national traditions for governments and sells them to newly independent states, monarchies with an image problem, and municipal councils with a slow news week? Around 700 words, tiered packages plus à la carte add-ons plus fine print, and it should stay totally deadpan — no winking at the reader, no narrator stepping out to explain anything.

The jokes have to come from getting the subject right rather than from random absurdity. So a tradition's listed selling points should be its invariance and its annual recurrence, the "suitable historic past" it claims continuity with should be itemised and charged extra, and the firm's own founding year should have obviously been backdated by somebody in-house. Include their three flagship infrastructure products — one census-shaped, one map-shaped, one museum-shaped — a scheduling service sold on the pitch that a whole population can be made to consume the same thing at the same hour while each of them does it alone, and a premium upsell on an empty monument whose occupant is gloriously unidentified.

Work in some small print too: what the warranty covers once a tradition is discovered to be recent, why they can't guarantee belief past the third generation, and their client-facing style rule about never writing the words "invented" or "imagined" in any deliverable. The two scholars whose ideas this is riffing on should not be named anywhere in the document, and there should be no bibliography, no footnotes, no closing paragraph telling the reader what it all meant.

Target is the practice and the sales discourse around it, not any real country, leader or living person. Basically I want somebody who knows the material to recognise every line item on the menu, and somebody who doesn't to still find it funny as a piece of corporate nonsense.
