ok so weird request but hear me out. for my AP human geo class we did a unit on famine and i went down a rabbit hole on Amartya Sen — specifically the entitlements thing, where his point about Bengal 1943 is that aggregate food availability barely dropped, so the standard "crop failure" explanation doesn't fit, and what actually happened is that whole groups lost the ability to *command* food because of prices, wages, hoarding, panic buying, wartime inflation, that kind of stuff. we also covered the Irish famine and the Great Leap Forward and the pattern is honestly infuriating.

my teacher lets us do a creative final project and i want to write something funny where the jokes only land if you actually understood the material. can you write me a blameless postmortem — like the incident-review doc a tech company's SRE team writes after a bad outage — except the incident under review is the Bengal famine of 1943? Pretend the whole food administration has been reorganized internally as an engineering org and this is their writeup, circulated to the team only, no external readers.

format: standard postmortem sections, so like Summary, Impact (with metrics — % of affected users in region, duration, availability SLO breach), Timeline, Contributing Factors, a Five Whys, Action Items with owners and ticket IDs (FAM-1943-0217 style), and a What Went Well section at the end. around 850 words, deadpan corporate incident-review voice throughout, bullets, jargon, no narrating.

the joke im going for: the team really wants to close this out as external / act-of-god — cyclone, brown spot disease, the war, the japanese next door — and the Five Whys refuses to cooperate. it keeps *not* terminating in weather and instead walks into procurement, price controls, confiscated boats, inter-provincial trade barriers, and the fact that nobody had a dashboard and nobody was allowed to publish about it. so the satire is aimed at the discourse that writes up these events as exogenous shocks instead of as a chain of decisions about who gets to command food. and the What Went Well section should be brutally awkward — the punchline is that aggregate supply metrics were basically within normal range for the period, and the team is quietly pleased about that. that's the whole Sen point, right, so it should feel like the report has just convicted itself without noticing.

two more things i want in there: one action item should encode the argument about how a free press and political accountability change whether a famine gets responded to, but only in incident-review language — like alerting was suppressed, or the escalation path routed to a stakeholder who had no incentive to page anyone — never actually using words like democracy or censorship. and if you can, slip in one reference to a similar incident at a sister org where regional reporting of yields turned out to be systematically inflated, and just leave it completely unexplained.

rules: dont put real named people in it as characters, use invented roles only (Incident Commander, Regional Supply Lead, On-Call, etc.) though real policies and institutions are fine. the joke has to be on the people writing the report and never on the people who starved — the numbers can be grim and its still funny because of the voice. and no winking, the doc should never seem aware that its being satirized. you can fudge or simplify specific details for the format but don't invent anything that contradicts what actually happened.
