I am setting the final for a graduate seminar in the philosophy of music, and I need one essay question on Schopenhauer's claim that music, unlike the other arts, does not copy the Ideas but is an immediate copy of the will itself. The question should be answerable in forty-five minutes, worth twenty-five marks, and built around a diagnostic case rather than a survey: two listeners at the same performance, one a professional analyst who can specify every harmonic and formal relation yet reports being unmoved, the other untrained and without any philosophical vocabulary, who is shaken but cannot say what she heard. Schopenhauer's account appears to entail that the second listener receives everything and the first nothing, even though he himself ranks composers and reserves the disclosure of the world's inner nature for genius. Please write the question, a model answer of roughly seven hundred words, and a marking scheme that names the moves an A answer must make — above all, how it blocks the inference that music yields knowledge of the thing-in-itself — and that specifies, with deductions, the errors characteristic of a B answer, such as treating music as the expression of determinate emotions or letting the case collapse Schopenhauer into a formalism he would reject.
