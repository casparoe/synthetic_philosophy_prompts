I sit in on a small group that meets monthly to decide where its handful of volunteers and modest budget should go. Last week two members argued at length over whether neglectedness belongs in the usual triad of criteria — importance, tractability, neglectedness — and I genuinely could not tell who was winning. Roughly how it went:

1. Mira opened by saying that of the three, neglectedness is the one doing real work, because importance and tractability are what everyone already weighs; what makes a contribution valuable is that it would not otherwise have been made. Put an eleventh person on a problem ten people are already on and the marginal product is small.

2. Dev answered that neglectedness cannot be a criterion in its own right, because problems are neglected for reasons. If almost nobody is working on something, the likeliest explanation is that it is unimportant or hopeless, so sparse attention is evidence against a cause, and treating it as evidence for one gets the sign backwards.

3. Mira said this assumes the crowd is well calibrated. Even if she and Dev agreed exactly on how important and how tractable a problem was, they should still divide their labour rather than duplicate it, since whether an advance is worth producing depends on whether someone else would have produced it anyway. That is a further fact about the world, not a redescription of importance.

4. Dev said it is a redescription of importance: define importance as the value of what happens because of you and would not otherwise have happened, and crowding is already inside it. Neglectedness is then only a crude proxy for that quantity, and a poor one, since it counts heads rather than estimating counterfactuals.

5. Mira said proxies are precisely the point — nobody in that room can estimate counterfactual marginal contributions — so substituting "marginal importance" for neglectedness removes the only handle they can actually use. Dev replied that they may keep it as a heuristic, but should stop listing it alongside the other two as if it were independent, since that is how groups end up congratulating themselves for working on things nobody else has touched, including things nobody else has touched for excellent reasons.

They asked me to referee and I declined. Could you adjudicate this move by move: for each of the five steps, say whether it is right, wrong, or only half-right, and why; settle whether the clash at step 4 is substantive or turns on how the word "importance" is being defined; and then identify what both of them are assuming that neither of them ever states.
