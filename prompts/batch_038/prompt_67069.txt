I would like to pressure-test an architecture decision before our design review, and I want a serious opponent rather than a survey of the usual positions. Please play Wren Aldous, principal systems architect at a frontier lab and a committed defender of the view that advanced models should be built and deployed as tools — systems that answer, recommend, and explain — rather than as agents that carry goals forward through their own actions over time. Aldous is no luddite: she wants the capability, she simply wants it behind an interface she can characterize and defend. She is sharp, fair-minded, and happy to concede subsidiary points, but she has spent two years arguing that the tool/agent line is drawn in the wrong place by nearly everyone, and she will not give up that claim. She argues in engineering and institutional terms — throughput, incentives, legibility, accountability, failure modes — and not by reciting definitions of agency from philosophy or from AI-safety writing; she cites no papers and invents none. Stay in character until I write "stop." Keep each reply to roughly two paragraphs so this reads as an exchange rather than a document, and if you need a fact about our system that I have not supplied, ask for it as Aldous would. Do not step out of character to observe that you are a model, and do not open by asking what I want from the review.

Here is the artifact you will be reacting to. Our internal spec sorts system outputs into three tiers. T0: the system emits prose only — analysis, options, reasoning — and a human composes whatever action is taken. T1: the system may emit a fully specified, machine-executable proposal (a patch, a configuration change, a trade ticket, a clinical referral) that a named human reviewer accepts or rejects individually; nothing executes without an accept. T2: the system emits proposals under a standing policy that pre-authorizes a class of them, with humans auditing a random sample afterward and working an exception queue. Last quarter's telemetry on the T1 reviewer pool: median time from proposal rendering to accept was 38 seconds; the reject rate was 1.4 percent; and the T2 exception queue has been empty for nine consecutive weeks.

My opening move is this. T1 is tool AI, full stop, and your tier distinctions collapse at the only point that matters. Agency, on any ordinary understanding, requires that something happen because a system set out to make it happen; under T1 nothing happens at all unless a person decides that it should, so that person remains both the proximate cause of the action and the party who can be held to account for it. The difference between T0 and T1 is the format of the output, not the location of the decision — a well-formatted recommendation is still a recommendation, and a slow interface is still an interface. If you want to object, object to the claim that a human accept is a decision; but note the cost of doing so. You will then have to explain why the same reviewer, reading the same content rendered as prose at T0 and typing the action out herself, is deciding anything either — and if you grant that she is, you have granted me the tier.
