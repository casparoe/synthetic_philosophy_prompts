Third year in a philosophy PhD, mostly in the bioethics/tech corner. I've been collecting a pattern in the treatment vs enhancement literature that I can't find a label for, and I'd like to know whether one exists or whether I'm going to have to make one up for my chapter.

Here's what I keep running into. The standard way to draw the line is by appeal to some baseline of normal functioning — bring someone back up into the normal range and you're treating them, push them past it and you're enhancing. That's basically the Daniels move and everyone downstream of it. Fine. But the baseline isn't a fixed natural kind, its a statistical fact about the population. So when enough people get treated, the distribution moves, and the line moves with it — which means the classification of the identical intervention changes over time without anyone arguing about it or deciding anything.

Two cases I've got written up:

- Height. A kid at the first percentile gets growth hormone; uncontroversially therapy, restoring normal function. After a couple of decades of that plus better childhood nutrition, the whole curve has shifted, and now a kid at the tenth percentile is getting called short, referred, treated — and it's all still logged as therapy. But the threshold that made the first kid's treatment therapeutic has slid quietly downward. Same protocol, same stated rationale, different implicit verdict about who counts as deficient.

- Focus/attention in grad school. Once a decent fraction of people in a program are on stimulants, the amount of sustained attention that counts as "what a normal person manages" creeps up, and the unmedicated students start to look impaired relative to their own peers. The baseline that was supposed to settle whether they need help has been manufactured by the helping.

So it isn't just the familiar complaint that the boundary is vague or that "normal" is contested. And it isn't anybody moving goalposts on purpose, or bad faith. It's that the criterion is defined in terms of an aggregate that the individual applications of the criterion alter, so the more successfully you apply it the less it means — and the drift is invisible from the inside, because every single step looks like a correct application of the rule. It's self-undermining by working well.

Question: is there an established name for this? I've poked at a few candidates and none of them sit right. The looping-effects stuff is about a classification changing the people it classifies rather than changing the standard they're measured against. The arms-race / positional-good story is about everyone needing to enhance just to hold their relative place, which feels downstream of what I'm after. Medicalization is about turning non-problems into problems, which is sort of the outcome but not the mechanism. So: what are the nearest real concepts, how exactly does my version differ from each, and if there genuinely isn't a name, can you coin one and tell me where its edges are — I care way more about the carving than the label. Concretely I need to know what makes something an instance: does it require a statistical baseline, or does it also cover baselines fixed by professional consensus or by statute, where the drift happens through changing committee judgement instead of changing distributions? And what's a clear near-miss that shouldn't count?
