I've been trying to figure out whether the naturalistic study of religion actually has any bearing on whether religion is true, and I've been sorting cases by gut feel. Here's the pile, verdicts and all, and I want to know what principle they add up to.

1. Kids posit invisible friends, and adults posit invisible agents, because we have a hair-trigger detector for agency and because mind-body dualism comes natural. Verdict: this counts against god-belief, but only softly. Same way that knowing about visual illusions doesn't make me distrust my eyes across the board — the machinery could be tuned to something real and just overshoot.

2. Moralizing high gods: watchful, punishing, interested in how you treat strangers. Beliefs like that spread because they let big anonymous societies hold together, and here's the part that rattles me — a watching god does that job exactly as well whether he exists or not. Verdict: this is the strongest debunker in the pile, stronger than 1.

3. Moral objectivity explained by reciprocity and kin selection — we feel that fairness is out there because tribes that felt it outcompeted tribes that didn't. Verdict: doesn't touch it. I'm a moral realist and I'm completely unbothered. But I cannot tell you why 2 bites and 3 doesn't, because those are the same shape of story. This is the pair I most need help with.

4. The all-night rites and the fasting and the tithing: perfectly explicable as commitment signals that screen out free riders. Verdict: explains why I keep showing up, not whether the creed is true. Neutral.

5. Afterlife belief out of death anxiety. Verdict: counts against, mildly — I keep reminding myself that a belief being comforting isn't a refutation, but I don't get to say that and then shrug off 2 either.

6. Had I been born in Lahore I'd be a Muslim. Verdict: defeats my particular creed, doesn't touch theism, and it's a fact about me rather than about religion.

7. The naturalist's own confidence is produced the same way — raised in a secular academic tribe, signalling to colleagues, existentially comfortable in a rich country. Verdict: in principle this should count exactly as much as 2 does. The fact that it doesn't feel that way to me is a mark against me, not against him.

8. My belief in modus ponens is also culturally transmitted, and there's probably an adaptive story for it. Verdict: obviously not debunked, and I have no account of why.

9. My professed God and my spontaneous God come apart. I say he knows everything at once; in practice I catch myself phrasing prayers as though he needs to be pointed at things, and I'm told this is general — under time pressure people answer from a much more limited god than the one they'd defend. Verdict: I don't know which belief the geneology is even aimed at, and that makes the whole enterprise shakier. But I don't feel it counts against truth at all.

My tentative principle is: a causal story about a belief counts against it only if the belief would have stuck around just as well had it been false — only if the story never has to mention the truth anywhere. That gets me 2 and it gets me 4 and 9, which I like. But it also gets me 3, since cooperative norms would have spread whether or not moral facts exist, and I'm not willing to give up moral realism. And it goes strange on 8: I don't know how to evaluate whether we'd have kept modus ponens if it were false, since nothing would have worked without it.

So: state the principle you think is actually in there, or two or three candidates, precisely, clause by clause, and tell me which case each clause is doing work for. Then say whether the clause that rescues 3 also rescues 8 and 2, or whether I can only save one of them. Run it on a case I didn't list — whether my political convictions being downstream of my class and my friends is a debunker by the same lights. And tell me straight whether I'd be better off with a fiddly principle that keeps all nine verdicts or a simple one that makes me eat one of them, and which one to eat. No reading lists or name-dropping, I'm not in a position to go look anything up.
