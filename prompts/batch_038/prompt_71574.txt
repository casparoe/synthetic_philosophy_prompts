Mises argued that if the means of production are commonly owned, capital goods are only transferred internally rather than exchanged, so there are no genuine money prices for them, and therefore nothing for a planner to calculate with — the objection is that a number of the required type does not exist, not merely that it is hard to obtain. Hayek later reframed the point as a knowledge problem: the economically relevant information is local, tacit, partial, and constantly changing, much of it known to people who could not state it if asked, so it cannot be aggregated at a center; he also suggested that competition is best understood as a procedure for discovering facts nobody possessed beforehand, rather than a mechanism for allocating facts already given. Lange's answer was that a central board could announce parametric prices, instruct enterprise managers to minimize average cost at those prices and to set output where marginal cost equals price, and then iterate on the prices by trial and error in response to observed surpluses and shortages — a tâtonnement run as an institutional procedure, with Lerner supplying the marginal-cost pricing rule.
