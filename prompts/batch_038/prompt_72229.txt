Currently stuck on Parfit: the hitch-hiker case, the stranded traveller who won't get a lift unless the driver can believe his promise of a reward, so that handing over the money once he's safely home is against his interest even though being the sort of person who hands it over is precisely what got him picked up. And the more general line he runs off the back of it, that it can be rational to acquire a disposition that will make you act irrationally on particular occassions.

What I want is a translation. Put that whole argument — the case and the general claim — into the vocabulary I actually work in: honest versus conventional signals, indices, costly signalling and the handicap, detection error rates, biological markets and partner choice, reputation, sanctions, reciprocity, and the difference between a phenotype that pays off on average and an act that pays off right now. One thing I've been chewing on. Parfit's traveller only gets the lift because he's transparent — the driver can read his disposition. In my field, a trait that can't be faked is an index rather than a signal, and an index needs no story at all about intentions, promises, or what the bearer means by his display. So does the hitch-hiker argument come out stronger or emptier once you treat the trustworthy disposition as an index? And where does the promise go — is there anything left for it to be doing?

Finish with a verdict I can argue with, not a hedge.
