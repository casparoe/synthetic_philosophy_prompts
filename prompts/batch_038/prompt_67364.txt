In my decision theory seminar I'm presenting a term paper next week and I want a prep sheet for the Q&A. My thesis: money pump arguments against intransitive preferences are dialectically toothless, because they only bite against agents who make myopic choice-by-choice decisions with no lookahead, and that's a claim about a decision procedure, not about the preference ordering itself. Here's the case I use in the talk: three prints, A, B, C, and the agent prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A, and is willing to pay a quarter at each step to trade. Run it as a sequence of offers and she ends up back at A a quarter poorer; but if she can see the whole sequence coming she just refuses the first trade and nothing is lost. So I say the pump shows intransitive preferences are dangerous only in combination with myopia, and there's no reason a cyclical agent can't adopt a no-lookahead procedure or a resolute one and keep her quarter. Objections to that move, I mean.

The room is maybe ten people: two decision theorists who work on diachronic consistency and think Hammond basically settled this, a couple of behavioral economists who study preference reversals and mostly think the whole framing is beside the point, and the rest of us grad students. Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually get from that crowd, ranked by how much damage they'd do, phrased the way each group would phrase them, with a two minute reply for each and an honest note about where the reply is soft. And include the one question I'm praying nobody asks.
