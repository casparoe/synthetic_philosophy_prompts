So I help run a little decision-theory reading group — mostly ex-quants plus a couple of philosophy people, we meet in a pub once a fortnight — and next week I've been volunteered to write up my actual position on Kelly betting and growth-rate rationality in the shared doc so everyone can take swings at it. Trouble is I've never written it down and I have a nagging feeling some of it doesn't hang together. Here's the list, roughly in the order it comes out of my mouth:

(1) When you're facing a gamble repeatedly, the thing to evaluate it by is the long-run growth rate of your own wealth — the time average along the one path you actually live — and not the expectation across a pile of parallel copies of you. A lot of the bad reasoning I see in this area comes from quietly swapping one for the other, since for a multiplicative process they aren't the same quantity.

(2) And this isn't utility theory wearing a disguise. There's no concave utility-of-money function doing work in the background, and I think "risk aversion" talk is mostly confusion. The growth rate is a property of the dynamics, not of my temperament. People who say that maximizing the growth rate is just maximizing expected log utility are, I think, missing the entire point, and I want to say that out loud in the doc.

(3) Given odds and a probability you actually know, full Kelly is the right fraction. Betting more than that is strictly worse — the growth rate comes back down — and betting less is leaving growth on the table for no reason at all.

(4) In practice I stake somewhere between a quarter and a half of that, because I basically never know the probability to more than a couple of signficant figures. I regard this as handling the estimation error honestly rather than as nerves.

(5) A one-off bet, never to be repeated: I think it's flatly irrational to take one that has positive expected value but a real chance of wiping you out. Single coin flip, heads you double, tails you lose ninety percent — I'd refuse that, and I don't think I'm being squeamish about it.

(6) If I've got several roughly independent opportunities going at the same time, I think the growth-rate logic tells me to take all of them at full Kelly on each, because the growth rate of the combined position beats the growth rate of any one of them on its own.

(7) And I dont think any of this needs an infinite horizon to get going. Ten or twenty repetitions is already plenty for the growth-rate story to be the right story; I'm not making an asymptotic promise I can't cash in.
