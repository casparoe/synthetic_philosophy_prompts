Our reading group has argued for a year about conversational implicature and arrived at a stalemate between two camps: a conservative Gricean, who holds that what is said is fixed by disambiguation and reference assignment alone, with everything further recoverable by calculation from the Cooperative Principle and the maxims; and a relevance-theoretic pragmatist, who holds that pragmatic enrichment routinely contributes to what is explicitly communicated, so that no pre-pragmatic notion of what is said does any explanatory work.

Draft the joint statement their adversarial collaboration should have produced. To stop either side retreating to its favourite illustration, both have agreed to work from this corpus:

(a) "Some of the applicants were shortlisted — indeed, all forty of them were."
(b) "If some of the plates are clean, we can start dinner."
(c) "Moira knows that some of the delegates voted against the motion, though she has seen the division list and knows that every one of them did."

I want: a numbered section of claims both camps can sign about speaker meaning, intention-recognition, and what the Cooperative Principle or the Principle of Relevance is there to explain; the residual disagreements, each worded so that its own camp would accept the formulation; the cruxes (I expect cancellability and embedding to be among them); and for each crux a diagnostic case, corpus item or experiment both would agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance. End by saying whether any part of the dispute turns only on the phrase "what is said". Give an agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and write in British English.
