Here's the position I've talked myself into. It starts from Popper's poverty-of-historicism argument but I think I've sharpened it into something a bit more specific and more mine, and that's what I want stress-tested:

1. The impossibility isn't about complexity or about our ignorance. Even a Laplacean intelligence with the whole physics in hand would fail, because social systems are prediction-sensitive in a peculiar way: publishing a forecast about such a system changes its inputs — triggering, pre-empting, or making the actors hedge — so the thing forecast is not the thing that then happens. I want to stress this isn't the observer effect from quantum mechanics and it isn't a measurement limit. It's that the forecast enters as a causal input of a kind the theory can't contain, since containing it would require a fixed point that needn't exist.

2. The state space isn't fixed either. Every genuinely new artefact, institution, or conceptual distinction adds dimensions that weren't there before, so a law of historical development would have to be a law over a domain whose variable set keeps growing. Physics gets laws because the variables are settled in advance. So at best we get laws of *society* in a thin sense: conditional regularities derived from psychology plus a specified institutional setup, holding within a specified range of conditions.

3. Therefore trends are not laws, and the difference isn't merely verbal. A trend is a claim about what has been happening under conditions that are themselves part of the explanandum, so its projectibility is hostage to boundary conditions you can neither hold fixed nor independently characterize. My working analogy: I could track hurricanes all day and be very good at five-day forecasts, and there are laws governing them (thermodynamics, the equations of motion) — but there is no law of hurricane trajectories. A track is a boundary-value outcome, not an instance of a law of development. History is like that: laws at the psychological/institutional level, no laws of development at the historical level.

So the slogan: laws of society, yes; laws of history, no — and the reason is structural, not epistemic.
