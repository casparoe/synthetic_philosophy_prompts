Coverage — at least one question on each of these, and they should build on each other rather than being a list of unrelated grievances:

- the arbitrariness objection: if the lump and the statue are distinct, why stop at two? What licenses the plurality being exactly the size it is?
- the grounding objection: what makes it true right now that the statue couldn't survive being flattened while the lump could, given they share all their matter and every qualitative property at this moment? I want the question to force her to say where that difference lives, not just to assert it.
- the four-dimensionalist deflation: same spacetime worm, different modal descriptions, no coincidence at all — what is actually left of her view once somebody says that?
- how much weight the whole picture rests on modal intuitions about squashing and melting, and what she says to the person who thinks those intuitions track how we talk rather than what there is.
- the dominant-kind escape (only one of the co-located things really exists, because one sortal dominates): is that a genuine alternative to pluralism or a rebranding of it?
- sortal-relative identity as the rival, and whether it collapses back into ordinary identity once you press it.
- the easy-ontology charge that the whole dispute is verbal. Here I want her to say what would settle it, if anything could.

Then the part I need most, because my character doesn't live in the textbook example.
