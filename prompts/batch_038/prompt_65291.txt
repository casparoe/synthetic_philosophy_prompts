I run a small consciousness seminar for second-year cog sci students and I keep hitting the same wall. They all nod politely when I explain the Orwellian vs. Stalinesque distinction, and then the second I hand them a case they haven't seen before they sort it into whichever of the two they like the sound of better. So I want to build a decision tree that sorts them by what they actually believe about when experience gets fixed, and I want every node to be a question about a mechanism or a case — nothing that just asks "do you think the rewrite happens before or after it becomes conscious?", because that hands them the answer with the label still on it.

Aim for ten to twelve question nodes and five or six leaves. The branches should turn on things like: where in the pipeline the content gets fixed; whether there's ever a moment at which the anomalous content is available to *any* subsystem in the head, even one that can't report it and can only press a key; whether what got revised was the experience, the memory of it, or only the report; whether the subject is a reliable witness about their own timings at all; and whether there's any fact of the matter about when the experience happened. Leaves should include the delayed-entry/show-trial view and the unpersoned-memory view, obviously, but also the deflationary no-priviledged-moment option, the view where the experience was perfectly veridical and only the report got cooked, and one where the pre-revision content is never conscious but does leak into behaviour. Name each leaf, invent a name if there isn't a good one already in circulation, and add one sentence about the most awkward thing that leaf has to swallow.

Three cases the tree has to be able to adjudicate, and I'd like you to actually run each one through it and tell me which leaf it lands on:

1. color phi — a red dot, then a green dot about half a blink later and a bit off to the side, and the subject reports a dot that travelled and changed colour somewhere along the way.
2. the forearm tapping illusion — taps at the wrist followed by taps up near the elbow, and the subject feels taps at spots in between that nothing ever touched.
3. one my lab is designing, which I don't think the standard write-ups cover. A dot jumps position three times. On every trial we log a forced-choice keypress that's time-locked to the moment of the third jump, so we have a motor readout of when the jump got registered, and separately we collect a verbal report about a second later. I want the tree to have somewhere to put the case where the keypress is locked to the real third jump but the verbal report describes the dot as still sitting at position two.

Related control for the same tree: identical sequence, except between the third jump and the report we tell the subject — falsely — that our equipment glitched and there was no third jump. If they then report smooth motion all the way through, has anything about their experience been revised, or just the record of it? The tree should say. If it can't say, tell me which node I'm missing.

Last thing: flag every pair of leaves the tree can't actually separate, and list the combinations of answers that are logically available but empty — nobody defends them — with a line each on whether that's because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just hasn't been picked up yet. It's a handout, so keep the whole thing to about two pages.
