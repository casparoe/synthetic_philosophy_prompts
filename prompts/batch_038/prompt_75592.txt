I'm helping run a small funding coalition — three organisations with genuinely different value systems (one focused on farmed animal welfare, one on global health, one on AI risk / the long-term future) that have agreed to pool a pot of money and make some of the calls jointly. It's going badly in a very predictable way. Someone proposes a split, someone else says it's unfair to their view, and we end up dividing it three ways and calling that a compromise, which nobody actually thinks is principled. A friend of mine who does game theory keeps telling me the whole thing is already solved by Nash bargaining, but I don't buy it, partly because I can't see how we'd ever compare our utilities against each other and partly because our disagreements aren't really about splitting a fixed pie. Anyway — before we start shopping round for procedures, I want to write down what we'd even want from one.

So: give me the desiderata, the full spec, for a procedure that settles joint decisions or divides resources between parties whose values are actually in conflict and probably incommensurable, where the point is to capture gains from trade rather than to discover some common morality nobody has. For each requirement, give the motivating case — the concrete situation that shows why a rule failing it would be unacceptable to us, not just an abstract complaint. And be blunt about which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just the pet preferences of one camp in this literature. In particular I want you to cover: what the rule can assume about its inputs (cardinal utilities? interpersonally comparable ones? honest reporting — what if a party just inflates how much it cares, since we all know that's tempting?); which distinctions it mustn't collapse, e.g. between compromise and straightforward trade, between splitting the difference and reaching a Pareto improvement, and between a rule being fair and a rule being one the losing party can live with; what happens when the membership or the intensity of people's convictions shifts over time (we might take on a fourth member next year and one of the current three is quietly becoming much less confident in its own view); and whether there are side constraints from ordinary morality that a compromise can't be allowed to trade away at all.

Most of all: if some subset of these requirements can't be jointly satisfied — if there's an impossibility sitting in the spec rather than a gap in our cleverness — say so and tell me which one to give up. That's the bit I'd actually take back to the group. Then flag which single requirement knocks out the most of the obvious candidates, and give me a very short verdict on the two or three standard answers (a parliamentarian-style voting method, a Nash or Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining solution, and some kind of running tab of moral credits between parties) measured against the list. Keep that last part brief — a couple of sentences each is plenty — because the requirements and the trade-offs between them are what I'm after.
