I chair the technical steering committee of a large open-source library of formalized mathematics: roughly two hundred contributors, a kernel built on a constructive dependent type theory in which the law of excluded middle is available only as an explicitly invoked axiom, and a corpus that serves two very different constituencies. A consortium of graduate programs uses it to accompany standard analysis and algebra texts, and two aerospace contractors use portions of it as the trusted basis for certifying numerical software. The item on next month's agenda is which choice principles the library will treat as ambient, and I need a memo to circulate beforehand.

Our present practice is inconsistent. Most of the analysis was formalized early on with classical logic and full choice invoked silently at the kernel level; a smaller body of work contributed later avoids both. Three weeks ago one of the certification teams asked us to state, for a fixed list of functional-analysis results their toolchain depends on, exactly which of them require full choice, which require only dependent choice, and which are available over the constructive base. We cannot answer. The dependency is recorded nowhere: choice enters through intermediate lemmas — a maximal-ideal argument here, a well-ordering there, an appeal to Zorn's lemma buried in a compactness proof — and then propagates transitively to everything downstream. One contractor has now said it will not accept any existence principle it cannot point to in an audit report; a course coordinator has said the opposite, that the corpus must mirror textbooks, which assume choice freely and without comment.

Four options are before the committee: (1) adopt classical logic and the full axiom of choice as ambient and untagged everywhere, on the grounds that this is what mathematics actually assumes and that bookkeeping diverts effort from content; (2) localize choice, requiring every result whose proof depends on it to carry an explicit tag so the dependency graph is queryable; (3) fix an intermediate ambient base — dependent choice together with the Boolean prime ideal theorem — with full choice available only behind an explicit tag; (4) maintain two parallel branches, one classical and one constructive, with a standing obligation to keep the overlapping material in sync.

Two of the arguments have stopped being about engineering. A senior contributor, who describes himself as a formalist, holds that the entire tagging proposal rests on a category mistake: axioms are not candidates for truth but moves that fix the rules of a corpus, so recording choice as a hypothesis on a theorem implies there is a genuine mathematics in which that theorem might fail — a claim nobody on the committee is prepared to defend. A constructive type theorist on the committee warns in reply that adding a set-extensional choice axiom at the kernel level would, by Diaconescu's theorem, drag excluded middle in with it and silently destroy the constructive reading of the whole corpus; a third member thinks she is overextending that result to a foundation where it may not apply, and has said so in writing. Two of our members are not logicians and have told me they cannot currently evaluate any of this exchange.

Also pending, and needing a disposition under whichever option we choose, are two pull requests: one adds the theorem that every vector space has a basis, which its author notes is equivalent over ZF to full choice, and which a graduate algebra course requires; the other proves Hahn–Banach for arbitrary Banach spaces by an ultrafilter argument, and its author maintains it needs nothing stronger than the Boolean prime ideal theorem.

Please draft the memo I will circulate, running to roughly 1,000 to 1,300 words, in American English. It should open with a single recommended option — or a definite variant of one — stated so plainly that the committee could vote on it as written, and then give the reasoning. I need, specifically: a transition plan with concrete milestones and dates, covering what happens to the results already in the corpus and to the two pending pull requests; a bright-line rule that a contributor can apply to an ordinary pull request without consulting the committee, together with a worked application of that rule to each of the two pending requests; a paragraph written for the two non-specialist members explaining why full choice and dependent choice differ in what they cost us, and stating whether the Diaconescu warning actually applies to the formulation under discussion or whether the third member is right to discount it; and the strongest consideration on the losing side stated at full force, including whichever of the formalist's and the auditor's objections cuts deepest, with an honest account of why it does not carry the decision. Do not recommend further study, a pilot, or a working group — we are being asked to decide. The memo should stand on its own argument; no bibliography or citations are needed, and please do not invent sources.
