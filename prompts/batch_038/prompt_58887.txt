Ok so this is kind of embarrassing, but I've been going in circles with my coffee setup for about two years now and I can't seem to get out of it.

It started with a nice espresso machine. I loved it for a few months, then decided it was too much of a production every morning — grinding, tamping, cleaning the group head, all before work. So I sold it (lost maybe 150 bucks after fees and shipping) and got a pour over setup, figuring simple is better. The pour over was great for like two months and then I got sick of how slow and fiddly it is, plus all the filters and the water I was dumping, so I sold that and got a pod machine. Fast, no cleanup at all. That lasted maybe three months before the coffee started tasting flat and I felt gross about the plastic waste, so I sold the pod thing and bought... another espresso machine. Basically the same one I started with.

Then it happened again. I'm on my third full loop now. Every round trip I come out a couple hundred dollars poorer, sometimes more, between what used gear actually sells for versus what I pay to replace it, shipping both ways, and the extra stuff I buy to go with each setup (burr grinder, gooseneck kettle, whatever the current thing needs).

Here's what bugs me. At the moment I make each switch I genuinely think it's the better option. Nobody's talking me into it, nobody's ripping me off, all the prices are fair — I just always end up back where I started with less money. And I can see the whole loop coming from a mile away. Halfway through the espresso phase I can already feel the pour-over pull starting, and I know exactly how this ends, and I still make the trade. I don't feel like I'm contradicting myself at any single point, which is the frustrating part.

My wife says the obvious fix is to just pick one and be done with it. But that feels like it's missing something, because whichever one I own is the one I'm tired of. If I forced myself to keep the pour over forever I'd just be miserable in a different way, and I'd be doing it to prove a point.

So: is there actually something wrong with how I'm making these decisions, or is my wife's "commit and move on" just not the answer? I've tried rules — I told myself I wouldn't sell anything for six months — and I blew through that in about a week, because at the time the trade really did look good and I couldn't see why I should pass on it. What should I be doing differently here? Is the problem the wanting, or the deciding?
