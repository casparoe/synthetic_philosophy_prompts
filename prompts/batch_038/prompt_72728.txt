Not an economist — I volunteer on a housing group in my city — and there's a pattern I keep running into in the rent control arguments that I don't have a word for.

Two things I keep seeing. First: a controlled apartment is, on paper, maybe six hundred dollars a month cheaper than what the same unit would rent for on the open market, and everyone in the meeting counts that six hundred as a straight benefit to the tenant. But when you actually talk to the people involved, the six hundred seems to leak off in several directions at once — a three-year waiting list, a broker's fee that works out to most of a year's rent, an envelope for the superintendent to get your name onto the short list, a landlord who stops fixing anything so nobody wants to transfer out, the tenant subletting the place at the real market price while living somewhere else. Nobody seems to end up actually holding the savings; its all spent in friction.

Second: it doesn't always leak. Sometimes it sticks to one particular person and turns into a thing they own. The woman who got her lease in 1978 has an asset — she can pass it to her daughter, or find someone to "buy the furniture" for five figures, and she effectively never leaves, and nobody counts her as a beneficiary of anything. Same shape with taxis: a city caps the number of permits and the permits themselves end up selling for more than the car, so the gain is real, but it's been converted into a price tag on a piece of paper, and whoever holds the paper collects it indefinitely while a newcomer gets none of it. I gather import quotas look like this too.

So: is there an established concept here? I can vaguely guess that the first case is what people mean when they say the gains get competed away, but what I really want to know is what the *residual* in the second case is called — the chunk that doesn't get competed away because it got welded to a person or a permit. Are those one phenomenon or two? What's the nearest existing vocabulary, how does each of those differ from what I described, and where does this stop being the thing and start being plain corruption, plain deadweight loss, or plain "the benefit landed on the wrong people"? And if the sticking version doesn't have its own name, invent one and define it tightly enough that I could use it in a public meeting without getting corrected by someone who reads more economics than I do. A bare label won't help me — I need the edges.
