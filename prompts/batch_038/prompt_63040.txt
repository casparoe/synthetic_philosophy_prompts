ok so I've been slowly reading Gödel, Escher, Bach over the summer and I keep going back to the careenium bit. Roughly as I remember it: you imagine a brain as a box full of a huge number of tiny balls that careen around and clump together into bigger clumps, and the walls of the box change how bouncy they are depending on what's going on outside, so the outside world gets let in. The little balls are the neurons/physical substrate and the big clumps are the symbols — simmballs, symbol + balls, get it. And Hofstadter's question is basically: who shoves whom around inside the box? Do the balls shove the symbols, or do the symbols shove the balls? And his answer is something like: both, they're two equally legitimate ways of describing the same events, it's just a matter of what level you zoom to.

I don't buy the both-ways answer, and I made up a variant case that I think shows why. Call it the locked careenium. Same box, same clumping, but the balls have zero motion of their own. No noise, no leftover careening, nothing bouncing that isn't doing symbolic work. At every instant, the positions and momenta of the balls are exactly and only whatever is required to realize the next simmball-level transition. If two clumps merge, the balls just are that merging. There's no independent physics running alongside the symbol level, ever. Total lockstep, zero slack.

Please argue FOR this position: in the locked careenium the simmballs genuinely shove the balls around — literally, not as a figure of speech — whereas in the original loose careenium they don't, and Hofstadter's "both descriptions are equally real" line is really just politeness toward the symbol level. I.e. the amount of slack in the substrate is what settles whether a symbol-level causal story is true or just a convenient gloss. I know this is backwards from the usual intuition (people think loose substrate = more room for the higher level to be its own thing), so part of the job is making the reversal feel inevitable rather than cute.

Here's the thing that's bugging me and your argument has to meet it head on, not step around it. The normal counterfactual test for one level pushing another seems to give the exact opposite verdict. To show simmballs shove balls you want something like: if I could have rearranged the clumps without touching the balls, the balls would then have moved differently. But in the locked careenium that counterfactual has no possible antecedent, because there's no way to change one without the other. In the loose careenium you kind of can do it — jiggle the balls and the clump patterns survive, or change the wall bounciness and watch the clumps change. So the loose one looks genuinely two-leveled and the locked one looks like one level described twice. Argue my position anyway and explain why that test misfires here, or why it should be run at the clump level rather than the ball level, or whatever move you actually need.
