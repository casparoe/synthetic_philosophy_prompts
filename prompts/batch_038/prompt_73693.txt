I'm on the science panel of a private conservation foundation. Our charter lets us grant only for "species on the brink," and in eleven years we've never once defined species — we just followed whatever the taxonomists happened to say. Now there's a petition about a population of tree frogs confined to a single valley, maybe four thousand adults and shrinking. On one mitochondrial marker it's a deeply diverged, clean lineage; in the field you can't tell it from its relative three valleys over; and where the two ranges touch in a wet year, they hybridize freely and the offspring breed. Our staff biologist says that settles it, it fails the biological species concept and we should pass. One board member says the whole point of the charter is evolutionary distinctiveness and this frog is about as distinct as it gets, so refusing is a betrayal of our own mission. A third points out we funded a subspecies last year without blinking. We vote Friday and I have to circulate something by Wednesday. Draft the memo: a clear recommendation on whether we fund, plus a workable rule the panel could apply to the next ten petitions like this one, and a real engagement with the distinctiveness argument rather than just noting that our charter is vague.
