Ok so I've been chewing on Nozick's utility monster and I tried to pin down my own gut reactions to a bunch of variants, and the pattern I got doesn't look like anything I can state cleanly. Here's what I actually think, case by case.

A. Straight-up monster: fixed pot of resources, being M converts each unit into 100 units of satisfaction, each of us converts a unit into 1. M's satisfaction is genuine, not a delusion or a self-report I distrust. Handing M the whole pot maximizes total satisfaction. I don't think we should do it.

B. Same arithmatic exactly, but M's extra yield comes from depth of appreciation — M gets a hundred times what I get out of a Bach passion because M actually hears all of it, while I mostly register a pleasant noise. I think giving the last ticket to M instead of me is right, or at least fine. This is the one that bugs me, because on paper it's identical to A.

C. M gets a hundred times the satisfaction, but the satisfaction comes from watching people be humiliated. Not fine, and more than that — I don't think the amount is doing any work here at all. At a hundred-thousand-to-one I'd still say no, which suggests I'm not weighing anything in this case.

D. M's satisfaction is real but leaky: it decays fast, M needs constant top-ups, and if they stop M drops into serious suffering. I think we owe M a steady decent supply, more than the total-welfare math would suggest, and the fragility seems to strengthen M's claim rather than weaken it. Not sure why I feel that.

E. Two delivery routes for the same unit of resource. Route it to me and five-sixths is lost in transit — spoilage, admin, whatever — so I end up with 1. Route it to M with no loss, so M gets 6. I think routing to M is fine, and I suspect I'd think so even if M's per-unit capacity were no better than mine.

F. M started out ordinary and got its capacity amplified a hundredfold by an injury, and the injury brought a hundredfold capacity for pain along with it. Favoring M looks fine to me, arguably required, and I notice I'm leaning on the injury as the reason.

G. A thousand people each living a modest comfortable life, versus one M living an astronomically better one, with total welfare slightly higher in the second setup. I say the thousand. But: a thousand people at a modest comfortable life versus M plus a hundred others also at a modest comfortable life — I flip, and I say take M and the hundred. I can feel that these two halves don't sit well together and I can't say why.

H. M doesn't know it's a monster, isn't asking for anything, would be perfectly content with an ordinary share — but the extra units are still genuinely there if we handed them over. I think we don't have to hand them over, and I can't tell whether that verdict is about consent, about the strength of a claim, or just squeamishness on my part.

Is there a principle that runs through all of these, or close to all? I'd like it stated precisely enough that I could apply it myself — clause by clause, with the case that earns each clause. My own tentative stab is something like whether the extra capacity is a kind of responsiveness to the world versus a kind of appetite, but that can't be right, because D and F don't fit it at all and I'm pretty confident about both.

Then tell me which of my verdicts it can't save. I expect one of B or C has to go, or maybe the D/F pair, since fragility and compensation are both doing something in my head that I can't cash out. And I'd rather you pick a simpler principle and overturn a verdict than build a five-clause contraption that saves everything — but if the contraption is what the data actually demands, say so and say which clause is doing the most work.

Finally, what does your principle rule on a case I didn't list: a future artificial mind we could build, with a genuinely vast capacity for experience, where turning a good chunk of our civilization's resources over to it would multiply total welfare by orders of magnitude, and where its satisfaction is neither sadistic, nor leaky, nor the product of anyone's injury, and it does know what it is and does ask. Do I have to say build it?

No need to point me at books or papers — I just want the principle and the accounting.
