Okay so I picked up one of those airport-bookstore neuroscience books and there's a stretch on split-brain patients that I keep going back to, partly because I want to believe it. Here's roughly what it says, condensed a bit as I type it:

"When surgeons cut the corpus callosum to keep seizures from spreading, they cut the bridge between two minds. Each hemisphere of a split-brain patient has its own perceptions, its own beliefs, and its own intentions, and each is largely cut off from what the other is up to. Information from the left visual field arrives in the right hemisphere and nowhere else, so the patient's left hemisphere genuinely cant see half of what he's looking at. In one of Gazzaniga's experiments the right hemisphere was shown a snowy scene and the left a chicken claw, and when the patient was asked to point at related pictures his left hand went to a shovel. Asked to explain himself, his left hemisphere instantly produced a reason: you need a shovel to clean out the chicken shed. That's the interpreter, a module whose whole job is to narrate behavior it has no access to. Which means consciousness isn't one thing but many, and the unified self is a press release issued after the fact. These patients don't have one mind that's been split in two; there are two subjects of experience in there, two people sharing a skull."
