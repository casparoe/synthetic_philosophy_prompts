I want a parody enterprise service-level agreement for a deployed LLM — title it something like "Alignment SLA, Standard Tier" and write it as numbered clauses with a definitions section, a metrics table like you'd see in an uptime SLA, exclusions, and remedies. Around 600 words, deadpan legal voice, no winking.

The whole joke should be that the vendor only ever has average-case guarantees and keeps sliding them into slots where a customer would expect worst-case ones: "aligned on 99.8% of measured prompts" presented as if it were a bound, distribution shift classed as force majeure, adversarial prompts excluded as "user-generated out-of-distribution traffic", the remedy for a misalignment incident being a service credit and a re-fine-tune. Get the actual technical content right — a reference distribution the vendor picks, eval contamination, Goodhart, the fact that an average over your benchmark says nothing at all about one catastrophic case.

Target the practice of assurance-by-evals, not any named company. And don't let the vendor accidently concede a worst-case claim anywhere; the fine print should stay internally consistent so that a reader who knows the literature can feel exactly where the gap is without the document ever admitting it. Should still be readable for a non-ML person.
