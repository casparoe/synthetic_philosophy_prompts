I spent thirty-four years directing an analytical chemistry laboratory and came to philosophy only after retiring; I now facilitate a monthly reading group of fourteen people, mostly former scientific colleagues, none of them professional philosophers, and we have ninety minutes per session. I should like you to design an activity that makes us collide with the dispute between conciliationism and steadfastness about peer disagreement rather than merely hear it summarised.

The specific complication I want, and the reason I am commissioning this rather than assembling it myself, is that the private evidence must be genuinely unequal in quality. Each participant should receive an indication of some quantity that has a determinate answer known only to the facilitator, but some indications should be clean and others deliberately degraded — a narrow range of possible values in one case, a wide and noisy one in another. The obvious move, weighting everyone equally and averaging, should then be visibly the wrong thing to do, and the group should have to work out for itself what follows.

Please provide: the rules; the materials (nothing beyond paper, dice, a deck of cards, and a whiteboard); a scoring scheme in points or tokens, with no money involved; a minute-by-minute timeline for the ninety minutes; and a debrief that maps particular moments of play onto particular claims, including what the steadfast position owes us in light of the fact that a stubborn participant can be right only by accident, and what the conciliationist owes the participant who held the better evidence. Withhold the names of the views and of their proponents until the debrief. Include a further round in which participants are told only that a disagreement exists among them, without being shown anybody's answer, and state plainly what that round is meant to expose.

Finally, be candid with me: identify which feature of genuine philosophical disagreement the exercise cannot reproduce and which I shall have to supply in conversation, and tell me what is most likely to go wrong the first time I run it. Please write the whole design in British English.
