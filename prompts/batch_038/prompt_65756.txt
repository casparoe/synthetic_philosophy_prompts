The one idea I need them to leave with is that moral status, if it attaches to computation at all, attaches to *which* computations and to *how they are run*, not to how human-like the output looks. Plan the session rather than explaining the idea: what has to be in place before the question can even be posed to them, and the order the rest must go in, given that I want to build it on two cases I have stipulated — one in which an identical set of weights is run as a single continuous stream and as a 4096-way batch of mutually independent requests, and one in which a partially completed rollout is discarded mid-generation and the checkpoint is rolled back, so exactly the same tokens get produced a second time. For each case, tell me what it has to have and what it must be careful not to smuggle in; then map the misconceptions the engineers will bring (I assume "bigger model, more to lose" and "if it didn't affect the loss curve, nothing was lost") and the precise point in the sequence where each should be headed off; then give me one question I can put to them at the end that would separate those who actually grasped the attribution problem from those who merely became politer toward machines; and finally, what I should openly decline to cover at this level.
