So I'm the secretary of the board of a small nonprofit makerspace — nine of us, all volunteers — and a former member left us a $40k bequest that we have to spend on exactly one thing by the end of the month. Four proposals are live: (L) a laser cutter plus a small CNC, which eats basically the whole bequest; (S) a half-time paid coordinator funded for about three years; (E) a deposit and buildout toward moving into a bigger unit; (R) bank it as a reserve fund. Our bylaws specify ranked-choice/instant runoff, which we adopted three years ago because somebody read a Wikipedia article about it and it sounded fancy. Here's my best read of the nine ballots, which I'm confident about because I've sat next to these people at potlucks for six years: Dale, Marcus, Ines and Bo are the tool people, L > S > E > R. Priya, Tomás and June are the space people, E > S > R > L. Ruth, our treasurer, is R > E > S > L. And me: S > E > R > L. Note that the coordinator is nobody's first choice except mine, and Marcus has hinted he could live with it, which might soften the tool bloc's lower ranks but I wouldn't bet money on it.

There are three fights happening at once and I can't tell which ones are real. Fight one: I did some napkin arithmetic and I'm pretty sure S dies in the first round, because S and R both have exactly one first-place vote and Dale chairs the meeting and gets the tiebreak, and Dale would rather eat glass than hire staff. But I also can't tell whether that tiebreak actually changes anything, because if R goes first instead then Ruth's ballot flows to E anyway. Fight two, about the rules themselves: Priya wants to switch to approval voting *for this decision only*, on the grounds that with approval you just tick every option you can live with and nobody has to pretend. Dale says that's naive, that some professor proved decades ago that every voting system is gameable, so we should stop burning the meeting on rules and just vote our guts. Ruth, who is dry as a bone, said "then let's draw names out of a hat, or let me pick, at least then nobody has to lie," and everyone laughed — but I've been thinking about it for three days and I'm genuinely not sure she was joking. What creeps me out is that Priya is advocating a rule that on my own numbers helps *my* favorite more than hers, which either means she's being weirdly public-spirited or she's imagining everyone bullet-voting. Fight three, the one actually eating me: I keep feeling like ranking something I don't want at the top of my ballot is a species of lying, and Dale tells me to grow up, and I can't articulate why neither of us is quite right.

I read a ton of rationalist blogs back in the day and I have a nagging feeling this exact situation has a name and a canonical writeup, but I can't look anything up right now and honestly I'd rather you reason from my numbers than from whatever the standard framing is. So: play a social choice theorist who has also spent a decade facilitating governance for small nonprofits — I want the math *and* the meeting advice, not just one of them. Please start by brainstorming out loud and actually building the round-by-round tally table before you give me a single recommendation, and think carefully about each person's claim rather than pattern-matching to "all voting systems are flawed, communicate openly."

Concretely, what I want:

1. Run the sincere IRV tally on my numbers, both tiebreak branches, and tell me who wins and whether the tiebreak is load-bearing at all.
2. Is S winnable under IRV given any ballot I could realistically cast, or any coalition of me plus one other person? Or is it simply dead on arrival, and if so, is that a fact about S or a fact about the rule?
3. Which other rules would actually have elected S, and — the part I can't settle — is "nobody's first choice but beats everything head-to-head" a feature or a bug? Tell me which side of that you'd defend and why, because I keep flip-flopping.
4. Sort the three board members: is Priya right that there's a rule where honesty is always your best move, is Dale right that it's provably impossible, and is Ruth's joke actually the technically correct answer? For each, say whether the claim is true, true-but-useless, or false, and in what sense.
5. What do I do Tuesday: what goes on my ballot, and what do I say about changing the rules for a decision that's already pending — including whether it's legitimate to switch procedures when everyone can see which proposal the switch favors.
