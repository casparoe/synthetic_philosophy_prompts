I direct a small judgment-and-forecasting program at a public policy school, and this year I have been asked to do something I would rather not do badly: write down, in advance of choosing anything, what would have to be true of an account of epistemic utility for it to count as adequate. Concretely, I mean the kind of account that is supposed to underwrite the two headline results of the accuracy-first program — that credences violating the probability axioms are accuracy-dominated by some coherent alternative under any respectable measure of inaccuracy, and that conditionalizing on one's evidence maximizes expected accuracy by the agent's own lights. I have seen those arguments, and I have also seen requirements deployed inside them that look to me less like constraints on any theory in the area than like commitments of one camp. What I want from you is the specification itself, not a survey of who defends what: no citations, no reading list, no author names, just the requirements and their motivation.

So give me the desiderata for an adequate theory of epistemic utility — covering both the inaccuracy measure and the surrounding normative package — and for each one state the consideration or the concrete case that motivates it, mark it as non-negotiable or as a partisan preference, and say which familiar proposals it eliminates. Please address at minimum: truth-directedness, and what it rules out; whether the measure must decompose additively across the propositions scored, or whether holistic scoring of an entire credal state is a requirement or a taste; continuity, and the treatment of extreme credences, including whether a measure that assigns an infinite penalty for credence zero in a truth is a virtue or a disqualifier; strict propriety — the demand that every credal state expect itself to be the most accurate — and what work that requirement is actually doing, given that it appears to guarantee in advance that no rational agent is ever obliged to distrust herself; whether the uniqueness of the measure (why the logarithmic score rather than the quadratic one) is a demand on the theory or a question the theory is permitted to bracket; sensitivity to how finely the question space is carved, i.e. whether a measure whose verdicts shift when a single proposition is split into two equiprobable sub-propositions is thereby defective; whether the account must extend to imprecise or merely comparative doxastic states; and whether the norms it delivers must be followable by an agent who cannot enumerate her own commitments — a constraint people seem to gesture at without ever stating. A table is acceptable if the reasoning behind each row is still visible.

The part I most want is the last. I suspect three of these requirements cannot all be met at once, and I would like the suspicion tested rather than indulged. Call them alethic purity — no premise about choice, preference, or action may be used to establish a doxastic norm, so that the derivation does not quietly become a piece of decision theory; immodesty, as above; and guidance — the theory must yield a determinate verdict for an agent who is unsure what her own evidence requires, including an agent unsure whether she has conditionalized correctly. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, identify it, give the argument, and say which one you would surrender and why; if they are compatible, I want the construction rather than a shrug. This matters for a practical reason. A substantial fraction of our tournament questions never resolve inside the funding period — twenty-five-year horizons, contested counterfactuals, questions whose resolution criteria get renegotiated midway — so for those we score forecasters against a proxy, either a committee's eventual judgment or the pool's aggregate. Consider, then, what I will call proxy divergence: a score should not reward a forecaster for moving toward what the resolver will say in a case where the resolver is expected to be wrong about the underlying fact. Tell me which of your desiderata survive verbatim when the target of assessment is a proxy rather than the truth, which must be restated, and which simply lapse; and tell me whether any strictly proper score can avoid rewarding divergence, or whether strict propriety relative to a proxy is precisely the feature that produces it. I am not asking you to design our scoring system — I am asking whether there is one list of requirements here or two, and if two, which single desideratum does the separating work.
