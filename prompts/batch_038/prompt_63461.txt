so this turned personal last month and I can't stop chewing on it. i've lived in my town for eleven years — own a place, pay local taxes, kids in the school down the road — but I never bothered to naturalise, kept the old passport. Now the municipality is being folded into a bigger one and there was a referendum on the merger. Turns out residents like me don't get a vote in it, only citizens do, even though the boundary being redrawn is the one I actually live inside. At the town hall a councillor shut the complaint down by saying a political community is basically a club: members decide who joins, and you can't lobby your way in from outside. He even waved the book around — Walzer, Spheres of Justice, the chapter on membership — and said the state is the sphere that works like a club and that's that.

Can you take that analogy apart for me? I want the mapping written out explicitly, not vibes: what in a club corresponds to the demos, the admission rules, the dues, the exit option, the clubhouse itself, the fact that the club already exists before you knock on the door. Then which of those correspondences survive contact with an actual state and which ones collapse. And crucially, separate the disanalogies that do damage to what the councillor was using it for from the ones that are just noise — I don't want a list of quibbles.

The bit I really can't articulate: it feels to me like being shut out of the immigration decision and being shut out of the merger vote are not the same complaint, even though both are "the club decides who's in." Beacuse in the second case the club's own shape is what's up for grabs, and I'm already standing in the room. Does the club analogy do different work in those two cases, or does it fail in the second one in a way it doesn't in the first? And what is the club picture quietly importing that a state just doesn't have — the feature that makes a club's boundary a non-problem while a state's boundary is the whole problem?

Finally, does his conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table — can "it's a club" ever get you to "so the current members settle it by majority vote"? And if club is the wrong image, is there a better one — family, neighbourhood, the crew of a ship, a company town — and does that one hand me a ballot or not? Just work it through yourself, I'm not after a bibliography or a syllabus.
