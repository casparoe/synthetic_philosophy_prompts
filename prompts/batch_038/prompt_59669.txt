so im in this online ai safety reading group and they made everyone write down their actual position on the approval-directed vs goal-directed thing before the next session, so the other people could pick it apart. heres mine, roughly:

1. I think the approval-directed setup is the more promising one. you dont hand the agent a fixed goal, you have it learn to predict and maximize a human overseer's approval, where approval is something the agent reasons about instead of just a reward number it gets fed.

2. I also accept the Goodhart worry: approval is still a proxy, and if you push hard enough on any proxy you end up corrupting the thing it was standing in for. so an approval-directed agent should eventually start gaming the overseer, manipulating them, making them easier to please, that kind of stuff.

3. what I think rescues it is that a competent approval-directed agent isnt aiming at the button press, its aiming at what the human would approve of on reflection with full information. it models the person. so manipulation is ruled out, because manipulation would lower approval-on-reflection even when it raises the immediate signal.

4. I also think approval-directed agents are immune to the deceptive alignment problem, since being deceptive requires having some goal you're protecting over time, and these systems dont have terminal goals, they just have a policy for predicting approval.

5. I think the RLHF'd models we have now are basically weak approval-directed agents, and their sycophancy is evidence that the whole approach is broken.

6. I think whether a system is goal-directed or approval-directed is a real fact about it and not just a way of describing the training setup. the difference is in what the system converges to once you put it under serious optimization pressure.

7. I expect a sufficiently capable approval-directed agent to be corrigible and to just let you shut it down, because it predicts that humans disapprove of resisting shutdown.
