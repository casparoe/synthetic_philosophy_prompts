Ok so I'm not academic at all, I manage a team at a distribution centre, and for two years I've also been the one running our recognition program — you know the type: peer points you can cash in for gift cards, a nominations wall, employee of the month, plus a 360 review where the people below you fill in a form about you.

Two things have been bugging me and I cant tell if they're just bad implementation or something more basic.

First one. My boss gets glowing upward reviews, near-perfect scores. He told me in the car park that he gets nothing out of them, feels nothing, because everyone writing them knows he signs off on their pay and their promotion. And then I realised I felt exactly the same when my own team nominated me last quarter.

Second one. The points are so easy to give that people just spray them around — you get five a week for doing normal stuff — and they've stopped meaning anything. The wall is basically a participation trophy that we pay a software company for.

Meanwhile HR talks about recognition like it's a resource we manufacture and hand out, and there's this constant language about people needing to feel seen and to bring their whole selves to work.

So here's what I want worked out. What theory of recognition does this whole apparatus presuppose? What has to be true about people, and about what they need from each other, for points-and-nominations to be a sensible thing to spend real money on — and which rival pictures does it quietly rule out, the ones where recognition couldnt be delivered this way at all, no matter how well you run the program?

Then: are my two gripes the same problem or two different ones, and is either fixable from inside the thing, or does the design guarantee them? I keep half-remembering that Hegel bit about the master and the slave and it feels relevant to the first one — the bloke at the top being praised by people who depend on him and getting zero satisfaction out of it — but I couldn't tell you what the actual argument is, so use it if it genuinely applies and tell me straight if I'm reaching.

Also flag where I'm overreading. Which parts of this practice don't commit us to anything much and would look identical under a completely different account of why people want to be acknowledged? I don't want to be told the points wall is a metaphysical crime if really it's just a badly calibrated points wall.

And finish with what would actually have to change — concretely, like what would we do differently on a Monday morning — if we took seriously the idea that recognition only counts when it's freely given and comes from someone you regard as your equal rather than someone you have power over.
