I'm a junior and I've been going down a rabbit hole about whether "better than" has to be transitive, and I've talked myself into thinking it doesn't. Here's the case that did it for me.

I was ranking ways to spend a free Saturday. Call the best one A — the perfect day, everything you like, the right length. Now imagine A-minus, which is exactly the same except one little part of it is a hair less good. I'd take A over A-minus, but barely, and if you offered me A-minus plus an extra hour of free time I'd take that. Then do it again: each step gives up a tiny sliver of quality and picks up a bit of length, and at every single step I honestly think the newer option is better than the one before. But after fifty steps I'm at a long, mostly-boring day, and I would take A over that in a heartbeat. So either I'm contradicting myself somewhere in the middle, or "better than" just doesn't chain. I went with the second one.
