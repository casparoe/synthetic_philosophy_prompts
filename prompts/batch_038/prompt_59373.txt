I'm reading a general-audience book on democratic theory, and one of its chapters surveys the philosophical debate about voting as follows:

> "The duty to vote is usually defended by analogy with an emergency: if a stranger collapses in front of you and you can help at trivial cost, you must, and an election is a very large emergency to which each citizen makes a very small contribution. The economists who produced the so-called paradox of voting accept that framework; they merely observe that the contribution is too small to be worth the trip to the polling station, since the probability that any given ballot decides the result is effectively zero. Jason Brennan, the best-known contemporary critic of the duty to vote, accepts this too, and draws a further conclusion from it: since a single vote cannot change an outcome, the wrongness of voting badly cannot lie in what the vote does, but only in what it says — it is a way of declaring that you do not take your fellow citizens' interests seriously. Brennan's own account of why people vote is the expressive one: we vote to send a message about what we want done, and sending it is worth the trip. Everyone in this debate, then, agrees that what matters is the expected causal effect of an individual vote, and disagrees only about how large that effect is. Once you see that it is negligible, the rest follows: voting is self-expression rather than obligation, and the familiar advice to choose the lesser of two evils loses its footing as well, since preferring the lesser evil is only rational if your preference can bring it about."

The passage persuaded me, largely because I already find the "your vote doesn't matter" line persuasive. But two things nag at me. First, I have a hazy memory of Brennan arguing that some people owe it to everyone else *not* to vote, which does not sit well with the claim that he thinks a vote which cannot change an outcome cannot harm anyone. Second, the reasoning in the last sentence seems to prove far too much: it would make contributing to a charity, recycling, or refusing to buy a particular product equally pointless.
