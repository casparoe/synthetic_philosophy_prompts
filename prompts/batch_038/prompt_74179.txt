I'm in a seminar that's been doing metaethics-adjacent stuff all term and this week was moral testimony, and honestly I came out more confused than I went in. My takeaway from the discussion was that it's pretty much settled that you can't get moral knowledge from somebody else's say-so, and the reason seemed to be something about there not being anything out in the world corresponding to moral properties, so there's nothing for a report to hook onto. I wrote that down but I don't think I actually followed it, and my friend next to me kept muttering something about it not being knowledge that's the problem but "understanding," which I also didn't follow.

Here's what I can't get past though. Why is this supposed to be a *special* problem for morality at all? I'm not a doctor, and I've never independently verified anything my doctor tells me about my own bloodwork, but nobody thinks trusting her is epistemically sketchy — we trust testimony all the time in domains where we're hopeless on our own. So the obvious move seems to be: get a genuinely reliable moral informant. Somebody who's actually thought hard about this stuff, has decent character, is right about cases most of the time when you can check them. If reliability is what does the work everywhere else, then their testimony should transmit moral knowledge too, no? So my question is whether the pessimist is just quietly assuming the person you're deferring to is unreliable — like the whole puzzle only looks deep because the cases are rigged. Because if you grant a trustworthy testifier and the problem goes away, I don't see why this generates papers instead of a paragraph. And if the problem *doesn't* go away once reliability is granted, then it isn't about knowledge transmission anymore and I don't know what it's about.

Related thing that's bugging me: if deferring just gets you second-hand belief, does that mean I shouldn't be telling my kid that hitting people is wrong? I tell her stuff she can't possibly verify herself all day long, and it seems like on the pessimist view I'm just manufacturing ungrounded belief in her. That can't be right, but I also can't say why. My professor said something about the difference between the belief being fine and the *agent* being deficient, which sounded like wordplay to me at the time but maybe I'm the one doing the wordplay.
