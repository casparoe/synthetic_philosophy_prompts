I'm teaching an upper-division seminar on Athenian democracy — 18 students, one 75-minute section, they've all read Thucydides Book 3 including the Mytilenean debate. I want to run an in-class simulation of the *second* assembly, the one where Athens walks back its decree to kill every adult male Mytilenian. Not a reenactment where they say the lines back at me: I want them to run into three features of the episode themselves, ideally before anyone names them.

- The reversal was a near thing. The vote should come out close, and close *structurally* — turned by a handful of people — not close because I stacked the roles. They should leave feeling that the atrocity got called off by an inch.
- The decision was already moving while they argued. The first trireme is at sea. So put a real, visible clock on the deliberation: time spent "gathering more information" or tabling it till next week has to cost them something concrete. Delaying should feel like a choice with a price, not a free escape hatch.
- The asymetry Diodotus points at: the man arguing against severity can be accused of having been paid off by the Lesbians or of being soft, while the man arguing for it is presumed sincere. Build that into the scoring so being effective on the moderate side carries a penalty the hardliners never pay. Make it a rule they discover, not something I announce up front.

One more constraint I care about a lot: Diodotus doesn't win on pity. He wins by arguing mass killing is strategically self-defeating because it takes away any besieged city's reason to surrender early. So in at least the first round, rule out mercy-and-justice appeals as scoring moves — only expediency arguments earn points. I want them to feel how strange it is that the humane outcome got delivered by a cold cost-benefit case.

Can you design the whole thing? I need: role cards or blocs that work for 18, rules for how speaking and voting actually run, a scoring scheme that doesn't need real money, a minute-by-minute timeline for the 75 minutes showing where the clock mechanism lives, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the room onto the three features above — including the fact that something like a thousand ringleaders were executed anyway and the walls came down, so the reversal wasnt a clean save. Then tell me honestly what will go wrong the first time I run it and how to steer, and which feature of the actual event the activity just can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself.
