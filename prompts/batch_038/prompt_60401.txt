Last weekend my flatmate and I burned about three hours arguing over whether doing an evil playthrough of an RPG is actually bad — he thinks you're rehearsing cruelty, I think it's obviously just play, neither of us budged — and somewhere around 2am I realised I'd stopped caring about that question, because the games themselves have already taken a position on it and nobody ever bothers to ask them what it is. I'm putting together a short piece for a games-and-philosophy meetup group I go to, and I want it to be about what the standard design conventions assume, not about whether players get corrupted. So I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit theory for me, as carefully as you can, treating the bundle of conventions below as a single object of interpretation.

Here's the bundle, all of it completely bog-standard in big narrative games. There's a morality meter — karma, reputation, light/dark side points, a paragon/renegade bar — that moves up and down with your choices, is visible to you as a number or a coloured bar, and gates at least one ending, so the world's verdict on you is a scalar you can read off your own HUD. The same game ships achievements or trophies for maxing out *both* ends, and the community norm is that "100%" means you got all of them, so the same player is expected to slaughter the town on one save and rescue everybody on another. Some characters are flagged essential and simply cannot die: you stab one, they grunt, drop to one knee, stand back up, dust themselves off, and the quest continues — but the unimportant ones do die, and plenty of them respawn, so the shopkeeper you murdered is behind the counter again next week saying the same three lines. Reloading a save to undo a bad moral choice isn't treated as cheating by anyone, not players, not reviewers, not the developers, and games that autosave aggressively specifically to stop you get reviewed as hostile design. A handful of games deliberately break that last rule — Undertale is the famous one, where the game keeps a flag for what you did even after you wipe the file and later endings are permanently altered, with characters calling you out for treating their world as something to experiment on and put back — and those get treated as clever subversions, which says something about what the default assumes. Meanwhile the emotional register is entirely sincere: strings, death scenes, companions who grieve, a final boss who is disappointed in you.

So: what does all that presuppose about the moral status of game characters? Does it presuppose that they're the sort of thing that can be wronged at all — otherwise why the meter, the gated endings, the disappointed boss — or is the meter tracking something else entirely, like the player's chosen playstyle, a reward schedule, the aesthetic coherence of a route? Which rival views does the bundle quietly rule out and which does it stay neutral between? I want you to be strict about separating what the practice actually requires from what merely fits it comfortably, because I suspect a lot of it is underdetermined; my guess is that the achievements-for-both-extremes convention alone could be licensed by at least three completely different theories of what's going on, and I want them laid out. Is the bundle internally consistent, or does the save-scumming tolerance plus the dual achievements contradict the meter's pretence of tracking something real about the character? And does the essential-NPC flag mean the morality here is engine-relative rather than world-relative — is that a flaw in the picture or a clue about what the picture really is? Finish with the counterfactuals: what would have to change mechanically, not just tonally, if the same game were built on the assumption that characters have welfare that killing permanently damages, and separately on the assumption that the only thing with moral weight is the player's own dispositions? I especially want to know whether those two redesigns would even be distinguishable from the outside, because if they aren't, that's a point about how much evidence the current design can be. sorry this is long, feel free to organise it however makes it clearest.
