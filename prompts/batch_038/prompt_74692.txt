I just got made tech lead on a thing that puts a learned controller inside a live datacenter — it owns cooling and power distribution — and my first deliverable is a written spec for what "low impact" is supposed to mean before anyone picks a formalism. Two complications. First, our do-nothing baseline is garbage: if the agent literally takes no action the hall overheats and we lose the building, so any measure that scores against inaction treats basically every intervention as a huge improvement and calls it all fine. Second, the people who have to sign off on flagged actions are facility ops, not ML people — they need to be able to look at a penalized action and understand *why*, and auditability doesn't seem to be on the usual lists even though I think it should be.

So: write the desiderata. What does an impact measure have to get right to count as adequate *for this deployment* — the cases it must handle, the distinctions it mustn't collapse (impact vs. just failing at the task, states vs. reachable futures), the failure modes it has to avoid — with the motivating case behind each one, and mark which are non-negiotiable versus which are just the preferences of one camp. Then tell me whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable; my suspicion is that value-neutrality and auditability can't both be had, and if so that's the part I most want spelled out. A short scorecard of the reachability-style measures and the ones that penalize losing the ability to achieve auxiliary goals is fine at the end, but keep the weight on the criteria.

Say how confident you are in the list as a whole, and single out the one desideratum you're least sure actually belongs on it.
