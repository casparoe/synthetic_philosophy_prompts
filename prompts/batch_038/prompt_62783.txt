Reading group on Hume's political essays and we hit a wall. One guy keeps insisting Hume already settled every modern trade question: specie flows out of the deficit country, its prices fall, exports come back, the balance repairs itself, so anyone wanting tariffs or a competitive devaluation just hasn't read Of the Balance of Trade. Fine, I said -- but then what does Hume make of the eurozone, where the adjustment can't happen through the currency at all and instead piles up as TARGET2 balances? Or of the US running deficits for decades while the dollar gets stronger? Or of QE, where bank reserves multiplied several times over and consumer prices basically didnt move -- his picture of money is water finding its level, and here the water just sat in the pipes. So take his three big claims (money as convention rather than real wealth, the self-correcting balance of trade, and his dread of a large funded public debt) and tell me which of them survive fiat money and which were quietly relying on gold actually moving across borders. And where do you draw the line between Hume predicting modern monetary policy and Hume being wrong in an interesting way?
