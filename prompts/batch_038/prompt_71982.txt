For my philosophy of mind seminar I'm assembling a set of test cases for Integrated Information Theory, and I'd like you to build one to specification. The system must satisfy all of the following. First, on IIT's own terms it comes out as a single, irreducibly integrated physical mechanism with high Φ — genuinely recurrent and mutually coupled, so that the usual deflations are unavailable: not a feedforward sensor array, not a set of independently operating modules, not an aggregate of already-conscious subjects, and not software running on general-purpose hardware, since what matters is the system's own causal power rather than what its activity is taken to model. Second, it is something most of us would flatly deny is conscious, and I want the denial to land as an intuition rather than as an argument. Third, no exotic physics and no civilisation-scale engineering: a device a well-funded laboratory could plausibly construct. Keep the description of the system to one paragraph, and do not let it report on its own experience in either direction, since reportability would be doing your persuading for you.

After the scenario: state which quantity you are claiming is high and why this particular architecture resists decomposition into independent parts; list the replies an IIT defender would actually make — biting the bullet, disputing the calculation, invoking exclusion and the right spatial or temporal grain, questioning whether the relevant causal power is there at all — and say for each whether it is available in your case; and finish by identifying the single feature that is doing the intuitive work and saying what the case collapses into if that feature is removed.
