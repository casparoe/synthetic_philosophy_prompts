My physio is a Nietzsche fan and drops "what doesn't kill you makes you stronger" on me about once a week — usually right after I've told her an exercise set off a flare, like surviving the flare is already proof the flare was training. Six months into this and I'm pretty sure the sentence is doing something slippery. Give me the menu of precise claims it could be making. What has to count as kill — immediate death, permanent damage, the end of the kind of life I had? Who's the you — this body, a person across a lifetime, a type of person, a whole population, and does the claim even survive being read statistically, given it only ever gets checked on the ones who came through? And what could stronger mean — more raw capacity, a higher threshold for what used to hurt, a change in what I want, or just "not weaker yet"? For each reading tell me whether its a platitude, a testable empirical claim, or a moral instruction wearing a lab coat, and which one if any would actually justify what she's doing to me.

Then bring in Nietzsche himself. The man wrote constantly about his own diet, digestion, climate, coffee, alcohol, convalescence, and about health as something you have to win rather than have. Which of your readings does that material support, does any of it flatly contradict the slogan, and where does his idea of great health fit in — if it fits at all.
