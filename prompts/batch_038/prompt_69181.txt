Development editor at a small university press, mostly history list — Atlantic slavery, empire, labour, gender. Two or three flagged passages a week come to me and the complaints runs in opposite directions on the same book: one reader says the author is judging 1780s people by 2025 standards, another says the author has laundered the atrocity with passive voice and euphemism. I can't send everything out to a specialist and I can't just side with whoever writes the angrier letter.

Write me a procedure I can run on a flagged paragraph in about fifteen minutes: questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, what edit follows from each finding, and the points where it stops being mine to decide and goes back to the author or to acquisitions because it's values rather than text. Ground it in what the historiography argument about presentism actually gives you, not in vibes. Two things I especially want kept apart, because readers never keep them apart: applying a concept the actors didn't have, blaming them for something they couldn't have known, and erasing the agency of the people it was done to. And it has to handle the 'that was simply the standard of their time' defence, which I'm fairly sure smuggles in a fake consensus, since there were contemporaries objecting to almost all of it — so tell me what evidence in the paragraph would settle whether the objection was available and thinkable.

Then run it on three paragraphs you make up that sound like what lands on my desk, and let at least one verdict be one I won't like. Finish with where the procedure will misfire and any two of its own criteria that pull against each other.
