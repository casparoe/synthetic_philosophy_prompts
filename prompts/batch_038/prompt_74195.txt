so, I cover science and ideas for a mid-size outlet — mostly the AI-policy beat, which is the beat where every press release contains the word "accountablity" and means very little by it. I'm working on a longer piece about how companies deploying autonomous systems talk about responsibility after something goes wrong, and I want a parody document to run alongside it (or honestly just to sharpen my own thinking about the shape of the discourse).

Here's what I'm after: a fake internal memo from a made-up company — something bland, "Meridian Mobility" — that has just had one of its level-4 robotaxis strike a pedestrian in a way that was nobody's fault and everybody's fault. The memo's job is to close out the responsibility question for the board and the insurer. Build it around a RACI chart: rows are the stages of the causal chain (the training corpus, the confidence threshold in the perception stack, the remote-assistance operator who was monitoring eleven vehicles at once, the counsel who signed off on the deployment envelope, the city that issued the permit, the pedestrian), columns are Responsible / Accountable / Consulted / Informed. Every cell gets filled in with total corporate sincerity, and by the end there is not one empty square in the table and not one blameworthy human being on the page.

The footnotes and headers do most of the philosophical work. Specifically:

- the memo has to be internally consistent about a distinction it doesn't realise it's collapsing — "accountable" in the RACI sense (the single throat to choke, a compliance function with an owner) versus accountable in the answerability sense (someone you can actually demand reasons from and who is an appropriate target of blame). Include one row where the vehicle itself gets nominated as Accountable, plus a straight-faced paragraph about the new "answerability interface" engineering has built so the car can be meaningfully sanctioned. Let the reader feel why that's deranged without anyone saying so.
- the control condition and foreseeability should be doing quiet work the whole way through: every human in the chart is cleared on the grounds that they couldn't have known or couldn't have intervened, and the memo treats this as an achievement — something like "full exoneration coverage across all identified contributors."
- the many-hands problem shows up as an actual engineering metric. Responsibility gets sharded across so many upstream contributors that each individual share falls below a "materiality threshold" and is written down like a rounding error.
- the human-in-the-loop whose real function is to absorb blame for a process they can't control should appear as a job title, with a salary band and a deadpan job description.
- the retribution gap: a short section noting, with mild irritation, that the prosecutor and the public keep insisting on a person, plus a recommended comms strategy.
- a definitions box near the top that uses "responsibility" in four incompatible senses across four consecutive bullets and never notices.

Tone: deadpan consultancy register, no winking, no asides, no nudging the reader toward the point. The irony has to stay legible purely from the content — the memo's own logic, followed honestly, keeps generating absurd results, and that should be enough. Roughly 800 words including the header block. No real company, no real incident, no real person, and please don't land on a moral or a reveal at the end — the last line should be a routine administrative instruction, like a deadline for countersignature.

One more thing: my editor is not a philosopher, so it needs to work on the surface as bureaucracy eating itself for someone who misses every technical point. Layer it. And if you can sneak in one line that would make an actual responsibility-gap person laugh out loud, do that.
