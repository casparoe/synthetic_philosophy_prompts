I am giving an invited colloquium in three weeks at a department whose centre of gravity is technical logic rather than metaphysics, and I want a realistic question-period prep sheet rather than a defence of my view.

Let me state the position precisely, because it is not a package you will find in the literature in this form. I call it minimal dialetheism, and it has three commitments.

First, gluts exist, but their class is fixed by a syntactic criterion. A sentence is eligible to be both true and false only if it is self-denying through negation, or through some operator that, like negation, does not license the detachment of an arbitrary further sentence: the Liar, Yablo-style sequences, and their kin. Self-reference through a conditional is excluded by stipulation. I adopt a formation restriction under which no sentence may be constructed that is, via the truth schema, equivalent to a conditional whose own antecedent is that sentence. My argument for the restriction is that negational self-denial is transparent — the content of such a sentence is exhausted by what it says about itself — whereas conditional self-reference is opaque, since its content can be unpacked only by iterating the schema, and each iteration yields a new conditional rather than a stable content. On that basis I claim the restriction tracks a genuine distinction and is not reverse-engineered from the result I want.

Second, apart from that restriction, my logic is fully classical. I keep unrestricted modus ponens and I keep contraction. What I surrender is ex contradictione quodlibet, and I surrender it locally: ECQ fails only for contradictory pairs both of whose members contain the truth predicate. Contradictions elsewhere — in arithmetic, in empirical theory, in law, in morality — still explode, and I regard that as the point of the view, since it means my dialetheism licenses no inconsistency whatever in ordinary reasoning. My argument here is that a glut in the truth predicate reflects a limit on what the language can express rather than anything about the world, so the ordinary evidential significance of a contradiction — its functioning as a signal that something has gone wrong upstream — is suspended exactly there and nowhere else.

Third, I reject the strategy of motivating dialetheism by way of the inclosure schema, precisely because that schema also delivers gluts for motion, for change, and for various normative conflicts. I want the Liar and none of what inclosure otherwise buys. I also claim that the strengthened Liar is handled by the same machinery, without any new device.

The audience will be the following. The two senior people are a model theorist who works on fixed-point and revision semantics for truth and thinks naive truth is something to be reconstructed with a well-behaved model — he will care above all about whether my view has any model, and about what happens at limit stages — and a proof theorist who works on substructural systems and holds that if you are going to give anything up, contraction is the honest thing to give up and ECQ is the dishonest one. There is a set theorist in the back who knows no philosophy and will treat all of this as word games unless it touches independence or large cardinals. Two graduate students are committed paracomplete theorists in the Kripke-Field line, they think gluts are gaps misdescribed, and they are fluent in the revenge literature and enjoy wielding it. One faculty member is a dialetheist of the Priestian stripe, sympathetic to gluts but hostile to my restriction, which she will read as a retreat to safety. The remainder are epistemologists and generalists who have heard of the Liar but not of LP, and who will ask the question in the plainest available terms.

Now the deliverable. Give me nine or ten objections this room will actually raise, ordered by the product of how likely each is to be asked and how much damage it does to the view. Phrase each the way that particular objector would phrase it, in their idiom and with their preoccupations visible — I do not want the popular version where someone shouts that anything follows, since everyone in that room already knows a dialetheist has to say something about explosion and will not be impressed by my saying it. For each objection: one line identifying who raises it and how likely they are to; the objection itself; a reply I can deliver in about two minutes, capped at roughly one hundred and fifty words; and then one or two sentences saying exactly where that reply is soft, since I would rather know now than in front of them. Mark which objections I should concede in part up front and which I should fight, and say what the concession should consist of. Then tell me the single reply that has to land for the view to survive the hour — the one without which everything else collapses into ad hocery. Finally, name the question I am hoping nobody asks, state it in the form it would take, and say why I have no good answer to it.

Two constraints. Do not give me references, a bibliography, or a list of further reading; I know the literature and citations would be of no use to me here. And do not write me a defence of minimal dialetheism in the abstract: I want the objections, in this room's voice, and what I say back.
