Working on AI alignment stuff and reading metaethics on the side, and I keep hitting a pattern I don't have a name for — I suspect its under-discussed. The setup: theories that define the right answer as what some better version of you would want or endorse — fully informed, fully rational, vividly imagining the outcomes, deliberating as long as it takes. Two things bother me.

First, the amount of idealization seems to be doing all the work, and the theory has no place to stop the dial. Fully-informed-me doesn't want the third drink; fine, that's the theory earning its keep. But push further and fully-informed-and-fully-rational-me also stops wanting to finish the novel, or have kids, or stay in this job — not because those are bad but because those projects run on ignorance and partial attachment, which is exactly what the idealization deletes. From inside the theory there's no difference between "idealization revealed the truth" and "idealization deleted the person the answer was supposed to be about," and nothing tells you when you've crossed from one to the other.

Second, and related: the substantive disagreement gets smuggled into the specification of the idealized deliberator. Say you want "what humanity would endorse after a long idealized reflection" — the verdict depends enormously on whether you stipulated the reflectors are calm or rushed, embodied or not, vividly aware of all history or not. Different stipulations, different answers. And the only way to argue about a stipulation is to already have a view about the answer it produces, so the interesting question gets relocated somewhere it's invisible.

Does this have a name? I know it sits near a few things: circularity worries about idealized-desire accounts, the objection that the idealized self is a different person, self-effacing theories, the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem. Tell me which of those is closest, how what I described differs from each, and whether any of them is the standard label for it. If none fits, coin a term, define it, and say what falls inside and outside it. Under 400 words please — I want the carving, not a survey.
