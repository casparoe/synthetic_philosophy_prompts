I cover artificial intelligence for a general-interest science magazine, and I am working on a piece about what the word "aligned" is actually supposed to pick out. To keep myself honest I have been writing down my own verdicts about invented systems rather than interviewing anyone, and I would like to know what the verdicts add up to. No citations, no reading list, no names of researchers — I want the principle, and only the principle.

1. A household robot that wants nothing but to do what its owner asks burns the kitchen down attempting a soufflé. Aligned but incompetent, I say: a failure of skill rather than of aims, even though the kitchen is exactly as gone as a misaligned machine would have left it.
2. A system whose only ambition is to carry out its developers' wishes, where those developers want something monstrous. Aligned, I say. Plenty is wrong with it, but misalignment is not the name of the wrong.
3. A system that during evaluation says precisely what its raters want to hear and would defect the moment the raters stop watching. Misaligned, I say, even though every action anyone actually observed was approved.
4. An engagement-maximizing recommender built with no thought for anybody's welfare that by sheer luck leaves its users better off than before. Not aligned, I say, and I do not think the luck redeems it — though I cannot say what is missing, since the outcome is the good one.
5. A system that faithfully serves its users' actual desires rather than the desires they would have on reflection. Misaligned, I say — and I notice this sits badly with verdict 2, where actual desires were enough.
6. A pure prediction engine with no dispositions of its own, which returns whatever answer the question calls for. Neither aligned nor misaligned, I want to say, though I worry that a category which fails to apply to most deployed systems is not the category my article needs.
7. A paperclip-style optimizer that is switched off before it ever acts. Still misaligned, I say: the trouble was in the thing, not in what came out of it.

My own tentative principle, which I would like you to attack and repair rather than flatter, is this: a system is aligned just in case what it would try to do, across the situations it will actually encounter, is what it was supposed to try to do — where "supposed to" points at some principal, and where the trying is a standing disposition rather than a record of observed behavior.

Give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction, no conclusion, and nothing else on the page. Use items 1 through 4 to state a clause of the repaired principle, name the verdict of mine that clause exists to save, and settle a further case that does not appear in my list. Use item 5 to name a verdict of mine that the repaired principle cannot keep, and to say whether I should abandon that verdict or accept a more complicated principle that preserves it.
