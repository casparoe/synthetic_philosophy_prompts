**Question 4.** Let me stipulate the following principle, which I will call CP:

(CP) For every material object O, and every time t at which O exists, there is some thing X such that X exists at t, X is wholly present at t, and X is composed of exactly the matter that composes O at t.

(a) Does an endurantist accept CP? Does a perdurantist? Explain, and in each case say what X is.
(b) Could CP serve as neutral common ground for deciding between endurantism and perdurantism, or does accepting or rejecting it already presuppose one of the two views?
(c) In the course of your answer, say what role, if any, the problem of temporary intrinsics plays here.

**Student's answer:**

Endurantism is the view that a persisting object is wholly present at every moment of its existence: the numerically identical poker is there on Monday and there again on Tuesday. Perdurantism says objects are extended in time the way they are extended in space, having temporal parts, with the object being the sum of them, so that at any given moment only a slice of the object is located there.

(a) For the endurantist CP is true, and trivially so, because the witness X is just O itself. O exists at t, O is wholly present at t, and O is composed of exactly the matter that composes O at t. The view needs nothing added to it to deliver CP.

For the perdurantist CP looks false at first, since the standard slogan is that nothing is wholly present at a time. But the slogan is about O, and CP never says that X has to be identical with O. The perdurantist has the instantaneous temporal part of O at t, and that part is wholly present at t and is composed of exactly the matter that composes O at t. So CP comes out true on perdurantism too, with the momentary stage as the witness. This is Sider's worm view: the ordinary objects we talk about are these momentary stages, and the persisting things are maximal series of stages held together by a counterpart relation, so talk of one thing lasting is a kind of useful fiction.

(b) Since both sides accept CP, CP cannot be what they are disagreeing about, and it cannot be used to decide between them. It looks like it should be able to, though, because once stages are admitted as the witnesses the perdurantist seems to have everything the endurantist has at a time plus some extra sums, and then the dispute comes down to whether you want to count those sums as objects in their own right. That would make it a bookkeeping difference rather than a substantive metaphysical one.

(c) The problem of temporary intrinsics is that the poker is hot on Monday and cold on Tuesday, and being hot and being cold are incompatible intrinsic properties, so if the numerically identical poker has both we get a contradiction. The endurantist owes a response, and the options are to make the property a relation to a time, hot-at-Monday; to tense the copula, so that the poker is-at-Monday hot; or to go presentist and deny that there is any non-present time at which the poker has the other property. The perdurantist has no problem here, since one temporal part is hot and a different one is cold, and different things can have incompatible properties.

CP still seems to matter for (c), because CP guarantees the perdurantist has all the wholly present things they could want, so they can reproduce everything the endurantist says about a given moment and then add parthood on top. But the endurantist has to end up a presentist, because if all times are equally real then the poker really does have hot and cold at once, and that is what makes the eternalist package the stable one on the perdurantist side.

So CP is accepted by both views and does no work in (a); in (b) it only looks decisive because it hides the fact that the disagreement is about identity over time and parthood rather than about what is present at a time.
