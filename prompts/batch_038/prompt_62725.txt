Ok so I moderate a fairly big gaming discord (40k-ish people) and a few weeks back we had a blowup about our slur policy — specifically whether the bot should just silently delete the word, or whether mods should also publicly call out the person who used it. One of the older mods, who's way sharper than me about this stuff, made an argument for why the public callouts are counterproductive, and I wrote it down because I kept finding it convincing even though I hate where it lands. Here's roughly how it goes:

(1) What a word means comes from how it's used, not from anything inside the word itself.
(2) A slur and its neutral counterpart pick out exactly the same people, so the slur adds no descriptive content — nothing you could write down as what it says about them.
(3) So everything the slur has that the neutral word lacks (the sting, the shock, the fact that saying it *does* something) has to come from the social prohibition on saying it. The taboo is what powers the word.
(4) Forbidding a thing is precisely what makes it worth doing as a signal — a transgression nobody disapproves of isn't a transgression and costs you nothing to perform.
(5) So every public condemnation of a slur — bot messages, mod callouts, HR trainings, the whole apparatus — raises the payoff of using one, and therefore produces more slur-use than it prevents.
(6) Therefore the campaign against slurs is self-defeating, and the way to actually drain them is to drop the taboo, let people say them freely, and wait for them to go as boring as their neutral counterparts.

I think (6) is awful both morally and as a prediction, and I've tried saying that, but people just go "okay so which premise?" and I don't have an answer. Half-formed worries I can't get traction on: (4) sounds like it's describing teenage edgelords and not most of the people who actually use these words, but I don't know if that's a real objection or just me not liking it. Or maybe (1) is too strong — a friend of mine says "meaning is use" is a slogan and not a premise. And my sister pointed out that "queer" got reclaimed and now alot of people say it with zero sting attached, which feels relevant but I can't figure out where to slot it in. There's also something queasy about (5) that I can't name: if condemning a thing makes people do more of it, that reasoning would seem to apply to condemning anything, which can't be right, but I don't know whether that shows a premise is false or just that I've stated the argument badly.

So: which step is the bad one? Walk me through exactly where the slide happens rather than just pronouncing it invalid, because I want to be able to explain this to the other mods at the next meeting. And whatever you reject, price it for me — (2) and (3) feel like the parts that people who study this stuff for a living mostly agree on, so if the flaw lives in there I want to know what I'd be giving up and what the alternatives cost. I gather some people think slurs really do have descriptive content after all, and some think they don't say anything at all but just express contempt, and there's something about whether you can even mention one of these words without using it, which seems like it should matter to (5) somehow. Which diagnosis survives contact with the reclamation case, and which ones don't?
