I'm trying to work out what I actually believe about taking other people's word on whether something is good, since I've started writing a small monthly arts column and half of what I cover I haven't got time to experience properly. Here are my verdicts so far, going by gut.

A mate whose film judgement I really rate tells me some obscure Romanian picture is superb. I'm not entilted to write that it's superb; at most I can say he thinks it is. Same mate tells me the last twenty minutes drag and there's a twist near the end — I'll repeat that flat out, no hedging at all. A wine critic I've never met tells me a bottle is worth the forty quid: I buy it, and I'll tell people it's worth it. My sister, who cooks far better than me, says a soup is perfectly seasoned while I've got a cold and can't taste anything: I accept that too. A translator friend says a Persian love poem is devastating in the original, and I'll say it's devastating, full stop.

Now the awkward one. A critic I trust tells me an out-of-print novel is magnificent. I'm happy to say so, I put it to my book group and defended the recomendation when someone objected. Then the novel gets reprinted, I could read it in a weekend, and suddenly I won't say it's magnificent on his word any more. So whether I may assert seems to depend on whether the library happens to have it in stock, which can't be right — the book hasn't changed, and my behaviour didn't change either, I still stand by the recommendation.

My working rule: testimony about aesthetic value is never a reason to assert, only a reason to go and look — except where the relevant features are simply inaccessible to me, in which case I may borrow the verdict of someone who'd notice roughly the same things I would. Does that rule actually deliver the verdicts above? Tell me which one or two it forces me to give up, and whether a simpler rule that overturns more of them would be better. Then say what it predicts for two cases I haven't listed: someone with much sharper ears than mine but completely different values telling me a piece I find moving is kitsch; and my partner saying a song is harmonically lazy, after which I genuinely start hearing it that way — is that verdict his or mine now?

Please answer in continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold or tables — just how you'd write an email.
