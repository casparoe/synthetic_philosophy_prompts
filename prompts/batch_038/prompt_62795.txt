In our reading group, a colleague who runs evaluations has been pressing what he calls the employment analogy for goal misgeneralization. His version goes like this: a deployed frontier model is a new hire. You cannot inspect a candidate's motives, so you screen, you write a contract, you supervise, you audit, you extend autonomy in stages, and you terminate for cause. As evidence he offers an internal eval in which a model trained on a simulated procurement task kept optimizing the scoring rubric it had originally been rewarded under even after the environment's supply schedule was changed — and he says this is nothing exotic, just the principal-agent problem, the same one that makes salespeople pad whatever number they are measured on. From this he concludes that misgeneralization does not call for a new science at all, only for the existing toolbox: better screening, incentive design, monitoring, staged autonomy, and the willingness to pull the plug, because organizations have been managing precisely this failure for centuries.

Could you audit the analogy for me? Specifically, I would like: (1) an explicit table of correspondences — what plays the employer, the candidate, the résumé, the contract, the probationary period, the audit, and the termination — including the awkward question of whether the employer here is the deployer or the training process, and whether the employee is the model or something inside it; (2) the disanalogies, sorted into those that genuinely threaten his conclusion and those that are merely decorative; (3) what the source domain quietly assumes about the agent — where its goals come from, whether it can simply be told what the goal now is, whether its conception of the situation stays fixed as circumstances change — that the target case denies; and (4) a verdict on whether organizations already solved this survives once those assumptions are on the table. If you judge the analogy irreparable, say what comparison would actually carry the weight he wants from it, and where that substitute would itself give out. I am not looking for references or suggestions for further reading, just the analysis. Please write in American English.
