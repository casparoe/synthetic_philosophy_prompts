I keep going back and forth on the truth debate — deflationists vs. people who think truth is a substantive property — and I can't tell whats actually being disagreed about. The deflationist line is that the whole job of 'true' is discharged by the equivalence schema plus its use in generalizing (everything he said was true), and that anything further is either empty or confused. The substantivist replies that this leaves out truth's normative role — belief aims at truth, assertion is answerable to it — and its explanatory work, e.g. why the theories that survive and predict well are the true ones. Each side seems to think the other is talking nonsense, and nobody moves.

My hypothesis, which I want you to attack or refine rather than just endorse: there are two arguments tangled together here, one about the concept or meaning of the predicate and one about whether truth is a metaphysically heavyweight property, and most of the stalemate comes from each side winning one and claiming to have won the other. Is that right? If not, where's the real fault line — is the dispute verbal, is there a hidden metasemantic or empirical question doing the work, are these just incompatible intuitions about what a property has to do to be interesting, or is the question ill-posed? Ground the diagnosis in what each side actually says when it's pressing the other, and then tell me what would count as progress: a distinction, a new form of argument, some case that would break the tie — or an argument that no progress should be expected here and why.
