I sit in on a reading group at my uni, mixed departments, no philosophy background required, and last week we did the chapter on the four kinds of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian. Two of the regulars, Nadia (computational neuroscience) and Emre (history of science), got into a fight about how you actually sort concrete cases into the boxes, and it went like five rounds and I honestly still can't tell who landed where correctly. I'm writing it out because I'd like a proper verdict on each step rather than a general essay.

(1) Nadia opened with three test cases: a robot vacuum that bumps into stuff and reverses; a warehouse robot that carries an internal map and simulates candidate routes, picking one before moving; and a game-playing agent trained purely by reinforcement learning over millions of episodes, with no explicit world model anywhere in the code. She said the first is clearly Skinnerian, the second clearly Popperian, and the third is Skinnerian too, since it's operant conditioning, which is the same mechanism the category was built around. (2) Emre said she had the third one exactly backwards: an RL agent carries a learned value function, and a value function is a compressed model of the future consequences of actions, so the agent ranks internally represented options before acting — that's the whole Popperian trick, letting hypotheses die instead of you. He said calling it Skinnerian just because the algorithm is named after a conditioning paradigm confuses the genealogy of the code with the architecture it produces. (3) Nadia said that criterion would empty the Skinnerian box entirely, since even a pigeon's learned association encodes something about consequences, and then the ladder has no second rung; her counter-proposal was that the real line is who supplies the selectivity — the environment's reinforcement schedule in a Skinnerian creature, the creature's own internal simulation in a Popperian one. (4) Emre conceded that formulation was better than his, and that on it the RL agent does look Skinnerian after all, since the selectivity that shaped its value function came from the reward signal rather than from anything the agent represented — but then he said this shows the boxes aren't a ladder at all, they're separate dimensions describing where design work is stored (genes, individual reinforcement history, internal models, externally inherited tools), and one system can be high on one and low on another; his agent is Skinnerian in training and Popperian at deployment. So all these placement fights come from drawing a staircase where there are axes. (5) Nadia said if its not cumulative the point evaporates — the story is about layers of design work piling on top of each other, each one letting a creature do something the previous kind couldn't, and the layers are nested, a Gregorian creature is still Popperian underneath. (6) Emre said nesting doesn't need a ladder, only that later layers presuppose earlier ones, the way a high-level language presupposes an assembler without there being a ranked order of kinds of programs. (7) Then Nadia threw in a large language model as the tiebreaker: it's built out of harvested human artifacts — words, notation, diagrams, tools designed by other minds — which is the Gregorian description almost word for word. Emre called that a category error: the model doesn't *use* the tools, it was manufactured *from* them by people who did; the Gregorian move is importing design work at runtime by reading and manipulating external artifacts, and the LLM's import happened once, during training, so at best it's a frozen deposit of Gregorian design that behaves Skinnerian-to-Popperian when you query it — which for him proves the taxonomy is missing a slot for artifacts that inherit cultural design without having a culture.

So: can you go through moves 1-7 and say who's right at each one, not just who wins overall? Specifically I want to know whether Emre's value-function point or Nadia's who-supplies-the-selectivity point is the better line between Skinnerian and Popperian, or whether both mislocate it; whether ladder or axes is the better reading and what actually turns on the difference; and where an LLM sits once you've settled the criterion. Also, both of them waved at the Gregorian box like it was unproblematic and neither one said a single thing about Darwinian creatures — tell me if that silence is hiding a real difficulty.
