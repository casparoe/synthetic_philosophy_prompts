Here's the list:

1. When I'm looking at a trait my first move is to build an optimality model, and I do think a good fit between the trait and some task is evidence that selection shaped the trait for that task. Not proof, but evidence.
2. I also think Gould and Lewontin are right in substance: current fit can't tell you design history, and architecture and development do a lot of the work adaptationists hand over to selection.
3. I won't call something a spandrel unless I can name the specific thing it's an unavoidable byproduct of. "Nobody can figure out what it's for" is not an explanation and I get irritated when people use it that way.
4. But when the adaptive story for a trait is unpersuasive, my default working position is that it's a spandrel.
5. I think most of what people in my field call spandrels are really exaptations, because the structures usually get modified once they're there, and I correct people on this constantly.
6. Drift and phylogenetic inertia are seperate non-adaptive explanations from spandrels and shouldn't be filed under the same heading.
7. Shared developmental machinery counts as evidence for byproduct status — if the same pathway builds the feature in every species that has it, that's a reason to think it's coming along for the ride.
