I'm a novelist, and I've got a book stuck. It's about a woman who signs an advance directive and then, nine years later, ends up on the other side of it — same person, fully competent, remembers signing, and wants the exact opposite of what she wrote. The family, the hospital, the paperwork, I've got all that. What I don't have is a case that holds up. Every draft I've done gets dissolved by somebody in the room saying "well, she can just revoke it" or "honestly, that's not her anymore," and I can't argue with them, because they're right.

So build me the case to spec. I don't want a survey of the debate, I know roughly what the two camps say. I want one scenario, engineered so the standard moves can't get any purchase.

The constraints:

1. Weld the identity escape hatch shut. The signer and the woman in the bed are, on any psychological-continuity account you care to apply, uncontroversiously the same person. She remembers signing it. She can read it aloud and explain her own reasoning better than her daughter can. Both ends competent.
2. She signed it *after having been inside the state*. Give me some temporary, reversible, medically supervised induction of the very condition she's legislating about — call it six hours, she comes out of it intact, she signs within a day. And it has to be on the face of the document, in her handwriting, so nobody can say she was guessing what it would be like. She wasn't guessing. That's the point.
3. The state she's in now is the same state, only permanent — and permanent turns out not to be six hours long. Tell me precisely what the difference is, and it has to be a difference in what matters, not just a difference in how it feels.
4. She is not fooling herself, and she has not gone cheerfully sour-grapes. She's clear-eyed: she says she knows what she wrote and why, and she wants it overridden anyway. If the case only works because the later self is deluded, or numb, or too tired to fight, it's no use to me.
5. Irreversable, with a deadline, and neither "just revoke it" nor "wait and see how she feels" is available. One sentence each for why not — and keep the reasons practical. A machine, a surgical window, a court order, a person who has to answer by morning. Not metaphysical reasons.
6. I'll allow you exactly one invented thing, which I assume is the induction in (2). Flag it, tell me how far off it is, and tell me whether the case survives if I swap it for something a hospital could do next Tuesday.

Verdicts I need the case to actually produce, not assert:

- The precedent-autonomy line — the earlier self legislated under better conditions, and some interests survive no longer caring about them — has to come out: honor the document.
- The present-interests line — all that's left is what she wants now, the rest is a dead woman's handwriting — has to come out: override.
- And a third view, the one that treats a life as a story whose later chapters get their meaning from the earlier ones, has to land on the same side as one of them but for a reason that would mortify its new ally. This is the one I want most, because it's the only one my character could say out loud in a hospital corridor without knowing she's doing philosophy.

Which means: the case has to generate the verdicts. If I catch a character thinking a thought no human being has ever thought while standing at the foot of a bed, start over.

Then the honest part — which feature is doing the load-bearing, what collapses if I remove it (I will remove it, if the book is better without it), how the losing side attacks the case, and where you fudged. And give me one line of dialogue, one, from anybody in the room, that does the philosophical work without anyone saying a word like "autonomy."

The case itself in about 400 words: one room, two or three people, a piece of paper. Analysis as long as it needs to be.

After that, a valid JSON object (no comments, no trailing commas) summarizing it, exactly these fields:

- `case.setting` — one line, where and when
- `case.condition` — what she has, in plain words
- `case.induction` — what was done to her beforehand, and what it cost her to find out
- `case.directive_text` — verbatim, 60 words max, in her voice; this goes straight into the novel
- `case.deadline` — what has to be decided, by when
- `case.why_not_revoke` — one sentence
- `case.why_not_wait` — one sentence
- `invented_element.what`, `invented_element.how_far_off` (today / 5 years / 20 years), `invented_element.survives_swap` (true/false), `invented_element.the_swap`
- `verdicts` — array of three objects: `view`, `says` ("honor" or "override"), `because` (40 words max), and for the third view only, `reason_its_ally_would_hate`
- `load_bearing_feature`
- `if_removed`
- `losing_side_best_attack`
- `where_i_fudged`
- `the_line.speaker`, `the_line.text`
