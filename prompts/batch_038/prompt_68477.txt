ok, weird request but here goes. Pretend there's an app that treats religious belief as a bundle of propositions you hold at various credences. It tracks them, it nudges you to update when new evidence comes in, it emails you a weekly calibration report (your Brier score on the Resurrection ticked up 0.03, nice!), and the premium tier is basically a Pascal's wager calculator with a subscription.

Write me the app store listing, then five or six customer support tickets with the dev replies, then a "known issues" changelog at the bottom. 800 words give or take.

The engine of the joke should be that the engineers keep filing the same unfixable bug: some users' numbers simply will not move, no matter what evidence is shown or which nudges fire. Not because the app is broken — because those users were never doing the thing the app assumes they're doing. I want the Wittgensteinian point (religious belief isn't a shaky hypothesis about how the world will turn out, its more like a picture that organizes a whole life, and "I might be wrong" just isn't a move available inside it) to come out entirely through the tickets, never stated. So: the guy who's been in the hospital six weeks and still refuses to say "possibly" when the app prompts him to hedge; the woman who cancels because it keeps asking her to bet on something she doesn't think is the kind of thing you bet on; the user who insists he isn't predicting anything and asks them to stop sending him forecasts. And include one ticket where a number *does* move and the devs celebrate, but it turns out the update was the wrong description of what happened — the guy just stopped going to church and stopped praying, which isn't a credence revision at all.

The devs shouldn't be strawmen. They're smart, sincere, fluent in the right jargon, and when the ticket gets escalated to a "belief systems" engineer he proposes a fix that's genuinely worse than the bug. Deadpan all the way through, no winking, no authorial asides, and don't explain the philosophy anywhere.

The target is the discourse that turns faith into a probability you can be nudged about — not religion, not atheists — so ideally a believer and a Bayesian both finish it feeling a little skewred. Don't quote any real text and don't put words in a real philosopher's mouth; a user name-dropping Wittgenstein in a ticket is fine and probably funny. American English please.
