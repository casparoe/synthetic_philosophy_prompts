Writing a novel where Maimonides gets hauled out of 12th century Cairo and dropped into a Jerusalem tech demo day, where he has to react to two pitches: a startup selling vat-grown meat they insist is fully kosher, and a group fundraising to restore animal sacrifice on the Temple Mount. My beta readers all say the scene writes itself — Maimonides read the whole sacrificial cult as a temporary concession to wean Israel off idolatry, so obviously he'd wave the lab meat through and tell the Temple crowd to go home. Is that actually where he'd land? Give me what he'd argue on each pitch, in his own reasoning rather than a generic wise-old-man speech, and tell me which of the two would make him more uncomforable — and whether he'd think anything at all about the natural order or the commandments changes in the messianic age.
