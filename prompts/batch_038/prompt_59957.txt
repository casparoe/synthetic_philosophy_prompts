I work on backend infrastructure and have no formal training in philosophy, so please explain any technical vocabulary you need along the way. Our platform writes every state change to an append-only log that no service can edit or delete, and the log is complete in the sense that nothing happening inside the system escapes it. While reading about Fitch's paradox of knowability — the argument that if every truth is knowable, then every truth is already known, since the claim that some particular truth is never known by anyone would itself have to be knowable — I noticed that nearly every proposed repair narrows the knowability principle to some restricted class of propositions. I'd like a rigorous defense of the other option instead: accepting the conclusion, on the grounds that a complete, tamper-proof, machine-readable record of everything that happens is itself a knower, so that no truth within its domain is ever unknown. Please build the strongest case you can for that position, then spell out what it commits one to about the nature of knowledge — in particular whether such a record can know truths about itself, and whether knowability preserved in this way still says anything a verificationist should want it to say.
