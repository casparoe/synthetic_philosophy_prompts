I'm 71, retired last spring after 34 years in freshwater ecology, and I've fallen into philosophy headfirst since — mostly ethics, mostly badly, I'll admit. I've also somehow ended up chairing the field-standards board of an international conservation trust: about 40 monitoring stations in 11 countries, all working under one global handbook written mostly by people at HQ. We vote in three weeks and I want to circulate a memo, but I'd rather see how someone else frames it first, so please draft it for me, in my voice, and be blunt where my own proposal is weak. If you land against it, say so.

Two incidents last year, both about the same clause, which requires any permit irregularity at a station to be escalated to the national ministry within ten days.

Station 1 is in a country about as tight as they come — rules get followed, deviation gets punished socially and legally. A junior staffer's paperwork for a community co-managed wetland was three weeks out of date. The station escalated exactly as written. The ministry opened an audit and the community's ecotourism concession — their income, and the whole reason the co-management deal existed — was suspended for eleven months. Nobody at the station saw that coming, and several now say they wouldn't have escalated if they'd had any alternative.

Station 2 is in a much looser setting. Same clause, same species of irregularity. The manager sat on it, got the paperwork fixed locally, told nobody, and that station now has the best community relationships in our network. We found out fourteen months later only because someone left the organisation and mentioned it in an exit conversation.

The director's proposal: one handbook, zero local variation, plus mandatory reporting of every near-miss. Mine is different and I want it argued for, not just described. I'd flag a subset of the handbook as locally calibratable — consent, safety, data integrity and money stay untouchable — and let stations deviate from the rest up to a per-year cap, provided every deviation goes into an open register within 72 hours with the reason and the expected cost of being wrong. Deviations lapse after 18 months unless the board reauthorises them. My hunch is that what actually went wrong at Station 2 wasnt the deviation, it was the invisibility.

Here's the objection I can't answer. One board member, a labour lawyer, says a rule you're permitted to break isn't a rule, it's a suggestion with paperwork; and worse, calibrating to local norms just imports whatever those norms already punish — in a tight place the person whose name ends up on a logged deviation is the junior local staffer, not the expat manager, so my flexibility lands on whoever has least cover. I think she's onto something and I don't want the memo swatting her away.

What I need: something the board can vote on — approve the director's version, approve mine, approve mine with named changes, or reject both — with the bottom line up front. It should give a criterion for what belongs in the untouchable core and say why that criterion rather than another obvious one; a pilot with dates and a stated endpoint; an explicit line on what evidence during the pilot would make me drop my own proposal, since I spent decades arguing about what counts as a fair comparison and I don't want anything uncheckable; and a paragraph on what we do about Station 2's manager now, given the conduct predates any policy we adopt. And please don't lean on tight/loose labels as if naming a culture explained something — the reasoning has to work for a member who's never heard the phrase. Two pages max, plain English, no jargon. These are ecologists and lawyers, and until about eighteen months ago, so was I.
