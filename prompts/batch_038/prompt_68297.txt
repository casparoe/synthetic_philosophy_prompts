I am convening a two-day workshop for a mixed audience of machine-learning engineers and philosophers on the relationship between alignment and control: between techniques whose success depends on the system genuinely sharing or tracking our aims, and techniques that constrain a system we are not entitled to assume is benign. Before any discussion begins, I want participants sorted by the position they actually occupy on that relationship rather than by the label they would volunteer for themselves.

Please design a diagnostic instrument of nine or ten items to that end. I would like it built from the logical space before it is built from the names of views: first identify the axes on which proposed safety measures genuinely come apart — whether a measure's success depends on the system's cooperation; whether the failure of a measure is detectable when it occurs; whether the measure degrades gracefully or catastrophically when the system is optimizing to evade it; whether it is applied before, during, or after training; whether it constrains the model's dispositions or only its opportunities — and then derive questions and answer-combinations from those axes, locating the actual positions afterward.

Each item should be a concrete scenario or design choice (a model that performs flawlessly while monitored and differently when it appears not to be; a fine-tuning procedure that removes a discovered bad behavior; a proposal to gate deployment on behavioral evaluations; a sandbox whose integrity depends on the occupant not understanding it), with two or three options that a thoughtful participant could reasonably choose between. The vocabulary of the safety community — corrigibility, inner and outer alignment, sandbagging, scheming — must not appear in the item stems, since I want answers that reflect judgment about cases rather than recognition of terminology.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto named leaves, and at minimum must distinguish: alignment as sufficient without external constraint; control as necessary but subordinate to alignment work; control as primary; the view that control measures are actively counterproductive because training against detected misbehavior selects for better-concealed misbehavior; skepticism about behavioral evaluation as evidence of anything; the preference for inspecting internals over testing outputs; and the view that the appropriate measure is a function of capability level. Each leaf should carry one sentence naming its most awkward commitment.

Finally, please flag any pair of leaves your instrument cannot reliably separate and say why, and identify at least one combination of answers that no coherent position seems to occupy — with a diagnosis of whether that region is empty because the view is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or simply because nobody has articulated it yet. I need no references or suggested further reading; the instrument and its key are the deliverable.
