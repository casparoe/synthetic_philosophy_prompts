I'm a staff analyst at a state gaming commission. We see maybe six filings a year for games where the top prize is effectively open-ended — uncapped progressive jackpots, payouts that scale with tickets sold, that kind of thing — and our current review only checks that the published odds are accurate, which is no help when the expected payout per ticket doesn't converge to a finite number at all. One of my colleagues keeps bringing up the St. Petersburg game as the reason to ban the whole category, and the applicants keep answering that real players have finite bankrolls so it doesn't apply, and honestly I can't tell which of them is abusing the math.

Write a review procedure my office can run on any filing of this type: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure lands on reject, and where it hands the call up to the five commissioners because the question is about what we're willing to let people be exposed to rather than about arithmetic. We have no economists on staff and roughly 20 working days per filing, so anything requiring a fitted utility curve or a house model of player psychology is out.

Then run it on two cases: an uncapped progressive jackpot on a $2 ticket, and our own existing state lottery game, which I suspect won't come out of the procedure any better then the filing does. Be straight with me about which steps are judgment calls wearing a procedural costume, and about what the procedure can't settle at all.
