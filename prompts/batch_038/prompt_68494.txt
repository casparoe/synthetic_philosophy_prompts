I'm in a seminar this term that's basically just Gödel, Escher, Bach start to finish, and we're on the part about where a message actually lives — the frame message vs the outer message vs the inner message, and the bit that convinced me, which is that the split between them is arbitrary because you can always widen the frame until the old frame turns into content. My prof did the vinyl example in section: the groove pattern is the inner message, the label on the record tells you how to take it, and the sheer fact that somebody handed you a record at all is doing work that neither of those two do.

The creative option for the assignment is to write a story that stages that idea without letting anyone in the story explain the idea. I've got a seed from my day job (I work in registration at a small regional museum) and I want to see how someone else would handle it before I write my own version, so please actually write the story rather than pitch it back to me.

Seed: during a rehang, a preparator swaps two wall labels between a sour 1870s portrait of a local mill owner and a big cheerful postwar abstraction. Nobody catches it for two weeks. A docent gives a tour reading the wrong label and her reading is so good a visitor quotes it in a letter to the local paper. Meanwhile the only place the original pairing still exists is the object record in our collections database, and the gallery itself is named after the donor family whose name is stencilled on the wall by the door. Three people in the building each think the meaning is somewhere different: the preparator thinks it's in the paint, the development officer thinks it's in the wall text and the donor wall, and the registrar — who narrates — thinks it's in the number, because an object without an accession number is just furniture. I'd like the plot to move the meaning between those levels at least twice, and I'd like the ending to land somewhere none of the three argue for.

Constraints, mostly about not letting it turn into an essay with characters:

- about 1500-1900 words, first person, give it a title
- nobody says the words frame message or outer message, nobody mentions the book, nobody gives a speech
- keep it physically concrete: the smell of a crate, Tyvek, the barrier coat you lay down before you write a number on an object, a #2 pencil
- include one object where the accession number was written directly onto the work decades ago in a way that can't be reversed without damaging it, so the number has literally become part of the thing itself
- no romance subplot, and the registrar should not come out as the hero

Then afterwards, in three or four sentences only, tell me which level the story finally leaves the meaning sitting at and which level got demoted along the way — my prof will ask me that and I want to see if we read it the same.
