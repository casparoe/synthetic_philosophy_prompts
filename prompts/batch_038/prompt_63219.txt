I'm an observational astronomer, and a philosopher friend of mine has defended presentism at me for years. When I told her that special relativity settles the question — that the argument usually traced to Rietdijk and Putnam shows the future is already out there — she asked me to write it down properly, premise by premise, because she suspected I was repeating something I had read rather than something I could defend. She was right. Writing it out took an afternoon, and once it was on the page I found variations on it that I cannot block. I now want a diagnosis before I hand it back to her.

Here is my reconstruction. Take me to be at rest, and consider someone jogging past me in the direction of the Andromeda galaxy.

(1) There are no privileged observers: if an event is real as reckoned in some legitimate inertial frame, then it is real, without qualification.

(2) Special relativity entails that any two events separated by a spacelike interval are simultaneous in some legitimate inertial frame.

(3) The jogger's plane of simultaneity is tilted with respect to mine by their velocity times the distance to Andromeda divided by the square of the speed of light; over roughly 2.5 million light-years, a pace of a couple of meters per second works out to several days. So events near Andromeda lying several days in my future are present for the jogger.

(4) By (1) through (3), those events are real.

(5) Co-reality is transitive: if a is co-real with b, and b with c, then a is co-real with c.

(6) The construction iterates: among the events in the jogger's present are some that are simultaneous, in yet other legitimate frames, with events still further removed from my now, each link in the chain certified by a different inertial observer.

(7) By (5) and (6), events arbitrarily far in my future are co-real with my present moment.

(8) Therefore the future is already real; spacetime is a fixed four-dimensional block and nothing genuinely comes into being.

Two features of this unsettle me, and I cannot tell whether they are one problem or two. First, run (1) in the opposite direction: if what it forbids is privileging a frame, then an event is real only if it is real for every legitimate observer — yet no event is present in every frame's now, so nothing is real at all. Second, apply (1) to a quantity that is frame-relative in precisely the same way and that nobody wants to treat as absolute. My desk is two meters long in its rest frame and very slightly shorter in the jogger's; if whatever holds in some legitimate frame holds without qualification, the desk is both two meters long and slightly less than two meters long, simpliciter — which is simply to say that length is a relation between the desk and a frame rather than a property of the desk. I see no reason why the same treatment would not apply to reality, and if it does, I no longer know what (8) has established that my friend could not cheerfully grant me.

So: do (1) through (7) in fact entail (8), and if they do, which step should I give up — and why did that step look perfectly unobjectionable to me when I wrote it? You should know where my sympathies lie. I want to keep (2) and (3), since I have no appetite for disputing relativity, and I would like to keep something in the neighborhood of (1), because refusing to privilege my own state of motion seems to be the whole point of taking the physics seriously. That leaves (5), (6), and the move from (3) to (4), and I cannot rank them. What I most need is a price list. If I surrender (5), am I left with a reality stitched together out of frame-local patches that never cohere into a single world? If I surrender (1), does reality become perspectival in a way that is genuinely intolerable, or only in a way that sounds intolerable? And if this argument fails, does the block universe go down with it, or does it survive on independent grounds? One further question she will certainly press: does any of this depend on there actually being a jogger, or would a merely possible inertial observer do identical work — and should the difference matter to me?
