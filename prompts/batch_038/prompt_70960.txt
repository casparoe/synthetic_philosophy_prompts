Last night a friend and I argued for hours about whether it can be rational to choose a film on the strength of a critic's review, and he closed the discussion with "there's no disputing about taste," apparently taking that to show that a stranger's verdict gives me no reason to expect I will like the film. I remain unconvinced but could not articulate why. Could you set out the distinct precise claims that proverb might be expressing — from the unremarkable observation that preferences in fact diverge, through theses about whether aesthetic judgments are apt to be true or false at all, to claims specifically about whether another person's verdict can transmit knowledge or justification to me who has not seen the thing, to a mere social norm against pressing someone about their likings — and for each say whether it is plausible and whether it would actually license his conclusion? Please also indicate which reading is typically in play when people reach for the saying, and which one he would have needed for his argument to go through. A numbered menu would be most useful, as I intend to bring it back to him.
