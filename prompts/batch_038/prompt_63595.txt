I'm trying to pin down a distinction and I need a hard case, not a textbook one. Take the usual Austin picture: a performative can misfire (no accepted procedure exists, or the person/circumstances aren't the right ones, or the procedure isn't executed correctly or completely) or be abused (the speaker is insincere, or has no intention to conduct themselves accordingly). Now suppose somebody adds a third bucket: everything checks out — right words, right person, right setting, honest speaker, procedure run all the way through — but the convention underneath it has gone stale, so nothing downstream actually treats the act as having happened. Call it a lapsed-performative case.

Give me one concrete example, as specific as you can make it: who says what to whom, in what institutional or social setting, and what the relevant people then do instead. It should be a case where you can see an Austinian arguing it's really an A-rule misfire in disguise (the procedure was never genuinely accepted with that effect anymore) and another arguing the act really was performed, its uptake just failed. Then commit to a verdict on which of those is right — pick one and argue for it, don't lay the two options out side by side and leave me to choose.
