I've basically settled on the view that Maxwell's demon gets exorcized at exactly one step: erasing the demon's memory. Landauer's kT ln 2 per bit is the whole bill, while both the measurement and the feedback can be run reversibly for free — which is why I think "information is physical" is a literal, localizable claim rather than a slogan. Tell me what would count against that. Sort the defeaters by how decisive they'd actually be: the argument that the cost shows up earlier, when the demon correlates itself with the gas (say, in a Szilard engine, exactly where does the entropy go?); the circularity worry that Landauer's principle is derived by assuming something second-law-like and so can't be used to rescue the second law; whether the quantum measurement-and-feedback versions cut differently from the classical one; and whether kT ln 2 is a genuine floor or just a limit you approach slowly. Also: which of these would a committed Landauerian themselves concede as a real problem, and which would they wave off? And has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability — any apparent perpetual-motion demon just means you havent found the erasure yet — and if so, is that fatal for a claim of this kind?
