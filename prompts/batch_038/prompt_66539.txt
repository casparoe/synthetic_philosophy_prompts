Our group is building an evaluation harness that asks language models for numerical credences in indicative conditionals — items of the form "if the patient is switched to the second-line drug, the tumour shrinks," "if the flag is set at build time, the binary links statically" — and scores those numbers against outcomes. I have been treating the conditional probability of consequent on antecedent as the obvious target, but that assumption silently commits me to a semantics, and I want the commitments written down before the scoring rule is locked. So: what would an account of the indicative conditional have to deliver in order to serve as the normative target of an instrument like this?

Write the desiderata. For each, give me (i) the requirement, stated precisely enough that I could tell whether a candidate account meets it; (ii) the case, linguistic datum, or theorem that motivates it; and (iii) a judgment on whether it is genuinely non-negotiable for my purpose or a partisan preference of some camp that I would be smuggling in as a criterion of adequacy. Mark clearly which items bear on the scoring of numbers, as opposed to mattering only for a semantic theory in the abstract.

Include the standard candidates and add any I have overlooked: that a rational agent's degree of belief in a conditional should equal the corresponding conditional probability; that the conditional must not behave truth-functionally, and in particular must not collapse into the material conditional, whose verdicts come out wrong whenever the antecedent is improbable; that contraposition, transitivity, and strengthening of the antecedent should fail while modus ponens survives; that import-export should fail; that the account should explain why a conditional is assertible on partial evidence in a way its conjunctive counterpart is not; that it should extend coherently to compounds of conditionals; and that acceptance of a conditional should relate to belief revision in the Ramsey-test manner, given that this relation is known to conflict with standard postulates for revision.

Then the two parts I care about most. First: which subsets of this list cannot be satisfied jointly. I know triviality results live in this neighbourhood, and I want them stated exactly — which desideratum each one puts in conflict with which other, what the proofs assume (in particular, whether the clash depends on demanding that the identity hold for every probability function in a family closed under conditioning), and whether what emerges is a genuine theorem about conditionals or an artifact of how the space of probability functions was set up. Second: given that, is there a requirement I must surrender to keep the harness coherent at all, and is there a weaker target short of assigning probabilities to conditionals — a conditional scoring rule evaluated only on trials in which the antecedent actually obtains, an imaging-style update of the reference distribution, something else you would propose — together with an accounting of how much of the list each fallback still buys.

Close with your view on which single requirement does the most work in eliminating candidate accounts, and, in a paragraph each at most, how the suppositional line, a selection-semantics account of the Stalnaker type, Lewis's own proposed alternative, and a frankly truth-functional treatment fare against the list. Prose or table, as you prefer. No bibliography and no reading list — I want the arguments, not the references.
