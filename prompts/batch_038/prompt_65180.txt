ok weird favor. I'm a junior and I sit on our school's "Heritage Committee" (that's genuinely what it's called) — 4 students, 2 teachers, and our principal. Next fall is the 100th anniversary of the school and there's a fight about the bronze bust of our namesake in the front hall. He was a mill owner who paid for the building in 1926, basically every family in town has a grandfather who worked for him, and there was also a strike where he brought in armed men and the town has never once mentioned it in a yearbook. Right now the committee has four options on the table: (a) leave the bust exactly where it is and add a nice quote of his on the plaque, (b) leave it but put up a second display across the hall telling the strike story, (c) move it down to the library archives, (d) remove it from the building entirely. It's tangled up with the centennial pageant too, whose script is currently a musical about what a great guy he was.

I've been reading Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life (the second untimely meditation) and I can't shake the feeling that our four options are just the three attitudes he describes, done badly. So I want to hand out a memo before the meeting and I want you to draft it.

What I need:

- Open with the bottom line: one sentence saying which option you'd actually pick, plus a concrete date by which the committee can vote and still get it done before the anniversary.
- Then argue it using monumental, antiquarian and critical history — not as decoration. I want each of the four options diagnosed as one of those modes and then hit with the specific failure mode Nietzsche says that mode falls into. Like the antiquarian one that ends up treating everything old as automatically venerable, or the critical one that forgets the judge is himself made of the stuff he's condemning and can't cut the whole chain. Map which option is which mode even when it isn't a clean fit, and say out loud when it isn't.
- Give the losing side its best real argument, not a strawman. The strongest version I keep hearing from the teachers is that the bust is the only reason half the town comes to anything, and that if we're being honest we can't stop at him — half the street names, the trophy cases, the library.

Under 350 words please, that's about as long as people will read. Plain memo paragraphs with a "Recommendation:" line at the top is fine, no bullet points. And don't put any actual quotes from Nietzsche in it, I don't want the principal thinking I plagiarized — paraphrase him and get the ideas right.
