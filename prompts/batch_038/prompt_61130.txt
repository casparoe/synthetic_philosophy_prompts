So I just lost two hours of my life arguing with a friend about the frame problem and we got exactly nowhere, and I figure I might as well put the argument to use.

Him: does symbolic AI, planner stuff. Me: more of a phenomenology / embodied cognition person, which he considers a disease.

Where we ended up. He says the frame problem is a solved technical problem — that it was never about some mystery of relevance, it was about how you write down compactly that most things stay the same when an action happens, and that the situation calculus work with successor state axioms plus default / nonmonotonic reasoning basically handled it, and that anyone still dragging it up has read the original AI paper and a thought experiment about a robot blowing itself up and nothing else. I say that's precisely the point: the formalism only works once someone has already decided which things to make fluents and which causal dependencies to encode, and that decision is the real problem, and it can't be made from inside the formalism because it depends on the agent's body, its history, and what it happens to be trying to do right now. He says thats mystical handwaving, that humans screw this up constantly too, so I haven't produced an objection to formalisms, just the news that the world is complicated.

Here's the setup I want the dialogue to happen in. The two of them are standing next to a warehouse robot that a human just had to reset. The job was to drag a heavy crate out of an aisle. It did that fine. But the crate had been pinning down the corner of a loose floor mat, and once the crate moved the mat curled up, and the robot's world model had no fluent for anything mat-shaped, so it drove over the curled edge, the wheels slipped, and it's now about two meters off on where it thinks it is. Neither of them wrote the code and neither of them has a laptop.

Write that dialogue. Ground rules: both of them are sharp and neither is a strawman, and they have to actually answer each other instead of trading speeches. The formalism guy needs a real reply to "but who decides what counts as a fluent" — I think he's got one, something about how you were never required to foresee every ramification, only to detect and recover cheaply, and about how most of what a person skips over is skipped by their hands and eyes rather than by any insight, so a chunk of what I'm crediting to embodiment is really just sensors plus defaults. My side needs a real reply to his "humans miss the mat too" jab, not a dodge — like what specifically is different about the way someone who's worked in that warehouse for six months walks past that spot. And keep them on the robot; if either one retreats into abstract talk about standpoints the other should call it out.

For the ending, they have to land somewhere concrete. Either one of them concedes a specific thing, or they work out that they've been using one phrase for two different problems — the enumerate-what-changes problem versus the what's-worth-enumerating problem — which is what I suspect actually happened with me and my friend. But if that's the ending, make them earn it rather than just announce it, and have them disagree about whether the split is a fair resolution or a cop-out.
