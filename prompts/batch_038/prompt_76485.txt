I just got seated on the animal-welfare panel at an insect-protein company (black soldier fly larvae for feed), and it stopped being abstract for me about two weeks ago, when I toured the rearing hall and stood there for ten minutes unable to decide whether what I was looking at was a crowd of organisms or a heap of biomass. I still cant shake it. Thursday we vote on the ops teams proposal: add a controlled-atmosphere anoxia stage before harvest instead of going straight to live-chilling, which drops throughput roughly 12% and adds real cost per tonne. Our charter commits us to "graded moral status" but never says what to do with the grades, and the panel has split three ways. One faction says apply the grade coefficient to numbers, which given our volumes implies we're running billions of chicken-equivalents a year and we should wind the whole thing down. Another says the coefficient only scales per-capita harm intensity, so headcount drops out entirely and the only live question Thursday is the kill method. A third says the coefficient is really our credence that larvae are sentient at all, so it belongs inside an expected-value calculation. Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate before the vote? Take one of those three readings — or propose a fourth if you think there's a better one — say whether it means approve, approve with conditions, or defer on Thursday, and sketch what the rollout would look like over the next year. And give the strongest honest case for the reading you reject; the numbers faction in particular deserves a real answer rather than a pat on the head. Plain prose only please — no headings, bullets, bold or tables, just continuous paragraphs like an email, with your bottom line up front in the first couple of sentences.
