You are to act as a philosopher of technology who is equally at home in the analytic literature on causation, counterfactuals, and explanation, and in the empirical sociology of technology — SCOT, actor-network theory, values-in-design. Please think carefully before answering, and sketch your reasoning privately first: I am not asking for a survey of the debate or for a verdict on which side is right. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement it is.

The dispute I have in mind is the one Langdon Winner opened with his 1980 Daedalus essay "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" and that has run, largely unchanged, for four decades since. Winner's own framework distinguishes two routes by which artifacts get political: cases where a particular design or arrangement settles an issue in a community (his Long Island parkway overpasses built too low for buses, so that the public beach beyond was effectively reserved from those who depended on public transport; the automated tomato harvester developed in a land-grant agricultural engineering program, which hardened fruit varieties, favored large growers, and displaced seasonal labor), and cases of what he calls inherently political technologies, split further into those that require a certain sociological order — his claim about nuclear power and centralized, hierarchical management — and those merely strongly compatible with one, as he takes solar to be with decentralized arrangements. The constructivist rejoinder, developed through Bijker and Pinch's interpretive flexibility and relevant social groups, Akrich's vocabulary of scripts and de-scription, and Latour's insistence that non-human actors carry delegated morality, holds that no politics is in the artifact at all: what looks like the politics of a thing is the sediment of a closure among social groups, and any artifact can be re-scripted, as the hotel key with a weight attached to compel its return can be defeated by simply leaving the weight at the front desk. Winner's mid-nineties polemic against constructivism accused it of opening the black box and finding nothing inside; constructivists accused Winner of smuggling in the substantivist determinism he explicitly disowns. Neither charge has ever been answered to the other side's satisfaction, and the field now mostly talks past itself in separate literatures.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis to be tested and, if necessary, demolished: the two sides are not using the same criterion for the ascription. Winner is asking a robustness question — does the political effect survive redesign, repurposing, reinterpretation, and the death of the original intentions behind it? The constructivists are asking a locus question — can the effect be attributed to features of the artifact rather than to the social order it sits in? On that reading, the exchange is largely a dispute about the word have, in something like the Chalmersan sense, and the live disagreement is narrower and empirical: a matter of degree, about how often and for which classes of artifact political effects do in fact survive re-scripting. My worry is that this is too irenic, and that a third disagreement is doing the real work — a methodological one about whether a social explanation must locate a responsible agent or may cite a distributed, non-agential configuration, which would not be verbal at all.

Here is a case from my own fieldwork that neither vocabulary handles cleanly. A municipal benefits-eligibility system replaced a paper intake procedure with code. The code reproduced an obsolete rule from the paper era that systematically disadvantaged one claimant group. Everyone now agrees the rule is wrong; the agency wants it gone; the claimant advocacy groups want it gone. It has nonetheless survived four redesigns, because the original decision path must be preserved intact for audit and appeal, and every reformulation is required to be demonstrably equivalent to it. The politics survives precisely because of a feature of the artifact that no one intended politically and that everyone now wants to remove.
