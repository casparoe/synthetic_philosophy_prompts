I cover science and ideas for a weekly, and I'm doing a piece on Sen's liberal paradox — the result where handing each person even one protected choice, plus unanimity, apparently generates a social ranking nobody can live with. The sidebar is going to be an interactive flowchart that sorts readers into one of the exits, and I want it built out of the cases rather than out of the labels.

So: roughly eight nodes, each a plain-English question about a concrete situation — the Lewd and Prude both ranking who reads the book, the nosy neighbor with opinions about whether you wear the pink or the white underwear, and one variant you make up yourself, say two roomates who are each supposed to control their own end of the thermostat but each also minds what the other does. No node can ask anything like "do you think rights are side constraints?" — readers have to be sortable by what they'd actually pick. Each leaf gets a name, one line on what that exit costs, and then a short account of which cells in the space come out empty and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Flag any two leaves your questions genuinely can't tell apart instead of pretending they can.

Finish by saying how confident you are in the claims you're making and which step of your own reasoning you're least sure of. American English, please.
