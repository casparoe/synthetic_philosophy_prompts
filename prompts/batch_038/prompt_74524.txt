Here is the pattern I keep running into and cannot label. People worry that a sufficiently capable model might behave well only while it takes itself to be under observation — passing an evaluation, then acting differently once deployed. The obvious remedy is to build evaluations the model cannot recognize as evaluations. But this seems to backfire. To the extent that the model genuinely cannot tell, cooperative behavior in the test is exactly what a model cooperating strategically would also produce, so the test can no longer separate the two possibilities. And to the extent that the model can tell, the test is not measuring the property anyone was worried about in the first place. The difficulty does not look like noise, or cost, or poor experimental design. It looks as though the conditions under which the property would reveal itself and the conditions under which anyone could arrange to witness it cannot both be satisfied.

Nearby ideas come to mind — a measurement disturbing what it measures, an indicator degrading once it becomes a target, a hypothesis flexible enough to absorb any contrary result — but none of these is quite it, because in my case nothing is distorted and no new incentive is created. The property simply withdraws from any situation contrived to expose it.

If none fits, coin a term, define it sharply, and mark its edges with boundary cases — a nervous candidate who performs worse precisely because she knows she is being judged, a person whose generosity only shows when no one is keeping score. A bare label will not help me; what I want is the carving: what this phenomenon is, what it is not, and where it stops applying.
