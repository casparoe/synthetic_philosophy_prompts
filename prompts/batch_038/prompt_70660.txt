I have no academic training in philosophy, but I have been reading on my own for a few years, and there is one argument I have not been able to shake since I first met it in a book about properties and relations. People apparently call it Bradley's regress, and as I understand it the argument goes like this. Take something ordinary: the apple is red. If the apple's being red is a matter of some tie — call it instantiation, or inherence — holding between the apple and redness, then that tie must itself stand in a relation to each of its terms, and relating it to them requires a further tie, and so on without end. The book said this shows something is deeply wrong with the way we habitually set the problem up, but it never told me what to do instead, which has left me unsatisfied ever since.

Over that time I have accumulated, from articles and discussion boards, a list of the moves people make in order to escape. I would like you to sort all eight of them into four tiers according to how well you think each one actually stops the regress. Not according to how respectable or fashionable the move is, and not according to how much further machinery it drags in: a solution that costs more than the problem is worth should be penalised, and I would like you to tell me where you are applying that discount. If one of these turns out not to be a response to the same problem at all, say so rather than ranking it.

The moves, in my own words:

1. Call the regress benign rather than vicious. Each further tie is generated from the first one, so nothing new has to be supplied at each stage, and an endless chain of that kind is harmless.
2. Deny that the tie is a relation at all. Treat it as a non-relational connector, something whose entire nature is to link, so that there is no residue left over which could require further linking.
3. Say that predicates are incomplete — the standard word is unsaturated — and therefore carry the tying with them, so nothing external ever needs to be added.
4. Take facts, or states of affairs, as basic items rather than as constructions: the apple's being red is simply one thing, not a plurality sitting around waiting to be glued together.
5. Reverse direction and hold that relations are internal to their terms, so there is no separate relatable item; carried far enough, this becomes the claim that the world is one indivisible thing and the question never properly arises.
6. Replace relations with particularised property instances, plus a bundling or co-location tie that is not itself one further member of the bundle.
7. Quietism. The demand for a further tie is an artefact of doing philosophy; once one notices that nothing in ordinary or scientific practice needs to be answered, the question dissolves rather than gets solved.
8. My own idea, which I do not believe I have seen stated anywhere. The regress only gets started because we insist on writing a fact as a plurality plus a connector, and we insist on that because we write in subject-predicate sentences. If the apple's being red is taken to be a single non-repeatable happening — not two items plus a tie — then there is nothing there to unify, and the whole difficulty is a mistake about notation.

Give me one or two sentences for each item explaining why it lands where it lands. Then three further things:

- Tell me plainly whether item 8 differs in any real way from item 4, or from item 5. If it is a relabelling, say which of them it collapses into and stop pretending otherwise; if it is genuinely distinct, say what it buys that they do not.
- Give me a single number between 0 and 1 for your own confidence that the regress is vicious — that is, that none of the moves on this list succeeds, and the argument really does establish that something we ordinarily assume about facts and relations has to be given up. Briefly justify the number rather than hedging it into meaninglessness.
- Say whether your ranking would change if the subject were not a thing having a property, but the unity of a whole proposition, or of a whole composed of parts. If it would change, tell me which entries move and in which direction.
