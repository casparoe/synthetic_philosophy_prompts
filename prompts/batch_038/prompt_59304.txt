Something I keep running into and can't find a word for. One party has, on paper, total power over another — could wreck them pretty much at will — but actually using it would cost the powerful one nearly as much as it costs the victim, everyone knows that, so they never do. No threats, no fear, nothing to guard against day to day. Two examples I've been chewing on: the single employer in a company town, who can fire anyone but would go under without the workforce; and a small country whose entire export market is one big neighbor that could close the border tomorrow but would then lose its cheapest supplier. In both, the weaker party still ends up not raising certain topics, not making plans that assume the arrangement lasts, generally keeping the other one comfortable. And yet it doesn't look like the strong party really has a power to weild — it's locked in just as tight.

Is there a concept for this exact shape, power that's total but too expensive to use? How does it differ from deterrence, from ordinary dependence, from a lopsided bargain both sides walked into? And where's the line — at what point does "too costly to exercise" shade into "not power at all"? I care more about the boundaries than the label, but if there is an established term I'd like to know it and what it's supposed to exclude.
