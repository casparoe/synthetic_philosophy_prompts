I serve on the seven-member community relations commission of a mid-sized American city, and the council votes in six weeks on a proposal it has asked us to advise on: a formal apology, delivered in open session, for the summer of 1919, when the city marshal's deputies and a crowd of residents drove some four hundred immigrant families out of the mill district and burned eleven houses, after which the council annexed the cleared land — land that is still on the tax rolls. The proposal pairs the apology with a two-million-dollar fund for down-payment assistance in those same neighborhoods.

The commission is split three ways. One bloc holds that no body can literally be guilty of anything, that none of us did it, and that an apology from people who were not yet born is theater that will cost us the fund as well. A second holds that the city is the same corporate person that did it, inheriting assets and liabilities together, and that the money without the apology is a payoff. A third holds that the money is what matters and should go to whoever in those neighborhoods is poorest now, ancestry irrelevant.

Write the opinion I will circulate before our meeting, in the voice of a political philosopher who has actually sat inside municipal government. Give me a clear recommendation — apologize, fund, both, neither, or the proposal restructured — with a time frame and a standing rule the council could reuse on the next such proposal. Take the first bloc seriously rather than waving it away: say whether an apology by non-perpetrators can be sincere, and what exactly survives across generations, guilt or only liability and obligation. You may use the Jaspers-style division among criminal, political, moral, and metaphysical guilt, but translate it; commissioners have not read him. Think the alternatives through first, then send me only the memo.

Continuous prose only — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables — written the way one writes a letter, with your bottom line in the first two or three sentences. American English.
