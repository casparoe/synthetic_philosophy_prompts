We keep an internal log in our eval group of votes on whether an experiment actually elicited latent knowledge from a model versus just reading out something trivial. So far:

**Yes:** (1) a linear probe on mid-layer activations decoded a fact the model had only seen in training at ~85%; the model flatly denied knowing it when asked, but in a forced binary choice it sided with the fact 65% of the time. (2) a fact we only ever stated in English during training changed its fill-in behavior in a language we never stated it in — no probe involved, we just noticed the behavior. (3) a question it answered at chance when generating freely jumped to ~80% once the right answer was sitting there among three distractors.

**No:** (4) a probe that decoded "the capital is Rome" when the word Rome was literally in the prompt. (5) a model that solved a chemistry problem correctly where the whole solution was derivable from the instructions in the same context window. (6) we fine-tuned in a fabricated fact about a made-up country, the probe read it straight back out, and the model then used it fluently in generation.
