I cover science and ideas for a magazine and I'm working on a piece about the transatlantic split on hate speech online — over here the state basically cant touch it, over there platforms have to take stuff down on a deadline or eat fines. I keep coming back to Kant, who obviously never saw any of this, so I want to know where he'd actually land on the machinery rather than the slogans.

His enlightenment essay separates the public use of reason, which he insisted has to be free, from the private use you make of it in a job or office, which can be constrained. Does a content moderator's shift fall on the private side, so a platform is entitled to gag its own staff, or is judging what the reading public gets to see itself a public act? And then the version I really want you to push on: suppose the European rule wasn't deletion at all, but that a post adjudicated to be hateful stays up, demoted, with a state-supplied note saying which dignity interest it runs over, plus a guaranteed right of reply for the target printed right alongside it. Would Kant sign onto that, or is compelled annotation its own violation for him?

Finally, where does his hostility to resistance bite — is he likelier than a modern American liberal to say the sovereign gets to decide what counts as hateful, or less, because the freedom of the pen is the one lever subjects keep against thier rulers? Give me an actual position with reasons drawn from his own commitments, not a both-sides shrug, and tell me where you think I'm reading him wrong.
