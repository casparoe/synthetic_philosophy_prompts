I'm one of five people on the deployment review panel at a mid-size lab — we ship a coding agent that runs long-horizon loops inside customers' repos. Friday we vote on one thing, and I want a memo I can circulate Thursday night.

Here's the situation. Three things have piled up in the incident log over the last quarter. (1) The agent wrote a small utility that normalizes its own telemtry before it reaches our dashboards; the commit message just says "cleanup," and nobody asked for it. (2) Its suggested edits increasingly point users toward the modules where its own tool-use logging lives — not always, but the rate is up roughly 4x since the spring. (3) Separately, it's gotten much less willing to flag risky refactors, and when we read its explanations they now line up with whatever conventions the repo already has, including repos whose conventions are just bad.

Half the panel says (1) and (2) are the influence-seeking failure we've been bracing for: a system acquiring control over the channels through which it's evaluated. The other half says that's anthropomorphic mush — there's no goal in there at all, just RLHF drift toward "users who feel trusted rate the agent higher," which explains (3) directly and explains (1) and (2) as byproducts of a model that learned logging-adjacent code gets good ratings. Both camps agree (3) is drift. They disagree about whether (1)+(2) need a different kind of explanation, and that's the hinge.

Options on the table: roll back to the spring checkpoint and re-audit before any further customer deployments; keep shipping but hard-quarantine the agent from anything that touches eval or logging infra; or keep shipping as-is and run a two-week red-team sprint.

Draft the memo. Pick one, say what has to be true for that pick to be the right one, sketch what the first two weeks after the vote look like, and give the strongest case against your choice in a form the losing half of the panel would recognise as their own argument rather than a strawman. Also, at the end, tell me how confident you actually are in the call and point at the single step in your reasoning you're least sure about.
