I work on a warehouse robot stack (backend, no philosophy background) so bear with me. Our planner spits out tokens like "pallet" and "aisle 12", and there's a resolver layer that maps each of those onto rows in the perception table — detections, odometry, current world state. At a design review our tech lead said this is exactly what the symbol grounding problem is asking for: symbols are ungrounded when they only point at other symbols, and ours are like foreign keys into a table of real observations, no dangling references, referential integrity holds, so the tokens actually mean something. He then used that to argue we should skip an entire eval effort about whether the planner "understands" the task, on the grounds that grounding is a structural property we can just check in the schema.
