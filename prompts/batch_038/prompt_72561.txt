I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and our fiction editor has asked several reporters to contribute a short story built around a concept from their own beat. Mine is the Dutch book argument: the result that an agent whose degrees of belief violate the probability axioms can be offered a package of wagers she would each accept at her own quoted prices, and which together guarantee her a net loss no matter how things turn out; and its diachronic version, where the leak is not an incoherent set of prices held at one moment but the rule she uses to revise them over time.

Write the story in 1,200 to 1,500 words. The protagonist spent thirty years compiling odds, first for a small bookmaker and later on prediction markets, and was very good at it. The counterparty who slowly cleans her out is her own future self: each wager is struck by the woman she currently is against the woman she is demonstrably on track to become, at prices that follow mechanically from the updating habit she knows she has. No stranger outsmarts her. She is exploited, in plain sight, by the person she keeps turning into.

Three requirements. First, the arithmetic must hold up: the wagers she accepts one at a time should sum to a loss that does not depend on how anything resolves, and at least one scene should let a general reader follow that without being shown a formula. Second, the story should identify, in ordinary English, the particular way she revises her mind that makes the sequence exploitable, and make clear that the defect lives in that rule rather than in any individual price she quoted. Third, no philosophers are named and no character explains the moral aloud; the closing scene should turn on whether the older woman settles the debt, and either answer must be defensible given the logic already established.

My editor trained as a decision theorist before moving into journalism and will not accept a story that treats vulnerability to a sure loss as automatically a vice. Keep genuinely open the question of whether what happened to her is a failure of rationality or simply the price of being someone who changes her mind.
