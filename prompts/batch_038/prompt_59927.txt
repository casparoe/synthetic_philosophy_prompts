The clause ties my client's payment to a metric my client can currently estimate at some figure V, and the counterparty's own estimate of the same metric is either half of V or twice V; neither side knows the other's number. R. wrote out the familiar calculation: half the time the counterparty's figure is 2V and half the time it is V/2, so the expected value of anchoring to their number rather than ours is 1.25V, and it is therefore always worth taking the other side's figure. I told him this is the two-envelope argument and it is a fallacy. He told me the fallacy only exists in the textbook version, where the amounts are written into the envelopes before anyone looks, and that once the numbers are generated by two people holding genuinely different information the calculation goes through.
