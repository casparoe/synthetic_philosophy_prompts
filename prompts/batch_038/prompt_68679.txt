ok so this is a weird one but it's based on something that almost actually happened in a club at my school and i genuinely cannot stop thinking about it.

write me a short story, like 1500–2500 words, where the whole plot is a student organization destroying itself using only its own rulebook. here's the setup i want you to use, make up everything else (the people, the school, what the club even does):

the bylaws have three provisions that matter.
- Article 9: ammendments need a two-thirds vote of the members present.
- Article 11: nothing in these bylaws may be changed in a way that weakens Article 9. Article 11 may not be changed.
- Article 14: the bylaws may be dissolved in their entirety and replaced with a new constitution, by unanimous vote.

then somebody who has read too much realizes Article 14 is a door in the wall. you can't weaken Article 9 by ammendment, but you CAN dissolve the whole thing unanimously and hand everyone a brand-new constitution that happens to contain Article 9 and Article 11 word for word, plus a new Article 14 that only needs a simple majority. after that the two-thirds requirement is dead at the next vote. the entrenchment worked exactly once, and it burned itself doing it.

the second problem is the part i want the story to actually be ABOUT: the only person allowed to certify that the new constitution is now in force is the secretary, and she's the secretary because the old bylaws say so. the new constitution says it takes effect the instant it's certified. so the second she signs, she's signing under an authority that the signature itself deletes. does the new constitution's first valid act create the office that created it? one character says obviously fine, the club is the people in the room and they all agreed. another says no, there's now a gap where nothing at all is in force, and technically nobody is an officer, including the person who's about to rule on the challenge.

things i need:
- make the reasoning followable by someone who's never heard of any of this, but nobody is allowed to give a speech that sounds like an essay. it should come out through an argument at a meeting, in the minutes, in a group chat, whatever feels right.
- put the secretary's official minutes somewhere in the middle as an actual formatted document, and let them get more and more uncomfortable as she writes them.
- i want one character who's basically right but for a garbage reason, and one who's wrong but whose reason would have been great if the facts were slightly different. don't label them or point at them.
- end on a real decision being made, not a shrug. the ending should show which principle won: that authority comes from the document, or that authority comes from the fact that everyone keeps showing up and doing what the last vote said. and it should be a little disturbing either way.

also if you can work in a bit where someone tries to take out Article 11 by first amending it to say "this article may be amended," and then amending it again — is that a weakening of it or is that using it — i would love that, but only if it doesn't feel crammed in.
