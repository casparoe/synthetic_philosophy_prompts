I don't have a background in philosophy or psychiatry; I came to Thomas Szasz on my own a few years ago and have kept returning to him, and there is a development since his death in 2012 that I cannot work out how he would take it. Psilocybin and MDMA are being turned into clinic-based treatments for diagnosed depression and post-traumatic stress: you cannot simply buy them, you are screened by professionals, dosed under supervision, and walked back through the experience afterwards in guided sessions, while licensing and approval processes decide who qualifies and who may administer it.

On the face of it this pulls in opposite directions against everything he argued. He held that adults should be free to take what they choose and that drug enforcement was persecution dressed up as medicine; yet here a forbidden substance is being made available precisely by being converted into medicine. He also held that most of what psychiatry calls illness is a problem in living rather than a disease; yet here the substance is offered as a cure for a diagnosed disorder, and only on condition that a physician stands between the person and it.

Which way do you think he would actually land? Please commit to one answer rather than splitting the difference, and tell me which of his own commitments you think does most of the work in getting him there. Then say what he would make of today's advocates who would like to claim him as an ally, and where his position would break most sharply with theirs. If the supervised, therapist-guided format changes your verdict compared with ordinary decriminalization, explain how and why.
