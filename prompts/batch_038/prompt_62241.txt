I'm in a philosophy of math seminar this term and our whole running argument has been about how to read Russell's paradox, and honestly it's gone stale — the two camps just keep restating themselves at each other. Let me set them up:

**Camp A (hierarchy):** the paradox shows that comprehension has to be constrained by something like size or stage. Sets are built up level by level in a cumulative hierarchy, and the Russell collection and the collection of all sets simply aren't sets — not because we haven't found the right axiom, but because there's no level at which they get generated. What the paradox teaches is a genuine discovery about how many sets there are.

**Camp B (stratification):** the paradox teaches nothing about size. It shows that some definitions are just ill-formed: you can't assign consistent types to the variables in x ∈ x. Restrict comprehension to stratified formulas and the trouble goes away, the universal set exists, complements of sets exist, and the whole thing turns out to have been bad syntax rather than a fact about the set-theoretic universe.
