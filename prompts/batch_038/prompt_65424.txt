Take the conclusion of the argument from illusion in its causal-matching version: that in at least some hallucinations generated by the same proximal causal chain a corresponding veridical perception would have had, the subject stands in an awareness relation to something that is no part of the mind-independent environment — a sense-datum, or at any rate a mind-dependent object of awareness. Give me your credence that this is true, and derive it twice so that I can check the derivations against one another.

First route, through the metaphysics of experience: the probability that the hallucinatory and the veridical episode share a highest common factor in phenomenal character; the probability, given that they do, that a character of that kind requires a relatum rather than being handled adverbially or representationally; and the probability that such a relatum would be a particular rather than a merely intentional object.

Second route, through the epistemology: the probability that what licenses the generalization from the hallucination case to perception generally is the subject's inability to discriminate the two episodes from the inside; the probability that this license can be detached from the subject's epistemic position and converted into a claim about the experience itself; plus any further terms you need to reach the conclusion.

A number and one line of justification per term, then the two products. Do they agree to within roughly 0.1? If they don't, which route is mis-specified, and which single term is the number most sensitive to? Finally: what does your credence become if the subject has been told in advance, and firmly believes, that the replay is underway, so that no introspective uncertainty remains?
