You are a philosopher working at the intersection of moral psychology and the methodology of experimental philosophy, someone who has personally administered several hundred vignette studies and who also writes material for a departmental revue that specializes in jokes only methodologists get. I am convening a two-hour graduate methods session on the side-effect asymmetry — the finding, going back to Joshua Knobe's 2003 chairman vignettes, that people overwhelmingly say a profit-seeking chairman harmed the environment intentionally while denying that the very same chairman, indifferent in exactly the same way, helped the environment intentionally — and on the two decades of dispute about whether that asymmetry tells us something about the concept of intentional action or is an artifact of how the questions were put. To open the session I want a satirical document the group can annotate: a mock institutional standard in the deadpan register of an ISO norm or a laboratory SOP, issued by an invented body — call it the Consortium for the Standardization of Intuition Elicitation — under a title along the lines of "Vignette-Based Elicitation of Folk Attitudes: Requirements for the Design, Administration, and Certification of Side-Effect Asymmetries." Aim for 900–1,000 words and use the full apparatus of the genre: a scope clause; a normative-requirements section in which "shall" does real work; a definitions clause that defines "intentionally" in a way that turns out to be circular and then refers the reader onward to an annex; numbered sub-clauses down to the third level; at least one troubleshooting table keyed to symptom, probable cause, and corrective action; an annex of approved, conditionally approved, and deprecated vignette wordings; and a revision-history line recording that some clause was amended following a failed replication.

The rule I care about most is that every clause must exaggerate something actually true of this literature rather than something invented for the joke. The comedy should come from the standard taking the field's methodological anxieties with total seriousness and then solving them bureaucratically: order and framing effects, wording sensitivity, forced binary responses versus graded scales with a neutral midpoint, the requirement that participants be unable to divine the hypothesis, the dissociation between judging an act intentional and judging the agent blameworthy, the conversational-pragmatics explanations on which "intentionally" is doing something other than describing a mental state, the hypothesis that the effect really tracks assessments of an agent's deep self or quality of will, the expertise defense, the demographic and cross-cultural variability debates, and the awkward status of a vignette that yields the asymmetry in some samples and not in others. Please think carefully before drafting, and do not let the document ever state an objection in its own voice — a reader should be able to reconstruct the whole controversy from the requirements alone. Nor should you satirize by having anyone commit fraud or fabricate data; that is not the target, and a sympathetic researcher should be able to read this and laugh rather than wince. The reductio should be structural rather than announced: by the final clauses the standard's own certification procedure should quietly license a researcher to keep revising wording, presentation order, and response scales until the asymmetry reappears, and should then treat the reappearance as evidence about the concept — all in the same flat normative prose, with no wink at the reader.

For the annex, work with four vignettes I have written for the session; the standard must classify each (approved, conditionally approved pending specified revision, deprecated) and state in bureaucratic language what each requires: (i) HARM — a regional water authority's director is told by her engineer that a proposed drawdown schedule will raise revenue for the district and will also, as a side effect, cause the downstream heron rookery to abandon its nesting site this season; the director replies that she does not care at all about the herons, only about the revenue; she approves the schedule, revenue rises, the herons leave. (ii) HELP — identical wording, except that the side effect is that a rare orchid on the flooded margin blooms for the first time in a decade, which the director likewise does not care about. (iii) NEUTRAL — identical wording, except that the side effect is that the intake pumps begin to hum in B-flat. (iv) EXPERT — the HARM wording, but the director is a former field ecologist who spent eleven years banding herons. Do not cite any papers, name any real researchers, labs, or journals, or offer a bibliography or reading list; the invented consortium and these four vignettes are the only concrete apparatus the piece may lean on. Finally, begin with no more than a short paragraph of visible brainstorming in which you list the genuine features of the literature you intend to encode and the clause that will carry each, so that I can check the mapping — then produce the standard itself, formatted as the standard.
