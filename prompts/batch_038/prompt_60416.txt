so my brother works in "model oversight" at one of the labs and last month he sent me their internal step-by-step grading rubric, and I could not stop laughing, because it is structurally identical to the way his company reviews its own human employees. that gave me an idea for a bit I want to run in our reading group's joke issue (philosophy-of-AI reading group, we do one every spring, mostly for each other). I want to write it as a parody of a corporate year-end performance calibration packet — the genre with the manager's narrative, the rubric table, and the "calibration committee notes" at the bottom — except the firm has decided that judging people on outcomes is barbaric and has fully switched to process-based supervision of its human staff. Make it a proprietary trading firm, invent the name and invent all the people, don't use any real company or real researcher. Around 750–850 words, deadpan HR voice the whole way through, no winking, and it needs to work on two levels: genuinely funny to someone who knows the process-vs-outcome supervision debate in AI safety, and still legible as a weird-workplace piece to my cousin who doesn't.

the jokes have to come from getting the structure right, not from generic corporate-bureaucracy boilerplate. concretely, I'd like the piece to dramatize — without ever announcing any of them as "issues" — that the firm's ground-truth labels for what counts as a good step were themselves generated by checking which positions ended up making money, so the whole process-supervision edifice is bootstrapped out of the outcome data it claims to have transcended, and nobody in the packet notices; that an analyst who called a trade correctly off an inarticulable hunch gets rated "does not meet expectations" while a colleague whose beautifully formatted forty-slide derivation led straight into a blowup gets "exceeds," and the firm's fix is to require even more narration; that the calibration committee physically cannot verify the steps of anyone sharper than they are, so every rating quietly caps out at the reviewers' own competence; that some poor person has to grade every step of every analyst's day and the annotation budget is eating the bonus pool; and that at least one employee has learned to farm it, filing immaculate reasoning traces that everyone in the room knows aren't what he actually did. maybe include one small rubric table with two or three columns and a couple of filled-in rows, and one reviewer comment that's devastating in the specific way only calibration comments are.

then end with a modest proposal from the head of "Human Oversight" — something like formally retiring P&L as a metric because it's an untrustworthy proxy that rewards luck — that follows the firm's own logic completely straight to a place the reader recoils from. keep the real jargon light, I don't want it reading like a survey paper, and pls don't have anyone break character to explain the joke.
