So I've managed three apartment buildings for going on fifteen years now — a bit over 400 units, mixed income, one of them answers to a co-op board I have to face twice a year. I dont have any college philosophy behind me, but I read on my own at night, and lately I keep coming back to the idea that some governments are legitimate basically because they deliver: the trains run, crime is down, incomes go up, nothing falls apart. China and Singapore always come up as the examples. The thing that won't leave me alone is that my tenants mostly don't care who picks the management company either, as long as the heat works and the hallway isn't a mess — and honestly, a fair number of them would rather have a competent outfit they can't fire than a friendly one they can.

Here's what I want. Translate that whole argument — is prosperity and order enough to justify ruling without real elections — into the vocabulary I actually live in: leases and renewals, work orders, turnover rates, reserve funds, notice periods, board packets, the difference between the tenant who complains every month and the tenant who says nothing and just doesn't re-sign. Be faithful to my world, don't rough it up to make the political theory look better by comparison. I've already sketched a mapping myself and I want it graded: citizens = tenants, the state = the managing agent, elections = the annual board vote, protest = a loud work order, legitimacy = renewal rate, loss of legitimacy = everyone moving out at once. Tell me which of those hold, which hold only halfway, and which are quietly doing something dishonest. Then do the part I actually care about — the residue. Which claims in the debate have no counterpart at all in property management? I can already guess two: that a tenant can leave and a citizen can't really, and that a tenant being satisfied isn't the same sort of thing as a citizen having authorised the arrangement. But find the ones I haven't got. And for each piece of residue, say plainly whether the gap is a limit on my end — my vocabulary is missing something real — or a sign that the people arguing this are talking less definitely than they sound like they are. I want a tally at the end: which of those two it turned out to be more often, since that's the question I can't settle by myself.

Format-wise: lead with the bottom line, one or two sentences telling me whether my tenant mapping is broadly sound or broadly a category mistake, before you get into any detail. Hard cap the whole thing at 900 words — I read this on my phone between showings, so make it dense rather than long. And if something important has to get cut for space, don't pad; just drop a one-line "didn't fit" list at the very end with the things you left out.
