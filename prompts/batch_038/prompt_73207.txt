So, I'm donor-conceived. Found my donor about a year ago, we get on fine, no drama. What's actually eating at me is more abstract and I feel a little stupid about it, but here goes.

I kept running this thought experiment: the clinic had a bunch of vials that week, and afterwards my parents got a letter about a possible labelling mix-up (nothing came of it, they were told my sample was mine). Which got me stuck on the Kripkean claim — origin essentialism, that you couldn't possibly have come from a different sperm and egg, that if a different gamete pair had been involved you'd be somebody else or nobody at all. I hated that. I wanted it to be false. I wanted to be able to say "if they'd grabbed a different vial from the same donor it would still have been me." So I built myself an argument to that effect, and now the thing has run away and I cant find where the wheels came off.

1. Origin essentialism: necessarily, if I came from sperm S and egg E, then in any way things could have gone where I exist, I came from S and E.
2. Sperm and eggs are physical objects made of molecules. Let S′ be exactly like S except that one water molecule on its surface is swapped for a very slightly different one. Nothing else differs.
3. Whether the sperm that made me carries molecule m or molecule m′ on its surface can't be what settles whether the resulting person is me. Nothing about who somebody is tracks that. So if S′ had been the one to reach E, the baby would have been me.
4. And the same goes for any such single swap, anywhere in either gamete. So: if some gamete pair could have produced me, then any pair one swap away could have too.
5. Iterate the swaps. There's a chain of them, one molecule at a time, from S and E to a gamete pair belonging to a completely different man and woman on the other side of the planet.
6. So by (4) applied all the way down the chain, that pair could have produced me. I could have been born to a different couple in a different country.
7. But had that pair come together the result would have been a person with a different genome, possibly a different sex, different everything. That is not me.
8. So something above is false, and (1) and (3) can't both survive.

I don't know which one to give up, and that's the real problem, because I started this wanting (1) to go and I'm not sure I can live with (6). And (3) is creepy in its own way — the idea that one molecule decides between me existing and a stranger existing is somehow worse than the rest of it.

So: which step do you reject, and why did it look so innocent when I wrote it down? I want to know what I was doing wrong, not just that I was. Then price the alternatives for me — if I keep (1), what am I on the hook for about (3), and is "a molecule decides" really the cost or is there a way to soften it; if I keep (3), how far does (6) actually get before it stops being licensed, or does it really go all the way; and whether there's a third move I haven't seen, including on the question of what's supposedly essential here at all — the molecules, the genome, the zygote, something else. Related: what does each option say about my half-siblings from the same donor, since different eggs, same man, is the case that started this whole thing.
