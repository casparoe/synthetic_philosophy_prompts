So I want a dialogue, roughly 1500–2000 words, between two philosophers arguing about a case that I think cuts against the usual way Frege's puzzle gets set up. Let me build the case first because the details are doing a lot of the work.

Dana meets the same man twice, years apart, in circumstances that don't overlap at all. First time: a wedding in Lisbon, 2011 — a guy she talks to at the bar for maybe ten minutes. No name exchanged, nothing learned about him, no story. All she's left with is a face and the memory of the linen jacket. Second time: a conference dinner in 2019, she's seated next to a man who introduces himself as Tomás, and she ends up with a fairly rich set of beliefs — Tomás restores pipe organs, Tomás is patient, Tomás hates airports. Then in 2024 someone hands her a photo from that wedding, says that's Tomás over there by the punch, and she recognizes the linen jacket instantly.

The thing I care about: the Lisbon episode gave Dana essentially *no* descriptive content that could distinguish Tomás from anyone else. There's nothing there for the classic descriptivist move to grab onto — no cluster of properties, no name, just a recognitional capacity. And yet the discovery feels genuinely informative in a way that "Tomás is Tomás" does not. So the two standard options both look bad.

Write the dialogue between:

(a) a Millian, who holds that both of Dana's states have the same singular proposition as content, so nothing new is believed at the wedding-photo moment and the informativeness is a fact about how she processes and stores things, not about content — and who has to defend that against the charge of changing the subject away from the phenomenon everyone was trying to explain; and

(b) a mental-file theorist, who thinks the file is the right unit of cognitive significance, that Dana ran two separate files on Tomás, and that merging them is a real event in her cognitive life rather than a semantic change or a mere psychological hiccup.

What I want out of it:

- Neither side is a strawman. The Millian gets real ammunition: the worry that file individuation just restates the data it was meant to explain, that files have no identity conditions, and the observation that everything about Dana's behavior before and after the photo is describable without adding or removing any content.
- The file theorist presses where it actually hurts. Dana can now *deduce* things she couldn't before — she can conclude that the organ restorer was at the wedding in Lisbon, and she can act on that. Is that a fact about her or a fact about what she has available? Also: what does the Millian say about the Lisbon state itself — if a nameless face-memory already counts as a singular belief about Tomás, then the Millian and the file theorist agree about the belief and disagree only about bookkeeping, which makes me suspect the file is doing nothing.
- At least one sustained round on whether merging is an explanation or a redescription, and whether the file theorist's story is any less circular than "they have different senses."
- Both of them run the Paderewski case through the grinder, and specifically the fact that Dana's situation is Paderewski without the contradiction — she doesn't end up with conflicting beliefs, she ends up with a *connection*. Say whether either view is better equipped for the no-conflict version, since that version is the one that actually happened here.
- No resolution. End with each of them giving, in a sentence or two, the single concession they'd need from the other in order to switch sides.

Then a short postscript, separate from the dialogue: point out anywhere one of them is making a move that only works becuase of how I've rigged the case — my suspicion is the recognitional-only Lisbon file is doing suspicious work, and its not clear the case survives if she'd learned his first name in 2011 — and tell me how to vary the setup to close those loopholes.
