so I'm in a philosophy of science seminar that's spent two weeks grinding through determinism vs. predictability and I've been volunteered to open friday's discussion. I want to start with a joke, but every chaos joke I can find is the same butterfly-causes-a-tornado thing, which isn't even the interesting part. here's the material I actually want the joke built out of:

- chaotic systems aren't indeterministic. the logistic map at r=4 is about as deterministic as anything gets — you can write down a closed form for the nth term using sines of doubled angles, no iteration required — and it's still totally unpredictable for anyone who only knows their starting point to finitely many digits.
- the reason is basically arithmetic. that map is a bit-shift in disguise: each step slides the binary expansion of your initial condition one place to the left and spits out the next digit. so it eats your information one binary digit at a time, and once it's chewed through everything you actually measured it's reading off digits that nobody ever wrote down. laplace's demon isn't beaten by randomness, he's beaten by rounding.
- and yet the same map is perfectly predictable statistically: the fraction of time the orbit spends near any little interval settles down to a fixed density. so you can know everything about where it'll be on average and nothing at all about where it'll be next tuesday.

write me a joke out of that. short enough to say out loud in roughly one breath, and the laugh has to come from the content — determinism and predictability coming apart, the demon getting outsmarted by his own decimal point, a system being exactly solved and exactly unknowable at once — not from a pun on the word chaos or a physicist walking into a bar. pick whichever of those three hooks is funniest, don't try to cram them all in. a character can show up if it helps (the demon, a weather forecaster, the map itself), and it's fine if it's a bit shaggy, but the person in my seminar who's actually done the reading should laugh first and then go oh that's exactly right. they know the concepts — just don't make them parse notation to get the punchline.
