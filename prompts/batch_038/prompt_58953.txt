So I run a monthly rationality-ish meetup — about 14 people, mostly smart amateurs who've read a bit of decision theory but never touched game theory formally. We've got 90 minutes, a whiteboard, and a bag of chocolate coins. I first ran into the absent-minded driver problem years ago in some comment thread and it's lived in my head rent-free ever since: two identical exits, the right plan is to blow past the first and take the second, except once you're sitting at an exit you can't tell which one it is, so the plan isn't executable and your at-the-junction reasoning drags you somewhere else. I want to make the room feel that, not be told about it.

Design a game they actually play. Constraints: nobody hears the name of the puzzle or the phrase "imperfect recall" until the debrief; the payoffs should be tuned so they physically hit the knife-edge where the expected-payoff-maximizing randomization is exactly the one that leaves you indifferent at the junction (derive the numbers rather than taking my word for what they are); and the hard part — the mechanic that puts someone at a junction without letting them know which junction — has to survive a room full of people who will start counting how many times they've been approached and reconstruct their location by round two. Stakes real but cheap, chocolate coins are fine.

Format: exactly five numbered items, two or three sentences each, no preamble and no wrap-up paragraph. Item 1 = setup and materials. Item 2 = the mechanic that produces genuine indistinguishability. Item 3 = payoff scheme and round structure, with the math in a single line. Item 4 = debrief, mapping specific moments from the play onto the ex-ante/ex-post split and onto the fact that your own strategy is what sets your credence about where you are. Item 5 = what predictably breaks the first time I run it and how I patch it mid-session, plus the one feature of the puzzle the activity can't reproduce that I'll have to supply by hand.

And commit — give me the single version you'd actually run, not a menu of three variants with tradeoffs for me to pick between. If you think something I asked for above is unrunnable or quietly wrong, say so inside the relevant item and fix it there instead of coming back with questions.
