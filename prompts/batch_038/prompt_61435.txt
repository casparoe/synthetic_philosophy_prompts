I'm in a reading group that has spent three weeks working through updatelessness, a mix of philosophy graduate students and two people who write agent code for a living. We agree on the shape of the doctrine: instead of conditioning on everything you've observed and then choosing the act that maximizes expected utility given that evidence, you choose the policy that maximizes expected utility as assessed from your prior, treating your own observations as outputs of the policy rather than as fixed background. Where we came apart is on how to explain it, and two analogies are now doing most of the work in the room. The first belongs to a senior student who thinks the whole business is deflationary: being updateless, he says, is just Ulysses having himself lashed to the mast, an agent binding itself in advance because it knows it will later occupy a position where its reasons look different. His punchline is that this shows updatelessness is a design principle wearing a decision theory's clothes. Precommitment delivers anything only if there was an earlier moment at which you could actually have bound yourself, and in the counterfactual mugging — the coin, the demand for a hundred dollars in one branch, the ten thousand in the other paid only if you would have handed over the hundred — there was no such moment, so the updateless verdict is either incoherent or plainly irrational. He adds, not unfairly, that everyone concedes you should precommit if you can, which leaves only the question of what to do if you didn't, and there the mast has nothing left to say. The second analogy belongs to someone defending the view, who says updatelessness is choosing behind a veil of ignorance over your own evidence: your various possible observational states are like parties deliberating jointly, and that is how we are meant to answer the objection that in the mugging the payoff lands only in a branch that never happened. You pay because all your possible selves settled the policy together. I have been asked to write up where the group landed, and I would rather not write it up until I know whether either analogy is entitled to the conclusion being drawn from it.

So: audit both. For the mast, give me the correspondence table explicitly — what plays Ulysses, the mast and the ropes, the sirens, the moment of lashing, the later self straining against them, the harbor he is trying to reach — and then separate the disanalogies that actually bear on the deflationary conclusion from the merely picturesque ones. I want you to say whether each of the following is fatal or survivable: that the ropes work by physically deleting actions from the later option set whereas updatelessness leaves the option set intact and changes which probabilities enter the evaluation, so the analogy may be mapping a constraint on action onto a claim about belief-relevance; that the ropes exist for Ulysses' own sake against a predicted shift in his desires, while in Newcomb-type problems the worth of a commitment depends on its being legible to a counterparty who models you, which changes what binding could even mean; that there is no earlier stage of the agent that exists without the evidence, so the prior self may be a formal device rather than someone who could have acted; and that Ulysses updates perfectly well while lashed, hearing the song and learning from it, which suggests the analogy is not about withholding evidence in the first place. For the veil, do the same: what plays the parties, the veil, the primary goods, the agreement condition, the return to the ordinary world — and then whether the veil's normative job, screening off information that is morally arbitrary in order to justify principles to a plurality of separate persons, can be transferred to the instrumental job of screening off information you cannot affect. Tell me whether treating counterfactual selves as parties with standing genuinely answers the objection that the benefit lands in a branch that did not happen, or merely restates it in moral vocabulary. State what each analogy quietly imports from its source that the target lacks.

Then the bottom line, in each case: does the senior student's conclusion — updatelessness collapses into precommitment and therefore has nothing to say to an agent already inside the problem — survive once the disanalogies are on the table, and does the veil answer the objection or relocate it? Finally, is there a better analogy at all? One of us floated a chess player who selects an entire strategy before seeing the opening rather than choosing move by move; another suggested a novelist fixing a character's dispositions instead of a character deliberating on the page. Say whether either does better, what each still fails to convey, and whether the honest answer is that this idea has no adequate analogy and should simply be presented formally. No bibliography, no paper titles, no name-dropping — argue from the cases themselves, and use the mugging, Newcomb's problem, and one deterrence case (following through on a costly threat after the provocation has already occurred) as your test bed.
