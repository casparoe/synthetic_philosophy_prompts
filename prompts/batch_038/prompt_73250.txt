I lead a small mechanistic-interpretability team at a company that ships large language models. For the better part of a year we have been deadlocked on a disagreement that turns out to matter for a concrete product decision — whether to deploy an interpretability-based monitor as a safety control, and what we would be entitled to claim it detects — and I would like you to draft the joint statement that both sides of my team could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The two sides, as I understand them:

Side A holds that our models literally contain representations. When a linear probe trained on mid-layer activations separates cases where the user's request conflicts with a system instruction from cases where it does not, and patching that direction reliably flips refusal behavior, A says the model represents that a conflict obtains. On A's account the direction is a discovery about the model, not an artifact of a choice we made.

Side B holds that "representation" here is a predictive instrument. On B's account all there is, in the case above, is a threshold-crossing in a gating circuit; the content we read off it is contributed by our labels and our probe, and the same weights would license a different content ascription under a different dictionary. B points to polysemantic units and to sparse-autoencoder features as evidence that what looks like a feature is largely a function of the decomposition method chosen, and worries that A's vocabulary will lead us to certify a monitor for a property it cannot guarantee.

I want the document built around two results we keep re-litigating. First, the conflict-direction result described above. Second, the observation that the direction generalizes: it decodes on paraphrases we never trained the probe on, and it licenses correct behavioral predictions in scenarios outside our decodable set. A says that extrapolation is precisely what distinguishes a representation with content from a correlation; B says extrapolation is what any well-fit linear readout of a high-dimensional circuit does and shows nothing about semantics.

We have also been fighting over an explicit criterion, and I want it handled clause by clause rather than replaced wholesale: a decoded direction counts as a genuine representation of content C only if (i) it is decodable across surface paraphrase and formatting variation of the same task, (ii) intervening on it changes behavior in the way C predicts, and (iii) it licenses correct predictions about held-out instances of C that the probe's own training data does not cover. For each clause, say which side accepts it, which rejects it, and whether either would accept a repaired version.

The document should contain: a numbered section of claims both sides can sign — about what the probing and patching results establish, about what they do not, and about the limits of our current methods; a table of residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording of its position and the other side would accept it as a fair statement of the opposing view; the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one side; and for each crux, a concrete experiment we could run on models we already have that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where you judge that part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part, and what the two sides would have to agree to say instead in order for a real disagreement to remain.

Two constraints. I have no philosophy training; people tell me this is a realism-versus-instrumentalism argument that philosophers have been having about minds for decades, but I do not want the document to depend on that lineage, and please do not include a bibliography or a reading list — it has to survive an engineering design review. Second, if you think I have drawn the fault line in the wrong place — for instance, if one of my two sides really contains two positions that should be separated, or if the disagreement worth having is about something other than the conflict-direction case — redraw it, but state explicitly what you changed and why the redrawn version is the one that can be settled.
