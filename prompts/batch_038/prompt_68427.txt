I am in the last weeks of an upper-level seminar on the metaphysics of consciousness, and this week's session concerns the timing of experience. Our instructor has asked each of us to circulate in advance a short statement of where we actually stand, so that the discussion begins from our own views rather than from the readings. A classmate suggested I have my statement stress-tested before I submit it, and that is what I would like done here.

The position, as I have written it down:

1. I am a physicalist about experience. There is nothing to an experience over and above neural activity, so every question about experience is ultimately a question about the brain, and the neural facts are as determinate as any other physical facts.
2. I accept the multiple-drafts picture. There is no finish line in the brain — no place-and-moment at which processing becomes conscious, and nothing like an inner theater at which a finished version of events is screened for somebody. Contents are fixed in parallel, at different times, in different channels.
3. In the color-phi case — two differently colored lights flashed in quick succession a short distance apart, with the subject reporting a single spot traveling between them and changing color partway — I favor the Stalinesque reading: the brain produces the motion-and-color-change content in real time, before any report, and nothing is rewritten afterwards. My reason is that talk of the record being revised after the fact only makes sense if there is somebody the revision must be concealed from, and that is exactly the picture I rejected in (2).
4. I want one account of temporal anomaly, not a disjunctive one. Whatever explains color phi should also explain the tactile cases in which taps delivered at two well-separated sites are felt as a series of taps at points in between them, and the Libet-style cases.
5. On Libet: I take the backward-referral result — the reported onset of the sensation is dated close to the stimulus, even though the cortical activity he treated as necessary for the experience had apparently to run its course first — as showing that the brain back-dates the record. On that case, then, I am an Orwellian.
6. I hold that there is a determinate fact about when a given experience occurs, and that this is in principle an empirical matter. I do not accept that the Stalinesque and Orwellian readings are beyond experimental discrimination: one should be able to establish when the relevant content became available by means other than a retrospective verbal report — forced-choice probes at very short latencies, or an online response that tracks the apparent spot as it moves.
7. Where a subject's first-person judgment about the timing of their own experience conflicts with third-person neural measurement, I give the report at least weak priority: it constrains the model rather than merely being explained away by it.
8. None of this is skeptical in import. I do not think the temporal-illusion cases show our phenomenology to be systematically illusory, and I do not think that describing the felt immediacy of the present as itself a construction amounts to saying that experience misrepresents anything.

I am deliberately not telling you which of these I am most attached to, since I would rather not bias the assessment.

What I want back is an audit, not a lecture and not a précis of the debate. Specifically: (a) identify which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, and rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad; (b) for each tension, say precisely what would have to be modified or abandoned, and where the cost falls — on my reading of the experiments, on my metaphysics, or on both; (c) tell me which pairs only look like tensions and say why they dissolve, since I expect that not everything that appears inconsistent here actually is; and (d) name the single revision that would buy me the most consistency for the least, and describe what residual problems would survive it. Finally, if any of my formulations is ambiguous in a way that matters to the verdict — readable either innocuously or damagingly — flag it and treat both readings separately rather than choosing for me.
