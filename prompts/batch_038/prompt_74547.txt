I'm auditing a graduate seminar in classical Chinese philosophy this term — I'm properly a philosophy-of-science student, and I'm sitting in mostly for the methodology — and our third-week discussion has stalled on a question that has been circling the field for decades: is the disagreement between Mencius and Xunzi over human nature an empirical one at all, or is it a dispute about how to carve up a body of observations that both sides already accept? Our instructor keeps pressing the point that the debate can only be moved forward by a case in which the two positions issue genuinely opposite predictions, and she has asked each of us to bring one to the next session. The examples circulated so far have been hopeless. A restorative-justice program, a Montessori preschool, a mutual-aid fund organized after a flood: in every one of them, a Mencian can say the arrangement worked because it finally gave the sprouts room to extend themselves, and a Xunzian can say it worked because it supplied the ritual form and the model that the sprouts were never going to generate unaided, and both are pointing at precisely the same facts with precisely the same satisfaction.

What I want from you is a single carefully specified example — a real institution or practice, or an invented one described in enough concrete detail that we can actually argue about it — where the two accounts come apart in the counterfactual sense: there is some particular feature of the case that could be withdrawn, and Mencius and Xunzi disagree about what happens once it is. Both sides should be able to agree on every observation the case has generated so far; the divergence should appear only in what each is committed to expecting after the withdrawal. Please name the feature exactly, state the two predictions separately, and then explain why each prediction follows from that side's specific account of human nature — from the doctrine that the incipient capacities for compassion, shame, deference, and discrimination are already present and merely require nourishment and extension, on the one hand, and from the doctrine that goodness is an artifact produced by deliberate activity and imposed from outside by ritual, teachers, and accumulated models, on the other — rather than from a general optimism or pessimism about people. That last step is where every earlier proposal in our seminar failed, since they all just restaged a temperament difference wearing ancient clothing.

Two further constraints. Keep the case outside the obvious territory: no classrooms, no prisons, no charities, no laboratory paradigms, and nothing from a therapeutic, workplace-training, or platform-moderation setting, since all of those come pre-loaded with the conclusion one wants. And once you have laid out the example and the two predictions, tell me what the side that loses the bet would have to give up — not merely adjust, but surrender. I am particularly interested in whether an example can be built with the standard escape hatches welded shut: the Mencian's retreat into the claim that a hostile or impoverished environment drowned the heart and starved what was there, and the Xunzian's retreat into the claim that the ritual was badly designed, or the teacher and model absent. If either maneuver remains available after the fact, the case proves nothing, so please state explicitly why, in your example, neither one is. Eight hundred words or so should be enough; write for an audience that has read both texts once and remembers the child at the well and the frame for straightening warped wood, but nothing more precise than that.
