You are a philosopher of science with genuine working familiarity with mechanistic interpretability — someone who has actually trained sparse autoencoders on the residual stream of a language model and run activation-patching experiments, and who can therefore evaluate a metaphysical claim against the details of the methodology rather than against a press-release version of it. Here is the position I want you to stress-test. It is my own, and I hold it with more confidence than my advisor thinks is warranted. I claim that trained neural networks have representations in a robustly realist sense: the features that interpretability work isolates are genuine kinds instantiated in the network, not observer-relative bookkeeping devices that we impose on an undifferentiated soup of activations, and not merely useful fictions along the lines of an intentional stance. More specifically — and this is the part of the view I want you to attack hardest, because it is where I think the realism actually earns its keep — I individuate features by an interventional equivalence criterion. Two internal directions, in the same network or across independently trained networks, realize the same feature if and only if they support the same profile of successful interchange interventions: patch them into matched contexts, and you get the same systematic changes in downstream behavior, with the same dependence on surrounding context. My argument for realism over instrumentalism is an indispensability argument in a broadly Quinean spirit: we are committed to these entities because our best causal-explanatory account of the network's behavior quantifies ineliminably over them, and because the criterion above is doing real work — it lets us correctly predict, in advance of running the experiment, which of two candidate feature directions will transfer and which will not. Note that on my view, the sparse-autoencoder latents themselves are only a fallible probe of this structure; the ontology is fixed by the interventional equivalence classes, not by whichever dictionary happens to fall out of a particular sparsity prior. That distinction is, I think, what saves the view from the obvious charge that monosemanticity is manufactured by the training objective of the probe.

What I want from you is a map of this position's exposure. Which findings, arguments, or methodological developments would count against it, and how decisively? Please sort your answer into tiers: fatal (the view could not survive it in anything like its current form), serious but survivable (would force a substantive reformulation), and merely awkward (an irritant a committed defender can absorb). For each tier, tell me explicitly which defeaters a sophisticated proponent of the view would themselves concede as counting against them — I want the honest concessions, not only what critics say — and which they would dismiss, and on what grounds. I am especially interested in whether there are empirical results that discriminate between my realism and its rivals, i.e. things my view predicts that an instrumentalist about features, or someone who thinks the relevant ontology is fixed by the training distribution rather than by the network, would predict differently; if there are no such discriminating predictions, say so plainly and explain what that implies about the view's content. Please also address several families of worry that have come up in conversation with friends, while not letting my list bound yours: the concern that sparsity-and-reconstruction priors manufacture the very monosemanticity they purport to discover, and that my equivalence-class move is an unfalsifiable retreat; the concern that successful interchange interventions may reflect overlap in the data distribution rather than shared structure in the model; findings of mixed selectivity and pervasive non-linearity, which seem to resist any clean partition into units with stable causal roles; the multiple-realizability worry that one "feature" is realized by heterogeneous mechanisms at different layers and scales, so that the equivalence classes come apart in exactly the cases that matter; and the historical analogy that this is a revival of faculty psychology or of phrenology, with the same localization-of-kinds ambitions and the same eventual fate. Finally, give me a candid verdict on insulation: has this position been qualified into a form that risks nothing, and if so, is that a defect for a view of this kind — a view about the ontology of an artifact we can, in principle, inspect exhaustively — or is insulation here benign? Do not flatter the position. If you conclude that it is largely insulated and that this is a genuine problem, that is a perfectly acceptable answer, and I would rather hear it now.
