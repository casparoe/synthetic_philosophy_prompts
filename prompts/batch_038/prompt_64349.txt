I've been going around in circles on the Fresnel drag coefficient thing lately — you know, the bit where the luminiferous ether turns out not to exist, but the formula Fresnel derived from it (and Fizeau's water-tube result) gets carried straight into relativity with the ether quietly deleted. The math is load-bearing, the ontology was a ghost. Anyway, I think that's a story premise and I want you to write it.

Here's the setup I've got, though move the furniture around however you like: an auditor or translator is contracted to go through a defunct but wildly successful technical tradition — a navigation school, a surveying guild, whatever feels alive — and mark line by line which parts were "really about the structure of the world" and which were false scaffolding that happened to work. The trouble is the marking can only be checked once the successor theory shows up, and a rival auditor demonstrates that for any batch of surviving predictions you can always invent relations among the observable stuff that reproduce them, so his ledger certifies exactly nothing. I want to feel the vertigo of that rather than be told about it.

So: real scenes, a person with a deadline and a bad boss and coffee going cold, not two philosophers talking in a room. About 1200 words. And please don't let it resolve into a tidy verdict on whether the ledger was worth keeping — I already know how that one ends and I'd rather it didn't.
