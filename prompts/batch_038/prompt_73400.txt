Here's what I hold:

1. Psychiatric categories aren't natural kinds. There's no joint there waiting to be carved; the DSM is a negotiated document, closer to a tariff schedule than to a periodic table.
2. That's not the same as saying they're made up though. When I went for my assessement and the clinician said ADHD, that got something right about me — it wasn't just a stipulation or a useful fiction.
3. My reason for (1) is looping: classify people and they change in response to being classified, so these are interactive kinds, unlike (say) kinds of rock. That's what blocks the natural-kind reading.
4. And yet the field has genuinely got better over time. Homosexuality coming out, the drift toward spectra and dimensions instead of hard boxes — that's the DSM converging on something, not just redecorating.
5. For me validity is a matter of clinical utility and predictive power, not of finding a biomarker. I don't think we're waiting on neuroscience to settle which diagnoses are real. The usual validity criteria — family studies, outcome at follow-up, clean boundaries with neighbouring diagnoses — are the right sort of thing.
6. I'm also firmly on the side of people owning their labels. "I am autistic" as an identity, and advocacy that spreads and broadens the category — that's emancipatory and I don't want to give it up.
