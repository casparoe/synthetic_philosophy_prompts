Week one of my mental causation seminar and two students reached for 'the physical is causally closed' to end an argument about whether a belief can cause an arm to go up. One treated it as a straight entailment — every such movement already has a sufficient physical cause, so nothing mental is doing any causing — and the other said closure was just conservation of energy in a trenchcoat. I'd rather hand out a page that makes both of them slow down.

Can you set out the distinct claims that slogan could be standing for, ordered by strength: closure versus completeness, token versus type, cause versus sufficient cause, the physical as described by the physics we have versus by some finished physics, and the version thats no empirical claim at all but a methodological rule about where to look for explanations? For each, say whether it's true, trivially true, or an empirical bet we might lose, and whether conservation of energy is the sort of thing that could support it. Then tell me which of them, together with exactly what extra premises, actually delivers 'the mental causes nothing', and flag anywhere a no-overdetermination assumption is being smuggled in rather than argued for. Finally, does anything change if causation is spelled out counterfactually or in interventionist terms rather than as sufficient-cause production?

British English please, and a numbered list I can photocopy.
