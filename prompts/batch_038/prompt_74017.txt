I'm doing the intro and Q&A for a talk in our dept's graduate series next Thursday. The speaker works on Wittgenstein's On Certainty and defends a pretty hardline hinge view — roughly: the sceptic is right that our hinge commitments (that the earth existed long before I was born, that testimony is broadly reliable, that objects don't quietly stop existing when nobody's looking) can't be given evidential support, but that this isn't a shortcoming, because hinges aren't in the business of being evidenced — they're the framework that makes evidential assessment possible in the first place. She also takes the communitarian line that an individual can't rationally acquire or shed a hinge on her own, since hinges come bundled with a form of life, and she's been arguing in print that climate deniers and vaccine refusers often aren't making errors at all, they're just running a different hinge set, so the right response isnt more data.

I want eight questions in a sensible order of escalation, and for each one a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. Heads up: she'll reach for some version of "you're asking for the wrong kind of support — you can't stand outside the framework and audit it," so the follow-ups need to be ones she can't bat away with that move.

Please make sure the set covers:

- whether hinges are genuinely propositions we believe, or rules of grammar dressed up as claims — and what her account loses if the honest answer is rules;
- the closure problem: if a hinge can't be known, how can anything resting on it be known, given that knowledge looks like it closes under known entailment — and why she doesn't just have to bite that bullet;
- what her view actualy buys against the sceptic that plain Mooreanism doesn't, if the sceptic was right all along about the missing warrant;
- the relativism charge, specifically what resources she has for saying a flat-earther is wrong rather than differently-hinged, and whether "different hinge set" is turning into an unfalsifiable alibi for anyone;
- whether hinges have ever been revised inside a scientific community that kept functioning throughout, and what that does to the inheritance story;
- how a child, or a religious convert, ends up with hinges at all if never by evidential uptake;
- the present-day case, where generated images and coordinated fabrication seem to be actively eroding commitments like "photographs are generally reliable" — does her framework predict or explain that, or is it silent on hinge decay?

Then close with a question about what evidence or argument could possibly count against the view, plus a follow-up for if she says it isn't an empirical theory and so isn't in that business. No "what first drew you to Wittgenstein" filler — everything should turn on the content of the position.
