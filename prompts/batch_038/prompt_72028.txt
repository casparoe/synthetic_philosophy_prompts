I manage a group home for teens aging out of foster care. Last week one of the kids told another staff member that Malik, our best counselor, has been taking money from a family to move their kid up the list for the independent-living slots. It's second hand, the kid who said it has made up stories before when he wanted attention, and Malik's paperwork has always been spotless. The catch is I can't look into it without tiping him off — an audit lands on his desk and he'll know exactly why — and if he thinks we suspect him he walks, which the kids can't absorb right now. My deputy says I'm not entitled to believe either way until I've got proof, and that becuase the other kids are the ones exposed, I should believe he's guilty and act accordingly until he's cleared. I keep feeling like something in her argument is crooked, but I also know Malik's the one I'd want to be innocent. Am I just believing what's convenient here, or has she actually got something wrong?
