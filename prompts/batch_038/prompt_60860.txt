I write about science and ideas for a magazine, but the vocabulary I actually think in comes from about a decade of data journalism: panels and cross-sections, time series, missingness and imputation, right-censoring, unbalanced panels, data vintages and revisions, nowcasting, prediction intervals. I'm fluent enough in it that I can't really think outside it anymore.

Can you restate the presentism vs. eternalism debate — including the growing block option — in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, term by term rather than as one big loose metaphor? My rough sketch is: presentism is only the current observation is real, eternalism is the whole panel is there whether or not anyone has observed any of it, growing block is an append-only log that keeps gaining rows. But don't just decorate the sketch, and don't let my framework's own assumptions do the work and then call it translation.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. What translates cleanly, what you had to bend to make fit, and what has no expression at all? Two spots I'd like you to press on. One: my language can say a datapoint is missing, revised, or not yet collected, but can it say a time doesnt exist, as opposed to just isn't observed? If it can't, is that a hole in my vocabulary or a sign the metaphysicians are having a verbal dispute — and what would settle which? Two: a prediction interval treats the future as a draw from a distribution whose parameters are already fixed, which sounds suspiciously like the future is already out there. Is my framework quietly eternalist without admitting it, and did I smuggle that in or did the translation?
