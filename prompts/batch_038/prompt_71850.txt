I am building a case for an advanced seminar on the moral emotions, and I need it engineered rather than merely illustrative. Please construct a thought experiment to the following specification, and then demonstrate that it meets every clause.

The target: a single agent in a single realistic situation for whom shame is the morally better response to her own wrongdoing than guilt — the reverse of the standard ranking, which makes guilt the paradigmatically moral emotion on the ground that it takes the agent's own act as its object, issues in repair, and respects the separateness of persons, while demoting shame as an emotion about the worth of the self and therefore hostage to appearances.

Specification:

1. Setting: contemporary and mundane — a hospital, a firm, a school, a family. No science fiction, no amnesia, no duplicated persons, no stipulated neurochemistry. The scenario must be self-contained, and its stakes must be legible to undergraduates who have not read the literature; no piece of technical vocabulary may do work inside the scenario itself.

2. The guilt in the case must be accurate, well-calibrated, and fully discharged. The agent correctly identifies what she did, confesses it, accepts the sanction, makes restitution to whoever is in a position to receive it, and apologizes to those able to accept the apology. She may not be neurotic, self-deceived, or blaming herself for the wrong thing, and the case may not work by making repair unavailable or by having the apology refused. If guilt comes out badly here only because it is being badly handled, the case fails.

3. The shame may not be reputational: not embarrassment at being seen, not dread of exposure, not loss of standing, not shame at having been found out. If there is an audience, its view must be one the agent has good reason to hold in esteem, and the shame must survive the audience's disappearance.

4. Shame and guilt must be genuinely available alternatives for the same agent at the same time, and the difference between them must show up in what she goes on to do, or in how she stands toward the person she harmed — not merely in the felt quality of her inner experience.

5. The scenario proper is to be one paragraph. Nothing in it may assert the conclusion: the verdict has to fall out of the two emotions' differing objects and differing action-guidance, or it does not count.

Then the demonstration:

- State the guilt-favoring account's verdict on the case and its reasoning, and say whether that account is actually wrong here or merely incomplete.
- State the shame-favoring reading, and name the precise feature of the case that makes shame do better moral work.
- Deletion test: remove that feature, report what happens to the verdict, and say whether the case collapses back into one in which guilt is superior.
- Give the strongest reply available to a partisan of the standard ranking — for instance, that what I am calling superior shame is really guilt with a wider temporal range, or guilt plus a standing disposition the guilt account can claim as its own — and assess whether that reply absorbs the case or merely redescribes it.
- Close with a paragraph on generalization: if a community adopted shame as its default sanction for wrongs of this kind, would the feature that makes shame work here survive the adoption, or does it depend on shame's being an uncoerced, individual response?

If the clauses turn out to be jointly unsatisfiable, do not quietly relax one. Tell me which clause has to give, and show that any case satisfying the others forces the target verdict to fail.
