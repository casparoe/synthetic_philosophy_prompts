I'm a software engineer with no philosophy training, and I got into an argument I can't close. We ship a churn-prediction model whose internals I know to be fiction: it represents each customer as a single latent engagement score that decays smoothly over time, when the actual driver is a handful of discrete events — a failed card charge, a competitor's outage, a pricing email. When I objected that the assumptions are false, a colleague answered with Milton Friedman's 1953 essay on the methodology of positive economics: realism of assumptions is not a criterion at all, predictive accuracy is the only one, and the more significant a theory is, the more unrealistic its assumptions tend to be. I tried to write his position out rigorously, and it proves far too much.

(1) A model is to be judged solely by how accurately it predicts the phenomenon it is offered to explain; the realism of its assumptions is not a separate criterion.
(2) So a model with false assumptions that predicts the phenomenon accurately is as good a theory as any rival that predicts it accurately.
(3) For any phenomenon and any finite set of observations of it, I can construct a model with one free parameter per observation that reproduces every observation exactly.
(4) Such a model assumes nothing beyond the numbers themselves, so its assumptions are about as unrealistic as assumptions get; by (1) and (2) it is as good a theory of the phenomenon as anything else on offer.
(5) It is not: it is silent about every observation not already in its table, and a model that cannot do that is not a model.
(6) Therefore (1) is false, and realism of assumptions must be a genuine criterion after all.

Where does it break? I don't want to surrender (1) — nearly everything I ship is a model whose mechanism I don't believe — so I'd like to know which step is doing the illegitimate work, why that step looks perfectly acceptable in isolation, and what an instrumentalist gives up if he cannot have it. If the repair is that (1) was always about accuracy on cases the model was not built to accommodate, tell me whether that is a clarification of the thesis or a different thesis, and whether the remark about significance going along with unrealistic assumptions survives it.
