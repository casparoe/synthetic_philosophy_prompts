**The stipulated case.** Consider an economy with the following features, all of which are stipulated and not to be argued with:

1. All material production — extraction, refining, component fabrication, assembly, maintenance, repair, and the construction of replacement equipment — is carried out by machine systems without any human labor input, direct or indirect.
2. Those machine systems were originally built by wage laborers several generations ago. The value of the original equipment has since been fully consumed and transferred into successive generations of machines, each generation built by the one before it.
3. There is no wage labor anywhere in production. Nobody sells labor power. There is consequently no variable capital at all.
4. The machine systems are privately owned. Their owners receive the consumer output as revenue; everyone else lives on a public transfer financed by taxing that output. Goods are still produced as commodities and still exchange at money prices.
5. Nothing turns on natural scarcity or on rent: energy and mineral inputs are abundant and unowned.

The intuitive pull of the case is that this looks like exploitation of the most extreme kind — one class appropriates the entire social product while everyone else subsists on a transfer. The theoretical pull is the opposite: if only living labor creates new value, and machinery merely transfers value it already embodies, then in this economy no new value is created at all, the rate of profit is zero, and the concept of surplus value has nothing to fasten onto. Something in the neighborhood of this is what Marx's notebooks on machinery in the Grundrisse gesture at when he suggests that once general social knowledge becomes the main productive force, production resting on the measure of labor time and hence on exchange value collapses of its own accord.

**The proposition whose credence I want.** Call it **P**: *On the correct interpretation of Marx's mature theory of value, the stipulated economy contains no exploitation in the strict Marxian sense — no surplus value is produced in it — and the theory delivers this as a determinate result rather than falling silent, becoming indeterminate, or ceasing to apply.*

Note the second clause. P is not simply "there is no surplus value here." P is the conjunction of that claim with the claim that Marx's framework is actually equipped to say so about this case.
