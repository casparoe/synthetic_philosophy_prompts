My neighbor Dara volunteers for the state bird atlas, which means every winter she drives the same fixed route around the county and writes down every raven she sees, plus a running list of every other bird. My coworker Wes took a couple philosophy classes years ago and has never let that go. He was over for chili last month, saw her logbook on the counter, and they went at it for close to two hours while I refilled drinks and took notes on my phone, mostly because I kept losing track of who was ahead. I'm hoping you can referee it for me, because I don't think either of them was just being stupid.

It went roughly like this. (1) Wes asked what would count against Dara's working claim, which was that every raven in the county is black. She said an albino or white one, obviously. He said then her claim is really the same claim as "anything that isn't black isn't a raven," so every time she walks past her own white fridge and it fails to be a raven, she's confirmed her hypothesis a tiny bit — and he said this is a famous old puzzle, Hempel's raven paradox, and that the mature response isn't to deny it but to admit the fridge counts, just barely. (2) Dara said the fridge counting at all is the problem, not the solution. Her route takes two winters of Saturdays, and if a fridge is evidence then evidence has stopped being about ravens and become a matter of how much non-black junk exists, so she asked him what his account would say if the universe just had ten times as much stuff in it. Would her actual ravens count for less? Wes said basically yes, a black raven confirms the claim more in a universe with fewer objects in it, and that's a feature, not a bug. (3) Dara said that makes the quality of her data depend on facts about shoes and fridges that she can't measure and has no opinion about, so "confirmation" in his sense is just bookkeeping wearing a logic costume. Wes said confirmation was always relative to what you already know, and since she already knows her fridge isn't a raven, looking at it teaches her nothing and confirms nothing — the paradox only bites for somebody who doesn't yet know what's in the universe, which is why he'd said "just barely," and in practice barely is zero. (4) Dara said if it's zero in practice he's given her the point and just doesn't want to say so out loud, and then she brought out the part I still can't stop thinking about: her other-bird list has a lot of black crows and black grackels on it. Under Wes's rule, a black crow is not a non-black non-raven, so it confirms nothing whatsoever. So the white fridge is evidence and a black crow sitting forty feet from a raven nest is not, which she said is backwards from anything a normal person would call evidence, seeing as a crow is about as close to a raven as a bird gets and shares the whole pigmentation setup. (5) Wes said that's not backwards, that's the point: the hypothesis is about ravens, not about similar-looking things, and a crow tells you nothing about ravens unless you already assume corvids share some pigment gene — and if you do assume that, the evidence is the assumption, not the crow. He said Dara was smuggling in a second hypothesis and then admiring it as an intuition. (6) Dara said fine, run your own move through the same filter: my fridge only confirms ravens-are-black if I already assume the fridge could have turned out to be a non-black raven, which I emphatically don't assume. So the crow and the fridge are both doing their work through background assumptions, and Wes gets to keep his assumptions invisible while hers get called smuggling. He said there was an asymmetry he could spell out, then the chili ran out and the night ended. Nobody budged.

So: can you go through those six moves one at a time and say who's right at each one — or whether it's a wash — instead of just telling me the standard textbook verdict on the whole puzzle? I'd especially like to know whether move 4 is a real problem for Wes's position or whether he actually handled it in move 5, and whether his comeback in move 6 (I mean Dara's comeback) lands or whether there is a genuine asymmetry between the two background assumptions the way Wes claimed. Then tell me what neither of them said that they should have. My hunch is that half the fight was about what the word "evidence" is allowed to mean rather than about ravens, but I couldn't get either of them to admit that, and maybe I'm the one who's confused.
