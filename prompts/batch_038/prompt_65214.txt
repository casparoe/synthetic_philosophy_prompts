I've been working through Parfit's repugnant conclusion on my own, and there is one step I cannot see why anyone would refuse to take. The argument ends with a population enormously larger than ours whose members lead lives that are barely worth living, and it tells us that world would be better than the one we have. But if an outcome is better, that is precisely what there is reason to bring about: so each of us should have as many children as we can support, and the more people who do so, the nearer we get to the better world. Yet everything I have read treats the conclusion as a catastrophe for total utilitarianism rather than as a practical imperative that follows from it. Am I missing a premise in that reasoning? If not, is the resistance to the repugnant conclusion anything more than a distaste for crowded, modest lives?
