Act as an expert in the technical and philosophical literature on process-based versus outcome-based supervision of AI systems, with working knowledge of the adjacent evaluation literature on proxy measures and on what is sometimes called monitorability. Think carefully before answering: begin by brainstorming a list of candidate concepts that might cover what I describe, and only then judge them against the case.

I am a novelist. For eleven years I taught an advanced fiction workshop whose signature exercise was the revision memo: with every draft, the student submitted a short account of why they had made each change — the problem diagnosed, the alternatives tried, the alternative rejected and why. The memo was weighted more heavily than the story, on the reasoning that craft can be taught and voice cannot.

Two things happened. The first is unremarkable: students acquired the vocabulary of the memo, memos improved, stories did not. I can describe that myself — a proxy being optimized in place of the thing. The second is what I need a name for. Over the years the memos stopped being reports of anything. The students were not lying, and they were not withholding a better private account of their own choices; when I asked in person what had led to a particular cut, they reconstructed something plausible on the spot, and the reconstruction was exactly as sincere as the memo had been. The requirement that the process be made legible produced fluent legible process with nothing behind it — and no residue of uncoached intention left to compare it against. I later saw the same shape in my own practice: after I began writing craft notes about my drafts for a column, I started making choices in order to have something to say about them, and then writing the note first and treating the story as its illustration.

I have since read the argument that supervising a system's stated reasoning is safer than supervising its output, because reasoning is harder to fake than results. My question is whether what I watched happen in that workshop is a recognized failure mode of that argument.

1. Is there an established concept for supervision that does not merely shift behavior toward the proxy, but manufactures the appearance of the underlying process while emptying it? Give me the nearest candidates, from the AI-supervision literature and from the wider evaluation literature, and for each say what it captures and what it misses.
2. Distinguish the thing carefully from ordinary proxy gaming, from post-hoc confabulation, and from the worry that a supervised agent will conceal its real reasoning from a monitor. In my case there may be nothing to conceal, which seems to make the situation worse rather than better — say whether you agree and why.
3. If nothing fits, coin a term, define it, and mark its edges: a borderline case, and a clear non-instance.
4. State whether this counts as evidence that process supervision is self-undermining in general, or only under conditions resembling my workshop's — and specify those conditions.

I want a carving, not a label. Do not answer with a single term or a list of sources; draw the distinctions explicitly.
