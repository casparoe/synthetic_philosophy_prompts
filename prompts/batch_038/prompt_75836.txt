I'm a value-theory PhD student preparing materials for a departmental workshop, and I'd like you to draft the joint statement that two adversaries could in good conscience both sign after a real adversarial collaboration over the spectrum arguments against the transitivity of betterness. Please fix the parties precisely rather than generically: on one side, a single-scale consequentialist who holds that all-things-considered betterness is a genuine transitive ordering and that cyclic betterness is excluded by dominance-style and money-pump considerations; on the other, an intransitivist in the Temkin/Rachels line who holds that betterness aggregates aspect-relative relations whose weights shift across a spectrum, so that adjacent comparisons and endpoint comparisons can pull in opposite directions without any of them being mistaken.

The document should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, including what each grants about a hangnail-versus-torture-style spectrum — whether every adjacent pair is genuinely comparable, whether there is a sharp cutoff, and whether the intuition of intransitivity is evidence about betterness or about the psychology of eliciting it; (2) a disagreements table giving each residual disagreement in wording its own side would accept, with an explicit flag on any part of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal — in particular, whether it is a fight about how many relations the phrase "better than" names; (3) the cruxes, each paired with a concrete case or argument both parties agree bears on it and with each side's prediction recorded in advance, including at least one case built to separate the claim that the weights shift from the claim that the comparison is vague; and (4) for each side, what would count as evidence against its own position, and an honest note on whether anything on that list would actually move it.

One constraint on tone: no agreed claim may be softened to the point where either party loses the substance of its view. Where the two genuinely cannot sign the same sentence, say so in the table instead of papering over it.
