I want the specification before I look at any proposals. The target is not a semantics for English but a normative account of what an agent's doxastic state must contain if it includes conditional beliefs: given a fixed conversational context and an agent with coherent, non-extreme credences, the account should say what believing "If the match is struck, it lights" amounts to, what probability — if any — attaches to it, and how that probability moves when the agent learns something else. Two further constraints on the target, which shape which desiderata matter: the same conditional may be assessed differently by two coherent agents who share the context, so whatever the account posits must be able to vary with the agent without collapsing into mere psychology; and the state it posits must be usable in deliberation, not merely reportable.

Write the desiderata such an account must meet, each with the case or consideration that motivates it and a verdict on whether it is non-negotiable or a commitment of one camp. Cover at least: the inference patterns it must validate or explain away — modus ponens, contraposition, transitivity, import-export — together with the counterexamples that drive each verdict; the probability constraints, including the Equation P(A→C) = P(C|A), the requirement that the Equation hold again after conditionalizing on arbitrary further evidence, and the requirement that the credal space not be trivial; and the usage data on assertability, on antecedents the agent already believes false or already believes true, and on the apparent irrelevance of some true connections.

Then the part I most want: identify the minimal subset of these requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, given the triviality results and any further result that forces a choice among the inference principles. Say which assumption in that subset does the work, and which single requirement — weakened rather than dropped — buys the most while leaving the rest intact. Note any requirement that turns out to be doing no work at all.

Finish briefly, no table: one or two sentences each on how a material-conditional treatment, a selection-semantics account with truth conditions, a non-truth-conditional conditional-credence view, and a contextualist or restrictor account fare against the list.
