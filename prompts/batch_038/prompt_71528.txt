Been chewing on this for a few weeks after rereading the animals material in Anarchy, State, and Utopia, and I think I've built myself an arguement that proves way too much. Roughly, as I reconstruct him:

(1) Nozick's own reason for side constraints on persons is that there's no social entity that experiences, so aggregating across people trades away the separateness of each individual life.
(2) Chickens, pigs, mice and so on are separate experiencers too; there's no animal-total that benefits from the sacrifice either.
(3) So the very consideration that generates constraints for people generates them for anything sentient, and the two-tier proposal — utilitarian treatment for animals, Kantian treatment for people — is refuted by its own foundation.
(4) His escape, as I read it, is to make the weight of the constraint depend on capacities of the being: something like self-consciousness, free will, an orientation to a long-term future you shape for yourself, moral agency.
(5) But scaling inviolability by capacities is just weighing the interests of the more capable against the inviolability of the less capable under a new label — consequentialism with extra steps, so the constraints do no independent work.
(6) Worse, the criterion cuts the wrong way: moral agency is the capacity to violate constraints, so its maximal in the ordinary competent adult (including the one who respects nobody's rights) and absent in infants, the severely disabled, and every nonhuman animal.
(7) Hence the beings who most clearly have the capacity that earns inviolability are exactly the ones least deserving of it, and the harmless are the violable.
(8) So either rights are unweighted — in which case you may not kill a wasp that lands on your sandwich, nor do mosquito control, which nobody including Nozick accepts — or they're weighted, in which case (5) through (7) bite and the whole edifice collapses into the utilitarianism he set out to refute.
(9) Therefore his position on animals isn't merely unstable, which is the impression he leaves, but incoherent.

I want to keep (2), and I'm quite attached to (8) as a dilemma. Which step should I reject, why did it look so innocent going in, and what does rejecting it cost? In particular: is there a version of weighting that isn't aggregation, and how much of this survives if the list of capacities is read as tracking something other than the ability to violate constraints?
