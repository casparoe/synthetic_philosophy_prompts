I co-teach a seminar on Molyneux's problem alongside a vision-science lab, and the lab members consistently recast the case in cross-modal transfer terms: the man's lifelong tactile and verbal knowledge of shapes is a pretrained representation, sight arrives as a new modality for which no paired data exists, and the question of whether he can name the sphere is simply the question of whether zero-shot cross-modal evaluation can succeed. Since alignment between modalities has to be learned from paired examples, they hold, the answer must be no — and philosophers who answer yes are confused about what transfer requires. Please audit the comparison. Give me the correspondences explicitly (what plays the pretrained encoder, the label space, the paired training set, the fine-tuning, the held-out test), then separate the disanalogies that actually bear on their conclusion from those that are merely decorative. In particular: does the analogy show that no transfer is possible, or does it presuppose a claim about how the man's shape knowledge is represented that is itself the thing in dispute? And if the comparison cannot carry the verdict, what would be a better one for the same problem?
