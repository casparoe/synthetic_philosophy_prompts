Christopher Nolan's Memento is nearly always discussed as a puzzle about memory, personal identity, or unreliable narration, but I think it is a sharper test case for Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument, and I want a reading that treats it as one. Leonard's killing of Teddy has, at every stage, a complete physical story — the tattoos, the notes, the dossier, the conditioning rehearsed through the Sammy Jankis material — and the reversed colour sequences let us watch that story assemble the act long before any reason of Leonard's is voiced. Yet the near-final sequence also shows him deliberately engineering the evidence he will later find compelling, choosing what his future self will take to be conclusive. Read the film through the exclusion problem: are Leonard's reasons doing genuine causal work, or are they post-hoc glosses on a process that is causally closed without them? In particular, take up the difference-making reply, on which a mental property is a cause if intervening on it would change the outcome, and ask whether Leonard's behaviour is counterfactually sensitive to the content of what he believes, or whether, given the conditioning the film implies, he would have shot Teddy under almost any content. Say plainly where the film resists the reading — a reading that makes Memento a tidy illustration of a philosophy paper is probably a bad reading — and end by saying what the film contributes to the debate that the argument alone cannot supply. I want a committed verdict, not a survey of the options: does Memento dramatize epiphenomenalism, or does it show from the inside what non-reductive mental causation would look like? Give that verdict in your first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
