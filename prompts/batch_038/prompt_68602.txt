Longtime reader of the usual rationalist blogs, been going through this stuff since the sequences, and I've accumulated a bunch of half-formed observations that I've never been able to file anywhere. Not an academic, so my vocabulary is whatever the internet gave me. Here's one I keep running into, both in my own arguments and watching other people's, and I genuinely cannot tell whether it's a known thing under a name I've missed or whether I'm describing a shape that doesn't exist yet.

Case one. Me and a friend, both data people, overlapping reading lists, roughly matched in how much I'd trust each other's judgment. We spent about a year arguing about whether a famous social-science finding holds up. We did the whole Double Crux ritual, wrote the cruxes out in a shared doc, sent each other every paper, agreed on base rates, agreed on how much weight a preregistered study gets versus an observational one, agreed on what would settle it. And at the end we were exactly as far apart as at the start, which is not the weird part. The weird part is *how* we got there. Somewhere around month three each of us started treating the other's continued disagreement as the primary evidence about the other's process. My reasoning went: he has now seen everything I've seen and still lands there, so something must be wrong in how he's processing it, so when he tells me "I read that replication and it didn't move me," that report itself isn't trustworthy — and he was running the exact mirror image of that on me. So every new piece of evidence got routed through a judgment about the other person's reliability, and that judgment had itself been produced by the fact of the disagreement. The loop eats whatever you feed it. Throwing more data in doesn't shrink the gap, it just generates more datapoints confirming the meta-judgment. Case two, slightly different flavor: a bunch of people in my local group arguing about AI timelines, two of whom are peers in every sense the literature cares about — same evidence, same smarts, neither one obviously motivated, both of them totally willing to flip on adjacent questions where the answer is visible. Same structure, and notably it does *not* feel like the framework-level kind of clash, they'd both happily sign the same methodology statement. Each concludes the other has a hidden variable they can't locate, and the failure to locate it gets read as proof the other person is confabulating their own reasons.

I know the neighborhood and none of the landmarks fit cleanly, which is why I'm asking. Aumann's agreement theorem says rational agents with a common prior can't agree to disagree, but the standard escapes — private information, or the posteriors not being common knowledge — don't apply here, we genuinely pooled everything and it was mutually obvious that we had. The priors debate is tempting, "aha, different priors," but that feels like a black box in these cases: if you asked either of us cold, out of the other's presence, we'd give the same prior; the priors only seem to diverge when we're in contact. Fogelin-style deep disagreement doesn't fit either, because these fights are shallow and specific and they survive total agreement on the framework stuff. And the peer disagreement material I've read is all about a single round — what should I do upon discovering a peer dissents — while I'm after the iterated thing, where the outcome of round N becomes an input to round N+1 and the whole process has a fixed point that isn't agreement. So: does this have a name, in game theory or social epistemology or wherever? And please don't just hand me a label and stop, the label is the least interesting part. I want the carving — a definition crisp enough that I could apply it to a new case and get a yes or no, the nearest established concepts with the specific difference from each spelled out, and where the edges are (does it still count if one of them really *is* biased and the other correctly spots it? does it count if there's genuine private info that just looks like the loop from outside? does the loop need mutual symmetry, or can one person be in it alone?). If there's no established term, coin one and give me the definition plus a test for when it's this versus an ordinary stubborn disagreement. No need to point me at papers or reading lists, I can't chase references anyway — concepts and distinctions only. And if you think my framing is a mess, that I've mashed two unrelated phenomena into one description, or that the loop I'm talking about is just some known failure mode wearing a hat, say that plainly instead of playing along politely; I'd much rather be told the second example isn't the same thing as the first.
