So at my school you can change your elective during the first three weeks, but every switch costs a $20 late-add fee. My friend Nour switched out of chemistry into photography in week one, out of photography into debate in week two, and then out of debate back into chemistry in week three. Same schedule she started with, sixty dollars gone.

Two other friends got into it about this at lunch and it went like four rounds and I couldnt tell who was winning:

1. Devin said that proves Nour's preferences go in a circle, and circular preferences are irrational. He said if some guy had been standing in the hallway offering to trade her classes for twenty bucks a pop he could have drained her all semester, so she's basically a walking ATM.
2. Rae said none of the individual switches were bad ones. Every time, Nour picked the class she wanted more at that moment. Her thing was that you cant call each step fine and then call the whole trip a mistake, because the whole trip was never something Nour chose — it's just what the steps add up to.
3. Devin said it *was* chosen, just spread out. By the third switch she knew perfectly well she was landing back in chemistry, so she was picking the pattern then even if she couldn't see it in week one. He said anyone who saw the fees coming would have sat still.
4. Rae said okay, but sitting still means keeping a class you like worse right now in order to protect the wallet of some future version of you. So Devin's asking her to choose the thing she prefers less and then calling that rational just because it makes a tidier shape.
5. Devin said the sixty dollars is the whole proof. She has the identical schedule and sixty dollars less. Nobody with coherent preferences does that to themselves.
6. Rae said the money is a red herring — people lose sixty dollars while being totally rational all the time, bad luck, expensive tastes, whatever. And then she said something I'm not sure about: that maybe there's no circle at all, because the debate Nour picked in week three, once she'd found out the team was going to state, isnt the same option she turned down in week one. Different options at different times, so nothing ever actually got compared to itself.
7. Devin said that's an escape hatch. If you're allowed to re-describe the options every time someone looks inconsistent, nobody could ever be caught in a circle and the whole argument is unfalsifiable.

Can you go through this move by move and tell me who's actually right at each step, not just who won overall? I especially want to know whether point 6 is a real answer or whether Devin's escape hatch objection kills it, and whether point 5 does the work he thinks it does.

Also, for what it's worth, Nour isnt embarrassed about any of it. She says she got to sit in on photography and debate, found out she genuinely wants chemistry, and thinks sixty dollars for that is a fine deal. Does that change the verdict on either of them, or is it beside the point? And is there some third position or distinction that both of them are just missing entirely?
