I have no academic training in philosophy — I spent my working life in a materials testing lab and came to these questions through the bench rather than through a syllabus. I've settled on a position about dispositions that I want audited before I write it up for a local discussion group, because I suspect something in it does not hold and I cannot see what. Here is what I believe:

1. Fragility is a fact about how a thing is built. The fragility of a glass just is its molecular structure; there is nothing further there. Two structurally identical glasses are identically fragile, and no rearranging of their surroundings can make one more fragile than the other.

2. Nevertheless I accept the fink verdicts. A live wire hooked to a device arranged to cut the current the instant anything is conducted through it is not, while so hooked, disposed to conduct when touched; and its mirror image — a dead wire hooked to a device arranged to energize it on contact — is not disposed to conduct either, even though it plainly would conduct if touched. I take the lesson to be that a disposition can be switched on and off by what stands around the thing, with nothing happening inside it.

3. Masks are the same story in homelier dress: a glass shipped in molded foam is still fragile, and the foam merely stops the breaking.

4. Finks and masks differ in kind, and the difference matters. In a mask the disposition survives and is blocked; in a fink it is destroyed. Any account that cannot state that difference is leaving something out.

5. On evidence I hold a strict rule: nothing counts for or against a disposition except what happens when you trigger it. Structure, theory, and analogy may suggest a hypothesis, but only the triggering settles it.

6. Dormant dispositions are no mystery. Something can be disposed in a way that never manifests anywhere in its history.

Please go through these pairwise and tell me which pairs genuinely conflict and which only look as though they do, ordering the real conflicts by severity. For each real conflict, say which commitment has to be surrendered or weakened, what the cheapest acceptable weakening is, and what that weakening costs me elsewhere in the list — I want the price of the repair, not a tour of the field. And if two of my conflicts turn out to be a single problem underneath, treat them as one rather than billing me twice.
