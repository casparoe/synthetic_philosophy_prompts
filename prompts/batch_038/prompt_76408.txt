I'm a junior and I sit on our student council's traditions committee. Every semester we get three or four proposals about school traditions — keep one, kill one, rename something, start a new thing — and we vote with no framework at all, its basically whoever talks loudest and whoever's closest to graduating. Last year I read Nietzsche's history essay (the second untimely meditation, the one about monumental / antiquarian / critical history?) and it weirdly feels like it's about our exact problem, except I can't figure out how to turn it into something a room full of teenagers who've never heard of it can actually run. I think critical history is the one where you judge and condemn the past, but I'm fuzzy on where the other two stop being useful and start being a disease.

So: can you write us a real decision procedure for a proposal? Ordered questions, what counts as a good enough answer to each one, where it should just stop at "no," and where it has to hand the decision to the whole student body because it's a values call and not a facts call. It needs to work in one 40-minute meeting with no reading assigned beforehand, so translate the three modes into plain questions instead of assuming we know them — and mark which mode each step is secretly doing, plus where you think the procedure is faking rigor or will misfire in practice.

Then run it on two. One: a founders' day assembly we've done since the 50s, and this year a bunch of freshmen found out the guy the school is named after owned a plantation, and they want it gone, and the alumni are furious and threatening to pull donations. Two: the hallway plaque listing every valedictorian since 1954 — nobody's asking to remove it, but a lot of people tell me it's the reason they stopped trying, since there's no version of them that ends up on it. I want at least one of the two answers to be an answer I'll hate.

And be honest about the shakiest part: which step are you least confident in, and what would change your mind — like is there a way of reading Nietzsche where this whole procedure is exactly the wrong thing to build?
