You are asked to act as an assessor applying Imre Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes to a wholly invented episode in the history of science. Everything you need is stated below. Nothing in your answer should depend on identifying real researchers, publications, or fields, and you should not supply citations; the episode is fictional and no external knowledge is either available or relevant. Reason only from the description given, and apply the framework consistently even where that produces uncomfortable verdicts.

**The phenomenon.** In year 1, Halvorsen reported that alloys of the element kestrium lose ductility abruptly below a sharply defined temperature, now called the Halvorsen threshold (T\*). Two features made the threshold famous: the transition is remarkably sharp, yet the value of T\* varies from specimen to specimen in a way that no one could predict in advance. Years are cited below as "year n," with year 1 being the year of discovery. Five programmes have addressed it.

**Programme 1 — the Interstitial Programme (founded year 1).** Hard core: T\* is fixed by nothing but the concentration and chemical identity of interstitial impurity atoms lodged between kestrium lattice sites; ductility fails when impurity-pinned dislocations can no longer glide. Negative heuristic: attribute nothing whatever to microstructure or to measurement.
1a (year 3): a second impurity species is added to the list of pinning agents after specimens with unexpectedly high T\* are assayed and found to contain it. Quantitative fit restored. No further claim made.
1b (year 7): a third species, on the same pattern.
1c (year 11): a fourth species, plus — for the first time — a forward prediction: that artificially doping kestrium with species 4 at a known concentration will raise T\* by a computed margin. Tested year 13; confirmed to within 2 per cent.
1d (year 19): predicts, before any measurement, that an untested family of high-melt kestrium alloys will show T\* depressed by an amount derivable from the concentrations of species 2 and 3 alone. Tested year 21; confirmed.
1e (years 26–33): a run of specimens shows T\* values that no combination of the four known species can produce. The belt is extended by postulating a fifth species, "spectroscopically inert," which no available assay registers. When critics object that this renders the hypothesis untestable, the belt is extended again (year 29) with an account of why each of three proposed detection methods fails to register the species, and once more (year 33) with an account of why the species is present in some laboratories' stock material and absent in others'. Between years 26 and 40 the programme issues no forward prediction.
1f (year 38): an adherent imports from Programme 2 the hypothesis that grain size modulates pinning strength; this recovers four of the six anomalous specimens. The importation is announced as a temporary expedient and the core is reaffirmed unchanged.

**Programme 2 — the Boundary Programme (founded year 5).** Hard core: T\* is set by grain-boundary geometry, specifically the distribution of misorientation angles across boundaries, and is independent of interstitial composition.
2a (year 9): predicts that a specified annealing schedule will raise T\* by a computed margin with no change in composition. Tested year 11; confirmed.
2b (year 14): predicts a monotone relation between T\* and mean misorientation in deliberately textured specimens. Tested year 16; the relation holds in sign but is far weaker than computed. A saturation effect at high angles is postulated and independently confirmed in year 20 on specimens prepared by a different method.
2c (year 27): predicts that boundary-character distribution, not mean angle, is the operative variable. Tested year 30; confirmed for one of the three alloy families examined.
2d (year 34): the programme's central quantitative prediction for severely cold-worked, recrystallized material fails outright. The response is a new auxiliary hypothesis, a second-order boundary relaxation mechanism, entailing a checkable acoustic-emission signature during the transition. As of year 40 the signature has not been sought, because the apparatus does not exist; the design has been published and a rival laboratory has announced it will run the test in year 43.
Programme 2 has never produced a quantitative account of the original year-1 specimens.

**Programme 3 — the Transition Programme (founded year 8).** Hard core as originally stated: the ductility loss is a continuous lattice transition and T\* is a critical temperature; the founding statement identifies continuity as not open to revision.
3a (year 12): predicts a specific-heat anomaly at T\*, its magnitude fixed in advance by the core's critical exponents. Measured year 15; found, magnitude within 15 per cent.
3b (year 17): predicts a pressure dependence of T\*. Tested year 18; confirmed.
3c (year 21): accumulating pressure anomalies are resolved by abandoning continuity. The core is restated so that the transition is discontinuous and disorder-driven; the critical-exponent apparatus is dropped and replaced by a nucleation formalism. The founders describe this as a revision of the same programme.
Thereafter: 3d (year 28) predicts, from the nucleation formalism alone, the existence of a distinct low-temperature kestrium phase nobody had observed, and specifies the pressure–composition window in which to look; found in year 33 inside that window. 3e (year 35) predicts a characteristic isotope shift in T\*; confirmed year 37. 3f (year 39) predicts that a phase boundary will bend at a computable impurity concentration, thereby accounting for the discrepancy Programme 1 had been patching since year 26; confirmed year 40. This successor line has never reproduced Programme 2's annealing results and offers no account of texture.

**Programme 4 — the Artefact Programme (founded year 4).** Hard core: there is no such phenomenon. The abrupt loss of ductility is an artefact of strain-rate coupling between specimen and testing rig; a properly designed rig yields a smooth curve with no threshold.
4a (year 6): proposes the decisive test, a rig geometry with decoupled drive, and predicts no threshold whatever. Built year 8; run year 9 by an independent group. The threshold persisted unaltered.
Since year 9 the programme has made no modification of any kind. Its adherents, a small but active group, continue to publish, re-arguing in each case that the year-9 result must be misinterpreted and that no rig yet built is genuinely decoupled. They have specified no further test, derived no auxiliary hypothesis, and offered no account of any phenomenon other than the one they deny. They maintain that their core will be vindicated by an instrument that does not exist and cannot presently be designed.

**Programme 5 — the Distributional Programme (founded year 23).** Hard core: T\* is not a property of a material at all but a specimen-specific random variable; the science is the science of its distribution, and every apparent deterministic law is a statement about that distribution's parameters.
5a (year 25): the belt is fitted to the entire accumulated archive, achieving a standard of quantitative fit no rival approaches. The fitted parameters are not constrained independently of that archive.
5b (year 28): announces that it predicts the pressure anomaly that Programme 3 resolved in year 21 by revising its core. That anomaly had been a matter of public record since year 20 and formed part of the archive used to fix the parameters of the year-25 fit.
5c (year 31): predicts that measured T\* in a single specimen varies with the number of prior load cycles, with a fitted exponent. Confirmed year 32 and independently year 34.
5d (year 37): predicts that in a family of kestrium alloys never tested and containing an element absent from the fitting archive, the T\* distribution will be bimodal rather than unimodal. Confirmed year 39.
5e (year 40): asked what the bimodality consists in, the programme adds a two-component mixture whose second component is defined so as to have no observable consequences beyond the bimodality it was introduced to capture. Its adherents state that an independent characterization will be given later.

**Rules of assessment, to be applied without exception.**
(i) A modification contributes excess empirical content only if it entails something not already known — or already known but not used in arriving at the modification. Accommodation is not prediction.
(ii) A change to a hard core is not a modification of a programme; it ends that programme and begins another. Where this occurs, appraise the pair as two programmes and say that you are doing so.
(iii) An auxiliary hypothesis that entails something independently checkable is not ad hoc merely because the check has not been made. Weigh the length of the delay, but do not equate delay with failure.
(iv) Progressiveness is judged over a history, not modification by modification — but the verdict you must deliver concerns each programme as it stands in year 40, and you must state explicitly how much of that standing is inherited from an earlier phase.
(v) If you judge any case underdescribed, say which further fact would change your verdict.

**Deliverables.**
1. Rank the programmes (including any successor identified under rule (ii)) from most to least progressive as of year 40, and assign each a progressiveness score from 0 to 10 to one decimal place, where 10 denotes a programme both theoretically and empirically progressive across its entire history and 0 one that has been uniformly degenerating. Justify each score in one to three sentences. Scores need not be evenly spaced; any tie must be argued for rather than declared.
2. Sort every lettered modification (1a–1f, 2a–2d, 3a–3f, 4a, 5a–5e) into exactly one of four classes: theoretically and empirically progressive; theoretically progressive but not yet empirically so; ad hoc in the strong sense of adding no independently testable content; ad hoc in the weaker sense of adding testable content that remains uncorroborated. Then list separately any modification you cannot place in this scheme, and identify which commitment of the framework the difficulty exposes.
3. Say which single programme you would advise a capable doctoral student beginning in year 41 to work in, and explain in no more than a paragraph why that advice may legitimately diverge from the head of your Part 1 ranking.
4. State whether any of the five is not strictly a research programme at all, on the ground that a programme requires a positive heuristic — a capacity to generate its own successive versions — rather than merely a hard core and adherents. Argue for or against that ground rather than assuming it.

Format your response in four labelled parts corresponding to the deliverables, using the lettered labels exactly as given above. Aim for 900–1,200 words in total.
