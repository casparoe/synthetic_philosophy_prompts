I convene a monthly ideas group at a public library: roughly twelve adults, mixed education, no philosophy training, no assigned reading, one evening, seventy-five minutes, seated around a table with no whiteboard and no slides. Next month's topic is the three rival accounts of political freedom — freedom as non-interference, freedom as self-direction or self-mastery, and freedom as non-domination. What I need from you is a teaching plan, not an explanation. I know the material; I need the sequencing, the choice of vehicle, and the diagnostics worked out.

Four things to settle.

The vehicle. I intend to build the whole session on one running case rather than three separate illustrations, and I want an honest verdict on whether that is feasible. The case: salaried staff at a firm are expected to keep the work messaging app installed on their personal phones and to answer within fifteen minutes outside working hours. Their manager almost never writes after six in the evening; but nothing in the contract, the handbook, or any practice limits when he may write, and there is no procedure by which an employee could object. Halfway through I introduce the proposed remedy — a rule, contractual or statutory, entitling staff not to be contacted after hours. Tell me what this case must have and must lack in order to keep the three accounts cleanly separable once it is in front of the room. My worry is that it does real work for non-interference and for domination but cannot generate self-mastery at all, since nothing in it makes the worker an obstacle to himself. If that is right, say whether to bolt a second element onto the same case, to run a parallel case alongside it, or to abandon the single-vehicle design, and justify the choice by reference to what each account needs from an example in order to become visible rather than merely definable.

The order. Non-interference presumably has to come first, because it is what the group already holds — most of them will arrive treating freedom as the absence of rules in the way, and two will hold that any regulation is a subtraction. But that opening creates a trap: introduced afterward, non-domination will look like a refinement of what they already believe rather than a rival analysis of the same thing, and if I instead present it as something wholly different I will have made it sound like a word game. I also do not know whether self-mastery belongs second or third. Give me the dependency structure — which components cannot be grasped before others, and why — and tell me exactly where the payoff claim lands, namely that a rule which forbids the worker to agree to after-hours contact can, on one account, leave his freedom untouched, on another enlarge it, and on a third diminish it while compensating him in something else.

The misconceptions, each mapped to a beat in that sequence. Two members manage teams and will object that the worker took the job and can leave it, so nothing is being done to him. Two others, I expect, will insist that non-domination is simply non-interference with anxiety added. And my own prediction is that most of the room will hear positive liberty as meaning the money, time, and confidence to do what one wants — which is not that conception at all, and I want it headed off before it sets, without spending twenty minutes defining something nobody has yet felt the need for. For each, tell me the earliest point at which it can be defused cheaply, and what the defusing costs me if it happens there rather than later.

The close and the cuts. One closing question or short variant case that would reveal whether they can sort a new situation by which account it presupposes, rather than by which verdict they find congenial. And an explicit list of what I should leave out at this level — I am thinking of the historiography of the tradition, the live disagreement among contemporary republicans over how far non-domination is a separate concept rather than a refinement of non-interference, and the question of whether an automated scheduling system can dominate anyone when no person intends it — with a line on each saying why it is safe to cut, and whether any should instead be named and parked in case the group raises it itself.

Deliver the plan as a sequence of timed beats, each with its purpose and its prerequisite, and be blunt wherever my proposed case or my proposed ordering is simply wrong.
