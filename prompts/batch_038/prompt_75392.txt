I'm putting together a session for a grad seminar on the timing of experience and instead of assigning a paper I want to hand the students a dialogue they can argue with. Can you write it for me?

Setup: Marisol and Dev are standing behind the one-way glass in a vision lab while a subject in the booth runs trials of the color phi thing — you know the one, a green dot flashed at one spot and a red dot flashed a few degrees over a couple hundred ms later, and the subject afterwards reports seeing a single dot that traveled across the gap and changed color somewhere in mid-path. Marisol is the Orwellian here: on her view the subject really did have an accurate experience of a stationary green dot, and what got rewritten afterwards was the memory and the judgment about it. Dev is the Stalinesque: on his view the reconciliation is finished before anything is experienced at all, so there was never a green-dot-only experience sitting around to be overwritten — the mid-path color change was genuinely seen.

I dont want them arguing about the general literature, though. The lab is running three conditions today and I want the whole fight to be about these:

(a) interference — on some trials a loud burst of white noise goes off right after the first dot and the subject counts backward out loud for about 800 ms before the second dot is even scheduled. The thought is that this should knock out whatever consolidates and revises. Marisol should predict that reports on these trials collapse toward "the first dot just sat there, then a second one popped up," since the editing machinery got jammed. Dev should predict literally no change, because on his account the content was settled before experience and messing with memory can't reach back into it.

(b) probe — on half the trials a dim, very brief flash is presented somewhere along the illusory path between the two dots, and the subject only has to hit a key for detected / not detected, with no verbal report until the end of the block. They should fight over whether a fast keypress is driven by pre-composed content or composed content, and over whether either of them is allowed to say "that button press wasn't mediated by consciousness anyway" without draining the whole dispute of content.

(c) titration — the spatial gap between the dots gets shrunk in small steps until the motion percept snaps in. Does the color-change report cross its threshold on the same step as the motion report? If those two come apart, what does each of them owe?

Also please build in what I've started calling the ledger problem, which is my own worry. Whichever cutoff you posit — a freeze on memory revision for Marisol, a broadcast deadline for Dev — it starts to look exactly like the finish line both of them are supposed to be avoiding. Make each of them say why their own cutoff isn't just the Cartesian theater wearing a trenchcoat, and make them press each other hard on this rather than letting it slide.

Ground rules for the writing:
- Neither one gets to be a strawman. Both should be the smartest available version of their camp, and each should land at least one blow that makes the other squirm.
- Somewhere in the middle they should notice that they agree about every neural event, every latency, and every report the subject could possibly produce, and then they have to argue about what is actually left to disagree about — a real difference, or just a quarrel over where the word "experience" gets pinned? Don't let them resolve that too neatly.
- Each should name one concrete result from those three conditions that would make them switch sides. And let one of them make a genuine concession, not a cosmetic one.
- They're allowed to accuse each other of sliding into the no-theater / multiple-drafts view, but neither of them may defect to it. They stay in their camps.

Aim for somewhere around 1,200 to 1,500 words, and please write it in American English.
