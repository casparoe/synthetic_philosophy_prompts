Six months ago I was assigned to design the evaluation of a program that convenes facilitated community dialogues about gender norms, after which girls' secondary-school enrollment reportedly climbs. My funder wants to know whether the shift in norms caused the shift in enrollment, and I have spent the whole time discovering that I cannot say what a norm is in the only language I am fluent in. My colleagues in the interpretive wing of my department tell me I am asking a category mistake: on their account, a norm is not an upstream variable but a shared frame of meaning, and the question is not whether it raised enrollment but what it means to the people who live under it. I keep reaching for Weber's argument about ascetic Protestantism and the spirit of capitalism as my precedent, since it is the canonical case of someone claiming that a body of religious ideas helped produce an economic orientation, and I want to find out whether that claim can be stated in my terms at all or whether it evaporates when I try.

My vocabulary is that of applied causal inference: potential outcomes and estimands; directed acyclic graphs; confounding, colliders, and post-treatment bias; mediation analysis and the assumptions required to license any statement about an indirect effect; instruments and the exclusion restriction; selection on observables; and a standing distinction I insist on between having identified a causal effect and having identified a mechanism. I am fully fluent in all of this. Please translate at that level rather than popularizing it, and please do not explain the notation back to me.

First, the translation. Restate the Protestant ethic thesis in this vocabulary as faithfully as you can manage. I want, specifically: what the treatment and outcome would be and what candidate estimand Weber is actually committed to; whether ascetic Protestant affiliation functions in his argument as a treatment, a confounder, a mediator, or a collider, with a defense of the assignment you choose; what identification strategy the thesis would require, and whether that strategy is available in principle or merely in practice; and what his idea of an elective affinity between religious doctrine and economic conduct corresponds to, if anything, in a graph or a mediation model. If some element of his argument is doing causal work that none of my categories can name, that is a finding, not a failure to answer.

Second, and more important to me, the residue report. Sort his claims into three piles: those that translate cleanly, those that translate only by being distorted, and those that have no expression in my vocabulary whatsoever. For the second pile, say precisely what gets lost or bent in each case. For the third, say what kind of thing the untranslatable claim is, and then take a position on the question I actually care about: is the residue a defect in causal inference, which perhaps has no way to represent the semantic content of a belief as opposed to the behavior that accompanies it, or does it show that the thesis was less determinate than it first appears, so that what remains after translation is either a selection story about who migrated where, a measurement story about how you code a value, or nothing at all?

Third, one check on myself. I have been told that Weber's own methodology already contains something like a counterfactual criterion for attributing causes in historical explanation, asking whether the outcome would have run a different course had the putative cause been absent, coupled with a notion of adequacy that concerns what a cause makes likely rather than what it guarantees. If that is roughly right, tell me how much of the distance between him and me disappears once I take his methodological writing seriously rather than only his substantive one, and whether the interpretive objection my colleagues press survives that concession or depends on ignoring it.

Two constraints on how you do this. Do not import my framework's assumptions into Weber and then credit the framework for the fit: if a Weberian claim has no place on a graph, say that it has no place on a graph rather than assigning it a node so the picture looks complete. And do not treat him as an econometrician with poor data; grading him against my standards is conquest, not translation, and it will tell me nothing about whether my own evaluation design is measuring anything.

Finally, please state how confident you are in the principal claims you make, distinguishing those you would defend without qualification from those you are merely inclined toward, and single out the one point in your residue report you are least sure of, with an explanation of what would have to be true for it to be wrong.
