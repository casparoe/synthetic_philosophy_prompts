ok so I'm a junior and for my ethics class I went way too deep on the question of whether it's wrong to spray pesticides, and there's a pattern I keep hitting that I don't have a name for. hoping you can tell me whether this is a real thing or something I made up.

it goes like this. someone will say something like: the number of insects we kill with pesticides every year is bigger than the number of animals affected by any other human practice, so if insects can feel anything at all, this is the worst thing going on. and the reply is: hold on, we barely know whether insects are conscious, their nervous systems are nothing like a dog's, you can't build an enormous moral claim on that much uncertainty. totally reasonable.

then like two weeks later, sometimes the same person, arguing the other direction: out in the wild insects starve, get eaten alive, get destroyed from the inside by parasites — there's this unimaginable amount of suffering happening in nature — but we shouldn't try to fix it, because messing with ecosystems always backfires. and now the exact question that was too shaky to lean on is being leaned on. nobody seems to notice the switch.

third version, same shape: if you say we should stop spraying, suddenly ecology is infinitely complicated and no human can predict what would happen. if you say we should bring in some predatory beetle to eat the aphids, suddenly we understand food webs well enough to redesign them.

so it's not that one side is dumb, it's that the confidence attached to a fact changes depending on what that fact would imply about us. when the implication is "you should stop doing something completely normal," the evidence gets treated as way too thin to act on. when the implication is "just leave it alone," the same evidence is treated as settled.

is there a name for this? I know its in the neighborhood of moving the goalposts and special pleading, but neither feels right — nobody announces a rule change and nobody is claiming an exemption for themselves. and I don't think it's just motivated reasoning either, because the people doing it are pretty sincere. they seem to be running on a background assumption that what nature does to animals isn't our business, and that assumption is doing all the work without ever getting said out loud.

what I actually want: what exactly is this pattern, what are the closest established concepts and how does it differ from each one, are the sentience version and the ecology version the same phenomenon or two different ones, and if there's no decent existing term can you coin one with a real definition and boundary cases. also tell me where the edge is — when is it legitimate to be extra skeptical of a claim that would require everyone to change everything, versus when does that skepticism turn into this thing. no need for a reading list, I just want the concept.
