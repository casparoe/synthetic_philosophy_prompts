Two weeks from my orals and my committee wants a one-pager on where I actually land on the ravens paradox, so I'd much rather you find the cracks now than have them find the cracks in the room. Here's what I've got:

1. Confirmation is nothing over and above probability raising. E confirms H relative to background K iff P(H | E∧K) > P(H | K). I don't think there's some separate relation of "being an instance of" doing independent work underneath that.
2. I keep the equivalence condition. So no escaping by saying the contrapositive is a different hypothesis with different evidence — whatever bears on "all ravens are black" bears on "all nonblack things are non-ravens," and back again.
3. Which means the white shoe does confirm, just by an amount nobody can feel. P(this thing is a raven) is astronomically low, so the shoe moves the needle in like the eigth decimal place. That's the whole solution as far as I'm concerned: the paradox is a failure of imagination about small magnitudes, not a paradox.
4. I also hold that background does real work — there are K's where seeing a nonblack nonraven would count *against* the hypothesis, and I think Good's point goes further than that, that under some backgrounds even a black raven can count against it. But I don't think that touches (3), since (3) is a claim about our actual background.
5. Not every universal generalization is the kind of thing that gets instance-confirmation, though. "All the coins in my pocket are silver" isn't supported by pulling out one more silver coin in any interesting sense. You need lawlikeness — call it entrenchment, call it counterfactual-supportingness, whatever — before Nicod-style reasoning gets off the ground.
6. I lean contrastive about the whole business. "E confirms H" is incomplete until you say which alternatives H is being scored against, and I suspect a big part of why the white shoe feels irrelevant is that nobody ever states the contrast class.
7. Case verdicts I'm commited to: a black raven confirms; a white raven refutes; a green leaf I'll grant is a hair of confirmation, per (2)-(3); but a black crow does nothing *at all* — not a hair, zero — because crows aren't ravens and I refuse to let pigmentation facts bleed across species.
8. On old evidence: I think what matters is learning, so evidence already sitting in K can't confirm anything, and my shoe has been in my total evidence since I was six.
