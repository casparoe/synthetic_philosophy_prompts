I'm on the policy committee of a local refugee-sponsorship group and a city councilwoman made an argument at our last meeting that's been rattling around in my head ever since. Her line was that immigration is a queue. You apply, you get a number, you wait your turn. There are only so many slots a year, so the line moves slowly, but it moves fairly, and everybody in it knows the rules. Somebody who pays a smuggler, crosses, and then files for asylum has cut to the front — and if we process them, we push everyone behind them back: a family in a camp in Jordan that did everything right now waits another three years beacuse of one boat. So the actually humane move, she said, is to make the spontaneous route worthless — turn people back, or hold them at the border — and pour all the money into lengthening the queue overseas, where the waiting is orderly. A bunch of people nodded. I think she's wrong, but when I tried to say why I came out sounding like I was defending line-cutting, which is exactly the frame I wanted to escape.

Can you audit the analogy for me? Start with the correspondence table: what plays the ticket counter, the numbered ticket, the fixed annual slots, the person standing behind you, the clerk who calls the next number, and the service being handed over the counter. Then where the mapping stops holding. Does the asylum system actually contain a queue you can join from that camp in Jordan, or is the queue something else entirely? What does "the slots are full" even mean when the thing being allocated is protection from persecution rather than a commodity with a supply cap? Is the harm to the people behind you real and traceable the way it is at a bank, or is the counterfactual doing something shakier? Is the person who skips ahead taking something that was theirs to take?

Then sort the disanalogies into the ones that actually threaten her conclusion and the ones she could shrug off without blinking — I suspect a lot of my objections were in the second pile, which is why they bounced. And tell me whether the conclusion survives: could she keep the "grow overseas resettlement, close the spontaneous route" half after the queue picture is scrapped, or was the queue carrying that half too? If something other than the analogy is doing the real work in her argument, name it. Last thing — if the queue is the wrong picture, what would a better one be that a councilwoman could actually say out loud at a public meeting, and does it point at the same policy or a different one?
