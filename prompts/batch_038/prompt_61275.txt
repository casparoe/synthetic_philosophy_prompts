I'm three years into a moral philosophy PhD and the chapter I'm stuck on is relativism, so I'd rather work through a real episode than another round of the textbook dialectic. Take the AAA's 1947 Statement on Human Rights — the one the executive board sent over to the UN Commission while the Declaration was being drafted, warning that a universal bill of rights would amount to an imposition of Western values on everybody else, produced by the committee Melville Herskovits chaired — and then the fact that in June 1999 the membership adopted a Declaration on Anthropology and Human Rights that effectively walked it back.

Run that through the standard three-way split: descriptive relativism (genuine fundamental moral disagreement across cultures), metaethical relativism (no framework-independent moral truths), normative relativism (so we ought to tolerate other frameworks). Which one does the 1947 document actually assert, and where does it slide between them? I specifically want to know whether the people who called it self-refuting — you cant consistently preach tolerance from a view that denies there's any framework-neutral ground to preach from — were attacking a position the statement holds or one they built for it.

And here's the bit I care about most. I've been playing with a distinction between relativism as a claim about truth and relativism as a claim about justification: maybe what the 1947 authors were really saying is that we have no available procedure for adjudicating between frameworks, which is an epistemic point, not a metaphysical one. Does that reading survive the actual argumentative content of the statement, or does it let them off too easy? Maritain, on the UNESCO side of the drafting, reportedly made a parallel observation — that the delegates could agree on which rights to list as long as nobody asked them why — and I want to know whether that's the same move or a different one.

Finally, what does the 1999 reversal do to the theory? Is it relativism proving unstable under pressure, evidence that the earlier statement was never metaethically relativist to begin with, or just an institution updating on new facts — genocide, state failure — with no philosophical content at all? Tell me which features of the fifty-year arc the relativism debate actually explains and which ones it can't touch, and don't force it to speak where it's silent.
