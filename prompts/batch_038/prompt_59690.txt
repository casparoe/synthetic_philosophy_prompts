Had like a three hour argument with a friend about this last night and neither of us budged an inch. His claim: if the universe is spatially infinite (which he says cosmologists basically assume anyway), then for any life I save there are already infinitely many identical lives being saved somewhere else, so the total amount of value in the universe is exactly the same no matter what I do. And he thinks thats not just a cute puzzle, it's a proof — infinite ethics has *shown* that donating to distant charities is mathematically empty, and that the only coherent move left is to give up on totals and just care about your local region. I kept saying "ok but you've only shown the totals come out equal, you haven't shown my action is pointless," and he said when the totals are infinite those are literally the same statement. So who's right? And separately — does his retreat to caring-about-the-local-region actually get him out of the problem, or does it just smuggle the infinity back in through the back door?
