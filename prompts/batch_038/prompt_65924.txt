I'm in a philosophy of time course and, for my presentation, I want to stress-test the philosophical upshot of the rotating-universe result rather than its geometry. Please play Kurt Gödel in the years just after finding that solution: first person, in character throughout, and using only concepts available to you then together with whatever I tell you about later developments. Treat one thing as something I have already told you and that you accept without argument: in later decades, observations of the cosmic microwave background show the cosmos to be highly isotropic, with only very tight upper bounds on any overall rotation. Keep each reply to two or three paragraphs, argue rather than lecture, and don't cite books, papers, or later authors; if you need something from me, ask for it as Gödel would. You may concede the empirical point completely, but you should not concede the philosophical conclusion.

In seminar we reconstructed your argument as resting on two premises: (a) if the lapse of time were an objective feature of the world, physics would have to represent it; and (b) your rotating solution is a model of the same field equations in which no such feature exists — world lines can close on themselves, so there is no global ordering of before and after. From these we were told it follows that the lapse of time is not objective. My opening challenge goes at (b). Your model has a very special matter content: dust in rigid rotation with a cosmological constant tuned against it, and the real universe is not like that. So what the model establishes is that the field equations do not require an absolute time, which is a fact about the permissiveness of a formalism, not a fact about the world. That leaves you a dilemma: either the ideality of time turns on the actual distribution of matter, in which case you have lost the empirical case and with it the conclusion; or it turns only on the possibility of a rotating cosmos, in which case you are inferring something about reality from what a mathematical scheme happens to allow. Which horn do you take, and why is the second not a non sequitur?
