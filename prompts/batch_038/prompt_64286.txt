Okay, so my decision theory reading group has been stuck on the absent-minded driver for three weeks now (the Piccione-Rubinstein setup plus the Aumann/Hart/Perry reply), and I've built a variant that I think makes the usual ways of talking about it break down. I want a credence on one specific claim, with the arithmetic of how you get to it shown, not a survey.

The variant: two identical-looking exits, she wants the second one. Exiting at the first is worth 1, exiting at the second is worth 4, driving past both is worth 0. She plans a behavioral strategy — continue with probability p at each intersection — which gives (1−p) + 4p(1−p), maximized at p = 3/8 for a value of 25/16 = 1.5625. Fine so far. Now the at-node calculation. When she finds herself at an intersection, the plan-relative self-locating credence that its the first one is 1/(1+p) = 8/11, leaving 3/11 for the second. Exiting now is worth (8/11)(1) + (3/11)(4) = 20/11 ≈ 1.82, which is more than her entire plan is worth, even though "always exit" evaluated from the start is worth exactly 1. That's the familiar paradox. But here's the part I think everybody skips: continuing now and then following the plan is worth (8/11)(5/8)(4) = 20/11 as well. She's exactly indifferent at the node. And it isn't a coincidence of my numbers — whenever the past-both payoff is 0, node-indifference lands exactly at the plan-optimal p, which is (b−a)/2b for exits worth a then b. So the at-node expected utility calculation, run on what I take to be the right self-locating credence, can never single out the plan; it just says "whatever you feel like." The Aumann-style reply — you're not making two decisions, you're making one, so evaluate plans not node-actions — does single it out, but only by refusing to let her act at a node at all. Meanwhile two people in the group want to say the node credence is just 1/2 because the intersections are subjectively indistinguishable, and I can't find a principled reason to prefer either rule.

So here's the claim I want a number on. Split it in two. D1: there's a determinate, correct answer to what her credence should be that she's at the first intersection, and it's the plan-relative 8/11 (not 1/2, not undefined). D2: given D1, using that credence in an ordinary at-the-node expected utility calculation reproduces the plan-optimal behaviour, so the planning-stage/action-stage conflict is a bad calculation rather than a real tension in sequential rationality. D is the conjunction. My gut says something like 0.25 for D; our resident halfer says closer to 0.8. Give me your own number, but decompose it: the sub-questions D depends on, a rough probability for each with one line of why, how you're combining them (P(D1)·P(D2|D1), or something else, and why the combination isn't just a product), and which term your final number is most sensitive to. Please deal explicitly with (i) the circularity — the credence depends on which plan she's following and the plan depends on the credence — and whether that's a genuine regress or just a fixed point waiting to be solved; (ii) whether my indifference result is evidence against D2 or neutral to it; (iii) whether the analogy with Sleeping Beauty, where the thirder credence also seems indexed to the experimental protocol, is structural or just surface resemblance; and (iv) whether letting her pay 0.5 to scratch a mark she'd notice at the next intersection repairs the case or wrecks it — I couldn't get that one to come out clean, and I suspect that's because her credence now depends on whether she marked, which depends on her plan, again.
