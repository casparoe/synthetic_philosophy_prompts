I'm a TA and in three weeks I have to run a 75-minute section, 18 students, mostly sophomores, on Browning vs. Goldhagen — Ordinary Men against Hitler's Willing Executioners. This stopped being abstract for me last week: at a family dinner my brother-in-law said something like "the men were told they could step out and barely any did, so they were willing, end of story," and I couldnt answer him. I'm fairly sure half my class will say the same thing.

Design an in-class activity that makes them feel the situationist point from the inside: the gap between refusal being formally available and almost nobody refusing, and why that gap doesn't by itself settle whether the men were willing or merely compliant. Here's the mechanic I want you to build around, adapt it however works better: squads have to complete a trivially annoying task under a token/status economy in which opting out is explicitly permitted but costs your squad its rating, plus a prediction round beforehand where each student privately writes down what fraction of the class they expect to opt out. The prediction-vs-outcome gap should be what they notice first.

Give me rules, materials, a minute-by-minute timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps what actually happened in the room onto the situational factors Browning leans on (peer standing, careerism, routinization, the way the first order was framed) and onto Goldhagen's claim that a shared ideological conviction is doing the explanatory work that situation can't. Then two honest notes I really need: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to recover in the moment, and which part of the Browning/Goldhagen disagreement the activity structurally cannot reproduce — the bit I'm going to have to supply myself with readings and testimony. The aversive task has to be genuinely harmless, and opting out of the activity itself has to be real opting out, not a stunt.

Start with one sentence saying what they'll come away seeing, then go straight into the run sheet — no preface, no restating what I asked.
