I convene a final-year seminar on metaontology, and for the closing week I have set Borges's story "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius" — the one in which a narrator chases an anomalous encyclopaedia entry about a country called Uqbar, then stumbles on an eleventh volume of an imaginary "First Encyclopaedia of Tlön", and learns from a postscript that a secret society, Orbis Tertius, has spent centuries fabricating a whole planet. The fabricated world is an idealist one: its inhabitants treat materialism as a heresy because it presupposes the persistence of unperceived things, they hold that the universe is not a collection of objects in space but a heterogeneous series of independent acts, successive and temporal; they regard metaphysics as a branch of fantastic literature; and their languages, having no nouns, build sentences out of impersonal verbs and adjectives. Objects there duplicate and fade as attention withdraws, and the copies generated by expectation — the hrönir — eventually acquire a purity that the originals lack. Once the encyclopedia is discovered and publicised, the invented ontology begins to colonise the actual one, and the narrator ends by withdrawing into a private translation of Sir Thomas Browne while the world becomes Tlön.

I would like a critical reading of the story set against the contemporary debate over whether ontological disputes are substantive or merely verbal: Carnap's distinction between internal and external questions and his pragmatic picture of framework choice, Hirsch's quantifier variance, Chalmers's method of elimination and his deflationism about ontology, and Sider's insistence that reality has a distinguished structure which joint-carving concepts track. Specifically:

1. Does Tlön's nounless language state an ontological thesis, or only a semantic one? The story seems to slide between "there are no persisting objects" and "we do not talk as though there were", and I want that slippage diagnosed carefully — is it a confusion, or is it precisely the point, given that quantifier variance trades on a similar slide?

2. Are the hrönir a picture of framework-relative existence, where adopting a scheme changes what counts as an object rather than what objects there are? Or do they model something stronger and more disturbing for Carnap: an ontology that becomes true because it is collectively adopted, so that the "external" question is answerable by social facts rather than settled by pragmatic convenience?

3. The story has Tlön's own philosophers dismiss metaphysics as fantastic literature. Is that ontological fictionalism, and is the ending a reductio of it — the claim that a fiction stops being fictional once enough people inhabit it — or is the ending better read as vindicating the fictionalists' own residual question about which conceptual scheme to adopt?

4. Could a Siderian reply be run against the story: that the spread of Tlön shows only that people can be made to speak in a non-joint-carving way, not that joint-carving-ness is up for grabs? What would the story's defender say back, and is there any resource left to them besides an appeal to the narrator's evident sense of loss?

5. Where must this reading be resisted? I want you to say honestly what the metaontological frame misses — the story's elegiac and anti-totalitarian temper, written as it was on the eve of the Second World War and revised in a postscript of 1947, and the possibility that Borges's target is the seductiveness of closed systems rather than the semantics of quantification. Then say what the story contributes to the debate that argument alone cannot: whether it exhibits what it is like to inhabit a rival framework rather than describing it from outside.

Please write in British English, in continuous prose with your five points treated in order, at around fifteen hundred words. Do not quote the story verbatim or supply a bibliography, references, or further reading; work from the outline above and from your own recollection of the text. Where your memory of a detail is uncertain, say so rather than filling the gap, and flag any point at which you think my summary has distorted the story.
