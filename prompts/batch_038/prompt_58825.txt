I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on divine providence and human freedom, and I want it to model cost–benefit reasoning about theories rather than a catalogue of who said what. The subject is the grounding objection to middle knowledge, pressed by Robert Adams and developed further by William Hasker: if a creature's act is libertarianly free, then no fact obtaining before God's creative decree settles what the creature would do in a given set of circumstances, yet Molinism holds that the corresponding counterfactual of creaturely freedom is determinately true at precisely that point in the explanatory order.

Evaluate the following six replies. Treat them as moves available in principle rather than as positions to be attributed to particular authors.

1. Brute truth: determinate truth does not require a truth-maker, so counterfactuals of creaturely freedom may simply be true with nothing grounding them.
2. Dispositional grounding: the counterfactual is grounded in the agent's own stable character and inclinations, which settle what she would do without causally determining that she does it.
3. Ockhamist relocation: the truth of the counterfactual is a soft fact about the pre-creative past, grounded in what the agent later freely does; grounding runs from act to truth while knowledge runs from truth to the divine mind, and these are different relations, so nothing circles.
4. Restricted Molinism: only probabilistic or statistical counterfactuals of freedom are true, singular determinate ones are neither true nor false, and providence is planned on that thinner basis.
5. Congruist softening: keep determinate counterfactuals but deny that the consequent act is wholly undetermined, since grace and circumstances jointly incline the will in a manner that leaves it free in the relevant sense.
6. Retreat: concede the objection and fall back either on simple foreknowledge or on open theism.

Rank the six from most to least successful, scoring each out of 10 for how well it preserves all three of these desiderata simultaneously: (i) determinate truth for exactly those counterfactuals that providential planning requires, (ii) libertarian freedom for the creatures, and (iii) no explanatory priority of God's knowledge over the free acts it concerns, so that the knowledge does not itself fix those acts.

Begin with the finished ranking as a bare ordered list of the six items with their scores. Then allow each entry exactly one sentence naming the specific cost it pays — which desideratum it damages most, and why. Close with no more than two sentences identifying the one desideratum you think no Molinist can keep intact, and what must be surrendered once it goes. The entire response must stay under 400 words.
