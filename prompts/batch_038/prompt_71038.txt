I am applying for a twelve-month postdoctoral fellowship in formal epistemology, to be assessed by a mixed panel of philosophers and mathematical statisticians, and I need the two-page research proposal (roughly 1,300 words) drafted from the half-formed idea set out below. Please write in British English, in a formal register, and do not include a bibliography or a reference list: I will supply the citations myself, so you should speak of positions, arguments and phenomena by description rather than by attribution. Where you refer to the state of the debate, do not rest anything on a claim you are not confident is accurate.

The idea. The dispute between imprecise Bayesians and their orthodox opponents has stalled because both sides argue almost exclusively over one canonical test case — the coin whose bias you know nothing about, or the urn whose composition you have not been told — and in that case the imprecise state in question is maximally unstructured, containing every probability function on the relevant algebra. There the whole disagreement collapses into the single question of whether absent evidence licences maximal ambiguity, and the standard objections are all calibrated to that case: that no learning can occur, that dilation shows conditioning to be perverse, that the agent is left without a uniquely recommended action and so is practically paralysed, and that the view is diachronically incoherent in the light of reflection-style requirements. My hypothesis is that the productive question concerns the structure of the licensed credal set rather than its size. Robust Bayesian practice does not generally work with vacuous sets; it works with bounded-distrust neighbourhoods of a single nominal prior — epsilon-contamination families, Gamma-minimax classes, sets fixed by moment constraints — in which imprecision expresses a limited distrust of a well-specified model rather than a confession of ignorance. I want the project to ask whether, once imprecise states are taken to be structured in one of these ways, the standard objections lose their force, are relocated to a narrower range of cases, or survive intact; and in particular whether dilation turns out to be a local artefact of evidence that is improbable under the nominal prior, rather than a general defect of the framework.

The proposal must contain the following, in whatever order you judge best:

1. A precise statement of the research question that separates the technical component (which classes of credal sets are dilation-free, and which dilate only under conditions that can be stated in advance) from the philosophical component (which norms governing the relation between a body of evidence and a credal structure those results would support). The question must have a determinate answer; it must not be an invitation to survey the field.

2. Why the existing answers leave the question open: what each camp has established, and why neither bears directly on the structural question. This is an argument about the shape of the debate, not a literature review.

3. Method, month by month across the twelve months: which formal results I would attempt and in what order, on which toy models; which conceptual arguments would be developed alongside them; and one concrete running example that is not the urn or the coin of unknown bias — something such as a diagnostic test whose manufacturer-reported sensitivity and specificity you have bounded but non-trivial reason to distrust, or an ensemble in which the nominal model is your best single estimate but known to be misspecified. Say what would be done with the example at each stage.

4. A brief definitional apparatus for the panel's benefit: two or three sentences each on dilation, on epsilon-contamination, and on the reflection-style diachronic objection, pitched so that a statistician with no philosophy and a philosopher with no decision theory can both follow the rest.

5. What a successful outcome would look like, what would count as the project failing, and which negative results would still be worth publishing. Address explicitly which of two bad outcomes would be worse: discovering that the objections survive under structured sets, or discovering that no interesting class of structured sets is characterisable within the time available.

6. The strongest objection to the whole enterprise, answered rather than deferred: if a nominal prior is available to the agent at all, why is she not simply a precise Bayesian whose credence is that prior, with the neighbourhood doing no genuine representational work? A proposal that cannot answer this is not fundable, so I want the answer sketched and its cost stated honestly, including what the imprecise Bayesian would have to concede in order to give it.

7. A candid assessment of scope: what would have to be cut if the formal results prove harder than expected, and what the fallback deliverable at twelve months would be in that event.
