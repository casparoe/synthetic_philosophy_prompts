my sister is a huge David Lewis fan and swears Everything Everywhere All At Once is basically a film version of the pluriverse: every choice spins off a universe, they're all equally real and concrete, and "actual" just picks out the one you're standing in. I keep arguing with her and want a third opinion before our next movie night, bc I think she's half right at best.

Worry one: verse-jumping. The alphas travel between universes, kill people in them, get killed in them, steal skills across them. But Lewis says there are no spatiotemporal relations at all between things in different worlds, nothing at one world causes anything at another, and a world is a maximal sum of interrelated parts. So on his own criteria isn't the film's whole multiverse just ONE world with weird pockets in it — not a plurality at all?

Worry two: counterparts. The film has Evelyn herself as a kung fu movie star, as a chef, and as a rock with googly eyes in a canyon where life never got going. Is the rock a counterpart of her under anything like Lewis's resemblance relation, or is the film quietly assuming the trans-world identity he spent his life denying?

Worry three: the ending, where she picks the laundromat-and-taxes universe. If no world is more real or more actual than another, what is that choice doing? Is the film landing on Lewis's indexicality of actuality, or on something he'd call a confusion?

I want a real reading, not a plot summary: where the lens lights something up, where the film fights it, and what the film adds to the debate that argument alone wouldn't. And tell me where I'm wrong.
