Setup. I've absorbed the rationalist-blog version of epistemic learned helplessness: you get burned enough times by arguments that felt airtight and turned out to be garbage, and eventually you stop trusting your argument-evaluation faculty in general rather than any specific argument. Fine, that's a thing that happens. But now ask what it would take to *respond correctly* to that kind of evidence about yourself, and I get stuck, because there are three claims I want to make and they seem to have no room for each other:

(1) If I get evidence that my belief that p was produced under bad conditions — sleep deprived, hyped up on a manic productive week, emotionally invested, talked into it by someone charismatic — that's a reason to lower my credence in p, even though it isn't evidence about p itself. Nothing in my first-order case changed.

(2) The only instrument available for weighing that evidence, and for producing whatever belief replaces p, is the same faculty that's under suspicion. There's no stepping outside my own reasoning to inspect it. Any discount I apply gets applied by the discounted thing.

(3) I can't rationally sit in the state where I believe p and also believe my believing p is irrational. So either the higher-order evidence moves my first-order belief, or I don't really accept it, and there's no third option where it just... floats there.

Individually each of these feels near-obvious to me. Together they feel like a locked room. What I want is the gut-level account of what actually gives, and why.

Two specific things I'd like you to dig into:

First, is (3) doing more work than it deserves? It seems to assume that "I think my belief was formed badly" is one state, when maybe there's a difference between judging that the process was unreliable and having any particular view about what I should now believe. If you go that route, tell me what the resulting state actually feels like from the inside, because I can't picture it — I can't picture believing p while thinking my p-belief is unjustified, which is maybe the whole problem.

But there I have an independent check — a second thermometer, or the weather report. Impeaching myself seems structurally different, and I have a suspicion that the difference isn't just "no independent instrument" but something about whether the second-order judgment is even the right kind of thing to have.
