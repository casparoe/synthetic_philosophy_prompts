been turning the testimony debate over in my head and I keep modelling it as a dial — call it the warrant floor: how much justification you get from someone's say-so before you've checked anything at all. Hume and the reductionists set it at zero, the Ceady/Burge types set it at the same height as perception. My problem tho: if the floor sits at perception-level, then anything anyone tells me is justified by default no matter how bad a witness they are, and the only way to stop that is to lower the floor in proportion to how unreliable they look — which is just reductionism with a head start. So is there any real middle position here, or does the anti-reductionist side have to concede the dial only runs from gullibility to reductionism?
