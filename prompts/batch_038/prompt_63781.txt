Here it is:

(1) A decision that binds people is legitimate only if those people authorised it.
(2) The generation that ratifies a constitution and the generation that lives under it decades later are, for political purposes, different bodies of people — elections turn over, the electorate is largely replaced, nobody alive voted for most of what binds them.
(3) So a constitution is only legitimate for the people who ratified it.
(4) The same holds at the individual level: a binding choice I make now is legitimate only if the me who has to live with it authorised it — and the me who has to live with it is precisely the one who will want out when the moment comes.
(5) Ulysses on the beach, ordering his crew to lash him to the mast and ignore him later, is the paradigm case of (4).
(6) Therefore Ulysses's order cannot legitimately bind the later Ulysses, who never authorised being tied up and is screaming to be released.
(7) So rational self-binding is impossible, and a fortiori constitutional precommitment is just one generation imposing its will on the next.
