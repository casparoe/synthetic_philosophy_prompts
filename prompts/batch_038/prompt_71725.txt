Running a reading group session on what you're supposed to do when your evidence is a conditional — Judy Benjamin style. You learn something like 'if the card isn't the ace of spades, it's the king of hearts', or in the die version, 'if the roll wasn't a 1 or a 2 then it was a 4'. Everybody agrees the credence in the antecedent should drop but not go to zero, and that's roughly where the agreement ends.

What I want is a decision tree rather than a lecture. Each node should be a question a reader can answer without already knowing which view it leads to — is learning a conditional learning a proposition at all, should the update only constrain ratios between posteriors, do you defer to what you'd believe about the consequent under a supposition of the antecedent, does the triviality result force you to give up truth conditions here — and each leaf a named rule or view, with the actual number it spits out for the die case. I think the leaves include the material-conditional-plus-Jeffrey answer, van Fraassen's reflection principle, and a couple of newer rules, McGee's and Kaufmann's if I'm rembering the names right, but don't take my word for the attributions. Then flag any two leaves that deliver the same number on the standard examples, and say what kind of case would actually separate them, plus any combination of node answers that no one occupies and why — incoherent, just unattractive, or nobody's got round to it.
