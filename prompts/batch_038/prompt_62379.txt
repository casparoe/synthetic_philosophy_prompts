I write about decision science and the philosophy of economics for a general-interest magazine, and I am reporting a piece on a reassurance I keep encountering in both places: the claim that a person who understands her own weaknesses is thereby safe from exploitation, because she can always decline the first offer in a sequence. I want to test that claim with a worked example whose numbers a reader can check with a pencil, and I want it built to specification, because the two economists I plan to interview will read it looking for the place where I forced the result.

Construct a single sequential decision problem, set in ordinary consumer life, that satisfies all six of the following.

1. The agent discounts the future in a present-biased way. State the discounting rule explicitly and apply the same rule to every agent in the case. At any given moment her ranking of the dated streams of payments actually open to her is complete and transitive; there is no preference cycle at any single moment anywhere in the case. Whatever exploitation occurs must be purely diachronic.

2. Embedded in the case is a strict pump against a naive agent — one who wrongly expects her future selves to discount the way she does now. She ends the episode holding exactly the entitlements she started with and strictly less cash, having accepted at least two separate offers along the way. Every offer's terms are fully transparent to her at the moment she accepts, and nothing is fraudulent or coerced.

3. A sophisticated agent facing the identical tree — one whose predictions about her own later choices are correct — is not pumped. If any of her predictions turns out to be wrong anywhere in the case, the construction fails.

4. The sophisticated agent is nonetheless left worse off than a resolute agent: one who at the outset selects the plan her initial preferences rank best among those available and then carries it out without revision at later nodes. The resolute agent must have no advantage of information, wealth, or technology — no contracts, no forfeited deposits, no apps, no spouse holding her to it. Her only asset is follow-through.

5. Measured in undiscounted dollars actually in hand at the end of the episode, the ranking is strict: resolute, then sophisticated, then naive.

6. Realism: the episode runs no longer than three months, no single amount exceeds a few hundred dollars, and nothing depends on a liquidity constraint or on anyone running out of money. No predictors, no exotic instruments, no casino.

The purpose fixes what counts as cheating: an ordering that is asserted rather than derived from the stated discounting rule is worse than useless to me, and so is a case in which one of the three agents has been quietly given different preferences.

Deliverables, in this order:

- The scenario, in under 250 words, with every date, offer, entitlement, and amount stated.
- A node-by-node walkthrough for each of the three agents, showing the value each assigns at each node under the stated rule and the choice that follows, ending in each agent's final cash.
- A line-by-line check against constraints 1 through 6, saying for each exactly how the case meets it.
- The single structural feature that makes foresight costly here — the reason the sophisticated agent walks away from something the resolute agent completes. Then the removal test: alter that feature and report what happens to the ranking.
- The counterattack. Someone who doubts that resolute choice is even available to an agent with these preferences, or who suspects constraint 5 was satisfied by stipulation, will object. Give the strongest version of that objection, say whether it lands, and if it does, say what minimal repair preserves the case.
- Finally, the question my piece actually turns on: the sophisticated agent is neither intransitive nor exploited, so which diachronic norm, if any, has she violated — and if the honest answer is that she has violated none, say so and explain what that implies about the connection between exploitability and rationality over time.

No bibliography, no citations, and no survey of the literature. I need the case and the arithmetic, not the references.
