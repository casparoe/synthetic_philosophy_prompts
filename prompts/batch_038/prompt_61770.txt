Every Thanksgiving my brother in law Dale holds court at the end of the table about who deserves their money and who doesn't, and this year he's been reading Nozick — he said the name like "nozz-ick" and I had to look it up afterwards — and now everything comes out of that book. He's not an academic either, he runs a landscaping crew, but he's very consistent, and that consistency is what's been bugging me, because I think I've found a case he can't get out of cleanly.

Here's his track record, this year and last, as near as I can remember it:

- the guy who got rich because millions of people each paid a few bucks to watch him play basketball: totally fine, Dale says this is the clearest case there is and he brings it up constantly
- a woman who bought a run-down duplex in the eighties and now rents it for triple what she paid: fine
- a lottery winner: fine
- somebody who inherited it from a grandmother who earned it honestly: fine
- the company that runs the toll bridge, which got an exclusive charter from the state legislature and no one else is allowed to build a bridge: not fine, but Dale is adamant its not about the tolls, it's about how they got the charter
- a drug company that paid Congress to extend its patent another ten years: not fine, same reason
- the family that fenced off the only spring on the open range back in the 1880s and still sells the water by the tanker: not fine
- a guy who owns every vacant lot on our block and won't sell any of them: fine, "ugly but fine, nobody needs a vacant lot"
- the only funeral home for two counties charging whatever it likes: fine, Dale says, because you can always drive forty minutes

The new case came up Sunday and Dale wouldn't answer it, he just said he'd think about it, which for him is basically a forfeit. A woman bought forty acres of scrub about fifteen years ago with her own savings — no loan, no permit, no subsidy, no government anything — and last year she drilled on it and hit the only deep aquifer in the county. Then the drought came. Now every farm for thirty miles has to buy water from her and she charges about ten times what it costs her to pump. Nobody forced anybody, the land was on the open market when she bought it, she took the risk on the drilling, and nothing illegal happened. Half the table wants her forced to sell at cost; Dale went quiet.

So: act like a political philosopher who genuinely knows this material and has also spent a lot of time arguing with actual people like Dale, and think it through properly before you write anything. I want (1) the candidate rules that would fit every verdict on that list — I can see at least three myself, something like "only how it happened matters," "anything the government hands out is tainted," and "not fine once people cant do without you" — tell me which of those survive all nine verdicts, which one gets killed by a specific verdict on the list (I suspect the funeral home does some work here), and whether there's a fourth rule I haven't thought of; (2) what each surviving rule predicts about the water woman; (3) your best guess at what Dale actually says when he comes back to it, with your reasons for preferring that guess; (4) which of his earlier verdicts is the closest analog to hers, and an honest word on whether his precedents really settle it or just look like they do; and (5) one more case I could put to him at Christmas, in a couple of sentences, that would force the surviving rules apart — I was thinking the same woman charging cost plus five percent, but tell me if theres a better probe.

Give me the bottom line up front in one or two sentences — what he says, and whether he should say it — and then the rest. Please keep the whole thing under 700 words, I'm printing it out to have in my pocket at the table.
