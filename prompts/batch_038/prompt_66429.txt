I'm three years into a political theory PhD and the second chapter of my dissertation argues that deplatforming is punishment — not "punishment-like," not "functionally analogous to," but literally an instance of it. My definition is the standard analytic one, roughly Hart's five elements: hard treatment, imposed for conduct taken to breach a rule binding on the target, on an actual or supposed offender, by other human beings, by a body constituted to impose it. I claim platforms clear all five. The ban is hard treatment (loss of audience, ad and sponsorship income, sometimes employability downstream); it's issued against a published rule violation; and the escalating strike systems — warning, temporary restriction, permanent suspension, with appeal channels bolted on over time — are effectively a tariff of penalties administered by a standing body, which is what separates this from me simply deciding not to read your blog.

What would count against that thesis? I want the defeaters ranked, most fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to be explicit about which ones a defender of the view would themselves concede as damaging rather than waving off — my committee's objection is that platforms are proprietors exercising contract and property rights, not governors, so the penal vocabulary is a category mistake. Say what the view predicts that the rival framings don't (the property/contract story, the "it's just market exit" story, the "moderation is safety engineering, not censure" story), and where those predictions are actually testable, including against the empirical work on whether bans really shrink a banned figure's reach — does that evidence confirm my thesis, or could it cut against it in a way I haven't noticed?

Also be honest about insulation. If my functional definition is loose enough that getting dropped by a publisher, unfollowed en masse, or fired for a tweet all count as punishment too, is that a reductio, or a bullet I can bite without the chapter collapsing into triviality? Tell me where you think the thesis is genuinely exposed and why. Don't give me a balanced menu of considerations and leave me to sort it out — take a position, commit to it, and give your reasons. American English, please.
