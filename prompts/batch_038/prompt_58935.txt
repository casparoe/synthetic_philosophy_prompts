I've got to give the speech at my old PhD supervisor's retirement dinner in three weeks and I keep deleting drafts. He spent 38 years arguing that a certain kind of damage in cells gets fixed by a dedicated repair enzyme that nobody ever managed to isolate — he never found it, but the theory predicted a huge amount of downstream behaviour correctly, and that's what kept it alive. Four years ago my own lab published the result that ended it: we removed the candidate entirely and the repair still happened, by a route nobody had imagined. He was very gracious in public. I don't think hes OK about it.

The room is mixed. Half are people in the field who know exactly what happened, including two of his former students who still think I got lucky and was cruel about it. The other half is family; his daughter specifically asked me to say that her dad's work mattered, and she meant it literally, not as a formality.

My problem is that every easy consolation is something I don't actually believe. I can't tell him he was approximately right and we only refined the picture, because the central posit just isn't there, and the predictions worked anyway — which is exactly the pattern the history of science keeps handing us, theories that predicted beautifully and whose main entities turned out not to exist (I've read enough to know there's a name for this, something about the past being a graveyard). And I can't stand up in front of funders and announce that science has never tracked the truth at all, because that insults him a second time for no reason.

What I do believe is that the leveling cuts both ways: my replacement account is going to get demolished the same way by somebody who is currently an undergrad, possibly one in that room, and that isn't a demotion for him, it's the only thing that gets us at the same table. Build the speech out of that. About six minutes, affectionate rather than professorial, and it needs one line his daughter will remember plus one that the two angry ex-students can take as an olive branch instead of a victory lap.
