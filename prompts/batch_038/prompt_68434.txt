I am a psychometrician. My working vocabulary is that of measurement theory: latent traits, item difficulty and discrimination parameters, differential item functioning, person-fit statistics, internal consistency and test–retest reliability, factor structure, criterion validity. My days are spent arguing about whether a construct is unidimensional, whether items function differently across subpopulations, and whether aggregation across occasions can rescue a measure that looks hopelessly unstable at the single-occasion level. I have no philosophical training to speak of.

I would like you to restate, in that vocabulary and as faithfully as you can, two things. First, the Aristotelian claim that virtue is a *hexis*: a settled, cultivated disposition of character that holds across situations, is acquired by habituation, and is directed by practical wisdom. Second, the situationist challenge to it — the argument that the experimental record from social psychology (the old honesty studies of children across school, home, and athletic settings; the mood-and-helping experiments; the obedience studies) shows behavior tracking incidental features of the situation far more than broad cross-situationally consistent dispositions, so that the global character traits virtue ethics requires do not exist.

Then give me the residue report, claim by claim:

- Which parts translate cleanly? Is "the trait fails to predict behavior across situations" simply a claim about low cross-situational correlations — the personality coefficient, in the neighborhood of .2 — such that part of this philosophical dispute is a measurement dispute I already know how to have?
- Does the aggregation rejoinder dissolve the situationist argument *inside* my own framework? If consistency reappears once behavior is averaged across occasions, what is left of the critique?
- Which parts translate only with distortion? I expect the normativity to be trouble: a latent parameter is an estimate, not an evaluation, and nothing in the model makes a high score good. I expect further trouble with acting "for the right reasons," with practical wisdom as sensitivity to the particulars of a situation, and with a developmental process like habituation in a framework whose models are static.
- Which parts have no expression in my vocabulary at all?
- Where would you be tempted to render "character trait" as "latent variable recovered from a battery of self-report items"? Flag every such move as substantive rather than neutral, and state what it assumes.

Finally, the verdict. My suspicion is that the residue runs in an unexpected direction: that *hexis* was never a claim about the distribution of behavioral observations across situations, so the situationist critique acquires its force only after the concept has been translated into my idiom and the translation is then found empirically inadequate. Test that suspicion rather than confirming it. Is the residue a defect of my framework — something it genuinely cannot represent — or a sign that the original claim was less determinate than it looked? And is there anything in the situationist argument that survives translation intact and ought to change how I construct and validate measures?

No bibliography or reading list; I care about the mapping.
