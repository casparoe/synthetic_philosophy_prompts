ok so I'm in a reading group doing the Meno and one of the guys — call him D — has decided the value problem is a nothingburger, and his whole case rests on taking the Daedalus bit completely at face value. His line: Socrates compares true beliefs to those statues that wander off unless they're tied down, and knowledge to the same statues once tethered. So knowledge isn't some extra ingredient bolted onto true belief, its just true belief that stays put. Stability — not justification, not credit, not reliability — and the entire modern industry arguing about why knowledge beats mere true belief is missing that the answer is sitting right there in the image: a thing you have to keep reacquiring is worth less than a thing you don't. He also reckons this makes Plato a reliabilist avant la lettre, becuase a reliably produced belief is exactly one that doesn't wander.

Can you audit this for me? I want the correspondences laid out properly — statue, owner, the running away, the tether, the labour of tethering, the untethered ones you keep having to replace, the price they fetch, whatever else the image is carrying — and then the disanalogies sorted into the ones that actually threaten D's use of it and the ones that are just decorative. A few I've been chewing on and want your take on:

- the statues move themselves, so the image isn't neutral about belief — it bakes in that untethered true belief is intrinsically restless, which is a substantive empirical claim about minds, not a feature of statues
- nothing can make me lose the belief the slave boy ends up with about the diagonal, so there's nothing to tether there, and the image seems to predict zero value difference exactly where Socrates wants the biggest one
- a tether can be a chain: a belief that stays put because it's load-bearing for someone's identity and never gets reopened is just as hard to lose, and D's account seems forced to call that knowledge
- is the value the image delivers only instrumental and agent-relative? if you never revisit the question, the runaway risk never cashes out, which sits awkwardly with wanting knowledge to be better as such
- and in the dialogue the tethering gets hooked up to recollection, so the tether isn't evidence piled on top but something recovered from inside — does that rescue D or finish him?

Then tell me straight whether "stability is the whole difference" survives the audit, and if it doesn't, give me a better analogy for the knowledge/true-belief gap — one that does the work D wants done without smuggling in what the statues smuggle in.
