Been going through the modern stochastic thermodynamics treatments of Maxwell's demon and there's a pattern I keep noticing that I can't find a word for. Every time somebody proposes a demon that looks like it beats the second law, the fix is to locate a compensating entropy cost somewhere in the cycle — but which step pays the bill keeps changing. Older accounts put it in the measurement itself, then Bennett argued measurement can be done reversibly so the cost migrated to memory erasure, then the autonomous-demon models push it into the demon's tape or information reservoir, and the recent feedback-control inequalities put it in the mutual information between system and controller. Nobody ever loses the argument; the bill just relocates to a different step and the second law comes out whole again.

Is there an established name for this — a diagnosis for when a conservation-style principle gets saved by moving where the cost sits rather than by any new mechanism? I know about ad hoc auxiliary hypotheses and Popper's immunizing strategies, but those feel wrong here, because the total really is pinned down and the relocations often come with sharper quantitative bounds rather than vaguer ones. So: what's the nearest existing concept, how does this differ from it, and if nothing fits, coin me a term with a real definition. What I mostly want is the carving — what would seperate a legitimate relocation (an actual advance) from a suspicious one, and does this pattern put the second law anywhere near unfalsifiable, or is it more like ordinary balance-sheet accounting where the terms move around but the total is what's being tested?
