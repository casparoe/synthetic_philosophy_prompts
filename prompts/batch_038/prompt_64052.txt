So I've just come off nearly three hours arguing with my flatmate Sam about the procreation asymmetry and my head is absolutely gone, but I've had an idea and I need you to help me turn it into something I can actually run in a room.

Where we got stuck: we both find it obvious that there's a strong moral reason not to bring into existence a child who'd have a wretched, suffering-dominated life, and we both find it obvious that there's no comparable reason to bring into existence a child who'd have a blissful life. Nobody's itching to create happy people who'd otherwise never exist. But we also both think it can't be right that never existing is straightforwardly good for the person who never exists, because there isn't one. And that's the whole mess, isn't it: the absence of the bad counts even when nobody's around to enjoy its absence, but the absence of the good doesn't count unless somebody's being deprived. Sam reckons this is a word trick dressed up as an insight — that if you phrase the same case differently people's verdicts flip around wholesale, and that nobody actually holds the view once you pin them down and make them choose.

I can't settle that by talking any more, so I want to test it on twenty people. You're an expert on the population ethics of coming into existence and also someone who's actually facilitated a room before, so please think hard about the design first — sketch out a few genuinely different options for which choices to put to them, which seat to put them in (deciding for someone? judging from outside? deciding for a group of possible people?), what to record and how — and only then commit to one.
