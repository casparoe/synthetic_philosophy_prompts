I'm a philosopher of chemistry by trade, though I teach the grad metaphysics seminar every few years, which is how I ended up down this hole. We were reading the usual conditional-analysis material and its discontents, and I found I could recite the standard toolkit fine: Martin's finks and Lewis's attempt to patch the analysis around them, masks and antidotes, mimics where the object lacks the disposition but behaves as if it had one, and the older Rylean point that dispositions are multi-track. What I couldn't do was place a configuration I keep running into in actual chemistry, and a colleague in materials assured me it's completely mundane over there, which makes me suspect the metaphysicians just haven't bothered. I'm trying to knock something into shape for a workshop on powers in the spring and I'd rather not stand up and announce a discovery that somebody made in 2004.

Here's the pattern. A thing is genuinely disposed to undergo some reaction, the stimulus condition obtains, the reaction starts — and the first bit of the manifestation produces exactly the condition that stops the rest of it. Not a fink: the basis is untouched, the metal under the film is as disposed to oxidize as it ever was, and it stays that way. Not a mask in the ordinary sense either: there's no independent interferer, no styrofoam, no sorcerer, nothing extrinsic wrapped around the object. I know people argue about whether masks can be intrinsic, but in an intrinsic-mask case the blocker is still supposed to be a standing feature of the thing, doing its blocking work whether or not the disposition ever gets going. Here the blocker only exists because the disposition got going. It is, so to speak, its own manifestation's exhaust. Aluminium in air is the canonical case: the oxide layer is literally the product of the oxidation it then prevents. Same shape when an enzyme's product inhibits the enzyme that made it, same shape in a pressure vessel whose relief valve is opened by the very pressure that would burst it, and arguably the same shape in the emotional case — someone disposed to blow up who, having started, is flooded with shame that cuts the episode off before it finishes.

There's a second wrinkle and I honestly dont know whether it's the same puzzle or a seperate one. Whether the self-blockade succeeds turns out to be a matter of rate. Slow, diffuse exposure lets the film form and hold; a big fast insult outpaces it and you get the full violent manifestation from an object that is, internally, exactly the same thing. So the truth of "if exposed, it would corrode" varies with how fast the stimulus is applied while nothing about the object's intrinsic makeup varies at all, and I don't see how a stimulus condition phrased in terms of *what* is applied rather than *how quickly* can capture that. Speeding up a scenario is supposed to be metaphysically innocent.

So: does this have a name? I want the nearest established concepts and a precise account of exactly where each one stops fitting — I'm braced for the answer to be "that's just an intrinsic mask" or "that's just a partial manifestation," and I think both of those describe the symptom rather than the mechanism, so tell me if I'm wrong about that and why. If nothing fits, coin something, but give me a real definition with boundary cases: does a match that consumes its own fuel count? a thermostat? a refractory period? what about a case where the manifestation destroys the basis outright instead of walling it off? I'd also like your view on whether this is a genuine problem for conditional analyses or only an artefact of sloppy stimulus-specification, and whether it has any bearing on the question of whether masks have to be extrinsic. No bibliography, please — I'll fill in the references myself once I know what I'm looking for. What I want is the carving, not the label.
