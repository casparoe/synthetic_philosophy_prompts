so we did Thomson's lamp in my metaphysics seminar this week and my TA thinks the whole thing is a pseudo-paradox, and the argument he leans on is an analogy. the lamp, he says, is just like a ball that bounces infinitely many times inside a finite stretch of time and then comes to rest: each bounce lasts less than the last the same way each interval between toggles is half the previous one, the bounces pile up against a final instant exactly like the toggles pile up against noon, and nobody thinks there's anything paradoxical about a ball that bounces forever and then lies still. so accepting that the lamp has run through its infinite sequence by noon is supposed to cost nothing extra. on his account the only thing Thomson's argument actually shows is that the description doesnt pin down whether the lamp is on or off at noon, which he compares to complaining that 0.999... = 1 needs a convention, and he says Benacerraf made basically this point decades ago.

one person in my section pushed back and said the analogy runs backwards: the ball's state at the accumulation instant is forced on you by continuity of position and velocity, whereas the lamp's binary state isnt, so the ball can't be used to show the lamp case is unproblematic. TA called it nitpicking.
