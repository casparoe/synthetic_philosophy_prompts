Quick backstory: I'm a data engineer at an insurance company and I've spent the last eight months buried in entity resolution — deciding when two customer records are actually the same human. Somewhere around month three I turned into a Millian about names with out meaning to, and last week I had to explain to a claims adjuster why our system simultaneously believed two incompatible things about her favorite customer, which is when this stopped being an abstraction for me.

Here's the view I've talked myself into. There is no layer of "sense" sitting between a name and its bearer. There's just the referent plus a tag. When "Hesperus is Phosphorus" strikes you as informative and "Hesperus is Hesperus" doesn't, nothing different is going on in the *content* of those two claims — the whole difference lives in what the thinker knows about which tags co-refer. Frege's puzzle, on my reading, is cognitive bookkeeping that got mistaken for semantics. I think of belief ascriptions as reports about tags-in-heads plus the world, and I think the hard cases Fregeans wave around dissolve once you stop pretending the tags carry content of their own.

So: what would count against this? I want a map of where the position is actually exposed, not a survey of the debate.

Things I specifically want you to deal with:

- The cases that are hard for me rather than merely hard-looking. Empty names are the one I can feel. "Vulcan" does real work in astronomers' reasoning while picking out nothing, and I want to know whether that's fatal or whether there's a standard rescue — pretense, gappy propositions, some free-logic move — and what each rescue costs the view it's rescuing.
- The rational/irrational material: Kripke's Pierre, and the nastier version where somebody is *told* that Hesperus is Phosphorus, nods, writes it down, and then carries on not updating. Can my view say what that person believes? And does it have to retreat into "well, she believes it *under a tag*" every single time it's pressed? If yes, how many of those retreats before the thesis stops asserting anything?
- What my view predicts that a Fregean view doesn't, and what would falsify it — thought experiments are fine, but if there's anything in the psycholinguistics of naming, coreference, or anaphora that bears on it, tell me. If the honest answer is "nothing observable, it's a difference in how you describe the same facts," say that plainly. I want to know whether I'm holding a position that risks anything at all, and whether being unfalsifiable is a defect here or just what a view of this kind is supposed to look like.
- Which of these objections a serious Millian would *concede* counts against them, and which they'd swat away, and on what grounds. I don't want only the prosecution's case.

And here's the thing that actually scared me. Our rating engine returns *different answers for the same person* depending on which identifier the record was recieved under. Same human, same risk profile; the tag from the broker feed says approved at price X, the tag from the portal says declined. My team calls it a duplicate-key bug and files a ticket. But structurally it's Pierre: two co-referential tags driving contradictory dispositions inside one system. One wrinkle — sometimes the two tags live in genuinely separate subsystems that never talk to each other, so there is no single place holding both. Does that matter philosophically, or is it just where the metaphor happens to break?

Take that case seriously: tell me whether it's evidence that tags carry content after all, or bad data engineering wearing a philosophy costume. And if you can build a *cleaner* version — two tags in one head or one subsystem, where the duplication can't be explained away as an architecture flaw, and where the system still behaves rationally under each tag separately — construct it in detail, because that's the case that would make me give the view up. If you think no such clean case can be built, argue that too.

Finally, rank the defeaters: fatal at the top, merely awkward at the bottom, and be willing to conclude "your position is well insulated, and that's fine" if that's where the evidence goes. I'd rather get that than get flattered.
