I teach a required methods course to analysts at a public-health forecasting unit: quantitatively strong, one semester of probability behind them, fluent in conditional probability and expectation, no philosophy whatsoever. In three weeks I have ninety minutes and a whiteboard, and the session has to leave Reflection — van Fraassen's principle relating what I believe now to what I now expect myself to believe later — securely in place, because the unit is about to adopt a pre-registration protocol and the staff justify it with a claim I cannot yet classify: that what you forecast now should equal what you expect yourself to forecast once the data arrive. Part of what I need from you is a verdict on whether that is a garbled statement of Reflection or a distinct claim that happens to be true if one expects to conditionalize, since the answer decides whether I spend ten minutes separating two ideas or ten minutes correcting one.

Beyond that I want the architecture, not the explanation:

- The order the components have to arrive in and what each one depends on. My guess is that the assumption of updating by conditionalizing on evidence has to be explicit before anything else means anything; tell me what specifically breaks in the rest of the session if I'm wrong about that.

- Which single case carries it. I have two candidates. In the first, an analyst is certain that after next week's stakeholder briefing she will be 0.9 confident the intervention works, and she asks whether she is permitted to hold at 0.6 until then. In the second, the same analyst knows she will review the numbers at the end of a night shift and will then be just as confident for that reason. Tell me which one should do the carrying, what features it needs and what it must not have — in particular, whether the later self has to be unimpaired for the example to have any force at all — and what the rejected case is best spent doing at a later point in the sequence.

- The misconceptions this audience will bring or manufacture, each with the exact point where it must be headed off and whether that point falls before or after the step it would otherwise poison. At minimum I expect: that Reflection is a general policy of deferring to your future self and therefore a piece of epistemic humility; that a tired, angry, or well-briefed future self refutes the principle outright; and that the principle is a rule of practical prudence about avoiding losses rather than a constraint on belief.

- What I should openly refuse to cover at this level, and say so to them. I assume the diachronic Dutch book construction is out of reach in ninety minutes, but tell me whether dropping it leaves the principle visibly unsupported for an audience that will immediately want to know why it should care.

- One closing question whose answers would separate people who have the principle from people who have memorized a sentence about it.
