Retired union guy, now basically the volunteer archivist for my local — boxes of minutes, a scrapbook, three plaques, a local paper that went under in the 90s. I've been reading around the memory-and-history stuff for fun and I keep hitting a wall: there's a pattern I see constantly and I don't have a word for it that fits.

Two examples, both from my own local.

Our founding story, told every spring at the memorial, is that the men walked out of the mill because a young kid got crushed in the line shaft and the company refused to shut it down. The minutes and the paper say the walkout was over a ten percent cut, and the death came eleven days later, in the middle of the strike. I put the minutes in the newsletter with photocopies. Nobody argued. Two of the old-timers thanked me and said it was about time someone pinned the dates down. At the memorial the next year the president told it exactly the way it's always been told, death first, and those same two were nodding along — not lying, not embarrassed, and they'd have recited the right dates if you'd quizzed them on the spot.

Second one: the plaque in the hall credits a business agent with saving the pension in '76. A woman on the staff did most of the actual work and he signed the letters. Her family got the record corrected in writing, the local voted to accept the correction, and a decade later the anniversary dinner story is still about him, told by people who know otherwise. It happens alot, in smaller ways too.

So: does this have a name? A group that takes a factual correction on board, sincerely, and the correction just never reaches the story it actually performs. It isn't denial, they aren't resisting the evidence. It isn't forgetting, they retain the correction fine. It isn't propaganda, nobody's coordinating it and the tellers aren't deceived. And its not quite "myth" as people usually mean that word, because nobody is fooling themselves about the facts.

I've half-heard of collective memory, and invented tradition, and "memory versus history" as a slogan. Tell me which of those comes closest and exactly where each one fails to cover what I'm describing — I want the boundaries drawn, not a reading list. If none of them is it, coin something and define it properly: what counts as an instance, what doesn't, and above all how you'd tell my thing apart from (a) a group that's just wrong and doesn't know it, (b) a group that knows it's telling a story and half-winks at it, the way everyone does with national founding myths, and (c) a group that has buried the correction so it isn't even available to anyone. Is the difference in what people believe, or in where the belief sits, or something else entirely?

And if you have a view on the ought side — whether the telling or the record is the one that should give way when they collide — say it, but do the carving first.

Last thing: be straight with me about confidence. Tell me how sure you are about the nearest-concept mapping, and single out the one claim in your whole answer you're least certain of. I'd much rather have an honest hedge than a tidy taxonomy you're improvising as you type.
