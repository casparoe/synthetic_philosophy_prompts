so my school's philosophy club is doing a whole free will thing this month and I'm supposed to give one of the short talks, and I've gotten kind of stuck on this one example my teacher threw out. He called it sphexishness, which apparently was coined by Hofstadter and then Dennett ran with it in a book called Elbow Room. The example is this digger wasp, Sphex something-or-other, that provisions a burrow for its larvae: it paralyses a cricket (or a grasshopper, I forget which) and drags it to the mouth of the burrow, then goes inside on its own to check that the tunnel's clear, then comes back out and drags the prey in. Now if an ethologist messes with it while it's down in the tunnel and scoots the cricket a few inches away, the wasp comes out, finds the prey displaced, drags it back to the lip of the burrow, and then — instead of pulling it in — goes back down to re-check the tunnel. Move the cricket again and the whole routine repeats. There's no limit to how many times you can do this, it just loops forever.

Here's what I can't get my head around. In class we did the compatibilist conditional analysis, where saying you could have done otherwise just means you *would* have done otherwise if you'd chosen or wanted to, and the whole debate gets framed around this ability-to-do-otherwise business. But the wasp seems to sail through a ton of that stuff: there are obviously heaps of nearby situations where it behaves differently than it actually did, since all it takes is a cricket three inches to the left and it does something else. It's not sitting there inert, it's constantly adjusting. And still it's the poster child for being a mindless machine, the thing we're supposedly *afraid* of being if determinism is true. So which knob is actually missing? My own guess, which I'm not confident about at all, is that it isn't flexibility but flexibility about flexibility — the wasp has no way to register that its own routine is looping, no representation along the lines of *I already checked that tunnel two minutes ago and it was fine*, so it can't audit its own conditionals. Related thing that's bugging me more: suppose you bolted some genuine randomness onto the wasp, so every time it came out it flipped a coin on whether to re-drag the cricket or wander off and start a new burrow. Would that make it one bit freer, or just a worse wasp? I have this feeling the answer to that last question is the actually important part of the whole example and I can't articulate why.

Could you explain what's really going on here?
