For thirty-seven years I worked in metrology — first calibrating electrical standards, later sitting on committees that argued about how units ought to be defined and how uncertainty ought to be reported. Two professional reflexes were beaten into me: an artifact-based standard is a stopgap, because the artifact drifts and cannot be interrogated about its own stability; and a measurement that perturbs the measurand must either be corrected for or have the perturbation carried explicitly in the uncertainty budget. Since retiring I have taken up philosophy of science, and then, by way of a neighbour's long illness, philosophy of psychiatry, and it strikes me that the central difficulty of psychiatric classification is a metrological difficulty in unfamiliar dress. Hacking's point about looping effects — that classifying people makes up new kinds of people, who in turn change what the classification picks out — reads to me as the statement that the instrument is not separable from the measurand, and that the measurand is not even fixed while it is being measured. When the 2019 revision of the SI anchored the units to fixed numerical values of fundamental constants rather than to a cylinder in a vault, the motive was precisely to escape drift and to make the standard independent of any particular realization of it. I want to know whether anything analogous is available here, and what must be surrendered to get it.

So before I read further I would like the specification rather than the candidates. Write out the desiderata that any account of psychiatric classification — any theory of what a diagnosis is, and of what makes a diagnostic taxonomy a good one — must satisfy to count as adequate. I want the complete list, each item accompanied by the consideration or the case that motivates it, and I want the items sorted: which are non-negotiable requirements of any classification worth the name, which are the preferences of a particular camp wearing the costume of requirements, and which pairs or triples cannot be satisfied together, so that the list is itself a result. Please consider at least the following as candidates for inclusion, though you should add to them, sharpen them, or reject them as you judge best: agreement between raters and across sites; stability of a category over time and across populations; validation by something other than the symptom list itself — the older program of establishing a diagnosis through its clinical description, its course, family aggregation, exclusion of other conditions, and laboratory findings; clinical utility in the sense of actually guiding treatment; avoidance of the circularity in which a category is validated by response to a treatment that was administered because of the category; and what I shall call the looping constraint, that a taxonomy must remain adequate to phenomena it is itself reshaping. I am especially interested in whether a taxonomy can simultaneously be transparent and communicable to the people it classifies — which is what makes it usable in clinic and consequential in society — and immune to movement once those people take the category up, organize around it, or refuse it. If that combination is impossible rather than merely expensive, say so plainly and give the argument. Tell me, too, which single desideratum does the most damage to the most widely held positions.

Once the list is settled, score the main contenders against it, briefly: the descriptive, deliberately operational approach that has governed the American diagnostic manual since 1980; the dimensional, biomarker- and circuit-based research programs proposed in its place; and the frankly pragmatic positions that treat categories as instruments, judged by consequences rather than by correspondence. I am not asking for a reading list and I have no appetite for a survey; I want a committed position. Take a definite stand on which of the desiderata are genuine and which are camp prejudice, on whether the joint-satisfiability question has a negative answer, and on which contender comes closest to surviving the list — with reasons, not with an even-handed statement of both sides. Be equally definite in the other direction at the end: tell me where my metrological framing distorts the problem instead of illuminating it. My suspicion is that there is no analogue of fixing a constant, because any candidate constant would have to be a feature of persons that does not change in response to being known, and I am not convinced such a feature exists; but I would rather you told me whether that suspicion is correct or is a physicist's prejudice smuggled into a field where it has no purchase.
