I'm prepping the first half of a grad seminar on the metaphysics of chance, and I wanted to use Bertrand's chord problem as the warm-up for the session on priors. Trouble is, I can't state the moral of the thing in a way that survives pushback. Here's my plan as it currently stands: I tell them that the principle of indifference is refuted by the paradox, because you can derive 1/3, 1/2 and 1/4 for the probability that a randomly chosen chord of a circle is longer than a side of the inscribed equilateral triangle, and all three derivations are perfectly legitimate applications of indifference to one and the same question — so a principle that licenses three incompatible answers to a single question can't be a principle about truth at all. Then I say the fix is that probability talk only becomes determinate once you specify the physical procedure that generates the chord, at which point the ambiguity disappears and the paradox dissolves. I've been teaching something like this for years in the intro course, where nobody complains.

A colleague down the hall tells me it's a mess and that half my incoming students will already know it's a mess. His objection, roughly: there's no contradiction, because the three derivations apply indifference to three different variables — an arc on the circumference, a distance along a radius, a point in the disc — so indifference never asserts anything inconsistent; the only trouble is that "pick a chord at random" fails to say which variable, which makes it a piece of sloppy language rather than the refutation of anything. On his telling the paradox is basically a grammar lesson, and I shouldn't be spending an hour of seminar time on it. I don't want to just cave to him, though, partly because it feels like he's explained away the thing rather than answered it.

So: who's right, and what's actually left standing? A few specific spots where I need help. Second: is the "just specify the procedure" move really a fix, or does it push indifference one level up, so that I now owe an account of how the procedure itself got chosen — and if it does regress, where does it stop?
