I run the psychometrics group at a large-scale assessment publisher. My working vocabulary is item writing and review, IRT calibration, item difficulty and discrimination parameters, guessing and upper-asymptote terms, local independence, dimensionality checks, differential item functioning flagged as uniform or nonuniform, measurement invariance across groups and languages, and construct-irrelevant variance — together with the standing methodological commitment that when an item behaves differently across groups matched on the latent trait, the item is defective and the construct is not.

Last night I spent four hours arguing about the Knobe effect with a friend who teaches philosophy of action. She walked me through the material: the corporate-officer vignette pair in which the only substantive change is whether the foreseen side effect of the new program harms or helps the environment, the officer's professed indifference to that side effect and single-minded pursuit of profit, and the resulting split — roughly four out of five respondents say he harmed the environment intentionally, roughly one in five say he helped it intentionally. Then she walked me through the competing diagnoses of that asymmetry: that the moral valence of the side effect enters the very content of the concept of intentional action rather than merely biasing its application; that the harm case is different because the officer accepted the harm as the price of his goal, whereas in the help case the benefit was genuinely incidental; that respondents infer more skill, awareness, or freedom in the harm case; that blame is assigned first and the intentionality verdict is read off it; that what drives the judgment is whether the outcome expresses the agent's standing attitudes; and, above all of these, the meta-level dispute over whether each account is a claim about the competence underlying the judgment or about performance errors in producing it.

Her position is that the asymmetry is a discovery about the concept. My position is that it is what a single dichotomous forced-choice item with an unmatched covariate looks like, and that she is inferring conceptual structure from an instrument nobody has ever validated.

Restate that whole debate — each diagnosis, plus the competence-versus-performance dispute — in my measurement vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. For each claim, tell me what it becomes: a location shift in the item's difficulty parameter across levels of a moral-valence grouping variable; nonuniform DIF; a violation of local independence because evaluative judgment and intentionality judgment load on a common factor; a response-mapping failure in an item that should have been polytomous; a multidimensional model whose second dimension is evaluative. Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually want. Flag every point at which you had to import an assumption from measurement theory to make the philosophy fit — above all the assumptions that there is a single latent trait of which items are more or less valid indicators, and that variance traceable to moral valence is construct-irrelevant by definition rather than by discovery. Say which of her claims translates intact, which translates only with distortion, and which cannot be formulated in my framework at all. Finally, give me your verdict on the residue: does it show that psychometrics is missing something, that some of her claims are exactly the kind that cannot be cast as a defect of the instrument, or does it show that our disagreement was less substantive than it felt once the vocabularies are shared? Treat my own position the same way — I am not looking for a ruling in my favor, and if the honest translation leaves her standing and me relying on an assumption I have not earned, say so plainly.
