Write a dialogue of roughly 900 words between two evolutionary naturalists who disagree about whether the origin of multicellular individuality — a population of single-celled organisms that acquires a sequestered germ line and begins policing cheating mutants — is a crane or a skyhook. The first holds that this transition is the paradigm case of a crane: a mind-last, substrate-neutral process that accelerates design work without borrowing any from elsewhere. The second, equally committed to there being no designer, argues that the new higher-level individual acquires a fitness only once lower-level competition has already been suppressed, so the explanation presupposes what it was supposed to produce — and that a process recognizable as doing design work only under a description framed in terms of its outcome is, by the criterion's own lights, a skyhook. Keep both characters on the mechanics (compartmentalization, limited migration, policing, fidelity of heredity, whether higher-level fitness gradients come in degrees or arrive at a threshold) and on what it would take for the crane/skyhook distinction to have determinate application conditions; neither may be a strawman, and neither may retreat to a general defense of naturalism. They must answer each other's actual moves rather than restate their positions, and the exchange should close with the disagreement narrowed to a single precise question that neither has settled. No citations, no name-dropping of the literature, and no invented empirical studies: assume the vocabulary of cranes, skyhooks, and greedy reductionism is shared between them.
