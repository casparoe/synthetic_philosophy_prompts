I'm writing the final for my upper-level seminar on the birth of modern logic, and the last session went of the rails in a way I haven't stopped thinking about: a student handed in a paper arguing that Frege's posthumously published political diary isn't just an embarrassing footnote about the man but actual evidence about the philosophy, and half the room agreed with her. So I want one essay question — 2 hours, 30 marks — that makes them argue the inferential question properly rather than reciting what's in the diary or announcing that platonism has no politics.

It should force them to say what shape an argument from the diary to the work would have to take: which of Frege's commitments (the objectivity of thoughts, the third realm, logic as applying to anything that can think or speak, the rejection of psychologism) could be supported or undermined by his political views, and whether the link is evidential at all or merely explanatory — i.e. whether the diary could tell us why he held a view without bearing on whether the view is true. I also want the reverse direction in play: whether the philosophy might explain the diary instead.

Give me the question itself, a model answer of about 700 words that actually picks a side instead of hedging both ways, and a marking scheme that names the specific moves separating a first from a 2:1, with marks attached. Then a short list of mistakes that should cost points — e.g. waving "genetic fallacy" at any biographical argument whatsoever, or treating the diary as though it were the whole of Frege's non-technical output. Don't build in anything that needs citations; students have the relevant extracts in the reader.
