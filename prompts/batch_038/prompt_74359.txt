So I'm one of the grad students organizing our department's workshop series this year and we've kind of hit a wall. The two faculty running it have been sniping at each other in footnotes for close to a decade and it's made the seminar genuinely unpleasant. One trained as a historical climatologist — tree rings, ice cores, long documentary price series, a lot of R — and holds that volcanic and climatic events are real causes of historical outcomes, and that refusing to say so out of political delicacy is just obscurantism with a moral alibi. The other is a social historian of famine and poor relief who thinks the whole "the eruption did it" framing is environmental determinism wearing a lab coat: famines get made by storage policy, market structure and who had claims on food, not by weather, and attribution language quietly exculpates the humans who were distributing the grain while people died. Neither is stupid and neither is arguing in bad faith, which is exactly why it's exhausting.

For the last session I want to hand them a document to finish instead of letting them have another go, so: draft the joint statement that a real adversarial collaboration between those two camps would end up with. Not a verdict on who's right, not a dialogue, and not a diagnosis of why they talk past each other — an actual text both could put their names on. To stop them from just replaying Tambora-and-the-year-without-a-summer, or the old argument about whether Laki mattered for Europe, here's the case I've invented for them to work on, and I want the statement to be about *this* case, concretely:

- A valley in an inland agrarian province, call it the Sarnau: one market town of about 9,000 and sixty-odd villages, mostly grain, some pastoral upland.
- A large distant eruption, dated by sulfate in two ice cores to 1841–42 with a ±2 year uncertainty on the layer counting. No local ashfall, no local crop-burning report, so any mechanism has to be aerosol-driven cooling and a shortened growing season.
- Regional tree-ring latewood density: about 2.5σ below the mean in 1842 and 1.9σ in 1843 across 14 sites, of which 6 are inside the valley.
- Weekly wheat prices in the town market roughly quadruple between the 1842 harvest and spring 1843.
- Parish burial registers for 22 of the 25 parishes show burials at 2.3× the 1830–40 baseline in 1843, heavily skewed to under-fives and over-sixties. The registers for three upland parishes are simply missing for that year and nobody knows why.
- In October 1842 the provincial governor issued permits moving an estimated 40% of the recorded harvest out of the valley to a coastal city where prices were already higher. A 1798 ordinance required the town granary to hold eighteen months of subsistence; an audit in January 1843 found three months. Tithe in kind was enforced that year rather than commuted to cash as it had been in most of the previous decade.
- A magistrate's report describes fourteen food riots between April and June 1843; six districts report none at all, and the six are exactly the six where the tithe collector was also the local justice.
- Two neighboring valleys, 80 and 150 km out, have their own ring-density series and show the same 1842–43 anomaly. One had the same export-permit regime and a similar granary audit failure; the other had a communal granary tradition and a standing ban on grain leaving the valley.

What I want out of the document:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can actually sign — about what the proxies license and what they don't, about what the paperwork shows, about what "cause" is doing in a sentence like "the eruption caused the famine of 1843," and about the determinist legacy in the discipline and why it's live rather than a strawman. Ten to twelve of them. None vacuous: if a sentence would survive being vague, cut it.
2. A disagreements table — issue | wording Camp A would sign | wording Camp B would sign | genuine or merely verbal? Each side's formulation has to be one its own side would accept without wincing, and the two formulations of the same row can't be paraphrases of each other or the row is fake. Mark at least one row as purely verbal and explain what's being equivocated on; I'd guess it's "agency" or "historical actor," but you tell me.
3. The cruxes: sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one side. For each, a concrete test or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance in a form someone could score five years later. My hunch is the big one is a difference-in-differences across the three valleys — same cooling signal, different grain institutions — so build that out properly, including what the design can't distinguish and what would have to be true of the archival record for it to work. Then say whether there's a second crux I'm missing; I suspect one is whether the missing upland registers and the six silent districts are evidence of absence or produced silence, and that the two camps read the same gaps differently for reasons that have nothing to do with volcanoes.
4. A short joint glossary fixing the meaning of maybe five terms they keep sliding between — cause, agency, determinism, vulnerability, something like "historical actor" — stated as definitions both would use in print.
5. A ~150-word paragraph, signed by both, for a general-audience outlet about what happened in the Sarnau in 1843. This is the hard part, because it can't fudge and it can't hedge into meaninglessness; if you think no such paragraph exists, say so and show me the closest thing that does.

Aim for around 1,400 words total. And be honest with me at the end about which parts of this are unsignable — if one of those two people, written realistically, would refuse some claim you've put in the agreed section because signing it would cost them something in their own literature, pull it out and tell me why rather than quietly weakening it until it means nothing.
