I'm in a rationalist-adjacent Discord where we run a weekly "hard cases" thread, and this week's was a guy who noticed a race condition in an open-source library that a bunch of hospital scheduling systems depend on, decided it wasn't his problem, told nobody, and six months later the bug ate a weekend's worth of appointments. One of the regulars — smart guy, reads a fair amount of the causation literature, argues like a debater — shut down every attempt to discuss whether the omission actually caused anything by saying "inaction is a choice," full stop, and then treated that as settling the causal question and the blame question simultaneously. I tried to push back and got nowhere, partly because its not clear to me what he's even committed to. The slogan seems to be doing at least three separate jobs: something about agency (he chose not to report it, and choosing not to X is a real mental event with a real explanation), something about causation (his silence is a cause of the outage in the same sense that shipping the bug would have been), and something about blameworthiness (he deserves the same treatment as whoever introduced the bug). I can't tell wether that's ambiguity living in the sentence or just him running three arguments at once and letting the vagueness do the lifting. I also suspect there are readings I haven't thought of, including some deflationary one where the slogan isn't about omissions at all but is just denying that there's any metaphysically interesting difference between doing and not-doing.

What I want is the menu, not an essay. Lay out exactly five distinct precise claims that "inaction is a choice" could be making, numbered, ordered from the one that's essentially a platitude to the one that's most radical and hardest to swallow, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion — just the five items. For each: (i) what it precisely claims, stated so someone could actually agree or disagree with it; (ii) whether it's true, false, or genuinely contested, and the one-line reason why; and (iii) whether it would actually get my Discord guy what he wants, i.e. that the non-reporter caused the outage and is blameworthy the way a bug-writer would be. Tag the reading you think he's actually leaning on, and if two of your five are the pair people slide between without noticing, say which slide is happening right inside that item.

Two constraints on the five. First, at least one should engage the promiscuity worry head-on: if not-doing counts as causing, then my failure to water a garden in Australia causes those plants to die and my failure to be a competent surgeon in 1840 causes whatever happened there, so either omissions aren't causes or causation is absurdly promiscuous — and I want to know whether some reading of the slogan survives that, or whether it just changes the subject to something non-causal. Second, at least one should engage the legal side, because I'm fairly sure common law says you have no duty to rescue a stranger absent a special relationship, a contract, or one of those handful of state statutes that criminalize walking away, which looks like the law deliberately refusing to treat a bare omission as a cause while still managing to punish some of them — and that split might be exactly the distinction the slogan blurs. Name positions and problems, not papers, and don't give me a bibliography; I'm reading this on a plane with no wifi and I want the five items to be enough to argue with when I land.
