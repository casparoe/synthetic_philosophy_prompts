Suppose Bentham got dropped into a modern RL lab and shown a deep agent learning from a single scalar reward. Two questions.

First: he wanted pleasure and pain measured along seven dimensions — intensity, duration, certainty, nearness, fecundity, purity, and extent. How much of that survives when all you have is one number per timestep plus a discount factor? Which of the seven would he be delighted to see handled by the machinery, and which would he say the reward signal just fakes? (Fecundity and extent seem like the interesting cases, since one maps onto expected future return and the other onto how many agents you've got in the environment.)

Second, and I think more interesting: what these agents actually learn from isn't the level of the reward, it's the gap between what arrived and what was predicted. Would Bentham have to become a contrastivist about pleasure — only deviations from expectation feel like anything — and if so, can his agent suffer at all? Concretely: an agent whose reward singal sits at some constant low value forever, so no error signal ever fires. Nothing is registering as bad, moment to moment. Is that a bad life for the thing, and does the felicific calculus have the resources to say so, or does it come out neutral?
