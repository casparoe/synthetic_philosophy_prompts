Our department head opens every public lecture and every funding application with the same image: excavating a site is like reading a book backwards — you begin with the last page, work toward the first, and the reading destroys the page you have just read. In the applications the image is made to do two quite different jobs. In one passage it licenses the claim that, because there is no second reading, interpretation must be fixed while the trowel is still in the ground and the record has to be exhaustive at the moment of removal. A few pages later it licenses the near-opposite claim: because the book can only be read once, and we presently lack the means to read it well, we should stop opening it and invest instead in non-invasive survey and in leaving the greater part of every site in the ground. A colleague objects that the image is worse than ambiguous — that it treats a deposit as an authored message when most of what we recover was never meant to communicate anything at all.

I would like an audit of the analogy rather than a verdict on the colleague. Please give me: (1) an explicit mapping — what plays the role of the page, the sentence, the fixed order of pages, the author, the act of reading, the destruction of the page, and the completed book; (2) a separation of the disanalogies that genuinely bear on the two conclusions from those that are merely picturesque, with the reasons for each assignment; (3) an account of what the source domain quietly imports that the target lacks, especially regarding authorship, semantic content, and the determinacy of meaning in advance of reading; and (4) a judgment on whether each of the two conclusions survives once those imports are stripped away, or whether the analogy was carrying them the whole time.

Please make the audit concrete against the material in front of me rather than against archaeology in general. In one trench we have a shallow pit cut through a buried soil and filled in several distinguishable episodes; sherds from a single vessel refit across three of those fills and across the pit and a ditch that appears to pre-date it; two of the contexts that produced joining sherds are stratigraphically inverted relative to one another; the whole sequence sits beneath a ploughsoil that has been moving for centuries, with visible burrow disturbance along several interfaces; we excavated perhaps a tenth of the feature's extent and left the remainder beneath an existing road; and our record consists of plans, sections, photogrammetry, and boxed samples, none of which can be re-excavated. Tell me which commitments of the book analogy that material positively falsifies, and which it merely fails to support.

Finally, if the book is the wrong source domain, say what would serve better for these purposes — and be explicit about what work the replacement can and cannot do, since I still have to write the same two passages of the application.
