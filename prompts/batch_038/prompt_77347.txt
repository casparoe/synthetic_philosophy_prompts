Last night a friend and I argued for three hours about Hegel's remarks on war in the *Philosophy of Right* and got nowhere. His view: Hegel is recommending something. The long Remark to §324 says war preserves the ethical health of peoples just as wind keeps the sea from going foul during a prolonged calm, that successful wars have quelled domestic unrest and consolidated the state, and that corruption in nations would be the product of prolonged, let alone perpetual, peace — so whatever the metaphysical packaging, the upshot is that a stable peace among sovereign states is undesirable, and Hegel owns that conclusion. My view: he is recommending nothing but exhibiting the ideality of the finite. Since no authority stands above sovereign states, war cannot be excluded as a possibility, and the claim that finite things must be negated in order to be comprehended within a larger whole is a claim about what the state is, not about what anyone should want; on my reading the wind remark is a self-citation Hegel flags as coming from elsewhere and does no argumentative work.

We agree on the text and we agree on the history of the twentieth century. So what kind of disagreement is this? A verbal one, about what "preserves" and "ethical health" are doing? A substantive one that turns on whether the passage is an empirical-functional claim — testable, and probably false — or a conceptual claim in Hegel's own idiom, which no amount of historical evidence could touch? Or are we answering different questions under one heading, namely "does Hegel think war is good?" and "does his account of sovereignty require the permanent possibility of war?"?

I want the diagnosis argued for, not a survey of the literature on Hegel and war, and not verbatim quotations — paraphrase however you like. Then tell me what would count as progress: a textual test that could decide between the two readings, a distinction that dissolves the standoff, or a reason to think the standoff is permanent because the question is ill-posed. Finally, say how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the point you are least sure of. Please write in American English.
