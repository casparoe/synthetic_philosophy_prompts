So I run a tiny philosophy podcast — basically me, a mic, and eleven listeners — and next month's guest is a philosopher who defends karma as a serious theory of moral desert. Not the pop-culture "what goes around comes around" version. Her view, roughly: a person's actual lot in life — health, wealth, the family they're born into, how their plans tend to turn out — is what they deserve, and it's produced by an impersonal moral-causal order that tracks their own past intentional actions across a beginningless series of lives. No god keeping a ledger, no punisher; rebirth just supplies the long time horizon the account needs.

I want eight to ten questions that get past having her restate all of that, and each one needs a follow-up attatched, keyed to the answer she's most likely to give rather than a generic "and why do you think that?"

Things I definitely want pressed:

- She's a Buddhist, so there's no enduring self. Then what makes the fruit of a given deed land on *this* mindstream rather than on anything else downstream of it in the causal chain? She'll reach for causal continuity and the flame-passing-from-lamp-to-lamp move, so have a follow-up ready for that — I'm causally downstream of a great many dead people too.
- The series is beginningless, so there's no first undeserved state. Does an infinite chain of "each link is deserved" ever justify the chain, or does it just postpone the question forever, like debt that keeps getting refinanced?
- No enforcer. Some of the classical theistic versions needed a god precisely to hold the merit and demerit and make sure the outcome matched the deed. What in her impersonal picture makes moral facts causally potent at all, and who sets the exchange rate between deed and consequence?
- She will almost certainly insist that karma isn't retribution, it's just description — a regularity in nature, like fire burning, nobody is doing anything *to* you. I want a run of questions that makes that move progressively harder to keep holding, because desert seems to need somebody who gives what's owed, and once you remove that somebody it starts to look like moral luck with extra steps.
- Merit-transfer and repentance: if you can hand your merit over to the dead, or dedicate it to all beings, in what sense is the recipient's improved condition deserved by them?
- The "we could never know which deed produced which suffering" reply to the victim-blaming objection — is that a genuine limit on knowledge, or a way of making the theory unfalsifiable? What would actually count as evidence against it?
- Whether the whole thing is long-run prudence instead of morality: if I act well partly because it pays off in a better rebirth, am I being moral or just investing?

Put them in the order I'd actually ask them so the thread builds, and end on the question that forces her to say what would make her give the view up. No "what first drew you to this material?" filler, and don't ask her to recommend readings — I'll handle that part off-mic.
