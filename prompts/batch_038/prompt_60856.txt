At a talk on machine creativity last week, a speaker argued that no algorithmic system could originate a genuinely novel mathematical proof, and the engine of his argument was the slogan "checking a proof is easy; finding one is hard," which he offered as a plain restatement of what the P versus NP problem is about. Several of us left feeling that the slogan was doing work it cannot bear, without being able to say what. Lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making: what the finding and the checking are operations on, what counts as easy in each case (polynomial in which parameter, worst-case or average-case, with or without a certificate of bounded length supplied), and whether the resulting asymmetry is something proved, something merely conjectured, or in some readings simply false. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive open question, or an error — and then tell me which reading, if any, would license the conclusion he wanted, and whether what he actually needs is a complexity claim at all or rather a claim about undecidability of proof search in the systems we reason in. Please give me the readings as an ordered list with the verdicts attached.
