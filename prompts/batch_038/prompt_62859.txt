Our department runs a podcast where grad students interview people who work in the weird corners of decision theory, and I've got next week's guest booked: a philosopher who's written the most careful defense of the policy-first version of updateless decision theory — the one where you pick a whole policy up front from your starting distribution and then just execute it, never conditioning on anything you learn along the way. Audience is grad students plus a big chunk of rationalist-adjacent listeners who've read every blog post, so we can be technical.

Trouble is, any question I ask comes back as a walkthrough of counterfactual mugging or the absentminded driver, which half the audience has seen six times. Draft me eight to ten questions that go for the soft spots instead. I want at least:

- one on "updateless with respect to what" — the starting distribution does all the work, so what becomes of an agent whose initial prior was arbitrary or just plain wrong? Is it condemned to optimizing for a universe it isn't in?
- one on why-ain't-cha-rich style arguments and what they can't establish; I want the winner-picks problem in there somewhere
- one on whether the absentminded driver actually motivates updatelessness at all, given the dispute about what the driver can coherently believe once he's at the intersection
- one on policy choice versus act choice, and on the charge that the whole thing collapses into evidential decision theory plus a commitment device — if that's right, is updatelessness a decision theory or a theory of rational commitment?
- one on self-location and indexicals
- one on the idealization: no bounded agent can compute the optimal policy, so what is the view actually telling us to do?

Then I need one question built on a case I made up that I'll read out on air: you sign up for an experiment; the night before, a very reliable predictor forecast which policy you'd adopt, and wired up the payoff boxes accordingly. This morning the lab director hands you a sealed note telling you, truthfully, that the predictor got you wrong — it forecast a policy you did not in fact adopt. Do you now exploit the fact that you're sitting in the branch where the prediction failed, or does updatelessness still have you playing to the correlation between your policy and the forecast? Ask him whether his view delivers an answer, and which act the recommended policy performs. If he says the optimal policy just contains a clause for receiving the note, the follow-up should be the variant where the note genuinely couldn't have been anticipated, because it comes from a process outside anything in the prior.

Close with a question he can't duck with some version of "a fully rational agent in the limit would be updateless" — something that forces him to say what an agent like me should do differently on Monday morning.

For each question, give me a one-line follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. I've listened to a few of his talks, so I know roughly where he'll try to go.
