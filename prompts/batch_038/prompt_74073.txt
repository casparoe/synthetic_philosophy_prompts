I am a junior at a large public high school in the Midwest, and last spring, while avoiding a history paper, I read Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility. It has quietly ruined my school's discipline policy for me. The version I have in mind runs roughly like this: what you do in any situation flows from the way you are; to be truly responsible for what you do, you would have to be responsible for the way you are, at least in the mental respects that matter; but nobody is responsible for the way they are, because deliberately shaping your own character would require already possessing a character to shape it from, and then a reason to reshape it, and so on forever; therefore nobody is truly responsible for what they do. Two things struck me. First, the argument does not need determinism to be true, since a cause that is random is no more mine than one that is determined. Second, it destroys praise, prizes, and honor roll exactly as thoroughly as it destroys blame, detention, and expulsion — which is the part my school has not noticed.

Could you write me a piece of satire about a district that has noticed? I want excerpts from the student handbook of a fictional public high school, as revised for the current school year by a committee chaired by a dean of students who has become a hard skeptic about desert. Around 800 to 900 words. Please invent the school's name and the people; I would rather the piece not name any real philosopher in the text. Format it as numbered handbook sections with bureaucratic titles, plus one blank disciplinary referral form, plus at least three footnotes in the defensive register of a committee that has been hearing objections since August.

The following should all be in there, and the jokes should come from the ideas being right rather than from anything being garbled:

- A preamble that states the argument in flat administrative language and explicitly notes that the district has drawn no conclusion about whether determinism is true, and does not intend to.
- A section retiring blame vocabulary. Show it in the referral form's fields: what used to be "description of the incident," "was the act intentional," and "student's responsibility," and what they have been replaced with.
- A section on the prizes. Honor roll, awards night, class rank, valedictorian, and — the part I care about most — how a teacher is now supposed to write a letter of recommendation for a student who cannot be credited with her own grades.
- In-school suspension renamed as a forward-looking, non-punitive intervention, with conditions identical to last year's, and a footnote acknowledging that students have remarked on this.
- A subsection closing the libertarian loophole: the district considered adding uncaused mental events to the analysis and found that it helped no one.
- A subsection closing the compatibilist escape hatch, showing that phrases like "she meant to do it" and "that is simply the kind of person he is" have changed sides and now exculpate.
- An admission that the faculty continues to feel resentment, and that the handbook has no ground on which to fault them, since nobody chose their feelings either.
- The self-referential bind: the committee cannot be thanked, the policy cannot be credited to anyone's wisdom, and the student council's attempt to give the dean an award for adopting it.
- An appeals procedure in which the word for what an appellant claims has happened has been struck from the form.

Two constraints on the execution. Keep it deadpan: no winking at the reader, no narrator telling me what is funny, and no punchline that depends on the handbook having made a philosophical mistake, since I want its reasoning to be valid by its own lights and the comedy to come from the price of perfect consistency. And I would like the ending to leave it genuinely unresolved whether the piece is a reductio of the argument or of my school's discipline bureaucracy. Please do not include citations, a bibliography, or any references I would have to look up to check whether you invented them.
