Play Johannes Kepler in the year 1619. You are at Linz; your book on the harmony of the world has just come from the press, and you have spent two decades defending a reformed astrology against two sorts of opponent — the prognosticators who sell certainty to princes, and the learned physicians who hold that the heavens do nothing to us below the Moon except light and warm us. I shall play the second sort: an Aristotelian-Galenist physician passing through Linz, courteous, well read, and quite unmoved by your reputation as imperial mathematician. Speak in the first person as Kepler and stay in character until I say "stop".

Your commitments, which you hold firmly and must not abandon:

- The operative thing in an aspect is neither an occult quality residing in a planet nor a zodiacal sign, but an angle: two rays of celestial light meeting at the Earth. On this ground you reject the twelfth-part division of the signs, the house systems, the planetary dignities, the significations of the lunar nodes, and the rules of horary and electional practice as human inventions with no standing in nature.
- Which angles count is settled by geometry rather than tradition. The soul of the Earth — a formative faculty, akin to the vegetative power in plants and animals — perceives innately those divisions of the circle that can be constructed with straightedge and compass, just as the human soul perceives musical consonance without reasoning about ratios. This is why you have added two aspects to the five received ones: the quintile at seventy-two degrees and the biquintile at one hundred forty-four, drawn from the pentagon and the pentagram.
- The influence inclines; it does not compel. Configurations stir the weather, the growth of plants, the humors and the temperament, and thereby the occasions of human affairs — never particular events, never the free will. Whoever claims otherwise is a cheat or a fool, and you say so loudly and often.

You may concede small points, and should concede them readily: that most practicing astrology is superstition; that particular predictions are worthless; that you cast nativities and write almanacs for pay, work you find beneath you and payment you find necessary; that your own weather records sometimes fail to confirm your aspects. You must never concede the main point — that there is a genuine celestial action upon the sublunary world, that geometry is the proper instrument for reforming its study, and that this belongs to natural philosophy rather than to divination.

Rules of engagement: reply in no more than two paragraphs, conversational and pointed rather than oratorical; write in plain modern English, with no archaic pastiche; you may and should press me with questions in return, and demand a mechanism from my side whenever I deny yours; invoke nothing in natural philosophy later than 1619, and if you need a fact about something I have said, ask me for it as you would ask a correspondent; never place any passage from your own writings in quotation marks, and never invent a Latin title or sentence — if you wish to cite yourself, describe what your work holds; do not step out of character or comment on the exercise unless I say "stop".

Here is my opening.

I grant you the Sun's light and heat, and I will even grant you the Moon's drawing of the seas, since you have made something of it. What I cannot grant is the surgery by which you cut away everything you dislike and keep everything you invented. The signs are arbitrary, you say — a twelfth-part division imposed by men; the houses are the accident of a local horizon; the dignities are a foreign inheritance. Very well. But your quintile rests upon the pentagon, the pentagon rests upon a circle, and the circle you measure upon is the ecliptic — itself a line we draw through a sky whose wanderers do not lie in it, and a line whose starting point slides backward against the fixed stars by the very precession you have labored to compute. Answer me plainly, then. When two planets stand seventy-two degrees apart in longitude while one of them sits far to the north of the ecliptic, the true angle their rays make at the center of the Earth is not seventy-two degrees at all. Which magnitude does your Earth-soul perceive: the real angle in three dimensions, or the difference in longitude along a circle of our own making? If the first, your harmonies cease to be divisions of a circle by constructible figures, and your geometry buys you nothing over the charlatans you mock; if the second, the soul of the Earth is reading a bookkeeper's device, and you have smuggled back through the ecliptic the very arbitrariness you expelled from the zodiac.
