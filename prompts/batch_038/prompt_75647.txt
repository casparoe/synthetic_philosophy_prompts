A bookmaker in a coastal city is taking wagers today on whether a naval engagement will occur tomorrow in a disputed strait. The evidence available to a careful bettor is sharply asymmetric — fleet dispositions, intercepted signals, and the collapse of the last round of talks would ordinarily support something like 0.85. Now suppose we grant the reading of Aristotle's sea-battle passage in *On Interpretation* on which there is presently no fact of the matter: tomorrow is not merely unknown to us but genuinely undetermined.

Give me your credence in this proposition: even if the future is open in that sense, it remains rationally permissible for the bettor to hold a credence well above 0.5 that the engagement occurs tomorrow. I want a number, not a survey of the semantic options — and I want the decomposition behind it, with a rough probability and one or two sentences of justification for each term. Suggested terms: whether openness, if granted, really does force rational belief to spread evenly across the possibilities it leaves open; whether present evidence can support one branch over another when neither branch is yet true; whether the verdict changes if you treat the claim about tomorrow as neither true nor false rather than as true relative to some branches and false relative to others; and any further term you think belongs. Show how the terms combine into your figure, identify which one that figure is most sensitive to, and say what argument or observation would move it the most.
