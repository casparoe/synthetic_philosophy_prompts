so I just had a three hour argument with a friend about whether germs and climate get to be called historical actors and neither of us budged, and now I can't tell if my own position is even coherent. Here's what I actually hold:

(1) Pathogens, climate variability and volcanic eruptions are real actors in history, not backdrop — agency doesn't require a mind, it can just mean being a locus of causal force that changes how events unfold.

(2) I reject environmental determinism completely. The same shock lands differently depending on institutions and bargaining power — the post-plague wage rises in western Europe are my go-to example — so the explanatory work is always being done on the human side of the interaction.

(3) To show that a non-human force mattered you need contrastive counterfactuals: say what would have happened absent the shock, holding the rest fixed.

(4) But complex systems don't let you hold things fixed. The shock is entangled with the structures it hits, so I'm deeply suspicous of exactly the kind of clean causal claim (3) demands.

(5) I'm a realist about causation — causal talk isn't just narrative varnish we apply afterwards.

(6) Wherever I can, I want history written in terms of people's choices and contingencies, because that's what makes the past intelligible at all.

My friend's parting shot was that (3) and (4) flatly contradict each other so I have to dump one, and that agency without a mind is empty words. I want an audit, not a lecture: which pairs genuinely pull against each other, which only look like they do, and for each real tension what has to give and what the cheapest fix costs. Rank them by severity rather than treating them as all equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Also — is my friend even right about where the problem is?
