so I've never taken a philosophy class but I've been chewing on the question of why god doesn't just make himself obvious for a while now, and I built an argument that I thought was pretty good until I noticed it seems to prove way more than I want. here it is:

1. if god exists and actually loves me, he wouldn't leave me in the dark about something I need in order to be in a relationship with him, when the dark isn't my own fault.
2. making a truth obvious to someone costs god basically nothing — he could make any proposition feel to me the way 2+2=4 feels.
3. so for any truth that matters a lot to me and is cheap for god to make obvious, he'd make it obvious.
4. there are tons of those. whether my wife is really faithful. whether my daughter's treatment is going to work. whether I'm the sort of person who'd actually fold under pressure. whether my clearest memory of the thing my brother says I did is right.
5. but he doesn't make those obvious. I sit in the dark about them my whole life.
6. so either there's no god or he doesn't love me.

obviously 6 can't really follow from that, because then the fact that I can't tell whether my coworker is embezelling would disprove god on its own, and that's silly. my gut says I want to hold onto 1 and 5, those feel like the actual content. so: which step is the bad one, why did it look fine to me when I wrote it down, and what do I give up by dropping it? and is there a narrower version that survives — some restricted class of truths where it still bites — and how would I know whether I've narrowed it enough to be defensible or just enough to be boring?
