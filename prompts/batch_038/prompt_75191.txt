I am giving a forty-minute paper at a workshop on demarcation and its discontents, followed by twenty minutes of questions. The thesis is that Kepler's attempt to reform astrology was not an embarrassing sideline that can be quarantined from his astronomy, but a continuous piece of natural philosophy, and that its methodological shape is close enough to what a falsificationist asks for to make the standard use of astrology as the paradigm pseudoscience unstable.

Here is the argument I will present, which you should take as the target the objections will aim at. Four moves. First, Kepler relocated the causal locus of celestial influence away from the twelve signs, the houses, and the significations of the fixed stars, and onto angular configurations between planets, and he tried to ground which configurations count in the harmonic divisions of the circle rather than in inherited tradition — the treatise on the more certain foundations of astrology, and later the astrological books of the Harmonice mundi. This is a restrictive move: it takes predictions away from practitioners rather than adding them. Second, he treated weather records, epidemics, nativities — including his own and his family's, cast and annotated — as the body of evidence against which the efficacy of the aspects is to be judged, and he accepted reductions in the stock of admissible aspects when the evidence went the wrong way, while insisting that what remains is a matter of experience rather than demonstration. Third, he posited a natural mechanism — a formative faculty in the Earth and in living things that responds to geometrical configuration — precisely in order to keep demonic agency and the coercion of the will out of the picture; the same move that appears without astrological content in the New Year's pamphlet on the six-cornered snowflake. Fourth, and this is the upshot: the drive to find physical causes of configurations is the same drive that produced the physical astronomy of the Astronomia nova, so Kepler's astrology and his astronomy are methodologically continuous, and what separates them is the truth of the posited mechanism and the quality of the data, not method. Hence a successful application of method to a false hypothesis, which is exactly the case a demarcation project that identifies scientificity with method cannot accommodate.

The room: a senior philosopher of science who opens graduate methods with astrology as the textbook pseudoscience and will not enjoy having his example taken from him; a historian of method in the anti-method line who will say my thesis proves too much and reduces to a footnote in a book he already agrees with; a Kepler specialist who works in the critical edition and is allergic to philosophers doing history from translations and secondhand Latin; a historian of early-modern medicine who will point out that celestial influence was mainstream medical theory and that narrowing it to aspects was a loss of explanatory resources, not a gain; a sociologist of science who will treat the whole reform as boundary-work and jurisdictional positioning against almanac-makers and rivals such as Röslin; and a graduate student in formal epistemology who will want likelihoods. Expect the Popperian and the Kepler specialist to dominate the floor.

Prepare my Q&A sheet. Phrase each the way that person would actually phrase it, in their own idiom and with their characteristic vocabulary, not in mine. Also: which two or three concessions I should bundle into my opening five minutes so they cannot be sprung later; the single objection that would actually sink the paper and the minimal reply that saves it; the question I am hoping nobody asks, with why it is the dangerous one and what my honest answer would be; and three traps where a plausible-sounding concession would quietly destroy my own upshot. Finally, tell me where the argument sketch above is overreaching on its own terms, given only what it says.
