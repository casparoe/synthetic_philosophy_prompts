I came to Hofstadter's careenium-and-simmballs thought experiment in *Gödel, Escher, Bach* as a teenager and never quite shook it: the picture of an enormous sealed vessel in which countless balls ricochet along channels, where clusters of collisions are supposed to stand for symbols and where the causal slippage from one configuration to the next is supposed to be isomorphic to the inferential structure of a train of thought. Since then I have spent years in the implementation debates, from the thread that begins with Putnam's suggestion that an ordinary open system trivially implements every finite-state automaton and never quite ends. I would like a story that takes the underlying question seriously instead of resolving it by fiat.

Please write a short story of roughly 1,800 to 2,500 words, in American English, set in and around an operational careenium. Build from the following situation, which you should treat as your own invention rather than as anything drawn from the book:

The careenium was built by a founder who died before completing the mapping document that specifies which physical configurations correspond to which symbols. Two readings of the machine's dynamics are now in circulation. Under Reading A, the last forty years of slippages form a long and valid derivation. Under Reading B, they form nonsense, a sequence that satisfies no inferential discipline whatever. Both readings are exact isomorphisms onto the same physical history; they disagree only about what the collisions mean. The complication is that the vessel's grooves exhibit hysteresis, in that each collision very slightly deepens the channel it traveled, so the machine accumulates physical preferences that no mapping document ever authorized. A maintenance engineer discovers that Reading B is the one the hysteresis has been selecting for, that the substrate left to itself drifts toward the nonsense interpretation because that interpretation wears less, and that keeping Reading A alive has required a crew working against the grain of the machine every single day for decades.

The story should turn on what the engineer concludes from this: whether symbols genuinely push the substrate around, or whether the substrate has been pushing back all along and the symbol level was only ever a story told about it from the outside. Give both sides a fair hearing inside the fiction, including at least one character who argues that a particular collision really did send a third ball into a slot it would otherwise never have reached, and at least one who argues that the derivation is no more a cause of anything than the average taxpayer is. Let physical texture carry much of the weight: noise, oil, wear, the labor of the crew, the particular loneliness of maintaining a machine whose meaning is in dispute. Keep explicit philosophical exposition minimal and let the situation do the arguing.

The ending may commit or refuse, but it should not shrug. If it commits, make it pay a cost.

After the story, add an author's note of no more than 250 words. Say where the piece takes liberties with the careenium idea or leans on a contested reading of it, state how confident you are in the philosophical claims the fiction quietly endorses, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
