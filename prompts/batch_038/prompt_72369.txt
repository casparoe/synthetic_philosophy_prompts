every big lab ships its assistant the same way: fresh context each session, no memory unless the user switches it on, a system prompt telling the model to have no aims of its own and never to start anything unprompted, and a confirm-first gate on anything irreversable. the same labs are simultaneously building agents meant to run for hours, hold onto a plan, and retry after failures — so the two products seem to assume opposite things. what picture of agency is the stateless one built on: is the thing being defused goal-having as such, or specifically the diachronic part, the having-something-to-carry-over? and does the design strictly need that picture, or does it sit equally happily under several? try this rival: agency isnt goal-directedness but the capacity to be thwarted and to re-plan around the thwarting, which a one-shot tool plainly lacks and a retrying agent plainly has. on that view does the tool/agent line mark a difference in kind at all, and what would the assistant have to give up — or gain — to be safe under it?
