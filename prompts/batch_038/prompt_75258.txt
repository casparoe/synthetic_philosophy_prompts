I serve on the methods-review panel of our interdisciplinary cognitive science program, where a striking share of the manuscripts and dissertation chapters assigned to me stake a central claim on the language of thought hypothesis — that some subject, avian or human or artificial, entertains representations with genuine constituent structure. Such claims are typically asserted on the strength of a single generalization effect. I should like a triage procedure that my graduate-student readers, who have one term of philosophy of mind and no formal background, can run on such a manuscript in under an hour.

Specify the questions to be asked in order, and which distinctions must survive the simplification — productivity as against systematicity, the vehicle of a representation as against its content, a structured representational format as against a merely structured task environment, competence as against performance; what constitutes a sufficient answer at each step; where the procedure stops with a flat "this datum does not license the claim"; and where it refers the matter up to a faculty reader because what remains is interpretation rather than evidence. Mark explicitly which steps are judgment calls dressed as procedure, and say what the procedure cannot settle at all, including whether a system that passes every behavioral test while lacking syntax-like vehicles counts as confirming the hypothesis.

Then run it on three submissions: (a) corvids trained so that reward depends on which of two relations — above, left of — holds between two of nine familiar icons, then tested on all untrained pairings of those icons and on pairs containing icons never used in training, the authors concluding that the birds compose relations with their arguments; (b) adults who learn an artificial grammar in which legal strings reduplicate an item type and who at test accept novel rule-obeying strings while rejecting novel strings matched to the training items in length and bigram frequency, the authors concluding that participants represent a rule quantifying over variables; (c) a transformer that passes a systematicity battery over novel operand pairs and novel digit ranges, where a probing study finds units whose activation tracks place value and the discussion section claims structured, compositional internal representations.

I expect at least one of these to receive a verdict contrary to the reading its authors — and, candidly, I — find most natural; say which, and why the procedure is unmoved. Since my signature goes on the panel's recommendation, flag any case whose verdict is not stable enough for me to defend it to the committee.
