Six weeks ago I got handed the job of running a small college endowment (~$40m) and I'm supposed to sign off on our investment policy statement, but I can't tell whether it's actually coherent. Roughly what it says: the objective is to maximize the long-run compounded growth of the corpus; positions get sized with Kelly-style logic but at half the modelled amount (the deck's justification being that half Kelly keeps about 75% of the growth for about 25% of the variance); if we're ever down 12% from the high-water mark we're required to cut risk in half; the board judges us on Sharpe ration; and we pay out 4.5% of the corpus every year in scholarships regardless of how the year went. Our consultant also insists that max-Sharpe and max-long-run-growth pick out the same portfolio as long as you're allowed to lever freely — except we're not allowed to lever at all.

What committments does this document actually presuppose? Is the growth-maximizing language a claim about our preferences (log utility), a claim about the dynamics of repeated multiplicative bets (time averages versus averages across parallel outcomes — the consultant swears this makes expected utility theory redundant), or neither? Are the drawdown rule and the half-sizing consistent with the stated objective, or are they a different objective wearing its clothes? And where is the policy underdetermined — which parts would look identical under a rival account of what makes a bet rational? Last thing: a colleague argues that if what we really care about is sustaining a fixed payout forever, growth rate isn't the right target at all, and you should sometimes bet more than Kelly when you're behind target. Is he right, and does it change what the policy is committed to?

Please say how confident you are in your main claims, and single out the one you're least sure about.
