my flatmate does ML research for a living and we got into it last night about the problem of old evidence in Bayesian epistemology -- the puzzle where the data was already on the table before the theory turned up, so P(e) is 1, and conditionalising on it leaves your credence in the hypothesis exactly where it was, even though it seems obvious that general relativity got a real boost from deriving the perihelion drift of Mercury that everyone already knew the number of.

His take is that philosophers have reinvented a distinction every practitioner already has. Old evidence is just test-set leakage, aka double dipping. You cant select on data and then evaluate on the same data, the fix is to hold data out, and a theory that accommodates already-known evidence gets exactly zero credit, full stop. He also said the usual repair attempts -- conditionalising instead on the proposition that the theory entails the evidence, or scoring the theory with an imagined probability function from before you knew e -- are the philosophy version of peeking at the test labels and then insisting you didn't.

So how far does the leakage analogy actually stretch? I want the correspondences laid out properly: what plays the training set, what the held-out test set, what the model-selection step is, what the generalisation error is that we're supposedly kidding ourselves about, and what "holding data out" would even mean for an agent who cant un-know something. Then where it breaks. My hunch is that the statistical worry is about selection effects across many candidate models on a finite sample, while the Bayesian puzzle is about one fixed pair of propositions and whether they stand in a support relation at all, and those are seperate questions -- but I cant tell whether that's a real disanalogy or just me defending the philosophers because I like them.

And does his conclusion survive once the breaks are on the table? Is it actually true that accommodation earns nothing, or has the analogy quietly imported a history-and-process-dependent notion of confirmation that the Bayesian framework has no room for -- in which case it isn't a diagnosis of a bug, it's a rival theory with extra steps? If it's the wrong analogy, what would be a better one for whatever is really going wrong in the old evidence cases?
