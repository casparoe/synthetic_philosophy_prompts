I am the honorary secretary of a historical society in a former cotton town in Lancashire, and I should say at the outset that I have no academic training of any kind: I left school at sixteen, spent my working life in a builders' merchants, and have done my reading about history on my own in the evenings. Next year the society is producing a booklet on our local dispute of 1843, when the weavers stopped the looms, there was a riot in the mill yard, and two men were subsequently transported. The surviving material is badly lopsided: the owners' letter books, a newspaper whose proprietor sat on the bench of magistrates that committed the men, depositions taken from the accused by those same authorities, a single handbill, and a memoir written down forty years afterwards by a weaver's granddaughter from what she had been told. At our last meeting someone raised the obvious worry — that what we can actually establish is largely what the owners and the magistrates said happened — and the chairman, a retired head of history from the comprehensive, tapped the table and replied that the historian's business is to show *wie es eigentlich gewesen*, how it actually was, and that everything beyond that is speculation. Another member objected that she had been reading Carr and Hayden White, and that on their account the facts never reach us except as already refracted through the minds of recorders, and that a narrative is a story with a shape imposed on it, which the chairman answered by saying that this turns history into fiction. The meeting ended rather badly, and I suspect the two sides were not so much disagreeing as using the same words for different things.

What I would like is a careful separation of the distinct claims that could be hiding inside that one formula, running from the near-platitude to something genuinely radical. Several ambiguities have occurred to me in my own fumbling way, and I would like them taken up or dismissed. Is the phrase a rule about what the historian ought to aim at, or a claim about what can actually be achieved? I have seen the German rendered into English in two quite different ways, one meaning something like 'really' or 'actually' and the other meaning 'essentially' or 'in its essence', and I cannot tell whether that difference carries any weight or is merely translators fussing. Does 'how it was' concern the particular events — who did what, on which day, in what order — or does it also concern their significance and their inner connection, and if the second, is that not precisely what my chairman says the sceptics are denying us? I have also come across the remark attributed to Ranke that every epoch stands in an immediate relation to God and is not simply a stage on the road to the present, and I cannot see whether that doctrine supports the way the chairman uses the phrase or quietly undermines it. And is there a further claim about the historian's own voice — that he should disappear from the account — bundled in with the claim about the account matching the world?

Please set this out as a numbered list of readings that I can bring to the next meeting, and for each one tell me whether it is a platitude nobody would deny, a substantive thesis that could be defended, or something that is straightforwardly false, with the reason in each case. Then tell me which reading my chairman actually needs in order for his move to work — that is, which reading turns an objection about one-sided sources into mere speculation — and which of the readings Carr's and White's criticisms genuinely bite on, whether there is a version that survives both of them untouched, and whether the two critics are even attacking the same thing. I would also like to know, if the honest conclusion is that the strong readings fail, what the strongest defensible claim about our 1843 booklet would be, so that I can propose a more precise sentence that keeps what is worth keeping in the chairman's formula without promising what nobody can deliver. I am not after a reading list or references, which I could not chase up in any case: it is the reasoning itself I want.
