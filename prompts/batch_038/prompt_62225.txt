I'm maybe 200 pages into a novel and I've hit a wall that I think is really a vocabulary problem, so I'm going to ask you something a little odd.

Some setup. One of my characters grew up somewhere where an insult to your name is an actual injury — you're expected to answer it, or you lose something real — and she's now a professor in a city that runs on the opposite rule: you have a worth that's yours regardless of what anyone says, so you don't answer slights, you shrug, you get on with it. I've been reading the sociology that lays out those two plus a newer third one, the one where people do feel the slight keenly but can't or won't handle it themselves, so they carry it to a third party. All very useful. But the thing I actually need a word for cuts accross that scheme, and I can't find it in there.

Here's the pattern. Two scenes from the book. Scene one: a big name at a conference makes a joke about my character's accent in front of a full room. She lets it go — correctly, by her culture's lights. Nothing happens. Her standing is exactly where it was, because it was high to begin with; the joke gets absorbed and forgotten. Scene two: an adjunct, same joke, same room. She also lets it go. Her standing drops — not much, but it drops, and the not-answering is read by the room as confirming the joke. Later she mentions it to someone senior and gets the same advice the first woman got, delivered with real warmth: it says nothing about you.

So: a rule that tells people not to defend their own standing. For the person who already has standing, following it costs nothing and is genuinely the right advice — she isn't a hypocrite, she's right about her own case. For the person who doesn't, following it costs something, and the culture has no way of seeing that the two of them are being handed the same instruction at very different prices. The price gap stays invisible because the rule is stated as a claim about everyone's inner worth, which is universal, rather than as a claim about social position, which isnt.

Is there a name for this? And I mean the specific shape of it, not the general fact that norms have unequal costs — I know that's true of nearly any norm and I'm not looking to be told that. What I'm after is the thing where a norm's forbearance clause (don't defend yourself here, it's beneath you) is affordable only to the people the norm was written around, and where the norm's own universalist justification is exactly what keeps the differential from showing up.

I've got some half-remembered words floating around — privilege, respectability, something about recognition, something about social death — and none of them are it, though I suspect a couple are neighbors. So tell me what the nearest established concepts are, and for each one, what precisely makes my thing different. I especially want to know how it's separate from (a) plain hypocrisy about who has to be gracious, (b) any view holding that dignity needs material or social preconditions before it can be exercised, and (c) the flat observation that low-status people simply receive more insults, no further mechanism required. And if it doesn't have a name, coin me one and give me the definition plus the boundary cases: what counts, what looks like it but doesn't, where the edges sit. Ideas, not a book list — I'll find the books myself once I know what I'm hunting. I have to write these two scenes and I need to know which one I'm actually writing.
