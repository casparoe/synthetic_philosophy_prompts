Spent 35 years at a national lab doing measurements, retired two years back, now reading philosophy of mind for fun — mostly the stuff on whether introspection is any good at all. There's a pattern I keep running into and I can't find a word for it.

Someone gives a confident report about their own experience. Later a record turns up — transcript, diary, video — and it contradicts the report. Instead of admitting they were wrong, they re-read the old words: they were "being loose," they "meant it in a different sense," they were just going along with whoever was asking. From the inside it doesn't feel like a correction. It feels like their earlier self always meant what they mean now. Two examples. My own notebooks: I distinctly remember thinking a small anomalous peak was noise, when the entry shows I flagged it as a candidate signal and followed it for months. And people who change their politics: the whole life story gets reorganized around the new belief, and they insist the change was gradual, they saw it coming the whole time.

So: is there an established name for this specifically backward-facing rewriting of your own earlier first-person reports? I know it sits next door to hindsight bias and to choice blindness, but those look to me like they're about judgments, or about a single switch, not about the running record of your own phenomonology. Tell me which neighbor is closest and exactly where mine departs from it — and if nothing fits, coin something and define it, including whether it still counts when there's no record to re-read, and whether it counts when the person knows they're doing it. I'm after the carving, not the label.
