Hi — I'm the instructor for an upper-level seminar on 19th-century British political thought and I'm writing the take-home final. The students have read Mill's Chapters on Socialism (we talked about Helen Taylor's note that these are unfinished drafts thrown down in 1869 and published after his death, so there's no finished Mill position to recite), plus the opening of Book II of the Principles, where he treats the rules of distribution — unlike the rules of production — as a matter of human institution.

I want ONE essay question. Three days, 1,200 words, 25 marks. It has to be unanswerable by the standard moves: "Mill softened toward socialism late in life," or a tour of his complaints about Owen, Fourier and Saint-Simon, or "he was a utilitarian so he weighed consequences."

The crux I want them to hit is Mill's refusal to settle the question a priori. Roughly: the ills of the existing order are known and felt, while the ills of socialism are merely predicted, so the thing has to be worked out gradually by experience rather than decided by a vote or a head count. My students read that as flat conservatism, which collides with his own view that present property arrangements have never lived up to the principles that would justify private property in the first place. So the question should force them to say what would have to be true for that asymmetry not to beg the question against the socialist.

I also want them to attack a gloss I've been pushing in seminar rather than just accept it — call it the containment reading. On that reading Mill's asymmetry isn't about the known versus the unknown at all but about reversibility: small-scale experiments (worker cooperatives, an Owenite village) can be run inside a competitive market order without abolishing it, whereas a competitive order can't be run inside a fully socialist one — so "let experience decide" is not neutral between the two systems after all. A strong answer should say whether this rescues Mill or just relocates the problem, and should at minimum press two things: whether the containment premise is itself an unargued prediction Mill has no better warrant for than the socialist's, and whether a cooperative that has to survive on market terms is a fair test of socialism at all.

Please give me: (1) the question exactly as students would see it, with the word limit and marks printed on it; (2) a model answer, 700 words maximum; (3) a marking scheme totalling 25 marks, as a table with four columns — component, marks, what a top answer does here, and the specific mistake that should cost the marks. Finish with two lines on what seperates an A from a B.

Two constraints: paraphrase Mill, don't quote him — I don't want to hand out a line I haven't checked against the text. And keep the whole response under 1,400 words, question first, no preamble, American English throughout.
