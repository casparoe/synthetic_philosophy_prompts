A constitutional convention is debating a court with the following design: eleven members drawn by lot from everyone with at least fifteen years of legal practice, single non-renewable nine-year terms staggered so roughly one seat turns over each year, and the power to invalidate a statute only on a vote of at least eight. Rank the standard justifications for letting unelected judges overrule a legislature — protecting minorities who cannot defend themselves through the electoral process; the precommitment argument, that a democracy may bind its future self against panic; the claim that adversarial, reason-giving institutions track principle better than majoritarian ones do; and the argument that rights are constitutive of democracy rather than a limit on it — by how much force each retains under this design rather than under a conventional life-tenured appointed court. Score each from 0 (the design destroys the justification) to 10 (the design is indifferent to it, or improves it), and for each say in two or three sentences which feature of the design does the work. Close by naming the one standard objection to judicial review that this design most weakens and the one it most strengthens.
