Ok so pretend you're a philosopher who actually works on desire-based theories of wellbeing and on the AI-alignment idea of coherent extrapolated volition. I'm not an academic, I just think about this stuff alot on my own, so I'll say what I want plainly and you can translate it into proper terms.

What I want is a thought experiment built to order. My intuition: people treat "what you'd want if you knew more and thought faster" and "what you'd want if you were more the person you wish you were" as two names for one single idealization. I think they're two seperate machines and they can output different answers. I want a case that shows it.

So: construct a scenario where the more-informed version of a person (has all the relevant facts, thinks faster, no fog, no bias) would choose A, and the better-character version of that same person (the one they aspire to be — kinder, more honest, whatever they'd count as better) would choose B, and A and B genuinely conflict. Not one being a stronger version of the other, not a difference in emphasis. Contradictory choices.

Constraints, please take these seriously:
- ordinary life. a workplace, a family, a small town, a hospital corridor. no sci-fi, no brain implants, no simulations, no omniscient oracle handing out facts.
- the person has to already be decent. if they're a jerk then "the better you" just means "stop being a jerk" and the case is boring.
- the scenario itself in one paragraph, two at most.
- no cheating by stipulation: the divergence has to actually fall out of running the two idealizations, not be smuggled into the setup as an assumption. That's the whole point of the exercise.

Before you write it, brainstorm three or four candidate setups and tell me which one you're picking and why the others fail — I want to see the constraints actually biting. Then write the case and show your work: run idealization one on it explicitly, run idealization two explicitly, and say in a sentence each why they land where they land. Then name the single feature of the story doing the separating work, and tell me what happens if you delete it — does the case collapse into the two idealizations agreeing?

Last thing, and think carefully about it: someone into CEV will say coherence handles this — where extrapolated wishes don't line up you don't get to pick a winner, you keep both avenues open and refuse to force the issue. Does that reply actually deal with my case, or does it change the subject? Tell me which, even if the answer is bad news for me.
