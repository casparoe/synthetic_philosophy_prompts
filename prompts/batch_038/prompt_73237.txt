ok so I'm taking a political philosophy course that keeps sliding into social science methodology, and this week we did the finding that the more gender-egalitarian a country is, the MORE gendered its study-field and occupational choices tend to be — fewer women in STEM, more in health and education, basically no men in nursing, that sort of thing. Half my class says that settles it in favor of biology, since once you strip away social pressure people sort themselves by disposition. The other half says it settles it the other way, that gendered choices are being produced by culture rather than suppressed by it.

what I cant get my head around is that both of those stories seem to predict the identical pattern. If equality unmasks innate differences, you get more gendered choices in equal countries. But if equality mostly means nobody's financially cornered into whatever field pays best, then people in secure countries are free to follow whatever inclinations their upbringing gave them, and those inclinations are already gendered — so you get more gendered choices again. So what is the finding actually evidence for? Walk me through what would have to differ between the two explanations for any cross-country comparison to speak to the dispute at all, and what's really being disagreed about if both sides accept the same data.

related, and maybe this is the crux: everyone talks about these choices as if picking freely means the pick measures some standing preference. But the equal countries also have welfare states and smaller pay gaps, so the same underlying taste — say, caring about the work itself vs. caring about the salary — gets expressed differently depending how much is riding on it. Is that a reason to call the egalitarian-country data a cleaner read on preferences, a dirtier one, or is it answering a different question entirely? People in our seminar seem to swap between those three without noticing.

and one more thing while you're at it — is "gender equality" even a single variable here, given the top-scoring countries are also the most individualist and the least family-obligated? how would you conceptually seperate equality from all that?
