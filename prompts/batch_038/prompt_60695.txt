hey — novelist here, deep in the second draft of a comic novel set in a present-day world where Ashʿarite occasionalism simply won: everybody from the physicists to the guy at the DMV accepts that created things have no causal power of their own, that God recreates each accident fresh at every instant, and that fire-and-cotton is the textbook example. Working title is Acts of God, which I know is on the nose, its a working title.

I need a prop document. Write me a claims-denial letter from a home insurer to a policyholder whose kitchen burned, hard cap of 550 words. It should read like completely ordinary corporate prose — warm sympathetic opener, clause references, a polite request for further documentation, a phone number nobody answers — but the metaphysics underneath has to be airtight occasionalism, so that the letter turns out to be genuinely unanswerable rather than just mean. There's no proximate cause to point at, the acts-of-God exclusion isn't an edge case but the entire schedule of covered perils, and the adjuster can't be accused of bad faith because on his own theory nobody did anything, ever.

The specific bits I'm building scenes around, so please work them in: subrogation (against whom, exactly?); the policyholder's obligation to demonstrate that the toaster caused the damage; "custom" as the closest thing the company has to a risk model, with the actuaries treating it as rock solid; and the adjuster's visibly thinning patience at a customer who keeps saying the toaster did it.

Two ground rules. First, be fair to the position — let the letter actually deploy the real Ashʿarite replies (God's habit is reliable, we know regularities by observing the custom rather than by grasping any necessary connection, and the denial is about natures not about observed sequence), so that a reader who's read the Tahāfut thinks I did the homework instead of thinking I built a strawman out of a caricature. The satire should land on the insurance industry's unexamined metaphysics of causation, not on the theology. Second, and this one is firm: do not put anything in quotation marks as though it came from al-Ghazālī or any other real author, no paraphrase-as-quote either. I'll source real quotations myself later and I don't want to have to hunt down fakes.

Keep it deadpan the whole way through, no winking, no line where the letter admits the joke. 550 words max.
