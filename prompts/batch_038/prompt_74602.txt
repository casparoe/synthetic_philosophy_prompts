I chair the working group on legislative selection for a state constitutional review commission. We have eleven members and rather more than eleven incompatible proposals on the table, and the last two sessions have collapsed into people announcing that they are for or against sortition, which settles nothing. I need an instrument that forces the disagreement into the open by sorting proposals rather than people.

Please write a decision tree for reform proposals concerning how legislators are to be selected. It should begin from the premise the commission does share — that election to multi-year terms, with incumbents usually returned, has produced a self-perpetinating political class — and end at named institutional designs. Nine to twelve branch nodes; at least twelve leaves.

Constraints on the nodes:

- Build the tree from design parameters, not from the familiar typology of mini-publics. Sorting by size and sponsor (citizens' jury, deliberative poll, citizens' assembly, consensus conference) tells us nothing we need. The load-bearing branches should be such things as: what mandate the sorted body holds (advisory, agenda-setting, suspensive, decisive); who is in the selection pool and whether service is compelled, volunteered, or opt-out; how lot and election are combined — whether the lot picks the deciders, or picks the people who pick the deciders, or whether a vote screens a pool from which a lot then draws; how long members serve and whether they may serve again; who sets the body's agenda; whether members are examined before taking office and made to render an account after leaving it; and what sanction, if any, operates when the body performs badly.
- Include at least one node that sorts by what the proposer would accept as evidence that the reform had failed. Designs that look identical from outside often diverge here, because the failure condition reveals whether the load-bearing justification is fairness, epistemic quality, or the breaking of the political class.
- Include one node on the fate of political careers: does the design make a career in politics impossible, unattractive, or merely irrelevant? Term limits without sortition and sortition without term limits must both be reachable.
- Phrase every node as a question a commissioner could answer without knowing where it leads. No node may ask whether one supports some named arrangement.

Constraints on the leaves:

- Three historical arrangements must land on three distinct leaves, separated by the parameters that actually distinguish them rather than by era or place: allotment from a volunteered pool with examination before entry, an accounting at the end of the term, and military commands excepted and filled by vote, as at Athens; the multi-stage Venetian procedure in which the lot was used to choose the choosers rather than the officeholders; and the Florentine arrangement in which nomination and voting built a pool from which the lot then drew. If any two of these cannot in fact be separated by your nodes, say so and name the parameter that would separate them.
- At least two leaves should correspond to live contemporary proposals — for instance a wholly allotted second chamber coexisting with an elected first, or a single officeholder chosen by lot holding only a blocking power. Attach an author's name only where you are confident the attribution is correct; otherwise leave the position unnamed and say that no canonical label exists. Do not guess.
- For each leaf give: a name, one sentence on the institutional logic that holds the arrangement together, and one sentence on the objection that survives the design — the specific objection its own parameter choices fail to answer, not the generic complaint that ordinary people are unqualified.
- Mark the cells that are logically available but unoccupied. Consider at least: a sorted member serving for life; a single executive chosen by lot with no deliberative body attached; a sorted chamber whose members vote immediately upon selection, with deliberation deliberately excluded; and a competence-screened pool whose draw is then submitted for popular ratification. Diagnose each vacancy in one line — incoherent, self-defeating, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
- Flag every pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and say what the ambiguity costs a drafter.

Present the tree indented so it can be handed to the group as it stands, then follow it with a compact table listing each leaf against the parameter values that define it, so that members can place their own proposals on it during the next session. No bibliography.
