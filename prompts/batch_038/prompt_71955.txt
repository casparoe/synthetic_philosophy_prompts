So there's this thing going on at home I can't stop turning over, and I don't really know who to ask.

Two years back we stopped shouting at our kids and stopped all punishments — we didn't want to be that household — and my wife replaced the whole thing with a payment system. Our son (11) gets £3 for every book he finishes, which we verify with one of those quiz apps, £2 a chore, plus a bonus tied to his school reports. And it worked, definately. He went from reading nothing to forty-odd books a year, grades came up, we've had almost no fights about homework in two years.

Two things are bugging me though. Last month his little brother had a nightmare at 3am and my son asked me what he'd get for sitting with him. I froze, and then I gave him a five. The other thing: I've never read a book for pleasure in my life. My dad paid me for grades too, I did brilliantly at school and ended up a lawyer, so it obviously "worked" on me as well.

My mum's verdict is that we're not raising a reader, we're raising an employee. He does the reading, but I'm not convinced he's becoming the sort of person who reads.

Is there something wrong in principle with what we're doing, or is this just squeamishness about money and kids? And if we're wrong — can we unwind it now without the whole thing collapsing? I genuinely don't know what would replace it. I want the actual argument, not a list of better chore apps.
