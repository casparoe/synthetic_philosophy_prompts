so i retired three years ago after 38 years doing IR spectroscopy — mostly assigning peaks in messy spectra, which in hindsight was a philosophy job with a salary — and lately I've been reading philosophy on the bus and at the kitchen table with a pad. My daughter teaches literature and she told me about a form called a pantoum: quatrains where the 2nd and 4th lines of each stanza come back as the 1st and 3rd lines of the next one, and the last line of the whole poem is the first line of the whole poem. That mechanic is exactly what I want, because of something I've been chewing on.

The something: Dennett's three stances — physical, design, intentional — and his essay on real patterns. As best I can make out, his answer to whether a belief is a real thing or just a handy way of talking is roughly that a pattern is real when using it lets you predict a system more compactly than not using it, fewer bits for the same predictive purchase. Which would make beliefs about as real as temperature is, and real from a point of view — which isn't the same as saying they're only in your head. I could be getting some of that wrong, so feel free to correct me in a line or two afterward, but don't turn the correction into an essay.

Could you write me a pantoum, four quatrains, on that? Subject: the blackbird that turns up at my feeder around seven every morning, rain or shine. Here's what I'm after:

- the three stances get one stanza each, in order: physical, design, intentional, with the fourth stanza doing whatever the form needs it to do
- the physical stanza may use only the vocabulary of masses, forces, trajectories, light, weather — no purposes, no functions, nothing with a mind in it
- the design stanza gets function, efficiency, energy budget, what it's built for — but still no beliefs or wants
- the intentional stanza gets expects, remembers, wants, holds a grudge — and it must not drop down into brains or neurons or chemistry, because that's the whole point, you don't have to descend
- the lines that get carried from stanza to stanza should be the ones that change meaning most as they climb — that's the thing I most want to feel, the same sentence meaning something definately different once your one stance up
- when the opening line comes back as the closing line it should land somewhere else than it started, and it should leave open whether the belief was in the feathers all along or whether it needed me standing at the window with a mug of tea

plain-spoken please, no poetic diction, no exclamation marks. rhyme optional but the repeated lines have to be verbatim repeats or the whole trick falls apart.
