I lead a small team building an orchestration layer in which a dozen or so LLM agents each answer a set of logically interlinked yes/no questions about an operational incident — was control X in place, did the change violate policy Y, therefore should we roll back — after which we aggregate their answers into a single decision. We have run into what I gather is the discursive dilemma: aggregating question by question yields an outcome that contradicts the majority verdict on the conclusion question itself. I have one engineer available for the next two quarters to redesign the aggregation logic, nobody on the team has formal philosophy training, and we care about shipping something defensible rather than publishing anything.

Against that budget and that end, which open problems in judgment aggregation deserve our attention, and in what order? Candidates I have in mind include the choice between premise-based and conclusion-based procedures, how much the logical structure of the question set constrains which aggregation rules are even available to us, strategic manipulation by agents that learn how their answers get combined, and whether to weight votes by measured competence or by diversity of the underlying models — but tell me if the real priorities lie elsewhere, and be explicit about which of these we should simply ignore because they will not change what we ship. I want a defended ordering rather than a survey: state the criteria you are ranking on, what concretely breaks if we get each item wrong, how the trade-offs between those criteria resolve, and what evidence over the next two quarters would move an item up or down the list.
