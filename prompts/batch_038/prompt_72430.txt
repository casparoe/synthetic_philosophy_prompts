I am a junior, and I organize the weekly meetings of my school's philosophy club. This week's topic is whether statements about tomorrow can already be true or false, and I would like to open the meeting with a short scripted exchange rather than a lecture.

Please write a dialogue of roughly 650 words between two officers on the deck of a warship the night before a possible engagement. One holds that there is no fact of the matter yet: neither "there will be a battle here tomorrow" nor its denial is true tonight, even though one of them will become true. The other holds that exactly one of them is already true tonight, and that this truth reports how things will go rather than forcing them to go that way.

A few requirements. Both officers should be fully competent advocates, and every reply should take up the previous remark instead of restating a position. The first should be pressed on what deliberation is even aimed at if nothing about tomorrow is yet true, and on why, once the order is given to sail for a distant port, she still cannot say tonight that there will be no battle here tomorrow. The second should be pressed on why a present truth about a decision that has not yet been made does not render that decision unavoidable. Please leave physics, causal determinism, and divine foreknowledge out of it entirely, so that the dispute stays about truth and time. Neither officer should change sides, though they should end by agreeing on what the fleet ought to do.
