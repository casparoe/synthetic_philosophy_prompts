Our reading group spent two hours on whether Xunzi's account of ritual refutes itself and I ended up writing the argument down, but now I think it proves way too much. Here's the chain as I have it.

(1) Xunzi holds that human nature, xing, is bad, and that whatever goodness shows up in people is the product of deliberate activity, wei — ritual and rightness are made by the sages, not born in us.
(2) The sages made them through wei, i.e. through accumulated thinking and practice, not by unfolding anything already sitting in their nature.
(3) Xunzi also insists the sage's nature is the same as anyone else's, since otherwise he can't hold (as he does) that the person in the street could become a sage.
(4) You can't produce what you have no resources to produce. If the sages could generate ritual and rightness out of deliberate activity, the capacity to generate them was in them before the activity started.
(5) Whatever is in someone prior to any deliberate activity belongs to their nature.
(6) So the capacity to produce ritual and rightness is part of human nature.
(7) But that's exactly what Mencius is claiming with the sprouts — a pre-teaching capacity to respond morally, which is the whole point of his bit about the child about to fall into the well.
(8) So Xunzi's own explanation of where ritual comes from commits him to the Mencian picture of nature. Xunzi collapses into Mencius.

What makes me suspicous is that (4)–(6) don't look specific to Xunzi at all. Run them on any view where virtue is aquired through training rather than developed out of what's already there and you get the same verdict, which would mean nobody who stresses nurture over nature has ever been able to hold that view coherently. That can't be right. But I can't find where the general argument breaks, and I can't find where it breaks here either.

So: which step do you reject? I'd like to keep (4) if I can, because dropping it feels like handing Mencius the whole point — but tell me what holding onto it costs, and what Xunzi has available to block the slide without just conceding the sprouts. And if the collapse fails, is there a weaker version of the worry that still bites against him? I dont need sources or a reading list, just the diagnosis and the price of each option.
