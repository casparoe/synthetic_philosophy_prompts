I am a retired physical chemist. For four decades I studied enzyme catalysis, and since giving up the laboratory I have been working my way through the philosophy of science on my own, more slowly than I had expected. I have now arrived at Popper's treatment of Darwinism, and I must confess that I cannot see what the difficulty is supposed to be.

Popper's complaint, as I read it, is that the central claim of natural selection is all but tautological: if fitness simply means whatever it is that allows organisms to survive and reproduce, then asserting that the fittest survive amounts to asserting that survivors survive. From this, I gather, he concluded for a time that Darwinism was not a testable theory at all but something closer to a research programme — a framework that organizes work without itself being exposed to refutation.

It seems to me, however, that this is a difficulty about a badly defined quantity, precisely the kind of thing my own discipline dealt with repeatedly. Catalytic efficiency was once a vague notion — the ability of an enzyme to make a reaction proceed faster — and it became unobjectionable when it was tied to the specificity constant, a ratio of two independently measurable kinetic parameters. Fitness has received the same treatment: it is now a number, the expected contribution of a genotype to the next generation, and from that definition together with a few modelling assumptions one can derive the change in genotype frequency as a theorem. The theorem is then checked in the ordinary way: estimate the fitness values, predict the frequencies, compare with what is observed. If the prediction fails, the assignment of fitness values was mistaken, and the theory is corrected in its details rather than abandoned.

So my question is whether Popper's objection has simply been superseded by that formalization, or whether it survives it. I suspect the answer turns on a distinction I would like you to sharpen for me: between what I shall call nominal circularity, where a term is loosely defined and the looseness could in principle be removed by defining it properly, and structural circularity, where the quantity is fixed only by reference to the very outcome the theory purports to predict, so that no amount of mathematical tidying helps. Which of these two does Popper's objection belong to? If the modern definition of fitness converts the claim that the fittest increase into a theorem rather than a tautology, does that tell in the theory's favour, or does it merely relocate the difficulty from the slogan to the definition? And is it fair to describe Popper's later revision of his verdict as a concession to what biologists had actually done, or was it a correction made within his own framework — and does that difference alter how much weight we should now give his original diagnosis?
