Three months ago I got appointed to the Fairmont Elections and Charter Commission — seven volunteer members, mid-sized city of about 210,000, in a state that lets citizens put measures directly on the ballot by petition. I mostly took the seat because I rent a duplex in the Riverbend neighborhood and I cared about a bike lane. It has turned into a firehose.

Here's the case in front of us. A group calling itself Riverbend Neighbors First turned in petitions for an initiative they're naming the Neighborhood Right to Vote Act. The clerk certified 4,812 valid signatures against a threshold of 3,900, so unless something is procedurally wrong it goes on the November ballot. Our charter gives the commission three jobs on an initiative: certify signatures, write the ballot title and summary (our standing rule says the language must be non-argumentative and must state the chief purpose and effect of the measure), and decide whether to print a fiscal-impact statement. We do not have the power to keep a measure off the ballot because we think it's bad policy.

The measure does three things:

- If someone wants to build 9 or more units, or the city wants to rezone a lot to allow more than 4 units, and the site is within 400 feet of any parcel, the project can't be approved unless the registered voters living inside that 400-foot radius vote yes at the next city election. Majority of those voting wins. No minimum turnout.
- Any units that do get built inside such a radius have to be offered first, at 20% below market, to households who lived inside the radius for at least 8 of the last 15 years, or to their adult children. Whatever's left goes to open market.
- It repeals the section of our comprehensive plan committing the city to permit 6,200 units by 2032 to meet the state's regional housing allocation. The sponsors own filing concedes this. State law says that without a compliant plan a court can let developers bypass local zoning outright, and the state has fined cities over it.

On the table Thursday: (1) the title and summary. The city attorney's draft reads "Limits Housing Construction and Requires Neighborhood Approval Votes." The sponsors want "Neighborhood Right to Vote and Local Homes First Act." Four commissioners want to split the difference with something vaguer, two want the attorney's draft verbatim, one thinks any honest title is going to sound like an attack ad no matter what we do. (2) Whether to print a fiscal statement, and if so whether it should mention the state-law exposure in bullet three. (3) A separate proposal to put a non-binding counter-question on the same ballot asking whether voters support more housing near transit — I think its a poison pill dressed up as balance, others love it. (4) Whether we certify as-is. The deadline for filing ballot language with the county is nine days after the meeting.

My own difficulty: I've lived in Riverbend three years, I rent, I'm registered. So under bullet one, if my landlord ever tried to put four units on our lot, the people deciding would be the owners on my block, several of whom have said at meetings that they moved here because it was quiet. And under bullet two I'd be locked out of buying anything built next door, since three years isn't eight of fifteen. At our hearing a woman who grew up two streets over and got priced out in 2011 spoke in favor, and a contractor who said he'd never bid in this city again spoke against, and both were telling the truth. I have a stake and I'm trying not to write the title like a person with a stake.

Can you draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting? I need a clear recommendation on all four items with a timeline that fits the nine-day filing deadline, the reasoning laid out so a commissioner who disagrees could actually answer it point by point, and — the part I keep failing at — the strongest honest case that the measure is legitimate and we're just wrapping our own preferences in procedural language. Don't hand me a reading list or footnotes, nobody on this commission reads them. Nine hundred to thirteen hundred words is about right.
