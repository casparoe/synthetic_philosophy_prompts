I maintain incremental build systems for a living — dependency graphs, memoization, cache keys, dirty/clean propagation, stale entries, hermetic rebuilds — and I have no philosophy training to speak of. Please restate the problem of logical omniscience in that vocabulary as faithfully as you can: an agent whose beliefs are closed under logical consequence is a build system whose cache is assumed to come pre-populated with every value derivable from its dependency graph, and knowing all the tautologies is having every constant already materialized. Then work through the standard fixes one at a time — impossible or non-normal worlds, awareness operators, syntactic belief bases that hold only what has actually been computed, and the fragmentation of belief across scopes that never share entries — saying for each what it maps onto on my side, what has to be distorted to fit, and what cannot be expressed in it at all.

Two things I want the residue report to settle. First: in my world a cache miss is a performance problem with a determinate answer waiting behind it, whereas the philosophers seem to think the idealization threatens the account of belief itself. Is that gap substantive, or does it just reflect the fact that build systems come with a built-in notion of correctness that the epistemic case lacks? Second, and more worryingly: does describing belief as a stored artifact with a query interface quietly commit me to a representational theory of mind, so that my translation begs the question against the possible-worlds tradition instead of staying neutral between it and the alternatives? Tell me where the mapping holds up and where it is doing more work than it can bear.
