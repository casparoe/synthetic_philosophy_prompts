I want a deployment scenario that generates a responsibility gap for an autonomous system without using any of the machinery that usually produces one — the point is to test whether the gap can come from the structure of a deployment rather than from epistemic opacity, which is where the literature tends to locate it. Setting: a civilian installation such as a hospital, a municipal utility, or a freight operator, described in one paragraph, with realistic present-day-or-near-future technology. The system must outperform the humans it replaced on average, must have acted within its published specification, and must not be defective; the harm must be a death that was foreseeable in the aggregate but unforeseeable in the particular, so that no one positioned to prevent it could have known this was the occasion. Constraints: no malicious or careless actors, no negligence in procurement, testing, or oversight, and a fully informed reasonable person would have made the same deployment decision. Stipulate that the system's decision procedure is completely interpretable and that everyone with authority understood precisely how it worked.

After the scenario, name each candidate bearer of responsibility — the engineers, the certifying body, the deploying institution, the individual operator on duty, and the system itself — and show for each why blame or liability fails to attach rather than merely being diluted across many hands. Then identify the single feature of the case doing the work, and say what happens to the verdict if it is removed: for instance, if the aggregate risk had been understated to the public, or if one identifiable person rather than a committee had authorized the deployment. Close with the strongest objection available to a theorist who denies that such gaps are real — one who holds that responsibility is distributed across the many hands, or that role responsibility survives the absence of fault — and say honestly whether your case earns its verdict from the structure of the situation or simply assumes it.
