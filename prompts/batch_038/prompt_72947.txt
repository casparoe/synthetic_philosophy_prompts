For about six years I've run a small theatre project here. We interview people who've been through homelessness — shelter residents, folks sleeping rough, caseworkers — and then actors perform the transcripts pretty much word for word, nothing invented, no added drama. After each show we pass a bucket, the donations are decent, people are often crying, a few come up afterwards and say it changed how they see things.

Here's what's been bugging me. In six years the volunteer roster at the shelter we work with hasn't grown by a single person. Not one. Meanwhile a big funder has offered to take the show on tour — three cities, professional cast, real marketing budget — and their pitch is basically that we'll move ten times as many people.

My co-director wants the money. She says someone crying in a seat is one step closer to doing something, and that we shouldn't demand a receipt for every feeling. But I keep having this nagging thought that the show might be the thing that *replaces* the doing — that people leave feeling like they've already given, like they've discharged something, and the bucket is how they close the account. And the tour version worries me worse: our whole method is that these are real words from real neighbours, and now it'd be hired performers doing an impression of them for strangers two states over, which starts to feel like we're packaging somebody else's misery as a product and getting paid to traffic in it.

There's a third option I keep circling. Skip the stage entirely — use the tour money for a long-table dinner in the parking lot behind the shelter. No script, no performers, no audience. Everyone cooks, everyone eats, housed and unhoused at the same tables, and the people who come are the whole show.

So — am I being unfair to the plays? Is my instinct that the tears are the problem rather than the proof of success actually defensible, or am I just being precious about purity? And does the dinner dodge the objection or does it have the same sickness in nicer clothes? Also, how would I even find out which of us is right — is there some evidence that could settle it, or is this the kind of argument where both sides just point at the same empty volunteer sheet?
