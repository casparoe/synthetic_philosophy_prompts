Ok so I've been going round and round with a friend about Dennett's four kinds of minds — the Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian ladder from *Kinds of Minds* — and I've finally admitted that we're not argueing about the same thing anymore, so what I want from you is the document we should have produced a year ago: the joint statement of a proper adversarial collaboration between our two camps.

Here's the setup, as fairly as I can manage it:

**Camp A (me, more or less):** the ladder is a real explanatory hierarchy, not a ranking of impressiveness. Each rung adds a distinct kind of selection acting on a distinct kind of item — genes, emitted behavior, internally represented options, and culturally inherited mind-tools — and it earns its keep because it predicts which capacities come bundled and which can come apart across species.

**Camp B (him):** there is one continuous variable here, roughly how wide and how re-tunable an agent's own control policy is, and the four boxes were drawn after the fact around whatever creatures we already find flattering. On his account Popperian-ness is just Skinnerian selection with the reinforcing events moved inside the skull (covert conditioning over imagined outcomes is still conditioning), and Gregorian-ness is just Popperian-ness with better external memory, so at most two rungs are doing any work.

Two stipulated cases I want handled explicitly, because this is where our argument keeps collapsing into shouting:

1. **The Split Pair.** Take creature X, which has a rich internal world-model but no transmitted culture or external artifacts at all, and creature Y, which has almost no internal model but survives indefinitely because every niche it occupies was pre-engineered by scaffolding it inherits. Suppose we build them so that their behavioral flexibility scores come out *identical* on every test we can think of. I say X and Y sit on different rungs and the taxonomy just told us something non-obvious; he says identical scores is exactly what you'd expect if there's only one variable and I'm reading metaphysics off a measurement.

2. **A current large language model.** Which rung, if any — and does the answer change depending on whether rungs are defined by mechanism or by manifested capacity? He thinks this case is a gift to him: one uniform architecture, no rungs visible from the inside, so the ladder must be tracking something about our descriptions rather than about the systems. I think it's a gift to me: you can watch representation-building and tool-use come apart in the failure modes.

What I want in the deliverable:

- A numbered section of claims both camps could actually sign, including the unglamorous ones about what the taxonomy is *for* and what would count as it having failed.
- Then a table of the disagreements that survive that section: one column for the claim as Camp A would word it, one as Camp B would word it, one for what would have to be true for A to win and for B to win. Each side has to be able to look at its own column and say yes, that's me.
- Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling the issue would actually move at least one camp — and for each crux, a concrete test, creature, experiment, or thought case that both camps agree in advance bears on it, with each camp's prediction written down before the result. Where the two camps genuinely predict different outcomes on the same case, flag it; where they'd predict the same outcome and just narrate it differently, flag that too and say so bluntly.
- Finally, a verdict on how much of this is verbal. My suspicion is that the fight over whether internal selection counts as a "new mechanism" is at least partly a fight over what counts as a mechanism, but I want you to say which part of the dispute survives that diagnosis and what each camp would have to give up to make the rest substantive. If you end up thinking one of us is just wrong about something checkable, say that too — don't split the difference to be polite.
