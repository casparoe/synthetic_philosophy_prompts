I have no academic training in philosophy — I read on my own, in the evenings, and for several months now I have been working through the Leibniz–Clarke correspondence in a free online edition, alongside Max Black's 1952 paper on the identity of indiscernibles. I have gotten thoroughly stuck on one stretch of the exchange, and I would be grateful for help understanding what the argument there actually amounts to.

Because I cannot transcribe the passage with any confidence that I would get the wording right, here is my own reconstruction of the reasoning, in the order in which I take it to unfold:

1. There is no such thing as two individuals that differ from one another merely numerically — that is, no two things that are exactly alike in every respect yet are nevertheless two.
2. For there must always be some reason why things are as they are rather than otherwise; and if two things were alike in every intrinsic respect, there could be no reason for God to treat them differently, or to set them down in different places.
3. Any genuine difference between two things must therefore be founded in the things themselves, in their qualities, and not in what Leibniz calls something extrinsic — a position, say, or a mere way of pointing at one of them from outside.
4. Which is why space cannot be a container existing independently of the things arranged in it: if it were, God would have had to choose where to put the whole material world, and there would have been no reason to prefer one arrangement to another.

I would like to know, first, whether this reconstruction is faithful, and in particular whether I have collapsed two distinct claims into one. As I read it, Leibniz sometimes seems to be saying that indiscernible things are impossible, and at other times only that God would have had no reason to make them. Are those the same claim? If they are not, which one does the argument establish, and does it matter for the rest of the exchange?

Second, I cannot tell what work step 3 is doing. Is it a premise, or simply step 1 restated in different vocabulary? And what exactly is excluded by insisting that a difference be founded in the thing itself? This is where Black's example presses on me. He asks us to imagine a universe containing nothing but two exactly similar spheres, a mile apart. But each of them stands a mile from the other, and a lone sphere could not stand in that relation. So there seems to be a relation each sphere bears that is true of it and not derivable from its qualities alone. Is that relation precisely the sort of extrinsic difference Leibniz has ruled out in advance, or is it a legitimate hole in his argument? I suspect the reciprocity of the relation is supposed to block the move, but I cannot say why.

Third, does Black's world actually contradict Leibniz, or does it merely show that if one abandons the principle of sufficient reason one can describe such a world without contradiction? Put differently: is the disagreement about what is possible, or about whether a possible world needs a reason to be the one that exists? And does Black's case depend on there being a God doing the choosing at all — would it survive if the universe were eternal and unmade?

Fourth, I want to test my own modification, and I would like an honest verdict on whether it is interesting or confused. Black's universe is frozen. Suppose instead that the two spheres orbit their common center, or that something happens in that universe — a flash, an impact — that reaches one sphere before the other. Now there is a temporal order, and it seems to me that time may be a resource for telling things apart that the original description quietly suppresses. If so, does Black's world look possible only because it has been deliberately immobilized? And what should I make of the related intuition that if I mentally exchange the two spheres nothing whatever has changed — is that a discovery that there is no fact of the matter about which is which, or a failure of imagination on my part?

Please explain all of this in plain terms rather than in the technical vocabulary I have been unable to acquire, and tell me where my reconstruction is wrong as bluntly as where it is right. What I most want is a clear answer to this: are the passage and the thought experiment genuinely in conflict, or are they using the word difference in two incompatible senses and so talking past one another?
