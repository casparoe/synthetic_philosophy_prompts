You are a philosopher of science with a working knowledge of mechanistic interpretability. I co-organise an interdisciplinary reading group, half of whose members do interpretability research and half of whom are philosophers, and one argument has now gone round three times without anybody moving.

The setting we keep arguing over: suppose we train a detector on the internals of a reference model to flag occasions on which a particular mechanism is running — say, the mechanism by which the model forms and executes a multi-step plan that it does not disclose to the user. We then want to point that detector at a fine-tuned or differently trained successor and ask whether the successor is running the same mechanism. Three criteria of sameness get proposed and clash. (i) Input–output: same mechanism if the subcomputation yields the same behavioural signature across the relevant distribution of cases. (ii) Structural: same mechanism if the circuit — the features involved and the weights implementing the transformations between them — is similar above some threshold. (iii) Interventional: same mechanism if it responds alike to the same interventions, so that patching in candidate activations reproduces the same downstream effects and ablating them removes the same behaviour.

Each camp has a case the others cannot absorb. Structure can persist while behaviour changes, because an intact circuit sits in a new context and the detector's readout no longer tracks the target. Behaviour can persist while the structure is wholly replaced by retraining. And interventional profiles are unstable, since the effect of any intervention depends on the rest of the network, so 'same response to same intervention' is either trivially satisfied or never satisfied. Nobody in the group disputes the empirical facts about any particular model.

My hypothesis, which I want tested rather than flattered, is that two questions are wearing one label: an engineering question about whether a detector trained on one model transfers to another, which is answerable and is what the interpretability people actually care about; and a metaphysical question about mechanism identity, which is being conscripted to settle the engineering one while doing no work of its own. If that is wrong, say so, and say what the real disagreement is instead — a dispute about criteria, about which cases count as data, about the grain at which mechanisms are individuated, or about something empirical nobody has yet measured.
