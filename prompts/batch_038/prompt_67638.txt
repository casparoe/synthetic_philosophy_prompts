so I've been reading about names and identity statements on my own for about a year now — no classes, just books at the kitchen table after work — and I finally wrote down what I actually believe so somebody could tell me if its a mess. Here's the list:

1. The meaning of a name is just the thing it names. When I say Aristotle I'm not running any list of descriptions through my head, and if it turned out the guy who taught Alexander was somebody else entirely, my word would still have meant Aristotle, not whoever fits the list best.
2. If you genuinely know what a name means, that's enough to settle what it picks out. There's no extra step left over.
3. "Hesperus is Hesperus" is empty and "Hesperus is Phosphorus" was a real discovery, so those two sentences cannot mean the same thing. I won't give that up.
4. And the people who made that discovery learned something about a planet, not about vocabulary. Nobody woke up richer in facts about words.
5. Swapping a name for another name that picks out the same thing shouldn't be able to turn a true sentence into a false one.
6. A name can be meaningful even when nothing answers to it. "Vulcan" was a serious hypothesis people did calculations with, and "Vulcan does not exist" came out true — I'm not going to call that sentence meaningless.

I keep going in circles and I can't tell whether the circle is in my head or in the list. Rank the places where these actually pull against each other, worst first, and for each one say which item has to go or get watered down and what that costs me elsewhere in the list — I'd rather pay a real price somewhere than end up with a view that explains nothing. Some of these probably only look like they conflict; tell me which ones those are and why I got fooled. And be straight about what's actually settled here versus what people who work on this still argue over — I don't want to walk away thinking there's one accepted answer if there isn't.
