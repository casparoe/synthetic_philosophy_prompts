this has been eating at me for about three weeks and I dont have anyone to talk to about it who won't just say I'm overthinking it, so here goes.

I run the Saturday morning shift at a community food pantry in my town. Nothing heroic. Groceries, diapers, some produce that a supermarket donates, maybe sixty families a week. I've been doing it five years.

My cousin sent me a video of some philosophy professor talking about ethics in an infinite universe, mostly as a joke because I never shut up about the pantry, and I made the mistake of watching the whole thing instead of just the first two minutes. His argument went roughly: if space is infinite and more or less the same everywhere, then somewhere out there there are infinitely many pantries, infinitely many copies of me handing out the same boxes, and infinitely many copies of me who stayed in bed. So the total amount of good that happens is already infinite no matter what I do. And you can't make an infinite total bigger by adding to it — infinity plus sixty families is still just infinity.

Two things happened after that. The obvious one is that the pantry stopped feeling like it means anything. The other one is weirder and I can't shake it. My friend Nikhil, who did a math degree, said no, that's wrong, there are different sizes of infinity, so what I should actually care about is getting myself into the bigger one. Which somehow felt worse, like I'm supposed to be comparison shopping between infinities. Then I tried to fix it on my own by thinking in proportions instead of totals — what fraction of everyone who needs food actually gets some — and that's zero, obviously, so that route was a dead end too. I even put the numbers in a spreadsheet for a while, which you can imagine went great.

So: which of them is right, the "you can't add to infinity" guy or the "bigger infinity" guy? They cant both be. And more practically — should I quit the Saturday shift? Not because I'm sick of it, I like the people, I like them a lot. But if what I do there genuinely makes zero difference, then I've been telling myself a story about why I go, every week for five years, and I'd rather know it's a story than keep believing it by accident.

Maria does the schedule and she emailed yesterday asking whether I'll take the same slot again next year. I've been sitting on the email.
