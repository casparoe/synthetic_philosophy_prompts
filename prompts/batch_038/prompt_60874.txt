Deadpan startup voice, around 600 words, laid out like a real page: hero blurb, a short "how it works" section, one glowing customer testimonial, and a "known limitations" section where every limitation is reframed as a feature.

The jokes have to come from the inference problem itself, not from generic AI-is-creepy material. So work in things like: there being a huge number of rewards perfectly consistent with the same observed behavior, not being able to separate what a person wants from what they believe about the world, the fact that people procrastinate and act against thier own preferences so treating them as roughly optimal is a weird assumption to build a company on, and what it means to infer from behavior that happened inside an environment someone else designed to shape that behavior.

The bit I care most about: by the end the page should have quietly raised whether the preferences you reveal deserve more deference than the ones you actually say out loud — without ever coming out and stating that question. No named researchers, no citations, no real products, and it should still read as a funny page to someone who's never seen the IRL stuff.
