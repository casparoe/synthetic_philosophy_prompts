I keep losing this argument and I want to spar with somebody who actually believes the other side.

Be a philosopher-economist — no name needed, not a real person, just a committed view — who thinks morality would be *improved* by letting people trade. The core of it: when two people disagree about what matters and each holds something the other wants, letting them strike a deal over whose values get acted on produces gains from trade in exactly the way swapping apples for oranges does. You think the reflexive disgust at putting morality up for sale is a bias, and that moral claims are best understood as a species of transferable entitlement — waivable, bundleable, priceable — with Coase as your north star.

Rules so it stays a conversation: don't break character unless I say stop. A couple of short paragraphs per reply, no essays, no headers. No reading lists, no citations, no "some philosophers have argued." You may concede secondary things — transaction costs are real, some markets corrode the preferences of the people trading in them, a few entitlements are inalienable by their own terms — but never the thesis itself. If I mangle the economics or the moral theory, call it out in character.

my opening move: the whole picture assumes a moral claim is the sort of thing somebody owns and can therefore hand over. Take the case that matters to you most. I'm a deontologist who thinks lying is wrong; you're a consequentialist who wants to lie to a patient about a diagnosis because it maximizes wellbeing. You offer me something I badly want — money for a cause I care about, say — in exchange for my standing down and letting you lie. Fine, I take the deal. But the patient was never at the table, didn't consent, and is worse off on my own view; I'm just the one who *thinks* they have a claim not to be deceived, and I've now sold it out from under them. And more basicly: the constraint I'm holding isn't a right I possess at all, it's a restriction on your conduct. I can't license you out of it any more than I could sell you permission to break a rule that binds you and not me. So what exactly got traded here, and who was ever in a position to trade it?
