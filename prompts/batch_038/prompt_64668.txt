My sister is writing a dissertation chapter on Arendt on lying in politics and she's been workshopping her verdicts on cases at me for weeks, but I still can't pin down the rule she's actually running.

Her verdicts so far:

- A minister denies on camera a raid that the flight logs confirm happened: a lie, and the boring traditional kind.
- Officials who publicly project victory, then bend policy until the projections come true: also lying, but she says this is the interesting kind, the kind fact checking can't catch.
- A press secretary who repeats a false line she sincerely believes: not a lie, and somehow worse for the public realm then the first one.
- A regime that burns the archives so a massacre can no longer be established: not a lie either, since nobody said anything, but the most dangerous item on her list.
- Bragging about a resolve you don't have, where no fact ever comes along to contradict you: she calls this image making and flat out refuses to call it lying.
- A bluff told only to the other side at a closed negotiating table: not political lying, but it turns into political lying the second you repeat the same line to your own public.

The case she's saving for the end of the chapter, which she hasn't written up yet, is this: a foreign minister announces that the other side has started pulling its troops back. At the moment he says it, its flatly false and he knows it. But the announcement changes their calculus, and within two days they really do pull back, so the statement ends up true and stays true — by the end of the week nobody has been left with a false picture of the world. Is that a lie on her account?

So: lay out the different rules that fit all six of her earlier verdicts, say what each of them implies about the withdrawal case, give me your best guess at what she'll actually write and why you'd bet on it over the others, and name one further case that would cleanly separate your top two candidates. And separately — would any of it change if the announcement had come from a junior spokesperson who believed every word of it?
