**Domain: Van Fraassen's Reflection principle. Task: design a case to specification.**

Reflection, as I understand it, requires that my present credence in a proposition equal my present expectation of the credence I will assign to it later, on pain of regarding my future self as someone I am not entitled to disagree with in advance. Construct a case in which Reflection and ordinary Bayesian conditionalization on one's evidence yield contradictory requirements, and in which neither principle can be dismissed as simply inapplicable.

The scenario must satisfy the following:

- It is realistic and contemporary — no amnesia drugs, no fission, no cognitively enhanced future selves, no changes in the agent's values.
- The agent's later change of mind is not a response to any new evidence; her later evidence is a strict subset of her present evidence.
- She is, at the time, convinced that her later self will be the better judge of the very question at issue.
- The conflict must issue in a choice she has to make now, under a deadline, so that the two principles recommend different actions and not merely different numbers.
- The scenario is one paragraph and fixes all the relevant credences and the decision threshold.

The purpose is not to illustrate that Reflection can be violated — I want a case that makes adhering to both principles impossible for an agent who is fully informed about her own future. Stipulating the two verdicts rather than deriving them from the principles would defeat that purpose.

After the scenario, supply the demonstration:

1. What Reflection requires of her now, and what conditionalization on her evidence requires, with the reasoning explicit in each case.
2. Whether any sequence of bets she would accept at both times guarantees her a loss; if not, identify the step in the usual construction that fails here.
3. Which single feature of the case does the work, and what verdicts remain if that feature is removed — in particular, whether the case collapses back into an instance in which Reflection is derivable from conditionalization.
4. How a defender of Reflection would resist your reading — by denying that her later credence is well defined, by re-describing what she defers to, or otherwise — and whether that response costs the principle anything.
