I write a weekly column on science-and-ideas stuff, mostly where AI and biology overlap, and I've just been commissioned a long feature on machine-assisted proof and what counts as "understanding a result." While outlining it I had the slightly horrible realization that I've never actually written my own view down — I've just absorbed it, interview by interview, from whoever I happened to be talking to that month. So I sat down with a coffee and made a numbered list of what I actually think, and now, before any of it goes in print, I want someone to go through it and tell me whether the whole thing hangs together. Please don't explain the background back to me, I've read all the popular versions and I know the vocabulary; just do the audit.

Here's the list. (1) I buy the inversion picture in both of its forms, Darwin's and Turing's — comprehension isn't the starting point, it's the end product. Reason-free, blind competence (selection on one side, mechanical rule-following on the other) is what comprehension gets built out of, so I reject any argument of the shape "but a mere process/statistics/mechanism can't produce genuine understanding." To me that's just the pre-Darwinian reflex wearing new clothes. (2) Same on the mathematics side: I accept a computer-assisted proof as a proof even when no human being has surveyed every case — the four-color result from '76 is my standing example. What matters is that the process is reliable and the result is right, not that anybody followed along. (3) I'm broadly a functionalist about minds: mental states are constituted by their roles and relations, and I'm perfectly happy to take the intentional stance toward things that aren't persons — a company, a research program, a model — as a predictive convenience. (4) Here's my own bit, the thing I want the column to hang on: I don't think understanding is a property a system possesses all by itself. I think it's a standing a community grants, much more like being admitted as a credible witness than like having a particular internal architecture. You understand X when your say-so about X works as a reason for the rest of us — when we let you answer for it, when we can hold you to it. Not a spark, not a mechanism: a social status, conferred and revocable, and domain-specific. (5) Which is why I say current language models don't understand anything, and I say it without embarrassment about (1)–(3): the deficit isn't hidden inside them, it's that we haven't granted them standing and shouldn't yet. I'm pretty hostile to the way the labs market this and I want the piece to say so plainly. (6) I also think the Turing test is a bad test — passing it wouldn't establish anything I care about. (7) Moral status is a seperate axis for me entirely: it tracks sentience, not comprehension, so I'm unmoved by LLM-welfare panics and very moved by factory farming.

So: which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which pairs only look bad sitting next to one another? I can feel that a few of them look awkward side by side but I can't tell a real contradiction from a clumsy phrasing, and I'd rather find out now than in a letter to the editor. Rank the real tensions worst-first, and for each one tell me specifically which item has to be dropped or weakened and what I lose by weakening it — "just hold them in productive tension" is not an answer I can use. Then name the single cheapest revision that cleans up the most damage. If a pair looks contradictory but isn't, say that explicitly and show me why, because I'd hate to have to abandon (4) merely for sounding odd next to (3), though tell me straight if that's what the audit says. Two extra things. Flag anything I've stated in a way a specialist would recognize as a garbled or self-defeating version of a real position — I suspect at least one of these is sloppier than I think it is. And a friend of mine who covers chips insists that (2) and (5) can't both be true: that if I'm content to bank a proof nobody understands, I have no standing to complain about a model nobody understands. Tell me whether he's actually onto something or just being cute, because I keep losing that argument at the bar and I don't know if that's because I'm wrong or because I'm inarticulate.
