I have spent twelve years building deterministic-replay and distributed-debugging tooling, and I am fluent in the standard vocabulary: Lamport's happened-before relation, vector clocks, consistent cuts, the Chandy–Lamport snapshot algorithm and its FIFO-channel assumption, and linear extensions of a partial order as the device you use to turn a causal history into a total order for replay. I have no philosophy training beyond popular accounts. I would like the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe, together with the standard replies to it, restated in that vocabulary as faithfully as you can manage, and then a residue report telling me where the translation breaks.

Please do the following in order.

1. Restate the argument premise by premise. Say explicitly what each ingredient becomes: an inertial observer's plane of simultaneity; the absence of a privileged frame; the relation of being real to an observer, which the argument needs to be symmetric across observers and transitive along a chain of relatively boosted observers; and the conclusion that all events, past and future, are equally real. I want to know in particular what, if anything, corresponds to the chain-of-boosts step, since I suspect the entire argument lives there.

2. Restate Howard Stein's 1968 reply — roughly, that the defensible conclusion is much weaker than the argument's authors claim, and that what an observer's own past light cone licenses falls far short of a global now — in the same terms. My working hypothesis is that Stein's position is simply "the causal past of my current local state is its down-set," which every distributed-systems engineer already assumes as a matter of course. Tell me whether that identification is right, or whether Stein is claiming something my vocabulary cannot express.

3. Give the residue report, sorted into three bins: claims that translate cleanly; claims that translate only with distortion, naming the distortion in each case; and claims with no counterpart at all. I want your assessment of the following four points in particular, plus any others you think matter:
   (a) Whether the target vocabulary already presupposes a completed event log, and so builds the eternalist conclusion into the translation instead of deriving it from the premises.
   (b) Whether concurrency in the happened-before sense is genuinely the analogue of spacelike separation, given that in relativity the order of spacelike-separated events is reversed between frames while in my framework concurrent events are simply unordered.
   (c) Whether anything at all corresponds to a boost — that is, to cut-dependence being a symmetry of the underlying theory rather than an artifact of when a particular recording happened to be initiated.
   (d) Whether the metric, proper time, and the timelike/spacelike distinction survive the translation, and if they do not, how much of the argument depended on them.

4. Here is my own view, which I want attacked rather than endorsed: that the Rietdijk–Putnam argument comes out trivially true once translated, and that a trivially true translation cannot be doing the metaphysical work its authors claim for it. Assess this. Separate the three conclusions usually attributed to the argument — presentism is false; the future is as real as the past; reality is a four-dimensional block — and say for each whether my translation can support it, can only gesture at it, or cannot state it at all.

Do not flatter me and do not force a tidy mapping where there is none; if a piece of the argument has to be dropped or bent to fit my vocabulary, say so plainly. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, and no restating of my question.
