I'm a TA for an upper-level philosophy of physics course and I need to grade a short-answer question worth 10 points. The question was:

"Special relativity is often said to be incompatible with presentism. Reconstruct the argument that is supposed to show this, and state explicitly which of its premises are supplied by the physics and which are not. Then say what a presentist has to give up in order to resist the conclusion."

The student wrote:

"Special relativity abolished absolute simultaneity: which distant events are happening now depends on the observer's state of motion. Let Alice float at rest and Bob fly past her, and consider a supernova in a distant galaxy. That event is simultaneous with Alice's present moment, but for Bob it may lie in his past or in his future, depending on his direction of travel; and equally, there are events in Bob's present moment that are not in Alice's. Both descriptions are equally correct, because the physics picks out no privileged state of motion. If what is real is what is happening now, then Alice and Bob make incompatible claims about what is real, and since neither of them is mistaken, reality itself cannot be relative to a frame. The only consistent option is that all of these events are equally real: the block universe. It follows that the future is already fixed and cannot be changed, which is why the argument is sometimes thought to threaten free will. A presentist who wants to resist the conclusion has to deny special relativity itself, because tests of Lorentz invariance have confirmed it to very high precision and rule out any preferred simultaneity."

Please assign a grade out of 10 and write the comments that justify it, keyed to specific claims in the answer: what it gets right, what it gets wrong, and what the question asked for that it never addresses. Wherever a quick read would award or withhold credit that the text doesn't earn, say so explicitly and explain why. Finish with the single comment most worth writing back to the student.
