im putting together a little series of sonnets that are actually about proofs rather than vaguely science-flavored, and the next one is the strategy-stealing argument — the hex / tic-tac-toe one where you show the first player cant lose without ever saying what they should actually do. Can you write it as a 14-line sonnet with real rhymes? doesnt have to be strict iambics or a petrarchan/shakespearean split, but i want the shape to be doing the argument's work, not just narrating it.

heres the mapping i have in mind:

- the octave is the reductio: assume, for the sake of it, that the second player has a strategy guaranteeing them the win, and picture them calmly running it move after move.
- the volta is the theft: first player drops one arbitrary stone, then starts answering with the stolen strategy, playing as though they were second.
- the sestet has to handle the two bits everyone glosses over. One: what happens the first time the stolen strategy calls for the cell you already filled — you play somewhere else instead and demote your extra stone to the ignored one, so the situation stays as it was. Two: the premise quietly carrying all the weight, that an extra stone of your own color on the board can never turn a win into a loss, and that the game is symmetric enough for the roles to swap at all. i want someone who knows the proof to be able to put a finger on the exact line where that premise gets used.
- the couplet should say what the argument gives and what it witholds: existence with no strategy attached. and if you can fit it without it feeling bolted on, the shadow of a game where the same trick dies — zugzwang, where being the one obliged to move is the penalty, or a board that starts asymmetric so theres nothing to mirror.

no jargon dumps please, and no name-dropping whoever came up with it — the argument should carry itself. also, if a draw is possible in the game you set it in, the honest conclusion is only win-or-tie, so dont oversell it in the last line. someone who plays hex should find it right; someone who's never heard of it should just find a good sonnet about stealing.
