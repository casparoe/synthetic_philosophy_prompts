A committee in one of the sciences is split over whether a category its members routinely use denotes anything real, and each side accuses the other of changing the subject. I want to know what an account of natural kinds would have to deliver before it could adjudicate a dispute of that kind without begging the question against either side. Take that as the fixed purpose of the specification below: adequacy means good enough to serve as a non-question-begging arbiter in live reclassification fights, not merely good enough to organize a literature. Please note where that purpose does substantive work in the rest of your answer, since it already rules out deferring to the ideal final theory and rules out reporting current scientific consensus, and I suspect it constrains more than those two things.

Write the desiderata — what any account of when a category carves nature at a joint, rather than slicing it conveniently, must be able to do — organized as follows.

First, verdicts it must return on hard cases, with the reasoning in each instance. At minimum:
1. A name used for centuries and still used in commerce and craft, whose samples turn out to be two chemically and structurally unrelated substances sharing only appearance and application. Say what, if anything, was discovered when the grouping was split, and whether the surviving umbrella term denotes a kind, a disjunction, or nothing.
2. A grouping whose members share a genuine underlying nature but which supports no generalization anyone would call lawlike — a category gerrymandered relative to every regularity in the domain.
3. A category that licenses reliable inferences in one discipline and is useless in another that describes the very same territory at the same level of detail.
4. A category that exists only because an institution enacted it — for instance a regulatory threshold, where samples shade continuously into non-members on both sides of the line — but which thereafter has measurable causal powers and supports dependable predictions about behavior, disposal, exposure, and cost. Say whether such a kind falls inside or outside the theory's domain, and why that answer is not merely stipulative.
5. Something treated as a kind by a working science but arguably a single historical individual rather than a class.

Second, distinctions the account must not collapse: real versus nominal; fundamental versus special-science; objective versus interest-relative; kind versus particular; and, most importantly, discovering that a category must be split versus deciding to split it.

Third, explanatory burdens: why some categories license counterfactuals and inductions and others do not; why reclassification sometimes feels like finding something out and sometimes like moving a boundary marker; and what the grain of the joints is supposed to be — must there be one joint per kind, and must the joints nest?

Fourth, consistency constraints: what the account cannot contradict given that idealized and knowingly false models figure in successful classification, and given that most entrenched categories have exceptions, borderline members, and extensions that have shifted over time without anyone concluding that the kind had been replaced.

Then three things I want more than the list itself.

Sort the requirements into the non-negotiable and those that are really the standing prejudices of particular camps. In particular, identify any requirement that is a verdict in disguise — one whose acceptance already settles what the theory is allowed to conclude. I suspect that at least one of the standard demands about underlying microstructure works this way; tell me whether I am right and, if so, what the honest version of the demand would be.

Tell me whether any subset of the genuine requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. My own suspicion is that requiring mind-independence, requiring support for reliable inference, and conceding that which inferences are reliable depends on the inquirer's interests and their resolution of the phenomenon cannot all be satisfied at once, and that any account appearing to meet all three does so by hollowing one of them out. If you agree, say which one collapses and how; if you disagree, exhibit the account that carries all three.

Finally, say which single requirement eliminates the most contenders, and give a short scorecard — one or two lines each — of the standard families of account against the surviving list, flagging any family whose chief claim to virtue rests on a requirement you have demoted to prejudice.

I am not after a survey, a history, or a bibliography. I want the specification, argued for, with its own fault lines exposed.
