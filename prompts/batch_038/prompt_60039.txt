I am applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship in philosophy of religion and need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea is this: the contingency version of the cosmological argument, even at its most successful, buys less than its proponents claim. A being that exists in every possible world contributes the same explanatory content to each of them, so it can account for there being a world rather than none, but not for why this world obtains rather than some other; and the standard repair — a free divine choice among worlds — appears to reintroduce the very demand for explanation that the argument set out to discharge. I want to press this as a dilemma rather than a mere objection, and to ask whether a proponent can absorb the residual contingency whilst still securing everything the argument was advertised to deliver.

Please turn this into a proposal containing: a precise statement of the question, framed so that it has a determinate answer; why the replies already available on both sides leave it open; my method, including how the work would be organised across the twelve months and what I would actually be doing in each; what a positive result would consist in, and what would count as the project's failure; which of the two horns you judge more likely to give way, and why; and a candid note on where this framing risks being too clever by half. No bibliography or literature review, please — I shall supply the references myself. Write the proposal in American English.
