I am putting the finishing touches on a chapter for an edited volume on the metaphysics of infinity, and before I send the draft to the volume's editor I would like an honest audit of the position it defends. The chapter is meant to stake out a middle path — realism about the mathematical infinite combined with finitism about the concrete infinite — and a graduate student in my seminar last term told me he thought the middle path was unstable, that I was borrowing from one domain to pay off debts in another. I did not think he was right at the time, but neither could I say precisely why, and I would rather discover the problem now than in print. Here are my commitments, stated as canonically as I can manage:

1. Cantorian realism. The transfinite cardinals are genuine features of an abstract reality rather than useful fictions or mere façons de parler; the diagonal argument establishes that there are strictly more reals than naturals; and the fact that an infinite set can be placed in one-to-one correspondence with a proper subset of itself is a theorem about these structures, not a paradox and not a reductio of anything.
2. Concrete finitism. No actually infinite collection of concrete, causally active things can exist. Hilbert's hotel — infinitely many rooms, all occupied, and yet a further guest accommodated by shifting everyone along — is not merely counterintuitive but impossible; the very idea is internally contradictory. I take the one-to-one correspondence between all the rooms and all the rooms but the first to be the source of the contradiction.
3. Independently of (2), I find compelling the thought that nothing can have an infinite causal history: an infinite regress of dependent causes cannot, as it were, have been handed over all at once, since there is no first link from which the transmission could begin.
4. Eternalism. All times and their contents are equally real; there is no objective becoming and no metaphysically privileged present. I hold this largely because I cannot see how to reconcile a robust becoming with the relativity of simultaneity.
5. Cosmology, as I read it, gives us a finite past of some 13.8 billion years and no final moment: the universe persists indefinitely, with physical processes occurring at every future time. I am aware that a continuum of spacetime points would already supply uncountably many concrete items; I sidestep that by treating spacetime points as abstractions from relations among events, so that only events and their denizens are concrete in the sense that matters to (2).
6. Probability. Rational credence must be countably additive. I reject strictly finitely additive probability as unmotivated, and I likewise reject infinitesimal-valued or non-Archimedean reconstructions as ad hoc repairs. It follows, and I am content to accept, that there can be no countably infinite fair lottery.

What I want is not a survey of the literature on infinity and not a general defense or refutation of any single thesis, but a consistency audit of the package. Tell me which of these pull against one another, ranking the tensions by severity rather than reporting them all as equally worrying; for each, say which commitment must be weakened or abandoned, what the weakest sufficient revision would be, and what that revision would cost me elsewhere — including whether it would undercut support I need for the remaining claims. Please separate the genuine tensions from the merely apparent ones, and address in particular my student's charge of equivocation: whether treating the infinite as at most potential in the concrete world, in the Aristotelian sense, while treating it as fully actual in the mathematical realm is a coherent division of labour or a straightforward slide between two senses of the term, and whether my insistence on countable additivity is any more vulnerable to that charge than my denial that any concrete infinity exists. Note that the same move may be innocent in one place and fatal in another, and say so if it is. Close with the single revision that would clean up the most, and be blunt about what I would be left arguing if I made it.
