I'm working through Davidson's essay on the very idea of a conceptual scheme and have stalled where he moves from total to partial failure of translation. I don't have the text to hand, so what follows is my own reconstruction of the stretch, in order, rather than his wording; the first thing I want from you is an account of where that reconstruction already distorts him.

1. A conceptual scheme can be given content only by being identified with a language, so schemes are genuinely distinct just in case their languages are not intertranslatable; and translation maps the sentences of one language onto true sentences of the other, so it is anchored to truth rather than to similarity of ideas or to shared patterns of stimulation.

2. Intertranslatability could fail totally or partially. Total failure cannot be coherely described, since recognizing some behaviour as speech already is interpreting it, which leaves no standpoint from which to identify a language and then find it untranslatable. Partial failure is supposed to be the relativist's live option — much in common, plus a residue that cannot be mapped — but a residue nobody could translate is a residue for which there could be no evidence, and therefore not a residue of belief at all.

3. The scheme-content dualism accordingly has no content; he calls it the third dogma; and giving it up does not cut us off from the world.

Three things I cannot settle. First, is the premise that translation preserves truth doing argumentative work in step 2, or is it an assumption the relativist may simply decline — for instance by refusing to identify schemes with languages at all, and treating a scheme instead as the role its concepts play within a total theory, which would make intertranslatability the wrong test from the outset? Second, when step 2 rules out partial failure, is the target its impossibility, or only its indistinguishability from ordinary local disagreement inside a shared scheme? Those require very different arguments, and I cannot tell which one he owes. Third, is charity here a premise he is entitled to assume, or something he takes to fall out of what interpretation requires — because I keep suspecting the real conclusion is that massive error is unintelligible rather than that relativism is false, and I don't know whether those two claims come apart.
