I have been going back and forth on Douglas Hofstadter's *Le Ton beau de Marot: In Praise of the Music of Language* (1997) — the book built out of several dozen English renderings of Clément Marot's 1537 trifle "A une damoyselle malade": twenty-eight lines of three syllables each, in consecutive rhyming couplets, closing by re-rhyming the word it opened on ("Ma mignonne, / Je vous donne … Santé bonne, / Ma mignonne"). I would like to stop collecting opinions about individual translations and get the specification first.

So: write the desiderata. What must any adequate account of translation under formal constraint deliver before it earns the right to pronounce on whether a given rendering succeeds?

Fix the purpose as follows. The account must be good enough to do two jobs. First, to adjudicate — not merely to describe — the standing dispute between a translator who holds the trisyllabic couplets and their rhyme scheme inviolable and will paraphrase the sense to keep them, and a translator who holds the sense and register inviolable and will drop the meter entirely. Second, to be actionable: a competent translator facing a single intractable line should be able to consult it and know which loss to take. If you think no account can do both jobs at once, that is a result I want stated, not smoothed over.

Framing I would like you to work inside, and to push back on if it is wrong: treat a candidate translation as a point in a search space of target-language strings, evaluated against two families of constraints — formal ones that look like gates (line count, syllables per line, rhyme scheme, the circular return of the final rhyme) and semantic-pragmatic ones that look like costs (propositional content line by line, register, imagery, sound-symbolic echoes, the relationship posited between speaker and addressee, the effect the poem has on a reader). A theory then owes us an answer to whether these are commensurable at all. If it treats them as weights on a single scale, it is committed to there being some exchange rate between a broken rhyme and a falsified clause; say what could possibly justify such a rate. If it treats them as gates plus costs, it owes us the ordering of the gates.

What I want in the deliverable:

1. A numbered list of requirements, each with the concrete case or consideration that motivates it. Cases, not slogans — for each, name a situation in which a theory lacking that requirement gives an obviously wrong verdict.
2. Each requirement tagged as non-negotiable, negotiable, or a prejudice of one camp masquerading as a criterion. Be candid about which of your own items are load-bearing and which are decorative; if a requirement is one that no candidate theory could ever fail, cut it and say you cut it.
3. The distinctions the theory must not collapse — among translation, crib, imitation, homage, and adaptation; between a sentence's content and the total effect of an utterance; between faithfulness to a text, to an author's intention, to the original audience's experience, and to the pattern the text realizes; between form as ornament and form as carrier of content. For each, say what turns on keeping it apart.
4. The empirical and structural facts the theory must be consistent with rather than contradict. Include at least: that the pool of strings satisfying a tight formal template in a given language is small, and that its size varies with the language's phonology, so a template nearly free in one language is expensive in another; that a form carried over intact may land in a different register entirely, so that formal success can produce the nursery-rhyme effect where the original had courtly lightness; and that some effects depend on properties of the source language itself and admit of no counterpart.
5. The jointly unsatisfiable subset. Identify the largest set of requirements you believe cannot all be met, and give the argument — a counting or pigeonhole argument is welcome if one is available — rather than an expression of regret. Tell me which single requirement does the most exclusionary work.
6. Whether the theory is permitted to return "incomparable" for some pairs of candidates, or whether that is a failure mode. State your own prior here and what would move it.
7. Finally, and briefly: how the three familiar stances — the literal crib, the free-verse rendering that keeps the spirit and abandons the letter, and the rhyme-for-rhyme and meter-for-meter reconstruction — score against your list. Keep this short; I care about the list, not the scorecard, and I suspect the scorecard will mostly reveal which items on the list are doing the work.

No bibliography, no citations, no reading list — I am not going to look anything up, and neither can you. Reason from the description of the case I have given you. If you think the whole enterprise rests on a category error, show me exactly where the error enters rather than telling me translation is a rich and contested field.
