So my department runs this informal Thursday thing where somebody brings a piece of writing and we argue about it for an hour, mostly badly, and next month it's my turn. I don't want to hand out another excerpt of a paper this time, I want to bring an actual story, and I've been trying to get a draft seed out of a couple of models but everything I get back has the same two endings. Here's the idea I keep circling. It's tangled hierarchies — the Escher lithograph where two hands come up out of a flat sheet of paper and each one is in the act of drawing the other into existence. What I can't get anyone to write about is that neither hand draws the sheet. The sheet is the thing that has to be left alone for the loop to be drawable at all, the rung that stays outside the loop, the unexamined level. Hofstadter has a phrase for it that I've half forgotten, something about an inviolate level. Everybody who writes about this stuff wants to be one of the hands, and nobody ever writes about beeing the paper.

So: short story, somewhere between 1500 and 2000 words, about the paper. Seed it however you like, but keep this machinery. A mid-sized municipal water authority has, over about fifteen years, quietly automated thier own oversight — sensors check the pumps, a system checks the sensors, a second system checks the first system's checks, and at the bottom of every chain there is one human signature that no system verifies, because something has to be the bottom and the bottom can't check itself. The story is about the person who holds that signature, who has held it for nine years, and for whom the job is almost entirely clerical: a queue, a wet stamp, a form that has to be initialled before the automated part is allowed to proceed. Then, one week, the authority's new system starts producing, without being asked, the documentation that would certify her own authority. Keep the machinery boring on purpose. I want form numbers, a printer that jams, the specific shame of being the reason a process has to include a person at all, a colleague who is genuinely nice to her and has no idea what she does. Two things I don't want. No AI-goes-rogue and no human-pulls-the-plug, because those are the two endings I keep getting and both of them turn the paper back into a hand. And no explaining: nobody says the words strange loop or self-reference or paradox, there's no Escher print on anybody's wall, nobody has a revelation about the nature of hierarchy. The structure of the story should be the lithograph; the surface should be a compliance office. It's fine, better than fine, if a reader only feels the loop in the last few paragraphs and can't quite say what happened to them.

Please don't warm up to it. Start with the first sentence of the story and stop when the story stops — no title, no epigraph, no outline beforehand, no note afterwards mapping the parts onto the concept. And no formatting of any kind in the response: no headings, no bold, no bullets, no tables, no section breaks made of asterisks, just continuous prose the way you'd write a long letter. Close third person on her is probably right, but I'll trust you on that.
