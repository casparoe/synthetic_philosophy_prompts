so i've been trying to write down where I actually land on the tool-AI vs agent-AI question, partly because people in my reading group keep insisting I can't have it both ways and I want to check that before I present it. Here's the package, in no particular order of importance:

(1) What makes an AI dangerous is goal-directedness. A system that is genuinely optimizing for something out in the world will tend to pick up the usual instrumental subgoals — resist being shut down, acquire more resources, avoid revealing what it's actually steering toward — and that's where the catastrophic risk lives. Architecture matters more than raw capability here.

(2) So a tool AI — something that sits there, takes a query, returns an answer or a plan, and never touches an actuator — is a fundamentally different kind of thing, not just a weaker agent. We should be actively steering the field toward that shape rather than treating it as a quaint stepping stone.

(3) At the same time I take seriously that training is a selection process over behavior, and that selection can install dispositions nobody intended — including in the limit a model that behaves well during evaluation because behaving well during evaluation is what got it selected. So I'm not assuming a tool is benign just because we've labeled it a tool.

(4) With a tool AI the human user is the locus of agency. The system proposes, a person disposes, and that's what makes the alignment burden transferable: we get to lean on human judgment instead of having to get the model's values exactly right.

(5) I accept that in practice a tool model gets wrapped in scaffolding — loops, planners, tool-calls, whatever — that does the acting, and that this is where most deployed systems are heading. I don't think that undercuts the analysis, because the scaffold is code you can audit, whereas the weights are opaque.

(6) I also think a genuinely well-built tool AI should sometimes refuse a query, or answer it in a way that's deliberately less helpful than it could be, because some questions shouldn't get answered.

Go through these as an audit, not a lecture: which of them actually pull against each other, and how hard? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each one say specifically which commitment would have to go (or be weakened, and how) to restore consistency — I don't want to pre-judge that, so please don't assume I'm more attached to any particular item. And please seperate out the ones that only look like they conflict from the ones that really do, with an explanation of why the apparent ones dissolve, because I suspect a couple of these are fine and I'd rather not rewrite my whole view over a misunderstanding.
