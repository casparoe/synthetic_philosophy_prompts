I'm TA-ing an intro consciousness seminar and before we read Dennett's Quining Qualia I want to hand out a 'where do you actually stand' quiz, mainly so people discover they don't all mean the same thing by experience. Ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three options and none of them using the words qualia, phenomenology or what-it's-like — the point is to sort people by what they say, not by what they call it. Start from the two coffee tasters (the one who says the brew tastes just as it always did but he's stopped liking it, and the one who says it doesn't taste the way it used to) and then vary the setup: a taster wired to a machine that reads what he can discriminate without reading what he reports, someone whose memory of the old taste is gone so there's nothing left to compare against, and a pair who flatly disagree about whether anything changed.

Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the positions: experience genuinely changed, only the judgment or the liking changed, higher-order-thought views, first-order representationalism, naive or relational realism, illusionism, and Dennett's own no-fact-of-the-matter verdict. Build the space first — what could have changed, crossed with whether there's a determinate answer — then locate the named views in it, list the combinations nobody occupies and say if that's because they're incoherent, unattractive or just unexplored, and flag which pairs my ten questions can't really separate. Say how confident you are in the mapping overall and single out the placement you're least sure of.
