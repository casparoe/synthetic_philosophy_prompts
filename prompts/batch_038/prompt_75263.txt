I've just come out of a two-hour argument with a friend who insists that Coetzee's *Disgrace* is in effect a verdict on South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission: Lurie before the university committee is the unrepentant perpetrator refusing to buy reintegration with testimony, and Lucy's decision not to report her rape is the victim declining a reconciliation she has no reason to accept. I suspect he is flattening the novel into an allegory, but I cannot yet say where the allegory stops holding. I'm putting the book in front of a political philosophy seminar on transitional justice, and I want a reading that takes both of us seriously.

Please work with the machinery rather than the mood. Amnesty under the Promotion of National Unity and Reconciliation Act was individualized and conditional on full disclosure and a political-objective test, not a blanket indemnity, and the Constitutional Court sustained it in 1996 largely on the ground that the truth would otherwise never surface; the Commission's mandate covered gross violations — killing, abduction, torture, severe ill-treatment — which is why critics say it left land dispossession and the structural violence of apartheid outside the truth it could establish; and the final report separates factual or forensic truth from personal and narrative truth, social or dialogical truth, and healing or restorative truth. Which of these does Lurie's hearing actually dramatize, and which does it refuse? Is Lucy's silence a critique of the state's claim to broker between victim and perpetrator, or something else — her language of debt, tax, and price is not the language of rights at all? And where does the clinic strand, with Lurie's insistence on tending the bodies of the dogs he has helped kill, fit a project whose most concrete achievement for many families was the recovery and return of remains?

Be honest in both directions: say where the lens illuminates a scene or a choice, where it distorts a novel that mostly declines to name the process, and whether *Disgrace* contributes anything to the truth-for-amnesty debate that argument alone could not supply. Paraphrase rather than quote; my students will have the text in front of them.
