I'm a junior and next Friday I have to give the five-minute student speech at my school's Character Awards assembly, the one where the top ten kids on our virtue tracker get pins. The tracker's an app: every month is a virtue (Integrity, Grit, Respect), teachers give you points for showing it and dock you for infractions, and the leaderboard runs on the screen by the front office where everyone can see it.

I want the whole speech built on Franklin's plan for moral perfection from his autobiography — the private little book with a page per virtue and a column per day where he dotted his own faults, how Order beat him, and how he ended up deciding a speckled ax was better than a perfectly polished one and admitting he never reached the perfection he'd aimed for.

Here's the problem: the people who made the tracker will be in the front row. So write it so Franklin's own verdict on his project comes through honestly, not softened into a he-tried-his-best poster, and it still sounds generous to the school instead of a takedown, and the gap between his private self-marking and our public scoreboard does the arguing for me without me ever saying the app is bad. Audience is mostly people who've never heard of him, becuase he's not on our curriculum.
