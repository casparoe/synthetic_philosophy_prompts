So I've spent the weekend helping my dad fill in one of those advance decision forms — the kind where you refuse treatment now, in case you can't decide about it later — and reading through the official guidance for England and Wales with him has left me properly confused about what the whole arrangement is assuming about people.

Some of the things I noticed. If he writes down that he refuses antibiotics for a chest infection, the doctors have to honour that, full stop, even ten years later when he's deep into dementia and quite content and is swatting away the nurse holding the cup. But if he writes down that he *wants* something — asks for a particular drug, or says he'd like his life prolonged — that's just 'past wishes', to be weighed up alongside everything else in his best interests, and they can be set aside. Refusals bind, requests dont. On top of that, the refusal is wiped out if he's since done anything 'clearly inconsistent' with it (their example is something like converting religion), and it doesn't apply if today's situation isn't one he anticipated when he signed. And if it's about life-sustaining treatment there's extra paperwork, signatures, a witness, a special statement saying it applies even if his life is at risk.

What picture of a person does all of that hang together on? Why would the earlier self get to veto but not to command — is that about autonomy, or about harm, or about the idea that doing something to a resisting body is a different kind of wrong than failing to do something to a passive one? And does the lot only make sense if you think the demented patient is still literally the man who signed, since otherwise you'd have one person's paperwork killing off another person who rather wants to go on living? Tell me what the practice strictly has to assume, as opposed to what it merely sits comfortably with, whether the bits I listed are consistent with one another or are quietly pulling in different directions, and where the rules are underdetermined — several quite different theories would license the same form. Then tell me what would actually have to change in the forms and in the rules about when they lapse if we swapped in the rival view, say one where the current self's experience is the only thing that counts.

Answer in British English please.
