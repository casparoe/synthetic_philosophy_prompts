I am taking a seminar this term on early modern natural philosophy, and one of our assignments is a piece of speculative fiction that treats a historical position seriously on its own terms rather than as a curiosity to be debunked. My section covers Kepler's astrology, and I would like a self-contained short story of roughly 1,500 to 2,200 words built around his actual view, which is considerably stranger than the usual textbook summary allows.

Here is the position as I understand it from our readings; the story should inhabit it rather than argue against it from the outside. Kepler was a working astrologer who cast nativities for princely patrons and for a general, yet he regarded most of the inherited apparatus as arbitrary convention: the twelve named signs of the zodiac and the qualities traditionally attached to them, the division of the chart into houses, the claims about which affairs each planet governs, and above all the practice of inferring particular events — a shipwreck, a marriage, a death, the outcome of a campaign — from where the planets happened to stand. What he kept was the geometry: the angular relations among the planets as seen from the Earth, understood through the regular polygons constructible in a circle and hence through the same ratios that make musical intervals consonant. On his account the soul, and something like a sensing faculty belonging to the Earth itself, is innately attuned to such configurations. A configuration at the moment of birth does not write a script of events; it impresses a temperament, a key the person is tuned to — closer to the way a piece of music moves a listener's mood than to the way one billiard ball moves another. He accordingly cast himself as a third party between two camps he considered equally wrong: the credulous practitioners, and the learned scoffers who, in rejecting prophecy, also dismissed a genuine natural-philosophical conjecture about how form in nature might act on a sensitive soul.

With that in mind, please observe the following.

1. Let the plot turn on a commission. A powerful patron demands a specific, dated prediction, and the astrologer cannot in conscience supply one because his own theory forbids it, while refusing outright would cost him the appointment on which his astronomical work depends. The interesting material is what a man can say when the most he can honestly offer is a description of what someone is like, not of what will happen to him.

2. Include a disputation or tribunal scene in which three positions are each given their strongest form: a practitioner for whom the heavens genuinely compel events; a theologian or learned skeptic for whom the entire enterprise is fraud or worse; and the astrologer, whose defense satisfies neither and who comes to see that this dissatisfaction is the price of his view.

3. Give the theory one concrete case that tests it inside the story — for instance, two children born under nearly identical configurations who diverge sharply, obliging the astrologer to maintain that the imprint supplies material which the soul then elaborates in its own way.

4. If you draw on the figure's circumstances, note that his mother, a village healer and herbalist, was prosecuted for witchcraft and that he spent years defending her. That history should complicate things: he is a man who believes nature is full of subtle but real correspondences, and who is paid to be the authority on them, watching a woman destroyed for a cruder version of the same belief.

On craft: the protagonist may be Kepler himself or an invented imperial mathematician modeled closely on him. Carry the ideas through situation and character rather than exposition, with no dialogue that reads as a lecture. Period detail should be plausible without becoming pedantic. Do not resolve whether astrology is true; the ending should leave a reader unable to wave the position away. Invent any letters, judgments, verses, or book titles that appear, and attribute nothing to real historical works. Please give me the story alone, without framing commentary.
