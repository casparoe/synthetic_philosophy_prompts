I have a forty-minute slot in three weeks at a joint philosophy–linguistics pragmatics seminar, followed by about fifteen minutes of questions. The room will be roughly a dozen people: two who work in the neo-Gricean tradition and treat generalized conversational implicature as the default output of lexical triggers; one committed relevance theorist; one experimentalist who runs reaction-time and picture–sentence verification studies on scalar implicature; several formal semanticists who take what is said to be fixed compositionally and regard pragmatics as the residue; and two or three graduate students who have read Grice for a seminar but are not specialists. Nobody in the room works primarily on the intention-based analysis of speaker meaning, though the two neo-Griceans will treat that analysis as non-negotiable background.

The paper argues that Grice's standard battery of diagnostics — cancellability, nondetachability, reinforceability — does not track a single distinction and should be retired. My three legs are these. First, explicit correction of a contextually supplied domain restriction ("Not every bottle is empty — there's one on the counter," after someone says every bottle is empty, meaning in the fridge) is formally indistinguishable from explicit cancellation, so cancellability cannot mark the boundary between what is said and what is implicated. Second, nondetachability fails in the paradigm case: replacing a scalar trigger with an explicitly glossed non-scalar counterpart — "some — possibly all — of the students passed," or a nonce quantifier defined as 'at least one' — kills the not-all inference, which suggests the inference is anchored to the trigger rather than to the proposition expressed. Third, reinforceability, which Grice himself allowed to fail for some implicatures, does no diagnostic work at all. In place of the battery I propose one graded test: a content counts as implicated to the degree that recovering it required the hearer to represent the speaker as having chosen among available alternative utterances.

I already expect the objection that "available alternatives" is not determinate enough to bear this weight. Tell me whether that is the angle it will actually come at me from, or whether this audience will attack the replacement test somewhere else first.

Prepare my Q&A. I want the eight or so objections this particular room will raise, ordered by the product of how likely each is to surface and how much damage it would do. Phrase each one in the idiom of the person who would raise it — I want to hear the experimentalist talk about embedding and processing cost and the relevance theorist talk about the maxims doing no explanatory work, not my own paraphrase of them. For each objection give me: a reply deliverable in about sixty seconds; an honest note on where that reply is soft; and a flag for whether it is better conceded up front during the talk to defuse it. Then identify the single reply that has to land for the replacement test to be taken seriously at all, and close with the question I am hoping nobody asks, together with the best answer I could give if it does.

Give me arguments rather than citations: I have to deliver these from the podium, so wherever a reply would ordinarily lean on someone's published result, state the reasoning instead of naming the source.
