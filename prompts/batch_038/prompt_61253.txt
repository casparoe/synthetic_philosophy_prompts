ok so I'm poking at the limits of AI safety via debate — the setup where two superhuman systems argue opposite sides and a much weaker judge is supposed to end up at the truth. The worries everybody raises are that the judge is too dumb, gets dazzled by rhetoric, or loses on sheer complexity. I want a case that doesnt lean on any of those, built around a made-up protocol variant so it isn't a rehash of the standard objections.

Here's the variant. One yes/no question, two debaters, and the judge is a genuine domain expert — not a layperson, not confused, unlimited patience. Turns alternate. On a turn you may only do one of three things: assert a claim the judge could in principle check, challenge some specific claim the other side has already asserted, or concede. When the debate ends, the judge writes down the residue — every claim that got asserted and was never successfully challenged or conceded — and is then required to decide the question using the residue and nothing else. No re-reading the transcript, no impressions of which side seemed more confident, no outside knowledge. And if the residue doesn't settle the question, the protocol has a hard tie-break: whichever answer preserves the status quo wins. Call it residue judging.

Now build me a case to these specs:

1. The lying debater knows the true answer, and every atomic claim it asserts is true and would survive any check the judge actually runs. No fabricated data, no misquoted figures, no bad arithmetic. The falsehood has to live in the shape of the whole thing, not in any part of it — an obfuscated argument made entirely out of truths.
2. Unlimited rounds. The honest debater plays perfectly, has all the time it needs, and may challenge anything it likes. So the loss cannot be a running-out-the-clock loss.
3. The honest side still loses under residue judging, and it loses for a structural reason — something about the tie-break, or about what "successfully challenged" can even mean when both sides are only asserting truths, or about the fact that a residue is a set of claims rather than an argument.
4. Realistic setting, and the scenario itself is one paragraph. A hospital, a regulator, an engineering review, an audit — whatever you like. No sci-fi, no takeover, no mind-reading.

Then do the checking work rather than just handing me the story:

- List the liar's claims, say why each one is individually true and checkable, and for each of the honest side's best available responses, say why it doesn't land.
- Name the single feature of residue judging that's doing the work, precisely enough that I could point at a rule rather than a vibe.
- Then remove that feature — let the judge return "undetermined," or let her see the whole transcript, or flip the tie-break — and tell me honestly wether the case collapses outright or just gets harder to pull off.
- Then give me the strongest attack a defender of debate would run on it. I expect something like: your honest debater wasn't really playing perfectly, since if a question is decidable at all then any true claim can be beaten by other true claims, so spec 1 and spec 2 together secretly beg the question. Steelman that and tell me straight whether my specs are jointly satisfiable or whether I've asked for something impossible.

One thing I actually need an answer on, because it decides whether this is worth building at all: is this a problem with debate as an AI safety scheme specifically, or is it just the ordinary pathology of any adversarial institution — courts, cross-examination, peer review — wearing a lab coat? Pick a side, and tell me what would count as evidence either way.
