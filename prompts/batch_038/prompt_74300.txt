Been rereading the old material on the centipede game and I keep coming back to the 2004–05 NHL lockout, which I think is about as close to a real-life centipede as history gets. Someone tell me how close.

Facts as I remember them, correct me if the shape is wrong: the old CBA expired and the league locked the players out Sept 16 2004. Bettman wanted "cost certainty" — a hard salary cap tying player salaries to league revenue — backed by an Arthur Levitt report saying clubs were spending about 76% of gross revenue on salaries and had collectively lost $273M in 2002–03. The union under Bob Goodenow disputed the numbers (Forbes put the losses at less then half the league's claim) and refused any cap, countering with revenue sharing, a luxury tax and rollbacks. Negotiations dragged on for months while the pie visibly shrank, because every cancelled regular-season game was money neither side could ever recover. Feb 16 2005 the entire season was called off, 1230 games, no Stanley Cup for the first time since 1919. Then in July, with Goodenow pushed out, the players accepted an eight-year CBA containing a hard cap plus a big across-the-board salary rollback — something like 24%, which is roughly the size of the rollback they'd reportedly been offering back in February with no cap attached, though I might have the number wrong. Salaries went from ~76% of revenue to ~54%.

Walk me through it as a centipede. Who are the two players, what counts as pass and what counts as take at each node, where does the shrinking pie sit in the payoffs, and why does backward induction say take at node one? Then tell me why that prediction lands so badly here and whether the standard escape hatches actually work: Kreps-Milgrom-Roberts-Wilson reputation effects with a small chance your opponent isn't the rational type; the self-defeating character of the BI argument itself, since being confident the other guy still cooperates at node 99 seems incompatible with being confident he's commonly known to be rational; or simply that the endpoint was never common knowledge.

Then the part I actually care about, two hunches I can't evaluate myself:

1. Neither player was unitary. The union was ~700 guys with wildly different discount rates — a 34-year-old goalie on his last contract lives through a lost season very differently than a 21-year-old rookie — and I have this intuition that a centipede where a *different* agent moves at each node, so nobody has to personally own the endgame, is exactly the structure that stops the unraveling. Is that a real result or am I inventing it?

2. Same on the other side. I'm fairly sure the small-market clubs that were bleeding money (Pittsburgh, the smaller Canadian markets) stood to *gain* from a cancelled season. If a chunk of one "player's" payoff from burning the pie is positive, does the centipede even apply anymore?

Finally run it the other direction: what does this episode do to the theory — confirmation, anomaly, or a case the centipede was never built for? And what's the honest verdict on how much content backward induction has left once you need this much scaffolding to save it from its own prediction. Don't make the model speak where it's silent; I'd genuinely rather you point at the places it says nothing.
