The episode: India, 1967 to 1980. As I understand it, the sequence ran roughly like this —

- Golak Nath (1967): the Supreme Court reverses its earlier line and holds that fundamental rights are beyond Parliament's amending power.
- Parliament responds with the 24th Amendment (1971), which restores and spells out the constituent power: Parliament may amend any provision, including Article 368 itself, following the Article 368 procedure.
- Kesavananda Bharati (1973), a 13-judge bench, 7–6: Parliament can amend anything, including Article 368, but it cannot destroy or alter the basic structure of the constitution. Note that the limit is nowhere in the text — it's read in.
- Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975): Parliament has in the meantime passed the 39th Amendment, which retroactively immunizes the Prime Minister's own election from judicial scrutiny while her appeal is pending; the Court strikes down the relevant part using basic structure.
- The 42nd Amendment (1976), during the Emergency, writes into Article 368 roughly two things: that there shall be no limitation whatever on Parliament's constituent power to amend, and that no amendment shall be called in question in any court on any ground.
- Minerva Mills (1980): the Court strikes those down, using — the basic structure limit, i.e. a limit whose only source is the Court's own 1973 reading, to defeat an amendment whose whole purpose was to declare that no such limit exists.
