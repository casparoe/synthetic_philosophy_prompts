I'm teaching the week on infinity and time in my film-and-philosophy seminar and I drew Groundhog Day, which means I get to sit through eleven undergrads doing the Nietzsche eternal-recurrence thing at each other. I want them doing something weirder and more precise, and I want to pressure-test my own idea before it goes on the syllabus.

My hunch: the loop is a supertask, and the live question is the Thomson's-lamp question — what fixes the state at the limit. Phil lives through ω-many Februaries and then wakes up on the 3rd, and the 3rd isn't a member of that sequence, it's the day after it. There's no last Feb 2, no final iteration on which he does the thing that releases him; the day he gets out is, from where he's standing, indistinguishable from however many others came before it until the morning fails to run its usual script. So the film is positing a state at ω that nothing in the finite stages determines — which is just the thing Benacerraf thinks Thomson botched: the trouble with the lamp isn't that its state at midnight is impossible, it's that the description of the switching simply doesn't say what it is.

Where I need you to push back, because a colleague already has: the days aren't squeezed into a finite interval, each one takes a day; and the iterations aren't the same act repeated, since the world resets and Phil doesn't — he keeps the piano and the ice carving but not the body that would have earned them (no calluses, no aging), and the old homeless man dies on Feb 2 no matter what Phil tries. Is the lamp analogy doing real work here or is it decoration I'm fond of?

Then: does the film cheat by stipulating the limit state, or is the stipulation the whole point? What exactly is it committing itself to when it makes the exit something that happens at no step — is that an argument against any finite-procedure account of becoming a decent person? And does the ending undercut it by paying him off, with Rita and the town and the whole gratitude montage? Be honest about that tension rather than tidying it, and tell me plainly where the film just refuses the reading.
