I am a novelist, and what I need from you is a classification instrument, not an essay.

My protagonist — call him Elias — is a municipal bridge inspector in a small port city. Every weekday at 05:40 he walks the same four-kilometre route across the same two spans, examining the same eleven expansion joints in the same order with the same steel feeler gauge. He devised the route himself, decades ago, after a span collapsed in another country; he has shortened it once, when a deck was replaced, and lengthened it once, when a new crack pattern appeared on the eastern pier. He has never missed a day. When the sequence is interrupted — a delivery truck across the walkway, a locked gate — he does not resume where he stopped; he returns to the start and begins again. Asked why he does it, he says it has to be done, and cannot elaborate. He reports no experience of choosing: the round presents itself each morning as something that must happen rather than as an option he takes up. In one scene his daughter moves his gauge two metres along the parapet while he is inside the maintenance hut; he finds it, carries it back to its place in the order of things, and starts the round over. He is untroubled by all of this and would refuse an offer to change.

A philosopher friend who read the draft says Elias exhibits sphexishness — the rigidity illustrated by the old digger-wasp anecdote, in which a wasp hauls paralysed prey to the mouth of its burrow, leaves it there while she goes inside to inspect, and, if the prey is displaced during that inspection, finds it, drags it back to the threshold, and begins again, apparently without end. He adds that the anecdote is disputed: the wasp may adapt after enough repetitions, and re-inspecting a disturbed nest may be a sensible standing policy rather than a malfunction. I want that dispute exposed by the instrument, not settled by it.

My difficulty is practical. Two early readers disagree about whether Elias is unfree, and their disagreement turns out to be a disagreement about what the question even is. Some hold that a fixed routine is beside the point so long as nothing prevents him; some that it is fatal because he could not have done otherwise; some that everything depends on where the routine came from; some that a routine this reliable is an accomplishment rather than a deficit; some that praise and blame survive all of it regardless; some that I am asking something malformed.
