Working on my quals and I've built an argument I can't decide about — half the time it looks airtight, half the time it looks like a magic trick, and I can't find the seam. I'm taking the descriptive picture seriously: people discount quasi-hyperbolically, so there's a β that makes the immediate count for extra, sitting on top of a δ that's perfectly time-consistent. All the trouble comes from β.

(1) If an agent's rankings of the same options conflict, at most one of them is her true ranking.
(2) Quasi-hyperbolic discounting guarantees conflict: for any β<1 and fixed δ, gym-next-week beats sleep-next-week while both are distant, and sleep-now beats gym-now once it's 11pm and one of them has become immediate.
(3) That reversal isn't produced by new information — she learns nothing about the gym, only that it got closer. So you can't privilege one ranking as the better-informed one.
(4) And you can't privilege one procedurally either. Any candidate for the true ranking has to be picked out somehow — what she'd endorse on reflection, what she'd choose if she could precommit, what she says when she's calm and well-rested — and every one of those procedures is executed by an agent who has a β. Contaminated all the way down.
(5) So there's no non-arbitrary way to select a true ranking, and by (1) she has none.
(6) Weakness of will is acting against your own true ranking.
(7) So there is no weakness of will. Which means discipline, resolution, and caving don't track anything real, and every practice built on those words — praise for self-control, the whole cognitive-behavioral apparatus — is marking a distinction that isn't there.
(8) So either β=1, which the evidence flatly rules out, or all that talk is unfounded.

I want to keep (2) and (3); (3) is the bit I'm proudest of becuase it kills the "her future self knows better" reply before it gets started. I really dont want (8). So: which step breaks, and what does breaking it cost? In particular tell me whether (4) actually closes off the second-order-endorsement and precommitment escapes the way I think it does — if it doesn't, the whole thing is just a curiosity and I want to know why.
