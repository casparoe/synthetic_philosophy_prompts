A reading group I'm running is on the verge of arguing past itself about whether dreams are experiences, and I want the specification fixed before anyone defends a view. Set out what an adequate account of dreaming has to deliver — the requirements, each paired with the phenomenon or consideration that motivates it. Draw those phenomena from the awkward cases rather than the tidy ones: awakenings from verified REM sleep that yield long narrative reports alongside awakenings that yield a sincere denial of having dreamed at all; people who insist they never dream yet report dreams when woken during the night; lucid sleepers executing pre-arranged eye signals from inside REM while afterwards maintaining they had been asleep and dreaming throughout; reports that grow, smooth out, and shift on a second telling minutes later; absurdities that would be glaring while awake going unremarked in the dream itself.

Then sort the list. Which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable, and which are one camp's commitments wearing a requirement's uniform — in particular whether "the account must not render ordinary dream talk systematically false" and "the account must treat dream reports as exactly as fallible as, and no more fallible than, memory reports elsewhere" are constraints or tastes. Say too whether each surviving requirement is one a purely conceptual analysis could satisfy or one that only an empirical hypothesis could, since that determines whether Malcolm and Dennett are even answering the same question.

Flag any pair or triple you think cannot be jointly met. My own suspicion is a hard tension between wanting a dream to be datable to a stretch of sleep, so it can be matched against physiology and against those eye signals, and wanting the only evidence for a dream to be the impression formed on waking.

Close briefly: what each deflationary answer — Malcolm's, on which a dream is not an event during sleep but is bound up with the waking impression, and Dennett's, on which dream content is written into memory at or around waking rather than lived through — loses on this list, and which single requirement does the most damage to each. Mark your confidence as you go, and end by naming the one point in your answer you are least sure of.
