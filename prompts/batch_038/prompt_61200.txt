I'm a philosophy-of-psychiatry PhD student and I'm stuck on a chapter. The set-up is Hacking: looping effects are supposed to be the big trouble for realism about psychiatric kinds, because the classification changes the people classified, so the kind isn't just sitting out there waiting to be discovered the way a natural kind is.

The distinction I want to build the chapter on — call it *entry looping* when a label changes who falls under it (a new diagnostic category recruits milder cases that used to count as merely eccentric), and *career looping* when a label changes how the trajectory of someone already under it unfolds (they take up the identity, learn the script, the prognosis shifts). My hunch is these pull in opposite directions: entry looping makes the extension look arbitrary, which hurts realism, while career looping looks like the kind actually doing causal work, which you'd think helped it. So the standard anti-realist move might be running an arbitrariness problem together with a causal-mechanism story that doesn't have the implications it's being given.

Write the essay that chapter is trying to be: argue for or against the claim that once you seperate entry looping from career looping, Hacking's looping effects stop being an argument against realism about psychiatric kinds. Steelman both sides, including the obvious comeback — that career looping is only "causally efficacious" in the same deflated sense as money or national borders, and nobody thinks that makes those natural kinds. Give me an actual verdict and defend it rather than a both-sides shrug, and tell me straight whether the distinction is load-bearing or just a relabelling of the old debate.
