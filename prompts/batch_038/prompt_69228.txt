I am designing the supervision architecture for a reasoning system whose intermediate steps I expect to become impossible for a human reader to check. Before I compare candidate schemes, I want the specification they will be judged against: the desiderata that any account of scalable oversight must meet in order to count as an adequate supervision signal rather than a merely convenient one.

Write that list. For each desideratum, give the consideration or failure case that motivates it — what concretely goes wrong when a scheme lacks it. I would like at least one entry in each of the following regions, though you may add others and should tell me where I have carved a region badly:

- The epistemic position of the supervisor: what a labeler must be able to know in order to produce the label at all, and how that requirement behaves once the supervised system reliably surpasses the labeler.
- Granularity and timing of feedback, and the credit-assignment problem that each available granularity inherits.
- Robustness under optimization pressure: the ways a signal that looks adequate on a held-out sample degrades precisely because a strong learner is being pushed against it.
- Cost and coverage, including whether the signal can be obtained where it is most needed rather than only where it is cheapest, and whether the schemes whose step-level labels are manufactured by looking at where the rollout eventually landed have thereby smuggled the outcome standard back in through the data pipeline. Say explicitly when a scheme's process labels turn out to be outcome labels in disguise.
- The semantic question of what a step-level judgment is a judgment about: whether "this step is good" has an account that does not reduce to "this step raises the probability of a result I can already recognise."

For each desideratum, mark it non-negotiable — a scheme that fails it is not a candidate at all — or camp-relative, in which case say which camp demands it and what the defensible grounds for dispensing with it would be. Then identify any subset of the list you believe to be jointly unsatisfiable and explain the tension rather than asserting it; this is the part I most want to see. Finally, state which single desideratum does the most excluding work, and score process reward models, outcome reward models, debate-style protocols, and weak-to-strong or recursively decomposed supervision against the list in a sentence each. A table is acceptable for the scoring, but keep the weight on the criteria.

Do not pad the entries with generic desiderata that any evaluation framework would list. Every item should be one whose denial would be contested by someone competent in this area.

End your answer with a line beginning "Verdict:" followed by exactly one of `{satisfiable}`, `{satisfiable-if-relaxed: <the desideratum>}`, or `{jointly-unsatisfiable: <the subset>}`, then a dash, then the label of the single desideratum with the greatest excluding power.
