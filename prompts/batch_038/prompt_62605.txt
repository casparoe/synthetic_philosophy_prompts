**Purpose first, because adequacy is relative to it.** I want a specification — not a defence, and not a survey — of what a theory of *geometric* rationality in stake-sizing must be able to do in order to count as a serious normative competitor to expected-utility maximisation over terminal wealth. The intended application is a single, indefinitely long-lived agent (a person, an endowment, or an autonomous system that must fund its own continued operation out of its own capital) who faces a long sequence of wagers and cannot recruit anyone else to share the risk. Treat the list as a specification that a reader could use to reject candidate theories without having to adjudicate the underlying philosophy.

**The cases the desiderata must be built around.** Please construct your requirements so that each is motivated by something like one of the following, and say explicitly which case each requirement is answering to.

1. *The reference wager.* A repeated even-money bet, staked as a fraction of current capital, with probability 0.6 of winning each round, independent across rounds. The growth-rate-maximising stake is 0.2 of the bankroll; the stake at which the long-run growth rate falls to zero is a little below 0.4; staking 0.5 is worse than not playing at all in the long run despite the bet having positive expected value. Any account that cannot explain why three different verdicts attach to three different stakes of the same favourable bet has not yet said what its criterion is about.

2. *The estimated edge.* Same wager, but the 0.6 is not known: the agent has seen 15 heads in 25 flips and has a posterior over the true probability wide enough that a non-negligible probability remains that the bet is in fact unfavourable. Does the criterion apply to the growth rate under the true distribution, under the agent's beliefs, or under neither? Is plugging the point estimate into the Kelly formula the answer, and if not, what does the theory owe instead?

3. *The operations floor.* The agent must pay a fixed cost each period to keep operating, and a bankroll below some multiple of that cost means it is switched off rather than merely poorer. The floor is endogenous: it moves with the agent's own plans. A stake that is growth-optimal on the unfloored problem can carry a small but real probability of breaching the floor, and breaching it is not a bad outcome on the scale of the criterion — it removes the agent from the sequence altogether.

4. *Two ledgers.* The agent keeps a floored operating pot and an unfloored expansion pot and wagers from both. Is growth-optimality applied to the sum, to each pot separately, or is the question ill-posed? Note that the two answers can diverge even when both pots face the same wager, and that they diverge further when the wagers differ.

5. *Withdrawals.* An agent that consumes, or that pays out to beneficiaries, rather than accumulating. A criterion defined purely over wealth at a terminal date has no verdict at all for an agent with no terminal date, and the growth-optimal stake plausibly changes once a fixed proportional withdrawal is introduced.

6. *Restoring the ensemble.* Suppose the agent can sell partial claims on its own future payoffs to many small counterparties, or buy insurance against a run of losses, at some price. This looks like a way of converting a path-dependent position into something closer to an average over parallel positions. Does the growth-rate norm lose its force once such markets exist — and if the transfer is costly, does the criterion apply before or after the cost?

7. *Dependence.* The i.i.d. structure is doing the work that lets realised growth converge to expected log return at all. State the dependence conditions the criterion needs, and what the theory says when rounds are positively autocorrelated or when the agent faces a persistent regime rather than a stable distribution.

**What I want in the deliverable.**

- A numbered list of requirements, each with the case or consideration behind it, stated so that a theory can be checked against it rather than admired. Include requirements about what the theory must *explain*, not only what it must recommend — in particular, whether it must account for the special status of logarithmic utility (the fact that it is the utility function under which short-horizon optimisation coincides with long-horizon optimisation in the independent setting) or whether it may simply assume it.
- Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp. A table is welcome here. I expect some of what growth-maximisers and expected-utility theorists each demand of the other is prejudice rather than desideratum, and I want that separated out.
- Flag any requirement that is question-begging — that is, any putative desideratum that already contains the answer to the central dispute, which as I understand it is Samuelson's objection that maximising expected log wealth is one more expected-utility calculation rather than an independent criterion of rationality, and that no finite-lived agent is compelled to adopt it.
- Identify any subset of the requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied. My suspicion is that demanding applicability under parameter uncertainty, demanding that the criterion track the growth rate of the agent's actual wealth, and demanding that the agent need not know the true distribution form such a subset, but I may be wrong, and if the tension is resolvable I want to see how.
- Say which single requirement does the most exclusionary work — which one, if insisted upon, eliminates the largest share of the positions people actually hold.
- Require the theory to state what geometric rationality adds over ordinary expected-utility maximisation with a logarithmic utility function. If the honest answer is "nothing but emphasis", that should itself be recorded as a possible outcome rather than papered over.

**Finally, and briefly:** no more than four lines each on how full-Kelly growth maximisation, fractional-Kelly-with-a-prudential-margin views, and unconstrained expected-utility maximisation over terminal wealth fare against your list. I care far more about the list than the scorecard, and I would rather you cut the scorecard than thin out the requirements. Please write throughout in British English.
