Two camps, both of them convinced they're the sane ones. Camp A says a human organism dies when it irreversibly loses its integrated functioning as a whole — the brain, brainstem included, is what makes the body one thing rather than a bag of organs on a ventilator, so total brain failure is death and the criterion is biological, not moral. Camp B says what dies is the person: death is the irreversible loss of the capacity for consciousness and integrated mental life, so a body that will never again be the subject of any experience is already dead even if it's warm, breathing on a machine, and passing urine, and the neurological criterion is right because of that. In practice they endorse nearly the same determinations and fight about the reasoning, which is the worst kind of fight, because it only surfaces at the edges.

Write the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between them would actually produce — the document both could put their names on. I want four parts.

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign. Not mushy ones; make them do work. At minimum I want agreed claims about what the dead donor rule requires and whether it's currently being satisfied, about how much of the integrative work in a living body the brain actually does versus the rest of the organism, about what the two camps think is happening when a body declared dead by neurological criteria goes on to heal wounds, fight infection, gestate a fetus, or secrete ADH, and — this is the one they keep skipping — about what irreversibility even means in controlled donation after circulatory death, where circulation has stopped and everyone has agreed not to restart it, but ECMO could restart it.

2. A disagreements table. Columns: the issue; Camp A's statement of its own position in wording Camp A would sign; Camp B's statement of its own position in wording Camp B would sign; and what, if anything, would actually settle it. If you can't write a formulation one side would accept without wincing, say so in the cell rather than papering over it.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions where an answer would move at least one side. I'd guess three of them are: whether a permanently unconscious patient breathing unaided is dead; whether irreversibility is a fact about the body or a fact about what anyone intends to do to it; and whether there's any line that lets you recover organs from someone whose brainstem alone has failed while refusing to recover them from someone in a permanent vegetative state. For each crux, name the concrete case, protocol detail, or empirical finding both sides accept as bearing on it, and write down each side's prediction about it in advance, so it can't be rationalized later. Fold in the material I think they can't dodge: thoracoabdominal normothermic regional perfusion, where after the circulatory declaration the surgeons clamp the aortic arch so the heart and brain aren't reperfused and then restore flow to the abdominal organs — is the clamp what makes the declaration true, or does it show the declaration was false, and can each camp live with its own answer here? Also the jurisdiction whose legal standard is brainstem death rather than whole-brain death; also the state that lets families refuse the neurological criterion on religious grounds; also the California girl declared brain dead in 2013 whose family kept her body supported for years afterward, with growth, menstruation, and some apparent responsiveness to her mother's voice.

4. A short section saying which parts of this dispute you think are verbal, which terms are doing the work, and what's left standing once those parts are dissolved. If nothing is left, say that plainly.

Ground rules: no bibliography, no reading lists, and don't attribute any of these positions to a named author, book, or report unless you're genuinely certain — I want the argument, not a reference section. Flag anywhere you catch a formulation doing covert work for one side. One or two typos from me is enough, you don't need to add your own, but do keep it readable enough that I could hand it to both camps without embarrassment.
