I'm one of seven county supervisors, and next month we vote on a permit for a big logistics campus out by the interchange. Six months back we ran a pilot I proposed: an internal prediction market, open to staff and residents, with two contracts paying out on the same metric five years from now — net property-tax revenue per daily truck trip — one conditional on approval, one on rejection. The "approve" contract closed at 68¢. Two problems have surfaced since. First, the resolution panel (three of us) has to fix the payout on the reject branch, and the developer has now floated a scaled-down version, so nobody can agree what "rejection" even refers to anymore. Second, roughly 40% of the volume came from five accounts, including the developer's own consultant and a colleague's brother-in-law, and the price jumped the day after that leaked staff traffic report. Two of my colleagues want to treat the market as advisory-in-name but binding-in-practice; another wants to scrap the whole thing as a giveaway to whoever can afford to move a thin market. Draft the memo I'll circulate before tuesday's work session: what should we do with the 68¢ — bind to it, weigh it alongside staff analysis, or set it aside — and what should the reject-branch resolution rule have been in the first place? Please engage the strongest version of the "this is just plutocracy with a bid-ask spread" objection rather than swatting it, and be concrete about whether the informed/insider trading here is a bug or the whole point.
