For my senior literature seminar each of us must bring one argument from outside the discipline to bear on a novel, and I have chosen The Great Gatsby together with the disagreement over whether the American or the European attitude toward inequality is more defensible. By the American attitude I mean the view that large inequalities are tolerable when positions are open to talent, since outcomes then track effort and choice and resentment becomes a defect of character. By the European attitude I mean the opposing emphasis: that where a person begins is largely a matter of luck he did nothing to earn, on Rawls's point that natural endowment and family circumstance are morally arbitrary and on Dworkin's distinction between brute luck and option luck, so that a distribution stands in need of correction however industrious everyone is. Attached to the first view is a further claim, that widespread belief in upward mobility is itself what makes inequality politically bearable.

The standard paper calls the novel a critique of the American Dream, and I do not intend to write that paper. Please read it through the luck-and-responsibility dispute instead, working from the details that make the question difficult rather than the famous ones: the boyhood timetable and self-improvement resolutions in Gatsby's copy of the cowboy book, which his father brings from Minnesota to the funeral and shows Nick with pride; the legacy Dan Cody leaves him that a legal device Gatsby never understood takes away; George Wilson, who labors without ceasing in the valley of ashes and gains nothing; and the fact that the one genuine ascent in the book is financed by bootlegging.

I want a verdict rather than a survey: tell me which of the two attitudes the novel makes more defensible and argue for it. Then tell me where the novel resists that reading, where it is simply not a book about distributive justice, and where the technical vocabulary adds nothing to a plain summary of the plot. I would rather be told the last of these by you than discover it written in the margin of my draft.
