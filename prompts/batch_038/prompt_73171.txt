ok so I'm a junior and for my creative writing elective final we have to write a short story that's secretly about a real scientific concept, but the teacher's one rule is that nobody in the story is allowed to give a lecture. she keeps saying "if a character explains the thing to another character who already knows it, you've failed" and honestly fair. I've been stuck on my topic for like two weeks and I finally picked one after an argument with my friend who does competitive rowing: heritability. specifically how badly people misuse the number. my psych teacher went over it once and I remember the part that broke my brain was that it's a statement about variation across a whole group, not about any one person, and that it goes UP when you make everyone's environment the same, which is the exact opposite of what everyone assumes it means.

here's the setting I want you to build the story in. There's this super competitive residential arts-and-science program — call it whatever you want — that every year runs something the students just call the Audit. Each student gets a card with a number on it, an h-squared for whatever trait their track cares about (pitch discrimination for the music kids, spatial reasoning for the architecture kids, whatever), pulled from some twin registry study. The administration reads a high number as "this one is finished clay, coaching won't do anything" and quietly stops paying for that student's lessons, while the low-number kids get all the resources because they're "environmentally responsive." My main character is a girl who gets a 0.78 for her trait and watches her funding evaporate overnight. The thing that has to blow up in the second half: the school decides to fix an equity problem by giving literally every student identical practice-room hours, identical instruction, identical everything — and the next year the Audit comes back with everybody's number higher than before, and the head of school is thrilled, because he thinks this proves talent is innate, and it proves the opposite. I also want her to have a half-sister who was raised in a totally different state and got a wildly different number for the same trait, and the two of them are supposed to compare cards over a video call that goes badly.

please write it as an actual story, somewhere around 1,500 to 1,900 words, with a title, and give me the whole thing rather than an outline. Third person on the main character is fine, or first if you think it's better. The concept has to come through in what happens to people, not in dialogue about statistics — so if you want the reader to get the "same seeds, two different beds of soil" idea, put it in the mouth of the groundskeeper who's doing something with his hands and doesn't know he's making a point. I'd also love for one short scene to turn on something like the PKU thing my bio teacher brought up (genetic and yet completely fixable by changing the diet) or on how average height in some countries shot up over a century without anybody's genes changing — use whichever fits better, just don't turn it into a fact dump. And end it at a public hearing where the school is deciding whether to keep the Audit, where she gets maybe thirty seconds to say something, and the last line should be what she actually says out loud. I don't need it to argue that the school is evil, I want it to be the sadder version where everyone involved is sincere and well-meaning and still gets it catastrophically wrong. Oh and one typo's fine if a character texts somebody, I don't care, just make the people sound like real teenagers and real administrators, not like a textbook wearing a hoodie.
