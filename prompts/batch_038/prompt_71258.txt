I ran a materials lab for 35 years and only got into philosophy after I retired, which is how I ended up with a small podcast (about 200 listeners, all of them very patient). Next week's guest is a philosopher of music who defends a broadly Schopenhauerian line: music isn't a picture of emotions and isn't a play of forms either, it's a copy of the will itself, which is why it lands on us differently from every other art. I don't want her reciting the standard map of the field back at me. Give me seven or eight questions, and I need at least one on each of these:

- whether 'copy of the will' is anything more than arousalism wearing a metaphysical costume -- what does it actually add over just saying music stirs us directly?
- the scheme she inherits where the low bass stands for inorganic mass and the top line for the striving of the individual, plus the link he draws between consonance and simple numerical ratios: is any of that doing explanatory work, or is it decoration, and if it's decoration what survives?
- his ranking of purely instrumental music above music yoked to words, and how she squares that with the fact that the doctrine was picked up most eagerly by people building music drama.
- the thing I can't shake: if music acts straight on the will and can overwhelm a listener, how is aesthetic experience supposed to quiet willing instead of feeding it?

For each one, add the follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. She's very good at retreating to 'the metaphysics is dispensable scaffolding, the real claim is about how music presents rather than depicts,' so make atleast two follow-ups that make that retreat expensive. Finish with a single question about what would count as evidence against her view -- what sort of music, or what finding about listeners, would make her drop it.
