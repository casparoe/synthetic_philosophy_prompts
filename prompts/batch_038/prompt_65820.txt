My friend and I argued about this for three hours last night and I still cant tell who won. Her argument is supposed to show supervaluationism isn't a genuine third option between epistemicism and degree theory — that it either turns into epistemicism or never bottoms out:

(1) On supervaluationism, "bald" applies to a man iff he comes out bald on every admissible precisification of "bald."
(2) So the truth-values of ordinary vague claims are fixed entirely by which precisifications count as admissible.
(3) But "admissible" is itself vague: there are borderline cases of being an acceptable sharpening of English, and no way to make that precise without begging the question.
(4) So the supervaluationist either accepts a vague metalanguage or draws a sharp line around admissibility.
(5) If she draws a sharp line, then there's a precise fact about which precisifications are in, hence a precise fact about who's bald, hence a determinate number of hairs for the last bald man — that's epistemicism with extra steps.
(6) If she accepts the vague metalanguage, the same argument applies to "admissible" one level up, and again above that, forever, so the semantics never gets fully stated and no truth-value is ever really determined.
(7) Either way there's no stable middle position: vagueness is either ignorance of a sharp boundary or it never terminates.
