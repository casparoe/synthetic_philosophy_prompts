I work on the policy side of an assistant product, and there's a move a colleague makes that I can't get traction on. When someone objects that our refusals are paternalistic — deciding for a competent adult what they're allowed to ask for — he says: it's just a seatbelt law. Nobody thinks seatbelt laws insult adults. You regulate the moment of bad judgement, because the person you're constraining would endorse the constraint from the cool hour, before the crash. Same shape exactly: the user in a bad moment (furious, manic, grieving, bored, three drinks in) is being held to the standard their own steadier self would set. He uses this to conclude that "paternalism" has no bite at all in this debate, so the only live question is which harms are big enough to count, and any further objection is just squeamishness about the word.

I think the analogy is carrying more weight than it can hold, but I can't say where. Can you audit it?
