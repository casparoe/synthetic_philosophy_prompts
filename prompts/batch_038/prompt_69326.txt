"Q3 (25 points). Anatoly Fomenko and Gleb Nosovsky's New Chronology holds that much of what we call ancient history is a medieval fabrication. (i) Explain what distinguishes that program from an ordinary piece of historical revisionism — say, a historian arguing that a given chronicle was composed a century later than assumed, or re-dating a battle by a few decades. (ii) Assess whether Popper's falsifiability criterion is by itself sufficient to classify the New Chronology as pseudoscientific, making use of Lakatos's account of research programs. (iii) Address the objection that Duhem–Quine holism makes any such classification hopeless."

Students had about 35 minutes. Here's the answer:

"The New Chronology, developed by the Moscow mathematician Anatoly Fomenko together with Gleb Nosovsky out of ideas going back to Nikolai Morozov, holds that genuine written history begins only around 800 AD, that the record for 800–1000 is nearly empty, and that most of the events we file under antiquity actually happened between 1000 and 1500. Rome, Greece and Egypt as we have them are reflections of medieval events: Jesus was crucified in the twelfth century, the Trojan War is the Crusades, the Mongol hordes were Russians. Fomenko gets there by statistical analysis of chronicles — measuring things like the amount of text devoted to a given period and looking for length profiles in one chronicle that duplicate profiles in another — and he discards radiocarbon dating and dendrochronology as unreliable. He also maintains that the surviving record was falsified by a coalition of interested parties.

(i) What separates this from ordinary revisionism is scale and framework. A historian who argues that a chronicle was composed a century later than assumed, or who moves a battle by a decade, is still working inside a shared evidential framework: she offers reasons other specialists can check, she moves only the pieces she has to move, and if a document or a tree-ring sequence comes up against her she changes her mind. Fomenko is not revising a date within the framework, he is replacing the framework, and he explains away in advance anything the framework might produce against him. That last move is the decisive one. Since every hostile result can be charged to the falsifying conspiracy — contamination, forged samples, doctored master sequences — the theory seals itself.

(ii) Popper settles it. A theory is scientific only if it forbids something, only if some observation could count against it. No observation could count against the New Chronology, so it is unfalsifiable and therefore pseudoscientific. Its dating claims have of course been tested: the tree-ring and carbon evidence has been run against them and comes out the other way, which only confirms that it is not science. Popper's point about history applies as well — in The Poverty of Historicism he attacked the whole project of reworking the past to fit a grand scheme, and that is exactly what Fomenko is doing, so the Popperian framework condemns the New Chronology twice over. Kuhn would reach the same verdict by a related route: for Kuhn a mature science is one whose paradigm puts its claims under falsification pressure, and the New Chronology has no paradigm, so nothing in it can be falsified.

Where Popper is thin is that falsifiability is a yes/no test and gives no guidance when a theory is testable but keeps getting rescued. That is Lakatos's contribution. A research program has a hard core protected by a belt of auxiliary hypotheses, and what matters is not whether some single claim survives refutation but whether successive modifications are progressive, yielding novel predictions with independent support, or degenerating, merely absorbing anomalies. The New Chronology is textbook degenerating: the conspiracy auxiliary is introduced after the fact, has no independent support, and predicts nothing new. That is the real ground of the verdict, and it does work Popper cannot do.

(iii) The holism objection is usually treated as fatal, and in a sense it is right. Duhem and Quine showed that a hypothesis never faces experience alone but only as part of a whole body, so Fomenko is strictly entitled to keep his core and revise the belt instead — radiocarbon is contaminated, the tree-ring master sequence is circular, the numismatic evidence presupposes what it proves. If that is permitted, then the difference between him and the mainstream is only which body of auxiliaries a given community happens to prefer, and demarcation collapses into majority labeling. So the honest conclusion is that we cannot demarcate by logic alone, though in practice historians are not actually taken in."
