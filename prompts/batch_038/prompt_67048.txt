For a second-year decision theory seminar — 24 students, one 90-minute session, four tables of six — I want an in-class game that makes them feel the force of Pascal's mugging, without the phrase being spoken until the debrief. The twist I want built in is that before anyone is mugged, every student spends a round *as* the mugger: each drafts an extortion or salvation pitch on an index card under a strict word limit, and is rewarded for whichever pitch extracts the most payment from the room. They then meet each other's pitches in the following round as victims, holding a budget of tokens convertible into a small, capped number of participation marks, so the stakes are real but cost me nothing. I also want a complexity tax — card length should visibly move how much people pay — and a second wave of muggers late in the session, so that students who paid early discover their budget is exhausted by claimants they ought to have paid too.

Please give me the following sections, in this order:

1. **Setup** (~150 words) — room, materials, what I need printed in advance, and a cover name for the game that students see on the handout.
2. **Rules and Payoffs** (~400 words) — the token economy, the exchange rate into marks, and exactly how the drafting round is scored.
3. **Run Sheet** (~250 words) — minute by minute.
4. **Debrief** (~400 words) — a sequence of questions leading students from what they actually did to the expected-value arithmetic, then to the three standard replies (a bounded utility function, a probability floor, a complexity penalty on the story), noting which reply their own behaviour has already committed them to; and whether threats and offers drew different payment rates, and what that asymmetry would imply.
5. **First-Time Failure Modes** (~250 words) — what will predictably break, including the student who refuses to play and the one who writes a joke pitch, and how to handle each without killing the session.
6. **What the Game Cannot Reproduce** (~200 words) — an honest account of the feature of the real problem the mechanics cannot generate and that I will have to supply by argument.

Write in British English.
