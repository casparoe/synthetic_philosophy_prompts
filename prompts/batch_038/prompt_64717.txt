I'm TA-ing an intro metaphysics section and this week is origins — necessity of origin, the whole Kripke line about whether a table could have been made from a different block of wood and whether the Queen could have come from different gametes. The class split in half and both halves are being smug about it: one side goes "obviously you couldn't have been made by a different sperm and egg, that's just what 'you' means," the other side goes "gametes are arbitrary little things, why on earth would it matter which one." I want to do something on Friday that isn't another slideshow, and I've decided a piece of satire will land better than a fourth reconstruction of the argument.

So: write a satirical customer-support email thread, 700–900 words, between a client and the "Origins Assurance" desk of a very expensive fertility clinic. The client is complaining about her child's origin exactly the way you'd complain about a hotel room — wrong item delivered, she wants an exchange, or failing that a refund, or failing that the manager. The desk has to answer every single message in flawless corporate boilerplate while being metaphysically incapable of granting anything she asks, because any alteration to the origin yields a different child, and about a different child she has no claim at all. The humour has to come from the metaphysics being correct rather than from the client being stupid — she should be a reasonable person who has simply been handed the vocabulary of consumer rights and is using it perfectly. No winking, deadpan throughout, and nobody states the philosophical moral out loud.

Beats I'd like worked in: the clinic's own terms distinguish the strict version of the thesis (one different sperm and it's a different person) from the hedged version that only rules out a *totally* different origin, and legal deploys that gap to weasel out of the refund. A second ticket in the queue is from someone who wants to swap origins with her brother, on the grounds that surely the two of them could have exchanged places. There's an egg-donation case where the desk has to explain that thier "origin of record" field has two slots, genetic and gestational, and that they do not always name the same woman. A child produced with mitochondrial replacement — nuclear DNA from two people, mitochondrial DNA from a third — and the warranty has to specify which of the three counts as the origin. And a twinning case, where the clinic's own lab records show the embryo split at day four, so the desk is forced to concede that whatever necessity an origin has, it apparently didn't settle how many people came out of it. Keep it self-contained, no citations or reading lists needed, and let the final message be the client's, landing the thing that's actually going on underneath the complaint without ever naming it.
