Marking qual papers for my phil-of-bio seminar and one of them is basically Dennett's crane/skyhook picture run as a decision procedure. The kid calls niche construction and the Baldwin effect cranes, calls Chomsky's innate UG a skyhook, and then says strong-emergence downward causation has to be a skyhook too since there's no lower level left to hang it from. He thinks the labels settle things.

Help me audit the metaphor before I grade it. What's playing the role of the crane, of the load being lifted, of the ground the crane stands on, of the height gained -- and of the crew that built the crane, which he never mentions? Then: where does the mapping break? I keep coming back to the fact that a real crane is a designed artifact sitting on a foundation that was already there, so the source domain quietly hands you both a designer and a settled hierarchy of levels, which is precisely what's in dispute. And if every crane is just the output of an earlier crane, isn't "skyhook" little more than a bookmark for the explanation we haven't found yet -- epistemic rather than metaphysical? Is there a principled difference between a crane and a skyhook, or only between a crane we can describe and one we can't?

Finally tell me whether his three verdicts survive the audit, and whether some other image would carry the distinction he actually wants (accelerator of design vs. smuggler of design, maybe). If the metaphor is doing more rhetorical work than argumentative work in the paper, just say so -- I'd rather tell him that now than at the viva.
