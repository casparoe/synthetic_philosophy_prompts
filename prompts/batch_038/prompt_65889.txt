Been going back and forth with a friend from my utilitarianism reading group about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — the charge that the pleasure of cold water on a hot day, the pleasure of finally seeing why a proof works, and the pleasure of schadenfreude don't share any one feel, so there's no single thing that is *the* good. His answer is that this is essence-mongering: pleasures are like Wittgenstein's games. Chess and ring-a-ring-a-roses and a kid throwing a ball for a dog overlap in criss-crossing ways with no one feature running thru all of them, and nobody thinks that makes "game" a bogus category or stops us saying which games are better. So demanding a common hedonic tone is asking for something concepts never owed us, and the objection just evaporates.

Audit that for me. Correspondence table first: what plays the games, what plays the overlapping similarities, what plays the ranking. Then the disanalogies, sorted into ones that actually threaten his conclusion and ones that are just noise. Two things I especially want pressed: whether a resemblance cluster can carry the weight hedonism puts on it, given that pleasures are supposed to be commensurible for tradeoffs and to be good-making rather than merely lumped-together; and whether the analogy secretly concedes the objection instead of answering it. If it fails, is there a better analogy for the unity of pleasure, or should the hedonist stop reaching for analogies and just say what pleasure is?
