I am a third-year double major in computer science and philosophy. This term I am in a seminar on the structure of justification, and we have just spent three weeks on the regress: foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism. Outside the seminar I maintain the build system for a large monorepo, and I am considerably more fluent there than in the epistemology literature — targets and dependencies, cycle detection, memoization and incremental rebuilds, content-addressed caches, lazily generated build graphs, shallow clones of history.

Translate the three positions into that vocabulary, one at a time, as faithfully as you can, without quietly loading my framework's assumptions into the positions and calling the result a translation. Be explicit about the mapping choices you make: what plays the role of a belief, what plays the role of the support relation, and what it amounts to for a target to be justified — up to date, successfully built, or merely checkable on demand.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims carry over exactly, which carry over only if I let you distort them, and which have no counterpart at all? In particular: is there anything in my vocabulary that expresses a cycle which confers support rather than being an error state, and anything that expresses an infinite chain which is never completed yet always locally checkable? Our tooling, when it finds a cycle, warns and drops one edge instead of failing, and cyclic dependencies are sometimes handled by fixed-point iteration from a bottom element — tell me whether either move rescues coherentism or smuggles a foundation back in.

For each piece of residue, say whether it marks a real gap in the build-system picture or shows the philosophers' disagreement is less substantive than it sounds, and end with a straight verdict on whether the trilemma itself survives the translation.
