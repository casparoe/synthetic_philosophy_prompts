Berkley's whole thing was that for an object to exist is just for it to be perceived, and the reason the table is still there after you leave the room is that God never stops perceiving it. Here's a case I can't run through his system, and I'd like someone to actually work it through rather than give me a general summary of idealism.

In an open-world game, the parts of the map nobody has walked into yet aren't rendered at all — no player, no camera, nothing. But the game runs off a fixed seed, so which trees sit on which hill out there is already settled, and every player who finally walks in finds the exact same forest. Meanwhile the server stays up around the clock holding all that state even at 4am with zero people logged in.

Two questions. One: does Berkeley count that unvisited region as existing, and if the answer is no, what does he do with the fact that its details were nailed down before anybody looked — that seems like a genuinely different situation from a rock nobody's ever seen. Two: can the always-on server take over the job God does in his system, or does it fall short, and if it falls short, what exactly is it missing? Please answer in American English.
