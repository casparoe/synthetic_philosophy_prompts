Since getting laid off in March I've had a formicarium on my desk — two nests joined by a tube, a few thousand workers — and it's done something to my head. I read the Aunt Hillary dialogue in Gödel, Escher, Bach years ago and filed it under cute thought experiment, but lately I can't shake the feeling that the thing next to my monitor is closer to what Hofstadter was pointing at than I'd like. I want to write an essay on whether an ant colony literally has a mind, and I want it to actually take a side rather than survey the field.

Two things I can't resolve. First, the usual move is that ants are to the colony what neurons are to the brain — but my ants walk through that tube all day, brood gets carried back and forth, and after my queen died I dropped a new one in and they just took her. Neurons dont defect. Does a group need a stable border to be a subject at all, or is the analogy fine and I'm being squeamish? Second, and I think this is the real crux: even granting that the colony picks nest sites, remembers where the food was, reallocates foragers when a trail gets blocked — is any of that a mind, or just a description that happens to be convenient at the group level, like saying the market panicked when nobody panicked? And where would colony-level cleverness stop and there being something it's like to be Aunt Hillary begin, if it ever does?

Argue it through and land somewhere. A qualified verdict is fine — a mind in one sense, not in another — but say which sense and why the other one fails.
