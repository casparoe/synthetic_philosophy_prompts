**Working title: "Order-dependence is the real problem with Jeffrey's rule"**

*§1 — the target.* Almost every discussion of Jeffrey updating spends its energy on the inputs problem: the rule tells you how to propagate a shift in P(E) through the rest of your credences, but is completely silent about where the new P(E) is supposed to come from. My draft says that's aiming at the wrong target. The inputs problem is a gap in the story, not a defect in the rule. The deeper worry is diachronic.

*§2 — what strict conditionalisation has that kinematics lacks.* Strict conditionalisation is order-invariant. Provided E and F both had positive prior probability, conditioning on E and then on F lands you in exactly the same place as conditioning on F and then on E: both routes terminate at P(·|E∧F). So on the strict picture, the *sequence* of your experiences makes no difference to your final doxastic state — only their total content does. That looks like a mark of the rule tracking something real.

*§3 — kinematics has no analogue.* Two successive Jeffrey updates on different partitions generally fail to commute, and the reason is structural rather than accidental: the rigidity conditon is always applied to the credence function you're currently in, not to the original one. So the second update reads its conditionals off a state the first update has already moved.

Toy case. Ω = {a, b, c, d} with prior 0.4, 0.1, 0.1, 0.4. Let E = {a, b} and F = {a, c}, each of prior 0.5. Update one: shift P(E) to 0.75. Update two: shift P(F) to 0.75.

- E first, then F: a = 9/13, b = 3/28, c = 3/52, d = 1/7.
- F first, then E: a = 9/13, b = 3/52, c = 3/28, d = 1/7.

Same two inputs, same magnitudes, and b and c come out swapped. Neither route has any claim to be *the* result of having had both experiences.

*§4 — the expansion reply, and why I think it doesn't rescue things.* The obvious defence is that a Jeffrey update can always be modelled as strict conditionalisation in an enlarged space: bolt on an auxiliary observation variable Y, tune its likelihoods so that P(Y = y\* | E_i) is proportional to q_i / P_old(E_i), and conditioning on Y = y\* reproduces the kinematic update exactly (you also need Y to be conditionally irrelevant to A given E_i, which is just rigidity restated). Formally this works. But notice that the tuning constant is a function of your *prior* for E. So the enlarged model is indexed to the credence function you're updating away from. Run a second update and you need a second enlargement built on top of the first — and the likelihoods you'd have to assign to the second auxiliary variable are different depending on which order you went in, because P(F) after the E-update isn't P(F) after the F-update. You cannot write down a single order-neutral enlarged model in which conditioning on both auxiliary variables in either sequence delivers both kinematic updates. What the reply recovers is commutativity inside one fixed model, not commutativity across a sequence of kinematic updates.

*§5 — conclusion sketch.* The radical probabilist therefore has no available notion of total evidence for their beliefs to converge on: their doxastic state is path-dependent in a way the strict conditionaliser's simply isn't. Which is embarrassing, because the entire motivation for radical probabilism was to be more *realistic* about how experience actually impinges on us.
