ok so — our philosophy-of-math reading group is closing the term with a session on the Gödel nobody assigns: that fourteen-point list of beliefs he left behind in his papers, and the modal ontological proof he sketched around 1941 and then let a younger logician copy out in early 1970 when he thought he was dying. I've been volenteered to read something aloud at the end of the session. We do this every year, it's a tradition, and it's usually bad. I'd like this one not to be. Write as someone who is both a working poet and has taught enough modal logic to know exactly what S5 buys you.

Here's the conceit. As the story goes, he wouldn't publish the proof because he was afraid people would read it as a confession of faith instead of as a demonstration that such a thing could be derived once you had the right axioms — and meanwhile he spent years arguing for an afterlife in letters to his mother and to a skeptical interviewer who kept telling him it wasn't landing, and he died in a Princeton hospital in January 1978 having starved, because he was sure his food was poisoned and would only eat what his wife prepared and she'd been away in hospital for months. I want the poem to hold both of those without explaining either. So: make the derivation the form. Twelve numbered steps in all — the five axioms, the three definitions, and then the derived results up to the final theorem, with the Q.E.D. as the twelfth step (three theorems or four, your call, as long as the count comes out right). Each step is a couplet, then one unnumbered closing line, so roughly 25 lines. The steps have to track the actual architecture: a primitive notion of a positive property, the closure rule that anything implied by a positive property is itself positive, the rule that exactly one of a property or its negation is positive, positivity being invariant across worlds, Godlikeness as having all of them, essence as the property that necessarily entails everything else a thing has, necessary existence as the axiom that springs the trap, and the modal step that turns possibly into everywhere. But the couplets can't read like two-line syllogisms — the second line of each is where the hospital, the untouched tray, and the Sunday mornings with the Bible propped up in bed come in, so the proof is simultaneously carrying the argument that this can't be the only life. Someone who knows the derivation cold should be able to say which step is which, and should notice that the axiom making necessary existence positive is the point where a logic exercise stops being one and becomes a man who needs food from one particular person.

A few ground rules: no mysticism-by-numbers, don't lean on the incompleteness theorem at all unless you can earn it in a single line, and don't turn him into a tragic mad genius or a saint — the whole interest is that he was a hard-nosed rationalist who held that the world is rational, that materialism is false, that there are other worlds and rational beings of a higher kind, and therefore that a life ending here would make the whole thing pointless. Dont quote him and don't cite anything (I can't check quotes and neither can you, and we've got no books in the room) — paraphrase throughout. Think out loud first with a brief step-by-step mapping of what each of the twelve steps will carry, then give me the poem. After the poem, add a valid JSON object (no comments, no trailing commas) with these fields: "line_count" (integer), "steps" (array of objects, each with "label" like "Axiom 2", "lines" containing the couplet's text, "formal_role" for what that step does in the real ontological proof, and "poem_role" for what it does here), "the_turn" (a string naming which step carries the irony about the unpublished-proof disclaimer and why it belongs there rather than elsewhere), and "bent" (array of strings for anywhere you knowingly deviated from the proof's structure or from the historical record).
