So I'm the student member on my residential college's standards panel this year, and I'm also in a moral sociology seminar where we just finished the honor / dignity / victimhood cultures unit (the microaggression-and-moral-cultures article, plus Nisbett and Cohen's culture-of-honor insult experiments), and honestly the panel has gotten more interesting than the readings. Our chair — I'll call her Dr. V — has been on it something like nine years and I've been keeping notes on basically every case she's ruled.

Her track record, roughly in order:

- Guy mocked a roommate's accent in a 40-person group chat. Finding against him, heavy sanction, plus a written apology to the target. The chat was semi-public.
- Two guys in a private argument, one told the other he was a worthless piece of shit. No finding. She said somthing like private insults between consenting adults aren't ours to police.
- Costume party, a white student showed up dressed as a day laborer. Finding against her, but only an educational sanction, and this came after she apologized unprompted and met with the complainants twice.
- A student accused a classmate of plagiarism in the middle of seminar, loudly, and it turned out to be false. Sanctioned the accuser, pretty hard, and the written reasoning was almost entirely about the damage to the accused's standing in the cohort — no mention of anyone's background.
- A prof made a remark in lecture that a student found demeaning. She declined jurisdiction: no policy breach, no evidence of intent, referred it to the dean.
- Anonymous shaming thread on the college forum targeting a student the panel had already cleared. Sanctioned the thread's author severely, mostly for continuing to litigate a closed case in public.
- One case where the complainant and the respondent were both from groups the college counts as underrepresented and the accusations ran both directions: no finding against either, just a facilitated meeting.

New one on Thursday's agenda. Anonymous post on the forum, very detailed, accusing a specific named RA of favoritism and of making the floor unsafe for queer students. Nothing in the handbook covers it. The RA responded publicly, apologized for whatever they did that landed badly, offered to step down from social programming, and asked for the thread to come down; the anonymous poster won't identify themself or retract anything. The RA says their reputation on the floor is shot. Four residents have written in saying the thread itself is what makes them feel unsafe.

What I want from you:

1. Predict Dr. V's verdict and sanction on this, and say which rule you think she's actually running. I can make her seven cases fit at least four different stories — a dignity one about policy breaches and intent, an honor one about reputation and public standing, a victimhood one keyed to who's experiencing the harm, and a restoration one where the apology does all the work — and I genuinely can't tell which she's on.
2. Be honest about which of those four fit the record equally well and which ones are strained by specific cases. Point at the case that does the straining.
3. Tell me how I should vote if I want to be consistent with the panel's own precedents rather than with my own gut. If the precedents really do underdetermine it, say what principle we'd be adopting with each option, because whatever we pick gets cited next year.
4. Name the one hypothetical future case that would best separate whichever rules you can't separate now.
5. Short version at the end, if you think one of these moral cultures is actually the right one for a body like ours to be running and why — but don't let that leak into the prediction.
