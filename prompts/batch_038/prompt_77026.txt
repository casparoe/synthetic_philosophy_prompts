I'm preparing the opening session of a philosophy-of-medicine seminar whose participants are mostly clinicians, and I want a decision tree that sorts them by what they take a disease to be — by how they rule on cases, not by which label they'd pick. Build it from the questions rather than from the list of views: the cases and the answer-combinations they permit come first, and the named positions get located in that space afterwards.

Nine to twelve nodes, each phrased so a clinician can answer it without knowing the literature — not "is disease value-laden?" but "she has an asymptomatic condition that will shorten her life by a decade if left untreated; is she already ill, and does your answer change if she'd rather not be told?" These cases must be load-bearing: asymptomatic hypertension; presbyopia at fifty, where the function loss is universal within her reference class; an asymptomatic BRCA1 carrier; Deafness or achondroplasia in someone who disavows any limitation and identifies with a community; sickle-cell trait in a malarial versus an urban environment; a statistically rare but harmless anatomical variant; and grief that outlasts a duration threshold and thereby becomes a diagnosis.

The branch questions should isolate the distinctions that actually do the work: whether biological normality is fixed independently of anyone's goals; whether normal function is relative to a reference class, and who gets to choose the class; whether harm belongs in the definition or only in the decision about what medicine should treat; and whether disease, illness-as-lived, and sickness-as-social-role are one thing or three.

Leaves: at least five named positions — the naturalist statistical-function account, the harmful-dysfunction hybrid, an ability-to-reach-vital-goals account, the view on which illness is essentially evaluative, and one you'd name better than I have — each with a sentence on its most awkward commitment and a sentence on which case above it must swallow hardest. Also mark at least two answer-combinations the tree makes coherent but nobody defends, diagnosing each as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves that come out identical across all seven cases.
