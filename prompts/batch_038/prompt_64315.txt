In my AP psychology class our teacher explained dreaming by saying that the sleeping brain runs a kind of virtual-reality simulation: a world is rendered from the inside, the body is taken offline, and almost nothing from outside gets through. She used that picture to settle a philosophical dispute for us, arguing that since the simulation is genuinely running for somebody, a dream must be an experience had during the night, and that the philosopher who denied this — Malcolm — was only noticing that we cannot inspect the simulation from the outside. A classmate said she had it backwards: the VR picture is precisely what makes the denial look plausible, because Dennett's suggestion that the whole thing might be written into memory at the moment of waking is just the difference between a headset that renders in real time and a headset that hands you a recording afterward.

Could you audit the analogy for me? I would like an explicit mapping — what plays the headset, the rendered world, the user inside it, and the machine's activity log — followed by the disanalogies, sorted into the ones that actually bear on whether a dream is an experience and the ones that are surface detail. REM paralysis seems to cut against the idea of an immersed user, and my teacher's further claim that rapid eye movements are the headset tracking what the dreamer watches seems worth examining too. Please say which features of a real VR setup the analogy quietly imports that the dream case lacks, whether an inner audience is among them, whether my teacher's conclusion survives once those imports are removed, and what analogy would carry that conclusion better if this one cannot.
