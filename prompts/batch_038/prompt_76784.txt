I'm chairing the Q&A after our department colloquium next month, and the speaker defends a Gödel-style modal ontological argument — axioms about "positive properties," God defined as the being that has all of them, necessary existence taken as positive, the whole thing running in S5. I get twenty-five minutes with her and I want questions that engage the actual machinery, not another round of "so do you think this proves God exists." Draft six to eight, each with the follow-up I'd fire back against the reply she's most likey to give.

Please make sure the set includes: one on the axiom that makes every property either positive or its negation positive — is there any reason to think the world sorts that neatly, and what does she say when you ask her to name the criterion; one on the axiom that whatever is positive is necessarily positive, and the modal collpase worry people raise about Gödel's original axiom set as opposed to the tidied-up versions; one on running the same schema with the polarity flipped (a necessarily existent devil, or the Anderson-type emendations that trade one axiom for another) and why that's a reductio rather than a compliment; one on how much work S5 is really doing and what she'd have to surrender if she gave it up; and a closer on the gap between "a necessary being with all the positive properties exists" and anything a theist would recognize as God.

One thing that matters, since there'll be undergrads in the room: for each question, flag whether the objection is one most people working on this argument now concede, or whether it's genuinely live and contested among those who know the literature. I don't want to walk away implying there's a settled verdict here when there isn't.
