I spent about thirty-five years modelling distributed control systems — process plants, mostly, lots of semi-autonomous units talking to each other over fairly narrow channels — and since I retired I've got properly hooked on philosophy of mind. Currently stuck on the split-brain business, the corpus callosum patients from Sperry and Gazzaniga's work and all the arguing that's gone on since about whether that gives you one person or two.

Here's my thing: it reads to me like a systems question wearing a metaphysical hat. What makes an architecture one thing rather than two things loosely coupled? I've spent a working life answering questions like that with bandwidth, shared state, common clocks, arbitration, observability, how much cross-talk before you stop calling it two subsystems and start calling it one. So I'd like you to translate the philosophers' positions on split-brain cases and the unity of consciousness into that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can manage.

Work through the main positions one at a time — whatever you take to be the real live options, the ones that say consciousness is disunified in these patients, the ones that say it stays unified despite the evidence, the ones that say unity comes in degrees or in more than one flavour. For each, give me the restatement in my terms: what does integration become, what does a single point of view become, what does the corpus callosum become once I'm not allowed to say anything except channels, shared memory, common clocks, modules and interfaces. Then I want a proper residue report, and this is honestly the part I care about most:

- which bits of each position carry over clean, with no fudging at all;
- which bits only carry over if I quietly stretch a term — tell me exactly where you had to bend something and what the bend costs;
- which bits have no counterpart whatsoever in my vocabulary, so that the best I could do is change the subject.

And then the verdict I'm actually after. For whatever fails to translate, is that because my engineering toolkit is missing something real — I suspect the candidate is anything about there being something it's like to be the system, viewed from the inside, which I can't see how to get out of channel capacities and coupling however clever I am about them — or is it because some of these claims about unity of consciousness don't amount to much once you strip the incense off them? Please come down on one side or the other per position rather than hedging, and say what made you pick it. Don't just smuggle my own assumptions back at me and call it translation; if a philosophical notion genuinely has no systems analogue, leave it untranslated and say so plainly.

Couple of practical points: British English please, and no reading lists or references — I want your reasoning, not a bibliography I'd have to go and dig up myself.
