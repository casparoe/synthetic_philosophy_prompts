Write an investor pitch deck, roughly 650 words, formatted as numbered slides with the founders' speaker notes underneath each one, for a startup selling a wearable that outputs a single real-time pleasure score. Invent the company and its founders: no real people, firms, or products, and no references or citations of any kind.

The target of the satire is the presupposition that pleasure is one kind of feel varying only in amount — the assumption a hedonist needs in order to add pleasures up and compare lives — and the deck must take that presupposition completely seriously and never once concede it. Every difficulty is absorbed as a feature, a roadmap item, a premium tier, or a known edge case. Stay deadpan throughout, with no winking, but write it so that a reader who has never met the debate still finds it funny, while someone who has recognizes each of the following being handled straight-faced: what the unit is calibrated against, and why the score for a warm bath and the score for a difficult piece of music come out suspiciously close; why the numbers are supposed to be comparable between users at all; a user study reporting that subjects cannot say what the high-scoring experiences have in common, which the founders read as proof the device tracks something introspection misses; a beta test in which the score for the relief of pain outranks the score for the pleasure it relieves; a module for being pleased that something is the case, which double-counts whenever what one is pleased about is itself pleasant; and a masochist tester whom the company reclassifies rather than explains.

By the final slide the deck should have quietly committed the firm to delivering the score without the experience that produces it, and the reader's recoil should come from that slide's own logic rather than from any authorial signal.
