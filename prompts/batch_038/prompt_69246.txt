For several years now I've been keeping a private list of places where the one-line summary of a debate actively misleads, and one entry keeps resurfacing in the philosophy-of-mind threads I read: the standard gloss on the Malcolm–Dennett dispute over whether dreams are experiences treats the two men as teammates. Both deny that dreaming consists in having experiences while asleep, the gloss goes, and they differ only in tone and decoration. I don't think that survives contact with what they actually claim. Malcolm's is a thesis about what a dream report means — the report is made true by the dreamer's impression upon waking and asserts nothing about the sleeping interval. Dennett's cassette theory is a thesis about how the machinery runs — the narrative is selected and installed at or very near the moment of waking, preceded by an interval in which nothing experiential happens at all, and on his telling the selection is cued by what is going on around and inside the sleeper. Those two positions answer differently a question I care about more than the ontology: can a dream report ever be evidence about what happened in the room while the subject was asleep? I want a thought experiment that puts the difference on display instead of arguing about it in the abstract.

The specifications are as follows. Scenario first, in one paragraph of no more than 150 words, set in a sleep laboratory equipped only as such labs were then: scalp EEG and EOG, an experimenter able to wake the subject at a chosen latency, and a tape player in the room. No imaging beyond that, no implants, no drugs, no unusual subjects — one ordinary adult volunteer. Something must happen in the room during the sleeping interval that the subject could only have learned of through her senses, and her waking report must track its content specifically enough that coincidence is unavailable as an explanation. Three further constraints. (a) The case must not turn on any mismatch between felt and clock duration; each of the three views has a way to absorb that, so leaning on it would make the exercise trivial. (b) The polysomnographic record must be compatible with all three views, so that whatever discrimination the case produces comes from the theories' own commitments rather than from how I have described the sleep stage. (c) Because Malcolm and Dennett agree that there was no experience, the case has to make them disagree on the evidential and semantic questions: whether the report is true, and whether it carries information about the sleeping interval.

Then give me the demonstration. For each of the three positions — the ordinary view on which the dream was a real experience occurring during sleep, Dennett's cassette theory, and Malcolm's account of dream reports — state the verdict on both questions: is the subject's report true, and does it tell anyone anything about what happened in the room while she slept, and if so, in virtue of what? I want the reasoning that generates each verdict from the view's commitments, not a list of conclusions. After that, name the single feature of the case that is doing the separating work, say what happens to the three verdicts if that feature is removed, and give me the Malcolm defender's strongest attack on the case. My guess is that it will be some version of the charge that the scenario looks like a counterexample only if one has already read the report as being about the sleeping interval; tell me whether that charge sticks or whether pressing it amounts to conceding the point.

Since the case exists to test the teammates gloss, let me fix what would count as cheating: a scenario that merely relabels the two positions, one that separates them only by handing either an ad hoc claim it does not independently need, or one that stipulates the verdicts rather than letting them fall out of the theories. I also do not want to be told that the dispute is irresolvable or that it is verbal — unless that is your honest assessment after actually building the case, in which case say so plainly at the end, since that would be an informative result rather than a dodge.

Open with a single sentence stating whether your case genuinely separates Malcolm from Dennett or only superficially, then the scenario, then the checks. Hard cap of 800 words. No references, no suggested further reading; I know the texts well enough.
