I am in the second year of a philosophy of biology module, and this week's seminar concerns whether talk of information in genetics is literal or merely a convenient metaphor. Before I begin the essay, I should like to be sure I have properly understood a tension that arose in supervision and that the set readings, so far as I can tell, do not confront directly.

The first strand is this. A well-known line of argument, associated with Maynard Smith, holds that the genome carries information in a semantic sense rather than merely in the sense of Shannon theory: the sequence of a gene can be about the trait it influences, and the notion of information at work here is bound up with evolved intentionality, in that both the form of the signal and the response to it have been shaped by selection. A natural way of spelling out what makes such aboutness possible is to appeal to the arbitrariness of the sign-to-referent mapping, in something like the Saussurean sense. Because nothing in the physical nature of the codon GGU resembles or necessitates glycine, the connection between the two can be regarded as settled by the conventions of the translation machinery; and it is arguably just this independence of vehicle from content that allows a misreading of the code to count as an error of interpretation rather than as a mere causal mishap.

The second strand pulls the other way. There is a substantial body of work in molecular evolution suggesting that the standard genetic code is far from arbitrary: single-base substitutions in the natural code have smaller average effects on the properties of the resulting amino acid than all but a minute fraction of randomly generated alternative codes of similar structure, which has been read as evidence that the assignment of codons to amino acids was itself shaped by selection for robustness against mutation and mistranslation. Chemically similar amino acids, in other words, tend to occupy similar codons for a reason.

My difficulty is whether these two strands can be held together. If the property that was supposed to license semantic information — the arbitrariness of the mapping — turns out to be absent from the genetic code, does the semantic reading of genetic information collapse, leaving only the deflationary causal-and-Shannon account? Or does the case merely reveal that arbitrariness was doing spurious work, so that aboutness and evolved intentionality can be had in a system whose sign-vehicle pairing is systematically optimised rather than conventional? I should be grateful for an explanation of what genuinely separates these two responses, and of whether arbitrariness is being asked to bear a load that it cannot carry in either direction. Please keep the discussion at the level of the concepts involved: I am not looking for a general survey of the case for and against the code metaphor, nor for pointers to further literature, but rather for an analysis of this particular incompatibility and of what would settle it.
