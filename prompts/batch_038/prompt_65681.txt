I'm in a seminar this term on population ethics and the normative foundations of public policy, and for my term paper I want to work from an institutional practice rather than a thought experiment. Could you help me reason through the following?

The practice. In most wealthy states, and now in a number of middle-income ones, a total fertility rate below roughly 2.1 children per woman is treated in official documents as a threshold being crossed and as an alarm in its own right — not merely as a fiscal inconvenience. Since 2020 Hungary has exempted women who have raised four or more children from personal income tax for life. South Korea, with the world's lowest fertility rate at around 0.7 children per woman, has committed very large sums over two decades to birth incentives and frames depopulation as a national emergency. Japan and Italy have assigned ministerial responsibility specifically for the falling birth rate. Yet the same officials who sound this alarm routinely present immigration as a legitimate partial substitute for the births that did not happen, and their states' development-assistance budgets continue to fund voluntary family planning in lower-income countries.

That combination is what I can't resolve. My questions:

What theory of value does the alarm presuppose? Does treating sub-replacement fertility as bad in itself require an impersonal totalist view, on which a larger population of people whose lives are worth living is better than a smaller one even when no particular person is thereby better off? Or can the whole practice be licensed by claims about identifiable present and near-future people — contribution bases, dependency ratios, the solvency of pay-as-you-go transfer systems, market and labour-force size — with no commitment in population axiology at all?

If the alarm does rest on something like totalism, is the immigration substitution coherent? Immigration does not increase the number of people in the world; it relocates them. What does offering it as a remedy reveal about what the state is actually tracking, and does it show the alarm to be a disguised claim about existing citizens' old age?

How should the non-identity difficulty bear on this? The future citizens a pronatalist policy aims at are not determinate individuals waiting to be helped or harmed, and which people will exist depends on the policy itself. Can a government coherently say it is acting for the sake of people who would not otherwise exist, and if not, what work is that rhetoric doing?

Which rival views does the practice quietly rule out — an average view, a strict person-affecting restriction, a critical-level view with number damping? Would an explicitly average view make falling fertility a gain, and is there any sense in which the practice already treats it that way, for instance when it celebrates higher income per head?

Are the parts of the practice consistent with one another: the tax subsidies, the openness to immigration as a substitute, the funding of contraception abroad? And where is the practice philosophically underdetermined — which distinct theories would license exactly the same policy package, so that the package cannot be used to infer any of them?

Finally, what would have to change in policy and in official justification if the presupposed view were replaced by an explicit totalist one, or by a strict person-affecting one?

Please answer in continuous prose. Then, after the prose, give me a JSON object summarising your reconstruction with exactly these fields: presuppositions, an array of objects with claim, necessity (either strictly-required or merely-congruent), and reason; rivals_excluded, an array of objects with view and how_the_practice_excludes_it; immigration_substitution, an object with what_it_shows and consistent_with_totalism (one of consistent, inconsistent, indeterminate); non_identity_bearing, a string; internal_consistency, an object with parts_compared and verdict (one of consistent, in-tension, outright-inconsistent); underdetermination, an array of objects with theory and why_it_licenses_the_same_policy; replacement_scenarios, an array of objects with replacing_view and policy_changes, the latter an array of strings. I'm after the reconstruction itself rather than sources, so please don't include citations, a bibliography, or a reading list.
