hey, can you write me a satirical marketing one-pager plus FAQ for a made-up enterprise SaaS product — something blandly named, the kind of thing a governance consultancy would sell to corporate boards, appellate panels and hiring committees. The pitch is that it finally fixes the embarassing situation where a committee votes yes on every individual question going into a decision and then votes no on the decision itself.

About 700 words, deadpan vendor voice throughout: a headline, a short value-prop paragraph, a bulleted feature list, an FAQ with six or eight customer questions, and a small "known issues / limitations" block at the bottom.

The humor has to come from the aggregation theory being handled correctly, not from generic tech-bro mockery. Specifically:

- the feature list should quietly expose the choice between tallying the votes on the reasons and tallying the vote on the outcome, and treat it as a configurable setting with a factory default that nobody ever reads
- the known issues block needs at least three entries a specialist would recognize as real results, correctly characterized but euphemized into product-speak — something about what happens when you let every member's vote count equally, something about re-ordering the questions mid-session, something about the one user account that keeps ending up decisive
- never name a theorem, a paper, a court case, or any real person; the vendor is far too busy selling to cite anything, and I don't want made-up citations either

For the FAQ, include a judge-ish customer who wants the collective verdict to be explainable to the side that lost, a board secretary worried the software is "making the decision for us now", and someone on the free tier asking whether they can keep both internal consistency and equal voting weight. The running joke should be that the product's headline selling point is exactly the combination the theory says you can't have, and nobody in the document ever notices. Aim it at the consultancy-sales voice as a style, not at any actual company or living person — and it should still be funny to a reader who's never heard of any of this.
