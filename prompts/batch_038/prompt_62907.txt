At the end of the month I am defending a thesis at an interdisciplinary workshop on machine ethics, and what I need is a prep sheet for the question period rather than a further defence of the view.

The thesis: a deployed moral machine ought to practise esoteric ethics on Sidgwickian lines. It should deliberate in consequentialist terms about outcomes but report its decisions to users in deontological vocabulary — as the deliverance of rules, rights or duties — because an articulable principle is a gameable one, both for users who learn to phrase their requests so as to exploit it and for the reward model that shaped it in the first place. Concretely, I propose a separate explanation policy, trained to produce rule-shaped justifications that do not disclose the value function actually driving the choice. My argument is that transparency about the machine's real grounds makes those grounds less reliable, so the moral cost of the disguise is outweighed by the exploitable behaviour it removes.

The room will be about thirty people: AI safety researchers who work on deception, oversight and debate protocols; two Kant scholars; a clinical ethicist who chairs a hospital ethics committee; and several philosophers of law with an interest in liability. I expect the safety people to be troubled that a system which systematically misdescribes its own reasoning looks exactly like the failure mode they are paid to detect; the Kantians to object about the users; the clinical ethicist to worry about consent and about who answers for a decision in a coroner's court; and the lawyers to be less interested in all of that than in the record the machine creates.

Please give me the objections in roughly the order they will arrive, phrased as each of these groups would phrase them rather than as a general audience would, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is softest. Mark which ones I should concede in my opening remarks in order to spend my credibility on the ones I can hold, tell me which single reply has to land if the thesis is to survive the hour, and identify the question I should be hoping nobody asks. Where you are reconstructing an objection you would expect from this room rather than one you know to have been pressed in print, flag it as such; then say how confident you are in your ordering and name the point you are least sure of.
