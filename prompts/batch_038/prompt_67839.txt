I work on a booking agent at a travel startup — user types something like "get me to Austin for my sister's wedding, cheapest you can find" and the thing actually buys the ticket. Not trying to make this a philosophy thing, but I've been going in circles with my tech lead for weeks and I want a third opinion.

v1 was literal: parse the constraint, optimize it. It booked a guy a two-leg itinerary that landed four hours after the ceremony started, because it was $90 cheaper than the nonstop. He was furious. My lead's take: the system did exactly what he asked for, so nothing is broken on our side, the fix is a confirmation screen. More broadly he says there's only ever one thing the agent can get wrong, which is not trying to do what we were trying to get it to do — and whether that was a good thing to be trying for is on us, and shouldn't get baked into an engineering spec because then nobody can tell whose judgment it's encoding.

We shipped the confirmation screen. People tap yes and then email support anyway. Which feels like it undercuts his story a bit, but maybe not, I can't tell.

So v2: we trained a ranker on side-by-side picks (show two itineraries, log which one they tap) and optimize for that instead. Comparisons look great, it reliably picks the nonstop morning flight on the carrier everyone's heard of. But it's ~$400 more on average, refund requests are up, and users who were totally fine with a layover can't get a cheap option at all anymore. Survey scores went up though.

Holiday surge is in six weeks and we ship one of them. He wants v1-plus-confirmation, on the grounds that at least it's honest about what it's doing, whereas v2 is just us guessing what people value and then being wrong in a way nobody can audit. Is that argument any good? I genuinely can't tell if v2 is worse in principle or just broken differently.

The part that actually bugs me is separate. The guy who missed the wedding now says "I obviously meant cheapest that gets me there in time." Did we fail to match something he wanted the whole time, or is what he wanted only partly settled by what he says afterwards? If it's the second one then I don't know what v1 was ever aiming at, and his whole argument loses its footing — but I also can't just write "aim at what they'd want if they thought about it harder" because thats not something I can put in a config file. What do you actually do here?
