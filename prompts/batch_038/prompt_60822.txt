I'm a staff engineer on my institute's compute-allocation panel, and I've been asked to write the decision memo for the spring round, which closes in nine days. I have no philosophy training, so I'd like the conceptual part spelled out rather than assumed. Our rulebook scores proposals on a weighted rubric, funds strictly by rank, and adds a clause that no funded proposal may be judged worse overall than some unfunded one — a clause we cannot satisfy this round. Three proposals form a cycle under that standard: pairwise, the panel prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A, each margin narrow. The rubric scores rank A, B, C with no cycle, though adding one criterion weighted at one percent reverses B and C, which is why several members distrust the scores at all. There are two slots. Separately, we had members walk a chain of twelve proposals trading a little breadth of access for a little depth of capability at each step; every adjacent pair was judged a difference not worth acting on, yet the same members rank the first well above the last. To me that looks like accumulated rounding error, except in judgment instead of arithmetic.

Draft the memo. It should say whom we fund this round and on what grounds; propose a standing rule that either repairs the clause or explains how to run the process without it; and state plainly whether a comparison like 'better than' has to be transitive, or whether an intransitive one can be made workable for a body like ours. Then give the strongest case against your recommendation — I assume it involves someone cycling us into approving the same resources twice — and answer it.
