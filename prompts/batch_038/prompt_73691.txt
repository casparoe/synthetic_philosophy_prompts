Trying to work out what I actually believe about the infinite, going by gut on cases, and I want to know what principle — if any — the verdicts add up to.

- Hilbert's hotel, every room full, one more guest turns up: possible, no problem. Infinitely many coaches of infinitely many guests: also possible, dont care.
- Zeno's runner finishing the race after infinitely many halvings: fine.
- A play with infinitely many lines, each taking half as long as the last, so the whole thing wraps at 8pm sharp: fine. But Thomson's lamp, flipped at half a minute, then three-quarters, then seven-eighths: here I think there's no fact about whether its on at 8pm, and that's a genuine impossibility, not just my ignorance.
- Same lamp flipped once a minute forever into the future with no deadline: fine.
- An infinite causal chain with no first member: fine. An infinite past of days, every one of them already elapsed: not fine, you'd never have got to today.
- Infinitely many books stacked with no bottom one: fine. Infinitely many train cars each pulled by the one in front, no engine: not fine.
- The urn: ten balls in, one out, every minute before noon. Take out the lowest-numbered each time and the urn is empty at noon; take out the highest-numbered one you just added and there's infinitely many left. I'll grant both arithmetics, but I don't see how which ball leaves gets to decide that.

Two things worth noticing. I'm perfectly happy with Cantor throughout — none of my "not fine" verdicts are claims that the maths is wrong, they're claims about the world doing it. So "you're a finitist" doesn't cut it, since I'm finitist-ish about some of these and not others. And the pairs above look like one structure with opposite verdicts.

So: give me the principle, precisely, clause by clause, saying which case each clause is there to handle. Then rule on the one I keep flip-flopping over — a hotel with a room for every point of [0,1], all occupied, one more guest arrives; my instinct says impossible because there's no next room to shift them into, but I don't trust the instinct. Add one case of your own that my principle ought to decide but never occurred to me. And if saving all of it makes the principle baroque, tell me which verdicts to drop instead and what the simpler version costs.
