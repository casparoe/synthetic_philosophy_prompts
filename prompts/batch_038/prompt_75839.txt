I'm a backend engineer on the governance committee of a mid-size open source project — think ~400 contributors, 11 maintainers with voting rights, and a single bylaws file living in the repo like a normal document. Article 9 of that file currently reads: "These bylaws may be amended, in whole or in part, by a two-thirds vote of the maintainers, provided quorum is met." Article 2 sets quorum at 8 of 11. That's literally the entire machinery — nothing anywhere says Article 9 is special, nothing says it's off limits, and until last month nobody had thought about it for six years. Then a fight over relicensing produced two competing amendments that went into the same voting round, because the tool we use tallies each proposal independently instead of running them off against each other. Proposal A, from the lock-the-license-down crowd, read: "Article 9 is repealed and replaced with the following: These bylaws may be amended only by the unanimous written consent of every maintainer. This Article may not itself be amended, and any amendment adopted in the same or any later round that conflicts with it is void." Proposal B read: "Article 9 is amended to read: These bylaws may be amended by a simple majority of the maintainers." Both passed. A got 8 of 11, B got 9 of 11 — overlapping but not identical voters, 10 people voted at all, so the round itself was clearly quorate. Our bot stamped both "adopted," and now the canonical file has two mutually exclusive Article 9s sitting back to back and nobody will open the PR that touches it.

It got worse over the weekend, which is why I'm asking instead of just calling a vote. Two maintainers are drafting a Proposal C that would delete Article 9 altogether and hand amendment authority to our corporate sponsor's legal team, on the theory that a body that can rewrite anything can rewrite away its own right to rewrite — and they say A already proved you can do that, since A abolished the two-thirds rule using the two-thirds rule. The A people counter that if C is allowed, then six votes under B could dissolve the whole project tomorrow, which is exactly what A was written to prevent, and that a vote can't be valid if it destroys the thing that makes votes count. Someone else pointed out that A's own last sentence purports to reach back and void B even though they were adopted simultaneously, and another person said the whole round should be treated as null because the bylaws never said what to do when two amendments to the same clause both clear the bar. My partner's advice after listening to me rant until 1am was "just re-run the ballot and make people pick one," which sounds sane but I can't tell if it actually solves anything or just moves the problem down a week, since whatever rule we use to re-run it is one of the two contested ones.

Could you draft the short decision memo I'll circulate to the other ten maintainers before Friday's call? I need it to land on one concrete resolution — which Article 9 text goes in the file, or whether the round is voided, or whether one of the proposals was never adoptable in the first place and why — with a recommended sequence of steps and dates so we're not still arguing in three months. Give the reasoning in terms the non-lawyers on the committee can actually follow, and give the losing side its best shot rather than a strawman, because whatever I post is going to get read hostilely by people who already think the committee is capturing the project. Two more things: I keep having the feeling there's a real name for this kind of knot and that people have thought about it way past my level — a friend who went to law school says the US Constitution has a couple of provisions written so they can't be amended, and that nobody has ever tested what happens if one gets amended anyway — so if there's an established frame for whether a group can vote away its own future power to vote, or for rules that try to bind their own revision, please build it into the memo and name it, just without a jargon dump. And please make the memo usable as-is: headers, a bottom line up top that says what to merge, and a short section on what we should add to Article 9 so this specific mess can't recur.
