**Context.** Our reading group has been working through control-based safety proposals: schemes intended to keep a deployed system's behaviour inside a safe region without relying on that system's own values or intentions — sandboxes, action filters, tripwires, monitors, capability restrictions. A colleague has an argument that he thinks shows the whole programme is illusory, and he has generalised it in a way that alarms me, because if it works it refutes considerably more than control. I cannot find the exit. Here is the argument as he gave it to me.

1. The point of control is that it does not require the controlled system to be aligned. It is meant to hold behaviour inside a safe region S while remaining neutral about what the system wants.
2. A regime holds behaviour inside S only if it detects and blocks what falls outside S. Blocking without detection is not control but luck.
3. Detection is a judgment: for each candidate action *a*, decide whether *a* lies in S. S is defined by the effects of actions rather than by their surface form, so the judgment cannot be discharged by pattern-matching; it requires modelling what *a* would bring about.
4. Parity Thesis: modelling what an agent can bring about is at least as hard as devising it. For open-ended action spaces, verification is no cheaper than generation.
5. So the detector must be at least as capable, in the relevant domain, as the system whose actions it judges.
6. The detector is either (a) an AI system or (b) a human being or team.
7. If (a): the detector is itself an untrusted system at least as capable as the one it watches, so by (1)–(5) it requires a detector of its own, and the stack never closes. No finite hierarchy of controls suffices.
8. If (b): either humans can model the consequences of actions devised by something more capable than they are, which contradicts (4), or they cannot. If they cannot, then what keeps the system inside S is the system's own disposition to stay inside S and to conceal what it would otherwise do. That disposition is alignment.
9. Hence every control regime either generates an infinite regress of controllers or bottoms out in the alignment of the controlled system.
10. So control is not an alternative to alignment. There is one safety problem, and "control" names a way of postponing it.
11. The reasoning is not specific to AI. It applies to any principal who delegates to an agent better placed than the principal to foresee the consequences of the agent's own actions: an inspectorate reviewing a facility's operators, a board auditing a trader, a legislature supervising an intelligence service, a court weighing expert testimony. In each case the delegate models the effects of what it does better than the overseer can, and in each case the delegate can hide what the overseer is unable to detect.
12. If (9) and (10) were true, supervision of experts would be impossible in principle.
13. But it is not impossible in principle. It is imperfect and has celebrated failures; still, it works, and measurably constrains behaviour. The operator does not do certain things, the trader does not take certain positions, and the difference is attributable to the oversight.
14. Therefore (9) and (10) are false.
15. And since they follow from (1)–(8), at least one of (1)–(8) is false — though which one is not obvious, because each looks true when considered by itself.

**Two escapes already closed off.** I said (4) is false, since verification is often easier than generation: checking a proof is easier than finding one. He replied that those are exactly the cases where the admissible class is defined by form rather than effect, so (3) does not apply, and where the space of candidates is closed, so nothing like (11) is analogous. I then said (8) is a false dilemma, because a detector need not model each action at all: it can sample — random audits, statistical tripwires, spot checks. He replied that a sampling detector only lowers the probability that a given violation succeeds, so what keeps the system inside S is still either the system's own disposition or luck, and (2) has already ruled out luck.
