I'm not a philosopher, just somebody who plays a lot of records and has been working thru Schopenhauer on his own — the part where he says the other arts copy the Ideas but music skips that step and is a copy of the will itself. Something in my own listening keeps nagging at me though. Take a piece I know cold, a late Beethoven quartet I've played maybe two hundred times: I can predict every bar before it lands, nothing surprises me, and its still gets me the same way it did the first time. If music has its grip on us by being the will made audible, you'd think total familiarity would drain it, the way rewatching a movie drains the tension.

Could you write a dialogue between two people who both know Schopenhauer's account of music well but split on whether my familiarity thing is a real problem for it? One thinks it shows music's hold on us has nothing to do with the will at all and that Schopenhauer is really just writing beautiful poetry about form and expectation; the other thinks the will is precisely the sort of thing you can never get used to, so predicting the notes changes nothing. I want them genuinely evenly matched, actually answering each others points instead of taking turns giving speeches, and arguing over concrete cases — my quartet, hearing something for the first time, a hook you hate but can't shake — rather than trading slogans. No cheap victory for either side at the end.
