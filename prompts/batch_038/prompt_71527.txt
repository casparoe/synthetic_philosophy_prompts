I'm writing a novel about a hospice nurse who reads some pop account of an integrated-information-flavored theory of consciousness and concludes that basically everything is quietly in agony — the walls, the rain, her own fillings. Her reasoning is meant to be a bit deranged but I dont want it to be dumb; I want the reader to feel it's a real live argument with a shaky step somewhere in it. My beta reader, who actually studied philosophy, says she's skipping at least five steps and that the conclusion doesn't come out even if you grant panpsychism.

So give me your credence in this proposition: "the total phenomenal suffering in the observable universe is dominated by non-biological micro-level subjects, exceeding the suffering of humans and other animals by orders of magnitude." I want the decomposition, not a vibe — roughly: the probability that panpsychism or panprotopsychism is true at all rather than emergence or illusionism; that the combination/bonding problem gets solved in a direction that yields actual unified micro-subjects instead of just micro-qualities spread thin; that those subjects have hedonic valence at all rather than something hedonically null or incomparable; that the valence isn't net positive or dead neutral; and that moral aggregation over that many tiny subjects is coherent enough to make the comparison meaningful. Multiply it out, show me the arithmetic, and tell me which term my nurse is most blithely ignoring — plus the one piece of news that would move your number furthest. Finish with \answer{0.XX}.
