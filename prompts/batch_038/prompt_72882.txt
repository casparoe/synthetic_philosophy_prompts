Can you write me a piece of satire — the minutes of a district council planning committee meeting in England, say 600–700 words, dealing with an application for eleven houses on the edge of a village? The funny should come from getting the discourse exactly right rather than from mugging: the objectors who swear they're not against housing in principle, only this housing; the overshadowing study, the trip-rate argument about the junction, the 'character of the conservation area', the member who reminds everyone he's lived in the parish for forty-three years; the planning officer's report recommending approval, which then gets ignored. Format it properly-ish — agenda item, representations recieved, debate, resolution — and keep it deadpan throughout, no winking, don't ever use the word NIMBY.

And woven in without being argued for: the fact that the only people who can speak are the ones who already own a house, so the twenty-four year old who grew up two streets away and can't afford to come back never gets a line of dialogue, and nobody in the room notices that's strange. British English please, throughout.
