ok so for my philosophy club I had to write a short paper on how objects persist through time, and I ended up defending something kind of weird. I want actual criticism, not encouragement — I already know my parents think it's smart. Tell me where the argument breaks.

THE PAPER:

**Persistence at a Resolution**

1. Setup. Endurantists say an object is wholly present at every moment of its life. Perdurantists say objects have temporal parts spread out along time the way a road has spatial parts spread out along space. The standard complaints are: endurantists can't explain how one and the same cup can be both bent and straight (temporary intrinsics), and perdurantists end up committed to there being a real object corresponding to any arbitrary chunk of the cup's career, plus a bunch of epistemic weirdness about which chunks are cups.

2. My proposal. I think the question "does the cup have temporal parts?" is more like "how long is the coastline?" than anyone admits. A coastline has no single length, because length depends on the size of the ruler you use, and both the coarse and the fine measurement are correct answers to correctly specified questions. Similarly, parthood holds relative to a temporal resolution. At a coarse resolution the cup is one thing that endures for ten years and is wholly present whenever you look. At a fine resolution there are cup-stages, and the cup is their sum. Neither description is more literally true than the other, and it isn't that we're ignorant of which is right — there is no fact to be ignorant of.

3. Why this fixes temporary intrinsics. Nothing has both bendings, because "the cup" doesn't pick out the same entity at every resolution, and within any single resolution the object's properties are indexed to regions the way the perdurantist wants. So the contradiction never gets going.

4. Why it isn't just stage theory. Stage theorists say only instantaneous stages really exist and that ordinary talk about cups and people is made true by counterpart relations. I don't want that. On my view the coarse-grained sums are genuinely real, not just convenient fictions. But I have to admit the obvious cost: nothing makes one resolution the correct one for counting objects, so how many cups are in my cupboard has no single answer either. I'm trying to be an anti-realist about the resolution parameter while staying a realist about the objects, and I'm not sure I'm allowed to do that.

5. Where I think I'm in trouble. (a) If identity and parthood are resolution-relative, then right at the boundary between two resolutions it seems indeterminate whether the thing over here is identical to the thing over there. But there's that argument that vague identity is impossible: if a is only vaguely identical to b, then a has the property of being vaguely identical to b, and b determinately lacks that property, so they differ in properties, so they aren't identical after all. I gestured at this in class and got nowhere. (b) Fission. If a person divides into two continuations, my view apparently has to say the number of people depends on how finely you track the branches, which makes something like survival and responsibility ride on a descriptive choice. That feels wrong.

So: some specific things I want comments on.

- Does section 3 actually escape temporary intrinsics, or have I just relabeled the ordinary move of indexing properties to times and dressed it up? If it's the latter, does the dressing buy anything at all?
- Is the coastline analogy carrying real weight, or is it a bad analogy because coastlines are about how we measure while parthood is about what exists? I keep going back and forth on this and I want someone to argue it out.
- In section 4, does the combination of "the sums are real" and "no resolution is correct" collapse into saying there are no objects at all? If it does, what's the minimal fix?
- On (a), can a resolution-relativist block the vague identity argument by denying that "a" and "b" refer to anything until a resolution is fixed? That's my instinct but I can't tell if it's an answer or just refusing to answer.
- Also tell me honestly whether the whole view is a real third option or just an evasion of the debate, and if it's an evasion, say which section gives that away.
