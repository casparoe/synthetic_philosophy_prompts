I am a high school senior and the host of my school's history podcast. Next month's guest is a historian who defends a precise version of the familiar claim that history is written by the victors. Her thesis is not about censorship: she argues that the winning side rarely destroys the losers' testimony, but supplies the periodization, the categories, and the vocabulary in which that testimony is afterward read, so the filter survives even when the archive is complete, and the clearest evidence is which year each era is said to begin. Draft eight to ten questions that test that refined thesis rather than the slogan. I want at least one question on episodes where the defeated plainly controlled the historical record, one on why archives survive at all (custodianship, institutional continuity, climate, sheer luck) and whether victory explains any part of it, and one on whether her thesis can be falsified, or whether any surviving loser's account can simply be redescribed as already victor-shaped — including the account in her own book. For each question, give the follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and mark the two or three she will be able to deflect by distinguishing authorship from interpretation.
