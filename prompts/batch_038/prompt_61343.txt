I run a monthly "how things work" evening session at our community centre: about twenty-five adults, mostly retired or on shift patterns, no maths past school level, forty minutes, a whiteboard and nothing else. For some months I have been reading around the idea of an information cascade on my own — how a queue forms outside a mediocre café, how a town ends up unanimously certain of something nobody in it actually has evidence for — and I want to teach it properly rather than as a slogan about sheep.

Please take the role of a social epistemologist and behavioural economist who has taught cascades to general audiences many times. Think carefully: before writing the plan, sketch out for yourself which parts of the idea depend on which other parts, and which of those dependencies a lay audience will get wrong if you invert them. Then give me the teaching plan, not the talk.

It should cover:

- the order the components must be introduced in, and why that order — my guess is that nothing works until they have the notion of a private reason that each person holds and cannot show, but tell me if I have that backwards;
- the one example to build the whole session on: what it must contain, what it must leave out, and whether the urn-and-coloured-balls apparatus is worth the minutes it costs at this level (I had assumed a restaurant queue, but say if that quietly smuggles in something false);
- the misconceptions my audience will arrive with or form partway through — I expect "so people are stupid and just follow the herd", "so always ignore the majority", and "this is only an internet thing" — and the precise point in the sequence at which each should be headed off;
- the two features I most want to land: that everyone inside a cascade is being entirely reasonable by their own lights, and that from the outside a cascade is indistinguishable from a crowd that has genuinely got it right;
- one closing question that would tell me whether they understood it or merely enjoyed it;
- what I should deliberately leave out, and announce that I am leaving out.

Please write your answer in British English.
