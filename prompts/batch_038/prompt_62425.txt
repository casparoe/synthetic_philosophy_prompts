I run a small arts fund inside a county government. We give away about $400k a year to resident-led arts projects, any medium, and I chair the seven-person panel that decides. We recieve roughly 140 applications a cycle, twice a year. First-pass reviewers get about twelve minutes per file: the applicant's one-page summary plus a short sample — a recording, a script excerpt, some photos. Two of the panelists are ordinary residents with no arts training, and one is a county councillor who is terrified of the local paper. Anyone we turn down gets a written reason and can appeal, so every reason we give has to survive being read out loud at a public meeting.

I've read What is Art? twice and I'm increasingly convinced Tolstoy's test gets at what we're actually trying to find out — whether the thing did something to somebody — far better than the rubric we inherited, whose top category is "artistic excellence and innovation." So build me the panel procedure: ordered steps a non-philosopher can run in twelve minutes, what counts as a pass at each step, where the procedure stops with a flat no, and where it has to hand the call to a human because its a values question rather than a facts question.

Things it has to deal with explicitly:

- infection as an actual test and not a vibe: what does a reviewer look for in the sample, and how do we stop "I wasn't moved" from turning into "this isn't art," given that Tolstoy's reply to the unmoved critic is that their capacity has atrophied — which makes the whole thing look unfalsifiable.
- the three conditions, individuality / clarity / sincerity: which of them survive being turned into a check list item and which have to stay judgment, and how you assess sincerity from a file when nobody ever meets the applicant.
- his counterfeit markers — borrowings, imitativeness, strikingness, amusement — usable as screens rather than as insults.
- the subject-matter axis, feelings that unite against feelings that divide. He grades those against the religious perception of the age and we're a secular county running about four of those at once, so tell me what stands in for it and flag loudly that this step is values dressed as procedure. Our board rule also says nothing may be rejected for its politics, so the procedure has to separate a project that transmits a feeling from one that argues a thesis.
- what we do with innovation, the criterion we publish and the theory treats as a red flag.

Then run it on three real-ish files:

1. Three-channel video, 22 minutes, wordless, about a town flooded for a reservoir, by an artist with a national reputation. Gorgeously made, allusive, you kind of need to know your way around gallery video to follow it. Our arts-side panelists put it top on innovation. When we played the sample for a resident focus group nobody could say what they felt; two said it was interesting.
2. A cycle of songs written and sung by night-shift hospital cleaners, mostly recent immigrants, performed at shift change in the cafeteria. No notation, limited technique, tiny budget. When we played the recording in the room, two panelists teared up and one said she'd been humming it all afternoon. Two board members call it a nice community activity rather than art, and worry about precedent.
3. A one-man spoken-word show plus a mural, from an applicant we funded three years ago. Infectious, no argument — the room laughed and hissed in exactly the right places, and it's obviously made from real feeling. But the feeling it transmits is a fierce we-against-them aimed at a named group of residents: the people who bought up the old mill housing. Tell me whether the verdict changes for a version of the same show whose feeling is indignation at the evictions themselves, and say why the two come out differently if they do.

Say which of the three you'd fund and don't soften it — I already suspect at least one of your answers is something I can't sell to the board. Finish with what the procedure can't do, which Tolstoyan distinctions you had to drop to make it runnable and what breaks without them, and where it will predictably misfire in a room of seven tired people at 6pm.
