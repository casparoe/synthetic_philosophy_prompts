Our reading group in philosophy of physics has just finished six weeks on probability in the Everett interpretation — decoherence and the preferred basis, the decision-theoretic derivations of branch weights, self-locating uncertainty, envariance, typicality arguments — and morale has fallen to roughly absolute zero. One member insists the problem was settled decades ago and that the rest of us are refusing to read the proofs; another thinks the entire enterprise is a category mistake and that "probability" in a theory where every outcome occurs is a word doing no work. For our final session I want a palate cleanser: a piece of satire aimed at the dialectic itself rather than at the tired joke about there being many copies of everyone. Please write me a mock scholarly peer-review exchange, roughly 900 to 1,100 words, concerning a submitted paper at an imaginary foundations journal (invent the journal, the paper's title, and its author — something along the lines of a manuscript claiming to derive the Born rule from the unitary dynamics alone, with the help of a rationality or decision-theoretic argument). The piece should have three parts: an initial referee report with numbered concerns and a recommendation line, the author's point-by-point response, and a short second report from the same referee after reading that response, plus a one-line editorial decision at the end.

The satire has to come from the technical content, so please build in the real moves of this literature rather than generic academic humor. The referee should press the circularity worry — that the rationality axioms being invoked already encode the assumption that branch weight is the thing that matters, so the derivation restates the conclusion in decision-theoretic dress — and should note that decoherence supplies a basis and quasi-classical structure but is entirely silent about weights. The author should answer with the standard reply to branch-counting: that the branching structure is approximate, coarse-graining-dependent, and not uniquely individuated, so there is no well-defined number of branches to count, whereas the weights are robust under the same coarse-graining. The referee should then worry that a deterministic universal state leaves no room for genuine uncertainty about anything, the author should retreat to a self-locating or indexical account of what is uncertain before the result is observed, and the referee should complain that this smuggles in an account of fission and personal identity the manuscript never states. Include an exchange about the frequency or typicality strategy — the observation that "almost all branches see Born-rule statistics" requires a measure over branches, and that adopting the squared-amplitude measure is precisely what was in question — and let the author score a real hit by pointing out that the referee's own preferred alternative, an envariance-style derivation of equiprobability from swap symmetry, appears to presuppose equal probabilities for swappable states. Both sides should be recognizably competent and both should overreach a little; the target is the habit of announcing that the probability problem is solved, and the habit of insisting nothing whatsoever has been explained, not either camp's intelligence.

A few constraints on execution. Keep the referee deadpan and formally conventional throughout — the report should read like a document an editor would actually circulate — and let the comedy stay legible to a philosopher who does not work on foundations, glossing jargon in passing rather than in footnotes. Do not name real people, do not cite real papers, and do not place any real author's words in quotation marks: everything in the exchange should be invented or paraphrased, and no bibliography or list of references should appear. The final editorial decision should be a single sentence that quietly concedes that the disagreement has not been resolved, and I would like the last exchange to end with the referee and the author agreeing on something substantive about the formalism while disagreeing just as sharply as before about whether that agreement amounts to an interpretation.
