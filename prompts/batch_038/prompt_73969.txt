I recently read a long essay in a general-audience science magazine about prediction, machine learning, and the old philosophical puzzle about induction. Its central section concerns Solomonoff induction, and I would like an accuracy audit before I build anything of my own on top of it. The passage reads as follows:

> Ray Solomonoff's discovery in the 1960s was to turn the vague slogan "prefer simpler explanations" into arithmetic. Take the data you have and ask for the shortest computer program that would print it out. The length of that program is the data's Kolmogorov complexity: a completely objective measure of how simple the data is, not a matter of taste, convention, or which descriptions you happened to think of first. Solomonoff then made this into a theory of prediction. Assign each possible hypothesis a prior probability equal to two raised to the negative length of the shortest program that generates it, and revise by ordinary Bayes' rule as evidence arrives. What you get is a single uniquely correct method of induction — the one that every other method is, wittingly or not, approximating. It has been proven optimal: if any other computable prediction scheme succeeds on a given sequence, Solomonoff's succeeds at least as well. The choice of programming language drops out entirely, because any two universal machines can simulate one another, so the shortest program for the same data differs by at most a fixed amount, and that fixed amount washes out as evidence accumulates. This is what Occam's razor should have meant all along, and it means Hume's problem of induction is not a philosophical puzzle but a settled theorem. The method cannot actually be executed, since finding the shortest program is uncomputable; but that is an engineering limitation rather than a conceptual one. Given enough computing time you can approximate the universal prior as closely as you like, and this is essentially what compression-based methods and model-selection criteria such as minimum description length and the Bayesian information criterion are doing when they penalize complicated models. The machinery inside today's machine learning systems is Solomonoff induction wearing practical clothes.
