I'm a fourth-year philosophy PhD student and I've been reading the supertask literture for two weeks straight for my quals and I can no longer feel my face. As a palate cleanser I want to commission a piece of satire from you, and I want it to actually be funny to someone who knows this stuff — not "infinity, wow" jokes.

Form: the public bug tracker for Thomson's lamp. Release notes for firmware ω.0, a short known-issues table, and three or four customer-filed tickets with maintainer replies. 650–800 words, in the flat, mildly apologetic register of real release notes, statuses and all (WONTFIX, BY DESIGN, DUPLICATE, NEEDS INFO).

The target isn't a person. It's the habit in thought-experiment philosophy of stipulating a machine and then acting as though the stipulation settles what happens at the end of it — plus, secondarily, support-speak that can reclassify any catastrophe as expected behavior.

Beats I want hit, all of them real features of the literature:
- nothing in the description determines the lamp's state once the ω-sequence is done, and there's no last toggle to point at (this is the Benacerraf point, but don't lecture about it);
- the reversed supertask: a unit found already in some state at noon, with no record of the toggles that put it there;
- whether an ω-length sequence of tasks can be completed at all — I want this as a ticket *status*, not an aside;
- at least one customer demanding a straight yes/no on whether the lamp is on or off at noon, whose reply is technically flawless and completely useless;
- one implementability complaint (contact bounce, energy, how fast the switch has to move) that the maintainer waves off on the grounds that this is a *thought* experiment, where the waving-off itself reads as the dodge it is;
- a DUPLICATE ruling against a very old ticket originally filed in Elea.

Deadpan throughout: no winking, no "and this is why metaphysics is silly." Invented maintainers and invented customers only — no living philosopher gets named as a character or quoted. And it has to work for my partner, who's a nurse and has never heard of Zeno, so if it needs a footnote explaining what a supertask is, it's failed.
