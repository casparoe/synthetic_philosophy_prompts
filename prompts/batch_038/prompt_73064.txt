I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and next month I have forty-five minutes with a philosopher whose book defends the situationist critique of character: the argument, roughly, that the evidence about how sharply small features of a setting shift behavior shows most people do not possess the cross-situationally consistent and temporally stable traits that Aristotelian virtue ethics assumes, and that the honest picture is one of narrow local dispositions plus a great deal of situational luck. What makes her worth the interview is that she is a situationist who has absorbed the replication crisis. She concedes that a number of the studies the critique was originally built on have not held up — the mood-induction-and-helping work, the bystander paradigm, the self-control-depletion literature that fared badly under large registered replications — and argues the conclusion survives anyway on narrower grounds. My editor wants a technically serious conversation, not a profile, and I want questions that force her to defend that survival claim rather than restate it.

Please draft nine interview questions, in the order I would ask them. For each, give me the question exactly as I would say it aloud; a one-line note in brackets saying what it is testing; and a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. The follow-up should be the specific second question her standard move invites, not a generic request for elaboration.

At least one question on each of the following, and you may fold several together:

1. What the argument now rests on once the failed studies are removed, and whether what remains supports a claim about ordinary people rather than about undergraduates in artificial eliciting conditions.
2. The aggregation reply to the low trait–behavior correlations: the reanalysis arguing that a correlation in the range situationists treated as negligible still predicts a great deal once behavior is summed over many occasions. Does she accept it, and if so what is left of the claim that traits are explanatorily idle?
3. The awkward fact that the psychologist who did most to establish cross-situational variability ended up with a model in which apparently inconsistent behavior is the signature of a stable system producing distinctive if–then patterns. Ask whether that collapses the dispute into a question of description, and what she says distinguishes a local trait plus its triggering situation from a character trait.
4. The revival of trait psychology around broad domains that are not moral traits, and the measures with demonstrated predictive validity for morally loaded outcomes. Was her target ever the construct that actually survived?
5. Whether her claim is that global traits do not exist or that the available evidence cannot establish that they do — and why the difference matters for blame.
6. The self-undermining charge: her positive program of designing situations and building social scaffolds for virtue appears to presuppose dispositions stable enough to be acquired and relied on by others.
7. Whether the loss falls on the agent, who on her view no longer deserves credit or blame, or on the assessor, who can no longer read character from behavior. Make her choose, and then ask what happens to praise, friendship, and promise-keeping in the version she picks.

The ninth question must be the convergence-plus-falsifiability question: after all her concessions, is her position distinguishable from the interactionist consensus that both persons and situations matter, and what finding would make her abandon the critique? Phrase it so that an answer about needing better measures or larger samples does not count as an answer.

Ground rules: nothing about her influences, her career, or her next book. Every spoken question under about fifty-five words and followable by a non-specialist, even where what is being tested is technical. Do not let me assert as established any empirical claim she could reasonably dispute in print — I have to stand behind every factual premise embedded in a question. No references or reading list; I will fact-check separately. If you think I have characterized her position or the state of the literature slightly wrong anywhere above, flag it in a short note at the end rather than quietly writing around the error.
