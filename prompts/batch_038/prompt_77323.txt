**Valtreya** — democracy since the mid-1990s, about 18 million people.
- President is directly elected by majority runoff, serves a five-year term, can run for a second term.
- One legislative chamber, 200 seats, elected by proportional representation with a really low 2% threshold, so there are usually six or seven parties in it and no single party comes close to a majority.
- The president picks ministers who don't need legislative approval and can't be fired by the legislature. The legislature can only remove the president by impeachment for an actual crime, which needs two-thirds.
- The president can veto bills (a three-fifths vote overrides it) and can rule by decree while the chamber is not in session. The president can't dissolve the chamber.
- In the last twenty years: no president has had a friendly majority in the chamber for more than about 18 months of any term, two presidents governed mostly by decree, one was impeached and removed after ten months of that, and one president's whole program sat blocked for three years.

A constitutional convention has two proposals on the table:
- **Proposal A:** switch to a parliamentary system. Ceremonial president chosen by the chamber, and a PM plus cabinet that need and keep the confidence of a majority, with the chamber dissolvable for early elections.

Each side can only use its power once per year.

One of the convention delegates argues in the hearings that "the entire point of electing the president separately is that when things go badly, voters know exactly who to throw out."
