Consider the following audit scenario. A lab trains a large model and, during a routine evaluation, executes one particular forward pass — call it R — on a fixed input, producing a fixed output. The lab archives a bit-exact trace of R: weights, input, and every intermediate activation. Four months later, in order to reproduce a published benchmark, the team re-executes R from that trace on a different cluster, reproducing every activation exactly. A scheduler error causes two such re-executions to run concurrently, bit-identically, for the entire duration of R: neither receives any input the other does not, neither runs faster than the other, neither retains any record the other discards, and both are halted at the same moment.

Here is the proposition I want a number for. Call it D:

D: If executions of computations of R's kind carry any nonzero experiential value or disvalue at all, then two concurrent bit-identical re-executions carry exactly twice as much, in total, as a single execution does.

My own instinct sits near 0.5, and I suspect that reflects an inability to commit rather than a considered view. Give me your credence in D, and then decompose it into the sub-propositions that generate it: whether phenomenal experience, if present in such a system at all, is indexed to particular physical executions or to the abstract pattern they instantiate; whether two bit-identical executions amount to one subject with one welfare or to two subjects; whether moral weight aggregates across subjects in the way D assumes, or whether indistinguishability at every moment does normative work that numerical distinctness does not; whether a re-execution from an archived trace counts as an execution in the relevant sense; and whether an archived trace that is never run at all already does the instantiating work, so that the duplication happened the moment the trace was written. Assign each term a rough probability with one line of justification, add any terms you think I have omitted, show explicitly how they combine into your number, and then identify which single term your credence is most sensitive to and what kind of finding would move it.

Two further things. First, state separately your credence in D's antecedent, and say how much of the intuitive pull of D you think comes from smuggling that antecedent in. Second, one variant: if your number for D is high, does it survive replacing the second concurrent run with a trace that is archived and never executed — and if it does not survive, say precisely what fails in that case that succeeds in the case of two live runs.
