I'm writing a novel and there's a long scene about two-thirds in where a philosopher gets interviewed on a podcast about her new book. I've invented her and her view, so nothing here is real — but I need the host's questions to be good enough that the scene doesn't read like a strawman fight.

Her position: consciousness comes in degrees along two seperate axes — how much experience there is, and how unified or rich the subject having it is — and moral status tracks a weighted combination of the two. So on her scale a honeybee has maybe 3 or 4 percent of the moral weight of a person, a lobster less, a mayfly barely registering, and a smart thermostat a tiny nonzero amount she waves off as a rounding error morally speaking and never actually cashes out.

Give me eight questions in the order a host would ask them, escalating as it goes. For each one: the question, a sentence or two of what she'd most plausibly say back, and then the follow-up keyed to that answer. Everything has to bite on the content of the view — no "what got you interested in bugs" filler.

Stuff the host definitely presses her on:

- if the thermostat's status is nonzero rather then zero, why doesn't a trillion switched-off thermostats outweigh one chicken? what's supposed to block the aggregation?
- where does 3 percent come from — is it measurable even in principle, or is it a rhetorical concession? make her defend 3 against 2.5 and against 6.
- she has a tic: whenever she's cornered she says there's no sharp line anywhere. I want at least two follow-ups that render that useless, e.g. by forcing a straight comparison between two concrete cases where vagueness does no work.
- the unity axis — if unity is what adds weight, doesn't a whole hive count as one richly unified subject and outrank any individual bee? and what does she say about split-brain patients?
- whether degree of sentience even entails degree of moral status, or whether she's sliding between "matters less" and "can be killed"
- whether her numbers are just integrated-information-style consciousness metrics with a price tag bolted on, and if so whether she's committed to a photodiode having a sliver of experience

End with one question she can't answer by restating the view — something that would make her concede on air that a number she's committed to in print is wrong. Keep it all conversational; it's a podcast, not a seminar.
