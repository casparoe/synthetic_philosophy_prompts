I am a novelist, and the middle section of my book in progress consists of the correspondence between two consciousness researchers who have been arguing with each other for a decade without ever pinning down what they actually disagree about. I want to write that section honestly, which means I first need the document they never managed to produce: the joint statement they would have signed had they been disciplined enough to attempt a real adversarial collaboration. Please write that document. I want the document itself, not a dramatized exchange and not a sketch of their personalities — I will handle the fiction.

The two positions are as follows. Researcher A holds a global-workspace view: she has no interest in defending a little theater with a single seat and an inner audience, and she says so constantly, but she maintains that ignition of a workspace at a determinate time is what makes it true that a given content was consciously available then, and that this settles temporal questions — when the change was seen, in what order two stimuli entered experience — that her rival treats as unanswerable. Researcher B is a committed multiple-drafts theorist: he holds that there is no fact of the matter about when a discrimination became conscious except insofar as some probe fixed a content in a form available to later report and action, so that A's question about the moment of fixation is a question with nothing behind it, and that the choice between a Stalinesque and an Orwellian account of cases like the colour-phi illusion cannot be made on the evidence because the two are empirically indistinguishable by construction.

Deliver these components:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. This should include what both accept about the phenomena to be explained — apparent motion, the colour-phi misalignment effect, cutaneous-rabbit-type tactile spreading, the general fact that report and task set shape what we can measure — as well as what both reject about the theatre, and what both concede about the causal work that reporting does.

2. A table of the residual disagreements. Each row should state the disagreement twice, once in wording A would accept as a fair statement of her own view and once in wording B would accept for his. Then mark, for each row, whether the disagreement is substantive or merely verbal or methodological, and say which. If a large part of the dispute turns out to be a disagreement about what would count as settling a temporal fact rather than about how the brain works, say so plainly and isolate exactly that part.

3. The cruxes: the few sub-questions such that settling them would genuinely move at least one of the two. Rank them by how much of the rest of the dispute depends on them, and state for each what A and what B would accept as evidence or argument bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Also state, for each, what result would count for that side as falsifying rather than as noise to be absorbed.

4. Treat this paradigm as though it existed and had been run: a subject fixates a dot that changes colour from red to green at a time unknown to them. On half the trials they press a key the instant they take the change to have occurred; on the other half they must withhold and press only when a tone sounds some seconds later. On a subset of both kinds, a pattern mask follows the change at varying delays. Report each side's prediction about how mask delay trades off against response condition in determining (a) the colour the subject says they saw and (b) the time they assign to the change — and then tell me whether the paradigm is actually diagnostic, or whether both theories can absorb every possible outcome. If it is not diagnostic, say what modification would make it so.

Do not give me citations or a reading list; I will supply those myself. Do end with a short note on where you think this draft is most likely to fail: which agreed claim a real partisan would refuse to sign, which crux may be an artefact of the way I have characterised the two positions, and where you have softened a genuine disagreement in order to make the document signable. Most importantly, tell me what would change your mind about the structure of the dispute — which argument, or which reading of the temporal cases, would lead you to a materially different document. I want the considerations that would move you, not a hedge about how hard consciousness is.
