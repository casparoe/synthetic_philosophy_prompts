I've been going around in circles on Project Cybersyn and I want an actual case analysis, not a summary of either side of the debate.

The facts I'm working from, so we're on the same page: Allende's Chile, 1971–73. CORFO brings in the British cybernetician Stafford Beer to build a decision-support network for the nationalised industrial sector. Telex machines installed in state factories send daily figures — raw material stock, output, absenteeism — to a mainframe in Santiago; software filters them against expected ranges and escalates anything badly out of tolerance; an unfinished operations room lets managers see the picture and push directives back down the telex network. Something like twenty factories ever actually got modelled and connected. In October 1972, when around 50,000 truckers struck against the government, the network was used to route the couple of hundred trucks that stayed loyal so food kept moving into Santiago — the one unambiguous success the system ever had. After the September 1973 coup the operations room was destroyed and the project ended.

Now run that through the socialist calculation debate, in both directions. Mises: no market prices for capital goods, so no rational calculation, full stop. Lange and Lerner: a board can simulate the market by announcing parametric prices and revising them by trial and error, with managers told to minimise cost at those prices and produce up to where marginal cost equals price. Hayek (the 1945 knowledge-in-society essay, plus his earlier reply to Lange): the relevant knowledge is dispersed, local and largely tacit, and the competitive process is what discovers it — a board solving Lange's equations has assumed away the whole difficulty.

So: map the actors onto the debate. Who is the planning board here, who are the managers, and where are the prices? My sense is "nowhere," and I think that matters more than people admit — Cybersyn tracked physical quantities and abnormality thresholds, which sounds much more like the thing Mises said couldn't be done than the thing Lange said could. Then tell me what each of the three positions would have predicted about this system in 1971, before knowing how it went, and which of those predictions the actual record supports versus which the record simply can't test. Be blunt about the underdetermination: it was never finished, covered a tiny fraction of the sector, and was destroyed by a coup, so it can't distinguish "failed because impossible" from "failed because it was shot."

The part I actually want you to argue with me about. I keep thinking the debate is missing a fourth horn, and Cybersyn is the case that exposes it. Call it the reporting problem: grant that the data can be gathered and that a board can compute with it — the numbers still only arrive if the people sending them have no reason to shade them. Soviet managers hoarded inputs and understated capacity; Cybersyn asked the factory floor to hand over daily indices of its own bottlenecks while the political stakes of the sector rising fast, and while the whole point of the design was to devolve decisions back to the people who had the local knowledge. Is that a genuinely distinct problem, or is it just Hayek's knowledge problem with a different haircut — incentives being part of the local knowledge that can't be centralised anyway?

And the truck strike. The one time the network demonstrably worked, it was doing something prices normally do: allocating scarce transport capacity under emergency conditions. Except the market had been deliberately switched off as a political weapon. Does that episode count as a point for Hayek — the state having to do by committee and teletype, in a month, what the price system does continuously — or against him, since the system did manage to do it? Say which reading survives scrutiny.

Finish with a one-line bottom line. Last line should start with "Verdict:" and contain exactly one of: MISES, HAYEK, LANGE, REPORTING, NONE — where NONE means Cybersyn isn't really an instance of the debate at all and the whole mapping is a category error.
