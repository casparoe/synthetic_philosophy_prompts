I lead the health economics function at a national technology-appraisal body. My working vocabulary is QALYs, incremental cost-effectiveness ratios, opportunity-cost-based thresholds, a fixed budget, and the distributional weighting schemes that add weight to treatments for patients with large expected lifetime health shortfalls. My exposure to the philosophical literature is limited: I know only in outline that prioritarianism holds that benefits matter more the worse off the recipient is in absolute terms, and that Parfit's leveling-down objection is meant to show why telic egalitarianism fails where prioritarianism does not.

Restate the priority view and the leveling-down objection into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can: what weighting scheme or social welfare function each corresponds to, what a comparative claim about relative position would have to look like in an appraisal, and what an allocation problem under a fixed budget can and cannot express. Then give me the residue report — which claims carry over cleanly, which you had to bend to fit (and say precisely how you bent them), and which have no counterpart at all in cost-effectiveness practice. Judge whether that residue is a limitation of my framework or evidence that the philosophical dispute is less substantive than it sounds, and test rather than accept one candidate I can think of: that a zero-sum budget makes it impossible to keep relative and absolute considerations apart, so that the leveling-down scenario may not be statable in my terms at all. Do not smuggle in the assumptions of welfare economics and present them as translation, and do not give me sources or a reading list — I want the reasoning.
