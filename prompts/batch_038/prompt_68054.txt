me and my roommate have been going in circles on uploading for like three weeks, every time ending up shouting past each other at 2am. he's a hardline gradual-replacement functionalist: swap a neuron for a chip that does exactly what it did, one at a time, and there's nothing left to go wrong — anyone who thinks otherwise is just smuggling in a soul and calling it intuition. i'm on the other side. i don't think he's earned the phrase "exactly what it did" until he says at what level of description, and i think the whole argument quietly assumes that two systems which can't be told apart at his favorite level of description can't be told apart from the inside, which is pretty much the thing in dispute. he's doing a rotation in a lab that models retinal prosthetics so he thinks he has skin in the game; i think that's exactly the confusion.

so instead of another 2am fight, write the document we should have actually produced: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed gradual-replacement functionalist and a committed continuity skeptic. the skeptic thinks survival requires the continued existence of the same living brain, or at least that functional isomorphism has never been shown to be sufficient for experience — but he's not a mysterian and he doesn't want to be handled with kid gloves, he wants to be argued with and is willing to be moved. no name-dropping philosophers, neither of us needs the referee report version.

what i want in it: (1) the claims both sides can sign, including claims about the thought experiments themselves and not just safe generalities; (2) the residual disagreements stated precisely, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording, with a table if you like; (3) which of those disagreements you think are merely verbal, and why — i suspect at least one of them is about what "the same process" means and i want it either cashed out or dissolved; (4) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling it would actually move one of us; and (5) for each crux, the case or argument both of us would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance so nobody can squirm later. one rule: the functionalist is not allowed to use "functionally identical" as an unexplained primitive — the statement has to fix the level at which the identity of function is being claimed, and if the two sides disagree about that level, that disagreement goes in the table rather than getting hidden inside a shared premise.

and build the whole thing around a case neither of us has a canned line for, becuase that's where we actually got stuck. call it the delayed handoff. the replacement proceeds one module at a time over a year, but each artificial module runs in lockstep with the biological module it's replacing — both connected, both receiving the same inputs, both driving the same outputs — for six months before the biological one is disconnected. so for six months every function has two realizers at once, nothing has been lost yet, and the question of which one is me is live while there's still an obvious candidate. then the switch is flipped. and here's the twist we added at 2am to make it worse: during lockstep the artificial module runs on a clock a thousand times faster than the biological one, so it completes a thousand internal cycles for every one of mine and then idles waiting. he says nothing relevant changed. i say he now owes an account of why processing speed isn't relevant, since on his own description a thousand internal states happen per one of mine and he's already conceded that internal states are what matter.

keep it to something we could both read without falling asleep — call it a page and a half — and don't let either of us win by definition.
