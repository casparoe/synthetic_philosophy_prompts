Three weeks into my first job at a mixed-animal practice and I've got whiplash, so I want someone to lay out what all of this actually assumes about animal minds, because nobody at work seems to think there's anything here to explain.

Monday: general anesthetic for a 9 year old labrador's dental. Premeds, IV catheter, gas, then a fentanyl drip, an NSAID going home, and a pain chart on the kennel door where a tech scores him 0 to 4 every four hours — posture, appetite, whether he looks up at you, whether he guards the side of his mouth. Nobody in that building doubts for a second that he was in pain and that it counted.

Wednesday: field call to a sheep place, rubber-ring castration and tail docking on a bunch of week-old lambs. No local, no pain relief, totally legal. Our state's cruelty statute carves out anything that counts as a normal agricultural practice, so the identical thing done to a dog would be a felony with my name on it.

And the university across town, where my roommate's in a pharmacology program, measures whether an analgesic works in mice by timing how long it takes them to flick their tails off a heat source. Which is a spinal withdrawal reflex — she told me herself it still happens in animals whose cord has been cut.

So what's the theory underneath this? Is it Descartes, with some animals as machines and some not? I don't think anyone in my clinic believes that about the labrador, and I don't think the sheep farmer thinks his lambs are wind-up toys either, which is what makes the pair of days so weird. But the pain charts bother me for a separate reason: every single item on them is inference from behaviour, and inference from behaviour is exactly the kind of evidence the beast-machine line says proves nothing. What's licensing it — best explanation, analogy, something about shared machinery — and why does the license run out at the farm fence? If the argument is same nervous system therefore same sort of experience, it covers the lambs too, and it covers a pile of animals we don't anesthetize at all.

Tell me what this practice strictly requires versus what it just happens to fit comfortably with, whether the two halves of my week are genuinely incoherent or whether one view generates both, and what would actually have to change — the chart, the statute, the hot plate, the pre-op conversation — if we swapped in a plain capacity-based account of who counts.
