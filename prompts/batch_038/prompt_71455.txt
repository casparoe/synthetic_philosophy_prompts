ok so this is a weird thing to ask about but here's the situation. my brother is 22 and he was in a car crash fourteen months ago. the diagnosis is unresponsive wakefulness syndrome — eyes open, sleep/wake cycles, reflexes, no command following anyone has been able to get consistently. three weeks ago they put him through one of those active brain scan tests, the kind where they ask the person to imagine doing something and watch for the matching brain regions, and he did it. twice out of two sessions. the neurologist who ran it told my mom it "suggests" something and wouldn't go past that word, and my mom has not stopped saying he's in there since.

on saturday i'm giving a five or six minute speech at the hospital's annual evening for that program — dinner, fundraiser, they asked me because i'm the sibling and apparently i'm articulate. the room is about half doctors, rehab researchers and program staff, and half donors, board people, and other families with someone in the same kind of bed. my parents will be there. my mom has already told two people i'm going to say he's in there.

the problem is i've been reading about this on my own and i know enough to know i can't just say it. i know there's a difference between being conscious in the sense of there being something it's like to be you, and being conscious in the sense of taking information in and being able to use it — report it, decide with it, act on it — and that people argue about whether those two can come apart. a kid at school told me that if my brother follows a command in a scanner he obviously has the second kind but maybe not the first, that it could be all lights on nobody home, and honestly i couldnt tell whether that's even a coherent thing to say or something he made up on the spot. i want the speech to not accidentally commit to either side of that, and definitely not to turn into a promise to 300 people that he's going to wake up.

also: the researcher who ran the scan pulled me aside afterward and said basically two sessions, one patient, please don't let your mother book a press conference. i want to honor that without being cold about it in a room full of parents.

so, write the speech. constraints i care about:

- it has to be something a 17 year old can say out loud without sounding like a seminar. no jargon, nobody should hear a phrase like "phenomenal consciousness" in it. but the neurologists should be able to tell i understood the distinction and am deliberately not overselling — there should be a line in there they recognize as the distinction, said in ordinary words.
- it has to give the families something real, not a wall of hedging. it's a speech, not a list of caveats, and it should be warm.
- at one point i want to address my brother directly, second person, and i want that moment to land whether or not anybody in the room knows if he can hear it. figure out how that's actually possible and do it — that's the part i most want to see work.
- don't use the v-word at all, and don't make a show of avoiding it either.

then, separately after the speech, tell me which claims i'm making in it are actually settled among people who study this and which are genuinely contested — like whether that kind of scan result is really evidence of awareness at all, whether the two flavors of consciousness can come apart in principle or in a real patient, and whether the global-workspace style story about information getting broadcast to the whole brain tells you anything at all about what it's like to be my brother. please don't quietly present a contested view as the consensus just to make the speech feel safer, and don't give me a reading list or citations, i can't use them anyway — i just want to know where the ground is soft under what i'm saying. and if there's a line in the speech you wrote that you can't actually defend, say which one and why.
