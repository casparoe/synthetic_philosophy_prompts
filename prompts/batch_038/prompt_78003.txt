I direct the Investment Review Office of a regional development bank with a public mandate and a portfolio of roughly $2 billion in long-horizon infrastructure lending. My office makes about fifty tranche-release decisions a year. A borrower has drawn one tranche of a staged facility, and we must decide whether to release the next, release it conditionally, restructure the facility, or stop disbursement and begin workout. Individual decisions run from roughly $2 million to $60 million. Each file is prepared by two analysts with finance training and one engineer, none of whom has studied economics or philosophy at any length, and each gets ninety minutes of committee time. The credit committee signs off. Our memoranda are auditable by a national audit office that reads them cold, years later, without any of the context we had, so a recommendation must be defensible to a stranger on the strength of the page alone.

Our board's standing instruction on this is one sentence long: past disbursements shall not influence the decision to release further funds. I have come to believe it is being applied badly in both directions. Analysts who want a project to survive reclassify old spending as committed future obligation; analysts who want to look tough declare that the money is gone and therefore irrelevant, in cases where it plainly changed what we now know. I want the instruction replaced by a procedure my staff can actually run. Please write one, and do not cite literature or recommend readings — it has to work at a desk without a library.

**1. Preamble (no more than four hundred words).** Settle the question the instruction assumes. Is honoring a sunk cost ever rational? Distinguish by name the descriptions under which a past expenditure can legitimately bear on a prospective decision — information actually purchased by the spending, exit and transition costs, retraining and requalification, guarantees and covenants that survive abandonment, the effect of the bank's abandonment record on how counterparties price future dealings with it, residual and salvage value — from the descriptions under which it cannot. For each legitimate channel, say whether it bears on the decision because it altered something forward-looking or because it is doing something else. If you think there are irreducibly retrospective reasons — a promise given, a debt to a community that took the bank's word, loyalty to co-investors who are no longer at the table — say so, and mark them as belonging to the committee rather than to the analyst. Close by stating which of these distinctions must survive translation into a checklist and which can be dropped without the checklist becoming dishonest.

**2. The procedure.** An ordered set of questions, each with: the evidence that counts as a sufficient answer; who has to supply it; what happens when it cannot be supplied; and the terminal states — stop disbursement, release, release conditionally pending a specified piece of information, or refer to the committee as a question of values rather than fact. Include an explicit default for the missing-number case: when a quantity the procedure needs does not exist, say what the analyst should do instead of guessing, and say whether the cheapest resolution is usually a small staged tranche bought for information rather than a decision about the project.

**3. A smuggling audit.** The standard disguises sunk costs wear in a tranche paper, each paired with a discriminating question that separates a genuine prospective effect from a disguised retrospective one. Cover at minimum: reclassification of past spending as committed obligation; "the learning is already purchased"; "the staff are already trained"; the write-off that would look bad; inflated transition costs for the alternative; and the argument that stopping now would prove the earlier tranches were mistakes.

**4. Three worked applications.** Run the procedure end to end on each, showing which question does the work and where the file terminates. State your verdict plainly even when it runs against the leaning I record, and flag it as unwelcome when it does.

- **Geothermal.** $41M drawn of a $55M facility. Four exploratory wells were planned; two are drilled and both came in below commercial flow. The remaining $14M drills two more. If the resource is confirmed, it supports a separate plant financing we would syndicate; if not, residual value of site and data is about $3M. The sponsor argues the two dry wells eliminated the north-east structure and that the remaining targets are the ones the new data favors, and notes the rig is mobilized with demobilization and remobilization costs of $4M. My leaning is to stop: the relationship manager who brought the deal has left, the committee chair championed it, and I would rather not fund a third dry well. I need the procedure to tell me whether "the dry wells narrowed the search" is the information channel or a rationalization, and what specific evidence would settle it.
- **Bespoke hospital information system.** $19M drawn of a $41M facility, $22M remaining. The alternative is to abandon and license a commercial system: $17M over two years, plus eighteen months of dual running, plus contractual exit costs, plus retraining roughly 2,400 staff. The health authority says abandonment would trigger departures among the forty clinicians who co-designed the bespoke system, and that a second abandoned IT program in five years would damage its ability to attract vendors at all. My leaning is to continue — I like the team and I distrust vendor estimates. The procedure should say whether "the staff are already trained" is a sunk cost or a prospective asset, whether the abandonment record is a real prospective cost or a story being told by people with a stake in it, and how to test the vendor figure.
- **Cold-chain facility, fishing cooperative.** In 2019 the bank's then-managing director told the cooperative in writing, in the presence of the regional minister, that the bank would see the project through to completion. Prospective return is now clearly negative: completion costs $4M more than the incremental revenue over the remaining life, and no restructuring we can construct makes the cooperative whole. Counsel confirms the statement was never a legal commitment and is unenforceable. Abandonment will be reported locally. My leaning is to complete it, but I want to know whether the procedure licenses that or merely fails to forbid it — and precisely where it stops being a procedure and becomes a decision the committee must own.

**5. Honest limits.** A section listing every step where the procedure is a judgment call dressed as arithmetic, including any threshold you introduce; give the threshold and label it a threshold rather than a finding. Then state what the procedure cannot do: the failure modes it will produce, the kinds of file on which it will be least reliable, and what a reviewer should distrust about its output.

**6. Reviewer's page.** A single page, no more than 350 words, that an analyst can keep beside the file — the questions in order and the terminal states, nothing else.
