For a graduate seminar on speech-act theory I want a case constructed to a fairly exacting specification, together with a demonstration that it satisfies every clause. Its purpose is to test whether Austin's division of infelicities into misfires and abuses marks a real difference between two kinds of defect, or whether it is an artifact of the promise-centered examples that motivate it. Write for readers who have worked through the lectures in which Austin lays out the taxonomy but who should not be assumed to remember the fine print; argue from the conceptual structure of the cases rather than from authority.

Target verdict. Build a case in which a token utterance that is expressly and recognizably performative — said in order to do something, within an institution where saying it is supposed to constitute the doing — fails to do the thing, and where the sole source of the failure is the participants' insincerity. Austin files insincerity under the Γ heading as an abuse, on the strength of the promise made with no intent to keep it: the promise is nonetheless made, hollow though it is, and the promisor is on the hook. I want the opposite direction — insincerity that defeats the performance outright rather than tarnishing it.

Constraints on the construction:

- One scenario, at most a paragraph, set in an ordinary contemporary institution. No invented states, no fictional or exotic practices, no science fiction, no urgency or emergency as a prop.
- Every participant knows precisely which procedure is being invoked, knows that they lack the attitudes it calls for, and knows that the others lack them too. No duress, no mistake, no ignorance, no incapacity, no undisclosed prior impediment — nothing that could be reclassified as a defect in the persons or circumstances, or in the manner of execution.
- The failure must be immediate and automatic under the practice's own constitutive rules. Nothing may afterwards repair, ratify, annul, or validate it. If your setting contains a tribunal or official who later pronounces on the matter, that pronouncement must be purely declaratory.
- You may stipulate the institutional rules of the setting. You may not stipulate the speech-act-theoretic verdict.

Deliverables:

1. The scenario.
2. The audit. Name the act purportedly performed and the procedure invoked, then run Austin's first four conditions one at a time — that an accepted conventional procedure exists with a conventional effect, that these persons and circumstances are appropriate for it, and that it is executed correctly and completely — showing each to be satisfied. Then state precisely where the failure sits in the scheme and why the scheme has no tidy slot for it.
3. The control case. Take insincerity of exactly the same degree and structure — mutual, openly avowed, disowned by nobody — and locate it in an ordinary promise or a commercial contract. Say which category it falls into there, and explain what it is about the two practices, rather than about the two mental states, that yields the different verdicts.
4. The honest note. Identify the single feature of your construction doing the work; state what the verdict becomes if that feature is removed, or replaced by a regime under which the act stands until set aside; and give the strongest reply available to a defender of the taxonomy — including the reply that a rule conditioning performance on the parties' attitudes is simply one further requirement about appropriate persons and circumstances, so that the case is a misfire after all and nothing strains.
5. Your own verdict: a genuine counterexample to the misfire/abuse distinction, or a misdescription of where the boundary lies. Say which, and defend it in a few sentences.

Please do not supply references or quotations. Roughly 700 to 900 words in total.
