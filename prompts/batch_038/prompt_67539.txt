Our philosophy-of-mind reading group spent last week on the Letter Spirit material — Hofstadter and McGraw turning the roman lowercase into a microworld where you can watch style and category come apart — and afterwards Sam, who letterforms for a living, and Nadia, who works on catgorization in cog sci, got into an argument I couldn't referee. Roughly:

(1) Sam: if 'a' had no essence you couldn't design one. A punchcutter cutting a new 'a' is meeting a constraint, not just picking a pleasing shape, and the fact that everyone in the room instantly agreed about which of his two candidate drawings wasn't an 'a' shows there's a fact of the matter.

(2) Nadia: agreement isn't definability — we agree about heaps and bald men too. And OCR systems sort 'a's perfectly well with no invariant anywhere inside them, they're fitted to distributions. The consensus is explained by shared exposure and shared perceptual machinery, not by everyone tracking a rule.

(3) Sam: fine, here's the invariant — a bowl closed at x-hieght with a stem joined on its right, the whole thing inside x-height. That's what survives from a Garamond to a geometric sans.

(4) Nadia: it doesn't survive. Plenty of stencil faces break the stroke so the bowl isn't closed at all, and handwriting leaves it open constantly, and nobody hesitates over those. Meanwhile your uppercase 'A' shares zero structure with either of them and is the same letter. And you can slide continuously from a double-story 'a' to an 'o' — tail unwinds, arch closes — with no frame where it stops being one and starts being the other. If there were an essence there'd be a boundary in that morph.

(5) Sam: so membership comes in degrees. Degrees of what? Resemblance to paradigms, which is itself a structure. Call it fuzzy, call it a gradient — you've conceded there's an answer to what makes it an 'a'.

(6) Nadia: resemblance-to-paradigms with graded membership is family resemblance, and that is precisely the view on which there's no essence. I haven't conceded anything, I've just told you what the category looks like.

Who's right at each of the six moves — not just overall? And I have a suspicion that some of this is the two of them sliding between 'letter', 'letterform' and 'essence' without noticing; tell me where that's happening if it is. Finally, what's the thing neither of them said that would actually move the argument forward?
