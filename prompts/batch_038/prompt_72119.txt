I design protocols for a fleet of services that must converge on configuration values without a central coordinator, and I have been reading David Lewis on convention as a way of thinking about how that kind of convergence becomes stable. My reading, which may well be crude, is that on Lewis's account a regularity in a population's behavior counts as a convention only if it is common knowledge in that population that the regularity obtains and that each member prefers to conform provided the others do. That suggests a direct engineering translation to me: get the participants into a state of common knowledge and the equilibrium will then sustain itself, with no monitoring or penalty mechanism behind it.

My proposed mechanism is nested acknowledgment. A node announces a change; every node that receives the announcement acknowledges it; every node that receives an acknowledgment acknowledges that in turn; and the process runs for k rounds over a channel that drops messages with some small fixed probability. I have two questions, and I would much rather be told where the framing breaks than be given a sympathetic answer. First, for a given round count k, what does the population actually end up knowing, and is there any k at which the resulting state satisfies Lewis's common-knowledge condition as opposed to merely approximating it? Second, because I cannot make delivery reliable, I am inclined to define an epsilon-common-knowledge predicate — mutual knowledge to depth k holding with probability at least one minus epsilon — and treat that as sufficient. Does Lewis's condition admit of satisfaction to a tolerance in this way, and would a convention resting on it still be self-perpetuating in his sense, or would it silently reintroduce the enforcement his analysis is meant to explain away?

Please answer in exactly five numbered points, each of one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. Within those points, keep separate what people who work on Lewis would regard as settled from what they genuinely dispute, and do not report a contested position to me as though it were the consensus.
