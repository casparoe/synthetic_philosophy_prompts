You are drafting the closing document of a year-long structured adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who have been arguing past one another about whether dreams are experiences. Both have signed the document in advance in principle; your job is to write the version that could actually survive their signatures.

**The two parties.**

*Party A* is a conceptualist in the line of Norman Malcolm's *Dreaming*: the word dream does not name an episode that unfolds at some time during the night; what we have is the waking person's disposition to recount, and the question whether she dreamt is settled by that disposition and by nothing else. On this view, asking what happened in the sleeper's mind at 3:10 a.m. is not a deep empirical question but a misuse of the grammar of experience, and no amount of neurophysiology could make it a good question.

*Party B* is a revisionist in the line of Dennett's response to Malcolm: Malcolm has not shown the rival pictures to be incoherent, only unfamiliar. Specifically, the hypothesis that a dream narrative is not laid down across a stretch of sleep at all but stamped onto the sleeper in the instant of waking — so that nothing answering to the narrative was ever lived through — is a coherent empirical possibility, and so is its denial. Whether a dream is an experience, and whether it has a duration in the night, are questions for evidence rather than for conceptual analysis.

**The evidence both have agreed in advance to admit.** Treat the following fictional protocol and its results as real, replicated across four labs, and accepted by both parties as accurately described. Do not question it, and do not invent further results beyond small clarifying stipulations that you flag as stipulations.

The lab calls it the probe-insertion study. Sleepers in REM are woken abruptly at pseudorandom times. In the experimental condition, during the roughly 250 milliseconds between the waking cue and the earliest moment at which a spoken report becomes possible, twelve images and two words are presented in rapid sequence — brief enough that waking controls, shown the same sequence at the same rate, can afterwards identify no more than one or two items and describe the display as a flash rather than as anything they watched unfold. Subjects in the experimental condition nonetheless produce a narrative which they say lasted several minutes, which has an arc, and which ends with the probed items appearing in the probed order; their identification of the items is far above the waking control rate. In 80 percent of such trials the probed items are located by the subject at the very end of the dream. Two further conditions are run. In the matched sham, sleepers are woken at identical times with no probe: narratives are reported as usual and identification is at chance. In the quiet-window condition, sleepers are woken from a stretch of REM showing none of the physiological markers that in this lab have reliably correlated with content reports, and they produce a full narrative which they rate as unusually vivid while being able to supply almost no specifics.

**The deliverable.** Write the joint statement. It should contain, in order:

1. A short preamble recording the protocol the parties adopted: what they agreed would count as settling a crux, and what each conceded to the other during the year that it would not have conceded at the outset.

2. A numbered list of claims both parties can now sign. At least three of these must be claims that a fair caricature of one side would have denied a year ago, and must be marked as such. The list must include claims fixing the relations among the notions of a dream, a dream report, a recollection of a dream, and its seeming to one on waking that one dreamt — and must say explicitly which of these the two theses are actually about. It must also include a claim stating what the probe-insertion study settles and a claim stating what it does not settle, both of which the two parties genuinely share rather than merely tolerate.

3. A table of the residual disagreements, with a row per dispute and the following columns: the dispute as Party A would phrase it, in vocabulary Party A is willing to be held to; the same dispute as Party B would phrase it; whether the divergence is substantive or verbal, with the reasoning; the crux to which it reduces; and each party's registered prediction. At least one row must be one that both parties agree was never anything but a verbal dispute, together with the disambiguation that dissolves it.

4. The cruxes themselves, each stated as a question on which at least one party's position would actually move. For each: the argument, case, or piece of evidence both accept as bearing on it; Party A's prediction and Party B's prediction, written down as commitments; and a note on whether the crux can in principle be settled by further experiment or is instead a question about what our concepts license. Include at minimum one crux on whether the word experience is being used univocally by the two camps, and one on whether a first-person report can be authoritative about the temporal properties of the episode it reports — in particular, about whether the probed items sat at the end of a several-minute episode or were the whole of it.

5. A section on falsification: what result or what argument each party would accept as counting decisively against their own view, phrased so that the party in question endorses it rather than merely acknowledges it; and whether there is any single finding that would move both. If you conclude that one party's thesis is untouched by the probe data in either direction, say so plainly and say what kind of consideration could nonetheless bear on it.

6. A closing paragraph, of a length both would sign, stating what the year's dispute was really about, how much of it was about words, and what the surviving substantive disagreement consists in.

Two constraints on the drafting. Every agreed claim must be specific enough that a reader could say which party would once have refused to sign it, and every disagreement must be phrased so that neither side is characterized in terms it would reject as a straw man; where a party is entitled to a self-serving gloss, give the gloss and then the neutral version. Second, produce no citations, no bibliography, no references to further literature and no attributions to real experiments: the document must stand on the two positions and the stipulated probe-insertion study alone.
