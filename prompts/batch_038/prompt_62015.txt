I'm an SRE at a mid-size logistics company and I'm stuck on something that sounds like a tooling complaint but doesn't feel like one.

Our incident review tool makes you name a single root-cause node for every Sev-1. Edges are typed "because," it computes the transitive closure for you, and it will not let you submit a report that either contains a loop or has more than one node with nothing underneath it. Mostly fine, we've lived with it for years.

Thursday we were down for three hours. There were two bugs and either one on its own would have taken us down: a cache TTL that got flipped to zero during a migration, and a retry config with no backoff. Genuinely independent, neither one needed the other, either one is sufficient. My teammate wants to just put the TTL in the box because its fix is already funded and the other one would reopen an argument with the platform team. I want to write one node that says something like "either the TTL was zero or the retries had no backoff," which the tool accepts without complaint, but compliance bounces those as we-dont-actually-know-what-happened and it delays the whole review.

Two extra wrinkles. If you follow the edges far enough, the retry config was set that way *because of* the postmortem from a similar outage two years ago, so the closure ends up with an outage explaining an outage, and the tool flags that too — and I don't think that's a glitch in the tool exactly, but I can't articulate why not. And my director keeps asking a different question than the one I'm answering: she wants to know why it went down Thursday and not Wednesday, and every answer I give her sounds like I'm dodging.

So: is the one-root-cause rule tracking something that's really out there in the incident, or is it a formatting preference dressed up as a fact about the world? And concretely, what do I type in the box on Friday, given I still want both bugs fixed?
