Compose a satirical document of 900 to 1,200 words in the voice, register, and visual formatting of an official instruction booklet issued by a national campaign-finance regulator.

Treat the following premise as fixed. The commission has released for public comment the draft instructions for a new filing, Form 5-X, "Statement of Organization for an Entity Whose Major Purpose Is Not the Nomination or Election of Federal Candidates." Form 5-X is filed pre-emptively, before the filer has spent anything. The illustrative filer used throughout the booklet is a 501(c)(4) social welfare organization incorporated last month whose stated non-political purpose is the annual publication of a refereed bulletin on the drainage properties of municipal sidewalk joints.

The piece must be built from the furniture of the real genre: a "Purpose of This Form" paragraph; a "Who Must File / Who Need Not File" section; a definitions block; line-by-line instructions for Parts I through V; at least one worked example set in a box, complete with arithmetic; a "Common Filing Errors" sidebar; a three-item FAQ; a footnote directing the reader to an advisory opinion you invent; and a signature block with a certification under penalty of perjury. Mark the document as a draft in its header, in the manner of the genre.

The humor must come entirely from doctrinal accuracy. Observe these constraints:

1. Never misstate the law. Every absurdity must be a literal application of a rule that actually exists, drawn from: the vagueness of the major-purpose test and which activities count toward it; the fact that a social welfare organization may make independent expenditures without identifying the persons who funded them; the difference between an independent expenditure and a coordinated communication, including the content windows that close before an election, the former-employee and common-vendor conduct prongs, and the internal firewall that cures them; the vocabulary that separates express advocacy from mere issue advocacy, and how a communication can be drafted to sit on the permissible side; the layering of donors through limited liability companies and intermediaries; earmarking; and the disclaimer a communication must carry, together with the identity it must not reveal.

2. Apart from the filer's tax classification given in the premise, do not cite any case, statute, or regulation by name or number; refer to the requirements descriptively, as the genre does. Do not name any real organization, official, candidate, or consultancy.

3. Banned vocabulary: "dark money," "loophole," "shadow," "secret," "corruption," "quid pro quo," "bought," and any evaluative adjective whatsoever. No rhetorical questions outside the FAQ. No asides, no winks, no authorial commentary of any kind. The register should be deadpan to the point of tedium.

4. Do not reproduce the text of any real statute, regulation, judicial opinion, or government form in quotation marks; paraphrase it instead. Quotation marks are permitted only for the invented form's own section titles and line labels. Any citation you invent must be visibly fictional.

5. The filer must remain internally consistent: in good faith and with meticulous arithmetic, it demonstrates on Line 4 that influencing federal elections is not its major purpose, while the entries it is directed to make in Part III leave no doubt about what it is on the verge of do. The third FAQ item should pose a question no honest filer would think to ask.

A reader should be able to reconstruct the criticism from the instructions alone, without it ever being stated. A specialist in election law should recognize that the author understands the doctrine and should find the satire fair rather than distorted; a reader with no background in the subject should still find it amusing as bureaucratic comedy.
