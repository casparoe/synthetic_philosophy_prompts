Completely fluent in it, no philosophy training beyond what you pick up incidentally. Could you restate Nozick's utility monster argument against utilitarianism in the vocabulary i use all day, as literally as you can manage? So: what is the monster in QALY terms, what does feeding it everything correspond to, and what does the claim that utilitarianism is *obliged* to feed it look like once there's a cost-per-QALY threshold and a fixed budget in the picture?

then the bit i actually care about, which is the residue. which of Nozick's claims translate cleanly, which ones you had to bend out of shape to fit, and which ones have no expression at all in HTA-speak. two suspicions i want tested rather than flattered:

1. our scale is bounded by construction. a QALY tops out at one year of perfect health and a person tops out at their remaining lifespan, so nobody can have the unlimited capacity for gain the monster supposedly has. is that a genuine refutation of Nozick, or are we just quietly capping the variable that was causing the trouble and a philosopher would say we've begged the question?

2. we already have something monster-shaped sitting in the model that we don't call that. QALY gains scale with remaining life expectancy, so a fit twenty-year-old generates many multiples of the QALYs an eighty-year-old with the same condition does, and the arithmetic then tells us to send everything to the young one. we paper over it with fair innings arguments, age weighting and the theshold modifiers for severe disease. is that just a utility monster with better manners, or is it a structurally different kind of thing, and can your translation tell them apart?

finally, give me a verdict on whether the residue is a defect in our vocabulary — something about welfare that HTA structurally cannot say — or evidence that Nozick was leaning on a conception of utility (cardinal, unbounded, interpersonally comparable) that welfare economics ditched a long time ago, in which case the thought experiment is aimed at a target nobody actually defends. i want you to pick a side and argue it, not give me a both-sides shrug.
