just got out of a three hour argument with a friend about whether race is socially constructed and I still don't know who won, so I'm outsourcing it.

Her argument went like this. Money is the clean case. A twenty dollar bill is physically just paper; what makes it money is that we collectively treat it as having a status it doesn't physically have -- a scrap of cotton that counts as a claim on stuff. Nobody says "therefore money isn't real." It's real, it does things, it ruins people's lives, it's just real in a different way than granite is. And race is exactly that: bodies get a status attached to them by collective practice. So "do races exist?" is a confused question, roughly as confused as asking whether money exists. And -- this is the part I couldn't get past -- she said that means fixing it is like fixing money: you change the status by changing the practice, the way a country can just declare a new currency or pull an old one out of circulation.

My objection, which I coudn't state cleanly at the time: the analogy feels like it's carrying way more weight than it can hold, and it's doing different work in the two halves of her argument. Money seems like a thing we can create and destroy by deciding, and it belongs to whoever's holding it and doesn't care who that is. Being assigned a race isn't like that -- nobody decided it, you can't opt out, there's no desk you could appeal to that would reissue you differently, and it isn't holder-neutral at all, the whole point is which side of it you're on. When I said that she told me I was taking the analogy too literally.

So can you audit it properly? I want the correspondences laid out explicitly: what in the race case is playing the role of the paper, of the status attached to it, of the collective acceptance, of the issuing authority, of the backing that keeps people accepting it tomorrow -- and I want you to say out loud where a slot has nothing in it, becuase I suspect that's where the trouble lives. Then sort the disanalogies into the ones that actually bite on her two conclusions and the ones that are just decorative, and tell me for each conclusion separately whether it survives once the disanalogies are on the table. I especially want to know whether "the question do-races-exist is confused" and "we could change this by changing the practice, like a currency reform" are being supported by the same features of the analogy or different ones, because my gut says she got the first one cheap and paid for it in the second.

Also tell me what the analogy quietly imports that the target doesn't have. I suspect "constructed" is smuggling in "made on purpose, by someone, who could therefore unmake it," and that's the only reason the currency-reform ending sounds plausible. If that's right I want to see exactly which premise of hers depends on the smuggled bit.

And if you think money is the wrong source, say what a better one would be and why -- something that keeps the real-but-not-natural part without dragging in the someone-could-have-decided-otherwise part. I'd rather hear that the whole family of examples is bad than get a fourth option bolted on.

Please don't give me the socially-constructed-doesn't-mean-fake speech, I heard versions of it from both sides all night. I want to know exactly where the thing snaps.
