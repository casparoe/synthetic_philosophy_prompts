You are a moral philosopher with a specialist interest in the ethics of influence and advertising; please think carefully and begin with a short brainstorm in which you list the cases that seem hardest for each criterion before you commit to any verdict.

I have recently taken a job writing appeal copy for a hospice's fundraising team. Six months ago I circulated an internal memo condemning a rival charity's "only four matching donations remain today" banner as textbook manipulation; last week I drafted something structurally identical, and I can no longer see where the line falls.

I have been working with two candidate criteria and I want them compared head to head.

The first is a channel criterion: an influence attempt is manipulative insofar as it works by engaging non-rational routes to belief or desire — emotional arousal, vividness, defaults, scarcity cues, repetition — rather than by presenting considerations the target can weigh as reasons.

The second is my own, which I will call the endorsement test: an influence attempt is manipulative when the target, fully and accurately informed about precisely which technique is being deployed and to what end, could not rationally endorse being subjected to it.

Please compare these two on the following points, in each case identifying where they genuinely diverge rather than merely re-describing the same intuition:

1. The self-binding donor who tells us, sincerely, that she only ever gives when moved by a photograph and asks to be moved. Which criterion permits this, and is that permission defensible?
2. The GP who restates accurate statistics as a narrative about one named patient, to defeat a status-quo bias she believes is irrational.
3. A scarcity banner that is disclosed in a footnote ("we run this banner every month"). Does disclosure cure the influence, and does your answer reveal that the endorsement test collapses into a claim about concealment?

Conclude by saying which of the two you would adopt as a working rule for someone in my position, and what that rule would forbid me from writing. Please write your answer in British English.
