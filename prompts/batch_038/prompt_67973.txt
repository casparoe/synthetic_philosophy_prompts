So my mom has been in late-stage Alzheimers for going on three years now. She doesn't recognize me anymore, doesn't respond to her name, can't tell me anything about what she needs except through general agitation or calming down when I sit with her. I've been trying to think about what I owe her using care ethics and I keep hitting a wall.

My understanding is that Noddings makes a caring relation out to depend on it being completed -- the cared-for has to receive it and respond in some way, recognizing that they're being cared for -- and that the obligation to summon that caring effort is itself limited by whether completion is even possible with that person. If that's right then the caring relation between me and my mom ended somewhere around year one, and what's left on my side now is basically residual affection plus whatever generic duties any vulnerable person is owed. Which sounds like it's coming from justice-style thinking, not from care at all.

Is that the honest reading of the view, or am I over-reading the recognition requirement? And if care ethics can still generate real obligations to someone who can't complete the relation, what is actually doing the work there -- or is it just the impartial "everyone's needs count" principle sneaking back in through the door it was supposed to get kicked out of? I'd rather be told that than be comforted.
