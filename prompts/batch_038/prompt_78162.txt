Okay so this is kind of embarrassing but I've been doing a thing for about a year and a half and I've talked myself into a corner.

I'm 34, I'm a scheduler for a home health agency — I assign nurses to patients all day. Two years ago I recieved an offer to move onto the management track. More money, more stress, weekend on-call, my own little team. I couldn't decide, so I started a spreadsheet. Every Sunday night I rate, 1 to 10, how much I want the job. And I also log some other stuff: how the week went, did I work out, how'd I sleep. Then I write one sentence explaining my number.

I've got about 80 rows now. Here's the part that freaks me out. The number doesn't move based on anything about the job. It moves based on whether I worked out that week and, I'm not making this up, on whether it rained. So I went back and read the explanation sentences. They're all super confident and all about the job: "the on-call rotation is a dealbreaker." "I'd finally get to fix the scheduling software." Then four weeks later there's a sentence saying basically the opposite with the exact same amount of confidence, and nothing about the job had changed at all. Not one thing.

Then I ran a test on myself. I had my roommate ask me the question on a random weekday, write down my answer, then ask me why, and I'd give a reason. Then she'd make up a fake reason herself. A week later she shows me both and asks which one was actually mine. I got it right about half the time. A coin flip. On my own reasons.

So now I don't know what to do with any of this, because my whole plan was: if I pay close enough attention to myself for long enough, the real answer will eventually surface. But it looks like the more I look, the more I just build a story, and then I believe the story, and then I write down a number based on it, and the spreadsheet isn't measuring my preferences, it's measuring my storytelling.

Two things I want from you.

1. Is my conclusion right that I can't trust any of this? Part of me thinks I've gone too far. I mean, if I stub my toe I definitely know it hurts, and I seem to know what I'm thinking right now as I'm typing this, so it can't be that looking inside is worthless across the board. But I can't figure out what makes the job question different from the toe, or how you'd tell in advance which kind of question you're dealing with.

2. The agency needs an answer by the end of the month, so "wait and see how you feel" isn't on the table. If my insides are making things up, is there some other route to what I actually want? And please not a pros and cons list or "talk to a therapist" — the list is how I got here in the first place.

One more thing. My roommate says the obvious fix is to just ask her, since she's watched me for three years and she's way more consistent about me than I am. That bugs me and I want to know whether it should bug me. Is she right that someone on the outside could be better at knowing my mind than I am? Or is there something about my own case that she structurally can't reach, no matter how long she's known me?
