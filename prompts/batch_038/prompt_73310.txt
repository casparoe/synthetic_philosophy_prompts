I am in the closing stages of a doctorate in philosophy, writing on relational conceptions of personhood in African moral and political thought, and my examiners have asked me to submit a short statement of my own position so that it can be interrogated directly rather than reconstructed from my chapters. Before I hand it over, I would like an unflinching audit of whether the package is jointly tenable. The commitments are these.

1. Personhood, in the normatively interesting sense, is not conferred by birth but achieved, and achieved in degrees. I follow Menkiti in holding that one becomes a person through progressively taking on and discharging obligations within a network of relationships, and that what has been achieved can be forfeited by a sustained failure to maintain those obligations.

2. This is symmetrical with what I hold about death. Someone who has died remains a person for as long as there are living people who remember them by name, and ceases to be a person, in that sense, when the last of those people dies. Personhood therefore both outlasts the body and is capable of lapsing.

3. Notwithstanding the first two, I maintain that every human being has a dignity that does not admit of degrees, and I want to ground it relationally rather than in rationality or autonomy — roughly, in the capacity to enter into relations of mutual identification and solidarity, both as subject and as object.

4. I deny that membership of the human species is in itself of any moral significance. Grounding dignity in humanity as such looks to me like a prejudice in favour of one's own kind wearing the costume of a principle, and I do not want to be caught relying on it, even indirectly.

5. I hold that the community is constitutive of the individual — that one's normative standing, one's identity as a particular person, and one's availability as an agent at all are products of being held in relations with others — and yet I also want the resulting view to furnish a standpoint from which to criticise both the atomised individualism of affluent liberal societies and the actual practices of particular communities, including practices that silence dissenters or that burden women under customary law.

6. Individuals are, for all that, properly answerable for the state of their own personhood. Someone who withdraws from communal life, or who refuses the work of self-cultivation it involves, has failed at something for which they may be blamed, even though the resources, the standards and the opportunities for becoming a person were supplied entirely by others.

7. I am an anti-essentialist about the cultural status of all of this. I do not think that the position I am describing names a worldview shared by all and only Africans, and I resist any presentation of it as the collective thought of a people rather than as a set of theses with argumentative content that anyone may assess on the merits.

8. Finally, a verdict I am determined to keep. Where a community ostracises someone unjustly — refuses them recognition, expels them, treats them as beyond the pale — the personhood of the ostracised individual is not diminished in the slightest. If standing is lost, it is lost on the other side: it is the community's own personhood, so to speak, that has been degraded.

What I want is an audit, not an exposition. Taking the set exactly as I have stated it, please tell me: which pairs or clusters genuinely pull against one another, ranked by severity rather than presented as an undifferentiated list; which tensions only look like tensions and dissolve once the commitments are stated precisely, together with the reason they dissolve; for each genuine tension, the least costly revision available and what that revision costs me elsewhere; whether any single change repairs more than one of them; and specifically whether verdict (8) is deliverable from principles (1) to (5) as written, or whether I have simply asserted it against my own premises. I have no antecedent view about which of these commitments is the weakest link, and I would rather not signal one — please tell me which of them the others are pressing against. Please work with my formulations rather than sending me to the secondary literature: I have read it, and a reading list would be of no use to me here. If you need to correct one of my formulations in order to make a tension visible, say explicitly which formulation you are correcting and how. Please write your answer in British English.
