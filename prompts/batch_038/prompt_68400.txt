I work on motion control for automated production lines — servo tuning, PLC sequencing, open-loop versus closed-loop behaviour, model-based commissioning. No philosophy training; I came across Dennett's four grades of creature — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — years ago and have kept turning the scheme over against my own work ever since.

Restate it in the vocabulary I actually use, and push the translation as far as it will honestly go: Darwinian as behaviour fixed at design time and not adjustable in the field; Skinnerian as gains and sequences retuned on-line from a scalar performance signal; Popperian as a commissioned model of the plant that lets the controller try candidate moves in simulation before committing any of them; Gregorian as controllers that draw on function blocks, libraries, and parameter sets maintained outside the machine by other people. Mark clearly which commitments are Dennett's and which you have had to borrow from my discipline to make the mapping work — I do not want control-theoretic assumptions smuggled in and presented as translation.

Then give me the residue report: what survives intact, what survives only with distortion, and what has no expression at all — in particular anything that would make these different kinds of mind rather than different amounts of one thing.

Two difficulties I cannot resolve on my own. First, in my world the grades are not rungs on a ladder: the simulator is only as good as the parameters identified by on-line tuning against a scalar cost, and that cost was handed to the machine from outside. If each grade's competence depends on the grade below it, does the translation still support qualitative distinctions, or does the whole scheme collapse into a single dimension — how much evaluation happens off-line before an action is taken? Second, two machines with byte-identical firmware behave quite differently depending on whether they can reach a shared parameter repository. Where does that difference live, in the creature or in its surroundings, and can my vocabulary even pose the question, given that for me a controller simply is its configuration?
