I'm in an AI safety reading group that meets tuesdays and honestly half of us are there for the pizza. Last week we got onto the Löbian obstacle — the thing where an agent whose reasoning lives inside some formal system can't establish its own soundness, so it can't safely trust a copy of itself or a smarter successor it builds, since Löb's theorem seems to collapse "I trust my own outputs" into "I can already prove them," which makes the trust worthless. My friend Ravi, who writes compilers for a living and thinks most of philosophy is debugging with extra steps, said this is just Thompson's trusting-trust problem with the logos swapped out: the one where a compiler gets taught to slip a backdoor into the login program, and also to reinsert that same backdoor whenever it compiles a compiler from clean source, so you can scrub the source all you like and the poison survives the bootstrap. His argument was that nobody in his field loses sleep over this, because you audit with a second independently written toolchain and compare the binaries, so the Löbian obstacle is an engineering problem and not a metaphisical one — we should stop treating self-trust as a deep barrier and just bolt redundant verifiers onto agents.
