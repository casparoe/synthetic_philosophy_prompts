ok so a friend and I burned most of last night arguing about whether nations are made up, and the depressing conclusion is that neither of us knows what would settle it. So before I read another page of the two obvious books I want the spec. Not a summary of the debate — the checklist itself.

Write out the requirements an account of national origins has to meet to count as adequate. Adequate for this, specifically: being able to say of some particular group whether it is a nation and roughly since when, in a way that isn't just a restatement of that group's own self-image. Give me each requirement with the case or consideration behind it, because I want to be able to check whether I actually accept it rather than inherit it.

A few I already suspect belong on there — fold them in, sharpen them, or tell me they're junk:

- it has to explain why the category felt ancient to the people who were busy inventing it, and equally why nobody in 1300 seems to have noticed its absence. An account that makes nations modern can't leave the experience of deep age as a mass delusion with no mechanism attached; an account that makes them ancient has to say what precisely was missing earlier.
- it can't turn the origin into a regress. If the nation is brought into being by a movement claiming to speak for an already-existing nation, something has to be there before the movement, and the theory owes me an account of what that something is and why it isn't yet a nation.
- it has to answer for the negative cases. There are language communities with all the usual raw material that never produced a movement or a state, and I think this is where most of these accounts die, because they were built backwards out of the successes.
- it has to handle one territory hosting a sequence of self-descriptions, each claiming to be the oldest continuous one, without making them all true or all false.

Also, here's a distinction I came up with in the car and I want an honest verdict on whether it does any work or is just a graceful way of losing the argument. My friend kept collapsing three seperate questions: when the general category became available for anyone to apply at all; when this particular group started applying it to itself; and when the thing it picks out, in this case, actually began to exist. Switzerland, Scotland, the Habsburg lands, and a bunch of postcolonial states come out differently on those three depending on who you ask, and I suspect a lot of the shouting is people answering different questions. Should a theory be required to answer each one seperately? If yes, state it as a requirement. If it's a distinction without a difference, say so and say why.

Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are really just one camp's pet commitment wearing a criterion costume — I strongly suspect at least one of my four above is a modernist prejudice. And tell me whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable. My hunch is that explaining the felt antiquity, avoiding the regress, and genuinely explaining the failures can't all be met by anything that assigns a single birth date, and if that's right the list is itself the result and I want to know which one to give up.

Finish with one line on which single requirement knocks out the most candidates. Skip the scorecard against the named positions, I'll do that myself, and no bibliography or further reading — I just want the criteria.
