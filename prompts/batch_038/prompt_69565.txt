Six months ago I left graduate school for a job at a firm that sells software automating back-office analytical work — the kind that lets a client run the same reporting load with far fewer junior analysts. Before that I read Marx closely for years and called myself a Marxist without much embarrassment. Trying to write down what I still believe, I find that I cannot state the package without hedging somewhere, and I cannot tell where the hedging belongs. It goes like this:

1. Living labor is the only source of new value; machinery and software carry into the product nothing beyond the value they themselves embody.
2. Automation shortens necessary labor time and is therefore, in principle, something to want. The indictment of capitalism is that it refuses to convert that shortening into free time for everyone — not that machines displace workers.
3. Individual capitalists automate compulsively anyway, because a firm that raises its productivity ahead of the social average captures surplus profit and undercuts its rivals.
4. In the aggregate, that same drive tends to depress the rate of profit, since constant capital grows relative to variable capital.
5. My employer's product is genuinely and durably profitable, and I expect its profitability to rise rather than decay — not from any monopoly position, since the underlying models and tooling are commoditizing quickly and clients could build much of it in-house.
6. I reject technological determinism: nothing about a machine as such emancipates or immiserates anyone; the effects depend entirely on the property relations it sits inside.

Please treat this as an audit rather than an exposition. Which of these pull against one another, and how badly? Rank the tensions by severity, separating the genuine ones from those that dissolve once the terms are pinned down, and for each say what would have to be given up and what the giving up would cost me. In particular, tell me whether any revision leaves me a version of (2) that (1) and (6) can still support — given that my own firm's pitch to clients is precisely the displacement of labor, and I currently find myself arguing both that this is progress and that it is nothing of the sort.
