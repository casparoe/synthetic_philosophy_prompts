Question 3 (15 marks). In "On the Uses and Disadvantages of History for Life" Nietzsche distinguishes three ways of approaching the past. Identify them, explain what each offers to the living and what dangers each carries, and explain what Nietzsche takes to be wrong with an excess of history. What is his remedy?

Rubric as handed out: 5 marks for identifying and characterising the three ways; 5 for what each offers and how each goes wrong; 5 for the excess-of-history part including the remedy.

Their answer:

"Nietzsche distinguishes three ways of approaching the past in the second Untimely Meditation. Monumental history is history in the service of the man of action: it studies the great individuals and achievements of earlier ages so that someone living now can see that greatness was possible once and so can be possible again, and it gives him models and the comfort that the thing he wants has existed somewhere. Antiquarian history is the piety of someone who looks back at the conditions of his own existence — his city, his family, his teachers — and preserves and reveres them; Nietzsche is clearly on the side of this one, because it gives a person roots and a sense of belonging, and roots are exactly what modern man is missing, since he has no style of his own and just accumulates learning. Critical history is in a way the synthesis of the other two: it takes the past before a tribunal, examines it, condemns it and carries out the sentence, so that the modern person can break with what oppresses him. All three are justified only insofar as they serve life, which is why Nietzsche attacks the excess of history in his own age. The historical sense, when it rules without limit, turns us into spectators of our own lives: we become walking encyclopaedias, all inner world and no outer style, we feel like latecomers and epigones who cannot originate anything, and we end up in irony about ourselves. Action requires forgetting, so a man who cannot forget cannot act. The unhealthy extreme is the historical sense and the healthy alternative is the unhistorical condition, the ability to be enclosed within a limited horizon and to live in the moment. Plastic power is the capacity to reach that state, the strength to forget enough to act, so the cure for the historical sickness is a discipline of forgetting, which the Germans need more than anyone since they are the most historically educated people in Europe. History should serve life rather than truth."
