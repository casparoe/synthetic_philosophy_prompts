I'm in an ethics of harm course and I've been trying to write down what I actually think about killing vs letting die, case by case, and the list is a mess. Verdicts:

1. Doctor switches off a vent at the request of a competent terminal patient: fine.
2. Doctor who never switches it on for that same patient, same wishes, same outcome: not fine, and worse somehow, which I know sounds backwards from what the doing/allowing distinction is supposed to say.
3. Two cousins after an inheritance: one holds the kid under in the bath, the other walks in, sees the kid slip and slide under, and just watches. Equally bad. No difference at all for me.
4. Surgeon kills one healthy visitor for organs to save five: not fine. Letting the five die rather than do it: fine, basically required. So here doing/allowing beats the 5-vs-1 numbers, but in 3 it beats nothing.
5. Kid drowning in a pond up to my knees and I'm wearing a suit I like: not fine, but a notch less bad than pushing him in.
6. The festival ticket money would buy a bednet that saves a kid: I keep writing "fine" but I definately don't think it is.
7. Swimmer is two strokes from a ring buoy on a post; I walk over and move the buoy to a post she can't reach. Never touched her, only moved an object. Feels exactly like killing.
8. I promised to sort my aunt's pills, then after two weeks I stop going over and she dies. Worse than a stranger who never went, and worse than if I'd never promised at all??

My tentative rule: you can't leave someone worse off than they'd be if you'd never been anywhere near the situation, but you don't have to make them better off. I think 2, 6 and 8 all break it.

Is there a principle, or two competing ones, that gets these? State it precisely, say which case each clause is there to handle, then run it on stuff I didn't list — a company that pulls a drug from the market vs one that raises the price tenfold, and a driver who swerves onto a path with one person on it. And be honest about which of my verdicts it can't keep: does the cheap principle that drops one beat the baroque one that saves all eight?
