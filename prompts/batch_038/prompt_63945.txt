I run QA for a broadband support team. My graders score about forty recorded calls a week against the script I wrote, and the scores feed into performance reviews, so they get disputed a lot. Lately I keep hitting calls where the agent does every required step — greets properly, verifies the account, reads the mandatory disclosure, walks the customer through a router reboot, confirms the customer "understands" — and the customer hangs up with the exact same fault they called about. It reminded me of the wasp case philosophers use for this, sphexishness I think it's called: the wasp drags its paralyzed cricket to the mouth of the burrow, goes in to check the nest, comes back out, and if you've moved the cricket while it was inside it just drags it back to the entrance and starts over, indefinitely. Nothing it does is incompetent. Each move is fine. The whole routine is stuck and can't tell.

Write me a procedure a grader can run on one transcript in about ten minutes that separates "compliant and solved it" from "compliant and looping." My graders have no technical background and no philosophy, so it needs to be concrete questions in order, what answer counts as passing each one, where it stops at a fail, and where it hands the call up to a human becuase the remaining question is about service quality rather than something checkable in the transcript. The part I care most about: a test for real verification versus fake verification — a step that looks like a check but whose only possible outcome is to run the loop again. And it has to hold the line that some script steps are regulatory and can't be skipped no matter how stuck the call has become.

Then run it on three cases. One: the agent asks the customer to reboot three times, gets a flat "yeah ok" each time, and twice the customer says something that doesn't fit the branch the agent is in. Two: the agent skips the mandatory disclosure, solves an unusual fault in four minutes, customer is delighted. Three: the agent repeats verbatim lines four times and sounds like a machine, but the customer's issue doesn't appear in any branch of my script.

Finish with where the procedure will misfire, and say something honest about the risk that grading for sphexishness turns into its own sphexish routine.
