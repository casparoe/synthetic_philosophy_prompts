I'm on my department's small grants committee (religious studies, about a dozen of us, we fund maybe 4 of the 20 proposals each cycle). One proposal this round wants to build a scoring tool for the criteria of authenticity — dissimilarity, embarrassment, multiple attestation, coherence, Aramaic flavor / Palestinian environment, that lot — and output a probability for each synoptic saying being traceable to Jesus rather than invented by the early communities. The PI's pitch is that past uses of the criteria have been pure vibes, so she wants to weight them and combine them into likelihood ratios so anyone can see exactly where each judgment came from and poke at it.

Two members are thrilled: finally something checkable. Three are hostile, and their objection is basically that the criteria aren't independent pieces of evidence — multiple attestation already presupposes a source model, coherence can only confirm what dissimilarity let through in the first place, and embarassment is scored against norms you've reconstructed from the very material you're trying to filter — so multiplying them together just launders the circularity as arithmetic. I find that pretty compelling, but I also think the tool would be worth having even as a crude way of forcing people to show their work.

Draft the committee's decision memo: fund as proposed, fund with conditions, or decline — with reasons the PI could actually respond to in a resubmission rather than generic committee-speak. Give the hostile camp's strongest version of their argument real airtime even if you land somewhere else, and close with the specific conditions or revisions you'd attach, phrased so we can vote on them individually on Thursday.
