Right, so I'm a data engineer at a mid-size fintech in London and I own what we call the metrics layer — the canonical definitions of things like "active account", "churn", "net revenue" and "fraud loss", all written in a semantic layer that every dashboard and every downstream SQL job pulls from. My problem is that the definitions change, maybe two or three substantive changes a quarter, and every single time we have the same argument from scratch and nobody can articulate why they think what they think.

Shape of the argument, real example from last year: we tightened "active account". It used to mean an account that had logged in at least once in the past 90 days; now it means logged in *and* holding at least one open position. Two weeks vanished into the question of whether we restate the last four years of history under the new definition — which makes the trend line properly comparable but silently changes numbers people have been quoting in board decks for years — or whether we freeze the historical figures and eat a visible discontinuity in the chart, or run both series in parallel for ever. And then there's the nastier version nobody thought about until it broke: someone wrote a scheduled job in 2022 that asks for "accounts that were active in 2021", and it still runs every night. When it runs today, should it apply the definition that was in force in 2021 or the one in force now? Both answers are defensible and we genuinely can't say which.

While trying to explain this to myself I fell down a philosophy-of-language rabbit hole and I'm fairly convinced it's the same problem wearing a different coat. Something about rigid versus non-rigid designation, and about two-dimensional semantics, where a term has one intension that gets evaluated against whatever situation you're treating as the actual one and a different one that holds the actual world fixed and tracks the same referent across counterfactual situations. I think that's roughly the right shape of it, though I've got no formal training so please correct me where I've mangled it.

What I actually need is a decision procedure my team can run, not an essay. Specifically:

- The questions to ask, in order, when a metric definition changes — and for each one, what counts as a sufficient answer rather than a shrug.
- A default that kicks in when nobody has time or appetite to decide, because that's most of the time and "we'll revisit it" means "we never will".
- Clear handling of the two distinct cases, which we currently conflate: (a) we changed how we describe the world, and (b) the world changed underneath the description, e.g. a fee that used to sit outside "net revenue" gets reclassified by a regulator so the same term now sweeps in more money. I don't think those should get the same treatment and I can't prove it.
- Where the procedure has to stop and hand the decision to a human, because it's a question of values or of who gets blamed rather than a question of fact.
- Which steps are honestly judgment calls dressed up as procedure. I'd rather know.

Then run it on three cases for me: the "active account" tightening above; the scheduled 2022 job asking about 2021; and a third one where I already know I won't like the answer — after a bad incident we broadened "fraud loss" to include a category we'd previously excluded, and leadership would really like the historical series restated under the new definition so the chart shows the new measure going down since the incident. That number feeds a comp plan and a regulatory return, so tell me straight what the procedure says and what it costs us.

Constraints on the thing you write: it has to be runnable in about half an hour by an engineer who has never heard the word intension, it needs a sign-off step (a data steward plus whoever owns the affected dashboard — I don't get to unilaterally rewrite history), and it should be usable as a written record afterwards, because "who decided this and why" is the question we can never answer eighteen months later. Please don't give me a reading list or citations, I have no interest and no access to a library; I just want the procedure.
