So there's this thing that keeps bugging me about costly signaling. There seem to be two different stories people tell for why an expensive signal stays honest, and they don't actually agree with each other. Story one is the handicap idea: the signal only carries information because it hurts the low-quality sender more than the high-quality one, so if the cost ever became flat — same absolute price for every type — the whole thing should collapse. Story two is the job-market-signaling idea: what's doing the work is just that a faker can't scrape together the resources, so the signal stays honest even when the cost is identical for everybody, and it dies the moment the low types get access to a bigger budget, not the moment the cost stops being differential.

Build me one thought experiment that forces the two stories to come out in opposite directions on the same signal, so I can see which one is really load-bearing.

Constraints:
- realistic and human — hiring, dating, a small business, a grad program admission, a neighborhood, whatever — no peacocks, no gazelles, no sci-fi, and the cost has to be one an ordinary person could actually face, not a fantasy number
- nobody lies, nobody commits fraud, nobody is dumb; the receiver updates sensibly. Both sides have to be honest equilibria, just differently explained.
- the disagreement has to fall out of the two accounts, not be built in. If you stipulate the verdicts, the case is worthless to me.
- written for someone with one semester of intro econ and zero biology, so no jargon you don't immediately gloss.

Format: lead with a single sentence giving the bottom line — does the signal stay honest in your case, yes or no. Then the scenario itself in one paragraph. Then walk me through what each of the two stories predicts and why. Then name the one feature of the case that's doing the work, and say what happens to both verdicts if you switch that feature off. Finish with an honest line on where you had to bend the rules to make it come out, where the case begs the question, and what a defender of the story that comes out worse would say to break it.

Please keep the whole answer under 450 words.
