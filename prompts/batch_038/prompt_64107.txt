my opening: two urns, and stipulate you're certain of everything I'm about to say — no hedging about wether the eccentric is lying or misremembering, that move is closed off. Urn A: 100 balls, 50 red and 50 black, you know this. Urn B: the eccentric chose a number of red balls uniformly out of {0, 1, ..., 100} and filled the urn accordingly, then told you precisely that. Bet on red pays $100 from either urn. Now, if you write your belief about B as a single prior — the mixture that puts red at 50% — your own maxmin rule collapses into plain expected utility and you're indifferent between the urns. But if you write the very same knowledge as the set of point-priors {0 red, 1 red, ..., 100 red}, your rule looks only at the worst member, 0 red, and you won't touch red-from-B at any price under $100 — even though you know 0 red was exactly as likely as 100 red, one chance in 101 each. Same facts, same agent, two verdicts, and the whole difference came from how you chose to package your beliefs. So: which packaging is correct, and what makes it correct? If the answer is "the singleton, whenever the generating process is known," then your theory never fires in the cases it's supposed to explain, and what you're calling ambiguity aversion is a stylistic preference about belief representation. If the answer is "the set," you're throwing away a probability distribution you just agreed you have. Pick one and defend it.
