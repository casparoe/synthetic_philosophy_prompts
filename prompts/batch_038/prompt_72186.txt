Last night a friend and I spent three hours on Dennett's proposal that the self is a center of narrative gravity and ended in stalemate. Her charge was that the view is either a dressed-up truism — of course people tell stories about themselves — or else eliminativism wearing a euphemism, and I found I could not show her that a third reading is available. I now have to teach the idea in forty-five minutes to an evening reading group of about a dozen adults with no philosophy beyond secondary school, who will arrive expecting either a spiritual vindication of the self or a debunking of it.

Do not explain the view to me; plan how I should explain it. Specifically: the order in which the components must be introduced and what each depends on, so that the center-of-gravity analogy carries the weight and the narrative element arrives only once the notion of an abstractum is secure; the single case that should run through the whole session, together with what that case must exhibit and what it must conspicuously lack; the misconceptions the group will form — I anticipate some version of "so I do not really exist" and some version of "so I am free to author a better story about myself" — and the exact point in the sequence at which each must be intercepted; one question I can pose at the end whose answers would distinguish those who have grasped the claim from those who have merely nodded along to it; and what I should openly decline to cover at this level, with the sentence I should say in place of it. Finally, if you can, resolve the stalemate: say whether a formulation exists that is neither trivial nor eliminativist, and if so, at which step it belongs.

Please return the plan as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- `sequence`: an array of objects, each with `step` (integer, order of delivery), `introduces` (the component presented), `depends_on` (array of step numbers this requires), and `why_here` (why it cannot come earlier or later).
- `load_bearing_case`: an object with `the_case` (the example itself), `must_exhibit` (array), `must_lack` (array), and `where_it_breaks` (the point past which the case should not be pushed).
- `misconception_map`: an array of objects with `misconception` (stated as a group member would state it), `trigger_step` (the step number that provokes it), and `interception` (what I do or say at that moment).
- `comprehension_check`: an object with `question` and `what_a_good_answer_shows`, plus `what_a_bad_answer_shows`.
- `declined_topics`: an array of objects with `topic`, `why_it_cannot_be_done_in_forty_five_minutes`, and `what_i_say_instead`.
- `stalemate_verdict`: an object with `trivial_horn` (whether the first charge succeeds, and why), `eliminativist_horn` (the same for the second), and `third_reading` (the formulation that escapes both, or an explanation of why none exists).
