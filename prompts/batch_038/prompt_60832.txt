Dark-forest dynamics as an explanation of the Great Silence: I want your credence that a substantial fraction of civilizations lasting long enough to become interstellar deliberately refrain from broadcasting, and destroy other civilizations on detecting them, because each assumes the others will reason the same way. Rather than a bare number, give me the decomposition. How likely is it that such civilizations converge on chain-of-suspicion reasoning at all, as against indifference, deterrence, or some norm against a first strike? How likely is it that striking beats those alternatives once you count that a strike is itself potentially detectable and so advertises the striker? How likely is it that concealment is imperfect enough for detection to occur at the rates the hypothesis needs — a genuinely silent forest gives its inhabitants nothing to fear and nothing to shoot at? And how likely is it that these dispositions persist across the timescales the silence actually spans, rather than being selected away? One number and one line of justification per term, then the combination, then which term your total is most sensitive to and which single observation — existing evidence or something plausibly detectable within fifty years — would move it most. Say also which term you think separates people who find the hypothesis compelling from those who find it naive, and close by reporting how confident you are in the decomposition itself and which step in it you trust least.
