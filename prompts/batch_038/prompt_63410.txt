I keep coming back to a clash between two bodies of reading, and I'd like you to adjudicate it from Popper's side of the table, on a question he never had to face.

The thing he never faced: a research program that builds explicit mathematical models of long-run social dynamics, codes up centuries of comparative data on population growth, real wages, the number of aspirants competing for a roughly fixed stock of elite positions, and state fiscal capacity, and claims to have identified a recurrent cycle in which popular immiseration, plus an oversupply of would-be elites, plus a fiscally weakening state reliably issues in a wave of political violence, with a rough period of a few generations. Its practitioners are insistent that the claims are falsifiable rather than prophetic, and they backed the program with a published decade-ahead forecast — issued around 2010 — that the coming decade would be a period of growing instability in the United States and western Europe, a forecast they have since scored as largely borne out.

The Poverty of Historicism was written against the idea that history has a direction which can be read off and prophesied. Would Popper have to concede this program as one of the things he always permitted — conditional, testable, small-scale prediction from universal laws, exactly what he distinguished from prophecy? Or would he say the cycle claims are historicism in a lab coat: singular trends of whole societies mistaken for laws, however many differential equations are wrapped around them?

Work through his apparatus rather than his reputation: laws versus trends, the argument that we cannot predict the future growth of our own knowledge, the license for conditional prediction, the prophecy/prediction distinction, and the Oedipus effect, whereby a forecast helps bring about or averts what it forecasts. Two pressure points I want decided rather than merely noted. First, does the knowledge-growth argument actually bite on a model that predicts from demographic and fiscal variables and makes no claim about discoveries or ideas? Second, is the Oedipus effect his best weapon here, or does it cut against him — since this forecast was published precisely to prompt preventive action, which looks more like engineering than prophecy.

Give me a committed answer: which of the two readings of Popper is right about the central claim, which parts of his case a program like this genuinely defeats, and where you would bet against him — with reasons, not a balanced display of both options.
