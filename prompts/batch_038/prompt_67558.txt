I am in my final year of high school and I help run an after-school reading group in our library — four of us plus the librarian, who read politics at university and lets us use the back room. For the past six weeks we have been working through the argument between negative liberty, positive liberty, and the republican conception of freedom as non-domination, and on Thursday we spent ninety minutes disagreeing without ever having agreed on what we were disagreeing about: two of us assumed a theory of freedom should tell us when a particular person is unfree, two assumed it should tell us when a social arrangement is objectionable regardless of how anyone feels about it, and one assumed it should rank societies. Nobody budged, because nobody could say what would count as settling the matter. I am writing a long research essay in the spring and I would rather get the specification right before I evaluate any answers, so what I want from you is the desiderata themselves: the list of things any adequate theory of political freedom has to deliver to count as adequate for that purpose — arbitrating disputes of the kind my group just had, and being usable as a standard against which real institutions can be compared.

Please build the list around cases rather than around slogans, and make sure it copes with three scenarios I wrote up myself after Thursday, none of which I have seen discussed anywhere. First: a hereditary ruler has, for reasons of his own, undergone a procedure that makes it physically impossible for him to issue any order that a subject has expressed unwillingness to obey; the office and its traditional powers otherwise remain intact, and the procedure cannot be reversed by him or by anyone else. Second: a block of flats is administered by a management system that screens every resident request — repairs, guests, deliveries, subletting — and its operators may decline any request at their discretion; in eleven years they have declined nothing, and they have published the source code, in which there is no path that permits a decline, but the operating company retains a unilateral right to push updates and has exercised it in other buildings. Third: a woman of thirty who has never been interfered with and over whom no identifiable person holds any discretionary power, but who grew up in a town where nobody ever mentioned that certain lives were available; she is content, and would decline an offer to be told otherwise. Alongside these, the list has to handle the ordinary traffic — taxation, traffic law, the difference between a rule I had a hand in making and an identical rule imposed on me — and it has to say which distinctions a theory is not permitted to run together, such as being unfree versus being unable, being unfree versus being badly off, interference versus the mere capacity to interfere, and freedom versus the worth of freedom.

Deliver it as a numbered set of requirements, each followed by the case or consideration that motivates it, so I can see why it belongs on the list at all. Then mark, for each one, whether it is non-negotiable for any theory that deserves the name or whether it is really the programme of a particular camp wearing a requirement's clothing — and if it is the latter, say which camp is smuggling it in and what the list looks like once it is removed. Flag at least one pair of requirements that you suspect cannot be satisfied together and give the argument for the incompatibility, since if the list is jointly unsatisfiable somewhere that is the single most useful thing you can tell me. Finish by naming the one requirement that does the most work in eliminating candidates. Only after all of that, and in a paragraph at most, give me your view of how the three main positions do against the list and whether non-domination is genuinely a third concept or a relabelling of the other two; keep the weight on the criteria, because the scorecard is the part I intend to argue about with my group myself. One constraint: no bibliography, no reading list, no suggestions of where I might look things up — I want the specification, not signposts.
