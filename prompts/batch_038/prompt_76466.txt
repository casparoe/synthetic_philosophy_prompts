I've been in a reading group for about a year now, half mathematicians and half philosophers, and our sessions on the infinite have turned into a stuck record. On one side there's a colleague who does constructive and recursive-flavored work and is basically a finitist about mathematical objects: he'll happily use induction over the naturals, but he thinks talk of a completed infinite totality — the reals as a set, the power set operation iterated up through the transfinite — is at best a useful fiction and at worst incoherent. On the other side there's a set theorist who considers the matter closed, on the grounds that the actual infinite is indispensable for ordinary analysis and probability and that nobody who wants to do mathematics gets to sit this out. Every argument comes out on schedule: Hilbert's hotel, the diagonal argument, indispensability, the potential versus actual distinction, Gödel and Gentzen. And we get the same non-result every time, which is that everyone agrees to disagree and goes to get a beer. Nobody has moved an inch in twelve months, and I've started to suspect the two sides arent even disagreeing about the same thing. One small thing that keeps striking me: when the hotel comes up, my colleague reads it as showing that a completed infinity leads to absurdity in the real world (same number of guests after infinitely many arrive, no room left but also a room left), and the set theorist reads the exact same story as a harmless illustration of a bijection. Neither of them seems to notice they're loading the thought experiment differently, or that its force depends entirely on whether the "no room left" intuition is a claim about metaphysics or a counting habit.

Here's my working hypothesis, which I want you to test rather than just accept. My colleague has a line he repeats constantly: any purported completed infinite can be re-read as an open-ended rule for generating more of it, and the two readings — the finished totality and the never-ending procedure — never come apart in anything you could check, prove, or compute. So, he concludes, arguing about whether an actual infinite exists has to be verbal. The set theorist's reply is that this begs the question, because whether the re-reading is available at all already presupposes the finitist's criterion for what counts as given. Is either of them right? Is this a verbal dispute about "exists" or about "completed," a substantive one about which principles are justified and on what grounds, a difference about what counts as evidence (fruitfulness and indispensability versus intuitive givenness), or a case of two groups answering different questions under one heading — one asking what we're entitled to assume, the other asking what there is? I'd also like you to take a run at the proof theory, because it seems to be doing something strange. My colleague treats Gentzen's consistency proof of arithmetic as a defeat for Hilbert, since it reduces arithmetic to transfinite induction up to epsilon-zero, which is exactly the sort of principle he refuses to grant. The set theorist treats conservation results — the fact that strong systems don't prove new concrete finitary statements beyond what weak ones already prove — as showing the finitist has nothing left to worry about, and therefore no case. Can the same formal facts really be read both ways, or is one side misusing them?

What I want is a diagnosis with reasons, anchored in what each side actually says, not a survey of finitism and its varieties. I've read the surveys. Then tell me honestly about the prospects: is there anything here that would count as progress — a distinction that dissolves something, a formal result that isn't hostage to the reading, a reframing of the question, a test case both sides would accept as data — or is this the kind of standoff where the right conclusion is that neither side can argue its way past a starting intuition about the infinite, and the honest move is to say so plainly and shift to questions that can actually be advanced? Im not a mathematician, so if part of your answer is that the impasse is downstream of something technical I should just go learn, say which thing and why it's the crux.
