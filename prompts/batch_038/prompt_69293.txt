**Reliable by accident: a Gettier case that survives the modal fixes**

Gettier's job-interview case and his Ford case have the same shape: a justified belief whose truth rides on a fact the justification doesn't track. Since then people have sorted these into cases where something goes wrong mid-chain and luck repairs it, and cases like Goldman's fake barn county, where the belief is formed perfectly well but the surroundings are hostile. Zagzebski's diagnosis is that they're all double-luck cases — first bad luck makes the justification point at something false, then more luck makes the belief come out true. The repairs have mostly been either etiological (no inference through a false lemma) or modal (the belief has to be safe or sensitive: it couldn't easily have been false, or the subject wouldn't have held it if it were false).

My case is meant to pass all of these and still not be knowledge. Priya works the night shift at the same sort of warehouse. She glances at the panel on freezer 4, which reads −18, and forms the belief that the freezer is at −18. It is. What nobody knows is that the probe has a manufacturing defect that makes it report four degrees too warm, and that the display unit's firmware — a version that was certified as fine — subtracts four degrees from whatever it receives. Both faults are constant offsets. They cancel across the whole range. The assembly is as a matter of fact perfectly accurate and will stay that way until someone replaces one of the parts. Priya has no reason to suspect either fault; both units passed inspection; she's read that panel the same way for two years.

Run the tests. No false lemma — she infers nothing, she just reads it. Safety: in the nearby worlds where she reads the panel, the reading is right, so the belief couldn't easily have been false. Sensitivity: if the freezer hadn't been at −18, the panel wouldn't have shown −18. Reliability: the process is 100% accurate in the actual world. On a virtue-style reading she exercises exactly the competences she normally exercises and they succeed because she exercised them. And yet I'm confident she doesn't know the freezer is at −18. What seems to matter is that the panel's accuracy is a coincidence of two independent breakdowns — and that fact leaves no trace in the modal profile of her belief.

A variant, to isolate the point. Suppose instead the cancellation is local: the probe's error grows the colder it gets, and the display's subtraction only kicks in below a threshold, and the two line up exactly at −18 and nowhere else. Now the panel is accurate at the actual temperature and wrong everywhere nearby, so the belief is unsafe, and everyone I've asked agrees she doesn't know. But my own verdict on the two versions is the same, and the same strength — which is odd, since the modal accounts predict a sharp difference between them.

So here's what I want to argue. The luck in a Gettier case isn't always luck about the belief's modal standing. In the first version the luck is *standing* rather than *episodic*: it's built into how the world happens to be arranged, so it doesn't show up in nearby worlds at all, and any account that defines its anti-luck condition over nearby worlds will miss it. What's doing the work seems to be that the reliability has the wrong history — nobody designed it, nobody maintains it, nobody could cite it as a reason to trust the panel. If that's right, the anti-luck and ability conditions need to be sensitive to the provenance of a source's reliability, not just to the counterfactual behaviour of the believer.

Objections I know about and haven't solved. (a) Contamination: my "she doesn't know" intuition may just come from my knowing about the two faults; a reader told only what Priya is told might happily ascribe knowledge, in which case my case is an artifact of how I described it. (b) Truetemp in disguise: someone will say this is Lehrer's case again — reliability without grounding — rather than a Gettier problem; my reply is that Priya has ordinary evidence and an ordinary perceptual route and Truetemp has neither, but I'm not sure that difference carries the weight. (c) Stakes: whether she knows sounds different if the freezer holds vaccine versus frozen peas, and if the verdict is stakes-sensitive my case is doing something other than what I claimed.
