Been a backend engineer for about 12 years, no philosophy background, so forgive the framing. There's a pattern I keep hitting at work and at home and I don't have a word for it.

The good version of an activity only exists while it's technically not allowed. When I'm supposed to be closing tickets, I'll happily burn an hour reading the source of some library we depend on, or restructuring code nobody asked me to touch — completely absorbing, zero effort. Then a manager notices and makes it official: 20% time, logged in the same tracker, mentioned in the same review. Same activity, same hour, same me, and within a few weeks it feels exactly like the tickets did. My actual leisure has drifted the other way — dashboard for my runs, spreadsheet for the books I read — and a Sunday with nothing scheduled on it just feels flat, like a day that didn't happen.

So I think these are one thing in two costumes: the enjoyment seems to need the activity to be unaccounted-for, and the moment it gets counted or permited it drains out. I know it's somewhere near the old idea that certain religious attitudes made work into a virtue and idleness into a sin, and near whatever psychologists call it when paying people for something they loved ruins the thing — but neither of those quite lands, because I'm not exhausted and I'm not trying to monetize my hobbies, I actually like the tracking. Is there an established concept for this, in philosophy or sociology or anywhere else? And specifically: is the second move — deliberately making something unauthorized again so it becomes fun — a known thing, or am I inventing it? If nothing fits, coin a term and give me the edges: what counts, what's a nearby case that doesn't, how it differs from plain burnout or from boredom.

Also tell me how confident you are in what you say here, and point at the single claim you're least sure of.
