we have to write a short opinion piece for my econ class and I picked rent control. basically everyone just repeated the textbook line — a price ceiling causes a shortage, builders stop building, deadweight loss, done. I want to argue something narrower and weirder: in a city where zoning already makes new construction basically impossible, the supply curve is nearly vertical, so the shortage arguement has nothing to bite on. the usual triangle shrinks toward nothing and what's left is a straight transfer from landlords to sitting tenants, which turns it into a distribution question instead of an efficiency one. can you build that argument as strongly as you can? it should do the diagram-in-words part properly rather than just asserting it. then face up to the two objections I know my teacher is gonna raise: that the units end up mismatched (retired couple in a four bedroom they lucked into in 1998 while a family of five rents a one bedroom), and that when a landlord can't respond on quantity they respond on quality instead. don't just name those, show me whether the position actually survives them.
