I am preparing the written position statement I will defend before my doctoral committee, and, quite separately, I author causation reports for an accident-reconstruction consultancy. **The package.**

1. The relata of causation are concrete, datable events — particular happenings in the world's history, not variable-settings, not aspects of happenings, and not contrasts between them. I see no reason to relativize causation to a granularity of description at the metaphysical level, whatever we do at the level of inquiry. Accordingly, for any two concrete events, there is a determinate fact of the matter whether the first caused the second.

2. Causation is the transitive closure of counterfactual dependence between distinct events. Dependence is not necessary for causation, and transitivity is what allows me to preserve the intuitive verdicts in cases where a standby would have taken over had the actual cause been absent.

3. Counterfactuals are evaluated non-backtracking, on a Lewisian similarity ordering: the closest worlds in which the antecedent holds match our world exactly up to a moment just before the antecedent event, depart from it by a single small and localized violation of law, and obey the actual laws thereafter.

4. The asymmetry of counterfactual dependence — that the future depends counterfactually on the past and not conversely — is grounded in the low-entropy boundary condition of the early universe together with the statistical-mechanical improbability of entropy-decreasing histories. I take this and item 3 to be two halves of one picture: the miracle in item 3 is small precisely because entropy-increasing divergence is cheap and entropy-decreasing convergence is not.

5. Absences are not events. No omission, no failure to occur, and no negative state of affairs is ever a cause or an effect.

6. Prevention and double prevention are nevertheless genuine causation, and double prevention is my principal argument against process or conserved-quantity accounts. My standing example: an escort pilot shoots down a missile that was about to shoot down a bomber, and the bomber goes on to reach its target. The pilot's firing caused the bomber's arrival. No process or conserved quantity runs from the pilot's firing to the bomber, so any account requiring such a process must deny the causal claim, and I treat that as a refutation of the account.

7. I also accept the interventionist criterion of causal relevance: X is causally relevant to Y just in case some intervention on X would change Y, where an intervention is characterized structurally — it changes X, it is not itself brought about by anything that affects Y otherwise than through X, and it leaves everything off a directed path from X to Y as it actually is — and where an intervention need only be coherent rather than physically realizable in the actual world. Variable sets are chosen on pragmatic grounds, but once a set is fixed the causal facts it delivers are objective, which I take to be wholly consistent with the realism of item 1. I regard interventionism as an epistemology laid over my counterfactualism rather than as a rival metaphysics: an intervention is simply a disciplined way of making a counterfactual antecedent true.

8. Causal direction is not explained by temporal order. It is read off the asymmetry secured by items 3 and 4.

9. A case I keep coming back to. A municipal pumping station holds mains pressure with two units. P1 is the duty pump and holds the mains at 4 bar. P2 is a variable-speed standby whose controller raises its output only when pressure falls below 3 bar, and which saturates at 3.5 bar. On the night in question P1 runs normally, the mains sit at 4 bar from midnight to 6 a.m., and P2 stays idle. Had P1 failed, pressure would have sagged, P2 would have cut in around 3 a.m., and the mains would have been held at 3.5 bar until dawn. I want to say all three of the following about that night: (a) "P1's running caused the state of the mains between midnight and 6 a.m.," since the pressure the mains actually had is a fact about that very state and it would have been different without P1; (b) "P1's running did not cause the state of the mains between midnight and 6 a.m.," since that state included the mains' being pressurized above the 3 bar threshold and they would have been pressurized above it anyway, courtesy of P2; and (c) "P1's running caused the fourth floor's having water at 3 a.m."
