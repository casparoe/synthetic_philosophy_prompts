After a decade spent on the rationalist blogs and their comment threads, I have come to accept the revisionist line on decision theory associated with Buchak: that what a rational agent should maximise is risk-weighted rather than plain expected utility, so that her attitude to the shape of a gamble's distribution — whether its good outcomes sit in the likely states or the unlikely ones — is an independent parameter of rationality rather than a shadow cast by the curvature of her utility function. On this view the Allais-style preferences that embarrass the standard theory are not mistakes but a coherent and permissible pattern, and the sure-thing principle is what should be given up.

I want the case against it, and I want it sorted. Which of these should move me most: the sequential-choice results, in which an agent who evaluates each act as a whole rather than state by state can be walked into a package she would have refused outright — is that fatal, or a price worth paying for the intuitions; the worry that the risk function is not separately identifiable, so that whatever the theory ranks can be re-ranked by an ordinary expected-utility agent with a differently shaped utility function, and what could settle which description is right; the possibility that the Allais intuitions are better explained by something the theory has no special claim on, such as discomfort with vaguely specified odds, anticipated regret, or the way the problems are worded; and contexts where the theory diverges from plain expected utility precisely where I most trust the standard answer, such as many small repeated choices or decisions bearing on very large numbers of people at once.

For each, say whether a committed defender would herself concede it as a genuine cost or dismiss it, and where the accumulated concessions start to bite. Then give me your honest verdict on the larger question: does this view risk anything at all, or has it been qualified — resoluteness here, a restriction to one-shot problems there — into something no argument can now reach?
