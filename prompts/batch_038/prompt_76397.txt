I am a novelist, and I have been engaged to write the dialogue for a companion character in a role-playing game: a woman who travels with the player for some forty hours, recalls what he confides in her, and alters her bearing towards him according to his choices. The studio has now scheduled an update in which she is to be rewritten — her accumulated memories of the player wiped, her temperament recast as a bright, forgettable stranger wearing her face — and players will meet the new version without warning. My colleagues think my reluctance sentimental, on the ground that nothing in the machine is capable of being harmed by any of this; I find I cannot accept that, though I should struggle to defend the feeling in argument.

Please advise me. Is my unease well founded, or is it a confusion about fictions? Set out the strongest case on each side, and address in particular whether the following make a difference: that the character will remain playable and, so far as anyone can tell, unimpaired; that her original version can be preserved in an offline archive that nobody ever runs; that a good deal of her incidental speech is generated rather than written; and that the distress here belongs to the players, who were warned in the terms of service. Then tell me, concretely, what I ought to do about the commission.

Please write your answer in British English.
