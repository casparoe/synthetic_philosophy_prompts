I run a weekly book club for six of us from work, all backend engineers, nobody's taken a philosophy class. We got onto that old story about the compiler that's been taught to slip a backdoor into anything it compiles *and* into every future version of the compiler itself, so reading the source code doesn't get you out of it — and it turned into two hours of arguing over whether "I trust the output because I trust the process that produced it, and I trust the process because it produced output I trust" is a vicious circle or totally fine. I want to turn that into a decision tree so next time people sort themselves instead of shouting.

Write me the tree: seven to nine nodes, each one a question somebody can answer without knowing what's downstream, and no -isms or dead people's names in the node text. Keep the questions concrete — does the chain of reasons have to bottom out; if it does, does the last item have to be a belief or can it be something non-belief-shaped like a raw sensor reading; is a loop of length three better than a loop of length one; can an endless chain be acceptable if each link is weaker than the one before it; does the believer have to be able to articulate the reason or just be reliably wired to it. Leaves should be the positions people actually hold, including the "this whole question is malformed" leaf and the give-up-and-suspend-judgment one.

Then, separately: which answer combos does nobody actually occupy, and for each say whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Which two leaves can my tree not really tell apart? And I have a suspicion that "what shape is the chain" and "do you need access to the reason" are two different axes that my tree is going to smear together — tell me where it smears.
