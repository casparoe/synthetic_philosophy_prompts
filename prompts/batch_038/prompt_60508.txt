Write me a short story, maybe 1500-2000 words, set in a court that evaluates character the way a materials lab evaluates steel. Two defendants both stood there and did nothing while a kid was drowning in a lock — but the scans come out differently. In one of them, the disposition to help was present and fully wired right up to the moment, and then something inside him deleted it a half-second early, so it was simply gone when the moment came. In the other, the disposition never went anywhere; it's intact, sitting there in perfect working order, but a second thing clamped on top of it and stopped the whole sequence from ever running through to behavior. The jury has to work out whether these two men are in the same moral position, and I want the story to make the reader feel the difference between a disposition that was removed before it could show itself and one that was blocked from showing itself while remaining fully there — without naming the technical vocabulary, just dramatizing it through testimony, scan printouts, cross-examination. Have the case turn on a demonstration the defense runs: they release the clamp on the second defendant in the courtroom and everyone watches what he does, but with the first defendant there's nothing left to release, only the question of whether you could regrow the thing and whether the regrown version would even be his. I'd also like one juror to hold out for the view that a disposition which can never, ever manifest isnt really a disposition at all, and lose the argument. Keep the sci-fi machinery light — the weight should be on the people arguing.
