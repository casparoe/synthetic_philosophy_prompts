Our department is running a one-off graduate seminar on the ethics of testimony, and the set reading for the opening week is Augustine's pair of treatises on lying: the early *De mendacio*, and the later *Contra mendacium*, written when a group of Catholics proposed feigning Priscillianist belief in order to infiltrate the sect and convict it from within. My students arrive assuming the interesting question is when a lie is excusable. Augustine's question is prior to that one: whether an utterance is a lie at all, which he settles by the relation between what the speaker holds in mind and what the speaker signifies — and he sets jokes aside at the very outset precisely because their manner announces that no deceit is intended. I want an instrument that forces that ordering on the seminar, rather than another essay about absolutism.

Please construct a decision tree that takes a speaker who has just made an utterance and lands them on a classification under Augustine's account. The requirements are as follows.

**1. Structure.** Present the tree as an indented outline. Every node must be a single question phrased in ordinary language about the speaker's own situation: what they believed, what they meant their words to be taken for, whom they were addressing, what they were trying to bring about in that person's mind. Every branch must be labelled with the claim that separates it from its siblings. Aim for eight to twelve leaves. Each leaf should carry a name — Augustine's own term where he has one, and the word *unnamed* where he supplies none, with a note on whether he simply never faced the case or faced it and declined to name it — plus one sentence on what the speaker has to accept once they land there.

**2. Prohibitions.** No node may ask about outcomes: nothing about harm avoided, benefit conferred, proportionality, whether anyone was actually deceived, or whether the lie was worth it. No node may ask the speaker to consult their own moral commitments, and none may use Augustine's technical vocabulary; if a question can only be asked with a term of art, the tree is asking the wrong question. The sequence of nodes must follow the order in which the treatises raise their questions — manner of utterance first, then the mind-to-signification relation, then the addressee and what is intended for them, and only then any question of gravity.

**3. Honest gaps.** Mark any node where Augustine's criteria do not actually determine an answer — ambiguous or literally true but calculated speech, deliberate silence, an utterance with no addressee capable of belief — as UNDERDETERMINED, and say what stipulation would be needed to close it and whether he has the resources to supply one. Then list every pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and say what further question would separate them. Finally, give three combinations of answers that are logically available but land nowhere: for each, say whether the cell is empty because the view is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or because Augustine simply never thought of it.

**4. Test cases.** Trace the path, node by node, for each of the following, and state the verdict.

(a) An investigative journalist who joins the organisation she is reporting on and, when asked point-blank, says she shares its convictions. She does not.
(b) A witness under cross-examination who answers every question literally and truly, in an order and with emphases chosen so that the court will draw a conclusion he knows to be false.
(c) A parent who tells a four-year-old that a parcel on the stairs was left overnight by a visitor who comes once a year, intending the child to believe it and enjoying the belief.
(d) A man at the door of a house in which his quarry is hiding in the cellar, asked directly by an armed pursuer where that person is, who answers that he is not here.
(e) A comedian's grossly inflated claim about his own evening, delivered in a register that makes the inflation obvious to the room.
(f) A colleague who says the meeting is at three, believing it to be at four; the meeting is in fact at three.
(g) The reading Jerome proposed for the Antioch incident — that the confrontation was a piece of theatre arranged between the two apostles for the benefit of Jewish and Gentile Christians — which Augustine rejected on the ground that admitting a single falsehood into Scripture collapses its authority. Show which node in your tree does the rejecting, and why that node has to come before any question about the good the arrangement would have done.

**5. Diagnosis.** Identify the one node whose alteration would convert your tree into an ordinary moderate account on which some lies are permissible. State exactly what would have to be inserted there, and what Augustine would say he had lost by the insertion. Do not soften this: the point of the exercise is for the seminar to see where the absolutism is structurally located, not distributed across the whole tree.

**6. Severity.** After the classification, add a short second section ordering the lies your tree has identified from worst to least bad, built out of the same materials — the object of the falsehood, whether it injures someone, whether it serves the speaker, whether it shields anyone. Where an ordering is your reconstruction rather than something Augustine states, mark it in a bracket as such.

Write the whole thing in British English. Do not append a bibliography, a reading list, or chapter and section references; if you are unsure of a citation, leave it out and describe the passage instead. Length: as much as the tree and the seven walk-throughs require, but the tree itself should fit on a single handout page.
