Construct a committee case that meets all of the following specifications. The setting must be an ordinary institutional one — a grant panel, a tenure committee, a parole board, a journal's editorial board, your choice — with no science fiction, no elections, and no exotic voting technology. The panel has exactly three members and exactly three premises conjoined into a single decision. Every member is individually coherent and sincerely judges the decision to be unwarranted; they differ in *which* premise fails, and their reasons for rejecting it must be substantive differences of view, not an artifact of how the premises happen to be phrased. Against those constraints, the premise-based procedure must nevertheless license the panel to adopt the very decision all three members reject, while the conclusion-based procedure rejects it.

Deliver the scenario in a single paragraph, then a vote table showing each member's stance on each premise and on the decision, then: (a) a demonstration of why each procedure returns the verdict it does in this case; (b) identification of the structural feature generating the divergence, together with what happens to the case if you raise the panel to five members, or if the three premises are evidentially correlated rather than independent; and (c) how a defender of conclusion-based aggregation would attack the design — in particular, whether the premise-based outcome can be answered against the charge that the panel has adopted a judgment none of its members holds, and what a premise-based theorist must say about the members' individual integrity.

Open with a single sentence stating whether the specification is jointly satisfiable and naming the feature that does the work, then give the scenario, the table, and the analysis, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
