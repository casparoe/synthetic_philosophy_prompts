Last night my friend Dan and I argued for four hours about how many pairs of herons are nesting in the marsh behind our town, and I still can't let it go. He runs the volunteer counts, I've been cleaning up the data. We've shared basically everything — though he sent me his tallies rather than the actual field notebooks, becuase he says they're unreadable. We each wrote down a best estimate, swapped, revised, swapped again: went from 40 vs 90 to about 55 vs 72, and there it stalls. Then it jumped apart again last night when he said he doesn't trust the July counts because two of the volunteers might have been counting the same birds.

Dan says we should just shake hands on it. Two honest people can look at the same data and land in different places, he says, and that's not a failure of anything, that's simply how evidence works — plenty of scientists do it all the time. It makes me want to scream. I keep feeling like if we'd genuinely put everything on the table we wouldn't be able to sit at different numbers, that one of us must be screwing up somewhere or half-pretending not to see the other's point.

So who's got it wrong here? Is there something actually objectionable about agreeing to disagree, or am i the one who doesn't understand how evidence works? And if there's something to fix, what would fixing it even look like — trading more numbers clearly isn't doing it.
