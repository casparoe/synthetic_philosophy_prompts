For the past three years I have kept a private notebook in which, whenever I face a decision I suspect I will not follow through on, I write out my reasons at length: the case for leaving my job, the case for ending a long-standing arrangement with a partner, the case for giving up a habit I know is doing me harm. The entries are careful, and while I am writing them they are entirely convincing. Within a fortnight the decision has invariably dissolved, and when I go back the reasons read like something assembled to order rather than arrived at. I have therefore begun treating the notebook as evidence against myself, which has created a difficulty I cannot think my way out of.

Last month a friend who has known me since we were children told me that I always know the answer before I begin the list, and that the list exists to make the answer bearable. I found this insulting and said so. She replied that my finding it insulting was precisely what she would have expected if she were right, and that any reaction I produce will confirm her rather than test her. At that point I noticed that her claim can no longer be wrong, and my denial can no longer count in my favour, and that neither of us has said anything capable of being checked.

So my question is this. Is there any way, from the inside, to tell whether a belief of mine is being produced by my evidence or by my preference for it? It seems to me that whatever test I devise, I am the one who administers it and interprets the result, which is the very thing my friend says makes my protest worthless. But that cannot be right either: if a test is disqualified simply because the person taking it also scores it, then no amount of reflection could ever establish anything about oneself, and I refuse to accept that. Where exactly does my reasoning go wrong, and is there a way of putting the question to myself that is not rigged in advance in favour of whoever is asking?
