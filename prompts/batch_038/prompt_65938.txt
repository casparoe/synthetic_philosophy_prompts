I'm trying to work out what I actually believe about when a promise binds, going case by case, and I keep hitting a wall. Here are my verdicts.

- I promised my grandmother, the day before she died, that I'd never let her letters get published. Binds, absolutely, still, 40 years on. Nobody alive can hold me to it and she can't be affected by it either way.
- Same shape, but the person is alive: my father made me promise I'd never put my mother in a care home. She has advanced dementia now, doesn't know who I am, and the home would clearly be better for her. I think that promise doesn't bind, or binds a lot less. I cannot make these two line up. Neither case has anyone in a position to complain.
- A guy at work got me to promise to cover his shifts by hinting he'd otherwise mention something I did to the manager. Void, obviously.
- But my brother in law, who I know full well is running the guilt routine on me every single time, got me to promise the loan. That one binds. Duress voids, manipulation doesn't, and I dont know why.
- I promised myself no drinking until March. I think it binds, and I think the whole point is that I can't release myself, because the me who'd release it is exactly the one being bound against.
- Raised my hand at an auction. Never said a word. Bound.
- Told a contractor "yeah, whatever, I'll cover the extra" when he found rot under the floorboards, purely to keep him working, fully intending to haggle later. Not fine. I'm on the hook for it.
- Promised my six year old we'd go to the pool Saturday, then it poured and I was knackered. Binds, and if anything harder than the same promise to an adult — which strikes me as backwards from what any planning based story would predict.
- The venue burnt down before the event I'd promised to cater. No performance owed, but I feel a residue: I owe them the phone call, and probably the deposit back plus something.
- "I promise I'll name my firstborn after you," to a friend at 1am, both of us laughing. Not binding. But something did happen. He'd feel a bit let down if the kid ended up a Gary, and I'd feel a bit cheap.

My own tentative principle, which I know is broken: a promise binds in proportion to how much the other person has rebuilt their plans on top of my word. I hand them my will, they build on it, and the bond just is the building. It gets the contractor and the shift cover about right. It totally whiffs on my grandmother (she stopped planning the next morning, so the bond should have decayed to nothing) and it predicts the six year old is the weakest case when I think it's one of the strongest.

So: is there a principle, or a small set of clauses, that gets most of these? State it precisely, clause by clause, and say which of my cases is doing the work for each clause — I want to see where the pressure is coming from and what would collapse if I dropped that clause. Then tell me what it says about cases I haven't raised, including at least one where I'd be genuinely surprised by the verdict, and be straight with me about which of my verdicts it can't save and which one I should give up. The grandmother/dementia pair is the one I most want cracked open: if no principle can hold both, tell me which of the two intuitions is the illusion and why I have it.

I don't want a tour of who in the literature said what — no names, no reading list. I want the principle and the reasoning.
