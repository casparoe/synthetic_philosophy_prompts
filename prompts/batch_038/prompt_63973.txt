For the past two years my coursework has been in behavior analysis: operant definitions of reinforcement and punishment, escape and avoidance contingencies, motivating operations, the status of private events, and the verbal-behavior material on mands, tacts, and rule-governed versus contingency-shaped behavior. I can read a single-subject design and I can argue about whether an inner state counts as an explanatory fiction. This term I am also sitting in on a philosophy of mind seminar whose central topic is the badness of pain, and I would like the two vocabularies brought into contact as carefully as possible.

The material I want translated is pain asymbolia: the syndrome in which patients with lesions around the insula and its limbic connections — and, in a related literature, some patients after cingulotomy — say that they still feel and can localize the pain but no longer mind it. They do not withdraw, they do not avoid, and they sometimes respond with an affect that looks incongruous, such as smiling. Philosophers treat this as evidence that unpleasantness is a separable component of pain rather than part of its felt quality, and the dispute that turns on it is roughly between views on which pain is bad in virtue of its intrinsic phenomenal character and views on which pain is bad in virtue of being disliked or disposed against. On the first, asymbolia is a puzzle; on the second, it is a prediction.

Please restate that dispute in behavior-analytic vocabulary, and then give me the residue report. Concretely:

(a) A claim-by-claim translation. For each of the following, give the counterpart in my vocabulary or state that there is none: pain is bad; its badness rides on its unpleasantness; unpleasantness is separable from the sensory-discriminative dimension; the asymbolic patient still feels pain; the pain that patient feels is not bad.

(b) An aversiveness audit. List the functional criteria I would actually apply to certify a stimulus as a punisher — immediate suppression, acquisition of escape and avoidance, conditioned suppression through pairing, motivating-operation effects on unrelated behavior, allocation under concurrent schedules — and say for each whether the asymbolia data settle it, leave it open, or cut against it. I am genuinely unsure whether the case reports document asymbolics acquiring avoidance; I would much rather you say that than supply a confident answer.

(c) The residue, sorted into what translates cleanly, what translates only under distortion (say what the distortion is), and what has no expression at all. Then the judgment I actually want: is the inexpressible portion a limitation of a framework that defines value entirely by effect on behavior, or evidence that the intrinsicalist claim — that pain would be bad even if it never moved anything — does not have determinate content?

Two constraints. Do not smuggle behavior-analytic commitments in under the heading of translation; where the framework forces a redescription of the datum rather than a rendering of it, mark that explicitly. And do not settle the question by declaring the phenomenology irrelevant, since that is one of the candidate verdicts rather than a neutral starting point.

One further suspicion to test, which I expect you may want to push back on: it looks to me as though the dispositional side of the debate is simply behavior analysis in philosophical dress, so that the translation succeeds by absorbing one party and leaving the other unable to speak. Tell me whether that is correct, or whether that account contains something my vocabulary cannot state.

No bibliography, citations, or reading list — I want the argument, not the references.
