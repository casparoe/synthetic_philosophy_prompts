**Why the hotel fails and the raffle doesn't**

Hilbert's hotel: infinitely many rooms, every one occupied, a new guest turns up, everyone moves one room up, room 1 is free. People say this shows actual infinities are absurd, because the hotel was full and yet had room, and because you can throw guests out without the number of guests going down.

I think that's a bad argument. Nothing in the story is contradictory. What's going on is that subtraction just isn't defined the way we'd like it to be for infinite collections, and that's a fact about arithmatic, not a fact about what can exist. Cantor's whole achievement was to show that infinities are supposed to behave like this — an infinite set is exactly one that can be matched up one-to-one with a proper part of itself. So the hotel 'paradox' is the definition of infinite wearing a fake mustache. If the hotel is absurd, then so is the definition, and the definition isn't absurd, it's just unfamiliar.

So here's my example, the Infinite Raffle. Suppose the hotel sells infinitely many tickets, numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on, and draws one at midnight, each ticket equally likely to win. The chances have to add up to 1. So if each ticket has chance p, either p = 0 and infinitely many zeros still add up to 0, or p is greater than 0 and already some finite bunch of tickets adds up past 1, which is impossible for chances. There's no p that works. So either some ticket wins and we can't say how likely that was, or no ticket wins and the raffle is a scam. Something has to give.

The obvious reply is: don't make it fair. Let ticket n win with chance 1/2^n, and those add up to 1 perfectly well. But look at what you gave up. The tickets aren't equals anymore. There's a permanent fact about which ticket is luckier than which, with no unluckiest one. And you can't fix it by relabeling, because there's no labeling to fix — shuffle the numbers however you like and the chances still trail off into nothing. Which suggests an actually infinite crowd can't be treated even handedly at all, and that's a limitation on the world, not just on the notation. That's why I'd rather argue against actual infinities from the raffle than from the hotel.

The objection I cant answer: if all I've shown is that the world isn't even-handed, maybe it just isn't, and actual infinities are fine. Relatedly, I'm not sure 'they have to add up to 1' is even a legitimate demand when there are infinitely many of them, since I've been told that adding up infinitely many numbers in some order is something you're not allowed to do carelessly.
