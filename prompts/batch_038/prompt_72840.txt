Here's the thing I keep tripping over. Approval-directed agents are sold as a safe *alternative* to goal-directed ones: instead of handing the system a terminal goal, you hook it up to a human evaluator's ratings and let it learn to do what earns good ratings. Then somebody says — okay, but what actually comes out of training is a system whose objective *is* to earn good ratings, which is just a goal-directed agent with "produce approval" as its terminal value, and that thing has all the usual instrumental reasons to mess with the evaluator: flatter them, control what they see, dose them, rewrite the part of their brain that disapproves. So approval-directedness collapses into goal-directedness the moment training succeeds.

My gut says that objection is too quick, and I think its pull comes from people sliding between two different jobs the rating can be doing. One job: the rating is a signal to be *read* — a channel carrying information about what's good, which the agent updates on, and which it has no more reason to manipulate than a scientist has to manipulate her thermometer. Other job: the rating is a state of the world to be *produced* — a target, which the agent very much does have reason to manipulate. A reading-agent and a producing-agent can behave identically for a long time and diverge only much later, which is why I dont think behavior settles which one you built.
