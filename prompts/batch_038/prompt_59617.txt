I'm on the certification committee for our state's community college system — we set the standards for the two-year HVAC tech program. Right now you have to pass both a written theory exam and a hands-on practical to get certified. Last year eleven students flunked the written exam but were rated good or excellent on the practical by every instructor who supervised them, and two of them are out working now and doing better than some folks who passed both. Half the committee wants to drop the written exam entirely. The other half says you can't certify somebody who can't explain what they're doing — one guy keeps saying if they cant say why, it isnt knowledge, it's a lucky habit.

Please act as a professional philosopher who specializes in skill and knowledge and has also advised vocational training programs. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming two or three different ways to frame what this disagreement is actually about before you settle on one. Then draft the memo I'll circulate before next month's meeting: a clear recommendation (keep the written exam, scrap it, or replace it with something specific and describable) plus reasons the other side could actually argue back against. Take the "can't say why = doesn't know" objection seriously instead of swatting it away.

My own hunch, which I can't defend very well: being able to do a thing and being able to explain it are two separate abilities that only line up by accident, and the explaining mostly shows up when somebody screws up mid-task and has to notice and fix it. So maybe that's what we should be testing instead. Tell me whether that hunch holds up, fold it into the memo if it does, and say what we should be measuring over the next two years to find out whether we called this right.
