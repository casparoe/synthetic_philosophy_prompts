I want a case analysis that runs in both directions: a real episode in animal-communication research examined through a specific theory of mental content, and then the theory tested against what the episode actually delivered.

The episode. Field work on East African vervet monkeys, beginning with Struhsaker's descriptions and culminating in the Seyfarth, Cheney and Marler playback experiments published in 1980, produced what became the canonical example of semantic communication in a non-human animal. Three acoustically distinct alarm calls are given to three predator classes — a bark to leopards, a short tonal call to raptors, a chutter to snakes — and each is met with a different escape response: up into a tree for leopards, into cover while scanning upward for raptors, bipedal scanning of the ground for snakes. The researchers recorded the calls and replayed them over a loudspeaker to wild groups with no predator anywhere near; listeners performed the response appropriate to the call type. The calls are arbitrary with respect to what elicits them — they do not imitate the predators or their sounds — and the result was read as showing that a free-ranging primate has signals that designate classes of external objects, which is how the vervet system entered the textbook literature and the debate about the origins of human language.

Two complications I want taken seriously rather than smoothed over. First, the field's own working concept turned out not to be a semantic one: Macedonia and Evans proposed "functional reference," cashed out entirely as production specificity (the signal is given to a narrow range of stimuli) plus perception specificity (the signal elicits, absent the stimulus, the response the stimulus would), with no commitment to content, truth, or representation. Second, a 2015 quantitative re-analysis found the three predator-associated call types distinguishable, but with genuine acoustic overlap once calls given in aggressive contexts were folded in — notably between female calls given to snakes, to raptors, and during aggression — and concluded that both the caller's appraisal of the situation and the caller's internal state shape call structure and use. A further wrinkle, which I state only as reported: young vervets appear to call at a much broader range of stimuli than adults do, including at birds that are not raptors, with the repertoire narrowing over time. If you think that detail is load-bearing for your analysis, say so and reason conditionally about it.

The theory. Teleosemantics: the selected-effects account of content, on which a state means what it does because tracking that condition is what the state was selected to do, and where that history — not the state's present causal profile — is what licenses calling the state a misrepresentation when tracking fails. Within the family, treat the producer/consumer split as the main lever. On producer accounts, content is fixed by the selection history of the mechanism generating the sign. On consumer accounts, content is fixed by what the system that *uses* the sign needs it to have correlated with in order to perform its function; and on that view a signal may be a pushmi-pullyu representation, carrying descriptive and directive content at once, something like leopard-here and climb! in a single state.

What I want from you:

1. The mapping, before any evaluation. For the leopard bark, identify the producing mechanism, the consuming mechanism, the correlation each version of the theory makes essential, and the resulting content — separately for producer and for consumer teleosemantics — and say whether the two converge or diverge here. Note that the stock illustration of the view, the frog's retinal bug-detector, is an intra-organism case in which producer and consumer sit in one nervous system and share one selection history. The vervet case splits them across two organisms with two histories, and possibly across a co-evolved signaling system as a whole. Say what that split does to the account, and whether "the consumer" here is the listener's auditory system, the listener's escape circuitry, or the dyad.

2. The falsity condition. State, for each of the three calls and each version of the theory, what would have to obtain for the call to be *false* rather than merely mistimed, degraded, or deployed in the wrong context. Then decide what the theory makes of the aggressive-context calls: misrepresentation, a second content, graded content, or evidence that the proper function was never predator-indication at all.

3. The question the empirical literature raised and left open — whether the call picks out the predator or the escape response — and whether teleosemantics resolves it or restates it. If the consumer answer is that the content is pushmi-pullyu, say whether that is a resolution or a redescription, and whether any of it is testable against the overlap data rather than merely compatible with it.

4. Lag, absence, and exploitation. Selection history looks backward. Work out what the theory is committed to in the case of a population whose habitat no longer contains leopards, a caller that has never encountered one, and a caller that elicits the escape response for its own advantage with no predator present. Does the theory return a determinate verdict in each case, or does it run out?

5. The verdict, both ways. Which features of the episode teleosemantics explains that functional reference cannot, which features it misses or misdescribes, and where it is simply silent. Then: does the episode support the theory — because it captures the intuition that made the calls look meaningful in the first place and supplies a principled account of misfire that the field's own vocabulary lacks — or does it undercut the theory, given that all the empirical work was done without content talk, and given that the theory's output shifts depending on which variant one adopts? Say plainly whether this is a confirmation, a strain, or a case the theory never anticipated.

My own hunch is that the consumer version is where an inter-organism case like this earns its keep, and that the producer version quietly collapses into a covariance account once the sign leaves the body that makes it. Tell me whether that is an insight or a confusion.
