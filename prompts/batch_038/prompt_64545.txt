I sit on the scientific advisory panel for a state environmental agency and we have to settle a fight before our meeting in two weeks. Right now our hazard assessments require each outside expert to hand in a single number for every risk parameter, and staff averages them into the figure we sign off on. Two of our toxicologists want to instead submit a lower and upper bound, plus a short note on how much relevant evidence actually backs the width of the range — their case is that for one particular contaminant the animal data is so thin that any point estimate is fake precision dressed up as a measurement.

The rest of the panel pushes back on two grounds. First, the statute we report under asks for the agency's best estimate, so ranges don't dissolve the problem, they just dump it on staff who have to pick a number anyway, and nobody elected them. Second, one member says he's personally seen assessments where more data came in and the reported range got *wider* rather than narrower, which he takes to mean interval reporting is worse than useless. Nobody has yet said what we'd even do with a mix of ranges from some experts and points from others.

Could you draft the memo I'll circulate? It should come down on what the panel adopts for this contaminant — allow ranges, keep point estimates, or some middle option — and it needs to be operational: what staff concretely does with each submission, and how a bound gets turned into the single figure the cleanup standard rests on, or why that step should be refused. Take the widening-range objection head-on rather than swatting it, and give the toxicologists' argument its real weight too. A page or so is plenty, and it's fine if the answer is condition-heavy.
