In my info-cascades seminar two people got into it over the reading and I honestly couldnt tell who was winning. Setup: we'd just done the standard sequential-decision / two-urn model where everyone sees the actions of the people before them but not their private signals.

Nadia brought up her building's group chat, where basically everyone signed up for the same meal-prep service after a few people posted about it. She said that's a textbook cascade and therefor nobody in it is being dumb — each person is just updating on the revealed choices of the people ahead of them, so it's fully rational herding, no epistemic vice involved.

Owen said "rational" was doing way too much work. His point: the interesting part of the result is that it's an aggregation failure. After the first couple of moves, people stop contributing any new information to the public history, so the group can lock onto the wrong answer indefinitely with non-trivial probability even though every single member is a perfect Bayesian. He said calling that rational is like calling a traffic jam rational because each driver reacts sensibly to the car in front.

Nadia said fine, but then it's fragile — the moment somebody in the middle has genuinely strong private info (say they actually tried the service and hated it), the cascade collapses, because a strong enough signal outweighs the herd. Owen said that's exactly backwards: in the model, once you're inside a cascade you ignore your private signal *completely*, even the strongest one available, because the public history already pins the action down. The only thing that can shatter a cascade is public information, so the fragility result is about public shocks, not about one skeptic with insider knowledge.

Nadia conceded that, but said it just proves the model is an idealization that shouldn't be used to describe her group chat, where people actually talk to each other instead of merely watching each other's actions. Owen said that's a difference without a difference — talking is just a louder signal.

And finally: what are both of them missing — is there any variant of the model where Owen's "no private signal can break it" claim stops being true?
