stuck on a paragraph in Federalist 62 (the Senate one) for a chapter I'm writing on constitutional precommitment and I keep going in circles on the logic. here it is:

"Another advantage accruing from this ingredient in the constitution of the Senate is, the additional impediment it must prove against improper acts of legislation. No law or resolution can now be passed without the concurrence, first, of a majority of the people, and then, of a majority of the States. It must be acknowledged that this complicated check on legislation may in some instances be injurious as well as beneficial; and that the peculiar defense which it involves in favor of the smaller States, would be more rational, if any interests common to them, and distinct from those of the other States, would otherwise be exposed to peculiar danger. But as the larger States will always be able, by their power over the supplies, to defeat unreasonable exertions of this prerogative of the lesser States, and as the faculty and excess of law-making seem to be the diseases to which our governments are most liable, it is not impossible that this part of the Constitution may be more convenient in practice than it appears to many in contemplation."

two things I can't parse. First, that "would be more rational, if..." sentence — is Publius conceding that equal state suffrage has *no* principled justification as minority protection, on the grounds that there aren't any interests common to the small states and distinct from everyone else's, and then rescuing it purely as friction, i.e. as an impediment to lawmaking as such? If so he's defending a veto point instrumentally while admitting it doesn't track anybody's interests, and I want help stating that without overstating it, because the sentence is conditional and hedged in a way that might not license the flat-footed version.

Second, the supplies bit reads like he's smuggling in a check on the check: the lesser states' veto is itself hostage to something sitting outside the constitutional text. does that make the device self-limiting in a way that matters for my framing? my worry is that a Ulysses contract is supposed to be one agent binding *itself*, whereas here the binding is done by a different set of principals (the state legislatures) who never made the promise, and policed by a threat to cut off supplies rather than by any prior commitment — so calling it precommitment at all might be a category error. Is that a fair reading of this paragraph, or am I importing a distinction Publius wouldn't have recognized?

small thing too: my edition prints "faculty and excess of law-making" where I've seen "facility" in others — does faculty have a live 18th-c sense here, or did one of them just get canonized?
