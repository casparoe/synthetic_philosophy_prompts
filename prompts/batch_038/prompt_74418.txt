I am designing the semantics for a self-auditing knowledge base, and before I evaluate any candidate theories of truth I want the specification written down: what an adequate theory has to deliver in this setting. Please treat the setting as fixed rather than arguing me out of it.

The store holds claims in a first-order base language (arithmetic plus finitely many domain predicates). Each claim carries a status token drawn from three values: accepted, rejected, and undecided. The language also contains a status predicate applying to names of claims in the store, including names of claims that themselves contain the status predicate, including names of the claim containing the name, and including quantified generalizations ranging over the whole store. Statuses are assigned by a procedure that, given any finite batch of claims, halts and returns a status for each. New claims may be appended at any time; nothing is ever deleted.

Against that setting, write the desiderata: the phenomena the theory must explain, the cases it must classify correctly, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the properties of the base language it must preserve. For each requirement, give the case or construction that motivates it, and where a diagonal sentence does the damage, construct it rather than merely gesturing at a famous paradox. Then:

1. Classify each requirement as non-negotiable, negotiable, a prejudice of one camp masquerading as a criterion, or ill-posed as I have stated it. Repair the ill-posed ones instead of discarding them.
2. Identify every subset of the requirements that is jointly unsatisfiable, and for each give the diagonal construction and the minimal assumption doing the work, in the style of an impossibility result rather than a survey.
3. Say which single requirement excludes the most candidates, and which one I should be prepared to sacrifice.

Address five points explicitly: why a claim asserting of itself only that it is accepted is not paradoxical while its negating counterpart is, and what a theory that treats them differently owes us; why a claim of the form "if this claim is accepted, then X" compels arbitrary X under full transparency, and why the structurally identical sentence is harmless when the predicate is provability instead of truth, and what that asymmetry implies about which direction of transparency must be surrendered; whether conservativeness over the base language is a genuine requirement or a philosophical scruple, given that the obvious reason to have a status predicate is to state the store's own soundness; whether status assignment must be monotone under appending, and exactly what breaks if it is not; and whether the object language must be able to say that a given claim is undecided, and if that generates a strengthened paradox, whether revenge-resistance is a real desideratum or a demand that the theory be incomplete on pain of incoherence.

Finally, in a few sentences each, note how partial or gapped treatments, paraconsistent treatments, and revision-theoretic treatments fare against the list. You may name the standard families of solutions. No citations, no bibliography, no dates. If one of my constraints makes the project impossible in an uninteresting way, say so plainly and give the nearest coherent weakening.
