Let me preface this by saying I spent forty years in analytical chemistry, mostly teaching, and I retired fourteen months ago. Since then I've taken to reading philosophy in the afternoons, which is new for me — I always assumed it was a thing you either did properly or not at all, but I find I enjoy it even though I'm sure I'm doing it badly. Anyway. My actual problem isn't a philosophy problem exactly, or if it is I don't know the name of it. I've always logged things, it's a tic, and about a year ago I started logging my own days, partly out of curiosity and partly because I had a decision coming up and I wanted to make it on evidence rather than on whatever mood I happened to be in when the question arose. So, two channels. Channel one is an app on my phone that goes off at random times, roughly four a day, and I tap a number 1 to 7 for how the day is going so far; the rule I set myself is I have to answer within ten seconds of the beep, before I've had a chance to think about it. Channel two is a notebook — every night after dinner I write four or five sentences about the day, what happened, how it felt, and why I think it felt that way. A year of both now.

Here's what's odd: the two channels agree about the pieces and disagree completely about the whole. If something actually happened, a bad phone call with my brother, a morning when the car wouldn't start, both records mark the same day as bad, they line up fine. But ask each one what the year was like and the beeps say roughly flat, mildly positive, nothing much wrong, while the notebook says a long slow slide into being bored and a bit useless. They can't both be right, and I cant audit either one. When I reread a miserable-sounding evening entry from a day whose beeps were all fives, I can't recover the feeling at all, I just have to take the notebook's word for it, and there's no experiment I can run on it. Worse, I've come to think both channels are contaminated and I can't tell which is worse. Some of the beeps catch me mid-conversation or half asleep and I'm fairly certain I just tap whatever I tapped last time. And the notebook I write after my wife and I have talked the day over, so a good chunk of what's in there may be her version of my day in my handwriting — she decided around month two that I'm miserable since retiring and I don't know how much of that got written into the entries afterwards.

The decision I actually need to make: an old colleague wants me back in the lab three days a week from the spring, and I owe him an answer by the end of the month. My wife says obviously take it, you're climbing the walls. The beeps say there's nothing wrong with your days whatsoever. The notebook says the days are empty and you know perfectly well they are. So what do I do, and I mean practically — which of these records should I believe about whether I want this? And is there even a fact of the matter about what the year as a whole felt like that one of them is getting wrong, or is the "overall shape" question a bad question that no instrument, pointed inward or outward, could ever answer? I keep having the thought that if the honest answer is "neither record, just decide," then the whole project of trying to know your own mind is a bit of a fraud, and I'm not ready to swallow that. But maybe I should be.
