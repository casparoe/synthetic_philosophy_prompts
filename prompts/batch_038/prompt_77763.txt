For my logical empiricism seminar I have to prep something for discussion section and I'm kind of stuck — can you write me a dialogue, around 1000 words?

Setting: Vienna, early 30s. A municipal committee wants to hand apartment and building-material allocation over to a central office instead of leaving it to the market, and two people who both want working families housed are arguing about whether that proposal is even intelligible.

One is a Carnapian socialist of the scientific-world-conception sort. For her, "efficiency," "need," even "the economy" only pick anything out relative to a chosen framework; external questions about frameworks are practical proposals rather than theoretical claims; and you settle between frameworks by comparing how testable and how coordinatable they make your hypotheses, not by pretending there's a view from nowhere. The other is a Hayekian: the fatal mistake is supposing the relevant knowledge could ever be assembled in one head or one office; prices aren't a framework choice but a discovery procedure, and the planner's aggregates have already destroyed the local, time-and-place particulars the decisions actually turn on.

Neither one gets to win cheaply — no shutting the other down with "that's just a pseudo-problem," and no appeal to human nature from the other side. The Carnapian has to say what framework-relativity leaves of the claim that planning can work, and the Hayekian has to explain why her arguement doesn't also sink vaccination logistics or wartime rationing, where the knowledge looks perfectly aggregable. And I want at least one exchange on whether "no one can possess all of it" is itself an internal or an external claim — do they even agree about that? They should end unconvinced, but each naming something that would move them.
