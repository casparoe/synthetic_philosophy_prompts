Been a Moloch person for like a decade now — ever since that SSC post its basically my default explanation for any bad thing that sticks around: not villains, not stupidity, just a multipolar trap where everyone acts rationally and everyone loses. Over time I've tried to make it more than a vibe, so here's my private test. I only call something a trap if (a) everyone involved would prefer the coordinated outcome to the equilibrium, (b) no single actor can do better by unilaterally deviating, and (c) it survives full transparency — the trap still holds even if everyone knew everyone else's payoffs and intentions. And honestly I've also come to think it's immune to counterexample, since any case where people did manage to coordinate just means someone changed the game (a hegemon, a treaty, repeated interaction, an internalized norm) rather than actually beat it.

So: what would count against the whole package? Not "sometimes people are just greedy" — I want the cases, results and literatures that should genuinely move me. Cooperation that happened with no Leviathan, no repetition and no payoff change; supposed races to the bottom that turn out to have had a unilateral improvement available the whole time; and whether clause (c) is doing any work at all or just sits there to shield the thesis. Also, what does the trap diagnosis predict that a boring principal-agent or rent-seeking story doesn't, and can you point at a real situation where the two come apart and Moloch could be the one that's wrong? Rank the defeaters from "I'd stop using the word" down to "mildly embarrassing," say which ones a committed Moloch person would themselves concede versus wave off, and then give it to me straight on whether the frame has been qualified into something that can't lose — and whether that's a real problem for a view whose job is explanatory rather than predictive.
