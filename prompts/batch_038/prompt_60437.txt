I work in compliance at a logistics company and I've been reading Dennett on the side for fun, mostly the bit about the three stances. Could you write me a parody of a completed internal audit form — call it something like a quarterly belief-and-desire register — for a company that's been told to certify what its customer-service chatbot believes? Around 600 words, laid out like a real form: numbered sections, checkboxes, blank fields, an auditor's notes in the margins.

The jokes should all come from the philosophy being right, not from AI-doom stuff. So: checking the design-stance box voids the section where you list beliefs; the belief inventory has a column for "holds under rephrasing" and it's all nos; the desire rows contradict each other; the whole exercise is justified by predictive usefulness and the auditor can't demonstrate it beats just knowing what the thing was trained on; and there's a threshold question about whether any of this differs from a thermostat that nobody has managed to answer since the form was invented.

Keep it deadpan, no winking, and let one section quietly confuse having beliefs with being conscious so you feel how easy that is to do. Funny to someone who knows the literature, readable to someone who doesnt.
