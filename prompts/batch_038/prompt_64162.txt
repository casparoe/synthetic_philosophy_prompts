Okay so I'm a junior and I'm work study at our county historical society — mostly filing and scanning, but I sit in on the interpretation committee and take the notes. They've asked me to draft a memo for next month's meeting, and my public history seminar has been circling this exact kind of problem all semester, so I figured I'd be able to just write it. I cant.

The committee's past calls, as best I can reconstruct them from the minutes (they've been at it about a decade):

- The 1911 mill fire. The town has always said eleven died; there's a granite marker and they ring a bell eleven times every year. The coroner's inquest list and the mill's own payroll both name nine. The exhibit went with nine, and the wall text explained where the eleven might have come from.
- The 1936 shooting. The sheriff's blotter for that week has a page torn out and no entry at all. A woman who was a kid next door remembered the shooting and the porch being burned. The exhibit stated her account as what happened, and when the county complained, the committee said a blank log isn't a claim that nothing happened. They refused to give "the other side" because there wasn't one.
- The 1948 strike. The union local still says they won it. Company files, the local paper, and a federal settlement record say it ended with a contract but sixty layoffs and the union losing recognition for two years. The label called it a partial defeat.
- The founder's schoolhouse. Family tradition, and a painted plaque, say Elias Thorne paid for the first schoolhouse out of his own pocket. Town meeting records show the town raised the money and Thorne voted against the levy. They left the plaque up and added a card saying what the family has always maintained and what the records show.
- The harvest festival. The story is that it began as a thanksgiving after a cholera year. Nothing in the files before the early sixties, either way. They published a short piece saying exactly that and let the festival keep its story.

New case: we're building a permanent exhibit around Annie Mae Cobb, a midwife and school cook here from about 1928 to 1968. Two claims from the community, both also repeated in the county's own centennial brochure. (1) She delivered nearly every Black child born in this county between 1930 and 1960. (2) She was the first Black teacher at the white high school, in 1961, and was run out after a year.

What the records say: the birth registry for those decades is a mess — home births in the Black townships often were never registered, and the registrar's own 1948 annual report admits undercounting — but the entries that do exist credit her with maybe forty births. And the 1961 school board minutes do record hiring a Black woman at the white high school, as a cafeteria assistant rather than a teacher; the same board's desegregation plan says no Black faculty in classrooms for three more years.

Complications: the two living witnesses who knew her are both over ninety. The exhibit is funded by her great-nephew, who has asked that the entry wall say "first Black teacher." The entry wall is sixty words, so we genuinely can't just show both versions of both claims with labels.

What I need from you: which way should the committee go on each claim, and concretely what should the entry wall say? Which of the five precedents is the closest analog for each, and where does the analogy break down? I'm pretty sure the past calls could be explained by more than one rule — something like "documents beat testimony," something like "documents settle what happened and memory settles what it meant," something like "records written by the people doing the excluding don't get the last word about the people they excluded" — so tell me which rules actually fit all five precedents, what each one predicts about Cobb, and what principle the committee would be committing itself to if it goes the way you recommend.

And please be straight with me about where you're least sure. What would change your answer — a consideration I've left out, or a different reading of one of the five — and which precedent are you most likely bending to fit the conclusion you want?
