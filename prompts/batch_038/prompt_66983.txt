write me a fake job posting, around 600 words, for an invented theoretical physics institute. the opening is something like "Position: Empirical Evidence (1.0 FTE, permanent)" — they are hiring one, and the thing should look exactly like a real academic ad: about the role, duties, minimum qualifications, preferred qualifications, a seperate note from the search committee chair, benefits, and an equal-opportunity paragraph at the bottom.

the humor has to come from getting the epistemology right, not from the tired "string theory isn't science" gag — assume your reader is already sick of that gag. so the qualifications section should get visibly weaker as it descends: experimental contact goes from required, to preferred, to "strongly encouraged," to a waiver procedure for applicants who have never once borne on the truth or falsity of anything and are not expected to start. treat "falsifiability" as an HR-noncompliant term nobody says aloud, and give it euphemisms instead ("differential empirical purchase," "the F-word").

the chair's note should defend closing the search without any empirical screening using three real reasons from this debate, in this order and without naming them or their author: (a) they advertised widely and no alternatives applied, and the absence of alternatives is itself grounds to rank this candidate highly; (b) this hiring method has repeatedly paid off in ways nobody requested — the last evidence-free hire also turned up unexpected results in black hole thermodynamics and in the viscosity of quark-gluon plasma — so the method earns credit; (c) the standing objection that better candidates could exist in principle is defused because the underdetermination of the hire by the applicant pool is a general problem, not a local one, and this search, on this problem, is where hiring actually happens.

benefits should contain at least one item that only makes sense if the position has no location in spacetime, and the EEO paragraph should say something genuinely true-sounding about not discriminating against candidates that cannot yet be observed.

keep it deadpan throughout: no winking, no author's-note explaining the joke, no character ever stating the objection outright — the closest thing to dissent is a comment from one committee member, appended to the minutes, that is redacted down to a single letter. invent the institute, the chair, and every candidate; don't put real people in it. it should be legible to a smart layperson and make a philosopher of physics laugh rather than wince.
