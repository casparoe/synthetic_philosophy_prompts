been arguing with a friend about this and ended up writing my actual views down, so: does the set hang together?

(1) The separateness of persons objection is decisive against total utilitarianism. There's no social entity that undergoes the sacrifice and gets compensated for it; there are only individual people, each living their own life.

(2) Intrapersonal tradeoffs are fine and dont need any special defense. I can sit through the root canal now for the sake of my mouth in ten years and nobody has been used as a means.

(3) I'm a reductionist about personal identity: identity over time is just psychological connectedness and continuity, it comes in degrees, and there's no further fact about me that persists.

(4) Because connectedness comes in degrees, I lean toward a time-relative account of interest — how much my present self should care about my future self depends on how much of me will actually be there.

(5) But none of that travels across persons for me. Twins raised together, a couple married fifty years, mother and infant — still fully separate, no partial aggregation, not even a little bit.

(6) I also don't think the objection needs metaphysics at all. It's about the structure of practical reasoning: utilitarianism just takes the principle of individual rational choice and applies it straight to the social case.

(7) Prioritarianism escapes the whole problem, since it takes distribution seriously.

Tell me which of these genuinely pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what the cheapest repair is in each case — ordered by severity, not listed flat. For each real tension, say what has to give and what giving it up costs me. Then tell me which single revision buys the most consistency overall. Bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning; no preamble, and don't restate the question back at me.
