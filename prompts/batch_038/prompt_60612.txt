I'm on a county grants panel that funds emergency winter shelter beds, and we have a weird one to decide next week. The applicant is an animal-liberation nonprofit that's the only group willing to operate the building out in the north end — they'd add 40 beds we otherwise can't fund this season, and their street-outreach record is genuinely good, better than ours. The catch: their intake screen turns away anyone who works in animal agriculture and anyone who hunts, on the grounds that housing such people would make the shelter complicit in what they do. Two people we know would actually be affected: a guy who's worked at the processing plant for fifteen years, and a woman who's a member of a fedrally recognized tribe and hunts to feed her family. Our funding rules bar discrimination on a list of grounds that includes ancestry and tribal affiliation but says nothing about occupation or lawful work — so our own rulebook reaches one of these two applicants and not the other. Half the panel wants to fund it as-is because beds are beds and it's January, half wants to reject the whole application.

Draft the short memo I'll circulate before Tuesday's meeting: fund, fund with conditions, or reject, with the conditions written concretely enough that we could put them in the contract. Two things I especially want handled: take seriously the operator's claim that we'd be asking them to give up a commitment that constitutes the organization rather than a mere preference (and say whether that claim actually holds here, not just in general), and then say what principle the panel would be locking itself into by voting yes, given that the next applicant to ask for an exemption will quote our reasoning back at us. If you recommend conditions, tell me who pays for them and what happens to those two people this winter.
