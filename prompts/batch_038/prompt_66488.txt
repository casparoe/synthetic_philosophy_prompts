write me a parody software changelog for the next release of a big psychiatric diagnostic manual — version numbers, ticket IDs, a "known issues" section, the whole release-notes genre, around 600 words. Invent the manual's name; no real manual, no real people.

The comedy has to come from getting Hacking's looping effects right rather than from mental-health jokes: every patch to a category changes the people who now get sorted under it, those people change how they describe themselves, and that breaks the category again by thier next release — and the team keeps filing this as a QA bug with a reproducible steps list. Include one entry where they try to beat the loop by shipping a diagnosis undocumented and it backfires in a way that's specifically worse, and one bitter note comparing themselves to the sister team who model something indifferent (weather, say) and never have this problem at all.

What's being satirized is the practice of writing categories up as though they were discovered natural kinds rather than as things that help make the people they classify — so the committee should sound like it genuinely believes the loop is a defect you can engineer away. Punch up at the committee, never at the people getting classified. Deadpan throughout: a reader should be able to tell exactly what's being ironized without anyone winking, and end on an entry that hints at the real problem without ever stating it.
