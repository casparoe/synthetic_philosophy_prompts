I have no academic training in any of this, but a question about rights has been occupying me for some months, and I should be grateful for an essay that takes it seriously.

Suppose two of us live in adjoining houses, and each has the sole say over whether he practises the piano in the evening. I would rather play than not; but if there is to be music through the wall at all, I would much rather it were my neighbour's, since he is a fine pianist and I am not. My neighbour ranks the possibilities the other way about: what he wants most is an evening with no piano at all, and if there must be one, he would rather it were his own, since he cannot abide the thought of my playing carrying into his house. Now let each of us be given the right over his own decision, in the sense that whatever is settled upon collectively must follow whichever of us prefers to play rather than not. My right makes my playing better than nobody's; his right makes nobody's playing better than his; and if those two verdicts are allowed to be strung together, my playing comes out better than his — which is precisely the one comparison on which we are perfectly agreed, both of us holding his playing to be the better of the two. So respecting each man's choice over his own affair leaves an arrangement in place that both parties would rather trade away.

I have a suggestion about where the trouble lies, and I want it tested rather than flattered. It seems to me that what does the damage is never a preference about one's own life but a preference about somebody else's choice; and I wonder whether a preference should count in the collective reckoning only if the person would still hold it in a world where the other's decision were made in secret and left no detectable trace. My preference for my neighbour's playing plainly fails that test, and once it is discarded the conflict disappears and the rights stand unharmed.

Write me an essay on whether that manoeuvre is legitimate or a cheat. State your verdict in the opening paragraph and defend it, rather than surveying the options; and give some real attention to the obvious objection, which is that the same test would discard preferences everyone thinks ought to count — I should rather my brother kept a promise he made me even if I never learnt that he had broken it. I suspect someone has set out my piano case more rigorously before now, and if so I should like to know where my version departs from theirs.

Please write in continuous prose throughout, in the manner of a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text and no tables.
