About a year ago I started a notes app file of how my brother's girlfriend answers rescue scenarios. She's a charge nurse in an ER, she answers fast, she never agonises, and she's never read a word of philosophy, which is exactly why I find her useful. I make up the case at dinner, she calls it, I write down the call and whatever one-line reason she gives.

The log so far. In every one you can only save one side, everyone's a stranger, and I deliberately leave out age, desert, who caused it, all of that.

1. Two overturned boats — one person in the first, eight in the second. Go for the eight, no agonising. Reason given: "there's more dying going on over there."
2. A kid going under in the deep end vs six adults on the shallow side, each with a dislocated shoulder. The kid. "Shoulders aren't in the same currency as a life."
3. One person who will die vs four hundred who will each lose a hand. The one. I pushed her on four thousand, then four hundred thousand. Same answer. "Nobody's dying over there."
4. One person who will lose a leg vs three who will each lose a hand. The three. "Nobody's dying, and three hands is more loss than one leg."
5. One person paralysed from the neck down vs two paralysed from the waist down. The one. "You can't add those up. That's not double, that's a different life."
6. One person who will certainly die vs six people who each have a one-in-six chance of dying. The certain one. "I don't do arithmetic on odds, I look at who's actually going to die."
7. One stranger vs one stranger, nothing to separate them. She won't pick — insists on drawing lots, and says choosing without the coin is treating one of them as already dead. But in case 1 she refuses a coin flip and calls it "a way of pretending the eight aren't eight."
8. Case 2 again except one of the shoulder adults is her brother. Takes her brother, won't apologise for it.

Tomorrow's case, the one I want you to work on: rockslide, single rescue team, one ledge they can reach in time. Upper ledge — one hiker who loses both legs above the knee if they don't get to him. Lower ledge — thirty hikers standing in meltwater, each of whom loses a foot to frostbite. Nobody on either ledge dies; everyone goes home afterwards. All strangers, no other information.

I can see her pointing at 4 (three hands beat one leg, so thirty feet beat two legs) and I can see her pointing at 5 (that's not addition, that's a different life), and I genuinly can't tell which way she tips. So:

- Give me the candidate rules her eight verdicts actually license — I'm assuming more than one survives — and what each of them says about the rockslide. I don't want a survey of positions from a textbook; I want the rules that are consistent with all eight calls, and where they're consistent with only some of them, say so. If it helps to mention which known view one of them resembles, fine, but I care about the rules, not the labels.
- Your best guess at her call, and the reason you'd put money on it rather than the other.
- Which single entry in the log is load-bearing — i.e. if she retracted it, which candidate rules would fall apart?
- One further scenario I could put to her next that would separate the surviving rules cleanly, plus what each rule predicts for it, so I can score her answer instead of just nodding along.

And if you think some of the eight aren't underdetermined at all but flatly inconsistent with each other, tell me which pair and don't smooth it over for me.
