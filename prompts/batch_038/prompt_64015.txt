For the past six years I have been one of three volunteer moderators of a small online forum (roughly four thousand members) devoted to comparative religion and Indian philosophy. Our founder, whom I will call Dev, ran the queue almost single-handedly and retired last month, so the decisions are now mine. Dev comes from a Jain family, is not an academic, and never cited texts; he simply said he was applying his tradition's doctrine of many-sidedness, and he had a few sayings he returned to constantly. One was that a thing can be spoken of as "in some sense it is" and "in some sense it is not," and that the second half is the one everybody forgets. Another was that his tradition has a word for standpoints that go bad, and that nearly everything he ever removed was not a wrong view but a spoiled one. He also loved the parable of the blind men and the elephant, but insisted people tell it wrong: they take it to show that every blind man was right about the elephant, when what it shows is that each was right about the part he touched and wrong that the part was the whole. I want to rule on a new post the way he would have, and I would like you to help me work out what rule he was actually running.

Here are his five most instructive verdicts, all of which I watched him make.

1. **Removed.** A post arguing that non-dualist Vedānta is the only metaphysics that survives scrutiny and that everyone else mistakes a limb for the body. His note: "He is welcome to his view. He is not welcome to say that no one else's is worth anything."
2. **Kept, after an edit.** A post stating flatly that the self is nothing but the body and that nothing survives death. Dev asked the author to insert a phrase marking it as one account among others, and then kept it. His note: "That is a real position held by real people. All it was missing was the mark that it is a position."
3. **Kept and pinned.** A post arguing that the Buddhist no-self teaching is not false but incomplete — right about the beads, silent about the string. Dev called it the best kind of contribution the forum gets.
4. **Removed.** A post saying that either the soul is eternal or it is not, that there is no third answer, and that the members hedging in the thread were afraid to commit. His note: "He is not arguing against a standpoint. He is arguing against there being standpoints."
5. **Both posts kept, argument allowed to run two weeks.** One member told another plainly that his reading of a scripture was simply wrong and that he could show it from the text. Afterwards Dev said: "They were inside the same house, arguing about the furniture. That is different from burning the house down."

The case now in front of me is a long, courteous, carefully argued essay by a new member whose conclusion is that no standpoint is more correct than any other: he is not saying it to silence anyone, he welcomes argument, but every answer to these questions is as good as every other, and disagreements only ever change which words we reach for. Half the membership wants me to remove it as a parody of the forum's whole philosophy; the other half says removing it would prove the first half right. A third member suggested I simply ask the author to mark his own thesis as one standpoint among others, the way Dev did in case 2 — and I genuinely cannot tell whether that is subtle or a cop-out. I have also noticed that the essay seems to undercut itself, since if every answer is as good as every other, then so is the answer that some answers are better; but Dev never removed anything merely for being illogical, and case 5 shows he tolerated an unqualified "you are simply wrong," so I do not know whether that self-reference is the crux or a red herring.

Please answer as a philosopher who works on the epistemology of disagreement and knows the Jain material well enough to use it, and think this through carefully before writing: begin by brainstorming candidate rules in rough form and testing each against all five verdicts, including the ones that look like outliers, rather than jumping to the new post. Specifically, I want (a) at least three candidate rules that generate all five of Dev's decisions, each stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a post I have never seen — I suspect one will concern explicit qualification or indexing, one will concern claims of exclusivity, and one will concern the health of the discussion itself, but tell me if you find others; (b) what each rule says about the new essay, remove, keep, or edit; (c) your best guess at Dev's verdict, which rule you would bet on, and what in the five precedents tips the balance; (d) what I should actually do, identifying which of the five is the closest analog and saying honestly what principle the forum would be adopting under each option, since I have to live with the precedent I set; and (e) one further hypothetical post — described concretely, as I have described these — that would discriminate between your top two rules. Do not give me a bibliography, a reading list, or citations; I cannot look anything up and do not want to pretend otherwise. Use the tradition's technical vocabulary only where it changes the analysis, and when you do, gloss it in a clause.
