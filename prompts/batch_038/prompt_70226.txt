He floats this hybrid there — roughly, that animals get a utilitarian treatment (we should maximize their total happiness and at minimum not make them miserable) while persons get the Kantian treatment (side constraints, individuals as ends and not merely means, so you don't get to aggregate across them) — and then he sort of backs off, admitting he's unclear what the moral side constraints on our treatment of animals actually are. Half my class reads that hedge as modesty and the other half reads it as him knowing the view doesn't hold together.

Could you write me an essay arguing one side of that? The question I care about: is Nozick's animals-are-utilitarian / people-are-Kantian hybrid stable, or does it collapse — downward, so animals get side constraints too, or upward, so persons end up aggregated after all? I'd rather you work the internal machinery than just line the animal liberation people up against him. One pressure point I've been mulling, which you're welcome to use or toss: the two halves seem to inherit the worst of their respective theories at once. The animal half is total-maximizing, so it apparently licenses — maybe even requires — bringing enormous numbers of animals into a mildly pleasant existence, which cuts hard against the intuition that factory farming is bad because of the suffering involved rather than because there isn't enough of it. The person half is side-constrained, so it refuses to violate a constraint even to prevent a greater number of identical violations elsewhere, which looks incoherent in exactly the way the animal half has just managed to avoid. So the hybrid may be doubly unstable: too demanding on one side, too rigid on the other. If you think there's a repair available — say, swapping the maximizing animal half for a weaker threshold constraint, on which animals may not be harmed unless doing so prevents much greater harm to animals or persons — I want to hear what the repair costs and whether what's left is still recognizably Nozick's view rather than a generic moderate animal ethic with his name stapled on.
