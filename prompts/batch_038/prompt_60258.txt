I am in the Upper Sixth at a school in West Yorkshire and I am writing my Extended Project Qualification on moral responsibility, though the problem in front of me is a practical one rather than an academic one. Our Philosophy Society has two members who have been arguing, cordially but fruitlessly, since the start of the Autumn term: one holds that our practices of holding one another responsible are constituted by the reactive attitudes, in the manner of Strawson's essay Freedom and Resentment, and are therefore left untouched by the truth of determinism; the other is a free will sceptic of Pereboom's persuasion, who denies basic desert altogether and would replace retributive punishment with something like preventive quarantine plus moral education. Last month we attempted to have them write a joint statement in the style of an adversarial collaboration, and it failed in the way such attempts always fail: each produced a lucid summary of their own view together with a list of the other's errors, and nothing whatever that both could sign. I have volunteered to draft the document a genuine collaboration would have produced, so that the Society can see what the format actually demands, and I should be grateful for your help in writing it.

What I need is the joint statement itself — a constructive text that a committed Strawsonian and a committed sceptic could both put their names to, not a dramatised dialogue between them and not an adjudication of which of them is right. It should open with a numbered list of agreed claims: propositions both would sign without reservation, and which must be substantive enough that an outsider to the debate would recognise them as real commitments rather than platitudes. I would expect agreements there about the difference between excusing an agent and exempting them, about what the reactive attitudes are responsive to in the first place, about which forms of moral protest the sceptic can retain and which they must surrender, and about what determinism does and does not by itself show. After that, a disagreements table: each live disagreement stated twice over, once in wording the Strawsonian would accept as a fair statement of their own position and once in wording the sceptic would accept for theirs, with a column recording whether the disagreement is substantive or merely verbal and, where it is verbal, an account of what is being traded on. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would genuinely move at least one of the two.

Three matters I would especially like the cruxes to engage with, since these are where our Society's argument keeps circling back on itself. First, a distinction I proposed myself and which neither party has yet accepted: resentment that is addressed, in that it makes a second-personal demand for acknowledgement of the person resented, as against resentment that is merely recorded, a private moral sorrow or disapproval that asks nothing of anybody. Does that distinction sharpen the disagreement or dissolve part of it? Can the Strawsonian's account of holding responsible be carried by recorded resentment alone, or does it require the addressed variety; and if it requires the addressed variety, does the sceptic's quarantine model — which also seems to involve addressing an offender with a demand — thereby collapse back into something Strawsonian? Second, whether the impossibility of adopting the objective attitude towards people generally does any normative work at all, or merely reports a psychological limitation of our species, together with the mirror-image question for the sceptic: is their proposed revision stable under that same limitation, given that reforming the practice from the inside is precisely what the limitation was supposed to rule out? Third, a concrete empirical test that both would accept as bearing on that second crux. Suppose participants read a vignette establishing thoroughgoing determinism and are then asked, at two delays and separately, how much resentment they feel towards the wrongdoer and how blameworthy they judge that person to be, with a further condition in which the harm is rendered vivid and second-personal. Each side should record its prediction in advance — my expectation is that the Strawsonian will forecast shallow, transient suppression confined to abstract reflection, while the sceptic will forecast suppression that deepens with further reflection and survives the vivid condition — and each should state in writing which pattern of results would count as a defeat for their own view rather than as methodological noise. Please define every technical term on first use, as some of our Year 12s will be reading the document aloud at the next meeting, and do not append a bibliography or a list of references: I want the argument, not the citations.
