I'm auditing my own department's graduate reading group this term (Thursdays, grad lounge, coffee best described as adversarial). The topic is mental causation, and two weeks ago the assignment was Hofstadter's old Achilles-and-the-Tortoise dialogue, the one titled Who Shoves Whom Around Inside the Careenium?, alongside the stretch of I Am a Strange Loop where he rebuilds the same toy: a billiard-table-ish substrate full of little magnetic balls he calls simms, which clump into bigger symbolic balls — simmballs — that careen around, collide, grow, split, and recruit fresh simms. I mostly sat there grading and kept my mouth shut, but two of the students carried the argument out to the pub and then into my inbox, and they're still stuck. Mira works on causation and has a statistics background; Tobias came over from ML and comp-neuro. I'd like a clean adjudication I can hand back to the group as a exercise, so I've written out the exchange as faithfully as I can from their emails.

(1) Mira: the careenium actually settles the debate this seminar keeps circling. The simmballs shove each other around, and simmballs are symbols, so symbols shove the substrate. Which collision happens next is a function of what the symbols mean, not just of where the simms happen to be.

(2) Tobias: that's a redescription, not a discovery. Every shove is a collision between magnetic balls with positions and velocities; you could compute the whole future of the table from the physics of the simms and never once use the word symbol. Tacking on "and it went that way because the TORTOISE simmball means something" contributes zero predictive content. Billiards is billiards; the meaning is a gloss we paint on afterwards.

(3) Mira: run that argument agaisnt pressure in a piston. You can compute everything there from molecular trajectories too, but nobody concludes the pressure is epiphenomenal, or that "the gas pushed the piston because it was heated" is a mere gloss. Macro-level causation survives micro-determination all day long. His principle proves too much — it would also eliminate temperature, liquidity, and center-of-mass motion.

(4) Tobias: fine, pressure is real. But notice what I've just conceded: reality of the macro isn't downward causation. Pressure is defined in terms of the micro-state and lawfully supervenes on it, so its causal claims are compressed restatements of micro-causation, not additional causes. And the careenium is much worse off than the piston, for a reason nobody in the group mentioned: Hofstadter hand-crafted the simmballs. Their shapes, clumping habits, and affinities were chosen by an author who already knew what they were supposed to mean. So the semantic regularities inside the machine are artifacts of a designer standing outside it. In a real brain there is no designer, which means you can't get from "these marbles behave as if they meant something" to "meanings move marbles." The thought experiment smuggles in precisely what it's offered as evidence for.

(5) Mira: that's his best move and it still fails. The hand-crafting is scaffolding, and the point of scaffolding is to make visible a structure that, once it exists, maintains itself: simmballs get their shapes adjusted by the very careening they cause, they replicate, they recruit, and they drift into alignment with whatever in the environment they keep getting correlated with. Exhibit the maintenance loop and the designer drops out of the explanation. Brains are just careenia whose shapes got tuned by feedback rather than by an author.

(6) Tobias: then give me a test. Two careenia with identical simm-physics, one populated by meaningful simmballs and one just clattering randomly — show me a single prediction the symbol level gets right that the physics can't in principle get. If you can't, "symbols push the substrate" isn't a claim about the world, it's a claim about which descriptions we like. Liking a description is legitimate, but it isn't causation, and promoting a descriptive convenience into a shove is a category mistake.

(7) Mira: he has the test backwards. The symbol level earns its keep on counterfactuals and interventions: perturb the environment — show the careenium something it's been coupled to before — and the symbol level tells you which simmballs will grow and which will dissolve, while the simm level owes you a simulation of trillions of collisions to say the same thing. Worse for him: at the simm level you can't even identify the same symbol twice, so the events he wants to relate (a belief, an intention, an action) aren't in his ontology to begin with. If the relata don't exist down there, the micro story doesn't merely omit the mental cause, it can't host it. That's indispensability, not convenience.

(8) Tobias, having the last word: indispensability for us still doesn't make something a cause. A demon tracking every simm could pick out the pattern, individuate it across time, predict everything, and would never need the vocabulary. So here's the deflationary answer to the title question: the simms always do the shoving, and "the simmballs shoved" is just our idiom for certain shovings of simms. Patterns are real, symbols are real as patterns, high-level causal talk is perfectly legitimate — but there is no extra push, and any philosophy of mind that needs one has misdiagnosed its own problem.

So: rate each of the eight moves on a scale running from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a sentence or two of justification per rating — I care much more about the ratings and the reasons for them than about a winner. Then tell me who's more right overall, and why. Two things I'd specifically like you to watch for: whether either of them slides between a production notion of causation (one event shoving another) and a difference-making notion (one event making a counterfactual difference to another) without noticing that they've switched; and whether move 4's designer objection is really an objection to the careenium's conclusion or only to its pedagogy. Finally, flag anything important that neither of them raised — I have a suspicion that the pair of them drifted away from the question Hofstadter was actually asking, but I want your read on that, not mine. No reading list, please, and don't dissolve the dispute by invoking a general theory of mental causation without getting your hands dirty on the careenium itself.
