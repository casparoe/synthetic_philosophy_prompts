Before our nine-judge appellate panel takes its next contract case, I need to fix our voting procedure. The case turns on two premises — that the representation was made, and that it was material — and our holding follows from their conjunction, so we face the familiar possibility that a majority accepts each premise while a majority rejects the holding. The candidates:

1. Outcome voting: judges vote on the holding only.
2. Premise voting: separate majorities on each premise, holding determined by the conjunction.
3. Sequential premise voting: the premises are taken in a fixed order, and each judge's later vote is constrained by her earlier ones.
4. A reason-based rule that counts only the premises each individual judge treats as load-bearing for her own vote on the holding, rather than every premise in the schema.
5. A colleague's proposal: first strike from the tally any judge whose stated premises do not entail her stated holding, then apply premise voting to the remainder.

The panel cares about three things. The opinion we publish must state premises that genuinely entail the holding we announce; we must not be easy prey for a judge who votes against a premise she believes in order to control the outcome; and the result must not turn on the order in which we happen to call the questions.

Rank the five procedures against those three concerns, and score each from 0 to 10 for how well it serves the panel as a collective reason-giver. Judge them on the merits rather than on who has defended what, and do not cite literature.

Return a single JSON object with one field, `ranking`, whose value is an array of five objects ordered from best to worst. Each object has: `rank` (integer 1–5); `procedure` (number and name as given above); `score` (integer 0–10); `entailment_risk` (one sentence: can the published premises come apart from the announced holding, and how?); `manipulation_exposure` (one sentence on the cheapest available insincere vote and what it buys); `order_sensitivity` (exactly one of `none`, `mild`, `severe`); and `verdict` (one sentence on why it lands where it does).
