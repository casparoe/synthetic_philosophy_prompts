I've been listening to a philosophy podcast on modality, and the host summarized Kripke's argument for the necessity of origin roughly as follows (this is my paraphrase of a few minutes of talk, not a transcript):

> Kripke asks whether Queen Elizabeth could have come from a different sperm and egg than the ones she actually did come from, and his answer is no. The reason isn't merely that we find it hard to picture: anyone who had come from different gametes would have been a different person altogether, however similar, because the gametes are what make her her. So origin is necessary. And the same line of thought shows the converse as well: given a particular sperm and egg, there is only one person who could possibly have resulted, so origin is sufficient too. Identity, on this picture, reduces to biological pedigree. That is why the complaint that the thesis is just a stipulation about how we use names misses the point, and why it also settles the cloning question: a copy grown from one of your cells could never be you, however exactly it matched your memories.
