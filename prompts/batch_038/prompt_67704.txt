Consider the thesis that omissions can be genuine causes, understood in a deliberately non-trivializing way: there are concrete cases in which a claim of the form that A's not having φ-ed caused outcome O is literally true, and its truth is not exhausted by (i) the truth of the corresponding counterfactual about what would have happened had A φ-ed, together with (ii) the ordinary causal transactions among positive events that did occur. Also excluded from the thesis's scope are cases in which the purported omission is merely a positive state under another description — the switch's being open rather than closed, the guard's standing at a different door, the absence of a particular molecular configuration — since such cases can be restated without any metaphysics of absences at all.

My credence in the thesis so understood is about 0.35; a colleague's is about 0.85. I want yours as a single number, together with the decomposition that produces it. At a minimum, break it into: the probability that causation requires a productive or physically connective relation, so that only what can transfer, sustain, or transmit something can stand in it; the probability that a purely dependence- or counterfactual-based account can accommodate omissions without generating intolerable causal proliferation and without collapsing back into redescription; the probability that background ontology about absences or negative facts does independent work beyond those two terms; and the probability that the dispute is merely verbal — that my colleague and I are deploying different concepts of causation, neither of which is more correct than the other. Give a number and one sentence of justification for each term, show how you combine them into the final credence, and identify the term to which that final number is most sensitive along with the consideration that would move it most.

I am not asking for a survey of the dialectic, nor for a compromise between my figure and my colleague's. Commit to a number and commit to a verdict about where our disagreement actually lives: which single term accounts for most of the gap between 0.35 and 0.85, and why that one rather than another. State your reasons as reasons rather than as options, and say explicitly, for any term where it applies, that your own figure is little better than a guess.
