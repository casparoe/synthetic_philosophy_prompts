Right, so I've got properly into Kelly betting over the last couple of months — my brother-in-law does a bit of trading and wouldn't shut up about it at Christmas — and I've finally got round to applying it to a side thing I run. But before I commit actual money I want to check I've understood the justification rather than just repeating something I read on a blog.

My understanding, which feels solid to me: Kelly is the stake size that maximises the expected value of your bankroll. That's the entire point of it. It isnt a personality thing, it isn't about how much risk you can stomach or how brave you are — it's just the arithmetic of what makes you the most money, and any other fraction is leaving expected value on the table. Bet more than Kelly and you're worse off, bet less and you're worse off, and Kelly is the peak of the curve. What I can't reconcile is what my brother-in-law said when I repeated this back to him. He said that's completely wrong, that if you genuinely maximised expected value you'd shove the whole bankroll in every single time, and that Kelly maximises something else entirely — a growth rate. Then he went on about a word, ergodicity I think, and about how expected value is the wrong thing to average because you don't get to live your life as an average across all the parallel copies of yourself, you just get to live this one run of it, one week after the next. I nodded along at the time but I don't really buy it, or rather I can't see how both things can be true. Because if staking the lot really does maximise expected value, then surely that's just what rational means, and Kelly is a timid compromise dressed up as a theorem. And if Kelly is the actual optimum, then what quantity is it optimising, and why should I care about that quantity instead of about money? (He also muttered something about it all being asymptotic, which seemed to me like an escape hatch — I'm not going to be doing anything ten thousand times over.)

Here's the concrete case, since I'd like the answer to land on something real rather than in the abstract. I buy clearance stock by the box and flog it online. Each box is £200 in, and from a year or so of keeping records I reckon about 55% of them come out ahead by an average of £300, and the rest finish about £200 down once I've shifted the dross cheap. So a healthy edge, about £75 a box on £200 outlaid. Full Kelly comes out at 25% of bankroll a go, which on the £6,000 I've got set aside is £1,500, so seven boxes at a time. He says no, use half Kelly, three or four boxes, you keep roughly three quarters of the growth for roughly half the swings. Two things I want an answer on. First: is half-Kelly just him being soft, or is there an argument for it that works on Kelly's own terms rather than importing some extra squeamishness about risk? Second: is there any version of my original belief that survives — can Kelly be defended as the rational stake rather than as one risk appetite among many — or do I have to give up on there being a uniquely right answer here at all? Please actually commit to a position on both and give me your reasons, show the working where it's numerical. I'd much rather be told plainly that I've got it wrong than be handed a balanced summary of the two sides and left to decide.
