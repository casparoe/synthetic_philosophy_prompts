I cover tech and economics for a science-and-ideas desk, and I'm trying to work out whether the column I want to write about money actually holds up. I've been reading Politics book I and the exchange chapter of Nicomachean Ethics book V on a flight, and I suspect I've absorbed some of it without noticing — which is the problem, since I'm about to publish. Play the part of a philosopher who works on Aristotle's practical thought but also reads monetary economics seriously, someone who could supervise a dissertation on Aristotle on money and not find the question strange. Think it through carefully first, then audit this set:

1. Money is money by convention, not by nature. No substance is intrinsically money; it's money because people take it as such.
2. But money has a job — being the common measure that lets unlike things (the builder's house against the shoemaker's shoes) get traded — and getting money out of money itself, interest above all, is a perversion of that job.
3. Exchange is just when it's proportionately reciprocal, not when it's equal.
4. Value isn't in the objects, it's in what people want. A price is just the record of that.
5. A competitive market setting prices by supply and demand is good, and it's the thing I'd defend in print against the people who want it abolished.
6. Bitcoin isn't really money — and I mean that as a criticism, not a description.
7. Capping interest on payday loans: yes. Capping interest on corporate bonds: obviously absurd, nobody argues for it.

Tell me which of these genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict and fall apart once you take them to pieces, and for each real one, what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me elsewhere in the list. Then say which single revision would clean up the most. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally broken, and say so explicitly if you think one of my items is doing double duty in two arguments that don't share a foundation. I'm not attached to any particular item, so don't spare my feelings, and don't lecture me on Aristotle generally. Keep the working out to yourself: hard cap of 450 words, numbered to match mine.
