Putting together a seminar on practical reason and I want to use a real conversion to stress-test Williams' internal reasons thesis — the story on which A has a reason to φ only if φ-ing could serve some element of A's subjective motivational set, via a sound deliberative route. Case: Newman going over to Rome in the 1840s. No biography and no church history, assume your reader knows the shape. The bits I want in play: Tract 90 blowing up in 1841, Newman resigning his cure at St Mary's and shutting himself away at Littlemore, his Anglican friends pushing back (Pusey most of all), what he said he took from Butler's Analogy about probability being the guide of life and about Rome still carrying the lineaments of the church of the Fathers, the reception in October 1845, and then two decades later Kingsley's charge of dissembling and the Apologia as the reply.

First, run the thesis properly: put the live reason-statements in Williams' form — Newman has a reason to stay at St Mary's; Newman has a reason to be received into the Roman church — and for each say which S it's being read against and whether it comes out true or false. Which is where the trouble starts, and here's the wrinkle I want taken seriously rather than waved at. The account looks synchronic: test the reason-statement against the S the agent actually has. But conversion is exactly the case where the deliberation rewrites the S it's meant to be justified by. Read it against the 1841 S and the move is exogenous — something occured to Newman rather than his having a reason; read it against the 1845 S and the reason-statement is true but vacuous, a restatement of the outcome. Call it bootstrapping. Is there a fix thats not ad hoc? One I half like and half hate: let a reason be grounded in the S the agent would have at the end of a sound deliberative route starting from the S he actually has — a prospective S, if you like. Does that rescue internalism, is it circular, and does it quietly collapse the internal/external distinction, given that McDowell's line (upbringing can put the reason there directly, no motivationally prior S to consult) licenses something structurally similar?

Two more things. Imagination: Williams allows that imagining things can alter the S, and the Littlemore years look like a great deal of imagining. Does that resource do the work here, or does it make the thesis unlosable — is there any conversion at all it could rule out? And the social side: if a lot of external reason talk is really blame, or an attempt to move someone, then what were Pusey and Kingsley doing — making false reason-claims, or just applying pressure? Awkward bit: the Apologia is itself a deeply internalist document, Newman defending himself by narrating the slow, reluctant movement of his own mind. Is that the best possible case for Williams, or evidence that internalism is just the self-understanding agents have, which is why no case could refute it?

Then give me the verdict, bluntly: does 1841-45 confirm the thesis, constitute an anomaly it can't absorb, or show that it was never aimed at cases like this — that it's really about the ordinary I've no reason to of someone refusing to do a thing, and gets stretched out of shape when you point it at a two-year reconstruction of a person's motivations. Where the theory is simply silent, say so instead of making it speak.
