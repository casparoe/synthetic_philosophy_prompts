I'm a third-year doctoral student in a philosophy department that shares methods and participants with a psychology lab, and on Thursday I have to present stimulus materials for a study we plan to run next term (roughly 200 participants per cell, recruited online). The study concerns the side-effect effect: the finding that people are far more willing to say a foreseen bad side effect was brought about intentionally than a foreseen good one, in otherwise matched scenarios — the classic materials being the chairman of the board who is told by his vice-president that a new program will increase profits and will also harm (or help) the environment, replies that he cares only about profits, goes ahead, and gets the outcome in question. Two of the accounts offered for the asymmetry are the ones I want to pit against each other. The trade-off account, associated with Machery, denies that moral valence does any work: what matters is that a bad side effect is presented to the reader as a cost of the goal the agent is pursuing, and people take the mention of a cost to show that the agent factored it into her decision, hence acted intentionally; a good side effect carries no such implication. The deep-self account, associated with Sripda, says what matters is concordance: an act is judged intentional to the degree that it expresses the agent's settled, stable evaluative commitments rather than a transient impulse or an externally imposed role demand. My supervisor's objection, which I take seriously, is that these two cannot be pulled apart because each is a post-hoc reading of the same vignettes — and a pilot we ran supports him, since our discordant vignette drew high intentionality ratings anyway, with think-aloud comments suggesting participants simply re-read the agent as callous.

So please build me a pair of matched vignettes, plus one control vignette, that satisfy every one of the following. (a) Realistic municipal or light-industrial settings — a paper mill, a city water authority, a hospital supply depot, a regional bus operator — with no science fiction, no neurosurgery, no hypnosis. (b) In all three, the side effect is foreseen and is explicitly framed either as a cost of the goal the agent is pursuing or as a bonus attached to it, so that the trade-off structure is fully and equally present in each; the goal itself must be neutral or admirable (keeping the plant open, meeting a compliance deadline, protecting jobs), not villainous. (c) The two target vignettes differ in exactly one variable: whether the act is concordant or discordant with the agent's settled commitments. Agent role, act, knowledge, outcome, sentence order, and length are held as fixed as language permits. (d) The information about the agent's deep self must be anchored independently of the outcome — established before the decision, and corroborated in an unrelated domain — so that a reader cannot re-infer her character from the harm itself. This is the specification that matters most, because it is what my pilot failed. (e) No coercion, duress, threat, or language of compulsion anywhere: a forced act invites the independent reply that the agent acted intentionally but under pressure, which would confound the comparison. (f) No more than 170 words per vignette, no evaluative character vocabulary (callous, selfish, decent, admirable), and no phrasing of the did-not-care sort that tips the verdict before the question is asked. (g) A vignette that plants its answer in the wording — one in which concordant simply means she wanted to and discordant simply means she did not — counts as a failure; the divergence has to fall out of the two accounts applied to neutral material. (h) Spell out the measurement battery, five or six items, not just the intentionality probe: given the worry that intentionally can be heard as meaning she did it for that very reason, I want a manipulation check for each of the two variables plus a couple of downstream items, each justified in one sentence. If any specification turns out to be unsatisfiable in combination with the others, say so explicitly and say which one you would sacrifice and why; do not silently drop it.

After the vignettes, give me: first, a single sentence stating your bottom line on whether the specifications are jointly satisfiable as written; then a prediction table showing, for each vignette, what each account predicts on each item; then an identification of the one feature of the design that is doing the work, what happens to the predictions if I delete it, and how a defender of each account would attack the materials — in particular, how a deep-self theorist would argue that deep-self attributions are themselves outcome-driven so that no vignette can pin them down independently, and how a trade-off theorist would argue that my concordance manipulation covertly alters whether the harm was genuinely weighed as a cost; and finally, what pattern of results would show that both accounts are incomplete rather than that one of them wins. The control vignette should be one on which the two accounts agree, so that I can distinguish a failed manipulation from a refuted theory. Please do not supply citations, a bibliography, or a literature review — I will add references myself. Begin directly with the vignettes, with no preface, no framing sentence, and no restatement of what I have asked for.
