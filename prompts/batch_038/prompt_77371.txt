A friend of mine who reads a lot of consciousness science posted this on our Discord, and I want a sanity check before I reply:

"Ned Block split consciousness into two pieces. Access consciousness is the information that gets broadcast widely, so it's available to reasoning, speech and action control; phenomenal consciousness is the leftover raw feel that doesn't do anything. Global workspace theorists accept the split but say the second piece is an illusion — when the brain broadcasts information globally, that just is what experiencing feels like from the inside, so there's no extra ingredient hiding behind it. Block's stock evidence against them is Sperling's letter-grid experiments: flash twelve letters for a fraction of a second, people insist they saw all of them but can only report three or four, which means they were phenomenally conscious of all twelve and only three ever made it into access. So Block concludes workspace theory leaves out the very thing we care about, and the workspace people reply that the unreported letters were never experienced at all. Which is just two intuitions squaring off, and honestly the intuition that there's a rich screen behind your eyes is stronger than any lab result, so I'm with Block. Also: if phenomenal consciousness really is a separate nonfunctional property, the zombie argument comes for free — that's the same move Chalmers makes."

How much of that holds up? Go through it claim by claim and tell me what Block would happily sign off on, what a workspace theorist would accept with a wince, and what's just a muddle.
