Background on me: I spent about 35 years in statistical mechanics, a good chunk of that worrying about pertubation series that refuse to converge, about cutoffs, and about when you're allowed to swap two limits. Retired four years now, and I've taken up reading philosophy of decision theory for fun, which may have been a mistake.

The thing I've got stuck on is what people call the Pasadena game: a coin-flip lottery where the payoffs alternate in sign, grow like 2^n, but get divided by something like n, so the expectation turns into an alternating harmonic series that lands on ln 2 — except it only converges conditionally, so re-ordering the terms can make it come out to anything you like. Before anything else, please write out the payoff schedule as you understand it and tell me plainly if I've mangled the description, because I'm working from a summary of a summary and no library access. There's also apparently a nastier variant where the positive half and the negative half each blow up separately, so there's no conditional convergence to fall back on; give me that one too, if it exists roughly the way I think it does.

What I actually want is a translation job. Restate the whole philosophical fuss about this game in the vocabulary I work in — absolute vs conditional convergence, Riemann rearrangement, Abel/Cesàro/zeta-type summation, UV cutoffs and regularization schemes, order of limits, divergent-but-resumable asymptotic series, ensemble average vs time average.
