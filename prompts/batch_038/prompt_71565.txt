For thirty-eight years I worked at the bench — microbial genetics first, then two decades running a small plant-pathology unit — and I retired three years ago. Since then I have taken up the philosophy of science with the diligence of a man who has suddenly acquired a great deal of time, and I have worked carefully through Popper: the falsifiability criterion, the problem of demarcation, the conventionalist stratagems, and, most arresting for a biologist, his short piece in *Nature* demoting Darwinism to a metaphysical research programme together with his later recantation of that demotion. Our university's emeriti association stages a revue each spring, and the philosophers have agreed, somewhat against their better judgement, to let an outsider contribute an item. Mine is to come just before the interval.

What I should like is a satirical document of roughly 700 to 800 words, written in the deadpan register of an official regulatory inspection: a report issued by a fictional licensing authority — call it the Office of Demarcation, or something apt if you can think of better — which has audited a university biology department and must now rule on whether natural selection may continue to trade as a testable scientific theory or must be reclassified as a reputable but unlicensed metaphysical research programme.

The comedy should come from the machinery being administered correctly, not from absurdity bolted on top of it. I would ask you to work in the following beats:

- The authority's own precedent: it has ruled on Darwinism once before, demoted it, and the ruling was subsequently withdrawn by the same office, so the inspector must explain at some length why the old file cannot simply be reopened, and what this does to the authority's standing.
- A finding citing "fitness" as a tautology risk — survival of whatever it is that survives — in which the department's written defence is reported fairly and taken seriously rather than swatted away.
- A finding on ad hoc modification and immunizing auxiliary hypotheses, with the inspector noting which of the department's patches were filed within the permitted window and which were retrofitted.
- The reflexivity problem, handled flatly and without emphasis: the licensing criterion was itself issued as a methodological proposal rather than an empirical claim, and has therefore never been inspected by anyone.
- One brief inspector's aside comparing the office's standard unfavourably with a rival consultancy that audits not testability but whether a programme is progressive or degenerating, and which charges considerably more for the privilege.

Format it as such a report would actually appear: a header block with invented reference numbers and a classification code; four or five numbered findings, each naming the criterion engaged and assigning a severity rating; a recommendations section; a paragraph on the right of appeal; and a closing footer. Please also include the audited unit's response, quoted from an invented head of department.

Two constraints. First, keep it deadpan throughout: nothing may wink, and the reader should be able to tell what is being ironized without being told. Second, I want it to be fair. I am no enemy of Popper — I count the recantation as the strongest evidence in his favour, since he applied his own standard to himself and revised in public — so the satire should fall on the notion of a sharp demarcation criterion being handed to an inspector with a clipboard and a schedule of fees, not on the man. Invent all dates, licence numbers and document references; do not attach any specific date, page number or quotation to a real publication, and do not append a bibliography or list of sources. Half the room will be biologists who have never opened a philosophy book, and the other half will be philosophers of science who have opened far too many: the piece must be legible to the first group and reward the second.
