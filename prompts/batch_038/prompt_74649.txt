The question as set, worth 10 marks:

"State the regress argument — the trilemma — that is supposed to show that no belief is justified. Explain how foundationalism, coherentism and infinitism each propose to escape it, and say for each whether the escape succeeds. Be explicit about which horn of the trilemma each view accepts and which it rejects."

The script, in full:

"The trilemma says that for any justified belief we can ask what justifies it, and there are only three answers: the chain of reasons runs back forever, or it comes back round to where it started, or it stops at a belief with nothing behind it. An infinite chain cannot be completed, a circle cannot support itself, and a bare stopping point is arbitrary. So nothing is justified.

Foundationalism refuses the third horn. It says some beliefs are basic: justified without inference, and passing justification up the chain. Foundationalists need not claim that basic beliefs are infallible — a basic belief can be defeasible and still do the work. The problem is Sellars. The given cannot justify, because a non-conceptual state can stand to a belief only as a cause and never as a reason. Since Sellars refuted the given, no version of foundationalism can appeal to experience at all, which leaves coherentism and infinitism as the only live options.

Coherentism escapes because its chains are finite. Justification is a property of a whole system of beliefs supporting one another rather than of a chain, so the demand that the chain terminate never arises: the system ends where it ends, and that is not arbitrary, because the ending is the whole. This answers the second horn — a circle is not a regress. The cost is that any internally coherent fairy tale is as justified as physics.

Infinitism accepts the first horn. It says a belief is justified only if the chain of reasons does not terminate. But infinitists also insist that nothing requires the subject to have travelled the chain: justification only demands that a further reason be available if it is asked for. Those two claims cannot both be true. If justification requires a non-terminating chain, then any justified subject has infinitely many justified beliefs, which is precisely what the availability clause was meant to deny. Infinitism therefore collapses."
