I write about science and ideas for a general-interest mag and I'm stuck on a piece about how the technology keeps outrunning the moral frameworks we use to talk about animals. My hook is Nozick. Back in Anarchy, State, and Utopia he ended up somewhere he himself admitted was complicated: maximize the happiness of animals, but never use them as mere means, plus the further admission that even that gives animals too little, plus the confession that he wasn't a vegetarian even though he thought the case for being one was strong. His idea seemed to be that what pulls a being over into the don't-use-it category is a bundle of capacities — self-consciousness, rationality, a life that hangs together as something the creature can shape over time, that sort of thing.

Now three things that postdate him. Lab-grown meat that's actually on sale somewhere: cells in a tank, no animal killed, arguably nobody harmed. A serious proposal to gene-edit farmed chickens and pigs so they can't feel pain, pitched by its advocates as the humane fix to factory farming. And the octopus farm — very smart cephalopods in tanks, bred for food. What does Nozick say about each?

The bit I really want you to get at: if moral status rides on a bundle of capacities, and we can now edit the bundle, does the line move with the edit? Is a pain-free pig simply outside the moral calculus, or is the editing itself the wrong? And if we crank a pig's cognition up, has it been promoted to the protected side, or is promoting it the deepest violation of all? The tech exists now so this isnt hypothetical.

Start with one sentence giving your best guess at his overall verdict on the three, then unpack. Hard cap: 350 words.
