I convene a graduate seminar on the epistemology of historical testimony, and one block of it is given over to the quest of the historical Jesus and to the standard battery of criteria: dissimilarity in its double form, embarrassment, multiple attestation, coherence with the admittedly secure material, and the criterion of Semitic idiom or Palestinian environment. My students have sorted themselves into two camps, and the dispute has proved remarkably resistant to argument. One camp holds that a saying which clears all five criteria has thereby been shown, to a considerable degree of confirmation, to go back to Jesus himself. The other holds that the criteria can at most measure how well a tradition fits a picture of first-century Palestine that the critic assembled in the first place, and so can never settle the question of what he actually said. I want to test that disagreement with a designed case rather than by another round of exegesis, and I have so far failed to produce one that does not cheat.

So: construct a thought experiment for me. Design a wholly fictitious saying attributed to Jesus, transmitted in a wholly fictitious early document — invent its title, provenance, date and physical description, and do not identify it with any real text, papyrus, version or church father, nor cite any real scholar or work anywhere in your answer. The case must satisfy each of the following. (a) The saying clears double dissimilarity: it is neither a development of contemporary Jewish teaching nor an expression of the theology or practice of any identifiable early Christian group. (b) It is attested in at least three witness-streams that a competent source critic would judge independent of one another, and you must say what makes each independent. (c) It is awkward or embarrassing for the community that transmits it, which nevertheless does transmit it. (d) It coheres with the narrow core of material that even the most sceptical critics treat as secure. (e) It bears the marks of an Aramaic substrate or of a specifically Palestinian setting. Now the crucial stipulation: as a fact about the case, inaccessible to any critic working inside it, the saying is a mid-second-century fabrication. And the fabrication may not be the work of a knowing forger. The community that produced it must have no concept of the criteria, no intention to deceive posterity, and no access to anything resembling modern scholarship; the saying must acquire its authentic-looking surface as an unintended by-product of that community's ordinary life. One candidate mechanism is the migration of a community's own inspired or prophetic speech into the Jesus-tradition, though you are not obliged to use it and I would rather see what you choose. If your case only works because the fabricator was in effect reading the criteria over my shoulder, it fails. Keep the case itself to about four hundred words.

After the case, give me the audit. First, a clause-by-clause demonstration that each of the five criteria is met, and for each one an account of the mechanism by which an invented saying came to exhibit that very feature. Second, identify the single feature of the case that is doing the most work: tell me which criterion fails first if I delete it, and whether that feature is itself one of the criteria or a piece of background knowledge the criteria were never meant to supply. Third, give the strongest attack a defender of the criteria can mount against my thought experiment — I expect something about the omniscient designer who simply stipulates the fabrication, and something about whether a case built to be indistinguishable from an authentic saying can carry any evidence about authenticity at all — and say whether that attack succeeds or merely relocates the difficulty. Finally, tell me which of my two camps the case favours, and whether a criterion-based method could ever in principle be made to discriminate a case of this kind.

Begin with your bottom line, in one sentence, stating whether a known fabrication can clear all five criteria without cheating; only then give the case and the audit. No preamble, no restatement of my question, no bibliography and no appeal to real secondary literature — the document you invent should be the only named text in your answer.
