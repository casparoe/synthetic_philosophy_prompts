**The examination question as set**

Dr V is a researcher who has submitted a paper to a journal. She knows that one of the three datasets she collected, if included in the analysis, would substantially weaken her central conclusion. She adopts the following maxim: "When my results are about to be judged by others who are themselves selectively reporting their results, I will withhold the evidence that most threatens my conclusion, so that my work stands the same chance as everyone else's."

(a) Apply Kant's Formula of Universal Law to this maxim. Distinguish a contradiction in conception from a contradiction in the will, state which, if either, the maxim runs into, and say what kind of duty the result yields. [12 marks]

(b) One of Dr V's colleagues replies: "You have described the maxim too broadly. Describe it instead as 'withhold disconfirming evidence when one's competitors are themselves withholding disconfirming evidence,' universalize that, and the contradiction disappears. Nothing about the practice of peer review breaks down, because the maxim only ever applies in circumstances where the breakdown has already happened. So your test proves nothing." Assess this reply. [13 marks]

**Candidate's answer, transcribed from the script**

The Formula of Universal Law asks whether I can will that my maxim become a universal law. Kant says we should ask two distinct things: whether the maxim can be conceived as a universal law of nature without contradiction, and whether the agent can will it as such a law.

Applied to Dr V: if every researcher withheld the results that most threatened their conclusions, the evidence base of science would degrade, journals could not rely on what was reported to them, and reviewers would discount everything they read. A maxim of concealment only makes sense against a background in which disclosure is the norm, so the maxim destroys the condition it needs in order to work. Dr V's own purpose, to give her paper the same chance as everyone else's, cannot be achieved in the world she is willing, since in that world nobody's paper has any special chance. The maxim therefore fails at the first stage, and the wrong is not merely that the consequences of generalizing would be unpleasant: as with the false promise, which ceases to be a possible way of getting money once everyone is entitled to lie, the action becomes impossible to perform successfully once universalized. That yields a perfect duty. There is no latitude here about when, or how much, to disclose.

The second stage is not really reached, but it is worth noting that Kant also asks whether the agent would want to live in the world in which her maxim is a law of nature. Dr V plainly would not. She is a scientist, she has willed the end of producing reliable knowledge, and she depends on the work of others; a world of universal concealment would frustrate an end she has already committed herself to. So the maxim would generate a contradiction in the will as well, and hence an imperfect duty to support the epistemic practices of her field.

As to (b), the colleague is reviving Hegel's complaint that the categorical imperative is an empty formalism, from which no determinate content can be got and which can therefore be made to deliver whatever verdict one wants by tweaking the description of the maxim. But Kant's own procedure blocks the move. Maxims are not bare descriptions of bodily movements: they include the end the agent has in view and the means she takes to it. Dr V's end is to have her paper judged on terms equal to those of her rivals. Re-describe the maxim so that it applies only where rivals are already concealing, and the end has changed: the agent is no longer seeking equality of chance but merely protecting herself inside an already degraded practice. More decisively, Kant's procedure includes a publicity test, and a maxim that cannot be avowed without defeating itself is forbidden. Dr V cannot write into her submission that she has omitted a disconfirming dataset on the ground that her rivals do the same, because the avowal destroys the point of the omission. So the colleague has not shown that the test proves nothing. He has shown only that the maxim must be described as the agent actually conceives it.
