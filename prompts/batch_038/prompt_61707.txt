We banned asphyxiation in air for farmed salmon and required either a percussive blow or an electrical stun before killing. We let a tilapia producer keep his ice slurry, on the grounds that unconsciousness comes quickly in that species and no percussive line existed at his throughput, and we said wed revisit it if a workable stun turned up. We turned down one trout producer's CO2 system even though he showed it beat air asphyxiation, but we did certify a CO2 system for a warmwater species where the exposure time is much shorter. We refused to certify farmed octopus at all — not on slaughter grounds, on the grounds that we couldn't rule out the grow-out phase. We brought decapod crustaceans into scope after an outside evidence review, then banned shipping live crabs packed in ice and required a stun first. And we said nothing whatsoever about sea urchins, because the evidence wouldn't support a verdict.

Predict what we should decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves, on both counts. Say which of the earlier rulings is the closest analog and why, set out the two or three principles that fit our record equally well up to here but split on this case, name what wed be committing ourselves to with each choice, and tell me what further case would settle which principle we're actually running.
