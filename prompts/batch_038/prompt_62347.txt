I'm in a reading group on how countries deal with their violent pasts and our convenor explains everything in therapy language: Germany did its working-through in the late sixties, Japan is a case of partial denial, Turkey has repressed 1915 the way a patient represses an unbearable memory, and the American South never mourned its dead so it keeps acting the loss out in statues. He leans on the Mitscherlichs for the mechanism -- a population that couldnt mourn its lost ideal, so the emotional energy got redirected into rebuilding the economy instead -- and he uses it to argue that public truth-telling is the treatment and that the timeline is predictable: name it, grieve it, get on with your life.

How far does the psychoanalytic analogy actually go? Lay out the mapping -- who's the patient, what's the repression, the symptom, the analyst, the cure -- then where it breaks and what it quietly imports from the one-person case that a polity just doesn't have. Does the truth-telling-is-therapy conclusion survive that, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time? And if it fails, what's a better picture of what those four cases actually share?
