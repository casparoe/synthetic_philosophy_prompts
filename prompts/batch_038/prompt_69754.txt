Just lost two hours to an argument with a friend who insists "sphexishness" isn't a real criticism of anything. His line: every rule-follower is that wasp — you drag its paralyzed prey a few inches while it's down the burrow, it hauls it back to the entrance, goes in, comes out, starts over, forever — so calling a language model sphexish is just saying "it follows rules," which is not an insult. I kept trying to insist there's a difference between running the routine and responding to the situation, but I couldn't produce a test that didn't collapse into "it seems smarter." Which maybe means he's right.

I teach a philosophy of mind seminar, 18 people, 75 minutes, mostly second-years plus two CS students who'll smugly say the answer is just more parameters. Design an in-class activity that makes them feel the distinction and then feel it fail. They should hit the problem before anyone says the word sphex — that name stays out until the debrief. I need: materials that are just paper and tokens I can buy, roles and phases with timings, the interruption mechanic and how it gets administered with no confederate, and the debrief mapping each moment onto the argument, including what happens when the "flexible" instruction (ask me if anything changes) turns out to be another script. Then tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which feature of the real case the activity cant reproduce and I have to talk them through.
