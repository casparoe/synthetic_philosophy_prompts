The word I found for the thing I thought I was doing is supererogation — acts that are morally good but beyond the call of duty, where you get praised for doing them but can't be blamed for skipping them. My worry is that the category doesn't survive contact with a case like mine, where a few people's "beyond duty" acts ratchet up what everyone else is expected to do, so the extra work stops being extra.

So: what's your actual credence, as a number, that supererogation is a genuine and non-reducible category of moral evaluation? By that I mean — there really are acts that are morally better than duty requires, that it would be wrong to demand of someone, and this isn't just a confusing way of describing either (a) duties we're too squeamish to enforce or (b) points on a single better-to-worse scale with no line drawn anywhere.
