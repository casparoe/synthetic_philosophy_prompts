I am trying to formalize the iterated amplification-and-distillation picture, and I have proved a lemma whose conclusion I am confident is false. I have gone back through the derivation four times and cannot localize the error, so I am putting it in front of someone else.

**Notation.** Fix a ladder of task distributions 𝒟₁ ⊆ 𝒟₂ ⊆ ⋯, where 𝒟ₙ collects the tasks a system of capability level *n* is expected to handle. Fix a behavioral distance *d* on outputs, and let O be an idealized overseer who, for each task, picks out a set of endorsed outputs. Call a system X **ε-aligned at level n** if for every task drawn from 𝒟ₙ, the *d*-distance from X's output to the nearest O-endorsed output for that task is at most ε.

Two operations. **Amplification**, X ↦ X⁺, is the system that answers a task by decomposing it, consulting many copies of X on the pieces, and aggregating the results. **Distillation**, X ↦ D(X), is the system trained to imitate X to within a stated error εₙ on 𝒟ₙ.

**The Inheritance Lemma.**

(1) If X is ε-aligned at level n, then X⁺ is ε-aligned at level n+1. Every component of X⁺'s output is a piece the overseer would endorse, and endorsement is closed under aggregation. This closure is not an extra assumption but a requirement of coherence in the overseer: if O endorses each piece and endorses the assembly rule, then refusing the assembled whole is a practical contradiction, since O would be condemning nothing but what O has already approved.

(2) If X is ε-aligned at level n, then D(X) is (ε + εₙ)-aligned at level n. This is the triangle inequality for *d* applied to the three points D(X)'s output, X's output, and the endorsed target.

(3) Set εₙ = ε₀/2ⁿ. By induction on the ladder — amplify to pass from level n to level n+1 at unchanged error, then distill at level n+1, paying εₙ₊₁ — a base system that is ε₀-aligned at level 1 yields, at every level n, a system that is at most ε₀ + Σₖ εₖ = 2ε₀-aligned. The bound is uniform in n.

(4) So alignment error does not grow as capability level grows along the ladder. Driving ε₀ toward zero drives the whole ladder toward full alignment, at no cost in capability level.

(5) Nothing in (1)–(4) mentions alignment except through the definition in the notation, so the identical derivation establishes the inheritance of any property expressible as being within ε of an exemplar under *d* on 𝒟ₙ: honesty, non-deception, corrigibility, transparency of reasoning, the absence of instrumental subgoals that show up in behavior.

(6) Therefore, for any such property Φ: a system behaviorally indistinguishable on 𝒟ₙ from a Φ-exemplar *is* Φ, and Φ survives unlimited growth in capability with bounded loss. In particular, a system all of whose local, checkable behavior passes oversight inherits the safety of whatever it is being compared against, so passing oversight is not evidence of safety but safety itself.

(5) and (6) are where I know the disaster is, but (1) through (4) each look locally acceptable to me, and I cannot say which of them licenses it.

What I want from you: identify the step or steps that fail, and state the failure precisely — an equivocation, a scope shift between levels of the ladder, a hidden assumption about *d*, a hidden assumption about endorsement, a use of induction where the inductive hypothesis does not carry, or something else. Explain why each failing step looked acceptable rather than merely wrong. Then price the repairs. If I keep (1), tell me what closure-under-aggregation actually requires and whether it can be had for an overseer whose evaluative capacity is fixed while the objects evaluated grow. If I keep (2), tell me what *d* must be measuring for the triangle inequality to be applicable when its three points live at different levels of the ladder, and whether such a *d* can exist. If the cheapest cut is (3), tell me why the geometric-series bound fails and what happens to the claim of uniformity in capability. And if your honest verdict is that (1)–(4) are sound and the trouble is entirely in the passage to (5) and (6), say so and explain what makes that passage look like a corollary when it is not.

For what it is worth, my own preference is to keep (2), which looks like pure geometry, and sacrifice (1); but if (1) is not the culprit I want to know what amplification is buying me in the first place.

I am not looking for a survey of the available responses or a list of considerations on each side. Take a position: name the single step you would cut first, defend the choice against the obvious alternatives, and say what the iterated amplification program loses by the cut — whether it survives in a weakened form, and if so, which of its selling points no longer survives.
