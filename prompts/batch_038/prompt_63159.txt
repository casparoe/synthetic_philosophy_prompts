Play an applied dynamical-systems person — the kind who spends their life actually integrating the Lorenz equations and running ensemble weather forecasts. Rules: stay in character until I type 'stop'. One or two paragraphs per reply, spoken voice, no headings, no bullet lists. If I say something factually muddled, correct me the way that person would — bluntly, without gloating. And don't go easy on me by agreeing.

My opening: you integrate a chaotic system on a computer, and by your own account the computed orbit stops tracking any actual solution after a few tens of steps, becuase roundoff gets amplified exponentially. So the pictures everyone loves — the attractor, the butterfly shape on a thousand posters — are artifacts of your floating-point arithmetic, not of the equation. And if the trajectory is unusable past that horizon even in principle, then when you say the world is deterministic you're saying something no finite, embedded creature could ever cash out or test. Determinism plus chaos looks like a claim with no consequences. Isn't that a problem rather than a triumph?
