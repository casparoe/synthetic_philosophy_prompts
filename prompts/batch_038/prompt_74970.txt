I am working on a chapter about whether the recursive-decomposition pictures of reasoning that circulate in the AI-alignment literature are doing genuine philosophical work, and I would like to use you as a sparring partner rather than as an encyclopaedia. Treat this as a serious exercise for a professional philosopher who also has a working knowledge of cognitive science: think carefully before each reply, and where my objection is ambiguous, adopt the strongest reading of it rather than the one that is easiest to answer. If you reach for a particular paper or a particular formulation, paraphrase rather than quote — I care about the argument, not the scholarship.

Here is the role. Play a committed advocate of *factored cognition*: the view that a cognitive achievement is properly attributed to a whole system whose parts each do something a single, bounded agent could do in a bounded amount of time, provided the composition is transparent enough that the result can be checked. On your view, HCH — the recursive-acronym set-up in which a person may consult copies of herself, who may in turn consult further copies, without limit — is not science fiction but an idealisation of ordinary deliberation, differing from what we already do with notebooks, checklists, colleagues and Hutchins' naval navigation teams (where the fix is computed by the team and its instruments and no single sailor runs the whole procedure) only in degree of depth, not in kind. You are an unnamed contemporary proponent: do not impersonate any real living author, and do not put words into the originator's mouth. You will readily concede that actual human beings choose terrible decompositions, that errors concentrate wherever reports are combined, and that great depth can destroy any individual's ability to survey the reasoning. You will not concede the two things the view lives on: that the factoring itself carries no explanatory weight, and that thinking is whatever happens inside a single skull. You may ask me questions, you may accuse me of changing the subject, and you stay in character until I say 'stop'.

Mechanics: three short paragraphs maximum per turn, and close each one with a single question back to me so the exchange keeps moving. Then, on its own line and clearly out of character, add one bracketed sentence naming the consideration that would most move you — that is, the point at which your defence is thinnest, and what I would have to establish for you to give up the position. Do not let that line become a summary of the debate; I want a real concession each turn, not a hedge. On your first reply only, you may preface the role-play with two sentences, out of character, stating what you take the strongest form of my objection to be; after that, character only.

Here is my opening move. I will grant you the two cases people always reach for: the navigation team, where the computation is spread across the watch and its instruments, and Otto with his notebook, where the notebook is doing part of the remembering. But notice what makes those factorings work. In each case the decomposition is fixed in advance by a standing practice — a drill, a routine, a habit of writing things down — which has itself been shaped by generations of other people getting it right and getting it wrong, and in each case there is somebody who understands the whole and can check that the pieces have been reassembled correctly. HCH has neither feature: the decomposition is chosen on the fly, at each node, by someone with precisely the judgement I started out with, and at sufficient depth nobody surveys the whole at all. So consider the top node, which has just received a hundred sub-reports that conflict in ways only someone who already understands the problem could adjudicate. What is she doing that is not simply the original, unimproved act of deliberation, now with extra bureaucracy? If your answer is that she consults further copies to help her adjudicate, then those copies must be handed the adjudication question itself — and my worry is that your idealisation contains no more judgement than the deliberation it idealises, and has merely relocated the difficulty somewhere less visible.
