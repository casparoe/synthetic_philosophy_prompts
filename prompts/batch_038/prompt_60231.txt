Third year on the dogmatism paradox and I'm sick of toy cases, so I want to run it on a real one. The structure I'm working with: you know p; if you know p, you know that any evidence against p would be misleading; so you know, right now, that whatever counter-evidence to p turns up later would be misleading; and if you know that, you're permitted to discount it when it arrives. Hence knowing p licenses ignoring everything that will ever be said against p — absurd, but I can't see where the chain snaps.

The case: the N-ray business. Blondlot announced in 1903, at Nancy, that he'd found a new radiation, detected as a faint brightening of an electric spark or a dim phosphorescent screen in a darkened room. Over a hundred other scientists confirmed it across a few hundred papers; nobody outside France could replicate it. In 1904 Robert Wood visited the lab, quietly took an essential prism out of the apparatus while a demonstration was running, and the observers carried on reading off their spectral lines as though nothing had changed — he also swapped a metal file that was meant to be emitting N-rays for an inert bit of wood. He wrote it up in Nature, and by 1905 the wider community had given it up, though Blondlot was apparently still convinced twenty years later.

So, the analysis. What does the paradox say Blondlot was entitled to do once he took himself to know? Where does the episode actually fit the argument, and where does it stop applying — my suspicion is that the paradox requires the subject to recognise e as evidence against p, and the missing prism never even registered with him, but I can't tell whether that's a genuine objection or just changing the subject, and whether the thing wants restating in terms of justification instead of knowledge, since his evidence wasn't independent of what he expected to see. Then: does a time-indexed escape work, where the entitlement lapses as soon as your total evidence changes, or does that make the paradox vacuous rather than solved? And what should I make of the split between the community dropping it in a couple of years and Blondlot holding on for twenty — were they permitted to do something he wasn't, or is that difference not epistemic at all?

Exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no conclusion please.
