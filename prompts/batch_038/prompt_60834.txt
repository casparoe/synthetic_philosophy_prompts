Next term I am teaching an eighty-minute session on embedded agency to a mixed seminar: second-year philosophy doctoral students who have done decision theory and philosophy of mind but no machine learning, and a handful of machine learning masters students with the reverse profile. I do not want an exposition of the topic from you. I want the architecture of the delivery, concrete enough that a colleague could take it and teach the session without consulting me.

Begin by checking my decomposition, because the ordering depends on it. As I currently have it, placing an agent inside the environment it models makes at least three things go wrong at once:

- the agent's own actions alter the evidence it will subsequently receive, so its observations are not the kind of sample from the environment that the standard picture assumes it has;
- the agent's decision procedure is itself part of the world, available in principle to be modelled by others and by itself, which puts pressure on the step in which the agent is simply represented as choosing;
- the division between agent and environment is not given but selected, and different selections yield different verdicts about what the agent knows and what it is trying to do.

Tell me whether these are genuinely three problems or whether one collapses into another, and whether I am missing a fourth without which these three do not bite. Then order them for delivery, and justify the ordering strictly in terms of what depends on what — which item a student must already have in hand before the next one is intelligible, rather than which is easiest.

Three constraints on the session itself.

First, the whole thing must be carried by a single running case, and that case may not involve an artificial agent: no robots, no language models, no idealised reasoners with utility functions over strings of bits. I want something whose stupidity is beside the point — a bacterium moving up a nutrient gradient it is simultaneously consuming, a slime mould, a firm, a thermostat in a room whose temperature it is altering. Choose one, or propose a better candidate and defend the choice, and tell me what the case must have for all three problems to surface in it, together with what it must lack. The danger I am managing is a case rich enough that the students dismiss the difficulty as an artefact of the creature being primitive.

Second, the words intelligence, artificial general intelligence, and superintelligence may not appear before the forty-minute mark. When they entered the room last year, the machine learning students took the floor and the philosophers stopped contributing. The conceptual work has to be finished before the vocabulary that triggers the takeover arrives.

Third, the students arrive holding the idealised picture: an agent as a function from observation histories to actions, equipped with a prior over environments in which the agent itself does not appear. That picture is the thing the session dismantles, and I would rather dismantle it than announce that it is wrong.

From last year's run I have four diagnosed misconceptions, and I want a placement for each: that this is the frame problem under a new name; that self-locating or indexical belief disposes of it, on the grounds that once the agent can think about where and when it is, it has located itself in its own model; that there is no conceptual problem here at all, only an engineering one about compute and model quality; and, from the philosophers, that this is simply intentionality restated — a mind is directed at a world, so what is new? For each, give me the precise point in the sequence at which it should be headed off, the single line or question I should deploy at that point, and why intervening earlier or later fails.

Close with two things. One question I can put to the room cold at minute seventy-five that a student who has memorised the vocabulary will answer wrongly and a student who has understood will answer rightly, together with what each of those two answers sounds like. And an explicit list of what I should leave out of eighty minutes and announce as left out, with the reason each omission pays for itself. Do not recommend readings; the syllabus is fixed.
