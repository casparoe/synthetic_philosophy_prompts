ok so my school's lit mag takes submissions and I'm in this after-school phil club where we spent like three weeks on Benatar's asymmetry — the four-part thing where pain being absent is good even when nobody's around to get the benefit, but pleasure being absent isn't bad unless there's somebody who misses out. I want to write a poem about it but I hate poems that just explain a theory, so here's the constraint I came up with and I need you to actually stick to it: every stanza is a quatrain where lines 1–2 are about something being present and lines 3–4 are about the same thing being absent, and then the last stanza just stops after two lines. That dropped half has to be the point — nobody complains about the gap because there was never anyone to complain, so the reader shouldn't feel cheated either. Make the present lines rhyme properly and the absent lines rhyme slantwise, almost matching but not quite, so you can hear the switch each time.

Ground it in something real instead of abstract. Maybe use my situation: my mom still talks about the third kid she decided not to have like it's a person who got left somewhere, which I think is exactly the confusion the whole argument is supposed to clear up. Four quatrains plus the two-line ending, 18 lines total. Don't use the words asymmetry, nonexistence, or harm anywhere, don't name any philosophers, and it should still work as a poem for someone who's never heard of any of this.
