So I co-host a small podcast on animal minds and next month's guest is an invertebrate-welfare researcher who's gotten known for a position I'd loosely call thresholded non-aggregation. Her view, as I understand it: there's some probability p* — she floats something around 5–10% — below which you shouldn't treat a creature as sentient for purposes of adding welfare up across huge populations, but above which you shouldn't ignore it when you're deciding about one individual facing a serious harm. So she'll happily grant that insects might well be sentient, and that if they are the numbers in play are staggering, but she denies that you should multiply a low credence by a huge number of lives and let the product drive policy. She isn't an expected-value sceptic in general. Her story is that the threshold tracks "thin" experience — a creature can have states with so little unified phenomenal character that they don't aggregate the way ordinary pains do — and she uses the usual indicator stuff (associative learning, nociceptive trade-offs, motivational prioritisation, that kind of thing) to say roughly where the line sits.

Write me 8–10 questions that don't just let her restate all this. I want at least one on how p* is suppossed to get fixed without being arbitrary, one on the incoherence / dutch-book style worry about having a sharp cutoff sitting inside your credences, one on what her rule implies for humans (someone under anaesthesia, a newborn, a patient with a disorder of consciousness), one on whether "thin experience" is doing any actual work or just redescribing her prior about insects, and one that forces her to say which empirical finding would make her move p* and in which direction. Also work in the puzzle that she'd apparently intervene for a single drowning bee but shrug at a pesticide regime killing trillions, and ask her why continuous credence-weighting of moral status isn't just the obvious alternative to a threshold — and if she says it is, what's left of her view.

For each question, give me the follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. She's very good at retreating to "we're just being appropriately humble under uncertainty," so at least two of the follow-ups need to close that door rather than walk into it. Then at the end add a couple of lines on where you think my description is a strawman of the strongest available version of this position, and what the strongest version would say instead.
