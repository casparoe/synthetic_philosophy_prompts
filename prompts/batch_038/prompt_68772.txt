Construct a thought experiment in which AI safety via debate fails for a reason that is not one of the usual suspects. The setup must be clean: two perfectly rational model-debaters, no private channel between them, no collusion, no smuggled-in deceptive alignment, and a judge who is neither stupid, inattentive, nor corrupt. Yet after optimal play the judge endorses a false answer. The mechanism must be quantifier structure rather than judge frailty: the winning side asserts that some item meeting a checkable condition exists, while the losing side must establish that no such item exists anywhere in a space too large to enumerate inside the round budget, so that the standard intuition that a lie is harder to defend than a truth simply does not apply.

Situate the debate in a realistic oversight task — for instance, whether a candidate model discloses a secret on some input, or whether a training corpus contains any prohibited example — and keep the scenario itself to two paragraphs.

Then verify your construction. State the question under debate, each side's optimal line of play, and why the honest side, though it never misstates anything, has no winning strategy at that round budget. Identify the single feature doing the work, and show that removing it — more rounds, a judge licensed to run randomized spot-checks, or recasting the question as a comparison instead of a search — restores the truth-wins result. Close with the strongest reply a defender of debate would make, including the charge that your honest debater was not in fact playing optimally, and an honest verdict on whether your case generates its outcome or merely stipulates it.
