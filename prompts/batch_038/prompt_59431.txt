I convene a monthly workshop whose members are split roughly evenly between philosophy graduate students and engineers who build and audit machine-learning systems. Our recurring argument is whether the internal states of a trained model are about anything in the way a belief is about anything. It never resolves, because the two halves of the room use the same words out of incompatible background commitments, and every attempt to settle it by asking people which view they hold degenerates into people reporting labels they have picked up secondhand. I want to replace that argument with a diagnostic instrument: a sorting quiz that assigns each member to a named position in the debate over underived versus derived intentionality strictly on the basis of what they say about concrete cases.

Deliverables, in this order:

1. **The logical space, before the instrument.** Identify the three or four independent axes along which the positions genuinely differ — what fixes a state's content (internal physical structure, causal history, current deployment environment, selective or design history, an interpreter's ascription), whether a state can be wrong about its object at all, whether the verdict depends on what the system is made of, whether content ascriptions are truth-apt rather than merely useful. State each axis as a question with a small number of answers, then show that the positions in item 3 occupy distinct cells.

2. **Nine to eleven diagnostic items.** Each item describes one engineered artifact with a build history and a deployment record, at the level of specificity an auditor would find in a system datasheet, and offers two or three statements the respondent may sign off on. Build the items from the following cases; rewrite them into your own scenarios rather than reproducing the standard tellings, so that a respondent who has memorized the canonical thought experiments cannot pattern-match to a memorized answer:
   - a coin-recognition mechanism of fixed physical design, built where its state-type is triggered only by one denomination and then shipped to a second currency regime whose coins are physically indistinguishable from that denomination, so that the same state-type is now triggered by them; nothing inside the machine changed when it crossed the border;
   - a component that is physically identical in two installations yet serves as a one-place register in one larger machine and a two-place register in the other;
   - a perception module whose designers intended its symbol to fire for a single natural kind but which in deployment is reliably triggered by a wider set — images, models, look-alikes — and whose downstream behavior succeeds on all of them;
   - a model whose entire training corpus concerned a kind of thing it has never been causally connected to, and which nevertheless uses its symbol for that kind fluently and productively;
   - an exact duplicate of a working system's architecture and current state, brought online for the first time in a fresh environment, with no causal commerce and no selective history of its own;
   - two systems with identical internal functional organization and identical behavior in the same environment, one of which was shaped by a selection process for that task and one of which was not;
   - a stateless device that sorts its inputs reliably enough that we all describe it as detecting a feature, though nothing persists in it between inputs;
   - a system that applies a label it would retract under questioning while acting correctly in every instance: is its state in error, or is its content other than we assumed;
   - a system whose behavior is predicted equally well by an intentional description and by a purely mechanical one, and where no one has ever in fact adopted the intentional description;
   - a wholly inorganic system otherwise indistinguishable from a colleague, together with a stipulation, from a source the respondent trusts, that there is nothing it is like to be it and that no further evidence will arrive.

3. **A scoring key.** For each item, state what each available answer commits the respondent to, then give the decision tree — text or ASCII — that carries a complete answer pattern to a leaf. The leaves must include at least: the Searlean position that only living nervous tissue underwrites underived content; Dennett's denial that the original/derived distinction survives the wandering two-bitser, with his insistence that this does not make the intentional description unreal; teleosemantic or etiological-function views; naturalized covariance views that owe an account of how a state can misrepresent; internal-role or two-factor views; views that tie content to what-it-is-like; and eliminativism about content. Add any further leaf the space requires.

4. **Unoccupied cells.** List the combinations of answers your axes permit that no leaf occupies, and for each say whether the cell is empty because the view is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or because it has merely gone unexplored.

5. **Inseparable leaves.** Flag every pair of positions your instrument cannot tell apart given datasheet-level evidence alone, and say what further evidence would separate them, or why nothing could. Include at least one item deliberately designed to drive a wedge between the etiological family and the covariance family, and one to drive a wedge between substrate-restricted views and what-it-is-like views; if either wedge fails, say so rather than pretending the tree is sharper than it is.

6. **Incoherent patterns.** These will be the plurality in my room. For each answer pattern that lands in no leaf, identify the single item whose reversal would restore consistency, and name the position the respondent is one step from.

Two constraints on the items themselves. No item may use the debate's own vocabulary — original, derived, intrinsic, aboutness, semantic, intentional, interpretation, representation — and no item may name a philosopher or a theory; a respondent with no background in philosophy of mind must be able to answer every item from the case description alone, and must not be able to tell from an item's phrasing which leaf it leads toward. And no citations, no bibliography, no reading list: the instrument should stand on the cases and the axes. Keep the whole thing under 1,600 words.
