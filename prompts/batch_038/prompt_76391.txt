Please write a consumer product manual — the thin grey booklet stapled into a cardboard box — for a fictional implanted medical device: a counterfactual intervener. The unit monitors a user's decision-making. If it detects that the user is about to choose something other than the outcome selected during configuration, it intervenes to bring about the configured outcome anyway. In normal operation it never fires, and the user's choices proceed entirely on their own.

**Form and voice.** Numbered sections under headings, roughly 900 to 1,100 words in total: Overview and Intended Use; Before Your First Decision; Operating the Detection Module; Troubleshooting (containing one short table with columns for Symptom, Probable Cause, Corrective Action); Safety Warnings; Accessories and Optional Modules; Warranty and Limitation of Liability; Recall Notice. Maintain a deadpan corporate-technical register throughout — model numbers, revision codes, torque-free prose, a customer-service footer, the flat cheerfulness of small-appliance documentation. No winks to the reader, and no passage that concedes the piece is about philosophy.

**What the manual must be doing.** The humor has to come from the manual getting the dialectic right, so that a reader acquainted with the debate over whether moral responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise (the principle of alternative possibilities) can name the objection each clause exists to answer. Work through the following in fine print, without naming any philosopher, paper, or principle, and without ever presenting any of it as an objection:

- whether anything alternative survives installation — the user might still have displayed the warning sign, might still have been intervened upon, might still have hesitated — and precisely what the manufacturer is prepared to concede about each residue;
- whether the detection module is set to fire on a sign that guarantees the configured outcome or merely on one that correlates with it, with the manual saying something different, and something consequential, under each setting;
- an optional upgrade that guarantees the configured outcome while emitting no detectable prior sign, marketed as remedying a design flaw in earlier revisions, complete with a matching revision history;
- a disclaimer covering users whose character, history, or circumstances already fix the outcome without the device, and the resulting question of what the device was ever sold to demonstrate in their case;
- a limitation-of-liability section that accepts full blame for bad outcomes while declining any corresponding credit for good ones, with the reasoning buried inside the hedging;
- a recall notice for a production batch whose detectors proved readable in advance, and whose users were consequently tested only under conditions that presupposed the matter in dispute;
- a short What Your Device Cannot Do section distinguishing outcomes the user could not have avoided from outcomes the user could not have avoided in any sense that would matter to a moral assessment.

**Target.** The satire is aimed at the practice of engineering thought experiments — of bolting on mechanisms until the case performs the work the argument needs it to perform — and at the serenity with which a specification sheet settles a question about responsibility. Every mechanism the manual describes should correspond to a move genuinely available in that debate, exaggerated rather than fabricated, so that a specialist finds the piece fair and can say what each clause costs its author. The manufacturer, model names, revision codes, batch numbers, and any customer testimonials must be invented; the piece must not lean on real names, titles, or dates.

**Legibility.** A reader with no background in the subject should still find the document coherent, plausible, and faintly sinister, carried entirely by its corporate register. Aim for a piece that means everything it says and is committed to nothing.

Close with a compliance line and one final small-print clause stating the manufacturer's position on whether the user could have done otherwise, in language so qualified that it cannot be read as taking one.
