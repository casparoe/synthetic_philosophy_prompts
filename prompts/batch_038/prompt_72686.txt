So I'm not a philosophy person, I write backend code, but my friend Nadia reads a lot of Marx and political theory and we argue about this stuff over lunch. She has very crisp verdicts on whether a given arrangement counts as exploitation, and I've been trying to reverse engineer her rule. Every rule I write down either overfits or contradicts one of her calls, which is were I'm stuck.

Her verdicts so far, all from the last couple of months:

- Plasma center paying $50 a visit, most donors clearly broke: exploitation. Legal, voluntary, and the donors are better off than not donating, and she still says yes. Her reason was "nobody with options does that."
- Grad TAs teaching the intro sections while the university collects full tuition: exploitation.
- Gig courier on his own scooter, no benefits, algorithm assigns his shifts: exploitation. I said he owns his scooter and she said that's beside the point.
- Tenant whose building got bought by an investment firm that hiked the rent 30%: exploitation. She said the firm isn't doing anything, it just owns the place.
- Drug company charging $700 for an adrenaline pen that costs a few dollars to make: exploitation, even though I can't see whose labor is being taken.
- Her brother in law the plumber, sets his own rates, owns his van: not exploitation.
- A friend of hers who quit to join a worker-owned bakery co-op: not exploitation, "they own it, that's the whole difference."
- A law firm partner making $400k: not exploitation, she's on the other side of the table.
- A freelance dev I know who bills $200/hr with three other clients lined up: not exploitation, "he can walk."

Here's the awkward part. The next case is me. I'm a staff engineer at a mid-size company. I make around $400k counting stock. I could quit tomorrow and be fine for a year. I also wrote most of the system the product runs on, revenue per employee is something like $1.2M, margins are in the 30s, and the founders and the VCs got the overwhelming majority of that rather than me. On top of which my manager can kill my project, put me on a performance plan, or lay me off, and I carry a pager.

So is she going to say I'm exploited? Lead with your best guess in one sentence, then do the real work: which candidate rules fit all nine of her verdicts (I'm assuming more than one does, that's the entire problem), what each of them predicts about me, which one you think she's actually running and why, and what single extra case you'd throw at her to break the tie along with what each candidate rule would say about that case.

Under 500 words please, and no reading list or citations — I just want the reasoning.
