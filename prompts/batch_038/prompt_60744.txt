No lecture up front, and nobody should hear the word indeterminacy until after the game is over.

Set it up as radical translation: small teams get a corpus and have to produce a translation manual that predicts a native's assent and disent. I don't want to need an actor, so please write the native's behavior as a printable response rule-sheet that a facilitator can just read off (plus a short variant for if I can get a TA to play the native live). Give me: rules, materials list, a scoring scheme that doesn't need real money, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the distinction I care about — including the moment someone inevitably says we should just be charitable and pick the natural reading, where I need a good comeback showing charity comes out symmetric here. Then three things that will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and what to do in the moment, and an honest paragraph on which feature of Quine's view the game can't reproduce, since in the game there obviously is a fact of the matter: the rule sheet.
