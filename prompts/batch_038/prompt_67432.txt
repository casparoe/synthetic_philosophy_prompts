I host on Airbnb and I've gotten weirdly absorbed in how the review system is actually built. Both sides rate each other, but neither can see the other's review until both are in or the 14 days are up, whichever comes first. And as far as I can tell, once they're published either author can still ask to have their own taken down for another 30 days. On top of that the platform tells hosts not to fish for 5 stars, doesn't moderate reviews beforehand, and reserves the right to just deactivate you.

What picture of cooperation does this whole apparatus presuppose? The two-way rating looks like a bet that reputation alone can police two strangers who will probably never meet again. But the blind simultaneous reveal looks like the opposite bet -- that a reputation you can aim at somebody turns your rating into a threat and theirs into a counter-threat. Reconstruct what the design is actually committed to, seperate what it strictly needs from what it merely happens to fit, and say whether the parts are consistent with each other. That 30-day take-back especially, since it seems to hand straight back the leverage the blind reveal was there to remove. And what would have to change if they dropped the incentives story entirely and treated trustworthiness as a trait of character instead? Please also tell me how confident you are in your reconstruction and which piece of it you're least sure about.
