I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training whatsoever — I work on a metrics/observability platform, so my whole life is emitters, cardinality, aggregation windows, dashboards, alert routing and being on call. A friend sent me a long piece about panpsychism a few weeks back and I have not been able to let it go, mostly because of the ethics angle rather than the consciousness angle. The version I read ran something like this: physics only ever tells you what matter *does* — how it behaves, what it disposes to — and never what it *is* in itself, so maybe the intrinsic side of physical stuff is experience, all the way down to the basics. And if that's right, then the universe contains an insane amount of experience, most of it not attached to anything with a brain, and possibly a lot of it is bad experience, and nobody anywhere is keeping a tally. I'm not asking for a lecture on the history of any of this and I don't want a reading list — I can't look anything up, so please don't send me off to go find sources. I want a translation, into the only vocabulary I'm actually fluent in.

Here's the specific job. Take three claims and restate each in the language of a large-scale telemetry system, as honestly as you can. First, the intrinsic-nature claim: physical structure is pure relation and disposition, and the categorical base filling it in is experiential. Second, the combination problem, which is the part that actually broke my brain: how do a huge number of tiny subject-ish things add up to one subject like me, with one unified point of view, when they're not literally fused together. Third, the ethical claim: that the moral inventory of reality is vastly bigger than we assume and includes morally relevant, possibly negatively valenced experience at the bottom layer, such that we might owe something to it, or at least can't treat the physical world as a morally empty substrate to build on. Use my terms freely — emitters, series cardinality, sampling, fan-in and fan-out, backpressure, aggregation windows, SLOs, error budgets, ownership and routing rules, the difference between a signal existing and someone being paged about it. For each of the three, tell me what maps cleanly, what only maps if you bend it out of shape, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Define any philosophy jargon in a clause when you use it, because I won't know it.

And then I want you to attack a hypothesis of mine, because I suspect it's self-serving. My gut says the combination problem is just an aggregation problem: thirty million emitters, one dashboard, one view, done, mystery solved. But there's a thing in my own system that nags at me — the aggregate doesn't exist anywhere as a running process, it only comes into being when somebody executes a query, and no component of the pipeline is the sum. So: is that precisely the panpsychist's complaint, that a rollup is not a subject? If yes, what would have to be true of my pipeline for there to be something it's like to be the top of it rather than just a query result sitting in a table — and can you say that without just restating "it would have to be conscious"? Finish with a verdict on the residue: does the stuff that refused to translate show that my vocabulary is missing something real, or does it show the original position was vaguer than it sounded when my friend described it? One last request — don't smuggle functionalist assumptions in and label them translation. If the honest answer is that "the information is integrated and routed" and "someone experiences it" have collapsed into the same move in my head, say so bluntly, because that collapse is probably why I thought the problem was easy.
