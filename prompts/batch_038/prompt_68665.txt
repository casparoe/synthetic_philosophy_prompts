I look after the fairness checks on our hiring models at a large-ish public-sector organisation — not a philosopher, just someone who reads around the edges of this. You're the expert here, so please actually think it through: if it helps, brainstorm the candidate readings first and then settle on one reconstruction.

Our written prodecure for any automated screening tool requires all three of these: (a) the tool's score must predict our annual performance rating, and one and the same numeric cutoff is applied to every applicant group; (b) each protected group's selection rate must come out at no less than four-fifths of the highest group's; (c) among applicants who would in fact have performed well, the share we wrongly screen out must be roughly equal across groups. If a tool fails (b) or (c) we first try and fix it upstream — drop features, retrain, widen the validation sample. If it still fails, we set a different cutoff per group until everything sits inside tolerance, and file a note saying the conflict was 'managed'. The performance rating itself is never questioned; the whole edifice is anchored to it.

I keep being told all three are measures of fairness, and they plainly pull against each other, but nobody has ever spelt out what the procedure assumes. So: what is fairness supposed to be a property of — the score, the decision, or the world the training data came from? Are the differing base rates between groups treated as legitimate inputs to condition on, or as the thing justice is about? Does the group-specific cutoff rescue sit inside (a), or quietly abandon what (a) claims? And what does anchoring everything to a supervisor's rating presuppose about merit — must the procedure have a theory of merit in order to work, or would several rival theories license it equally well?

Please seperate what it strictly commits us to from what it merely fits comfortably with, say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, name the rival views it rules out without anyone having decided to, and tell me what we'd have to change if we swapped one of them in. No reading list or citations — I only want the reconstruction. American English, please.
