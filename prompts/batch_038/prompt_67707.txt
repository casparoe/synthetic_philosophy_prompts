so my labmate and I have been going in circles about pain asymbolia for like three weeks and it's starting to wreck our reading group. quick recap of the case as I understand it, since everything turns on it: patients with lesions around the posterior insula / parietal operculum who, when you pinch them or bring something hot near their hand, say yes that's pain, can localize it and describe its quality, but just don't seem to mind — no grimace, no withdrawal, sometimes they chat or even laugh, and in at least one reported case the patient let a cigarette burn down between her fingers without moving. They aren't globally flattened: fear and disgust look intact, they can tell you pain is supposed to be bad, they still classify the stimulus as painful. Then on one side you have the morphine and cingulotomy reports, which as far as I can tell amount to the pain still being present but distant, no longer bothering the person; and on the other side the chili-heads and deep-tissue-massage people who claim to enjoy the hurting, wounded soldiers who turn down analgesia, and people born without pain who end up with chewed-through tongues and fractures that never got noticed. I'm trying to write the intro to a proposal about weighing pain relief against other claims in priority setting, and I can't do it until I know what I'd even be asking a theory to do.

Neither of us is willing to just declare the patient a misreporter.
