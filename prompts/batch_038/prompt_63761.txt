Contractualism about wrongness, in Scanlon's formulation, asks which principles for the general regulation of behaviour no one could reasonably reject, and answers by comparing the strength of the complaints individuals would have against candidate principles. Its most distinctive commitment is the individualist restriction: only the complaints of individuals, taken one at a time, carry justificatory weight. That commitment appears to block pooling many small burdens against one large one, and so appears to commit the contractualist to letting one person die rather than relieving a billion headaches. Every repair seems either to abandon the restriction or to smuggle in a threshold whose location is stipulated rather than argued. I want a ranked assessment of the repairs on offer.

Outright consequentialism is excluded from the shortlist by stipulation: a view that discards the individualist restriction and simply minimises total burden is not a repair of contractualism but a surrender, and the list below contains only proposals that retain some role for complaint-by-complaint justification.

**The shortlist.** Each principle is defined operationally. Judge it exactly as defined here.

- **P1 — Strict Pairwise.** Rejectability is settled by comparing the single strongest complaint on each side. The side with the weaker strongest complaint loses, no matter how many people stand behind it. Complaints of different individuals never combine. Where the two strongest complaints are exactly equal in severity, the options are balanced.
- **P2 — Discounted Justifiability.** A person's complaint is discounted by the cost that honouring it would impose on others, so that it carries full weight only when the alternative is cheap for everyone else. The discount applied to a complaint increases with both the number and the severity of the burdens that honouring it would impose, approaching full discount only asymptotically; the theory supplies no formula for the discount function.
- **P3 — Tiered Lexicography.** Complaints are sorted into severity bands: death or permanent disabling injury; serious but fully reversible injury; substantial but temporary pain or loss; minor inconvenience. Only the highest band anyone occupies counts; complaints in lower bands receive zero weight. Within the highest occupied band, complaints are summed on a cardinal scale and the larger sum prevails.
- **P4 — Significance Floor.** Fix a floor at some fraction — say one per cent — of the strongest complaint in the choice situation, measured on a cardinal scale of burden. Every complaint at or above the floor is summed across persons; every complaint below the floor is discarded. The larger sum prevails.
- **P5 — Dampened Amplification.** The complaint on the many side equals the strength of one complaint on that side multiplied by a factor that increases with the number of complainants but sub-linearly — for instance log(n+1) or n^0.5. The amplified complaint is then compared directly with the single complaint on the other side, and the stronger prevails.
- **P6 — Weighted Lottery.** Each person holds a claim whose weight is proportional to the severity of the burden she faces. When claims conflict and cannot all be honoured, the agent randomises, giving each option a chance of selection proportional to the sum of the claim-weights on its side. Aggregation re-enters as chances rather than as sums, and the output is a probability rather than a choice.
- **P7 — Dual Standpoint.** Evaluate each option twice: impersonally, by the total well-being it produces, and person-affectingly, by how it treats the particular individuals it affects. When both standpoints favour the same option, choose it. When they conflict, follow the person-affecting verdict unless the impersonal advantage of the other option exceeds the person-affecting advantage by a fixed factor F greater than 1; the theory does not derive F.

**The battery.** In every case the agent can bring about exactly one of the two options, there are no further effects on anyone, all individuals are strangers to the agent and to one another, and outcomes are as described with certainty unless the case says otherwise.

- **C1 — Headaches.** A: prevent one person's death within the hour. B: prevent a mild one-hour headache for each of 10,000 people.
- **C2 — Embarrassments.** A: prevent one person's death. B: prevent one second of mild embarrassment for each of 1,000,000,000 people.
- **C3 — Two and three.** A: prevent two people from dying. B: prevent three people from dying; all five are identically situated in every other respect.
- **C4 — The trifle.** A: prevent one person's death. B: prevent a different person's death and, in addition, spare a third person a papercut.
- **C5 — Near threshold.** A: prevent one person from permanently losing the use of both legs. B: prevent each of 100,000 people from breaking a finger, with complete recovery and no lasting impairment within a month.
- **C6 — Equal expectations.** A: one identified person dies for certain. B: each of 1,000,000 people independently faces a one-in-a-million chance of death from the same cause, so that the expected number of deaths is exactly one.
- **C7 — Concentrated migraines.** A: prevent one person's death. B: spare a different person one hour of severe migraine each week for ten years — roughly 520 hours — with no lasting injury.

**What I want back.**

1. A verdict matrix: rows P1–P7, columns C1–C7. Each cell contains only one of the following codes — **A**, **B**, **BAL** (the principle holds the options to be exactly balanced), **LOT** (the principle outputs chances; give the probability it assigns to A in parentheses), or **IND** (the principle as defined here does not determine an answer). No prose inside the matrix.
2. Your own verdict in each of the seven cases, with one sentence of support for each. Commit to a verdict; do not answer that the case turns on further facts.
3. Four scores for each principle, each on a 0–10 scale to one decimal place, with one sentence per principle covering all four:
   - *Structural fidelity* (weight 0.30): how well the principle preserves the distinctively contractualist idea that justification runs through each individual's own complaint rather than through a sum over states of affairs.
   - *Extensional adequacy* (weight 0.35): not a matter of judgement — compute it as 10 × (number of the seven cases in which the principle's output matches your own verdict from item 2) ÷ 7. A LOT cell matches if it assigns a probability above one half to your favoured option and not otherwise; an IND cell matches only if your own verdict is that the case is genuinely indeterminate; BAL matches only if your own verdict is that the options are exactly balanced.
   - *Determinacy* (weight 0.20): whether it yields a unique verdict across the battery without free parameters the agent must set case by case. IND cells are direct evidence against a high score.
   - *Non-ad-hocery* (weight 0.15): whether its thresholds, bands, damping exponents, and multipliers are motivated independently of the verdicts they happen to produce.
4. The weighted total for each principle (0.30, 0.35, 0.20, 0.15) to one decimal place, and a strict ranking consistent with those totals — no ties, and no ranking that your own arithmetic contradicts. If two totals tie at one decimal, break the tie on structural fidelity, then on non-ad-hocery. State in one or two sentences whether you would choose different weights and whether any defensible change would move the top of your ranking.
5. For each principle, the single case in the battery that does it the most damage, and one sentence saying why. Where you recorded IND, name the most natural completion you would supply and what the principle would then say in that case.
6. Which principle you would be willing to defend in print, and the specific commitment you accept as the price of defending it, identifying the case in which that price is highest.
7. Any pair of the seven that you believe differ only verbally, together with what would have to be true for the difference between them to be substantive.

No citations, no attributions, no reading list, and no appeals to what any philosopher has in fact said about these matters: the definitions above are the whole of the material, and your job is to evaluate them rather than to locate them.
