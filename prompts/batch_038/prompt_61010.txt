I spent thirty-eight years as a comparative physiologist, most of them working on soil-dwelling acellular organisms, and I have read philosophy seriously only since retiring. I should like your help with a comparison that has come to preoccupy me, and I will describe my organism carefully enough that you will not need any prior acquaintance with the literature on it.

Dennett's fourfold scheme, set out in Kinds of Minds (1996), sorts creatures by the means they have of adjusting themselves to the world. Darwinian creatures are adjusted by natural selection alone, their dispositions fixed at design time. Skinnerian creatures can be reshaped within a lifetime by reinforcement. Popperian creatures carry an internal model that permits candidate actions to be generated and rejected before they are performed, so that, in Dennett's borrowing from Popper, the hypotheses are allowed to perish in the organism's stead. Gregorian creatures go further by using externally fashioned tools, language above all, which enlarge what they are able to think about. As I read him, these four are cumulative rather than merely contrastive: each higher kind retains the mechanisms of the kinds below it and adds a new one.

My organism appears to violate that ordering. It has no nervous system, and in three decades I never obtained evidence that persuaded me it represented alternative futures to itself in any sense worth calling a model. What it does have is a habit of depositing an inert secretion wherever it has already crept, and of subsequently refusing to cross that secretion. Over months, in ground that is not disturbed, this accumulates into an extensive and physically persistent record of where the colony has been. A naive fragment introduced into that ground, one that has never encountered the terrain, negotiates it with an efficiency comparable to that of the colony which laid the record down; and if a heavy rain washes the ground clean, the efficiency vanishes, although the genetic material and the immediate sensory apparatus are quite unchanged. Two features strike me as philosophically load-bearing. First, the record is a byproduct of locomotion and not something the organism makes in order to remember; so far as I can tell, selection has never shaped it for that function. Second, it outlives the individuals that produced it, and later individuals inherit it by contact with the ground rather than through the germ line.

Here is the comparison I want you to make. On one side stands the cumulative reading of the hierarchy, on which a creature cannot be Gregorian unless it is already Popperian, because only an internal modeler could recognize an aid as an aid and deploy it deliberately. On the other side stands a rival reading I have been turning over, according to which internal simulation and externalized memory are independent axes rather than successive rungs: a lineage may acquire a store outside its own body without ever having modeled anything, and may conversely be a sophisticated modeler with no external scaffolding whatsoever.

Lay the two accounts against one another and judge them. Which better accommodates the organism I have described? Does the cumulative reading have resources to absorb it, by denying that an undesigned secretion is a tool, or that trail-following is memory in the relevant sense, and if so, is that absorption principled or merely a means of insulating the hierarchy from counterexample? Does the two-axis reading, in its turn, forfeit something the hierarchy was meant to capture, namely the intuition that each stage makes available forms of flexibility that the preceding one could not achieve at any cost? And please be explicit about evidential standards: what observation, in the field or at the bench, ought to make me abandon one account in favor of the other?

A smaller question may bear on the larger one. Ants likewise navigate by traces they leave behind, and nobody is tempted to call them Gregorian. If you judge my organism to differ from an ant in the relevant respect, say precisely where the difference lies; if you judge that it does not differ, say what that implies for how much work the Gregorian category can be asked to do.
