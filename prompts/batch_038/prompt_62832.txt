**The case (the Harbour case).** Dana owns the cottage. Three holdings are available to her at every moment of the sequence, and there is no uncertainty about the world: the cottage, the mill, and the ferry licence. Her all-things-considered preferences at a time are cyclic: cottage ≻ mill ≻ ferry ≻ cottage. Each swap incurs a fixed fee of 1. I stipulate that each pairwise margin is strong but bounded: she prefers any holding she ranks higher even after paying a fee of 1, but she prefers a holding she ranks lower to a higher-ranked holding that has cost her 2 or more in fees. She also has the ordinary attitude toward fees: for any holding, keeping it beats keeping it after paying a fee. An adversarial trader who knows all of this offers, in sequence: pay 1 to swap the cottage for the ferry; then pay 1 to swap the ferry for the mill; then pay 1 to swap the mill for the cottage. A myopic Dana takes all three and ends where she started, minus 3.

Additional stipulations: Dana cannot precommit, self-bind, or buy any commitment device; she has perfect foresight of her own future choices and perfect memory of what she has already paid; and she chooses by sophisticated choice, i.e. at each node she picks whichever option leads to the final bundle she most prefers, taking her own future choice behaviour as given.

**Step one, before any probabilities.** Redo the backward induction from the last node forward and tell me what the sophisticated Dana actually does. I got a result that surprised me: she does *not* refuse the first trade; she accepts it, refuses the second, and ends up holding the ferry with 1 paid — so the standard pump fails against her, but she still pays something and still refuses, at the second node, a swap she prefers at that moment. Then answer two structural questions, because I think they decide everything: (i) in order to refuse at the second node, Dana must compare the ferry-minus-1 against the cottage-minus-3 — is that comparison determined by what I have stipulated, or does it require extra structure she does not have? (ii) if she could precommit, she would have to rank whole plans, and the plan-space seems to inherit her cycle, so there may be no best plan to commit to. Does that defeat the standard "cyclicity is harmless if you can precommit" reply?

**The proposition I want a number on.** P: cyclicity is not in itself a rational defect. That is, an agent who meets every other requirement of instrumental rationality but has cyclic all-things-considered preferences over simultaneously available outcomes is not irrational *in virtue of the cyclicity*; the intuitive force of the money-pump argument is fully explained by auxiliary assumptions — myopia, no precommitment, imperfect foresight or memory, or transaction costs — and once those are controlled for, nothing objectionable is left standing.

**Three objections I want you to weigh, in my labels.**
1. *Relocation.* The escape route requires Dana to rank money-soured bundles and, for precommitment, to rank plans. If those rankings must be acyclic for the escape to work, then acyclicity has been moved up a level rather than shown to be unnecessary.
2. *Fragility of the entry point.* Her immunity depends on the shape of the offer sequence and on her bookkeeping. Degrade her self-prediction or her memory of fees slightly, or let the trader vary the fee at one node, and part of the loss returns. If immunity survives only under idealisation, is it really a fact about her rationality rather than about my stipulations?
3. *The frozen preference.* Under sophisticated choice she systematically refuses, at the very node where she faces it, a swap her own ranking favours. If rationality requires responding to the reasons present where one stands, the sophisticated cyclical agent has a defect too — just a different one.
