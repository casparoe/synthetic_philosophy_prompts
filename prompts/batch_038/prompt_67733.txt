Our little podcast has a philosopher coming on next month who argues that a heavy daily LLM user's assistant is literally part of her cognitive system — same parity move as Otto's notebook, just fancier hardware. I've lurked on enough rationalist forums to know not to burn the hour on the standard "a notebook isn't really cognition" stuff, I want questions that make him sweat on his own commitments. So: seven or eight of them, with at least one on what happens to the extended system when the vendor silently ships a new version overnight (he wants some stability condition and I suspect its ad hoc), one on the fact that part of this so-called mind is authored by a company with commercial incentives, one on whether confident confabulation breaks the automatic-endorsement condition his argument leans on, and one on whether the view earns its keep — does treating the assistant as literally cognitive predict anything that "she's using a really good tool" doesn't? For each question, give me the follow-up aimed at whatever reply he's most likely to reach for. And end somewhere around model sunsets: if it's genuinely part of my mind, what exactly is it to have it turned off?
