I cover science and ideas for a weekly magazine, and over the last couple years I've slid into a workflow that I cant tell is clever or a disaster. When two researchers flatly disagree about something — whether a famous result replicates, whether some effect is real — I'm not equipped to check the stats myself, so I put them on a call together and let them go at it, and I basically write up whoever lands the cleaner hits. On simple stuff this is great, because one of them can just show me the thing and I can see it. On the technical disputes, though, I've noticed the one who says "it's complicated, here are three caveats" always loses to the one who's confident and crisp, and my notebook ends up reading like a verdict even when the hedger was probably right. A colleague says that's what happens when you outsource your judgment and I should go back to reading the papers myself, slowly. Is there a version of my two-on-a-call thing that actually gets me nearer the truth, or am I just picking out the better talker and calling it reporting?
