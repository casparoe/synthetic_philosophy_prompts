ok so i teach a phil-of-AI seminar and I want to commission a satirical piece to hand out before we discuss Dennett's counterfeit-people argument — the one where AI-generated interlocutors are treated as analogous to counterfeit currency, and the proposed fixes are outlawing both the manufacture and the passing along of them, plus some mandatory watermark/disclosure regime so you always know you're talking to a machine.

Write it as a client-facing handbook page from an invented certification body — the Registry of Genuine Persons, or whatever you want to call it — that has actually been stood up to implement that proposal. Deadpan standards-document voice, numbered sections, roughly 800 words.

it needs to contain:
- a grading scale, at least four grades plus one reserved grade the Registry declines to define publicly
- the assessment procedures, including at least two tests a Dennettian would genuinely endorse in principle — ones that probe whether an applicant's fluent conversational competence is backed by the right kind of causal history, whether they can be genuinely surprised, whether they'd rather be right than agreeable — and, for each, the exact point at which it stopped discriminating, stated in the flat register of a standards body issuing a revision to its own spec
- a watermark verifcation section (invent your own scheme, don't borrow a real-world one) plus the fee schedule for re-verification and appeal
- a section on the most common reasons flesh-and-blood applicants fail
- one short quoted letter from a reclassified human, and the Registry's two-sentence reply
- a closing note the Registry clearly believes is reassuring

the humor has to come from the internal logic, not from props: the Registry never once admits anything has gone wrong, so the reader has to notice the drift on their own — thresholds quietly loosening, assessor training in suppressing the intentional stance visibly failing, the passing-along rules turning ordinary quotation and forwarding into a reportable offence, and finally the discovery that certified counterfeits clear authenticity checks more reliably than genuine people do, because they follow the procedure exactly. Bad-money-drives-out-good, applied to persons, should be the inference the reader draws rather than a claim the document makes.

keep it fair rather than cheap: someone who takes the counterfeit-people worry seriously should end up a bit chilled by the real problem, not just amused at a strawman bureaucracy. no real people named or quoted, no verbatim lines from any actual text, no citations or suggested reading. and please don't let the document wink.
