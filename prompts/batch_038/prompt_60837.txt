So I fell down a rabbit hole last night on Kepler and astrology and I'm properly stuck on some translated excerpts I found on a webpage (English translations of his own words, pulled from a few different works). I don't have the history background to know if I'm reading them right.

The long one is from something he wrote in January 1625 when he redid Wallenstein's horoscope:

"Herr von Taxus writes with the request to analyze the nativity [of Wallenstein] as extensively and with as much detail as possible. Furthermore, after I have rectified it, then I am supposed to discourse upon it at even greater length. In carrying out this request, I limited the scope of the mundane particularities. Of course astrologers are not lacking in techniques for satisfying peoples' curiosity. But I have put such "rules" to the philosophical test, and I have no use for them whatsoever. Am I nevertheless supposed to let myself be used as a comedian, a performer, or some other kind of marketplace entertainer? There are plenty of young astrologers who are so inclined, and have faith in such games. Whoever wants to be fooled with eyes wide open, let him make use of their efforts and entertainment. Philosophy, and therefore genuine astrology, is a testimony of God's works, and is therefore holy. It is by no means a frivolous thing. And I, for my part, do not wish to dishonor it."

Then these two shorter bits, the first from his 1610 Tertius Interveniens and the second from the 1619 harmony-of-the-world book:

"The geometry or harmony of aspects is not between the stars in the sky, but is located rather down here on earth in the point that collects all these rays."

"The heavens do not act without mediation, for the vital power of the soul maintains its sovereignty as it brings its works into consonance with the heavenly harmonies, under this so-called 'influence' of the heavens. Unfortunately the term 'influence' has blinded philosophers to the extent that they would prefer to engage in mad ravings with the uneducated masses, than to think scientifically as I do."

What I can't work out:

1. In the first passage he basically trashes everything working astrologers actually do, refuses to be a marketplace entertainer, and then in the last line calls "genuine astrology" holy and a testimony of God's works. What is left over for him? Is "genuine astrology" a thing with actual content, or is it just face-saving — a way to attack the practitioners without conceding the practice?

2. Smaller stuff: what does he mean by "after I have rectified it," and what are the "mundane particularities" he says he limited? I assume both are technical terms of the trade but I don't know which trade meaning applies.

3. The aspects sentence is the one that has me spinning. How can a geometric relation between two planets NOT be up in the sky between them, but instead be located at a point down here on earth? Is he saying an aspect isn't a fact about the heavens at all unless there's a reciever somewhere that the rays converge on?

4. And then "the heavens do not act without mediation, for the vital power of the soul..." — whose soul is this? The earth's, an animal's, mine? If the soul is what does the work of bringing things into consonance with the harmonies, is the sky causing anything, or is it more like a pattern the soul reads and then acts on? Is that even still astrology at that point, or has he quietly swapped in something else and kept the old name?

5. Related: if the aspect lives at the receiving point rather than between the stars, then two people born at the same instant in different places would sit under different charts. Does his picture handle that better or worse than the usual version? I vaguely remember same-birth-moment cases being a stock objection to astrology even back then.

You don't need to point me at books or sources, I just want to know where I'm reading these correctly and where I'm imposing something modern on them.
