So my grandparents have a small egg farm, like 200 hens, and grandpa's knees are shot so he's retiring at the end of the year. At the family dinner on Sunday we have to decide what happens to it and everybody has a diffrent plan:

Option A – sell the land to a guy who wants to put storage units on it, and all the hens go to a processor (grandpa's word, not mine).
Option B – keep it running with a hired manager, so basically nothing changes, hens get culled around 2 when they slow down laying and new pullets come in every year to replace them.
Option C – turn it into a little sanctuary, which is what my aunt wants, but the actual math is we could only afford to keep and care for about 40 of them, so the other 160 still go to the processor because nobody is adopting 160 old hens.

My cousin, who's a sophomore in college, says C is the worst option and he's got this argument I can't get around. He says if the farm shuts down, those future hens just never exist, and you can't claim a world with 200 chickens living okay lives is worse than a world with zero chickens, because there's literally nobody the zero world is better for. So the farm is a machine for making 200 lives that otherwise wouldn't happen, and that's a plus, and the sanctuary is just spending money on 40 birds so my aunt feels good about herself.

I think something's wrong with this but I can't say what, and separately I still have to pick one of A, B or C and argue for it at the table, since my parents have decided I'm "the animal person" now. So two things: which option should I actually push for, and is my cousin right that closing the farm can't be better for the hens who never get born, or is he doing something sneaky with the word better?

Please don't lay out both sides and tell me it's up to me, I can't do that in front of my whole family. Pick an answer and defend it. And give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up paragraph, so I can just read it off my phone.
