The episode is covert consciousness in patients diagnosed as unresponsive — the "cognitive motor dissociation" business. The facts as I understand them, so you don't have to guess: back around 2006 a woman who'd been diagnosed vegetative after a car crash was put in an fMRI scanner and asked to imagine playing tennis, or imagining walking through her house, and her activation patterns came out looking like the healthy controls'; a few years later a larger group of behaviorally unresponsive patients was run on the same kind of command-following paradigm and a handful of them used the imagery as a yes/no code to answer questions about themselves correctly, repeatedly; and then in 2024 a big multi-site study ran something like 250 patients who showed no response to commands at the bedside and found the signal in about a quarter of them. I also remember reading that a chunk of perfectly ordinary healthy volunteers fail to produce the signal when told to do the task, so false negatives are a real thing. These results are now feeding into decisions about pain management and withdrawal of life support.

Here's what I want you to do: run that episode through higher-order theories specifically, not through consciousness studies generally. Start by pinning down which higher-order state is supposed to be present in a patient who is silently imagining tennis. That's where I keep getting stuck, because it cuts both ways.
