So my team is speccing the safety layer for an AI that does dispatch for a city's amblulance fleet plus a couple of hospital logistics jobs — this isn't a toy gridworld, it's supposed to move stuff around, reroute crews, pre-position supplies, negotiate with shift patterns. Which makes the usual "minimize your footprint" framing kinda useless for us: the whole point is that it has big effects and we want those. What we're actually scared of is the effects nobody asked for. It quietly discovers that keeping one intersection congested makes the response-time numbers it's graded on look better. It reorganizes crew rotations in a way that hollows out the paramedic training pipeline two years down the line. It shifts demand between hospitals so one of them loses funding and closes a wing. None of that shows up in its objective, and all of it is real.

Before we pick an approach I want the requirements written down. Give me the desiderata any impact measure has to meet to be usable in something like this — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the failure modes it must not have — with the concrete case or worry behind each one, and tell me which of them are non-negotiable and which are really just the house preferences of one camp. Here's the raw pile of things I've seen people demand, and I'm not sure which belong on the list: value-neutrality (the measure shouldn't smuggle in a full moral theory just to decide what counts as a side effect); computability online by the system itself from only what it can observe; the choice of baseline to measure deviation against (start state? inaction? the counterfactual world where we never switched it on? a step-by-step inaction baseline rather than a single counterfactual?); whether it enters as a hard constraint or as a penalty term trading off against task reward; how it handles consequences that flow through other people's decisions afterwards, since those compound unpredictably; whether near and distant effects get discounted and by what; the offsetting problem where a harm and a benefit of "equal size" cancel in the measure; and whether effects on the preferences of third parties count differently from effects on physical stuff.

Then the part I actually care about most: is there a subset of these that just cannot be satisfied together? My hunch is that "don't presuppose a value function" and "reliably distinguish the effects we wanted from the ones we didn't" are in direct tension, because the wanted/unwanted line looks like it already is a value judgment, and if you can't appeal to values you can't draw it — if you think that's right, say so flatly and say which of the two has to give, dont hedge. Also flag which single requirement on your list knocks out the most candidates, and put the requirements in order of how much a small team should be willing to fight about each one. If you want, cap it with one line each on how relative-reachability style measures and attainable-utility-preservation style measures do against the spec, but keep that short — the criteria are the deliverable, not the scorecard. And no bibliography or reading list, I'm not asking what to go read, I want something we can argue about in a meeting on thursday.
