I'm co-running a two-day workshop on the Anthropocene, mixed room — maybe six geoscience people, a pile of historians, two or three philosophers — and I want to open with a sorting exercise instead of the usual round of self-introductions. Not "which start date do you pick?" because everyone's already read the list of candidates and will just perform for their team. I want something that sorts people by what they think would *make* a start date correct, so two people who both say the mid-century one land in different spots and two people who name completely different centuries land together.

So: eight or nine questions, each a concrete case or a claim they react to, two or three answer options each, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto named positions. Cases I definitely want in there: the difference between a marker that's globally detectable in one layer of sediment and a change (like the dip in ice-core CO2 around the colonial-era depopulation of the Americas) that was real, global-ish and completely unexperienced by anyone at the time; whether the date has to be readable in rock a million years from now or just has to be when the causal story actually starts; whether it matters *why* the change happened — which humans, doing what, to whom — or only that it happened; whether a shift that could in principle be reversed still gets to draw a boundary; and something that gets at whether this is a stratigraphy question at all or a periodization question wearing a lab coat.

Every question has to be answerable cold. No option may contain the name of a position or hypothesis, and nobody should be able to tell which leaf they're steering toward.

The scoring key needs, for each leaf, the position's name (or "unnamed" if you think nobody actually holds that combination) and a sentence or two on its most awkward commitment. But here's the part I care most about: do the logical space first, then populate it. Show me the answer combinations your questions genuinely allow, mark which cells are occupied and which are empty, and for each empty cell say whether its empty because the view is incoherent, because it's coherent but nobody wants it, or because it's just unexplored. I'd rather get three well-diagnosed empty cells than a tidy tree with no gaps.

Also flag where the instrument doesn't work. My guess is you won't cleanly separate the industrial-era people from the great-acceleration people, since they might share a criterion and disagree only about where to cut — if that's right, say so, and say what extra question you'd need. And with the formal epoch proposal having been knocked back by the stratigraphy bodies, I need one leaf that treats the exercise as a category error rather than a live date question.

Two more must-haves as leaves: the view that there's no single right date, only a date relative to your explanatory question; and the family of moves that renames the cause instead of pinning the date (capitalism, colonialism, plantations, that neighborhood). If you think either of those collapses into another leaf under a different name, collapse it and tell me why.

No reading list, no citations — just the instrument and the map of the space around it.
