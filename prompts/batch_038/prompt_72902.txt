I am assembling a set of judgements about historical inevitability, and I should like one of them from you in the form of a number with its derivation exposed.

Consider the following proposition, which I shall call **P**:

> **P:** A general war among the great powers of Europe would have broken out by the end of 1919 even if the events at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 had never happened — that is, even if the Archduke and his wife had returned safely to Vienna that afternoon and no comparable crisis had arisen out of that particular incident.

Please state your credence in P on a 0–1 scale, and then decompose it into the following terms. You may add, drop or re-cut terms if you think my list is misshapen, but you must say why.

**(a) The well-posedness term.** On a Lewis-style account, the truth of a counterfactual depends on a similarity ordering over possible worlds; the closest world in which the Archduke survives might be one produced by a tiny local divergence minutes before the motorcade turns, or one in which the preceding two decades ran differently. Give me the probability that P is well-posed enough for a careful historian and a careful philosopher, reasoning from the same evidence, to converge on a verdict — rather than P's truth value flipping with how much of 1914 Europe we hold fixed.

**(b) The structural-sufficiency term.** Conditional on (a), the probability that the alliance system, the mobilisation timetables, the arms-race dynamics and the widespread expectation of a coming war were jointly sufficient to produce a general war inside that five-and-a-half-year window.

**(c) The trigger-availability term.** Conditional on (b)'s failing — the structural conditions being necessary but not sufficient — the probability that some alternative spark (another Moroccan crisis, a Balkan incident, a naval scare, a colonial affair, a dynastic death) would have become available within the window.

**(d) The opaqueness term.** The probability that the system was sensitive enough to small perturbations that no body of evidence, however complete, could underwrite a claim about its trajectory over five years. Tell me explicitly where this term belongs: multiplying against (b) and (c), or against (a). I suspect I have mis-filed it.

**(e) The prior-tracking term.** The probability that my credence in P, and yours, are tracking a standing belief about whether history is driven by structures or by individuals, rather than by anything in the 1914 record.
