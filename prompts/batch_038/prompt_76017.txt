Translate the current debate about how to act under moral uncertainty into the working vocabulary of psychometrics, and give me a residue report on what the translation cannot carry.

My background, so you know what you may use without explanation: I have spent twenty years building and validating rating instruments — classical test theory and item response theory, Rasch and two-parameter models, measurement invariance testing across groups, differential item functioning, linking and equating of alternate forms via anchor items, the ordinal/interval scale distinction, construct validity argumentation, reliability generalization. I have no philosophical training. I turned to this topic because acting under moral uncertainty looked to me like a measurement problem in disguise, and I want to know whether that instinct survives contact with what the philosophers actually claim.

Render the following positions one at a time, in my terms:

1. The view that an agent uncertain between moral theories should maximize expected choice-worthiness, weighting each theory's verdict by her credence in it — which appears to presuppose that choice-worthiness sits on a single common scale across theories that otherwise disagree about everything.
2. The objection that no such scale exists: that a deontological constraint and a consequentialist aggregation are not two instruments measuring one quantity with different error profiles, but attempts to measure quantities that are not the same quantity, so that the credence-weighted average is arithmetically well formed and semantically empty.
3. The proposal to aggregate ordinally instead — rank the available acts under each theory, weight the rankings by credence, and select the act with the best weighted rank — as a way of reaching a verdict without cardinal comparability.
4. The favourite-theory proposal: act as the theory in which you place the most confidence directs, and set the remainder aside.
5. The parliamentary proposal: let representatives of the competing theories bargain or vote, with voting power proportional to the agent's credences.
6. The maximin-style proposal: choose so as to avoid the gravest possible wrong, even at small probability of that wrong being the correct description.
7. The separate question of whether an agent who does wrong while uncertain which theory is true is blameworthy, or excused.

For each, state what translates cleanly, what can be rendered only by distorting my vocabulary, and what has no rendering at all. Be explicit about which of my assumptions each distortion breaks: if you find yourself using reliability language where the moral case has no repeated-measurement structure, or invariance language where there are no groups to test across, say so rather than sliding past it.

Then the residue report. At minimum, I want your considered view — not a hedge — on these three points. First, whether moral theories really are like competing instruments aimed at one latent construct, or whether the possibility that there is no such construct, so that the disagreement is not about a quantity any instrument could pick up, has any expression in a framework built entirely around true scores and estimation error. Second, whether equating two moral scales faces the ordinary technical difficulty of linking, or a structurally different problem because there are no theory-neutral anchor items whose difficulty is invariant across the two forms. Third, whether anything in item 7 has a counterpart at all, given that my vocabulary concerns the accuracy of scores and never the standing of scorers.

Conclude by telling me whether the residue marks a limitation of psychometrics or evidence that the dispute was less well posed than it sounds. Where my instinct that this is a measurement problem is simply wrong, say so directly and identify which part of the dispute is doing work that measurement language cannot do.

Do not smuggle my framework's commitments in as though they were neutral translations. If a claim can be rendered only by assuming that the construct exists and that a true score is estimable, flag the assumption instead of burying it inside the rendering.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — your overall verdict on where the translation succeeds and where it fails — and then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restatement of the question.
