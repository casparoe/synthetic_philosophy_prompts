My argument felt airtight to me: a Pigouvian tax set at the social cost of carbon internalizes the externality, full stop. Whatever the economy looks like once that price is actually in place is the efficient one. So if GDP ends up 15% lower than it would otherwise have been, then that 15%-lower economy *is* the degrowth he says he's after — he just gets it through prices instead of through a ministry telling people what to produce. Which would mean degrowth and carbon pricing can't be in any real conflict. Either he accepts the tax and gets degrowth for free, or he rejects the tax and he's not a degrowth person at all, he's a planner who found a nicer word for it.

His whole response was "you're confusing the level with the growth rate," then something about the tax being fiscally growth-dependent, and then he refused to unpack either one because he said if I couldn't see it he wasn't drawing me diagrams at 1am. He also kept insisting the discount rate smuggles in a moral claim about people who don't exist yet, like that was a gotcha — but every loan and every capital budget discounts the future, so I don't get what's supposedly special about doing it here.
