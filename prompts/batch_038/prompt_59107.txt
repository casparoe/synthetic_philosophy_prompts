I am drafting the theoretical section of a paper on what it takes to say that a trained neural network represents something, and before I engage any of the existing accounts I want the specification. State the desiderata: what an account of representation-attribution must deliver to count as adequate — the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the background facts about how these systems actually behave that it cannot contradict. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it either non-negotiable or a preference belonging to a particular camp (feature-level locality, linearity, discreteness, human-legible content, and so forth).

Fix the purpose as follows, since I take adequacy to be relative to it: the account must be good enough to license inferences from a claim of the form the model represents that p to predictions and interventions concerning the model's behavior — the standard I would be willing to defend to a skeptical external auditor, not merely to a colleague.

To keep the exercise from drifting into generality, here is the sort of result I want the list to be able to adjudicate. In a 7B model fine-tuned on multilingual geographic question-answering, a probe trained on residual-stream activations at layer 14 recovers a binary feature we would gloss as is-in-France at roughly 96 percent balanced accuracy, and the recovered direction is stable across three unrelated prompt templates. Three further findings complicate the picture. First, zeroing the component of the residual stream along that direction leaves next-token behavior statistically indistinguishable from baseline on the evaluation set. Second, adding a synthetically constructed vector along the same direction to the residual stream reliably flips the model's answers on a held-out set of questions it had previously answered correctly. Third, the direction is not monosemantic: a sparse autoencoder trained at the same layer resolves it into several components that co-activate, and ablating any one of them individually reproduces the first finding while leaving the second partly intact.

I am not asking for a verdict on this case, though you should say what verdicts an adequate account is obliged to deliver here and why. I want the list of requirements that would license a verdict, and the reasons each belongs on it. In particular, address the following. Which of the following are genuine desiderata and which are load-bearing assumptions of one research program: that representations be identifiable with directions or subspaces; that they be causally necessary for the behaviors they explain; that they be causally sufficient for those behaviors; that an account be complete rather than merely local; that attributed content be something a human could state; that the account survive changes of prompt, template, and fine-tuning data. Which distinctions must the account keep apart — presence of information in activations versus use of that information, content versus vehicle, a feature versus a direction, decodability versus functional role, representing-that versus representing-as — and which collapses are fatal versus merely inelegant.

Flag any subset of the requirements you believe cannot be jointly satisfied. My own suspicion is that causal necessity, causal sufficiency, and identifiability with localized features cannot all hold once superposition and redundancy are on the table, and the case above is meant to press exactly that; but I want to know whether the suspicion is correct, or whether one of the three can be weakened without the account ceasing to serve the purpose stated above. Then identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidates, and say which contenders it excludes and how.

Finally, briefly and without turning this into a survey: how do interventionist accounts that tie representation to the results of activation-level manipulations, informational or teleological accounts that tie it to what a component carries or was selected for, and deflationary accounts on which representation talk is a useful fiction about weight-level functions fare against the list. A paragraph each is plenty; the thinking should be in the requirements.
