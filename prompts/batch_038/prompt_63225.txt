I have no academic training in philosophy — this is all reading on my own — so please correct me where I've described things loosely.

Two people I know, Dev and Mara, both work at a county benefits office, and they got into a long argument after Dev changed the form for a heating-assistance program. He replaced the empty checkbox with one that arrives already ticked, printed next to a sentence noting that the office's records suggest the household may be eligible unless the box is cleared. Applications rose by about a third. Mara called it manipulation dressed up as paperwork. Their exchange went roughly like this:

(1) Mara: an empty box leaves the person to say something. A pre-ticked box lets the agency say it for them, and a benefits office has no business holding a view about whether this household wants heating assistance.
(2) Dev: the old form had a default too. An empty box isn't a neutral starting point, it's a decision to let friction decide. We weren't declining to steer before; we were steering badly.
(3) Mara: those aren't the same kind of thing. Cutting friction — moving the program to page one, dropping the document requirement — helps people do what they already want to do. Pre-ticking puts words in their mouth, so that their signature now carries a claim they never formulated.
(4) Dev: then your line should also condemn moving the box to the top of the page, which is just as deliberate and just as effective. If the difference is that the tick makes the person's own hand assert something, that's a difference about the form's meaning rather than about anyone's freedom, and I don't see why meaning is the part that matters morally.
(5) Mara: meaning is the part that matters because it's the part they can't take back. Someone who wants to decline now has to contradict a statement the office has already made on their behalf, and doing that costs more than leaving a box empty.
(6) Dev: then disclose it. Print a line saying the box arrives ticked and should be cleared by anyone who doesn't want to apply. Nothing covert remains, so the objection goes away.
(7) Mara: it doesn't go away, and you know the disclosure would blunt the effect — which is how you can tell the effect was never coming from the person's judgment in the first place. A nudge that has to be muted to work isn't respecting anyone, it's just being candid about it.
(8) Dev: your standard would rule out nearly every form, sign, and default an office has ever printed. Either that's right and we should stop pretending otherwise, or it isn't a standard at all.
(9) Mara: I'll take the first horn. Most of what we print ought to embarrass us.
(10) Dev: then ask the households who enrolled whether they're glad they did. If nearly all say yes, tell me what was taken from them — and note that the ones who cleared the box were free to, and did.

They asked me to say who's right and I couldn't, partly because I suspect they're using words like manipulation and default in two different senses. Please take (1) through (10) one at a time and rate each — clearly right, partly right, or clearly wrong — with a sentence or two of justification, and tell me where they're simply talking past each other. Then say what each of them has left out of the case entirely, and who has the better of it overall. Since I can't check any of this myself, be explicit about which parts of your verdict reflect a settled view and which are live disputes among people who work on this, and don't hand me a contested position as though it were the consensus.
