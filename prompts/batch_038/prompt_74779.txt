I write a fortnightly-ish column about risk and uncertainty for a science mag, and I've spent the last few months tagging along with a philosopher who works on decision theory. She's the sort who thinks straight expected utility is too permissive about longshots, and she's written a book arguing that a rational person is allowed to be risk-averse in a particular technical sense. For a sidebar I ran her through a bunch of made-up funding gambles on the record. Everything was denominated in units of benefit for a hypothetical aid foundation, and she insisted we stipulate that utility is linear in those units — twice the units is exactly twice the good — so the only thing left to argue about was the probabilities. In each case the alternative to the gamble was a sure thing.

Her verdicts:

1. Sure 400 units vs. a 12% chance of 3,600 units, otherwise nothing. Took the sure 400, no hesitation.
2. Sure 300 vs. an 88% chance of 400, otherwise nothing. Took the 88% gamble.
3. Sure 250 vs. a 50% chance of 560, otherwise nothing. Sure 250.
4. Sure 300 vs. an 80% chance of 500, otherwise nothing. The gamble.
5. Sure 100 vs. a 5% chance of 2,200, otherwise nothing. Sure 100.

Every one of those is a gains-only, two-outcome bet, and I know thats a limitation — it's kind of the point of my question. When I tried to hand her a bet with three possible outcomes she waved it off and said she'd need to think about how to combine the branches. When I tried one with a downside she said losses were 'a different animal' and refused to rule on it at all.

Here's the bet I want to put to her next. She hasn't seen it. The alternative is to fund nothing, which is worth 0.

- 4% chance of +10,000 units
- 60% chance of +100 units
- 36% chance of −300 units (it burns a scarce window and leaves things worse than doing nothing)

Expected value is +352, for what that's worth.

So what do you reckon she says? I care way more about the machinery than the yes or no. Lay out the candidate rules that fit all five verdicts — off the top of my head there's a concave probability-weighting function of some kind, a flat premium for certainty with plain expected value everywhere else, a hard floor on how likely a bet has to be to pay off at all before she'll touch it, something about whether you weight each branch on its own versus weighting cumulatively, and whatever the untouched loss side lets in — and say what each predicts here. If it turns out a whole family of rules fits rather than one, tell me how tightly those five verdicts actually pin the parameters down, because I suspect the answer is "not very." Then give me your best single guess at her verdict and why you'd back it over the rivals, and name the one further bet I should put to her that would most cleanly seperate the two rules you're most torn between. Short enough to fit in a sidebar, ideally.

Last thing: for each rule, what would she be signing up for if it turned out to be the right description of her? I mean philosophically — what she'd owe me on the old Allais-style pairs, and whether her view can handle a bet with a real downside in it at all. Show your arithmetic where it matters so I can check it before print.
