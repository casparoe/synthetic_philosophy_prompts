Been doing long-range forecasting on and off for about eight years, mostly geopolitics and tech, and I've realized the part I actually can't do reliably is picking the reference class. I've read the reference-class-forecasting stuff, and the argument that the reference class problem infects basically every probability judgment anyone ever makes, but knowing its a problem doesn't tell you what to do. So here are my real verdicts, and I want to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

1. How long will the current great-power rivalry last — I refuse to use "all great-power rivalries since 1500" and insist on "rivalries where both sides have nuclear weapons." The n is tiny and everyone tells me that's disqualifying. I still think it's the right call.
2. Will this seed-stage startup be worth over a billion in five years — I'm perfectly happy using "all seed-stage startups in this sector and vintage," even though I agree their specific approach has no precedent.
3. A pundit says this coup will play out like one famous earlier coup — I think single-analogy reasoning is worthless, but averaging over "all coups everywhere" is worse, and I can't say why.
4. Predicting the decline of the current hegemon from the base rate of all past hegemons — astrology. But not because each empire is unique; because I don't think "hegemon" picks out a real kind at all.
5. Will a country democratize after a mass protest movement — I use "all mass protest movements," not "all countries" and not "all protest movements that reached this size." I have no way to choose between those three nested levels.
6. For any duration question I always condition on having already survived this long rather than using the unconditional distribution. I feel very strongly about this and have never had an argument for it.

My gut rule is: use the broadest class that shares the mechanism. But I can't say what counts as "the mechanism" without already knowing the answer, so I suspect that's fake.

Also, I preach that the class has to be fixed before you look at any outcomes and I won't let anyone move it after scoring badly — but I've personally moved one mid-forecast, twice, when I realized the original class had been defined after the fact.

So what's the principle? State it precisely, say which clause each of my cases motivates, tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, and run it on two cases I haven't listed. And if your honest answer is that there's no principle here and it's judgment all the way down, say so, and tell me what the judgment is tracking.
