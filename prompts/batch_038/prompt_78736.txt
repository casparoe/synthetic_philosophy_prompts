Draft the joint statement that would issue from an adversarial collaboration between two camps in population ethics: a totalist, who holds that a larger population of people whose lives are worth living is better, other things equal, than a smaller one, and who draws pronatalist policy conclusions from this; and a person-affecting restrictivist, who holds that one outcome can be better than another only if it is better for someone, and who resists those conclusions. Both camps have accepted a stipulated case, called Colony: a sealed settlement with fixed arable land, in which the live choice is between 10 million inhabitants living well and 30 million whose lives all parties agree are worth living, though markedly less well.

Produce (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can sign — about Colony, about what each rival theory implies there, and about which empirical facts would bear on the choice; (2) a table of the residual disagreements, each row phrased so that the proponent of that view would accept the wording of her own position, with a column identifying the crux the row turns on; and (3) for each crux, the argument, test, or further case both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I expect at least two cruxes to be whether existence can be good for someone who would not otherwise have existed, and whether the total view can be patched near the margin of worth-living without becoming ad hoc — but say so if you think these are the wrong ones, and substitute better candidates. Where you judge some part of the dispute to be verbal rather than substantive, identify that part and explain why. Write the answer in American English.
