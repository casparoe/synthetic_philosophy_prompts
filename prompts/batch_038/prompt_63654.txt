Write an essay settling the following dispute about heritability.

The familiar claim is that heritability says nothing about modifiability: an intervention that improves everyone's environment equally can shift a trait's mean arbitrarily far while leaving its variance, and hence its heritability, untouched. From this it is often concluded that heritability estimates carry no policy relevance at all.

Against that, note that the same statistic does genuine predictive work in breeding. The breeder's equation says that the response to selection equals narrow-sense heritability multiplied by the selection differential — that is, h² tells you how far a population's mean will move when you select parents on the trait. Some argue that this licenses a parallel inference in social settings: if a program selects its participants on a highly heritable measure, then h² constrains what the program can be expected to achieve, and it is no accident that some intervention effects fade out.

Weigh these two lines of thought and defend a definite verdict on whether importing the breeder's equation into social policy is legitimate, illegitimate, or legitimate only under stated conditions. A shrug is not acceptable; a qualified position is. Along the way, address at least: (i) whether the "selection" performed by an admissions or targeting scheme is relevantly like the selection performed by a breeder, given that breeding works through differential reproduction rather than differential treatment; (ii) what the equation assumes about genotype–environment correlation, about random mating, and about the new environment lying inside the range of environments the population has already sampled; and (iii) whether it matters that the policy question is typically about a change in the mean, when h² is defined only over variances. If you discuss fade-out, say explicitly whether you take it to be evidence about heritability or about the difference between stable and transient environmental variance.

After the essay, output a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- `verdict`: one of `"legitimate"`, `"illegitimate"`, `"legitimate_under_conditions"`
- `conditions`: array of strings stating the conditions, empty if the verdict is not `"legitimate_under_conditions"`
- `decisive_reason`: one sentence naming the single consideration that most drove your verdict
- `strongest_objection`: the best objection to your verdict, in one or two sentences
- `reply_to_objection`: your response, in one or two sentences
- `confidence`: a number from 0 to 1 indicating how confident you are in the verdict
