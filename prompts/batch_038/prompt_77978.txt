The episode is the prosecution of Dražen Erdemović at the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia; the lens is the principle of alternate possibilities and the compatibilist apparatus built to replace it. What I want is a case analysis that runs in both directions: what the theory says about the man, and what the man does to the theory.

Take the following as given; I am not asking you to reconstruct or supplement the history. In July 1995 Erdemović, a soldier in a Bosnian Serb sabotage unit, was among those ordered to execute roughly 1,200 Bosniak men and boys at a farm near the village of Pilica after the fall of Srebrenica. He estimated that he personally shot about seventy of them. He said that he resisted the order and was told that if he would not shoot he should hand over his rifle and join the men who were about to be killed; another shooter was available. He pleaded guilty — the first guilty plea the tribunal accepted — and was sentenced to ten years in 1996, later reduced to five, with early release in 1999. Before any of this he had gone to a journalist and confessed on camera, unprompted and unarrested; afterward he was shot and wounded by a fellow soldier; he later testified for the prosecution in another case and expressed remorse for the victims. On the law, the appellate bench divided: a majority held that duress cannot be a complete defence to the killing of innocent persons but must count in mitigation, while dissenting judges argued that it can excuse entirely where the actor's own life is threatened and refusal would have saved no one. Because the matter came in on a plea, no tribunal ever made a finding of fact about whether the threat would have been carried out; the judges reasoned on the assumption that it was real.

For the lens, use the principle of alternate possibilities — that an agent is morally responsible for an act only if he could have done otherwise — together with the Frankfurt-style attack on it and what was put in its place: the distinction between regulative and guidance control, the claim that responsibility tracks the actual sequence rather than the availability of alternatives, and the requirement that the mechanism issuing in the action be moderately responsive to reasons. You may also invoke the idea that an alternative must be robust, in the sense of being relevant to the agent's credit or blame, in order to count, and the broader division between sourcehood and leeway accounts of freedom.

Three further pieces of apparatus I am stipulating rather than borrowing. Define them as you use them, and if you think one of them is confused or does no work, argue that instead of deploying it decoratively.

(a) Call an impossibility *deliberatively live* when the closure of the agent's alternatives is communicated to him by another agent and so enters his reasoning as a consideration he must weigh, rather than remaining a background structural fact he never encounters. Constructed Frankfurt cases are engineered so that the impossibility is never live: the intervener is undetectable and reliable, and the agent deliberates exactly as though he were free. Say whether that difference is philosophically load-bearing here or merely dramatic.

(b) Call a case *redundant* when the alternative sequence is already occupied — when doing otherwise would change nothing for anyone but the agent. Erdemović's refusal would have added one corpse to twelve hundred and saved none. That is precisely the structure Frankfurt secures by stipulation. Determine whether the real instance is stronger or weaker evidence for the compatibilist than the constructed one, and say why in terms of what each side is entitled to assume.

(c) Distinguish responsibility as *liability* — whether the act is properly attributed to the agent at all, such that a verdict of guilt is apt — from responsibility as *imposition* — how much may be exacted from him once attribution is settled. The outcome here is full conviction plus heavy discount. Ask whether a framework built to answer a yes-or-no question about attribution has any resources for a verdict of that shape, or whether the shape of the verdict is itself evidence that the operative question was never about alternate possibilities.

Then produce the analysis. Map the case explicitly: who the agent is, what his alternatives were, whether any of them was robust, what the actual sequence consisted in, which mechanism issued in the shooting, and what evidence bears on whether that mechanism was reasons-responsive — including whether his later confession and remorse may be used as evidence about the earlier act, and if so whether that makes remorse retroactively constitutive of guilt. Say which features of the episode the compatibilist account explains well, which it misdescribes, and on which it is simply silent; do not force it to speak where it has nothing to say. Address in particular the circumstance that the impossibility of doing otherwise was never adjudicated and could not have been: what does it do to the Frankfurt strategy that, in the real case, the closure of alternatives is an empirical claim about another person's credibility, contested rather than stipulated?

Close with a verdict rather than a summary. Does this episode support compatibilism about moral responsibility, support incompatibilism, or show that the dispute over alternate possibilities is largely orthogonal to what doctrines of duress are actually deciding? If the last, say what the doctrine is deciding instead, and identify which account of freedom, if any, is implicated by it.

No bibliography, no reading list, no citations — assume I have the literature and want your reasoning.
