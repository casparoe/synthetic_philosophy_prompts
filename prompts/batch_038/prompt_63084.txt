Grant that your future credence in A is just your present credence conditionalized on the evidence you'll have by then, and grant that your probability model covers facts about your own future credences. Then Reflection follows as arithmetic. Calling it a principle of rationality makes it sound optional.

(2) Ines: If it's arithmetic it can't criticize anybody, and yet people invoke it to do exactly that. Here's a case: I know that at nine tomorrow morning I'll be given a sedative before an oral examination, and I know from prior experience that it reliably makes me overconfident about whichever answer I reach first. My credence in A now is 0.6. I know my credence at ten will be 0.95. Reflection tells me my present credence in A, conditional on my future credence being 0.95, must be 0.95. So Reflection tells me 0.6 = 0.95. One of those premises has to go, and it obviously isn't my arithmetic: it's not irrational to know that you're about to be impaired. So Reflection is a norm with exceptions, not a theorem.

(3) Dev: Agreed, and that's my point. In your case the variable "my future credence" isn't a report of an evidential state; it's a symptom of a chemical process you already know severs the link between evidence and belief. Reflection applies when your future credence is a faithful record of evidence you don't yet have. (4) Ines: Then the proviso collapses, for two reasons. First, unpack it and you get "conditionalize on your evidence" plus "your model of your own future credences is accurate." Second, and worse: there are foreseeable changes that are neither conditionalization nor corruption. Expecting that isn't irrational. Nor is holding the position now. But Reflection requires me to pre-emptively adopt the beliefs of a self I have correctly identified as degraded.

(5) Dev: You've misdescribed the case, and the misdescription is what makes it look like a counterexample. At two in the morning you foresee a direction, not a value: you expect your credence to drop, but you have no view about what it will drop to. Reflection constrains your present credence conditional on each specific value the future credence might take. If you don't know which value, no constraint bites and the principle is silent. If you do know — you're confident it will be 0.2 — then you're claiming knowledge about your future evidential state after all, and the honest description is that you'll have lost track of a distinction, which is a failure of memory and attention, which is corruption. Either the principle is silent or the proviso applies. There's no third case.

(6) Ines: There is a third case and it's the one that matters: conversion. Within a year I will hold Q with credence 0.9, and I know this because I've decided to move into a community where Q is taken for granted, and I've watched enough people make that move to know how it goes. My credence in Q now is 0.1. Nobody drugs me. I acquire no evidence. I will not be reasoning badly by my future lights — only badly by my present ones. Reflection requires my present credence in Q, conditional on my future credence being 0.9, to be 0.9: it requires me to surrender now, for nothing, a belief I'm going to lose for reasons I currently regard as bad. Call that corruption and your proviso swallows every foreseeable loss of belief, so Reflection can never be violated — and a principle that can't be violated can't be satisfied either, it just stops being about anything. Don't call it corruption and Reflection is false as a requirement of rationality. What survives is weaker and better: defer to your future self only where you take your future self to be better informed than you are. That's a principle about experts, not about time.

What's left between us is whether a principle that needs that proviso deserves to be called a principle of rationality at all. The bookie who sells you the first bet now at your current prices and the second bet later at your future prices can only guarantee you a loss if your future prices are governed by rules you now endorse. In your conversion case they aren't. So the argument shows you're unstable, and instability is not incoherence.
