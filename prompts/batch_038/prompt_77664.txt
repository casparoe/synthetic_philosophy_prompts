In six weeks I give a colloquium talk in a personality psychology department — mostly people who came up through the replication crisis, zero moral psychologists, one behavioral geneticist, and a couple of measurement folks who care about construct validity way more than about Aristotle. My thesis is deliberately provocative: the collapse of situationism has been worse for virtue ethics than situationism ever was.

Sketch of the argument, so you know what the objections should be aimed at:
- the philosophers' comeback to the situationist attack has been (a) accept local/dispositional traits and (b) point out that half the famous studies don't replicate;
- but what replaced situationism isn't Aristotelian character — it's rank-ordered, moderately heritable traits that predict aggregated behavior at around .30 and are basically descriptive;
- under situationism, character could still be an achievement of cultivation and situational design; under trait psychology it looks like a temperament endowment, which turns virtue into luck and makes habituation look empirically implausible;
- so the friendlier picture for the virtue ethicist was the one they spent twenty years fighting.
