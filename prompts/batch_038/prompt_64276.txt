Our incident-severity model has a structural property I've never seen named. A single data-corruption outage outranks any number of minor tickets — a thousand "button is misaligned" reports do not sum to one "records are being destroyed" — and nobody treats this as a weighting we could recalibrate; it functions as a different kind of claim entirely. I keep meeting the same shape outside engineering, in arguments about suffering: a position on which an experience past some intensity of badness cannot be outweighed by any quantity of mild good experience, however many people have it, while experiences below that intensity trade off smoothly against one another. What I want is the general structure — a ranking in which enough small amounts can never reach one large amount, applied to bad states specifically, and only in one direction. I would have guessed this was lexicographic ordering, but that fails to fit: everything below the cutoff remains fully comparable, and the cutoff sits inside a single scale rather than between two criteria. Nor is it simply the idea that two things cannot be measured against each other, since here they mostly can be. Is there an established concept or literature for this? If so, what are the closest candidates and precisely where does each fall short of what I described? If not, coin a term, define it, and mark its boundaries — in particular, what such a view should say when two catastrophes above the cutoff compete, and whether it survives the cutoff being vague.
