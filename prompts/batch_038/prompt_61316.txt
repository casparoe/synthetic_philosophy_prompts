Our department is running an adversarial collaboration between two camps that we believe have been arguing past each other for decades, and I need the document such a collaboration should produce at the end.

The two camps are these.

Camp P, the pessimist about moral responsibility, accepts Galen Strawson's Basic Argument in the following form: we do what we do, in the circumstances in which we find ourselves, because of the way we then are; so if we are to be truly responsible for what we do, we must be truly responsible for the way we are, at least in certain mental respects; but nothing can be truly responsible for its own nature in any respect; therefore true moral responsibility — the kind that would make desert-based punishment and reward ultimately fitting — is impossible. Camp P insists that this is not an argument from determinism and does not turn on whether determinism is true.

Camp C, the reactive-attitudes compatibilist, accepts the line of P.F. Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment": to be morally responsible is to be an apt object of the interpersonal reactive attitudes; the demand for self-origination does not arise within the practice of holding one another responsible but is imported into it from outside; and the impossibility of being causa sui therefore shows nothing about whether anyone is responsible. Camp C's charge is that the pessimist has not refuted our concept of responsibility but has described a concept nobody ever had.

The deliverable is a joint statement both camps could put their names to. It should contain: (1) the claims both accept; (2) the residual disagreements, each stated twice — once in wording Camp P's advocate would sign without qualification, once in Camp C's; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side; (4) for each crux, a case or argument both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance and each side's own falsifier, i.e. the result that would count against its own position; and (5) an explicit account of which portion of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal, and why.

Two constraints on that. First, every agreed claim must be one that some third philosopher could deny; if a claim would be accepted alike by a libertarian, a hard incompatibilist, and a skeptic about free will, it is not doing any work and should not appear on the list. Second, do not adjudicate. The statement must not conclude that either camp is right about responsibility itself.

I also want you to test a hypothesis of mine, which may well be wrong. I suspect the disagreement decomposes once three notions are separated: (a) answerability — being the apt subject of appraisal and of the reactive attitudes because an action expresses one's quality of will; (b) accountability — being liable to demands and sanctions, and standing to be called to account; and (c) ultimate responsibility — responsibility requiring that one be the ultimate source of one's own nature. My guess is that Camp P and Camp C both accept (a) and (b) as descriptions of actual practice and disagree only about (c) and about whether (c) is already presupposed by (a) and (b). Tell me whether that decomposition dissolves the dispute, merely relocates it, or fails outright; if it fails, identify the premise of the Basic Argument at which the split makes no difference.

Finally, one of the cruxes must be a concrete empirical case, because I want predictions on the record. The case: a state sentencing commission pilots a randomized program in which, for half of 200 otherwise comparable cases, defense counsel may file a formative-history memorandum — a structured account of the causal origins of the defendant's character, upbringing, exposure to violence, and untreated illness — which the judge reads before sentencing, while the other half receive only ordinary presentence reports. Four measures are recorded: sentence length; the judges' own reported sense that the sentence was fair and legitimate; whether the practice spreads within three years to other institutions such as school discipline, employment decisions, and licensing boards; and the reaction of victims' advocates. For that crux, give each camp's prediction on all four measures, state what result each camp would treat as evidence against its own position, and flag any measure on which the two predictions coincide. Where they do coincide, say what that coincidence shows about whether the disagreement is practical or purely descriptive.

Format. Write the joint statement itself in prose, with numbered sections for agreed claims, disagreements, cruxes, and the verdict on what is verbal. Do not cast it as a dialogue, and do not quote either Strawson verbatim — paraphrase, and paraphrase in wording each advocate would accept. After the statement, give me a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- agreed_claims: array of objects, each with id, claim, and why_not_vacuous (which third party could deny it)
- disagreements: array of objects, each with id, question, camp_p_statement, camp_c_statement, classification (one of substantive, verbal, verbal_with_substantive_residue), what_turns_on_it, and residual_objection (what each side might still object to in the other's wording)
- decomposition_test: object with three_notions_stated, verdict (one of dissolves, partially_dissolves, relocates, fails), reasoning, and premise_where_split_fails (null if none)
- cruxes: array of objects, each with id, crux, which_disagreements_it_settles (array of ids), test_or_case, camp_p_prediction, camp_c_prediction, predictions_diverge (boolean), what_convergence_shows (null if the predictions diverge), camp_p_falsifier, camp_c_falsifier
- verbal_disputes: array of objects, each with claim_at_issue, why_verbal, and substantive_residue_left_over (null if none)
- unresolved: array of objects, each with item and why_it_cannot_be_settled_in_a_joint_statement
