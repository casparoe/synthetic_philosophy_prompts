I am applying for a twelve-month postdoctoral fellowship at an institute working on the safety of autonomous systems, and I need a two-page research statement drafted from a half-formed idea. Please sharpen the idea rather than replace it.

The idea: work on penalizing an agent's unintended disruptions to its environment has settled into a familiar architecture, in which a side-effect penalty is assembled from two choices — a baseline state (how the world would have looked had the agent done nothing, or had it followed some default policy) and a deviation measure (how far the actual state is from that baseline, often cashed out in terms of which states remain reachable, or which auxiliary goals the agent can still achieve). My suspicion is that the standing question — whether low impact can be defined without a full specification of what the agent ought to value — has been argued almost entirely over baselines, while the harder problem sits in the deviation measure. Any measure that ranks states by how much of the future they foreclose, or by how easily a change can be undone, is covertly ranking outcomes. The claim I want tested is a dilemma: either the deviation measure smuggles in a value function in disguise, or it is too coarse to separate benign irreversible change from catastrophic irreversible change.

Turn this into a proposal containing:

1. A statement of the research question precise enough to admit of a negative answer — ideally framed as a claim about what no deviation measure satisfying an explicitly listed set of conditions can do, with the conditions written out rather than gestured at.

2. An account of why the existing positions leave the question open, referring to the arguments and the design choices in play rather than to particular papers; I will supply citations myself.

3. The method, month by month: which toy environments I would construct, what I would attempt to prove as opposed to merely measure, and what would count as a result in either direction.

4. A treatment of two objections a reviewer will certainly raise. First, that reachability-flavored measures make the size of the penalty a function of the agent's own capacities, so a weaker agent is charged less for the same physical change. Second, that measures permitting a destroyed object to be offset by constructing an equivalent one appear to presuppose knowing what mattered about the original. For each, say whether it is a bug or a feature and how I would determine which.

5. An honest list of the ways the project could fail, ranked by how much each should worry me, together with a realistic scope statement for twelve months.

6. A description of the deliverable that would actually be useful to someone who has to choose a side-effect penalty for a deployed system.

Write for a technically literate reader who has not worked in this area, glossing every term of art in a clause. Do not include a bibliography or reading list.
