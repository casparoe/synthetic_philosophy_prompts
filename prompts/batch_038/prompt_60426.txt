I'm trying to work out whether I actually believe in imprecise credences — ranges instead of single numbers — or whether I just like the sound of them. Here's my gut on a bunch of cases.

1. A jar, 100 marbles, 40 definitely blue, the other 60 either green or orange in a split settled by some process I know literally nothing about (not even that it was random). Bet on blue vs. bet on green: I take blue. Bet on blue-or-green vs. bet on orange: I take blue-or-green. That's the usual sure-thing violation and I dont think it makes me irrational. I just don't have a number, and inventing one wouldn't change my bets.

2. A doctor who says "somewhere between 20 and 70 percent, the trials are too heterogeneous to narrow it" — honest. A second doctor, same literature, says "55 percent" flat — I think he's making it up. Neither would treat you any differently. So the gap between them is real but makes no difference to anything anyone does, and I'm fine with that.

3. Three forecasters on a panel give [0.3, 0.7], [0.4, 0.8], [0.2, 0.5]. Average the midpoints and the panel's number is 0.5, sharp. I want to say a group can be sharp when nobody in it is, and that this isn't a paradox.

4. A stranger hands me a coin and tells me nothing about it except that it has two sides. My credence for heads should be the whole interval; not even 1/2 is licensed. But after 20 heads in a row I insist my credence is high — at least 0.9 through and through. I don't accept that starting mushy condemns you to never learning. Relatedly, after ten thousand flips coming up 5,012 heads I say 0.5 flat and I'm not going to write [0.48, 0.52] to look humble.

5. Two flips. I'm told the second is either a fair coin flipped independently or a coin rigged to match the first, and which one it is was settled by something nobody told me about. My credence that flip 1 is heads is exactly 1/2, and my credence that flip 2 is heads is exactly 1/2 — sharp, both of them, no mushiness at all. But whichever way flip 1 comes up, my opinion about flip 2 is going to blow out into a range. I want to call that unacceptable. Something in the setup has to give and I'd rather it wasn't the sharpness.

6. A jury is told the defendant is one of a hundred people who had equal opportunity and nothing in the evidence distinguishes them. My credence he did it: 1/100, sharp, and indifference is exactly right here. Compare the jar in case 1, where I said indifference is not right. Both are supposed to be cases of knowing nothing relevant. I cannot tell you what the difference is.

7. I tell a friend my credence that the project ships is between 0.3 and 0.7. She relays it to someone else as 0.5. That's a distortion of my view, not a summary of it.

Is there one principle — about when a credal state has to be a single number, when it may be a range, and when it must be — that delivers all of these? State it precisely, and tell me which case is doing the work for each clause. Then apply it to a case I haven't given you: pick one yourself, make it concrete, and report the verdict. And be straight about which of my verdicts it can't reach. If the real choice is between a simple principle that overturns one of them and a baroque one that saves all seven, lay out both and say which you'd take and why.

The one I keep going back and forth on: a founder tells investors her chance of success is "between 10 and 40 percent" and won't narrow it, and the investors trust her more than the guy who says 25 percent flat. I think the investors are right about her. I also think she should be made to pick a number, because you can't run a company on a range — at some point you sign the lease or you don't. Both feel true and I know they can't both be.

Two last things. Tell me how much of your answer is finding a principle and how much is reverse-engineering one to fit my quirks — if the honest read is that these verdicts are just a grab bag of moods with nothing systematic behind them, say so. And if you think the whole framing is confused, e.g. that talk of sets of probabilities does no work beyond describing dispositions to bet, so there's nothing here for a principle to be about, push back on that rather than humoring me.
