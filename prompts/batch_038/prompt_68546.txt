I sit on a hospital ethics committee, and we have each been asked to submit a position statement before the protocol for allocating scarce resources is revised. Here is mine, in six claims. I would like to know whether they hang together.

(1) I accept Taurek's conclusion that the numbers should not count: each person's death is a loss to her and to her alone, there is no such thing as the sum of losses across different people, and it is therefore a mistake to say that five deaths are worse than one.
(2) What convinces me of (1) is respect for the separateness of persons — the same consideration that rules out sacrificing one patient for the greater good of others.
(3) When resources permit me to save only some, and there are no special obligations and no differences in need among the candidates, I would allocate by what I call a serial lottery: a run of pairwise draws, each between one candidate and the survivor of the previous draw, so that no draw ever sets a group against a group. I take it this gives every individual candidate exactly the same chance of being saved, whatever the sizes of the groups they happen to come from.
(4) Saving my own mother rather than a stranger, when I can save only one, is morally correct and not merely excusable.
(5) Nothing counts against an allocation except a complaint from an identifiable patient whom it leaves worse off; there is no standpoint from which outcomes are better or worse overall.
(6) I want to be able to tell the committee that our protocol treats every patient's life as mattering equally, and equally as much as any other's.

Something in this package feels unstable, but I cannot locate it, and I am not looking to be converted to consequentialism or told simply to drop (1). Audit the set and give me a table, ordered by severity from highest to lowest, with the merely apparent tensions grouped at the bottom. Use exactly these columns:

- **id** — a short label for the finding
- **claims** — which of the numbered claims are involved
- **verdict** — genuine tension or apparent tension
- **severity** — high, medium, or low for genuine tensions; leave blank for apparent ones
- **diagnosis** — what specifically in one claim licenses or forbids what in the other, with the reasoning shown rather than asserted
- **cheapest repair** — the smallest revision that removes the tension, saying which claim is dropped, weakened, or reinterpreted, and how
- **what the repair costs** — what I give up by making it, including any other claim on my list that the revision puts at risk

After the table, add three or four sentences: which single revision would resolve the most at once, and whether the package as it stands can still deliver the ranking of candidate protocols that the committee has asked me for.
