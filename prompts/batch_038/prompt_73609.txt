i'm doing a political philosophy module this term and a friend who read TJ last year sent me this rundown of Rawls's argument:

'Rawls wants principles that don't depend on any particular moral or religious view, so he asks what you'd choose if you didn't know who you are -- your class, your talents, your race, your religion, or even what you want out of life. Since nobody in that position is going to gamble, they take the option that makes the worst-off outcome as good as it can be, which is the difference principle: inequality is fine only if it leaves the least advantaged better off. Rawls thinks that's legitimate because nobody deserves their natural gifts, so the talented have no claim on what those gifts let them earn and society can take it off them. And because everyone behind the veil would agree to it, we're bound -- that's what makes it a real contract theory rather than just a bundle of intuitions.'

He reckons the upshot is that Rawls is really a luck egalitarian, and that Nozick's complaint about patterned principles of distribution therefore lands squarely.
