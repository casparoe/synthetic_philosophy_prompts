For three years I ran operations at a mid-sized lender, and twice we retired a control on the grounds that it was vestigial — a manual second review of small-balance disputes, and a bar on loan officers approving files for their own relatives — and twice we paid a good deal to discover what the control had been doing. I have since gone back to finish a philosophy degree, and next month I am presenting a paper at an interdisciplinary workshop on institutions and evidence. The room is mostly formal epistemologists and public-policy economists, plus two political philosophers who work on legitimacy; nobody in it is a conservative in any ordinary sense.

My thesis is that Chesterton's fence, read properly, is not a precautionary principle but an evidential one: that a practice's still standing is evidence that reasons for it once existed, and that the weight of that evidence varies with how recently and how deliberately the practice has been reaffirmed by people who were in a position to drop it. Fences inherited but not maintained carry little weight; fences that have survived recurrent live opportunities for repeal carry a great deal. I want to anchor this in Burke's notion of prescription rather than in risk aversion, and to resist the collapse of the view into a generic precautionary principle about irreversibility.

Prepare me for the question period. I want the six or eight objections this particular room will raise, roughly in the order they will come and phrased the way a Bayesian or an economist would phrase them — I expect some version of the charge that I have merely named status quo bias and dressed it as an argument, and some version of the charge that my decay condition is untestable, since any repeal that went well will be said to have proved the fence unmaintained. For each, give me a reply deliverable in about ninety seconds and a note on where that reply is softest. Then tell me which objection has real force and ought to change how I state the thesis, which one I should concede early to buy credibility, what I should refuse to give up under any pressure, and the question I am hoping nobody asks.
