right so I'm in year 13 and I've got a bit obsessed with the arguments about how much we owe to people who aren't born yet, and there's a pattern I keep running into that nobody ever seems to name — which makes me think either it has an obvious name that no one's told me, or I'm making it up in my head.

Here's what happens. Someone says, quite forcefully, that you can't mark a person down just for being later: a kid in 2200 counts exactly as much as a kid now, the date they happen to exist at isn't a reason to weigh them less. Fine, I agree. Then the same person, sometimes in the same conversation, says something like well obviously we don't owe anything to people who've already lived, the past is the past. And everyone nods. If I try saying but that's the same principle pointed backwards, they look at me like I've missed the point, and I can't work out whether I have.

Becuase when I press people on it, the reason they give isn't "I count dead people less". It's "you can't do anything about the past". Which sounds reasonable. But the reason doesn't stay where it's put. The same person, arguing about whether we should spend huge amounts now to stop some catastrophe happening in 2400, will say it doesn't matter that we can't possibly check whether it'll work or that anyone will be around to notice — and if I say so the difference is just that you can act forwards and not backwards, they say no, that's not the point either, the point is they *would* have been owed it. So the can't-reach-it reason gets used to shut down claims about the past, then gets waved away when someone turns it on the future, and neither time does anyone treat that as a problem.

And then there's the cases that don't fit either version. People who say the past can't generate claims get absolutely furious about artefacts taken from other countries a hundred and fifty years ago, and about clearing up contamination somebody dumped in the sixties, and they'll say we owe it to people who are dead. Which is exactly the thing they said was impossible.

So — does this have a name? I don't want "that's just inconsistency", I can see that it might be. What I'm after is the carving. Is it one mistake, or two that look identical from the outside — like, one where someone really is counting past people for less without admitting it, and one where they're only pointing out you can't act backwards? How do you tell from inside an argument which one you're actually looking at? What's the closest thing that does already have a name, and why doesn't it cover this? And where's the edge — is "you can't affect the past" a legitimate reason or a discount rate in disguise, and does the answer change for the artefacts and the contamination cases? If there's no term for it, make one up properly: a definition, what falls outside it, and a couple of examples that look like they should be inside but aren't.
