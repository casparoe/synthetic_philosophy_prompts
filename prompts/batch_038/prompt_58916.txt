Stuff I already know I want on the list, plus whatever I'm missing: it has to pin down effective procedure without just redefining that phrase as Turing-computable, or the whole thing is circular; it has to face the old dilemma where either the thesis is an analytic truth about what we mean by following a rule, in which case no experiment could ever threaten it, or it's an empirical claim about matter and energy, in which case we should be able to say which observation would refute it — and my suspicion is you can't dodge both horns, so if that's right, say so, since a jointly unsatisfiable subset is the actually interesting result; it should deal with machines that take real numbers as exact inputs and do exact real arithmetic in a single step, the BSS-style models, and tell me whether those are counterexamples or just a different subject; it should have an answer for the general-relativistic setups of the Malament–Hogarth kind, where an observer can in principle recieve the output of a machine that has been computing for infinite proper time — is that a physical refutation, or does it show the notion of physically realizable doing all the work; and it should explain the wierd epistemic status, i.e. nobody has a proof but essentially everyone in the field is confident anyway, so what kind of warrant is that and does an adequate formulation have to make it intelligible.
