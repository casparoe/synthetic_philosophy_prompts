My friend is a hardcore takeoff-skeptic and he gave me an argument that I think is better then the ones you usually hear, but I can't accept the last line and I also can't find the flaw. It goes:

(1) If some activity makes itself better at improving itself, and nothing outside that activity caps how much better it can get, then the rate at which it improves grows without bound.
(2) Scientific research is that kind of activity: better methods, tools and training make research better at producing further improvements in research.
(3) So the rate of scientific improvement should have been accelerating for a long time, and should have visibly kinked upward when computers arrived.
(4) It hasn't. The growth rate of research output shows no such acceleration, output per researcher has if anything been falling, and ideas seem to be getting harder to find rather than easier.
(5) So (1) or (2) is false.
(6) If (2) is false — if research isn't genuinely self-amplifying — then AI systems doing AI research aren't self-amplifying either, because they'd be performing the same activity under the same constraints.
(7) So automating AI research won't produce a takeoff.

Obviously I want to keep (4), and if it comes down to it I'd rather keep (2) than (1), since giving up (2) feels like handing over the whole recursive self-improvement story I care about. Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no introduction and no wrap-up. Name the step or steps that fail, say why each of them looked fine on a first reading, and tell me what it costs to give up whichever premise you think I should give up — and if your view is that it's valid and the only real question is which premise is cheapest to drop, make that one of the five.
