So — I'm 41, and a couple weeks ago my cousin's sixteen-year-old informed me, very kindly, that I'm not a Millennial, I'm a "Zillennial," and that the cutoff moved. That sent me down a hole I have not climbed out of, and I've now committed to a 40-minute talk at my town's philosophy meetup (half the room is over 65 and will definately feel ambushed) on whether generations are real kinds. I want the talk built around a sorting instrument rather than a lecture: a decision tree that starts someone at either "obviously generations are a thing" or "obviously it's astrology for people with LinkedIn accounts" and walks them to a named position, without ever asking what they'd call themselves.

Do the logical space before the names. Lay out the questions and the answer-combinations they permit, then place the actual positions in the cells, and tell me about the empty ones — is nobody there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored?

Then the tree. Seven to nine branch questions, drawable on one slide. Each one has to be a concrete case or a claim someone can react to without knowing which way it points. Nothing shaped like "do you think generations are real?"

Nodes I think have to be in there, and tell me if I'm wrong:
- Which sense of "generation" they're even answering about — the demographic one (everyone born between two dates; real by stipulation, boring) versus the character one (a shared temperament). Make them commit early, and let the two branches diverge for the rest of the tree.
- That demography thing I half-remember: you can't statistically separate age, period, and cohort effects from survey data because they're locked together, since age is just period minus birth year, so any claimed cohort effect needs an extra assumption. Is that a strike against the kind, or only a measurement limit?
- Boundaries. Use the person born Dec 31 versus the one born Jan 1, and the micro-generation fudge.
- Whether the label feeding back changes the answer — classifications that alter the thing classified. Is a kind that partly exists because we named it second-rate, or is that just what social kinds are?
- Mechanism versus bare pattern: does the kind need a story about formative years, or is a persistent statistical difference enough?
- Transportability: someone born 1990 in Lagos who moved to Ohio at 12.
- And whether a kind can be real-but-useless, or useful-but-unreal, and which of those the tree is actually tracking.

Leaves I want reachable: generations as genuine social kinds; flat eliminativism (it's just age plus current circumstances, nothing left over); the pragmatist middle; the loop/performative view; fictionalism; reduction to some other variable entirely (class, or the media environment everyone happened to be inside); and the one I suspect is right — that there are real cohorts but the pop labels aren't them. "Unnamed" is fine anywhere nobody defends a view.

Also flag the pairs of leaves your tree can't actually separate, and say what extra question would do it or admit that none would. And give me the collapsing key: with eight binary questions there's way more paths than leaves, so show how paths fold together, and tell me what a contradictory path means — the person hasn't thought it through, or my nodes aren't independent?

No bibliography, no citations, don't recommend books. And phrase everything so the 70-year-old in the third row doesn't feel like the tree is scoring his generation.
