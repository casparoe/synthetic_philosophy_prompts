I'm prepping a two-hour session for a research-ethics working group — mixed bag, some med researchers, a couple of lawyers, one philospher — on when consent actually counts as valid. The easy cases are settled for everyone in the room: a signature on a form isn't sufficient, and a competent adult saying yes has to matter somehow. Everything downstream is where they fight, and they fight past each other because nobody can say which question they disagree about.

So I want a decision tree that starts at "the person did express agreement" and ends at a named position on whether that agreement is valid, and on what makes it valid or invalid. Seven-ish branch nodes, no more than ten leaves.

Ground rules:

- Every node question must be answerable by someone who has never heard any of the labels, and phrased as a concrete case or a plain yes/no about a principle — never "do you accept theory X?"
- The splits I actually care about, roughly in this order: is consent an attitude, a performance, or both; does validity come from the consent being genuine or from it doing moral work; deception — being wrong about what you're agreeing to versus being wrong about why it's worth agreeing to; coercion — threats versus offers, and whether a rotten background can make a choice nobody forced into coerced anyway; competence — is it about capacity or about knowing enough; and whether there's room for agreement you don't take yourself to be giving to still count.
- Work out the logical space before you name anything: say which combinations of answers are genuinely available and which are empty. For each empty cell, diagnose it — incoherent, just unappealing, or simply never explored.
- Each leaf gets a short name for the view and one sentence on the most awkward thing it's committed to.
- Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate, and say whether some question would separate them or whether they're the same view wearing two hats.
- No attributions, no citations, no bibliography — positions only.

Where a bare principle would be ambiguous, pin it with a case. Useful ones: a trial participant told a drug is experimental but not that the sponsor also markets the drug in the control arm; a surrogacy agreement signed under financial pressure nobody applied; a patient who agrees to a procedure whose name means nothing to her but whose effects she understands fine; a night-shift worker who signs because refusing costs more than he can lose.

Keep the whole thing under 800 words including the tree — indent hard, abbreviate hard, one sentence per node question.
