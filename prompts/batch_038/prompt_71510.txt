So my friend Nadia, who's finishing an STS PhD two states over, came to stay for the weekend and we ended up in a two-hour kitchen-table argument about Langdon Winner's "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" I'd read it once for a required ethics-of-engineering module and, full disclosure, skimmed the back half. Her opener was the Robert Moses thing: build the parkway overpasses too low for buses and you've excluded a whole class of people in concrete, no statute required. My response was that this is both true and boring — it's a spec failure, and spec failures get fixed. I do eligibility systems for a living, eight years of it, the back end that decides whether somebody actually gets a housing subsidy: schemas and constraints, nullability and sentinel values, migrations and backfill cost, defaults, breaking changes, permission models, audit trails, on-call burden, blast radius, who files the ticket and who prioritizes it. If a column is NOT NULL that some applicants can't populate, its a data modeling bug, you make it nullable or add an explicit unknown value, ship it, done. She said I'd just conceded Winner's first move without noticing and was refusing to engage the second one, the one about technologies that *require* certain kinds of social arrangement whether anyone designed them that way or not, and she made some sub-distinctions I only half followed — requiring a social system vs merely being strongly compatible with one, and conditions internal to how a system runs vs external to it.

What I want from you: restate Winner's argument as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually live in, both moves, including those sub-distinctions. Don't leave it in the abstract — run it against a real-ish case from my world. Our household entity is keyed on a residential address, mailing_address is NOT NULL, and there's an enum for immigration status with no value that means "I don't know" or "it's not safe to say." Unhoused applicants and mixed-status families fall out at intake, silently, becuase the row never validates. The fix is maybe a day of work and nobody has ever filed it, and my PM's line in the design review was that the schema is neutral, it's just modeling the world as it is. Three rules. First, translate honestly: don't smuggle my assumptions in and then call it translation — if the only way to render one of Winner's claims is to quietly convert it into a claim about sloppy data modeling, flag that instead of doing it. Second, give me a residue report in three buckets: what maps cleanly, what maps only with distortion (and say precisely what gets distorted in the process), and what has no expression at all in schema-and-migration terms. Third, resist the easy win of agreeing with me; I want to know if there's anything in Winner that my vocabulary structurally cannot hold, and if you think it can hold all of it, name the counterpart concept for the second move rather than just asserting one exists.

Format: bottom line first, three sentences max, telling me whether the residue is a defect in my framework or a sign that Winner's second move was vaguer than it sounds. Then the translation. Then the residue as a table with columns Winner's claim | how it renders in my vocabulary | what breaks or gets lost. If the working ends up moving you off your opening verdict, say so in one line at the end. Hard cap of 800 words, table included — I'd rather you cut an example than go over.
