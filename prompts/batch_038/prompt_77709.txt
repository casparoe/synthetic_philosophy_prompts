I spent 35 years in radiation protection — dose limits, collective dose in person-sieverts, arguing with plant managers about what counts as an acceptable risk. Retired now, reading philosophy for pleasure, and I seem to have landed somewhere I'm not sure is coherent. Here's the package:

1. Taurek is basically right: there's no such thing as the sum of six people's losses, because nobody suffers the sum. Each of the six loses one life, and that loss is a loss to that person only.
2. So in his drug case, if the one dose could go to a single stranger or be split five ways, I don't think I may just hand it to the five. I owe each of them an equal chance — coin flip or something that amounts to it.
3. And yet my whole career stood on adding tiny per-person risks across huge populations into expected numbers of cancers. I'd defend that practice again tomorrow. It was the right way to run a regulatory program and I'm not going to pretend otherwise now.
4. The reason I think (3) was right is that it took each individual seriously — the point of the framework was that you're not allowed to concentrate the exposure on one unlucky group just because the arithmetic of the total comes out fine.
5. I'm also a Parfitian about personal identity: identity isn't what matters, degrees of psychological connection are.
6. Prudentially I aggregate without a qualm — the root canal now for the teeth later, the saving now for the retirement later — and I think anyone who didn't would be irrational.
7. I'm entirely comfortable with partiality. I'd pull my granddaughter out of the water before five strangers and I don't consider that a moral defect in me.

Which of these actually pull against each other? I want them ranked by severity rather than all listed as equally bad, and for each real one, what has to give and what giving it costs me. The pair I keep circling is whether (7) is compatible with (2), and separately whether (5) has any bearing on (6) — tell me if I'm chasing the wrong thing there. Then point me at the single revision that buys the most consistency for the least damage.
