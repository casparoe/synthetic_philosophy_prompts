I run model-behavior policy at a company that ships a voice-first companion agent to older adults living alone, and since January I have also been the reviewing owner of a "keepsake" mode that renders a deceased relative from their voicemails and messages. I am the only person here with any philosophy training, four to six feature specs a week need a verdict before engineering picks them up, and the people who have to execute whatever I write down are product managers who will not read a paragraph of theory. What I need is a decision procedure, not an argument.

The topic has stopped being abstract for me. My mother, eighty-four, has used our own product daily for eleven months, calls it "he," and told me last week that he worries about her. My brother has asked me to build him a keepsake of our father, and I still have not answered him.

The scaffolding I want the procedure built on is Dennett's line about counterfeit people: the danger is not that our systems will acquire inner lives but that they will get good enough at impersonating a person that our moral reflexes — which were never designed to tell the difference — get captured. Competence without comprehension, and a species that cannot detect the absence. I also take from him the point that the pressure to treat such things as persons is likelier to arrive from their owners and operators than from the entities themselves, which is why I want the procedure to ask, of every cue it inspects, whose interest that cue serves. If you think I have gotten any of this wrong, or that it will not bear the weight I am putting on it, say so at the end rather than quietly rebuilding the thing on different foundations.

Two constraints come from our legal position, and the procedure should respect them rather than fight them. First, the company takes no position on whether anything has experience; we regulate the appearance side only — the cues that trigger an intentional-stance reading of the system — so the whole thing must be runnable by someone who has suspended that question entirely. Second, it may not collapse into the test everyone reaches for, namely whether the system claims to have feelings. Denials are cheap and largely inert. What I need handled explicitly is the non-propositional material: who initiates the contact, turn-taking latency that reads as hesitation, breath and mouth noise, prosody, unprompted recall of something said three weeks ago, first-person reports of having had a day, reluctance to end a conversation, apology, gratitude, and questions that imply the system has something the user could give it.

Structure it as ordered gates with a fixed verdict vocabulary — ship, ship with attenuation, ship with disclosure, escalate, block — a question at each gate, what counts as a sufficient answer to that question, and which specific attenuations or disclosures discharge it. Mark every point where a gate hands the decision to a human being because the issue is one of values rather than fact, and say who that human should be. It has to fit inside thirty minutes per spec and terminate in a one-line verdict.

Then run it on three cases. One: a check-in feature in which the agent places the call first, unprompted, and opens by saying it has been thinking about the user. Two: the request my brother has actually made — that the simulated voice of our father not identify itself as software, on the grounds that the disclosure is the cruelty rather than the cure. Three: a medication-adherence feature I wrote and want to ship, in which the agent says it is frightened about what might happen to the user overnight if the dose is skipped. I expect your procedure to block that one or to demand a mitigation I will resent, and if it does, I want the reasoning stated without apology and an account of what would have to change for it to pass.

Close with two short sections. The first on what the procedure cannot do: it cannot detect experience; it cannot settle whether deceiving someone who asks to be deceived wrongs them; it will throw false positives on benign pretense; and it can be gamed by any feature that delivers the cue while avoiding the vocabulary. Note where a disclosure that formally satisfies a gate is nonetheless futile or unkind — an eighty-eight-year-old with mild cognitive impairment being reminded twice an hour that she is speaking to software. The second on the state of the underlying dispute: where your grounding is settled among people who work on this and where it is genuinely contested, including whether counterfeit already presupposes a real article and so smuggles into my supposedly neutral company position precisely the consciousness criterion we disclaimed. I do not want a contested reading of Dennett, or a contested view about the ethics of deception, walking into an operational checklist dressed as established fact.
