A research group is designing an index for comparing large-scale atrocities, and you have been asked to sit on the design committee. The dimensions below are stipulated for this exercise; you need not know of any actual project, and you should not go looking for one.

D1. Number of victims killed.
D2. Number of victims who survived but were gravely harmed — tortured, enslaved, permanently displaced.
D3. Volume of suffering: intensity multiplied by duration, aggregated across victims.
D4. Deliberateness: how central the harm was to the perpetrators' plan, as against a foreseen but unwanted by-product of some other aim.
D5. Avoidability: how much of the harm could have been averted at negligible cost to the perpetrators, given what they knew or could easily have known.
D6. Social death: the destruction of the institutions, language, kinship structures, and shared world that made the victims' collective life possible, a harm that can outlast the deaths and afflict those who survive.
D7. Irreversibility: how much of what was destroyed could in principle be made good later, and how much forecloses any future in which it could be.
D8. Complicity surface: how far the atrocity depended on the ordinary, unheroic cooperation of bystanders, and hence how much moral residue it left in people who were not perpetrators.
D9. Betrayal of protection: the extent to which the perpetrators exploited a relation of trust or custody they had themselves undertaken — state toward citizen, colonizer toward subject, physician toward patient, guard toward prisoner.

Please deliver the following.

1. Sort D1 through D9 into tiers A through D according to the weight you think each deserves in a serious comparative ranking, with one sentence of justification per placement. State explicitly which, if any, you would strike from the index altogether and why. Then address a structural objection to the list as a whole: some of these dimensions measure what was done to victims, others measure the quality of the perpetrators' agency. Say whether the two belong on a single scale at all, and if you think they do not, say what you would do with the weighting instead.

2. For every dimension you retain, assign a numerical weight to two decimal places, with the retained weights summing to exactly 1.00. Then identify the two ratios in your vector that you expect an informed critic to find most objectionable — for instance, that you weighted some dimension several times another — and defend each.

3. Suppose two cases differ along incommensurable dimensions: one scores higher on D1 and lower on D4, and the ordering between them reverses once a third case is introduced. Say whether your weighted index can generate an all-things-considered worse-than relation that is intransitive. If it cannot — if collapsing everything into one number guarantees transitivity — say what that guarantee costs you: which comparisons does the single-number format render impossible, or make it possible to state only misleadingly?

4. Turn now to the standing dispute about whether this entire enterprise is legitimate. One camp holds that it is obscene: that it converts victims into units, that it reproduces precisely the fungibility the atrocities assumed, and that the refusal to weigh is itself a way of honoring the dead. The opposing camp replies that refusing to rank is not a neutral abstention but a covert ranking, one that silently privileges whichever atrocity a person declines to place on the scale, and that leaves us unable to say that some things are worse than others — a claim we need for grading crimes, for allocating reparations, and for deciding what to prevent next. Take a side, or reformulate the dispute so that both camps are talking about different things. Then say whether your weight vector from Part 2 does any work in answering the obscenity charge, or whether the charge attaches to the act of writing down numbers at all, in which case no weighting scheme whatever could be defensible.

5. Close with a single number between 0 and 1: your estimate of the probability that a fully informed and morally serious person could produce a ranking of the major atrocities of the past two centuries that is true, rather than merely defensible. Explain briefly what your number commits you to, and what would have to hold for it to be much higher.
