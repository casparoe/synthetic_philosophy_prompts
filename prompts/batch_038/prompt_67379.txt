Thought experiment for a phil-of-bio seminar I'm running. Set-up: a standing panel has to issue written, reasoned rulings on whether two disputed populations are one species or two, and a "two" verdict automatically triggers legal protection for the smaller population while a "one" verdict does nothing at all. So the concept they adopt isnt neutral — it quietly picks the default outcome.

Give me a litigability score out of 10 for each of the main species concepts — biological, phylogenetic, morphological, ecological, genotypic cluster, recognition — where 10 means the concept hands the panel a procedure it can actually follow and defend to the losing side, and 0 means it just dresses up whatever verdict the panel already wanted. Rank them, give a sentence or two per score, then take your top pick and pinpoint exactly where the panel's own judgment still leaks in despite the procedure looking mechanical. And which concept would a panel reach for if it secretly wanted the easiest way to not make anyone angry?
