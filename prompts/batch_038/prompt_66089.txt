Three months ago I became the manager of a team operating a large production service, and last week we had our first serious outage: a bad configuration change pushed by one of my engineers, undetected for forty minutes. I ran the review under the norm our organization has formally adopted, the blameless postmortem. Its rules are specific. The write-up may not name an individual as a cause; "human error" may not appear as a root cause; every decision in the timeline must be reconstructed as reasonable given what the person knew at the moment they made it; and all action items must be changes to systems, tooling, or documentation. Yet the same organization runs an annual performance cycle in which I must rate that engineer individually on ownership and accountability, and if the outage turns into litigation, counsel will want a record of exactly who did what and when.

What picture of human agency does the blameless rule presuppose? Since the incident I have been back in Nietzsche's *Genealogy of Morality*, and I keep landing on his treatment of the lightning and its flash — the claim that there is no being behind the doing, that the doer is a fiction added to the deed, one that serves those too weak to act by letting them hold the strong answerable. Is our postmortem policy that argument put into institutional practice, or does it quietly retain the very doer it claims to dissolve?

Reconstruct it carefully: what the rule strictly requires a review committee to believe about persons, choice, and causation; which rival accounts of responsibility it excludes rather than merely fails to fit; and where it is underdetermined, so that committees holding quite different views could run the identical procedure honestly. Then tell me whether it is coherent alongside the performance review and the litigation file, or whether one of the three must give — and if we applied the Genealogy's picture of agency company-wide rather than only in incident reviews, what would have to change about accountability, and whether any sense of it survives the change.
