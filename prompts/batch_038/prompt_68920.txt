I am a doctoral student in cognitive science designing a real-time fMRI neurofeedback study in which participants watch a moving bar that tracks multivariate activity in a region implicated in aversive learning, and are asked both to down-regulate that activity and to report, continuously, the intensity of the unpleasant feeling they take themselves to be having. Our pilot data show a systematic dissociation: participants can move the bar without any corresponding movement in their reports, and can be induced to report changes while the bar stays flat. My supervisor's response is to invoke the user-illusion picture of consciousness, on which the brain runs a simplified, action-oriented model of its own processing and hands that model to the system in place of the processing itself, so that first-person reports are readouts from an interface rather than from the machinery. He thinks the design is salvageable because, given enough training, participants will learn to introspect one level down and acquire the bar as a new introspective variable. Please describe the five most important implications of the user-illusion thesis for whether that optimism is warranted, addressing in particular whether an interface can be extended downward at all, what would count as evidence that a genuinely new introspective variable has been acquired rather than a second interface constructed on top of the first, and what my dissociation data do and do not license me to conclude. Please answer as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
