I am preparing a seminar paper on the ethics of historical judgment, and I need help putting a thesis in its strongest form rather than its balanced form.

The thesis concerns the Fischer controversy. My claim is that the quarrel between Fritz Fischer and his critics was never principally about the documents, but about two incompatible grammars of state responsibility, which I will call the occurrent and the dispositional. On the occurrent grammar a state is responsible for the difference its particular decisions made in July and August 1914; on the dispositional grammar a state is responsible for what its settled aims made the crisis likely to produce, whether or not those aims surface in any single decision. I want to argue that Article 231 of the Treaty of Versailles can only be read on the dispositional grammar — it fixes liability for loss and damage caused by a war it describes as imposed by aggression, and it was signed in 1919 by a republic that had neither declared nor fought that war under its own constitution — and that the Fischer thesis is best understood as a defence of that grammar rather than as a claim about the July crisis. The load-bearing step is inheritance: occurrent responsibility is exhausted by the ministers who acted, whereas a settled aim is the property of a polity that outlives them, and only that is what would allow a clause signed by one government to bind successors who signed nothing.

Please make that argument for me. The obvious objection is that dispositional responsibility is a verdict on national character dressed up as a claim about causes, and so is far too unmoored from events to ground a legal instrument; a second is that the grammar is promiscuous, since on it every great power of 1914 had aims that made war likelier. Answer both inside the argument rather than conceding them in passing, and commit to the thesis throughout — I do not want a weighing of the two sides.

Give me exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
