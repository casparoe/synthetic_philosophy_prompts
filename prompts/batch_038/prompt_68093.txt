so I'm giving a talk at a joint behavioral-ecology / behavioral-econ seminar next month and I want someone to poke at my view before I stand up in front of a room and present it. Here's roughly the package:

1. What makes a signal honest isn't that it's expensive, it's that it's *differentially* expensive — adding another unit of display has to cost a low-quality individual more than it costs a high-quality one. Absolute wastefulness does zero explanatory work, and it drives me up a wall when people just say "the tail is honest because it's costly" and stop there.
2. Related: I'm totally fine with indices, i.e. traits that are honest because they're physically impossible to fake, like a call whose pitch is locked to body size. Those don't have to cost anything.
3. I think preference-driven runaway exaggeration is real, and that some of the traits people wave around as handicaps are better explained by the receiver's own bias bootstrapping the trait upward with no information content at all.
4. On the human side: conspicuous consumption — the mechanical watch that keeps worse time than a twelve dollar quartz, the glass-walled office nobody works in, the fifth year of grad school that produces no marketable skill — is mostly costly signaling of some underlying quality, and the wastefulness is the point.
5. But I also think a big chunk of human status display is cheap talk held up by audience costs and repeated interaction rather than by expenditure.
6. Efficiency commitment: if there were a cheaper display that conveyed exactly the same information to exactly the same audience, selection would have moved the system to it. Signaling systems sit at or near the cheapest honest configuration available to them.
7. The empirical bit I want to build the talk around: in the charitable giving data I'm working with, people who publicly announce their donations give about a third as much as people who give quietly, and the announcers skew heavily toward the lower end of the income distribution in the sample. My gloss is that the announcement is a cheap substitute that crowds out the expensive act — once talk is on the menu, the money stops flowing.

Go through these and tell me which ones actually pull against each other and which only look like they do. I'd want the real tensions ranked by severity rather than listed as equally bad, and for each one, which commitment has to be dropped or rewritten and what the rewrite costs me. Then tell me the single revision that buys the most consistency for the least damage to everything else. Please don't give me a history of the signaling literature or a summary of the standard positions — I know all that, I just want the audit of my own set. And if one of these is doing sneaky work that the others forbid, say which one it is.
