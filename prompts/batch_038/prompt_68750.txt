**DRAFT: "Bundling and the Individuation of Choice: Why Risk-Weighted Expected Utility Needs a Canonical Description and Cannot Have One"**

**§1. A non-separability fact.** Risk-weighted expected utility theory evaluates an act by ranking its possible outcomes from worst to best and weighting each outcome's marginal utility by a risk function applied to the cumulative probability of doing at least that well: REU(f) = Σᵢ r(P(f ≥ aᵢ))·(u(aᵢ) − u(aᵢ₋₁)). When r is the identity function this reduces to expected utility; a convex r yields the global risk aversion that the theory needs in order to accommodate the Allais preferences.

The feature the whole paper turns on is that this functional is not additive across acts. Let r(x) = x², let utility be linear in money, and consider two independent fair coins each paying one dollar on heads. Evaluated separately, each coin is worth r(0.5)·$1 = $0.25, so the pair is worth $0.50. Evaluated jointly, the outcome distribution is $0 with probability 0.25, $1 with probability 0.5, and $2 with probability 0.25, giving r(0.75)·$1 + r(0.25)·$1 = $0.5625 + $0.0625 = $0.625. Bundling the two gambles makes them worth more than the sum of their unbundled values.

That is not a paradox; it is the ordinary pooling effect that makes diversification attractive. But it means REU does not assign a value to a gamble as such. It assigns a value to a gamble-as-bundled.

**§2. A conjecture, and its failure.** This suggests a conjecture, which I state and then immediately undercut: for convex r and linear u, joint evaluation of independent acts weakly exceeds the sum of separate evaluations. If something like this held generally, REU agents would not merely be sensitive to bracketing; there would be a systematic pull toward widening it.

The conjecture is false as stated. Perfect correlation reverses it. Let A pay $100 on heads and $0 on tails, and let B pay −$100 on that same heads and $0 on that same tails. Separately, each is worth r(0.5)·$100 = $25, for $50 between them; jointly, the payoff is a sure $0, worth $0. Bundling can destroy value.

What survives, I think, is the weaker claim that for any convex r there is a large and identifiable class of cases — independent risks that diversify — in which wide bracketing strictly dominates. That weaker claim is all I need, and I am not confident it is true either.

**§3. The objection and the reply it invites.** Critics press §1 as an individuation objection: REU's verdicts depend on how the decision problem is carved up, carving is up for grabs, and so the theory can license contradictory verdicts about one and the same real choice. The reply the framework invites is that the agent evaluates the choice she actually faces — two separate decisions are evaluated separately, one package is evaluated as a package. Rationality is relativized to the description under which the agent deliberates, and this is presented as a virtue rather than an embarrassment, since r is meant to capture an attitude toward the prospect as the agent conceives it.

**§4. My argument: the reply is static-only.** My claim is that this reply is available only for one-shot choices, and that it fails precisely in the dynamic case where the REU theorist most needs it. The standard dynamic worry is that a globally risk-averse agent who evaluates myopically at each node can be led into a sequence of choices that is worse, by her own lights at the outset, than a sequence she would have rejected as a package. The natural response is sophisticated planning: choose at the outset over complete plans, evaluated globally, and hold to the plan.

But sophisticated planning just is maximal wide bracketing. And if §2's surviving claim is right, wide bracketing is systematically advantageous across a broad class of cases, so sophistication is not a costless defensive maneuver — it is a positive prescription to keep widening the package. There is no largest package: any bundle of decisions can be bundled with more. So the theory faces a dilemma. Either (a) rationality is relativized to bracketing, in which case REU cannot answer the local question it was built to answer and, worse, the relativity looks exactly like the framing sensitivity that the theory's own proponents use to distinguish it from the descriptive weighting functions of rank-dependent and cumulative prospect theories; or (b) the theory mandates maximal bracketing, in which case the only unit of evaluation is the agent's entire decision career and no individual choice has a value at all.

**§5. Diagnosis and repair.** My diagnosis is that r is a transform of cumulative probabilities over a ranking of outcomes, so REU requires a determinate outcome distribution before it can get going. Utilities are fixed by the agent's values; distributions are fixed by how the problem is bundled. Expected utility theory is immune because expectation is additive, so the bundling drops out of the calculation. REU gives up additivity in order to get global risk sensitivity, and gives up bundling-invariance as an unadvertised consequence.

The obvious repair is to constrain r so that REU is additive across independent acts. My second conjecture is that the identity function is the only admissible r satisfying this constraint, so the repair collapses into expected utility. If that is right, the paper has a clean conclusion: global risk sensitivity and bundling-invariance are incompatible, and the REU theorist must say which she surrenders. If the conjecture is wrong and some non-trivial additive REU functional exists, the paper needs a different punchline.
