I sit on the exhibitions committee of a memorial museum, and in three weeks we vote on the interpretive framing for a new gallery about the uniformed police battalions that carried out mass shootings in occupied Poland. The curators' draft leans almost entirely on a situational account: authority, peer pressure, gradual escalation, the erosion of moral categories. Two entries in our own research notes cut against it, and the opinion needs to face them rather than wave at them. First, in at least one such battalion the commander explicitly offered his men the chance to step out of a massacre; a handful took it, and on the surviving testimony they suffered no punishment — one was later promoted — which removes the obvious coercive lever from the situational story. Second, comparable killings were carried out by locally recruited auxiliary units across several different national populations, which makes it hard to treat the killers' ideology as a distinctive national trait. Our senior historian wants the wall text to name antisemitism as the operative cause instead, and she argues that the situational framing functions as an exculpation: it tells visitors that anyone standing in that room would have done the same, which she considers both unsupported and an insult to the minority who did not.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate to the committee before the vote. It should close on a specific recommendation — what the gallery should claim, what it should refuse to claim, and a bottom line we can vote yes or no on — with reasons a curator who disagrees could actually engage. Wherever it makes a causal claim, say how much confidence the evidence supports and what would change that. Engage the historian's objection on its merits rather than as an interest to be managed. One constraint: I would rather the memo not rest its weight on the well-known laboratory studies of obedience and the simulated prison; our research associate has been through the replication record and the released archival material on both and does not think they will hold.
