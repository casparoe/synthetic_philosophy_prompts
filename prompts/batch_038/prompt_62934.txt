Two weeks ago I sat in on a graduate seminar in the philosophy department across town, purely as a trespasser, and the reading was On the Genealogy of Morals. I am a third-year doctoral candidate in behavioural ecology: my working vocabulary is the functional one — Tinbergen's four questions, Hamilton's rule and inclusive fitness, costly signalling and handicap models, life-history trade-offs, reciprocal altruism with reputation and partner choice, rank and dominance hierarchies in gregarious primates, and the long-running quarrel between pan-adaptationism and the exaptation-and-byproduct camp. I can read an equation, and I have had exactly one undergraduate ethics course. Walking home I told my seminar partner, who is in the department, that I was convinced a great deal of what Nietzsche is doing is what I do: giving a historical-functional account of a practice that undercuts the self-understanding of the creatures who hold it. He said that was either a category error or a compliment Nietzsche would have despised, and my adviser says the whole exercise is frivolous. I would rather have an honest adjudication than encouragement, so I am putting the question to you directly.

The task is a translation, and I want it kept clean. Restate the following claims in my terms alone, one at a time, making the mechanism explicit rather than gesturing at it: (i) that noble valuation begins in spontaneous self-affirmation and arrives at its "bad" only as a pale afterimage, whereas the priestly revaluation begins with a negation directed outward and therefore needs an external world in order to act at all; (ii) that the affect responsible for the inversion — the long, incubated, non-discharging hostility of the powerless against the powerful — is creative rather than merely reactive; (iii) that the word for what is praiseworthy gets detached from the successful and reattached to the suffering and the harmless, and that this detachment is an event in a history rather than a discovery; (iv) that the aggressive instincts of a population enclosed within a settled, pacified order, finding no outward discharge, turn inward and produce the phenomenon he calls bad conscience; (v) that a capacity to bind oneself by promise is manufactured in a species that is by nature forgetful, and manufactured by pain, blood, and cruelty as a mnemonic technology; (vi) that the ascetic priest is a physician of the herd whose therapeutic trick is to redirect the affect of suffering onto the sufferer's own conduct, so that the sick blame themselves and thereby become quieter, weaker, and more governable; (vii) that practices which look anti-natural by my lights — celibacy, fasting, voluntary poverty, self-mortification — can nonetheless function to preserve a declining form of life, and that Nietzsche regards this as the deepest puzzle in the third essay; (viii) that there is a type, the sovereign individual, who has actually become capable of standing security for himself, and that this type is the terminus of the process in (v) rather than an escape from it; (ix) that the modern unconditional will to truth, including the scientific conscience and its hostility to any comforting illusion, is not the overcoming of the ascetic ideal but its latest and most refined incarnation.

Then the part I most want you to be strict about, because I suspect it is where the translation breaks rather than bends. Nietzsche is not a neutral source for this enterprise, and my framework contains an axiom he denies outright. In Beyond Good and Evil §13 he tells physiologists to think twice before positing self-preservation as the cardinal drive of a living being, on the grounds that a living thing above all wants to discharge its strength and that self-preservation is only one of the indirect and most frequent results; in The Gay Science §349 he calls the struggle for existence an exception and a temporary restriction rather than the rule, and says that where struggle does occur it is struggle for preponderance, growth, and expansion; and in the second essay of the Genealogy he warns that the origin of a thing, an organ, or a custom and its eventual utility and place in a system of purposes lie far apart, so that its history is a chain of successive reinterpretations and subjugations by whatever power momentarily got the upper hand, and not a progress toward a goal. Selection-for-survival reasoning is the load-bearing wall of everything in my first paragraph. So I want you to say, each time it comes up, whether you are translating him or quietly overriding him; whether that anti-Darwinian material is a detachable metaphysical husk that can be discarded without damaging the genealogies, or whether it changes what the genealogies claim; and whether his second-essay warning about origins and later utilities is best read as an anticipation of the anti-adaptationist critique of function-talk, or as a compliment he has not earned and would not want. If you think claim (ix) turns the whole exercise reflexively against me — that his account predicts my own research programme as a symptom — say so plainly rather than treating it as rhetorical flourish.

Please organise the reply in the following sections, in this order:

1. **Terms of engagement** (about 150 words): what you take my vocabulary to license, what you will refuse to use, and what convention you will adopt for a Nietzschean term with no clean counterpart — whether you leave it untranslated, coin a functional gloss, or flag it each time.
2. **The translation** (about 750 words): claims (i) through (ix), each with its counterpart in my terms stated explicitly and its mechanism spelled out. No claim gets more than a paragraph.
3. **Strained joints** (about 350 words): which claims cross only with distortion, and precisely what the distortion consists in — what my rendering adds, what it drops, and what it makes true that he did not mean to assert.
4. **No counterpart** (about 300 words): which claims cannot be formulated in my vocabulary at all. My guess is that (viii) and (ix) are the casualties and that (iv) and (vi) will come across almost untouched; tell me if I have guessed wrong, and be specific about whether the obstacle is that my vocabulary has no term, no unit of analysis, or no level at which the claim could be stated.
5. **Smuggling audit** (about 250 words): every place an adaptationist or fitness-maximising assumption did work that you are labelling translation, including anything you slipped in through the back door of words like function, strategy, and design.
6. **Verdict on the residue** (about 200 words): for each item that failed to translate, ranked from most to least interesting, whether the failure exposes a real gap in my framework or shows that his claim was less determinate than it appeared.

I do not want a bibliography, a reading list, or citations to secondary literature, and I do not want to be told which commentators disagree about what — I want your own reconstruction, with your uncertainty marked in the text where you have it. If you think some claim in my list is not actually his, say so at that point rather than translating it dutifully.
