I'm a junior and a group of exchange students from Norway is coming to our school in October. My sociology teacher assigned me a 45-minute session to lead on the American Dream — classroom, whiteboard, me plus about twenty kids who've mostly learned about America from TV. Don't write me a script, I need the plan for how to run it. The teacher organizing the exchange explained janteloven to me, the whole thing about not thinking you're better than anyone else around you, and I'm now pretty sure that's the frame they'll walk in with, so my big fear is they hear the Dream as either bragging or a lie about how easy it is to get rich. What I actually want them to leave with is this: the Dream isn't mainly a prediction about how many people make it, it's what makes a wildly unequal society feel legitimate to the people on the losing end of it, and janteloven is an answer to that same problem pointed the other direction. So tell me what has to be in place before that can even register for them, which single story or example should carry the whole 45 minutes and what it needs and what it absolutely can't have (I have a feeling a real rags-to-riches person would wreck it but I can't say why), the misconceptions they'll bring plus the ones I'll accidentally create in the first ten minutes, and exactly where in the sequence I should head off each one. Then give me one question to ask at the end that would show it landed instead of them just being polite, and tell me what I should say out loud that I'm skipping — tall poppy syndrome is definitely on that list since I barely understand it myself. Answer in plain paragraphs like an email would, no headings, no bullet points, no bold.
