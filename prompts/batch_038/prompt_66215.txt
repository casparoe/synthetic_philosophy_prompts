I'm giving the baccalaureate address at my sister's small nondenominational Christian college in three weeks — about 400 graduates, their parents, the trustees, and the town's mayor plus two council members who have been publicly feuding since a homeless shelter got sited in the college's neighborhood last fall. The president asked me to speak on faith and public life. Here's the bind: I work on public reason, so I actually hold something like the Rawlsian line — when we're arguing about who gets what and who's allowed where, we owe each other reasons that don't depend on signing up to any one comprehensive doctrine. But I also think the proviso does real work: you're allowed to bring your faith into public argument so long as you'll give properly public reasons in due course. I don't want a speech that hides that, and the room will smell hypocrisy if I lecture believers about restraint while standing in a chapel.

So write the address, roughly nine minutes spoken. It should *do* the thing rather than describe it — model what arguing from faith-plus-public-reasons actually sounds like, and take a definite stand on the shelter fight without letting either side feel endorsed. It has to land for the pastors' kids in the front row and for the mayor. It can't use the phrases "public reason" or "overlapping consensus," and it can't name Rawls. And please no "we're all on a journey together" graduation fog — I'd rather it had a spine.

Be committed: don't hand me a menu of options or alternative endings. Pick, and say why. Format exactly:

1. **The address** — full text, ~1100 words, with a bracketed mark at the two or three spots where you're cashing the proviso.
2. **Why it's built this way** — ~300 words on the structural choices, especially how you made the shelter fight carry philosophical weight without turning the graduates into a case study.
3. **Where it's vulnerable** — ~200 words: the sharpest objection from a Rawlsian, and the sharpest from someone who thinks public reason is a gag on religious citizens, both aimed at this specific text — plus which of the two you think is right.
