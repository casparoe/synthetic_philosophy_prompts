**Commission:** an opening-night review, 800–1,000 words, in the voice and register of a metropolitan daily's chief drama critic — a little pompous, generously disposed, fond of the building's history, given to describing the interval. Headline, byline, dateline, body, and a star rating at the foot.

**The production:** a revival of a long-running piece called *The Inner Light*, staged at the Cartesian Theatre. The critic has come, as audiences always have, on the settled assumption that somewhere behind the proscenium there is a single seat, a single spectator, and a single moment at which the performance is finally *presented* — and that this presentation is what makes the evening happen at all. The review must be written from inside that assumption and never once step outside it. The critic is a sincere enthusiast, not a skeptic, and he must remain sympathetic; the target of the piece is the assumption itself, not the man holding it.

**The reportage:** everything the critic notices must be, to a philosophically literate reader, precisely the evidence that would demolish his assumption — while he files it as color and atmosphere. At minimum:

- The house turns out not to be one auditorium but a great many, each running its own rehearsal of the same evening, at its own tempo, some of them ahead of the action he is watching and some behind it, with no version ever declared final. He finds this bustling rather than alarming.
- He checks the royal box twice. It is occupied by a smaller critic in a smaller royal box, who is watching a smaller critic in a smaller royal box, and so on without end. He treats the nesting as a charming local tradition and does not ask who, at the end of the chain, is actually seeing the play.
- A lighting effect in which a red lamp is swapped for a green one so quickly that he will swear on his life he watched the red *turn into* green in mid-flight. The front-of-house manager offers him two explanations of this, and the two cannot both be true: either the effect was corrected in his recollection after the fact, or the effect he reports was never delivered to him at all and something else was assembled in its place. The manager cannot tell him which, and cannot tell him at what time the correction happened. The critic notes the staff's uncertainty as a charming provincial vagueness.
- He is obliged to concede that the note in his own programme, which explains his enthusiasm for the piece, may have been written after the enthusiasm it explains was formed. He waves this off.
- One compliment, meant sincerely, that inverts his whole premise without his realizing it: he praises the production for its *publicity* — for the fact that what makes any moment of the show count is not that it reached the royal box but that enough of the other auditoria picked it up, quoted it back to one another, and kept it in circulation afterward. He should call this the evening's great democratic achievement.

**The constraints that matter most.** Not one word of philosophical vocabulary may appear anywhere in the piece: no homunculus, no qualia, no binding, no representation, no consciousness, no stream, no multiple drafts, no user illusion, and no philosopher named or alluded to. Every idea above has to be dramatized as theater journalism — as seats, sightlines, ushers, programmes, lighting, and house management — or it doesn't appear. This is the point of the exercise: a reader who knows nothing about the philosophy should enjoy a slightly absurd night at the repertory, and a reader who does know it should recognize every single beat and be able to say exactly which argument each one is.

The tone must stay deadpan throughout; no winking, no nudge to the reader, no sentence that could only be written by someone outside the critic's assumptions. The irony has to be entirely in the gap between what he reports and what he concludes.

**The close:** a star rating — full marks, and generous ones — and a one-line verdict praising the evening for delivering, flawlessly, the illusion of a single continuous show happening in one place at one time to somebody. He must not notice what he has just conceded. End, if you can manage it, with him unable to say which of the many rehearsals he has reviewed, cheerfully proposing that it makes no difference.
