So I run an informal ethics-and-food meetup at my workplace, about a dozen of us, a mix of engineers and one landscape ecologist who keeps winning arguments I'm pretty sure I should be winning. Next month it's my turn to present, and I've decided to just put my actual views on the table as a list people can attack, rather than doing a talk about the literature. Before I do that I want a consistency check from you, because I have a creeping suspicion that some of these don't sit together and I can't work out which ones, or how badly. Here's where I've landed: (1) I'm a welfarist on a roughly total view — what matters is the sum of wellbeing across all sentient beings, or equivalently the avoidance of suffering, and I'm not a speciesist, so sentience is the only thing that gives a being weight, though I do think the weight scales with how much a being can suffer, so a cow counts for more then a beetle, maybe a lot more. (2) I eat beef but not chicken or pork, and I defend this on deaths-per-meal grounds: one steer gives you thousands of meals while you need hundreds of chickens for the same protein, and those chickens live short, wretched lives in enormous numbers, so fewer deaths and fewer bad lives per plate is my metric. (3) I also think the standard "meat is worse because of all the crops fed to it" line mostly doesn't apply to the beef I buy, since pasture-raised cattle eat grass we couldn't eat anyway and don't compete with cropland. (4) I take crop deaths seriously — the voles, mice, and insects killed by ploughing, harvesting and pest control mean a plant-based diet is not a death-free diet, and I get irritated when people talk as if it were. (5) I think most wild animal lives are, on balance, not worth living, especially for insects and other r-strategists, most of whom die very young in unpleasant ways. (6) So when I weigh intensive farming on less land against extensive farming on more land, I don't give any credit to "sparing habitat for wildlife," because spared habitat just means more of the suffering in (5) — but I still prefer intensive, because it kills fewer crop animals per calorie. (7) I'm against deliberate large-scale interventions to reduce wild animal suffering, like wildlife contraception programmes or fertility tweaks, because we understand ecosystems far too poorly and could easily make things worse; I think we should restrict ourselves to reducing the number of animals we bring into existence on farms, where the effects are legible. (8) I eat oysters and mussels freely — I think they're probably not sentient, I'm not certain of that, and I don't feel bad about it.

Go through these and tell me which ones actually pull against each other and how badly, ranked worst first rather than listed as if every conflict were equally serious. For each genuine one, say what has to give and what the cheapest revision would be, and whether that revision costs me something I'd actually notice or something I'd barely miss. I especially want to know whether (2), (3) and (4) can all survive together once you take (5) and (6) into account, and whether the way I talk in (6) is compatible with the humility I insist on in (7) — i.e. am I allowing myself a confident ecological claim in one place and pleading ignorance in another, or is there a real difference between the two situations? And if some item only looks like a problem and dissolves once you spell it out properly, say so and say why, instead of lumping it in with the genuine conflicts; I'd rather have one clean "this one's fine" than a wall of vague unease. At the end, tell me which single change to the list would repair the most. I'm deliberately not telling you which commitments I'd rather keep, so please don't infer my priorities from the order I happened to write them in.

Please answer in plain continuous prose, like an email or a letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, and don't fire a numbered list back at me. If you need to point at one of my items, just do it in the sentence, e.g. "your third" or "the oyster one."
