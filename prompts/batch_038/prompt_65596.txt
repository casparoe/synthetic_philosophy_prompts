Some context: I do blind listening tests for a small audio company. We compare signal chains — DACs, amps, interconnects — by having trained listeners A/B them in randomized pairs and report "first is better," "second is better," or "no difference." Our internal stopping rule is that a difference only counts if it clears what we call the panel threshold: enough listeners preferring the same unit, over enough trials, to be miles past chance.

Here's what started it. I ran a chain of 200 units, each a hair different from the previous one — a slightly different reconstruction filter here, a slightly different power supply there. Unit 1 against unit 2: no difference. 2 against 3: no difference. Every adjacent pair in the chain came back "no difference," all 199 of them. But unit 1 against unit 200 was a landslide, listeners picked 200 basically every time. So "sounds the same as" isn't transitive, fine, everyone knows indiscriminability is intransitive. Then I made the move I now regret:

(1) If two things sound the same to a competent listener in a fair paired test, then neither is better than the other in the only respect that matters here, which is how they sound. (2) Every adjacent pair in my chain sounds the same in such a test. (3) So for every adjacent pair, neither member is better than the other. (4) Either "better than" is a genuine ordering, in which case it's transitive, or it isn't a genuine comparative at all. (5) If it's a genuine ordering, its complement is transitive too: from "a is no better than b" and "b is no better than c" you get "a is no better than c." (6) So chaining (3) and (5), unit 1 is no better than unit 200. (7) But the endpoint tests say 200 is plainly better than 1, and not marginally. (8) So "better than" is not transitive after all, and by (4) it isn't a genuine comparative: there's no fact of the matter about which chain is better, only facts about whether a given pair clears the panel threshold. (9) And that generalizes — apply it to welfare and the whole spectrum business goes away, the repugnant conclusion, mere addition, all of it, because those arguments need "better than" to be a real transitive relation and I've just shown it isn't, so every argument of the form "no step is worse than the last, so the end isn't worse than the start" is invalid and nothing in that neighborhood gets off the ground.
