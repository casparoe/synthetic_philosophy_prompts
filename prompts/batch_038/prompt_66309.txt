I am drafting the conceptual section of a proposed audit standard for automated choice architectures — recommendation feeds, eligibility screeners, personalised routing and pricing — that would let an outside auditor rate such a system on how much freedom it leaves the people subject to it. Before committing the standard to any particular conception of liberty (Berlin-style negative liberty, republican non-domination, or a positive or capability-inflected account of effective agency), I want the specification: the requirements any conception must meet to be usable for this purpose. Adequacy is therefore relative to a concrete job, and the job is demanding. The conception must yield judgments an auditor could defend both to the system's operators and to the affected users, on the basis of evidence about the system's design and behaviour rather than introspective reports from the people living under it, and it must yield judgments that are comparable across users and across systems.

Please write the desiderata list. For each requirement, give: the requirement stated precisely enough that a conception could determinately fail it; the case or consideration that motivates it; whether it is genuinely non-negotiable for this purpose or is in fact a partisan preference of one camp wearing the clothes of a requirement; and how a candidate conception would be tested against it. For each, also say whether it is a constraint on the concept of freedom itself or on the measurability of freedom, and flag the places where you think that distinction stops holding.

Treat the following scenarios as the pressure points. You may add others, and you should say explicitly which of these you think are not really demanding anything of a theory:

(a) Option inflation. A platform adds ten thousand items a given user would never select. On any metric that counts open doors, her freedom has increased; nothing she has any reason to want is more available to her than before.

(b) Silent pre-emption. An employment platform stops surfacing certain postings to a user because it predicts a low probability of application. She is never refused anything, notices no absence, and is presented with no decision she could contest. A metric keyed to interference actually suffered, or to interference the subject could register, reports no loss.

(c) Accommodated adaptation. A system learns a user's contracted ambitions and satisfies them flawlessly. Interference is nil and satisfaction is high; yet the same user two years earlier had access to a wider range of pursuits she might have come to want. Contrast her with a user whose system fits her noticeably worse but keeps surfacing options she initially resists and sometimes takes up. Any conception that scores the two users by their own reported experience of freedom ranks the first above the second.

(d) Domination without a dominator. The constraint on a user arises from a decision threshold combined with a training objective. No one — not the vendor, not its staff, not any public body — is aware of her case or able to adjust the rule in response to her interests. There is thus no agent whose power over her could be called arbitrary in the sense of tracking someone's will rather than her interests; but equally there is no one she could appeal to and no way for her to predict or plan around the rule.

(e) Liberty-enhancing constraint. A cooldown period, a default setting, a rate limit, a friction step. The option set narrows at the moment of choice, while the user's capacity to act on her own considered ends over a longer horizon improves. A conception that scores only the instantaneous option set will misclassify these, and one that scores only long-run agency will license a great deal of substitution of the auditor's or the designer's ends for the user's.

(f) Comparability. The standard must produce ratings that can be compared across differently situated users and across differently designed systems, so it cannot bottom out in each person's own sense of how free she was.

Having laid out the list, I want three things from you on top of it.

First, identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidate conceptions, and explain why that one rather than another — I expect several of the scenarios above to load onto the same requirement, and I want to know which.

Second, and this is what I most want: determine whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. If so, do not merely assert the tension — lay out the steps by which satisfying one requirement forces the violation of another, and state which requirement the audit purpose should give up, or which should be weakened and how. If you conclude that no subset is genuinely unsatisfiable, say which apparent tension you think is most often mistaken for one, and dissolve it.

Third, note where a requirement, if adopted, would make the standard gameable — that is, cheaply satisfiable by a system that changes nothing anyone would care about — since a specification that operators can pass without improving anything is worse than no specification.

Close with a short assessment, a few lines each, of how a non-interference conception, a non-domination conception, and an effective-agency or capability conception fare against the list as you have drawn it. Name positions and their commitments; I do not want sources, references, or a reading list, and please do not pad the assessment — the requirements and their motivating cases are the deliverable.
