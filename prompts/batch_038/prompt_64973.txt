I sit on the drafting committee of a citizens' assembly charged with proposing an electoral framework for a new 240-seat legislature. One delegate has tabled a proposal derived explicitly from Simone Weil's case for the general suppression of political parties. Its clauses: (1) every candidate stands as an individual, and no party label or emblem may appear on the ballot; (2) no elected member may be bound by any prior undertaking to vote with others, and caucus discipline is legally void; (3) any organization that endorses, finances, or campaigns for candidates — party, union, trade association, advocacy group — loses its legal standing to do so; (4) after the election members may form study groups with no disciplinary powers, no public funding, and no entitlement to a share of floor time. The delegate's argument is Weil's: a party is a machine for manufacturing collective passion and an organization built to exert collective pressure on the thought of each of its members, its first and ultimately its only end being its own unlimited growth, so that the operative question for a member ceases to be whether a position is just or true and becomes whether it serves growth — which is why she thinks independent judgment is not merely discouraged in a party member but structurally unavailable.

Draft the committee's written opinion — adopt, reject, or adopt with amendments — in a form I can circulate before Friday's vote. It should engage Weil's reasoning on its own terms rather than resting on the observation that no modern democracy has actually abolished parties, and it should say what the proposal does to the functions parties are usually credited with: aggregating dispersed, lightly held opinion into coherent programs, recruiting and vetting candidates, and letting the public identify who is responsible for outcomes and hold them to account at the next election. If you recommend amendments, give criteria the committee can apply to further clauses as they come in. Close by stating the strongest consideration on the losing side and why it does not carry the decision.
