A friend and I have just come off a two-hour argument about whether the cyclical theorists of history amount to anything, and we have reached an impasse I would like you to break. His position is that Polybius, Ibn Khaldun, Spengler, and Toynbee fail together, for the same reason: each smuggles in the state as the only kind of thing capable of carrying a cycle. Mine is that at least one of the four states a mechanism that is not essentially about polities at all.

Please adjudicate by rating each of the four on a single dimension I will call institutional portability: the extent to which the theorist's explanatory machinery can be lifted, causal structure intact, onto a unit of analysis that is not a state — a trading firm, a scientific specialty, a monastic order, an open-source project. Score each from 0 to 10, where 10 means the mechanism was never really about states and the polity is incidental to it, and 0 means it cannot be stated at all without reference to a coercive and fiscal apparatus.

For each score, do two things. First, name the single component of the theory you had to abandon or reinterpret in order to make the transfer work. Second, say whether what survives the transfer is still a cycle or has quietly become a one-way process. Then tell me which of the four you judge the transfer to fail outright, and — if any two of them turn out to be the same mechanism in different vocabulary — which two, and what the shared mechanism is.

Assume I know the theories well; I want the numbers defended, not the systems summarized.
