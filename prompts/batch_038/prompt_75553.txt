I work in communications for a city council in the north of England, which means I spend an unhealthy amount of my life dealing with statues and street names. Something keeps happening that I've never been able to put a name to, and I want to know whether there's an established concept for it. No references or reading lists please — I'm reading this on the bus and I don't have the background anyway. I just want to know whether the thing I keep seeing is a thing.

Here's the pattern. A statue of some Victorian worthy stands in a square for ninety years. In all that time it provokes nothing: nobody writes to us about it, no one petitions, most people can't tell you who it is. Then a campaign gets it taken down and the volume is enormous — letters, national coverage, people who had never once expressed a view on the man in question suddenly furious that he's gone. And the empty plinth turns into more of a talking point than the statue ever was. Same with street names: a road named after a slave trader gets renamed and it's a six-month fight, whereas the thousands of roads named after nothing in particular, or after a hedge, attract no comment at all.

What strikes me is the asymmetry. Leaving an honour in place seems to cost nothing expressively, while undoing one costs a fortune, even though the two situations differ only in which one you happen to be in. The removal reads as a much bigger statement than the retention, and I can't work out whether that's a fact about the act, about attention, or about loss.

A colleague says it's the Streisand effect but I dont think so — nobody's trying to suppress anything, and it carries on after the news cycle dies. Is there a proper term for this, from philosophy or heritage studies or wherever? And if there isn't, could you coin one? What I actually want is the carving rather than the label: what the phenomenon is, what it isn't — including which near-miss concepts get closest and where they strain — and where the edges are. Does it still bite if you replace the statue with a different one rather than leaving the plinth bare? Does renaming a street behave differently from knocking something down? And is there any case where it flips, so that keeping something becomes the louder statement than removing it?
