You are a professional philosopher of moral responsibility with a specialty in the ethics of blame, and you have sat through — and coached others through — enough hostile question periods to know that the danger is never the objection you expect. I want you to prepare me for the Q&A after a talk I am giving, and I want you to be adversarial on my behalf rather than reassuring. Think carefully and step by step; do not produce the prep sheet until you have brainstormed candidate objections.

**The setting.** Forty-five minutes, interdisciplinary workshop on moral psychology and moral responsibility. The room is not a general philosophy audience: (a) two committed Strawsonians who hold that blame is the expression of a reactive attitude and that questions of "standing" must be answered inside that framework or not at all; (b) one second-personalist who thinks standing is a matter of the blamer's entitlement to make a demand, grounded in the equality of persons; (c) one skeptic who denies that standing is a distinct normative condition at all and treats the whole debate as a confusion about blameworthiness; (d) two legal theorists, one working on the clean-hands defense in equity, one on the tu quoque argument in international criminal law, both interested in whether philosophy can give them anything operational; (e) one empirical moral psychologist who runs vignette studies on how ordinary people rate hypocritical blame; (f) a Kantian who cares about respect for persons and the treatment of the blamed as an end.

**My thesis.** Two claims. First, the *calibration account*: standing is not a moral status but an epistemic one. Blame functions to transmit moral reasons — to the wrongdoer and to the community. A blamer lacks standing exactly when her disposition to resent is an uncalibrated detector of wrongness: when the intensity of her response is modulated by her own stake in the act, so that identical conduct registers differently depending on who performs it. Hypocrisy is the paradigm case of lost standing not because it forfeits a second-personal entitlement, but because it is public evidence that the detector is biased. Second, *relativity*: standing is a property of blame-tokens, not of persons, and is indexed to audience — wrongdoer, community, self — so that standing can be lost with respect to one and retained with respect to another, and it comes in degrees, with thresholds fixed by what blame has to accomplish for each audience.

**My argument sketch, for you to attack.** (i) The point of blame is the conveyance of moral reasons. (ii) Conveyance requires that the recipient have reason to treat the signal as tracking wrongness rather than the blamer's interests. (iii) Uncalibrated resentment destroys that reason. (iv) Therefore standing is calibration, relativized to audience and function.

**Deliverables.**

1. A brainstorming pass first: twelve to fifteen candidate objections from *this* audience, one line each, no analysis.
2. Then the prep sheet: the eight to ten I will actually get, ranked by how much damage they would do, each with — who raises it and how they would phrase it aloud, in their own vocabulary and including their technical terms (write it as the spoken question, not as a summary); a reply I can deliver in about two minutes; a one-line note on where the reply is soft; and what I should concede up front versus hold to the end.
3. The question I am hoping nobody asks: the objection the calibration account cannot absorb. Say honestly whether it is fatal, merely costly, or survivable with a retreat I should rehearse now.
4. Two or three places where I am likely to overstate the view under pressure, and where a strategic retreat would buy more than a defense.

**Constraints.** No bibliography, no reading list, no citations, and do not put words in the mouths of real authors — if you invoke a published position, characterize it generally rather than attributing specific claims to named texts. No generic presentation coaching; every line should be about the argument. Assume I already anticipate the obvious versions of the self-blame problem, the fact that apology seems to restore standing without improving calibration, the reliable-but-unrelated busybody, and the mismatch between graded standing and the binary verdicts legal practice needs: for those, tell me where my draft replies fail, and spend most of your effort on objections I have not listed. An objection that is merely true of my account but not damaging does not earn a slot.
