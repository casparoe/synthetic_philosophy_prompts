I am putting together a two-page handout for a mixed graduate seminar — roughly half philosophy, half law — whose topic is what survives of desert-based punishment once Galen Strawson's Basic Argument is on the table. For reference, since the handout must stand alone: the argument runs that nothing can be the cause of itself; that to be truly morally responsible for what one does one would have to be responsible for how one is, in certain crucial mental respects, since every intentional act issues from the particular motives and dispositions one happens to have; that this would require an endless backwards chain of self-formation terminating in self-creation; and that therefore no one is truly morally responsible. Note that it is meant to bite whether or not determinism is true, which is exactly why the seminar keeps drifting into the wrong debate.

The centre of the handout is a decision tree that sorts a participant by what they say about cases rather than by what they call themselves. Nobody should be able to reach their preferred leaf by recognising its name. Please build it as follows.

**1. The logical space first, before any tree.** Identify the small number of binary questions doing the real work here — my own candidates are whether blame for an act requires authorship of the character the act issues from; whether one act of deliberate self-formation stops the regress or merely moves it back a step; whether indeterminacy at the decisive moment adds anything to responsibility; whether the impossibility result depends on determinism at all; and whether the concept of desert should be revised, retained as a fiction, discarded, or dissolved. Display the combinations these questions generate, and for each say whether a real position occupies it. Where a cell is empty, diagnose the emptiness: is the combination incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored? For at least one cell you judge unexplored rather than impossible, spell out what someone standing there would have to believe in order to get away with it.

**2. The tree.** Eight to eleven nodes, each a verdict question answerable by someone with no vocabulary for the debate. No node may contain the name of a position, and no node may ask a participant to declare a doctrine ("do you believe in free will", "do you think determinism is true", "is ultimate responsibility required?"). Each node should be a case, or a direct question about a case, with two or three answer options that are themselves stated in case-language. I have drafted four cases below; use them, reorder or split them as the structure requires, and amend them only where an amendment makes them sharper — but tell me what you changed and why.

- **The Curator.** From age twenty-two to thirty-two, Dana was the undisclosed subject of a longitudinal social experiment. The research team shaped which people she met, which jobs she was passed over for, which books reached her, and which conversations she overheard, in order to instil a stable, thoroughly non-confrontational character. No single decision of hers was coerced, and she was never lied to about any particular fact; the shaping worked entirely through her circumstances. At thirty-four she reflects on her character, endorses it without reservation, and cannot conceive of wanting to be otherwise. When a colleague is systematically destroyed by a superior over several months, Dana sees everything, could speak up at negligible cost to herself, and says nothing. Her silence issues, by hypothesis, from exactly the dispositions the team installed, operating normally. Is it blameworthy?
- **The Loop.** Eitan, at thirty, soberly and for reasons he fully endorses, undertakes a treatment he knows will leave him permanently indifferent to gambling, which he currently overvalues. Ten years later the indifference is complete, and he has never relapsed. Was the adult choice that reshaped him an act of self-authorship, given that he made it because he was already the kind of person who wanted to change — a person whose materials he did not choose?
- **The Twin Worlds.** Wren-1 and Wren-2 are alike in every physical and psychological respect and face the same decision: betray a friend for money. In Wren-1's world the outcome was objectively open at the moment of decision, settled by an indeterministic process in her head; in Wren-2's world the same sequence unfolds deterministically from the distant past. Both betray. Now repeat the comparison with the openness relocated — in Wren-1's world the indeterminacy sits not at the decision but in an earlier choice she made about what sort of person to become. Does your verdict change between the two versions?
- **The Bench.** A sentencing judge privately accepts that no offender is ultimately responsible, inside a system whose stated rationale is desert and whose public legitimacy depends on citizens believing that offenders get what they deserve. Which is closest to what should happen next: the practices continue and the metaphysical claims are quietly dropped; the practices and the claims both continue, because dropping the claims would cost more than the error does; the practices are dismantled; or nothing need happen, because the supposed error was an artefact of doing philosophy about a social institution?

**3. The leaves.** Between eight and eleven, each carrying: the name of the position in parentheses only if it has a standard one, one sentence on the commitment its occupant is least able to defend, and one sentence on what the position leaves of retributive sentencing — desert intact, desert retained as an admitted social technology, or desert gone. The leaves I want the tree to be able to reach include: Strawson's own pessimism, on which moral responsibility is impossible yet our reactive attitudes neither can nor need be abandoned; hard determinism or hard incompatibilism, which treats the impossibility as flowing from the causal closure of the past; event-causal libertarianism of Kane's kind, which keeps ultimacy by locating uncaused-but-willed self-forming choices earlier in the agent's history; agent-causal libertarianism proper; reasons-responsiveness compatibilism, which denies that responsibility for one's character is a precondition of responsibility for one's acts; real-self or reflective-endorsement views, on which wholehearted identification with one's motives is all the authorship there could be; the view that splits responsibility into mere attributability, which survives, and accountability-blame, which does not; revisionism about desert, which keeps a scaled-down notion; illusionism, which keeps the practice and the public claim while denying the metaphysics; eliminativism, which wants the practice dismantled; and a deflationary quietism on which the whole dispute is a philosopher's artefact. You may collapse or add leaves if the case-verdicts demand it, but say so.

**4. What the tree cannot do.** Flag every pair of leaves your node set does not genuinely separate — I suspect hard determinism and Strawsonian pessimism, since they differ only about what does the work, and illusionism and eliminativism, since they differ only about a policy that a case-verdict may not track. Say what further question would separate them, if any exists. Also include one consistency check: an answer pattern that no single position licenses — for instance, denying in the abstract that blame requires authorship of one's character while saying that the Curator's silence is not blameworthy — together with a sentence telling such a participant what to make of themselves.

Two further constraints. The handout carries no citations, no reading list, and no publication dates; it must be usable by someone who has read nothing but the argument sketched above and the four cases. And please do not pad it with a history of the free will debate — the seminar has already had that lecture.

Finally, tell me how confident you are that your node set is jointly exhaustive of the real positions and independent of one another, and single out the placement or branch you are least sure about, together with the alternative you considered and why you set it aside.
