I'm one of two philosophers on the grants panel of a mid-sized animal welfare funder — the rest are field ecologists, two vets, and a program officer who keeps us on schedule. Our whole remit is wild animal welfare, which sounds simple right up until you read our charter, which contains a line nobody seems to have thought hard about when it was drafted: we fund work that makes existing or future animals better off, and we do not fund work whose main effect is to change which animals exist. In four years we've never once had to apply that clause. This cycle we have to.

Here's the situation. Same population, same money, two applications, and the budget stretches to one. There's a warren of wild rabbits on a fenced reserve, maybe 30–40k animals, and the regional climate outlook says a bad drought is likely within three years — on this reserve that has historically meant mass starvation plus a slow, ugly viral outbreak on top of it. Applicant 1 wants a three-year field trial of an oral immunocontraceptive delivered in bait; their population model says numbers drop roughly 70% with no animal harmed or killed at any point, and the animals who "lose out" are ones who never get conceived. Applicant 2 wants a humane cull of about the same number of animals ahead of the drought, plus suplementary feeding for the survivors; on their numbers it's slightly worse on the aggregate metric, but every animal affected is an animal you can point at. Our scoring rubric spits out something called welfare-adjusted animal-years and it's totalist in structure — it just sums across every animal that will ever live — and it rates the two proposals within a couple of percent, which is well inside the error bars of both models. So the panel keeps going in circles on two things: our own metric calls it a tie, while the charter clause seems to point in opposite directions for each proposal, and I genuinely cant tell whether it forbids both, or forbids the one that harms nobody and permits the one that kills. Two further facts that keep coming up: a cull was run on the neighbouring reserve and the population bounced back inside two years, whereas the contraceptive is claimed to hold for about five; and one reviewer thinks bait-based contraception will shove predator pressure onto a ground-nesting bird that nobody has costed, which may or may not sink proposal 1 on its own.

Can you draft the memo I'll circulate 48 hours before the meeting? Two pages max, and it has to land somewhere — fund 1, fund 2, fund neither, or fund both at partial scale with specific conditions attached. I want you to actually pick and defend it rather than hand me a menu and a shrug, and I don't want a lit review or a list of which philosophers say what: half the panel has read no philosophy at all and nobody expects references. It does need to give the ecologists and vets reasons they can argue back against, so state plainly what you'd do with the rubric (does a totalist sum even mean anything when most of its terms are animals who won't exist?) and what you'd do with the charter clause — if you think the clause is incoherent, or has to be read against its own stated purpose, say so directly and say what we should tell the board about amending it. And give the losing proposal its strongest case in a paragraph of its own, not a strawman, so I can tell whether I'm on the wrong side of this.
