I am a third-year doctoral student in reinforcement learning theory. My exposure to decision theory amounts to one seminar this term, two weeks of which were devoted to the absent-minded driver problem — the example Piccione and Rubinstein use to raise questions about decision problems with imperfect recall. I would like you to restate that debate in the vocabulary I actually work in, and then give me an honest account of what the translation fails to carry.

The setup as I have it: a driver's route home requires leaving the highway at the second of two exits, so he plans to continue at the first and leave at the second. Executing the plan yields strictly more than either deviation — leaving at the first exit, or continuing past both. When he reaches an exit he cannot tell which one it is; he does not even know whether he has already made a decision. The treatments I read hold that this generates a genuine puzzle about what he ought to do at the exit and about what he may believe there, and they propose consistency conditions tying his interim beliefs back to his ex ante plan. My reading is that the upshot is that he ought to randomize at the exit, though I cannot tell whether that follows from the payoffs or from the consistency requirement, and I would rather be corrected than confirmed.

Please do the following.

First, translate. Take the substantive claims in this debate — those concerning ex ante planning versus interim choice, what the driver should believe when he cannot locate himself, the proposed consistency conditions, and whether the driver at the exit is in any sense the same agent as the driver who planned the route — and restate each in my terms: MDPs and POMDPs, belief states as sufficient statistics, policy classes and the restrictions placed on them (memoryless, bounded-memory, recurrent), Bellman optimality, planning as search over policies versus acting as execution of one, off-policy evaluation, and regret. Where there is no clean counterpart, say so rather than reaching for the nearest thing.

Second, derive. For a generic payoff triple in which the plan is best and both deviations worse, work out what the optimal policy restricted to the exit decision looks like and what belief over locations it implies, and tell me whether that derivation requires anything the game-theoretic treatments add.

Third, and most importantly, the residue report. Sort the claims into those that translate without distortion, those that translate only at the cost of changing what they mean, and those with no expression in my framework at all. For the distorted ones, name what the distortion smuggles in. Then run the translation in reverse: which distinctions my vocabulary draws routinely — between restricting a policy class and updating a belief, between a stochastic policy and indecision, between a non-Markovian environment and an agent with truncated memory — are missing from the treatments I read, and does their absence explain why the problem looks paradoxical there? I have also seen it suggested that this is structurally the Sleeping Beauty puzzle, and separately that it motivates decision theories that evaluate policies rather than acts; tell me whether either suggestion survives translation into my vocabulary, or whether both are casualties.

Finally, tell me where you think the residue belongs. Is it a defect in my framework — a missing primitive, having to do with commitment or with the identity of the deciding agent across time — or does it show that the disagreement is less substantive than it appears once the policy class is stated explicitly? My working instinct is that this is a bounded-memory policy problem wearing a philosophical costume, and I want to know what that instinct is missing, not have it flattered by a translation that quietly assumes it.

One caution: do not settle the matter by declaring the driver a POMDP agent. A POMDP policy conditions on the full history of observations and actions, and my driver does not; whether his information structure is representable in my framework at all is part of what I am asking.

Please reconstruct the arguments from their structure rather than pointing me to sources; I will chase down the references myself.
