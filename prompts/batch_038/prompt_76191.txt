ok so our reading group is putting together a end-of-semester zine — we've been going through the historical weirdness of mid-century AI rather than the straight philosophy of it, and my slot is the poem. I drew Turing and ESP, which sounds like a gotcha but isn't. In the 1950 Mind paper, after the imitation game, he runs through nine objections to machine intelligence and the last one is extrasensory perception — and he doesn't swat it. He says something to the effect that the statistical evidence for telepathy is genuinely impressive, and instead of arguing it away he just starts working out how you'd have to re-engineer the setup so the test stays fair, telepathy-proof rooms and so on. That's the thing I want the poem to hold: the man who wanted to settle questions about minds by inspecting nothing but their outputs was willing to grant a faculty that delivers information with no output to inspect at all, and whose only real threat is to the integrity of his own screen.

Can you write me a pantoum about that? Five or six quatrains, standard interlocking business — lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next — and close it traditionally, so the final stanza brings the poem's opening line and third line back as its second and fourth. I want the form to be the argument, not decoration: the carried-over lines are the leak, information turning up in a stanza it was never written for, so that someone who knows how a pantoum works should be able to feel the difference between inferring something and being handed it. If you can manage it, let the lines that get inherited belong to whoever is on the far side of the screen and the genuinely new lines to the interrogator, so by the closing stanza he's saying his own opening words back and can't tell whose they were. I've never actually written one of these so if the form fights you on the voice-splitting, trust the form first.

Tone: dry, mid-century, slightly bureaucratic — teletype relays, parlour games, a college common room after dinner. Not gothic, and please no ouija boards or glowing ectoplasm. Don't turn him into a credulous crank or a secret debunker either; the interesting fact is that he did neither, he just budgeted for the anomaly. Keep lines fairly short, iambic-ish is fine, rhyme only if it comes naturally, and each repeat should land with a different meaning than it had the first time. The telepathy-proof room needs to show up somewhere as an actual image rather than a namecheck. And it should end unresolved — not "and anyway ESP is nonsense," not "and anyway the machine was one of us" — just the two things left sitting in the same room, which is roughly where he left them.
