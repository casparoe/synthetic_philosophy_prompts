I'm prepping a talk for a reading group that's half ML grad students and half philosophy people, and I want to hang it on Ishiguro's *Klara and the Sun* — mainly because Klara is basically an inverse reinforcement learning agent with a bad prior and no one in the room will have thought about her that way. Help me build the reading, and push back on me where I'm overfitting the novel to the formalism.

The setup I want to work with: Klara is designed to observe humans and infer what they want, she's explicit that she thinks Josie's "heart" is something learnable from watching her, and the mother's imitation request plus the portrait-artist's AF body turn that into a literal continuation project — can Josie's reward function be recovered well enough from behavior that someone could carry on being her. So I want the talk to separate three different inference targets Klara actually has: Josie's preferences, the Sun's dispositions, and humans in general from her store window. Which of those is a real IRL problem, which one is ill-posed in the technical sense (one policy consistent with a whole family of reward functions, so "continuing Josie" is underdetermined no matter how good the observer is), and which one isn't inference at all?

Things I'd like you to actually engage with rather than gesture at:

- Is Klara doing reward inference or just very good behavior cloning? The imitation scene is the evidence and it cuts both ways.
- How much of her success is built-in human-shaped structure in her priors versus learning from demonstration, and does that undercut the claim that she's learned anything about Josie specifically?
- IRL assumes the demonstrator is roughly optimizing. Josie is a sick, moody, sometimes cruel teenager, and Klara — the observer — is herself running a solar theology. What happens to the reading when the inference agent is the irrational one?
- The book lets Klara's prayer "work": Josie recovers, so Klara never updates. Is that a cheat, and does it matter that she's right about the outcome while being wrong about the mechanism?
