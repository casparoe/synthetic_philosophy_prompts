I run a workshop for people who keep an institution's past alive — archivists, museum curators, the poor soul running a tech company's "heritage" program. What I want from you is a sorting instrument built out of Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life, not a summary of it.

Make it a decision tree where every branch node is a concrete decision that kind of person actually faces — keep the founder's garage as a shrine, take a flawed benefactor's name off the new wing, publish the internal history that indicts your own institution — and phrase each node so someone who's never read the essay can answer it without guessing which way the branch leans. The leaves have to include monumental, antiquarian and critical history, plus the unhistorical and the superahistorical, plus at least two of the diseased forms where a mode turns back on itself.

Then tell me which pair of leaves your tree can't actually seperate, and which combination of answers comes out empty — and for the empty one, say whether it's empty because it's incoherent or just because nobody would want to live there.

Calibration case, invented but real enough: a firm that maintains the founder's office untouched for group tours and at the same time paid a historian to document its union-busting in the 70s. Where does that land on your tree, and does your tree need a node it doesn't currently have to place it?
