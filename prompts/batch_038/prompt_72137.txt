My thesis is going to annoy all four groups in different ways, so here it is with the argument sketch, so you can aim the objections at the actual load-bearing joints.

Thesis: religion is a natural phenomenon whose engine is timing rather than belief. I've been calling it the cadence hypothesis.

1. Seperate two things that get run together. Credence is how strongly someone accepts supernatural claims. Cadence is how precisely and periodically a community repeats its practices: fixed-hour prayer, sabbath, fasting windows, pilgrimage seasons, annual liturgical cycles.
2. Cadence, not credence, is what predicts which communities survive demographic shocks — migration, plague, a generation of low fertility, a hostile state. Groups with rigid shared periodicity outlast groups whose members report equally intense belief but schedule nothing.
3. The mechanism is legibility. A skipped service is observable to the group; a quietly weakened private belief isn't. So the doctrinal surface of a religion works as a read-out that makes commitment mutually visible, and belief reports are largely downstream rationalizations of continued participation. I want to say the community phase-locks and the theology is the story it tells itself afterward.
