Right — bit of an unusual request. I spent thirty-five years watching birds and fish signal at each other (gull chicks pecking at the red spot on the parent's bill, sticklebacks flashing their bellies, that sort of thing), retired four years back, and have since fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole. I now run a fortnightly thinking group in our village hall: twelve of us, average age about seventy, one chap did some OU philosophy in the eighties, nobody's a specialist. Ninety minutes on a Thursday afternoon, then tea.

We've been reading the people who say a signal's aboutness is fixed by what it was selected for rather than by what it currently correlates with — Millikan, Papineau, that crowd — and it struck me this is just my old day job with the vocabulary changed. I'd rather the group hit that for themselves than have me lecture at them.

Could you design a game the twelve of us can actually run in ninety minutes? No screens please — half of us can't read small print and nobody wants an app — and biscuits for payoffs rather than money. What I need it to produce:

- a few of us playing signallers who get trained over several rounds, so the group watches a correlation get built and then quietly broken;
- a moment where they have to rule on whether a signaller got it wrong, and find they can't settle it without knowing the signaller's history;
- a moment where two signallers behave identically in the room and yet the group wants to say they aren't in the same state;
- nobody hears the jargon until afterwards.

The frogs snapping at little dark specks should be in there somewhere, and so should the chap who gets struck by lightning and reassembled.

Then: a debrief mapping each of those moments onto the real arguments; what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it with this crowd (two of them keep dogs and one trained gundogs professionally, so expect interference); and a straight answer on which feature of the theory the game cannot reproduce and I'll have to supply out of my own mouth. I've a suspicion its something about whoever does the training playing God, but tell me if I've got that wrong.
