I am drafting the second chapter of a thesis on the individuation of spacetime points, and my supervisor has insisted — reasonably, I think — that before I defend any positive account I be able to state what would count as solving the problem at all. So I would like the specification written out first, independently of the candidates. Suppose a theory T purports to tell us what makes a point of spacetime the very point it is: what fixes the identity and distinctness of the manifold elements over which metric and matter fields are defined, prior to or independently of the field structure itself. Give me the full list of desiderata such a T has to meet to be acceptable, with the case or consideration behind each one. I want the motivating material to be concrete — a construction, a class of models, a specific thought experiment — rather than a slogan, because that is the part I currently cannot write.

One stipulation on the target of adequacy, since adequacy is relative to purpose. I need T to be usable in two settings at once: classical general relativity as we actually have it, and a candidate quantum theory of gravity in which there is no differentiable manifold at the fundamental level — only a discrete order, a combinatorial structure, or an algebra of observables, with continuum geometry recovered approximately and only in a regime. So each desideratum should be stated in a manifold-agnostic form wherever that is possible, and you should tell me explicitly which ones cannot be so stated, i.e. which demands on an account of point-identity quietly presuppose the very apparatus the hole argument is supposed to put in question.

To calibrate the level, here is what I already have, badly. T must imply that the hole construction does not generate genuine physical indeterminism, or else explain why indeterminism of that kind would be tolerable here when it is intolerable elsewhere in physics. T must say whether haecceitistic differences between the original and the dragged model are real, and if not, what grounds that. T must not be circular in the obvious way: points cannot be individuated by the field values they carry if the fields are precisely what the dragging varies. T must be modally explicit — the whole argument trades on claims about the space of possibilities, so T has to say whether diffeomorphic models are two possibilities or one possibility twice represented, and on what the answer is supposed to supervene. T must handle spacetimes with no Cauchy surface, where the initial-value framing of the dilemma simply does not apply, and manifolds that are not Hausdorff, where two distinct points may fail to be separable by disjoint neighborhoods and the counting question ("one world or two?") becomes acute. T must still function in a large homogeneous region — de Sitter, or any vacuum solution with high symmetry — where no point is distinguishable from another by its intrinsic qualitative profile, which is exactly where an individuation-by-field-structure criterion either collapses points or must be supplemented. T must explain why the analogous construction does or does not go through for matter fields on a fixed background, since if it does, the dilemma is not really about general covariance at all. T must respect the active/passive distinction and say whether the dispute is about active diffeomorphisms at all. And T must connect to observables: given that in the Hamiltonian formulation the diffeomorphism-invariant quantities look frozen, T should say how, if at all, it leaves room for localized dynamical beables, since a criterion of point-identity that licenses no localized quantities makes it unclear what the points are points of.

For each item, tell me whether it is non-negotiable for anything that deserves the name of an account, or whether it is a demand made only by a particular camp — and if the latter, which camp and what they gain by making it. Then identify the pairs you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, with the reasoning that shows it; I most want to know whether "the fields evolve deterministically from data specified outside a region" and "manifold points have identities independent of the fields" can both be preserved by something short of surrendering one of them, or whether the appearance of a forced choice is an artifact of how the argument is set up in the first place. Then tell me which single desideratum does the most work in eliminating candidate accounts. Also — and this is worth as much to me as the list itself — go through my seeded items above and flag any that you think are bogus, or are two demands wearing one coat, or are smuggled statements of a substantive position rather than neutral criteria. I should also mention that my reading group spent last week on the point-coincidence response and half of us came away thinking it either trivial or question-begging, so I would rather the specification not lean on it. Finish with a short assessment — a paragraph each, no more — of how substantivalism, the Leibniz-equivalence line, and the more recent sophistication-style view that keeps haecceitistic differences but declares them physically surplus fare against the list. No bibliography and no reading list, please; I know where to look, and what I need is the reasoning.
