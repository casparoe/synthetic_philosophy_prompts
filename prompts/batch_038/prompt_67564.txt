I'm trying to work through cliodynamics, and I think I have the skeleton of a secular cycle: population grows until it presses against the productive capacity of the land, real wages fall, misery spreads, the tax base erodes, and eventually the polity destabilizes and the cycle resets. What I don't follow is why the formal models carry a separate variable for elite numbers rather than just commoner population and state finances. If the masses are being immiserated and state revenues are shrinking, doesn't that already supply the whole mechanism — pressure from below and insolvency from above? I've also seen it said that instability tends to peak well after the population peak, but I assume that is simply a lag in how slowly misery translates into revolt. What causal work is the elite variable doing that population and state revenue cannot do on their own?
