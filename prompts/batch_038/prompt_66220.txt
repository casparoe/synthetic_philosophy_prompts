I'm a first-year graduate student working on prioritarianism, and there's a recurring move in the literature I can't name.

Parfit's leveling-down objection is aimed at egalitarianism: an outcome can be in one way better, namely more equal, even though it is better for no one. Prioritarians reply that this cannot touch them, since they attach no value to relations between people's levels, only to how much absolute benefit accrues to people at lower levels, so their view never favors making anyone worse off. But once that reply is on the table, the objection doing the real work has changed shape. It is no longer whether the view can countenance leveling down; it is whether the concave weight function the view needs is anything but an arbitrary pick, since any concave function whatsoever buys the escape. The crux has migrated from a claim about distribution to a parameter nobody had to specify before the objection arrived, and both sides keep answering the earlier question. I see the same thing next door in population ethics: critical-level views buy their way out of the repugnant conclusion with a positive critical level, and what follows is an argument entirely about the value of that level, which is not what the repugnant conclusion was about.

Is there an established term for this? I know it sits near moving the goalposts, motte-and-bailey, the ad hoc auxiliary hypothesis, and something like a merely verbal dispute, but it doesn't look like any of them, and I'd want the differences spelled out rather than assumed. If nothing names it, propose a term, define it, and mark the boundary cases where it does and doesn't apply. More useful to me than the label, though: give me an account of what the phenomenon actually consists in, and tell me whether this kind of migration is a defect in the theory or an ordinary stage in a research program's life.
