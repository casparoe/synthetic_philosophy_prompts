My thesis: historians treat gaps in the documentary record as though they were random, but they almost never are, because the probability that an event was written down depends systematically on who was involved and, in the worst cases, on precisely those features of the event we most want to estimate. I sort gaps into four kinds — never recorded; recorded and later destroyed; recorded and surviving but never deposited where anyone could reach it; and recorded, deposited, and preserved but never described in a way that makes it findable — and I argue each kind carries different evidential weight, so that an inference from silence can be sound for one and vicious for another. I also claim that certain silences are themselves informative, since what a recorder left out tells us something about the recorder's interests.
