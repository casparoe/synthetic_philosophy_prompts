Consider the absent-minded driver. A motorist must negotiate two intersections that he cannot tell apart, and he cannot recall whether he has already passed one. Exiting at the first intersection pays $a$, exiting at the second pays $b$, and driving past both pays $c$; in the standard version $a=0$, $b=4$, $c=1$. Before setting out he may adopt a plan consisting in a probability $p$ of continuing at each intersection, the draws being independent of one another and of which intersection he is at. Such a plan yields expected payoff $(1-p)a + p(1-p)b + p^{2}c$, which in the standard version is $4p-3p^{2}$, maximized at $p=2/3$ with value $4/3$.

The familiar objection to treating that plan as the driver's rational course runs as follows. At an intersection the driver's informational situation is exactly the same whether he is at the first or at the second; he is the same agent, with the same evidence, facing the same choice; so whatever he decides at one he decides at the other. The acts genuinely open to him are therefore only the global ones — exit at both, which pays $a=0$, and continue at both, which pays $c=1$. Continuing is thus action-optimal, and the plan $p=2/3$ is not one the driver can rationally execute: the planning-stage verdict and the action-stage verdict come apart, and the action-stage verdict governs.

Now modify the case in a single respect. Before the drive, a friend hands the driver a device which, when activated at an intersection, displays CONTINUE with probability $2/3$ and EXIT with probability $1/3$, independently of everything else — including the signal it displayed at any earlier intersection, which intersection he is at, and anything he does. He observes the display. His pre-drive plan is the policy: activate the device at each intersection and do exactly what it displays. This policy reproduces the $p=2/3$ plan and its payoff of $4/3$. Note that the policy is itself a single determinate decision, made identically at both intersections; what differs between the intersections is the device's output, which is not the driver's decision at all.

Argue for the following thesis: the device defeats the objection, and does so on the objection's own terms. Specifically, argue that once the symmetry premise is applied to the enlarged option set, the three global acts rank as exit-at-both ($0$), continue-at-both ($1$), obey-the-device ($4/3$), so that the very reasoning which was supposed to show that the driver must continue with certainty in fact recommends obeying the device; and that the case is therefore not a counterexample to the primacy of ex ante planning but an exposure of a hidden premise in the objection — namely, that a forgetful agent's option set is exhausted by the deterministic acts he can guarantee to perform identically at each indistinguishable node.

Your argument should do all of the following.

1. Reconstruct the objection as an explicit argument with numbered premises and conclusions, and identify precisely which premise your reply targets. Be clear whether you deny that the driver decides identically at both intersections, or accept that he does and deny the inference from identical decisions to identical realized actions.

2. Present the positive case, showing that the objection's symmetry premise is what licenses the device rather than what rules it out.

3. Answer the relocation counter. Suppose the device has displayed EXIT. The driver still faces a choice whether to obey, and the situation at that node — being at an intersection, having just seen EXIT — is again informationally identical at both intersections, so the symmetry argument appears to apply once more, one level up: either he obeys EXIT displays at both intersections or he overrides them at both. Determine whether the objection genuinely re-emerges there and whether, if it does, it bites. Your answer should compute what obeying and what overriding are worth at that node.

4. Carry out the local computation explicitly. Derive the driver's conditional credence, given that he is at an intersection and given that his policy continues with probability $p$, that he is at the first rather than the second intersection. Then compute, for general $p$ and again for $p=2/3$, the conditional expected payoff of exiting now and of continuing now, each on the assumption that he obeys the device at any later intersection. Show that these two quantities coincide at $p=2/3$ and only there, and explain what that local indifference contributes to your case. In particular, address whether the action-stage objection requires a strict local incentive to deviate from the plan, or whether it is instead a claim about which acts exist for the driver at all — and say which of the two, if either, your reply actually defeats.

5. Generalize. For arbitrary payoffs $a$, $b$, $c$, state the condition under which an optimally calibrated device-obeying policy strictly outperforms both deterministic global acts, giving the optimal $p$ and the corresponding value in terms of $a$, $b$, and $c$. Note where the standard triple sits relative to that condition, and describe a triple for which the device-based reply would fail.

6. Take up two further challenges, conceding what you must. First: that handing the decision to an exogenous randomizer is an abdication of agency rather than a decision, so the driver has not made the planning-stage choice executable but has outsourced it, and a theory of rational choice should not be impressed by the availability of outsourcing. Second: that if the objection is sound it proves too much, since it would eliminate behavioral strategies for every agent with imperfect recall, a class of strategies used throughout the theory of extensive games; so the objection must be rejected on that independent ground, and the device is dialectically idle.

7. Close by stating the strongest objection to your position that you cannot answer, and saying exactly where your argument gives way.

Write between 1,200 and 1,600 words, in a formal register, with the core argument set out in numbered premises and conclusions and every computation shown. Refer to positions and arguments by description rather than by citation; include no bibliography.
