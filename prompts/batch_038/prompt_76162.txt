Here's the question. S = at some point within the next 200 years there exists an agency — state, corporate, treaty-body-with-teeth, or machine system — that (a) can veto or override any large-scale coordinated project anywhere on Earth (a nuclear exchange, a major geoengineering deployment, training frontier-scale AI outside its own systems) and (b) keeps that veto for at least 25 years despite at least one serious internal or external attempt to break it.

Notice this isn't "world government," isn't "world peace," and doesn't have to be benevolent or even intentional. There seem to be two very different pictures: the coercive singleton, where one power enforces compliance on reluctant others; and the attractor singleton, where everyone voluntarily runs the same thing because it's the only one that works — a standard, a protocol, a deployed system that becomes load-bearing infrastructure. Should those get separate numbers? And does the second one even involve an "agency" in the sense my definition needs? That genuinely worries me, because for an attractor singleton there may be nobody to ask, in which case clause (a) fails and I've defined the interesting case right out of existence.
