I sit on my school district's instructional materials review panel — seven of us, mostly parents, two teachers. A junior English class was assigned a canonical American novel that uses the n-word something like 200 times, and the teacher read several of those passages aloud in class. Two Black students in the class filed a complaint; a third Black student filed a statement against it, arguing the complaint is the more damaging thing of the two. Our policy just says materials must be "free of gratuitously offensive language," which nobody has ever bothered to define.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before Tuesdays meeting. It needs a bottom line — keep it, keep it with conditions, or pull it — plus a test the panel could actually reuse on next year's complaint. And I want it to take a position on something the complaint keeps sliding past: is the harm in the word itself, sitting on the page whether or not anyone voices it, or is it in a particular person saying it in a particular room? If it's the latter, what follows about who's allowed to read it aloud? Give the teacher's side its strongest form too — I suspect she's right and I still don't know how I'm voting.
