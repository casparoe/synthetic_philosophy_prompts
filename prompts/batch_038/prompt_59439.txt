I have spent most of my career compiling national accounts, and there is a feature of the numbers I publish that I cannot defend without sounding either naive or evasive. A substantial share of what we report as economic output was never bought or sold. Where a family lives in a house it owns, we estimate the rent that house would command and book it as income to the household and as production. For most of government — police, courts, schools, road maintenance — we book the wages, materials, and depreciation going in, because nobody purchases the service and there is no observable price to use. And for the cooking, cleaning, and childcare done unpaid inside households we book nothing at all, on the stated ground that services produced and consumed by the same household fall outside the production boundary, even though the goods such households produce fall inside it: a subsistence farmer's own harvest is counted, and so is a dwelling a household builds itself, while the meal cooked from that harvest and the cleaning of that dwelling are not. The boundary itself is fixed by international administrative agreement and defended in our manuals as a convention rather than a claim about value.

What theory of price, and of economic calculation, does this arrangement presuppose? Reconstruct it as explicitly as you can: on what principle does an imputed figure earn the status of a price rather than a guess, and why does the practice admit the owner-occupied dwelling while excluding the labour that maintains it, when both have market surrogates of the same kind? Then set it against the older argument — Mises and Hayek holding that without real exchanges of privately owned assets there are no genuine prices and therefore no rational calculation at all, Lange holding that a central board can announce parametric prices and revise them by shortage and surplus. Does our practice quietly concede Lange's point, since it does generate usable magnitudes for goods with no market, or does it confirm the other side, since those magnitudes track inputs rather than anything anyone forwent? Say what the practice strictly requires as opposed to what merely happens to fit it, mark the places where it is theoretically underdetermined and several rival accounts of value would license the very same numbers, and describe what an accounting system built on the opposite view would actually look like in operation.
