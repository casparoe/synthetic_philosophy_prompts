Case analysis wanted, not a book report on Popper. Take the collapse of the USSR, 1989–91, and run it through The Poverty of Historicism — the prophecy vs. prediction distinction, and the claim that a trend is a singular historical statement, not a universal law.

Be literal about the mapping: what was the supposed law, what were the initial conditions, what actually came out wrong. Then pick between two readings — either the theory was falsified in 1991, or it never applied, since the revolution landed in a backward peasant country instead of the advanced capitalism Marx was describing (which was Lenin's own worry from the start). Which of those is a real use of Popper's framework and which is just the ad hoc rescue he'd have called immunizing the theory? He needs a principled way to tell them apart or the critique is toothless.

Then the harder bit. Amalrik, the dissident historian, published an essay in 1970 asking whether the Soviet Union would survive until 1984. He predicted a breakup driven by ethnic and social antagonism between the republics and the center, with a war against China as the trigger. The breakup happened; the war didn't, and he was twenty years early. What does Popper's framework do with that? If someone reasoning from trends about a decaying regime gets it roughly right, has historicism scored exactly where Popper said it couldn't? Or does his reply — trends can switch off at any moment, one hit isn't a law, and Amalrik had the mechanism wrong — actually handle it, or does it leave Popper's own critique about as unfalsifiable as the thing he's attacking?

So: does 1991 help or hurt him? A lot of people treat it as his vindication and I suspect its more awkward than that, but tell me if I'm wrong. Please also separate what's settled from what's still live among people who actually work on Popper — don't hand me a contested reading of the prophecy/prediction distinction as if it were the textbook line.
