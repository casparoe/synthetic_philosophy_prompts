Retired biologist here, spending my retirement on decision theory and philosophy, mostly for fun. There's a pattern I keep running into that I don't have a word for, and I'd like to know whether the field does.

A friend of mine will happily take a bet he figures at 55-45 for a hundred bucks — nothing to him. But if the same kind of bet turns into a standing thing, every week, same setup, he starts passing on it, or taking half of it, or hedging. He's nowhere near going broke; the stakes are pocket change against his savings. And it isn't the usual diminishing-returns story either, since that would make him *more* comfortable the more times he plays at that size, not less. I've seen the same shape elsewhere: back in the lab, people were fine with a protocol that had one risky step in it, and didn't want it at all if the step had to be repeated a dozen times, even though a failure just cost an afternoon.

My guess is that what he really doesn't trust is his own 55%. If he's off by a little in a random way, playing more should wash it out; if he's off in the same direction every time, playing more turns a coin flip into a sure loss. But I could be wrong about the diagnosis, and either way I don't know if the thing itself is named.

So: what are the nearest established concepts, how does this differ from each of them — I'm thinking especially of the difference between what happens to one player over time and what happens on average across alot of players — and where are the edges? If nothing fits, make something up that does, define it, and give me a case that looks like it but isn't. I don't just want a label, I want to know how to carve it.
