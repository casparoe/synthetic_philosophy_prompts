I trained as a mathematician and came to the literature on personal identity late, by way of the teletransporter cases. I can follow the standard moves, but I keep running into a structural feature of these discussions that I cannot name, and I want to know whether it is a recognised thing with an established label or something that has gone largely unremarked. A label alone would not help me much: what I am after is the carving — what the feature is, what it is not, and where its edges lie — because I suspect several distinct things in this neighbourhood get run together, and I am not certain which one I am reacting to.

The first case that made me notice it. A scan-and-rebuild procedure produces one successor who carries on my memories, my projects and my character. Now suppose the procedure malfunctions and produces a second successor as well — a botched one, with my voice and a handful of my recollections but almost none of my dispositions and no grip on the life I was leading. Nothing has happened to me or to the first successor; intrinsically they are exactly as they were in the one-successor case. And yet their standing seems to have changed. Whether that successor is the one whose future matters to me specially — whether it is me at all — now turns on a comparison with the botched one, and nobody seems able to say how botched the botched one would have to be before it stopped mattering that it exists.

The second case is the same shape with the ranking reversed. Suppose the extra successor is not botched but better: sharper recall, a firmer hold on my commitments, more of my life carried forward. The first successor is unchanged in every intrinsic respect, and yet on some accounts it goes from being the thing I should care about as I care about myself to being a stranger whose future concerns me no more than any other person's. A third party turns up, nothing happens to me or to my original successor, and the standing of that successor's future is apparently destroyed.

The third case is what makes me think I have hold of something more specific than "these cases are hard." Suppose there are not two successors but a thousand, each carrying on my memories and character just as fully as the others. Is what matters to me then the same amount as in the one-successor case, merely divided a thousand ways; or a thousand times as much; or is it not the sort of thing that comes in amounts at all, so that it holds in full with each of the thousand without being spread across them? These options strike me as mutually exclusive, and authors seem to move between them without comment — and which one is presupposed does much of the work in the conclusions reached about whether producing a thousand successors is a catastrophe, a neutral rearrangement, or a windfall. As far as I can see this is a question about whether a certain quantity is additive, and the literature often argues as though additivity were a fact rather than a choice.

So: does this have a name? Specifically —

1. Is there an established term, or family of terms, for the phenomenon whereby what holds between me and one given future depends on which other futures there happen to be? If several candidates suggest themselves, set them out with the differences between them made explicit, say which fits what I have described best, and say which I have probably mistaken for it. I expect that some obvious suggestions are *responses* to this feature rather than names for it; tell me where that line falls.
2. Is the third case an instance of the same feature as the first two, or a different one? I have assumed they belong together and am not confident that is right. If they come apart, say what each of them is.
3. Does the same structure recur outside personal identity — in reasoning about counterfactuals, about distributive choices, about the semantics of "the same" — and is it recognised there under another name? Whether this is a local puzzle or a general one tells me how much weight to put on it.
4. If nothing in the literature names it precisely, coin something: a definition with its boundary conditions built in, plus what would count as a case of the phenomenon that is not, and a case that looks like it but is not.

Throughout, and this matters to me more than the rest, please mark clearly where the field is settled and where it is genuinely divided. I can tell this is an area with a great deal of confident prose in it, and I do not want one side of a live dispute handed to me as the common ground. Where you are reporting a majority view, say so, and say how confident you are that it is one; where you are advancing a reading of the cases that is yours rather than the literature's, flag it as such.
