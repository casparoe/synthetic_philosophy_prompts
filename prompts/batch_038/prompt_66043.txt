She's very polished, and she tends to absorb every challenge by repeating some version of "the diary isn't philosophy, it's bile."

Facts you can lean on (the audience already knows them, so don't waste a question establishing them): the diary Frege kept between 10 March and 9 April 1924 was left in his Nachlass and only came out decades after his death, and it's full of vicious antisemitism plus attacks on parliamentarism, socialism and the Catholic church. There's also a separate 1918 piece where he lays out a proposed electoral law for Germany, which is much closer to being a straightforward bit of political/legal philosophy. A good chunk of the political writings were lost precisely because his son and later editors didn't think them worth keeping — and in a letter of January 1925 handing his papers over to that son, Frege wrote: "Even if all is not gold, there is gold in them."
