I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and for the past four months I have been reporting a profile of an epistemologist who works on pragmatic encroachment and on contextualist accounts of knowledge attribution. She is unusually willing to give crisp verdicts on described cases, and I have kept a record of five of hers, all concerning one invented subject: Ms. Aldridge, who has run a university reading room for nine years and is certain, on the strength of that long acquaintance, that the room is open on Saturdays.

1. Asked whether Ms. Aldridge knows this when nothing at all turns on it, she said yes, plainly.
2. Asked about a version in which Ms. Aldridge has committed her department's entire operating budget to an accreditation visit scheduled for a Saturday and would be professionally ruined if the room were shut, she said no: in that version Ms. Aldridge does not know, and she ought to have verified the date.
3. Asked about a version in which Ms. Aldridge's own situation is unchanged but a visiting researcher has booked a nonrefundable flight in reliance on her word, without her knowledge, she said Ms. Aldridge still knows — then added, unprompted, that the researcher should not have booked on that basis alone.
4. In a seminar where someone noted, in Ms. Aldridge's absence and without her ever learning of it, that the Saturday hours had once been altered without notice and that the university's online calendar is unreliable, she agreed that Ms. Aldridge's evidence and practical situation were exactly as before, and said nonetheless that we can no longer say she knows.
5. Asked whether a mountaineer who has tied the same knot ten thousand times, whose life depends on it, and who has no way to check it again from the ledge he is standing on, knows the knot is secure, she said yes — and said that any view implying otherwise would be refuted by that very implication.

I now want to put a case from my own trade to her on the record, and I would rather predict her answer than be surprised by it. The case: a stringer who works for us sat in a courthouse for eleven hours and read the entered judgment herself. Nothing in her own life turns on whether we act on what she reports; she is paid by the piece and has no plans that depend on the story. My editor, however, is deciding whether to run it tonight, and during the morning meeting another desk raised the possibility that the docket entry is a clerk's placeholder rather than a final disposition, so that possibility is now explicitly on the table among the people deciding whether to say the stringer knows. I will ask her two things: whether the stringer knows, and whether the editor speaks truly in the meeting when she says that they do not know that, and equally truly a week later at a dinner party when she says the stringer knew the appeal had been dismissed.

Please answer in exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no closing remark.

1. Name the accounts of knowledge attribution that her five verdicts leave live: accounts on which the subject's practical stakes change whether she knows, accounts on which the truth of an attribution shifts with the attributor's conversational context, accounts on which knowledge is fixed by evidence alone while the propriety of asserting it or relying on it varies, accounts on which what someone knows depends on which decision the knowledge would license, and anything else her pattern permits.
2. For each surviving account, state what it says about my two questions, marking the places where the answers genuinely differ rather than differ only in how the same answer is phrased.
3. Say which of her five recorded verdicts strain or partly exclude each account, and which pair of verdicts is the most diagnostic about what she is actually running.
4. Give your best guess at her two answers, together with the consideration that tips the balance.
5. Propose the single further case I should put to her that would most sharply separate the remaining accounts, and say what each of those accounts would say about it.
