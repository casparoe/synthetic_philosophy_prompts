"Aumann's agreement theorem says that two rational Bayesians who start from a common prior can't end up disagreeing, so long as each one knows what the other believes. Which means a stubborn disagreement is a symptom: somebody is being irrational, or somebody is withholding. Milgrom and Stokey ran the same machinery through markets and got the no-trade theorem — if everyone is rational and prices have already soaked up all the available information, there's no reason to trade at all, because the fact that the person across from you wants to buy is itself news about what you're holding, and bad news. Since trillions of dollars change hands every day, that's a reductio of market efficiency. Grossman and Stiglitz are the ones who show why the reductio doesn't quite land, and their paradox is the more interesting result: if prices really did reflect all available information, there'd be no profit in digging any up, so nobody would dig, so they couldn't. Their fix is that prices have to be wrong by exactly enough to pay the people doing the digging — and the only way to arrange that is to let some traders cash in on what others don't know, which is to say, insider trading. So Hayek was right that markets are knowledge machines, and wrong to think they run benignly."
