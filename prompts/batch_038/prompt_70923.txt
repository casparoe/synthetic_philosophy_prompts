write me some corporate API documentation — deadpan, straight-faced, never winking — for a made-up commercial service that resolves definite descriptions, a la Russell. Company: Denoting Systems. Product: something like DESC-RESOLVE. Around 700 words, formatted like real docs (headings, a param list, a table, a changelog).

The thing I care about most: the docs have to be technically correct Russellian analysis all the way through. A description resolves only if exactly one object in the supplied domain satisfies it; failed descriptions come back as false rather than as a gap; scope interacts with negation and other operators in exactly the primary-versus-secondary-occurrence way; identity claims get the descriptive treatment rather than a bare two-name equation. The jokes should land because the philosophy is right, not because its being fudged for the gag.

Beats I want in there:

- a support ticket from an events company who plainly meant one specific guest at a party holding a glass of water, got back the only actual cocktail-drinker in the room, and were closed out as works-as-intended; there's also a deprecation notice for a referential mode, wording to the effect that the service can only honour the description, never the speaker's intention
- the uniqueness-failure error returns the list of satisfiers capped at 50 entries, plus a postmortem about a municipal dispatch client who got a silent wrong answer because of the cap
- a ticket in the identity section from a royal client who asked whether a certain novelist wrote a certain famous historical novel: the system returned true and informative, and the client still cannot work out how he could ever have been in doubt
- a do-not-resolve warning that sorts descriptions into three buckets for three different reasons — nothing satisfies it (safe, returns false, just don't infer anything exists), it's a statistical construction rather than a way of singling out a person, and its satisfaction is vague enough that uniqueness is a coin flip (resolves with a confidence score). The reasons should be right without naming any philosophers
- a changelog entry where an outside consultant talked them into returning truth-value gaps instead of false, and it got reverted the next release because negative existentials stopped working

Two constraints. Don't put anything in quotation marks that comes from an actual philosophical text — no invented quotes, no recalled quotes; paraphrase and get the content right. And it should still read fine for someone who has never encountered any of this material: the corporate voice carries the piece and the exact philosophy is the garnish. No real named people as targets, no reading list, no bibliography.
