ok so — this term I'm running a small grad seminar on Popper and biology, and to stop the students hiding behind the secondary literature I've decided to put my own position on the table in week one and let them shoot at it. Before I do that, I want it audited by someone who isn't in the room and doesn't owe me anything. You're a philosopher of biology: assume you know the demarcation criterion, the fitness-tautology business and the propensity-style replies to it, Popper's mid-seventies remark filing Darwinism under metaphysical research programmes rather than testable science, and his later recantation in the piece on natural selection and the emergence of mind. Think properly before answering — first jot down, briefly, which pairs of my commitments you'd want to test against each other and why, then give me the audit.

Here's what I hold.

(i) Demarcation, roughly Popper's: a theory is scientific to the degree that it rules out observable states of affairs — it prohibits something, and the more it prohibits the better.

(ii) The "survival of the fittest is a tautology" worry is a non-problem. Fitness can be pinned down independently of who actually reproduced — from organism design plus environment — so selection is a substantive hypothesis with content, not a truism dressed up as one.

(iii) That said, any empirical test of a selectionist claim is statistical and comes bundled with auxiliaries: population structure, heritability, drift, what the environment actually was. So a recalcitrant result can, as a matter of fact, always be laid at the door of the auxiliaries instead of the core.

(iv) I still think Popper was basically right to file Darwinism as a metaphysical research programme, and that his later change of heart was a capitulation to the mood of the sociobiology era rather than a response to an argument. Nobody should follow him there.

(v) At the same time I think Popper's evolutionary epistemology is among his best work — knowledge growth as variation and selective retention, conjecture and refutation being the same pattern Darwin described, run one level up. I use that framing all the time.

(vi) I'm an anti-justificationist all the way down. There are no positive grounds for a theory, only surviving criticism; "it has been tested a lot" adds not one shred of probability to it.

(vii) Case verdicts, because principles without verdicts are cheap. The peppered moth work is a genuine severe test of selectionism and I'd stake a fair amount on it. Whereas adaptationist accounts of, say, human mate jealousy are just-so stories, and I say so loudly every year.

(viii) Related: Popper overstated the anti-historicism point. Game-theoretic and cultural-evolution models do deliver testable predictions about social change, so it isn't true that prediction about historical processes is impossible in principle.

So — does this hang together? Go through it and tell me which commitments really pull against each other and which pairs only look like they do. I'd much rather you discriminate than flag everything; I have no use for an answer that concludes "well, there are tensions on both sides." Rank the genuine ones by severity, say for each what has to give, and say what it costs me to give it up — if the fix is just a distinction I haven't drawn, draw it, but tell me whether the distinction actually survives the case verdicts in (vii) rather than merely looking tidy. I'm deliberately not signalling which commitment I'd sacrifice, because I honestly don't know where the soft spot is, and I don't want you reading a preference into the list. Don't bother with references, I'll fill those in myself.

Two last things. If some part of this is fine exactly as it stands and I should stop worrying about it, say that plainly instead of manufacturing a problem. And close with the epistemics on your own answer: what would change your mind about the audit — which commitment, read differently, would flip your ranking, which of my moves would have to be right for your worst tension to dissolve, and where is your reasoning here most likely to break down? Be as definite as you can, including about that last part.
