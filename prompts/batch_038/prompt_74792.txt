Our reading group assembled an argument to show that the inefficacy objection to ethical consumption ends up destroying the consequentialism that motivated it. Each step seemed defensible in the room; the conclusion is not something I can accept, and I cannot locate the failure.

The case, as our group modelled it: a vertically integrated producer contracts its flocks a quarter in advance, so weekly slaughter volumes are settled before any shopper's decision could reach them. Aggregate demand fixes the volume only in steps of N birds, N being very large, and no single purchase can move a step.

(1) An act is wrong only if, had the agent omitted it while everyone else's conduct stayed exactly as it actually was, someone would have been better off. Call this the Difference Condition.

(2) For each shopper, that counterfactual yields an identical schedule: the same birds are hatched, raised, and killed. The abstention shifts no probabilities either, since the contracts were signed before the shopper entered the store.

(3) So no individual purchase makes any difference to any harm.

(4) What a set of acts does is the sum of what its members do; there is no group-level fact over and above the facts about the individual acts.

(5) So all the purchases together make no difference: the flock would have been raised and killed even had none of them bought any chicken.

(6) The obvious reply — that the flock would not have been raised had everyone abstained — concerns a different act, universal abstention, which no one in fact performs; the Difference Condition governs acts, not collective conversions nobody undertakes.

(7) The same structure recurs wherever production and harm are scheduled by aggregate plans: personal driving and atmospheric concentration, one ballot and an election outcome, one donation and a relief operation.

(8) So no individual act ever makes a difference to any collective harm, and the harms of industrial agriculture, of emissions, and of electoral politics are nobody's doing.

(9) Yet (1) is the only principle I have for saying why those things are wrong. Therefore (1) is false: anyone who takes inefficacy seriously must give up the Difference Condition.

I want to keep (1), which is the whole of my consequentialism, and (4), which looks like nothing more contentious than the claim that a group is its members. Tell me which step fails and why it looked all right; then price the exits, including the two I would reach for first — attacking the empirical assumption that supply really is stepped, and replacing harm with expected harm or with risk — and any repair I have not thought of.
