so i run the philosophy club at my high school and next month we're doing a session on that idea that you couldn't possibly have come from a different sperm and egg, like that where-you-came-from is locked in and can't be changed. half the club has read two paragraphs about it online and now everyone's using words like "essence" wrong, so instead of a talk i want a quiz: "which view about where you came from do you actually hold." it has to sort people by what they say about weird cases, not by which label they think sounds smart.

can you write it? here's what i need

first, before the quiz itself, sketch the underlying space in like five bullet points: what are the 2-3 axes the questions are secretly probing? i mean stuff like — does where you came from pin down who you are, could it pin down more than one person, is it the physical ingredients or the arrangement of them that's doing the work. build the space first and then put the positions into it, don't just start from a list of names.

then the quiz, 9 questions. each one is a concrete scenario with 2-3 answer options. zero jargon in the question text, no "possible worlds" or "transworld" anything, half my club would just freeze. the scoring key at the end can be as technical as it wants.

the key has to map answer patterns onto real positions, and i want these on there: the strict one where the exact gametes are required or it isn't you; a version that tolerates small changes but not big ones; a version where the genetic blueprint is what matters and not the matter it's written in; a version where what matters is the unbroken growing process rather than the starting ingredients; a version that puts your origin in the pregnancy/gestation instead of the gametes; and a version where where-you-came-from puts no constraint on who you are at all. if there's a real position i'm missing, add it. if you think one of mine is bogus, say so and why instead of quietly dropping it.

cases i definitely want used: identical twins (same sperm and egg, two different people, so i want a question that makes people notice that origin can't be the whole story on its own); the slow-swap one where a lab keeps a sperm alive and replaces its molecules one at a time until none of the original stuff is left, plus a paired version where only 1% got swapped; a frozen embryo that the clinic splits in half; and something like a person assembled atom by atom in a lab from brand new matter, same genome, grown in an artificial womb. you can add more.

after the key, tell me straight which pairs of positions this quiz CANNOT actually seperate, and don't paper over it. also some people will answer inconsistently across the nine questions, guaranteed, so give me a "if you landed here and here, the question that should decide it for you is #__" note for the two or three most likely unstable mixes.

last thing: a short section on the answer combinations that are logically possible but that nobody ever actually holds, one line each on why the spot is empty — incoherent, just really unappealing, or nobody's thought of it yet.

it's for 16-18 year olds, smart but basically zero background. make the cases vivid and a little gross/weird, that's the fun part, but dont dumb the distinctions down because the arguing afterwards is the whole point.
