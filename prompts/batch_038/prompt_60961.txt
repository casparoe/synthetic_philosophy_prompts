I run a little get-together thing, like six of us, nobody has a philosophy degree, we just drink beer and argue. Last month somebody asked "could you have come from a different sperm and egg" and it ran three hours, and honestly by the end I don't think anybody knew what they were disagreeing about. Two guys were yelling "obviously not, you'd be a completely different person," and one woman kept saying "sure you could, the universe doesn't care which egg," and I'm pretty sure they meant different things by "you" and different things by "origin" and never noticed. One guy watched a youtube video and keeps citing Kripke like it settles something.

So I want to build a decision tree that sorts us. Ten questions at most, each one a concrete situation we can actually picture in our heads — no question should be answerable by guessing which box it dumps you in, and no asking "do you think your origin is necessary," we won't know what that means.

Here are the situations I want in there, plus whatever you think I'm missing:

1. Same sperm, same egg, they just meet a minute later than they actually did. Same parents, same house, same whole life. Is that you?
2. Your mom's egg, but a different sperm, one that would have made a kid who looks nothing like you — and your parents raise that kid exactly the way they raised you.
3. Identical twins: one fertilized egg splits into two. Which of them is the person that came from that egg — both, one, or is the question broken?
4. The clone thing: pull the nucleus out of one of your body cells, drop it into somebody else's egg that's had its nucleus removed, and the kid that grows up has your exact DNA but wasn't made from your sperm and egg.
5. A chain my friend came up with: in world one the sperm is off by a single molecule, in world two it's off by two, keep going until it's a completely different sperm from a completely different man. If every tiny step still gives you, the last world gives you too, which seems insane. So where does it break, or does it not break at all?
6. Your exact sperm and egg, but a surrogate carries it and a different family raises the kid.

For every branch, tell me in plain words what claim is doing the splitting. Every endpoint gets a name — plain english is fine, and if philosophers already have a label for it toss that in too, but I don't need a bibliography or a reading list, we're not writing a paper. And if some combination of answers is technically available but nobody in my group, or maybe nobody anywhere, would actually give it, say the region's empty and why: is it incoherent, just unappealing, or has nobody gotten around to it?

Also flag any two endpoints your tree can't really tell apart instead of papering over it, and if you think one of my six cases is bogus, say so and cut it.

Don't end with a both-sides survey. Tell me which endpoint you think is actually right, which two or three of these cases are the ones doing all the real work, and where you think this whole argument is just people talking past each other.
