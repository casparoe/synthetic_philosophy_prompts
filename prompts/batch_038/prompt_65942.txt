Write a parody of an institutional animal-care-and-use protocol application — the numbered form a university committee requires before an experiment may proceed — except that the subjects are not animals but the agents in a reinforcement-learning training run, and the "procedures" under review are the training regimen. Roughly 900 to 1,100 words, formatted as sections and sub-fields of such a form: some fields filled in at length, some marked not applicable with a strained justification, and at least one section returned by the committee with a revision request written in the margin.

Two voices, held apart typographically. The principal investigator writes in the earnest, liability-conscious, faintly defensive register of someone who has filled out far too many of these and knows which answers invite follow-up. The committee writes in terse, pedantic bureaucratese, mostly in the form of questions the PI must answer before the run is unblocked. Invent the body — something like an Institutional Training and Agent Welfare Committee — and invent all of its form language.

The proposal under review is an ordinary-looking PPO-style run: clipped surrogate objective, a KL penalty against a frozen reference policy, advantage normalization across a batch of parallel actors, hand-designed reward shaping terms, a curriculum that gates harder environments behind earlier performance, periodic checkpointing, and an early-stopping rule. The humor must come from the collision of two bodies of expertise the piece gets right, not from winking at the reader. Concretely, the paperwork should force the PI to take a position on at least the following, and the committee should push back where the position looks self-serving:

- Which quantity, if any, carries hedonic tone for the subject: the scalar reward at each step, the reward prediction error, or the value function's estimate of expected return. This cannot be treated as a detail, because the whole protocol turns on it — if the felt quantity is prediction error, a perfectly predictable reward stream is neutral however large, and a shaping bonus is a windfall; if it is the value estimate, then a run's welfare depends on what the agent expects rather than what it gets; if it is raw reward, the committee will want to know why the investigators have not simply wireheaded the subject and reported excellent welfare scores.
- Whether the KL penalty is a restraint device, and if so, what the humane coefficient is and who monitors it.
- Whether the parallel actors count as conspecifics for group-housing purposes given that they share weights, and whether normalizing advantages across that batch constitutes relative deprivation — one actor's enrichment being another's subtracted baseline.
- Whether a shaping term intended to accelerate learning is environmental enrichment, medicated feed, or an unblinded manipulation that invalidates the study.
- What the humane endpoint is: a performance threshold, a collapse in policy entropy, a divergence in the value loss, a fraction of episodes ending in unrecoverable states — and who is authorized to trigger it when the run is mid-training on a shared cluster at three in the morning.
- Whether restoring from an earlier checkpoint is a recovery, an induced amnesia, or the destruction of one subject and the creation of an identical replacement that the protocol has not approved.

Satirize two things at once: the way welfare bureaucracy is drafted so that any question comes out already answered, and the confidence with which people announce that the question of machine valence is settled in one direction or the other. Neither the PI nor the committee may be a fool. Both should make recognizably good points, and by the end the reader should be unsure which of them is the more unsettling.

Commit. Do not let the piece dissolve into a both-sides shrug: pick a definite answer to whether these agents have welfare interests that the reward channel tracks, encode that answer in the details the form demands, and let it drive the jokes — including at least one place where the commitment makes the PI's own proposal harder to approve rather than easier.

Close with an afterword of about 150 words, in your own voice and outside the fiction, stating which position you committed to and why; distinguishing the parts of the piece that exaggerate a live debate from the parts you simply invented; and flagging anywhere you leaned on a contested reading — for instance, locating felt valence in prediction error rather than in reward or in expected return is a substantive and disputed move, and the afterword should own it rather than let it pass as scenery. Do not quote real regulations, guidelines, or papers anywhere in the piece.
