Retired from a materials lab about five years back and have fallen into philosophy, mostly the mind stuff. Two things have collided and I cant tell which one is the mistake.

I was in Philadelphia recently and stood in front of a museum case holding microscope slides that are slices of Einstein's brain — the pathologist who did the autopsy kept it, it got cut into a couple of hundred blocks, and ever since there's been a steady trickle of papers measuring glia-to-neuron ratios and the shape of the parietal lobes, hunting for where the genius actually was. People queue up to look at the slides. At the same time I'm reading that old Hofstadter and Dennett anthology The Mind's I, including the piece where Dennett gets his brain lifted out and put in a vat and then can't settle where he is. The book mostly runs on the idea that a mind is a pattern, a way of organising information, and that the particular stuff doing the organising is beside the point.

So here's what I want worked out. What has to be true of minds for the brain-in-a-case business to make sense at all? Reconstruct the theory it presupposes — is the tissue being treated as a container of the person, as an index (there's an unbroken physical causal chain back to the man), or just as a specimen, the way a meteorite is a specimen and nobody thinks a piece of one is haunted? Which rival views does the display quietly rule out? And do the people doing the measuring and the people doing the queuing presuppose the same thing, or is the practice running two incompatible jobs at once?

I keep thinking: if the pattern view is right, a complete copy of Einstein's papers carries vastly more of him than a few grams of fixed tissue, and nobody queues to touch it or publishes on its glia ratio. Tell me where that reasoning goes wrong, if it does — or whether the whole relic business is philosophically underdetermined and several theories of mind would license it equally. And then tell me what a museum, and the neuroscientists, would have to change if they took the pattern view seriously instead of just saying it out loud.
