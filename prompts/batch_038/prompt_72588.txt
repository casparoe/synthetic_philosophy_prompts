I have been running an agent-based simulation of a commons dilemma, and I want to ground the agents' reasoning in Hofstadter's notion of superrationality rather than in any cooperative or repeated-game machinery: the game is played exactly once, the agents cannot communicate, and none of them can observe or sanction the others afterwards. My understanding is that this symmetry of reasoning is precisely what superrationality exploits — a superrational agent treats its own choice as simultaneously settling the choice of all the others, so the cooperative outcome follows whenever the symmetry holds. Yet in my runs the agents defect in roughly half of the encounters, because they are initialised with different random seeds and receive different local readings of the state of the resource. I have assumed the fault lies in my encoding of the symmetry rather than in the account itself, since the account is supposed to guarantee cooperation given identical reasoners. Could you set out what a faithful implementation of the superrational procedure would have to consist of, so that I can repair the code?
