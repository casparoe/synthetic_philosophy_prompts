Been going in circles on the Löbian obstacle for like three years now, mostly by way of the Vingean reflection / self-modification stuff, and last night I finally wrote out what looks to me like a knockdown argument that the obstacle is way worse than the writeups admit. The problem is that the conclusion is so bad that I must have an error somewhere, and I can't find it, and neither could the two people I dumped it on in a discord.

(1) Reasoning is only of any use to a reasoner who trusts its own conclusions. If I grind out a proof that p and then treat p as still up in the air pending independent confirmation, the grinding bought me nothing -- I'm exactly where I started.

(2) Trusting your own reasoning, cashed out formally, means endorsing the schema: for any p, if I can prove p, then p. Call it TRUST. Anything weaker isn't trust, it's hope.

(3) Löb's theorem: if a system S proves "if I can prove p then p," then S proves p. So instantiate p as a contradiction: any S that proves TRUST proves the false sentence.

(4) Therefore every system that proves TRUST is inconsistent. And an inconsistent system proves everything, which is the opposite of trustworthy.

(5) So nothing can trust its own reasoning. Not PA, not PA plus its own consistency, not some reflective oracle rig, not me.

(6) Kicker, and the part that makes me think it proves too much: getting to (5) required me to reason about my own reasoning, and (1) says I can't rely on that reasoning unless I trust it, and (5) says I can't. So (5) is either false or unassertable-by-me.

(7) And checking any argument at all is reasoning about your reasoning -- you're evaluating whether your evaluation is any good -- so if (5) holds then no reasoning whatsoever can be rationally accepted, arithmetic included, this argument included.

(8) So either Löb's theorem is false or nothing is rationally acceptable. Löb's theorem isn't false. Nothing is rationally acceptable.

Where's the hole? I've stared at (3) and it looks right to me -- Löb is Löb, and the contradiction instance is just second incompleteness wearing a hat. I also don't want to take the Lucas-Penrose exit (humans aren't formal systems, blah blah) because I don't believe it and it wouldn't help the AI case anyway, so please don't route me there. If I have to bleed I'd rather cut (1) than admit that Löb simply doesn't apply to me when I'm checking my own work, but I have a suspicion that preference is motivated: (1) feels obviously true, and obvious-feeling is exactly the property that shouldn't get a free pass.

So: which step goes, why did it look fine standing up, and what does giving it up actually cost me -- concretely, what am I no longer able to do? I'd especially like to know whether the usual moves (bounded proof search, reflective oracles, trusting a weaker system instead of yourself, whatever else) count as rejecting a step or just as hiding it behind notation. And if you think (8) is correct and I should just accept the global skepticism, say that rather than humoring me.
