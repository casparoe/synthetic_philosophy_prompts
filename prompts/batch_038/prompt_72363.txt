I set out to write a short, irenic defence of content externalism — the point being that Twin Earth establishes only something modest about reference — and I appear to have ended up with an argument for the conclusion that there are no beliefs. I would like a diagnosis rather than reassurance.

Please write in British English, and organise the answer under these headings in this order: **Bottom line** (around 80 words); **Step-by-step audit** (around 700 words; for each numbered step, say whether it is valid, questionable or simply false, and say what made it look acceptable as I wrote it); **The load-bearing failure** (around 250 words); **Costs of the available repairs** (around 400 words); **What I should keep** (around 150 words).

Here is the argument.

1. Putnam's Twin Earth: Oscar and Twin Oscar are molecule-for-molecule duplicates who cannot tell their situations apart, even on reflection; yet Oscar's 'water' picks out H₂O and Twin Oscar's picks out XYZ.
2. So what settles the difference is not anything intrinsic to the two thinkers but their differing surroundings.
3. Therefore the content of a thought is not determined by any intrinsic property of the thinker.
4. Nothing in that mechanism is special to water, or to a physical rather than a social environment. My own variant: two duplicates are trained side by side to sort documents, one handling only genuine medieval manuscripts, the other only forgeries too good for any expert to detect; their intrinsic states are matched throughout, but what 'authentic' applies to differs between them. The same shape appears wherever a speaker's application of a term outstrips what he could articulate himself.
5. Generalising from 3 and 4: to be in a state with a given content is to stand in a relation to one's environment, not to be in an intrinsic condition.
6. Relations can lack a far end. On a planet where the relevant kind never existed — call it Dry Earth — the duplicate's tokenings of 'water' are about nothing at all; likewise for a term whose putative kind turned out not to exist, and for my forgery-trained duplicate if he is relocated somewhere no genuine manuscript was ever produced.
7. A state about nothing cannot explain behaviour. There is nothing for the behaviour to be directed at, and no principled way to distinguish it from a state about something else, or about nothing in a different manner.
8. A science of behaviour that explains action by appeal to what agents believe would therefore have to appeal to relations that may be empty, and that are in any case not fixed by anything the agent brings to the encounter — that is, to no states of the agent at all.
9. So either beliefs of the kind folk psychology posits do not exist, or they exist but can play no explanatory role. Externalism, pursued consistently, removes its own subject matter.

I cannot accept 9, and it is not what I took myself to be buying at 1. If I am forced to choose, I would rather keep 1–3 and the Twin Earth intuition than any later step, and I would rather keep the claim that beliefs explain behaviour than the claim that beliefs are respectable objects of a science. I also suspect that several steps are merely unappetising rather than false, and I do not want those treated as though they were the problem.

So: which step should I reject, if any? Why did each of the others look safe at the time — I want the appeal of the step, not only its defect? And for each live repair — positing a narrow component alongside the wide one; treating narrow content as indexical or as a species of conceptual role; denying that wide content is the explanatory kind of content; allowing that some tokens are about nothing while insisting they still explain; or simply accepting 9 — tell me what it costs, whether it is stable, and whether it preserves the two things I said I most want to keep.
