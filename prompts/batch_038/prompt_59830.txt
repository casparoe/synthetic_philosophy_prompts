I direct a memory-consolidation study in which volunteers are woken once or twice according to a randomised allocation, receive a drug between sessions that erases any memory of the earlier one, and are asked at each waking what probability they assign to being in the double-wake arm. A philosopher on the team insists that we cannot score anyone's answer as correct until we have decided whether a half or a third is right; our statistician considers the question empty and wants the responses recorded without comment. I would rather not take either side until I have the specification.

Write the desiderata an account of rational self-locating credence must meet in order to settle what counts as the correct answer under this protocol: the phenomena it must explain (the pull of the counting intuition; why a subject who cannot locate herself within the sequence still treats the allocation as fixed and fully known to her), the cases it must handle, the distinctions it may not collapse — above all between what she ought to believe, what she ought to accept as a wager, and how two answers from a single person should be pooled — and the formal constraints it must respect, including conditionalisation and whatever ties credence to known chance. For each item give the consideration behind it, and mark it either non-negotiable or a local preference of the halfer or thirder camp. If some subset cannot be satisfied jointly, say so plainly: I suspect that respecting the chance and conditionalising on the total evidence, centred propositions included, are precisely such a pair, and if the list turns out to be the result, that is what I most need.

One wrinkle the requirements must bear on: volunteers are paid per session, so reasoning that is identical throughout earns one payment in the double-wake arm and two in the other. Which single requirement excludes the most candidate accounts? Please write in British English, indicate how confident you are in your principal claims, and single out the point about which you are least sure.
