I am a doctoral candidate in economic history, and my program is hosting a workshop panel that puts two camps in the same room for the first time in years: the structural-demographic or "secular cycles" tradition in cliodynamics, and a group of quantitative comparative-historical sociologists who are not hostile to formalization at all but who think that tradition's models are under-identified. The panel will flounder unless the participants have something to react to, so I would like you to draft, in advance, the joint statement such an adversarial collaboration ought to have produced. No reference list, please — I want reasoning, not a bibliography — and I would rather you flag a specific figure or date you are unsure of than fill the gap with something invented.

Please characterize the two parties as follows, and do not soften either one into a position its own adherents would disown:

**Party A** is a structural-demographic modeler in the secular-cycles tradition descended from Goldstone's account of early modern revolutions and extended into the cliodynamics program and its coded global historical databanks. She holds that agrarian states obeyed a Malthusian engine — population growth outstripping carrying capacity, immiseration of commoners, overproduction of credentialed aspirants relative to the fixed number of power positions, formation of counter-elites, fiscal strain on the state, and then a phase of political instability and demographic contraction — that this engine generates multi-generational oscillations with roughly characteristic phase structure, that the oscillations are real dynamical phenomena rather than narrative impositions, and that the tradition earned genuine credibility by writing down a forecast of rising instability in the United States and western Europe in the 2010s before the events of that decade.

**Party B** is herself quantitative and perfectly willing to model long-run history. Her objection is not that history resists numbers. It is that the tradition's central variables are mutually constitutive and coded from overlapping source bases, so that the model's key construct is measured by indicators that are simultaneously posited as its consequences; that a system whose equations are jointly determined and whose parameters are tuned on the same case studies used to test it can reproduce a great deal of the observed record without identifying a unique causal ordering; that apparent periodicity can be manufactured by smoothing, by case selection toward societies with well-documented collapses, and by non-independence among neighboring polities that borrowed institutions from one another; and that the tradition's public forecasts were hedged in a way that makes it unclear what observation would have counted against them.

The document should contain the following, in this order:

1. **Agreed claims.** A numbered list of propositions both parties could sign without embarrassment: about what the historical record actually shows and what remains contested; about what the structural-demographic models do and do not assert (including whether they claim cyclicality or only recurrent phase sequences); about the epistemic status of the 2010s forecast; about the reliability and the known defects of large coded historical databases; and about which rival explanations of instability both sides regard as live rather than dead. For each claim, add one line noting what a hardliner on each side would object to about the wording — this is how I will check whether the claim is genuinely joint or only looks joint.

2. **Disagreements table.** Columns: the disputed proposition; Party A's formulation in words she would accept as her own; Party B's formulation in words she would accept as her own; the specific crux that separates them; the operationalized test or argument both accept as bearing on that crux; Party A's prediction recorded in advance; Party B's prediction recorded in advance; and what each side has agreed to treat as disconfirming for itself.

3. **Cruxes.** Identify the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one party — not merely restate the disagreement. I expect candidates to include whether elite overproduction can be measured independently of the phenomena it is invoked to explain; whether the phase structure survives out-of-sample case selection and explicit correction for spatial and temporal non-independence among polities; whether the tradition has a stated stopping rule; and whether the Malthusian engine can be switched off by demographic transition, financialized elite recruitment, and endogenous growth, or whether it merely runs slower. If you think some of these are not real cruxes, or that a crux I have not listed dominates the others, say so and argue it.

4. **Verbal disputes.** State which portions of this disagreement are about words rather than about the world. I suspect at least three terms are doing concealed work — what counts as a prediction when the claim is a shift in hazard rather than a point forecast; what counts as a mechanism when the mechanism is specified as a system of differential equations rather than as an actor-level story; and what counts as a cycle when the period varies by a factor of two across cases. For each, say what would remain in dispute once the terminology is fixed, and whether fixing it favors one side.

5. **Preregistration.** Take the single crux you judge most decisive and write it as a genuine preregistration both parties would sign: the dependent variable and how instability events are counted, the source classes to be coded and the rule for excluding sources that presuppose the theory, the coding of the key independent construct from evidence that does not itself consist of the construct's expected effects, the model specification and the lag structure, the case set and how it was drawn, the threshold for a successful versus a failed test, and the time window. Note explicitly where this preregistration cannot be made symmetric because the data exist on one side of the question and not the other.

6. **Self-critical coda.** Tell me what would change your mind about the document you have just written: where you think you have most likely mis-stated one of the two positions, which crux you have probably resolved too easily and in whose favor, and what consideration would lead you to a different verdict on the question you take to be central — whether the dispute between these camps is substantive or largely an artifact of how each uses the words prediction, mechanism, and cycle. If you conclude that no jointly signable statement is achievable on some portion of the disagreement, say which portion and why, rather than papering over it with a claim that is true only because it says nothing.
