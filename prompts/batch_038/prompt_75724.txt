I'm a contract theorist — principal-agent models, incomplete contracting, hold-up, renegotation-proofness, residual control rights. Fluent enough that I don't need any of those defined, but I have basically no philosophy training. Here's what I want translated into that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can.

The setup: someone at 55, fully competent, writes a directive saying that if she ends up with severe dementia she doesn't want to be kept alive by assisted feeding. At 78 she has advanced dementia, seems content, eats happily when food is offered, and her family plus some clinicians want to feed her. One camp of philosophers says the 55-year-old's document settles the matter; the other says the 78-year-old has interests of her own that a document can't silence.

Restate that fight in my terms: the directive as a commitment device or ex ante contract, her present contentment as revealed preference, the disagreement as a question about who gets to reopen an incomplete contract and who holds residual control. Then give me the residue report — what translates cleanly, what you had to bend my framework to fit, and what the philosophers are saying that contract theory just has no way to express. Two things I specifically want pressed on: (a) does "the person who signed is no longer the person here" have any counterpart in my tools, or does my vocabulary quietly assume a single continuous principal? and (b) does your translation smuggle in preference stability, which is exactly what this case denies?

One wrinkle, which I think I made up rather than lifted from the literature: suppose the signer adds a clause stating that if her future self appears happy, that happiness is to be read as evidence she mispredicted her own adaptation, not as grounds to override the directive. Can contract theory price that clause — self-referential evidence about the signer's own forecasting error — or does it fall outside the framework entirely?

Be honest about failures of fit. If a chunk of the debate turns out to be relabeled economics I already know, say so; but don't tidy up the mapping to make it look better than it is.
