You are to work in the capacity of a philosopher of physics with substantial experience in the foundations of statistical mechanics and the thermodynamics of information, and with long experience teaching this material to graduate students. Please think carefully, and treat what follows as a design problem rather than a retrieval problem: the value of your answer lies in the structure you build, not in the survey you can recite.

**Context.** I teach a graduate seminar whose students have one term of classical statistical mechanics and one term of information theory, but no exposure to the secondary literature on Maxwell's demon. I am preparing a single-page handout for one session, and the session's purpose is diagnostic: I want each student to work out, from their own answers to well-posed questions, which resolution of the demon they are implicitly committed to — and to notice when two students who believe they disagree are in fact answering every question the same way.

Our department's habitual presentation collapses this debate into a two-way choice between "the compensating cost is paid when the demon's memory is erased" and "there is no cost anywhere; the second law is only a high-probability generalization." I take that to be badly under-described, and I want the handout to break the logical space open considerably more finely.

**Deliverable.** Design a decision tree that begins from a fixed starting commitment — the student accepts that a Szilard-style one-molecule engine can be constructed and cycled, and accepts that the second law holds in some form — and terminates in a named position on how the demon is to be exorcised. Requirements:

1. Between six and nine branch nodes. Each node must be a question the student can answer from physics and philosophy alone, without knowing which position any given answer leads to. So no node may be phrased as "do you accept Landauer's principle?", "are you a Bayesian about entropy?", or "do you side with the exorcists or the deflationists?" Nodes should instead ask substantive questions about: where in the cycle the compensating cost falls (acquisition, storage, use, disposal, or nowhere); what quantity is doing the accounting (thermodynamic entropy of the gas, Gibbs or Shannon entropy of a distribution over microstates, mutual information between record and system, or something else); where the boundary of the system of interest is drawn, and whether the record counts as inside or outside it; whether the resolution requires a special low-entropy condition on the early universe; whether it generalizes to every conceivable demon or only to the cases actually examined; and whether it survives the demon's replacement by a device with no representational states whatever — a ratchet and pawl, a semipermeable membrane, a mechanical governor.

2. At least one node must test whether the resolution depends on the demon *using* its record, as against a control device that acquires and stores the same information but is oblivious to it.

3. Between ten and fourteen leaves. Name each leaf with the label its defenders use, if it has one; otherwise mark it "unnamed" and add one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

4. The tree must be neutral. No branch may be worded so that one answer sounds obviously correct to a student who has already formed a view, and the ordering of nodes must not privilege any path.

5. After the tree, provide three short analyses: (a) two or three combinations of branch answers that your tree makes available but that no position occupies, each with a diagnosis of whether the gap exists because the combination is incoherent, because it is theoretically unattractive, or because it has simply not been tried; (b) every pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate — views that answer every node identically yet are supposed to be rivals — with your judgment on whether the residual difference is substantive or a matter of emphasis and vocabulary; (c) which single node is doing the most work, in the sense that settling it would collapse the largest number of leaves.

**Process.** Before drafting the tree, spend a few paragraphs brainstorming candidate branch variables, ranking them by discriminative power (roughly, how many leaves each one separates), and explicitly discarding those you judge to track merely verbal differences. I want to see that triage; it is part of what I am assessing.

**Constraints.** No references, no bibliography, no quoted passages, and no attribution of views to particular authors beyond the shortest possible naming of a position that already has a name. If you are unsure whether a named position occupies a given leaf, describe the view and mark the leaf unnamed rather than guessing. Deliver the handout in clearly delimited tree notation with polished prose commentary, sized to fit on one page.
