I run a small ethics podcast and next month's guest defends the view that all-things-considered betterness isn't transitive. His signature move is that betterness is pair-relative: which factors matter, and how heavily they get weighted, depends on which two things you're comparing — so there's no single relation there to be transitive or intransitive in the first place. I don't want questions that just let him restate that. Give me eight or so, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give.

Must-haves: one on the money-pump objection (he says pumps assume a one-shot choice where all the options are on the table at once — press him on whether a sequence of individually fine choices can leave you worse of, and if so why that isn't a reductio rather than a technicality); one on whether pair-relativity is anything more than saying there are lots of betterness relations indexed to pairs, in which case what's left of the original claim; and one asking whether his view is really a vagueness view in disguise — if 'better than' is vague, the spectrum argument equivocates and he never needed intransitivity.

Then one where he has to apply it to something boring, like whether one slightly better cup of coffee beats two mediocre ones, and a closer on what evidence or argument would actually make him give the view up.
