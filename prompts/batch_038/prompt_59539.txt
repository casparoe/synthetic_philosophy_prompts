so im a junior and i got way too deep into Malthus for a history paper and now i have a question that's not really about Malthus anymore, it's about a pattern i keep seeing and dont have a word for.

the setup: Malthus says population grows geometrically, food grows arithmetically, so you get famine and misery as a permanent feature. that obviously didn't play out in England — yields went up like crazy, and he kept rewriting the book, six editions over ~30 years, each one soaking up whatever the critics threw at it (and i think later on he leans way harder on people just choosing to marry late). but basically everyone, even people who think he was wrong, says some version of "he was right about the mechanism, he was just early."

and then i noticed the exact same move all over the place. peak oil. the population bomb — Ehrlich literally lost a public bet about metal prices over ten years and then said he'd happily make the same bet over a longer stretch. robots taking all the jobs, AI timelines, whatever. the prediction has a rough date attached, the date passes, nothing happens, but the causal story is still sort of running underneath, so the theory gets scored as a near-miss instead of a miss.

what i want a name for isnt just "the prediction failed." it's the scoring rule, and i think it has like three parts:

1. mechanism and timing get graded separately, and only the mechanism grade counts. if the causal story still looks true, the blown date is written off. but flip it — something happens right on schedule for reasons that had nothing to do with the theory — and everyone calls that a failure. so we say prediction is the whole point of the theory and then we grade the theory on something else.
2. the thing that saves it is always an offsetting factor that only gets identified *after* the failure. technology, trade, institutions, substitution, prices. there's always a candidate sitting around, and the theory never said in advance how big that factor would have to be to count as a refutation. so it feels like the theory literally cant lose on timing.
3. being right about the past gets cashed in as credit for the future. Malthus was pretty correct about basically all of human history up to his own lifetime, and that retro-credit is what makes "right about then, wrong about now" come out as "right, just early" instead of "wrong."

but heres the thing that makes me think its not just one obvious bad-habit name: sometimes the identical-looking verdict is completely legit. guy says continents move, everyone asks what force could possibly do that, he has no good answer, gets destroyed, then fifty years later the mechanism turns up and he's a hero. nobody calls that an excuse. so there's a real version and a fake version and i want to know where the line is, not just which bucket Malthus goes in.

things ive heard that feel adjacent but not it: ad hoc, moving the goalposts, unfalsifiable, self-sealing, and "right for the wrong reasons" / a stopped clock being right twice. i know Popper and the whole auxiliary-assumption debate is in this neighborhood but that seems to be about one-off patches, whereas this is a standing policy about how to score dated predictions.

so: what are the closest established concepts, and for each one exactly which piece of my description does it miss? if nothing fits all three parts, tell me that plainly and then coin something — with a definition and boundary conditions, and at least one case that *looks* like it but isn't (Wegener is my guess but i could be wrong). i dont want just a label, i want the carving. and be honest about your confidence: if youre not sure whether one of these terms is actually used the way im assuming, say that instead of sounding certain. also if you think my premise is confused — like if the three parts are really just one thing dressed up, or if part 1 is actually backwards — say so straight up rather than playing along with me.
