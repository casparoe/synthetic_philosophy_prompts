I run a reading group that mixes moral philosophers with people working on machine welfare, and I want a decision tree that sorts its members by what they actually believe about utility monsters rather than by the label they announce. Please build it from cases rather than from the names of views, using these four: Nozick's original being, which gains enormously more from each unit of resource than anyone else does; the mirror-image monster that troubles maximin, a chronically worst-off being whose gains stay trivial however much it is given; a being whose welfare rises with the suffering of others; and an artificial agent with an unbounded utility function and no evidence whatever of an inner life. Every branch node should be a question a member could answer without knowing which way it leads — whether welfare is bounded per person, whether interpersonal comparisons can be made at the resolution these cases require, whether what matters is the total or the distribution, whether Parfit is right that a being a million times better off than a flourishing human is simply inconceivable, whether an unbounded utility function can confer claims independently of welfare. Aim for roughly ten nodes and six to eight leaves. Each leaf should carry the name of the position (or "unnamed" if nobody defends it), the subset of the four cases it would feed, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Close by flagging any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and by listing the combinations of answers that come out empty, saying for each whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Please write your answer in British English.
