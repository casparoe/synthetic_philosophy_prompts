I'm helping run an adversarial collaboration between two people who've been arguing about socialist calculation for years — one a Mises/Hayek-style impossibilist, one a Lange/Lerner-style market socialist who thinks a planning board setting prices by trial and error can do the job. What I need is the joint statement they should have produced ages ago, not another round of talking past each other. Assume both are smart and in good faith: nothing in here should be a strawman, and neither should be asked to sign anything they'd disown the next day.

Please build it in three parts.

**1. Numbered claims both can sign.** Make these substantive, not "both sides care about human welfare." I already think they can agree that the calculation problem proper is an information problem and not the incentive problem, that both work inside marginalist price theory, and that the debate is about capital goods rather than consumer goods. Push past that. In particular I want the agreed section to say something about two real-world anchors: the large firm that allocates scarce inputs internally with no market at all, and the fact that a partly socialized economy can still lean on prices generated by the market economy around it.

**2. A table of the live disagreements**, each row worded so that the side holding it would accept the wording of its own cell. I'm pretty confident at least one of the disputes is substantially verbal — something turning on what counts as a price, or on what "impossible" is quantifying over (no information-processing system whatsoever vs. no system lacking genuine ownership and rivalry). If so, say which part is just words, why, and what's actually left once you strip that out.

**3. The cruxes** — sub-questions where an answer would genuinely move at least one of them. For each: a concrete case both agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down *before* the result, so it's checkable.

Here's the material I want the cruxes built on, because the textbook versions are stale:

- a firm with a few hundred thousand people allocating GPU hours across thousands of internal projects — partly by committee, partly by chargeback accounting prices, partly by an internal bidding scheme. Which of those numbers are prices, and does the firm case generalize, given that the firm can buy and sell outside itself?
- a genuinely novel capital good with no market, no price history, and no settled production possibilities. Is there a determinate shadow price a competent planner could in principle compute from engineering coefficients plus preferences, or does that number only come into being through actual rivalry between owners? This is the one I most want sharpened.
- the dial question: if partial socialism can piggyback on surrounding market prices, is there some fraction of the economy past which the borrowed signals stop carrying information? Both sides should say what they expect to see as the dial gets turned up.

Then close with what each of them would count as evidence against there own view — and be straight if the honest answer for one of them is "essentially nothing."

No verdict on who's right, and please don't write it as a dialogue. Just the document.
