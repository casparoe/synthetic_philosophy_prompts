Our nonprofit is deciding whether to adopt software that drafts and sends replies to people who contact our helpline and books their follow-up appointments on its own. When I asked what happens if it tells someone something harmful, a board member waved the question off: it's a copilot, not an autopilot. Everyone nodded and we moved on. I've been turning that phrase over ever since and I'm no longer sure it says anything at all, given that the messages would still go out with nobody reading them first.

Could you lay out, as a list, the different precise claims a person might be making with that one sentence? I'd like the axes made explicit — who initiates each action, who can stop it, who is attending to it while it runs, who answers for it afterwards — and for each reading, whether it is empty reassurance or a substantive claim, whether it could plausibly be true of a system like ours, and whether it would actually license the board's conclusion. Finally, tell me whether the flying metaphor helps or misleads here: a real autopilot does fly the aircraft for most of the journey, with pilots supervising, and my understanding is that supervising something reliably is harder than it sounds.
