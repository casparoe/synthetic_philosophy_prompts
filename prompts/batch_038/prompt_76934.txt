I spent forty years at the bench in experimental physics and have come to philosophy late, so I hope you will forgive a plain formulation. Consider alethic deflationism: the thesis that truth is no substantive property at all, but only a device for disquotation and for stating generalizations we could not otherwise express. My credence in it is about 0.6, and I should like yours as a number, together with the decomposition behind it. Please break the question into at least these terms and assign each a rough figure: the probability that the dispute is substantive rather than a terminological stalemate with no fact of the matter; the probability that a deflationary truth predicate suffices for the generalizations we actually need in mathematics and in quantifying over indefinitely many claims; the probability that truth must be a substantive property if it is to explain why a mature theory keeps making novel predictions that come out right, as the realist urges; and the probability of the converse suspicion I have picked up, namely that deflationism far from damaging realism in fact serves it, by refusing to tie truth to our capacity to detect it. Show me how those combine into your total, identify the term to which that total is most sensitive, and say what kind of argument or result would move that term by the largest margin. If you think my framing is confused -- if, say, the thesis I have stated is not apt for a probability, or I have conflated a claim about truth with a claim about our warrant for asserting it -- tell me so directly and repair the question instead of answering it politely.
