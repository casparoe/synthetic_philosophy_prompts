Just walked home from a three hour argument with a friend of mine who does philosophy of mind and I'm still worked up, so bear with the dump.

Her whole case for teleosemantics rests on one analogy and she leans on it constantly: the heart's function is to pump blood because pumping blood is what got hearts selected, and a failing heart has that function just as much as a healthy one. Swap the frog's tongue-flick in for the heart and you have content. When the frog snaps at a BB pellet dragged along on a string -- which it does, the old frog-vision work -- the response misrepresents in exactly the way a bad heart malfunctions. She says that's the whole trick: no mysterious norms, you get falsity for free, and the disjunction problem stops being a problem because you stop looking at correlations and look at selection history instead.

Where I think I landed a hit: I asked about a smoke detector, since on her story an artifact gets a function too, derived from its designers, and she said yeah sure, a smoke detector going off for burnt toast has a false belief, why not. She didn't seem to notice that I took that as a concession rather than a bullet she was biting.

So audit the analogy for me. Give me the correspondence explicitly -- a table is fine -- what plays the heart, the blood, the fitness payoff, the lineage, malfunction, and the normal conditions everyone waves at. Then separate the disanalogies that actually threaten the argument from the ones that are cosmetic. I especially want to know what the heart story quietly imports that beliefs lack: an engineer holding a specification, one stable selection pressure, a clean producer/consumer split (does the frog's content come from what selected the detectors or from what the gut needs them to be about?), mechanisms that were learned or culturally passed along and never had a lineage at all, swampman. And was the smoke detector a real problem or was I just enjoying myself? Finish by telling me whether misrepresentation-for-free survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and if the heart turns out to be the wrong vehicle, what analogy would carry that weight instead.
