ok so my stepdad read something about memes recently — not the picture ones, the Dawkins kind — and at dinner Friday he was explaining to my cousin Dev (he's a sophmore in college, philosophy-ish) that culture evolves by natural selection exactly like living things do: ideas vary, some get copied way more than others, and over long stretches you wind up with religions and languages and manners that look designed but nobody designed. Dev said that's not evolution, that's a comparison doing an enormous amount of unpaid work.

Dev's first move: real natural selection needs copying that's actually faithful, and when you hear a story and retell it you don't copy anything, you rebuild it out of your own head, so nothing persists. A joke after a hundred retellings isn't one thing being transmitted, it's a hundred separate reconstructions that happen to land in a similar place. My stepdad said so what, that's how genes work too — what persists isn't the molecule, it's the pattern, and mutation means genetic copying isn't faithful either, so if sloppy copying disqualifies memes it disqualifies genes. Dev said there's a difference between imperfect and guided: mutations are random with respect to what the organism needs, but people retell a story so it's funnier, or scarier, or more flattering to their side, so the variation is aimed, and aimed variation is a totally different process, that's basically design. My stepdad said fine, call it design, except there's no designer — the person retelling isn't optimizing anything, they just tell it the way it stuck, and it stuck because it was catchy, so there's still a filter, and a filter plus variation is all Darwin ever needed.

Then Dev asked him to name one meme and say exactly what it consists of. My stepdad said Happy Birthday to You. Dev said great — a bunch of words, sung to several different tunes, with endless variations, shortened, translated, put on cards, hummed — so which part is the meme and where does it stop? He said in biology there's a clean line between what gets passed on and what's just carrying it around, and culture has nothing like that, the transmitter and the transmitted are the same soup, so you can't even count the units, and if you can't say what the units are you can't say what's being selected. My stepdad said languages change in patterns you can study, words shift their meaning on something close to a schedule. Dev said change isn't selection. They went in circles for another twenty minutes and I honestly couldn't tell which one was talking nonsense, if either.

Is the Happy Birthday thing a real objection or just a technicality about counting?
