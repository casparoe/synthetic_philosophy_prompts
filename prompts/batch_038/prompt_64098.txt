The idea: Parfit's hitchhiker gets used in two ways and I dont think anyone has clocked that the two uses pull against each other. In the decision theory literature it's the case where CDT and EDT die in the desert and the smarter theories pay up in town — the moral being that handing over the money just is the rational act. But Parfit's own point was the other thing, rational irrationality: it's rational to make yourself the sort of person who then performs the locally irrational act. Those two readings want different things out of the word "rational." The first needs the paying itself to come out rational all-in. The second needs the paying to be irrational when you assess the act on its own, but rational to have arranged, and if the act is really irrational once you're at the ATM then the decision-theory version of the case loses its punch.

So the question I want the project to answer: is there one notion of rationality that can cover both the cultivating and the paying, or does the case force a split — and if it forces a split, which way does that cut for the whole TDT/FDT programme?

Give me: the question stated tightly enough that it could come out either way; why the usual resources (resolute choice, global or dispositional evaluation, treating it as a cousin of the toxin puzzle, the schmagent-style reply) leave it open rather than settle it; a stage-by-stage plan of what I'd actually be doing each month; what a result would look like; and what would count as failure, including the worry that I'm manufacturing a distinction rather than finding one.
