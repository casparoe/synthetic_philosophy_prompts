Argued with a friend for like two hours last night about the separateness-of-persons objection and he texted me his whole position afterward, so now I cant argue with it, I can only get it audited. Roughly what he said:

"The objection goes: utilitarianism treats society as one big person. A prudent individual trades a pain now against a pleasure later because the same consciousness ends up holding the net balance — the sacrifice gets compensated. There's no social consciousness holding any balance, so when you add my loss to your gain nobody is ever compensated, and the utilitarian is just describing a bookkeeping fiction. That's also why the sums are meaningless: there's no fact of the matter about whether my pleasure outweighs your pain, so adding them up isn't even a real operation. And it's unanswerable — a utilitarian either denies there are seperate people (nobody does) or concedes that aggregation is unjust, which is the same as giving up. Which is why it doesn't just kill utilitarianism, it kills anything that trades off: QALY thresholds, carbon budgets, eminent domain, taxation. Unless you go the veil-of-ignorance route, where you really are one person gambling on which life you'll get, so aggregation is fine after all — that's Harsanyi's move, and Rawls basically accepted it but got different distributive conclusions because of how he set up the gamble. Also the whole thing is probably undercut anyway by Parfit: if personal identity isn't what matters, the boundaries between persons aren't deep, and then 'nobody receives the compensation' stops having any force."
