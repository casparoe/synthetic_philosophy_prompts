I keep a running list of referendum verdicts and I cant make them cohere. Facts I'm working from: the 2009 Swiss popular initiative banning new minarets passed with 57.5% of votes cast, turnout about 54%, plus a majority of cantons — I think it shouldn't have been on the ballot at all, and yet the fact that it passed and was immediately implemented doesn't strike me as a scandal. Brexit, June 2016, 51.9 to 48.1, legally advisory, turnout 72% — I think it should have been honored, and also that a second vote on the actual negotiated deal would have been perfectly legitimate, and those two sit badly together without my being able to say why. Prop 13 in California, 1978, passed by initiative nearly two to one: awful policy, entirely legitimate vote. My town's 60/40 vote to block a housing project last spring, turnout 22%: shouldn't count at all. But the Swiss minaret vote had barely higher turnout and I don't want to say turnout is what disqualifies that one, something else does. Italian abrogative referendums only count if a majority of all eligible voters actually casts a ballot, so the no side can win by staying home, and it has won that way many times over: I think that's legitimate, which flatly contradicts my verdict on the town. A 51/49 vote here to rename a street: totally fine, nobody asks about turnout or margin. A referendum raising taxes on the top few percent, where the people paying are a minority: fine. A referendum banning a visible symbol of one religion: not fine, whatever the margin or the turnout.

My tentative rule is reversibility — a majority decision binds if the losers can undo it later by the same means, and doesn't if it forecloses their options. But Brexit was reversible in principle since Parliament could repeal, and I still feel that vote was unusually weighty, and a constitutional amendment banning minarets can itself be reversed by another initiative, so the rule seems to say nothing where I need it to. Is there a principle here, or a small set of them each earning its place, that gets all of these verdicts? Which of them does it force me to give up? And what does it say about two cases I haven't listed: a ballot measure that would let 51% permanently strip the franchise from some group, and one where the people most affected by the outcome — future residents, non-citizens, the people the vote will price out of town — get no vote at all.
