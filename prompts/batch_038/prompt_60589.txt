Here's what I've been staring at. I wrote a little script that flashes a red dot about two inches to the left of where you're looking on my laptop, then maybe a sixth of a second later flashes a green dot two inches to the right. Every single person I show it to swears they saw ONE dot slide across and change colour somewhere in the middle. Nobody reports red-there-then-green-there. I know this is the color phi business and I know the two standard stories:

- the Orwellian one: your visual system really did get red then green, you really did experience that, but afterwards the record gets rewritten, so you honestly report a colour change;
- the Stalinesque one: there was a backstage rehearsal, and whatever finally gets "seen" is already the edited version, so there was never a moment when you experienced plain red-then-green.

And I know the Dennett-side punchline is that these two are indistinguisable, that there's no fact of the matter, no screen where the final cut gets shown once and for all. The other side says that's a dodge — there IS a fact about what was going on at each moment, the illusion just shows the report is assembled from a late draft.

What I want you to draft is the joint statement those two camps would both put their names to after a genuinely good-faith adverserial collaboration. Not a dialogue, not you telling me who's right. Specifically:
