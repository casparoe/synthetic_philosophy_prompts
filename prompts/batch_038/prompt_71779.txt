Draft the joint statement that a subject-sensitive invariantist (pragmatic encroacher) and an epistemic contextualist about "knows" could both sign after a serious adversarial collaboration. The two are allies against classical invariantism, which makes their boundary easy to blur; I want the document that keeps it sharp.

Open with a numbered list of claims both can sign: what the bank-style cases do and do not establish, how much weight the variability in competent speakers' willingness to attribute knowledge can bear, what the norms of assertion and of practical reasoning commit each side to, and what each view owes to the invariantist it rejects.

Then a disagreements table. For each crux give (a) the contextualist's formulation, worded so a contextualist would accept it, (b) the encroacher's, likewise, (c) a case or consideration both agree bears on it, and (d) each side's prediction, recorded in advance. Organize the table around this designed case, which must be treated explicitly: at noon Ines, a trainee, reads a pressure gauge with nothing riding on it; at 12:01 she silently becomes the only qualified operator on shift, so that a false belief would be catastrophic. Her evidence is unchanged, no attributor's conversational context has changed, and nobody has told her. Does she still know at 12:05 that the pressure is within tolerance? Have each side commit to an answer and to a fallback: if the encroacher retreats to the view that what matters is the subject's evidence about her stakes rather than the stakes themselves, state what that retreat costs; if the contextualist says the question was ill-posed because it asked for knowledge simpliciter rather than truth-in-a-context, state what that concession gives up.

Close with what each party would count as evidence against their own view, whether any consideration is agreed to be capable of actually moving the other, and which portions of the dispute you now judge to be merely verbal — together with the test you used to decide.
