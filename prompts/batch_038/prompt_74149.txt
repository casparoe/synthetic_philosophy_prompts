You are an expert in contemporary epistemology and moral philosophy, with particular command of the debate over moral testimony and moral deference. I direct a public philosophy series at my university, and next month's guest is a philosopher who defends a strong form of moral testimony pessimism: on her account, a moral belief formed solely on another person's say-so is epistemically defective even when the speaker is reliable and the belief true, and acting on such a belief is morally deficient as well. Think carefully before drafting. Begin by working out, in the opening section, which commitments her view actually carries and where the standard arguments press hardest on them.

Please give me the following, in this order:

1. **The target** (roughly 150 words): a reconstruction of her position that separates the epistemic charge from the aretaic one, and identifies which of the two her arguments genuinely require.

2. **The questions** (ten to twelve numbered items): for each, a question I could ask as written, the reply you expect from her, the follow-up that reply should provoke, and a short tag naming the pressure point being tested. Cover, at minimum: whether the defect is epistemic or a matter of moral worth, and what she forfeits by retreating to the second; the difficulty that there appear to be no moral experts, and no way of identifying one without antecedent moral judgment of one's own; the agent raised in a corrupt community whose best available move is to take a reformer's word; moral testimony delivered with reasons attached rather than as bare assertion; and the comparison with testimony about aesthetic value, where her proposed mechanism seems to apply with equal force.

3. **Escape routes** (roughly 150 words): the two or three deflections she will reach for, and what to say to close each off.

4. **One question not to ask** (roughly 80 words): the question that sounds incisive but quietly concedes ground she would like to have.

Nothing generic about her intellectual formation, and nothing that allows her simply to restate the view.
