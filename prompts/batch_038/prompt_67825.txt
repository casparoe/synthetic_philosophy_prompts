Thirty-eight years in experimental geophysics, retired last spring, and now for some reason I'm the "independent lay member" on a county panel that reviews risk numbers coming in from engineering consultants — tailings dams, a solvent plume under an old works, that sort of thing. Since retiring I've been reading philosophy of science and got hooked on the argument about what a probability even is: the frequency view, the personal-degree-of-belief view, and the propensity view where the number is supposed to be a physical disposition of the setup itself. Turns out our panel has been having this argument for years without knowing it. Last month a consultant handed us an annual failure probability of 3 × 10⁻⁴ for a dam that gets closed in forty years, derived mostly from eliciting engineers' opinions, and called it objective. One of my colleagues says if no failure happens in those forty years the claim was simply false and the firm should be sanctioned. Another says the number means nothing unless somebody will actually bet on it at those odds. I dont think either of them is right, but I couldn't say why.

So write me a procedure the panel can run on any submitted number. What do we ask, in what order, and what counts as a decent answer to each question? It has to make the submitter say which of the three readings they're offering the number under — and cope with the honest answer, which is usually that they havent thought about it — tell us what each reading licenses us to do with the figure and what it forbids, and say plainly where we should just send the thing back rather than argue. Mark the steps that look procedural but are really judgement calls, and say where the whole apparatus gives out, e.g. a genuinely one-off event with no sensible reference class.

Then run it on two cases: the 3 × 10⁻⁴ dam figure, and a valve model with a documented failure record across a hundred similar plants. I expect one of the two to come out somewhere I won't like — don't soften it.

No headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables: I want to forward this to the other four members as an email, so plain continuous prose with the steps signposted inside the sentences, first this then that. And no references or reading list, I can find my own books.
