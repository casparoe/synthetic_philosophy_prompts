Six years ago I started a free after-school math program in my neighborhood. No salary, runs out of a church basement, about 30 kids. A former colleague who now runs a for-profit test-prep chain just offered me a regional director job — triple what I make now (I teach part-time at a community college), and he'd genuinely be happy to have me.

Two friends say I'd be crazy to turn it down. Their argument: the guy they'd hire instead is a pure bottom-line optimizer who'd cut corners, so the students who buy the test prep are better off with me than without me. Plus I could donate half the raise and do more good that way than I do with 30 kids in a basement. One of them said that if I still feel queasy about it, that's self-indulgence — I'm protecting my own sense of being a decent person ahead of the good I could actually do. The other said the program would survive without me since two volunteers could run it, though I dont think it would.

Here's my problem: when I picture myself in that job I feel like I'd be betraying something, but I can't point to any particular bad outcome it causes, and I know how that sounds. Is that all it is — self-image maintenance? Or is there something real in the feeling that my friends' accounting is leaving out? Give me the bottom line in your first sentence, then the reasoning. Under 250 words please.
