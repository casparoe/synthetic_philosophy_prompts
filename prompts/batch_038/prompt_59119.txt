I run the software side of a modular robotics program. Our units are identical tracked robots, each individually capable, that latch together at runtime onto a shared truss to act as one larger vehicle and unlatch again to work alone. Roughly half of our design reviews end in the same unresolvable argument: is a latched cluster one robot or six? A colleague of mine who did a philosophy degree insists this is a real question with a real literature — that metaphysicians call it the special composition question, meaning: when, if ever, do some things compose a further thing? — and that the two most systematic answers are the extremes, namely that any group of material objects always composes something, and that composition never happens at all, so that everything there is consists of simples arranged clusterwise, or arranged tablewise, or whatever. She also mentions a middle answer she attributes to van Inwagen, roughly that composition occurs only when the activities of the parts add up to a life.

I have read none of this and I do not want a bibliography.
