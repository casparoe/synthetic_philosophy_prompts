Write me a satirical piece, about 700 words, in the form of a morning trading note from the desk of a firm that clears "moral trades" — negotiated compromises between people or groups who hold different value systems and can both come out ahead by swapping concessions. Full genre treatment: overnight market summary, notable executions, liquidity warnings on thinly held positions, a risk bullet, a compliance note, and one client question answered at the bottom.

The desk is completely sincere. It believes what it does is Pareto-improving and that anyone who refuses to trade is leaving welfare on the table for no reason. The target of the satire is the market-clearing way of talking about moral compromise as such — not a real person, org, or school of thought. Invent your own firm, clients, tickers and instruments, and describe the positions rather than naming them (a client who counts total wellbeing, a client for whom certain acts are off the books entirely regardless of consequences, a client whose whole position is concentrated in a single very local obligation).

I want the jokes to come from getting both the economics and the ethics right, so someone who has thought hard about this laughs instead of winces. Pressure points you can build gags out of — but dont list them as objections, let each one surface as an ordinary trading problem:

- the units aren't fungible. Two clients can't actually be paid in the same currency, so the desk has to invent an exchange rate and then defend it in front of the committee, and the rate moves depending on who's asking.
- liquidity. Views with few holders trade badly. There's no bid for a position nobody else wants, and one client's entire book is stuck in something with a single market maker.
- settlement risk from value drift. A client who was one thing at trade date is another thing at settlement, and now the desk is holding a contract with a stranger who never agreed to it. Include a margin call over this.
- adverse selection. The counterparty most eager to trade is the one who knows something about your values that you don't.
- standing. Several clients are trading away the interests of parties who aren't at the desk and can't be, and the note should treat this purely as an operational headache, the way a bank treats a missing signature.
- and the one I most want nailed: the whole business depends on the disagreement persisting. The better a trade works at actually bringing people together, the less there is left to trade next quarter — and its a wasting asset that everyone at the firm has quietly priced in. There should be at least one line where a trader's cheerful framing of this makes the reader feel it without being told what to feel.

Deadpan throughout. No winking, no footnotes, no author's note explaining the joke, no real philosophers named. Make up specific numbers, spreads, a block trade, and one execution that goes wrong in an instructive way. Readable for someone who's never heard the phrase "gains from trade," funny for someone who has. End on the client question and its answer, where the answer is technically correct and quietly devastating.
