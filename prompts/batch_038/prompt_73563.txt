So my brother is a sophomore in college and he brought his roommate Dev over for dinner last week, and somehow the conversation turned into an hour-long argument about whether a mosquito matters morally. I mostly just sat there eating, but my mom thought it was hilarious and told me to keep track, so here's roughly how it went, move by move:

1. Dev said moral status isnt a switch, it's a dimmer. A dog has more of whatever-the-relevant-thing-is than a bee, a bee has more than a worm, and it shades gradually down toward basically nothing. So he thinks asking "is it sentient, yes or no" is the wrong question and you should be asking "how much."

2. My brother said okay but then you inherit math you can't pay for. If a bee has a hundredth of a dog's moral weight, then a hundred bees outweigh a dog, and the trillion bacteria in my gut outweigh me. He called it the swamping problem: there's always way more of the small stuff, so any dimmer view gets buried by sheer numbers.

3. Dev's answer was weird because he half-conceded. He started to say that's just aggregation and we accept it elsewhere, but then he backed off and said actually no, a hundred papercuts don't outweigh one broken arm either, so tiny amounts maybe dont add up at all. Then he restated his view as: degrees of sentience don't map onto degrees of moral weight in a straight line, there's a floor underneath which it doesn't register, no matter how many of them you pile up.

4. My brother said that's cheating. You can't advertise a smooth dimmer and then smuggle in a magic cutoff. Either it's a spectrum all the way down or it isn't. And if there's a floor, name it — tell me the exact animal where it flips, he said, and Dev couldn't.

5. Dev said not being able to name a line doesn't prove there isn't one, same as how you can't name the exact grain of sand where a pile stops being a heap, but a pile of two grains and a pile of ten thousand obviously aren't the same situation.

6. Then my brother went for the thermostat. He said if the thing that matters is sensing your environment and adjusting your behavior based on feedback, our smart thermostat does that, and a Roomba does it too, so Dev owes him some moral status for appliances — and a whole house of networked devices might collectively out-weigh a goldfish. Dev said sure, whatever, the amount is a rounding error, nobody loses sleep over it. My brother said that's exactly the lazy move: you set the number wherever it costs you nothing, and you'd say the same thing about any group you don't care about.
