A doctoral proposal in our department offers, as its entire contribution, an answer to a contrastive question of the form: why did sustained industrialization get underway in England and not in the Yangzi Delta? Before we bother evaluating its particular thesis, I want the standard any such answer has to meet.

Give me the desiderata for an adequate answer to a large-scale contrastive question of this kind, with the consideration behind each requirement. Cover at least: how the contrast class has to be fixed, and what changes when it is fixed differently (England versus the Delta; Britain versus China; sustained growth versus one-off growth; first mover versus everyone who came later); what the explanans must amount to — a necessary condition, a difference-maker, a probability-raiser, a mechanism — and what each choice commits an answer to; which presuppositions the question itself carries, and whether an answer is obliged to accept them or may reformulate them; which findings it must be able to accommodate, including the contested revisionist claim that the two regions looked comparable on a number of measures not long before the divergence; and which distinctions it may not collapse, such as cause of onset versus cause of acceleration, properties of a unit versus relations between units, singular event versus recurrent pattern, and causal explanation versus a well-ordered narrative of the sequence.

Then tell me which single desideratum does the most to knock out popular answers (ecological windfall, factor prices, institutions, ideas, political fragmentation), and whether any subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable — if so, which subset and why; that is the part I most want to see. Close with two or three sentences on whether a single-pair, single-variable proposal can survive the list at all.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning. No preamble, and do not restate the question.
