Three weeks ago I paid for next-day shipping on a bike light that would have arrived free in eight days, and the same morning I closed the tab my bank keeps opening to tell me I haven't put anything into my pension since 2019. Since then I've gotten roped into running the monthly money nights at the credit union where I work — 15 to 25 adults, folding tables, a whiteboard, 75 minutes, nobody a student, just people who came after their shift.

I want to build one of those nights around discounting the future, but not the version where I show compound interest on a slide and everyone nods. What I actually want them to feel is that there's a chunk of impatience in their choices that isn't about risk and isn't about money growing. So the constraint I care most about: the game has to run under total certainty and zero interest. No dice, no chance cards, no returns, no inflation to blame. Everyone knows exactly what they'll get and exactly when. If they still grab the sooner option, the leftover is the thing I want sitting on the table where they can stare at it.

Then I want a reversal. Halfway through, the players who've been stuck waiting get to allocate costs backwards onto the earlier rounds — onto the versions of themselves and the other tables that took the sooner thing. In real life the future can't do that. In the game it can, and I think thats where the argument actually bites: you make the symmetry visible, and then you take it away and ask what's left over.

So: rules and materials, a token scheme that isn't real money but is something they'd genuinely be annoyed to lose, handed over only at the end so the waiting is physically felt; minute-by-minute for 75; roles or table structure if that helps; and a debrief that maps specific moments in the room onto the difference between impatience, risk, and productivity of capital. Nobody hears the words hyperbolic or present bias until after. I'll be playing too, not just refereeing, so build in whatever you need me to do from inside the game.

Also tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time — I'm certain somebody games the tokens within ten minutes — and how to handle it live. And be straight with me about which feature of the idea a room full of living people in 2024 just cant reproduce, so I know what I have to supply by saying it out loud. No reading list, I won't read it.
