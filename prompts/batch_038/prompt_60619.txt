Committee made me write down where I actually stand on collapse before quals so here it is — tell me if it hangs together.

(1) Collapse isn't a real category, its a label for rapid reorganization that someone retroactively decided was bad; the Classic Maya lowlands is the standing counterexample to the whole discourse.
(2) Rapa Nui is my exception — there I think the ecocide story basically holds, and the Hunt/Lipo rat work explains the palms going away without explaining away the disaster.
(3) On mechanism I'm a Tainterian: complexity is costly, marginal returns to it decline, and shedding it is frequently the rational move for the people doing the shedding.
(4) But my actual modeling framework is resilience theory / panarchy — adaptive cycles, regime shifts, cross-scale rescue, that vocabulary.
(5) Demography is the only honest metric. Everything institutional is storytelling.
(6) I refuse to write a single sentence of the "lessons for us today" kind, tho my program requires impact statements about contemporary sustainability.

Go pair by pair. Which of these genuinely can't both be held, and which only look bad until you spell them out? For the real ones rank them by severity, say which commitment has to go, and tell me what giving it up costs me downstream — I don't want to find out in chapter two that fixing one blew up another. I'm pretty sure at least one pair I'm worried about is fine, and I have no idea which. Audit, not lecture: no reading list, no historiography of the field, just trace what commits me to what.
