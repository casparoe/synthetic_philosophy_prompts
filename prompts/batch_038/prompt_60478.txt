ok so I'm in a philosophy of science seminar on scientific realism and the pessimistic meta-induction, and our prof runs this reccuring exercise: she names a theoretical posit and rules on whether the realists or the pessimists came out right about it — i.e. was there anything actually there. She frames every ruling as a bet that gets settled in the year 2200: will people still be using the term, and will they agree with us about what it picks out?

her record so far:
- phlogiston: pessimists, nothing there
- caloric: split — pessimists about the fluid, realists about the equations of heat flow
- the luminiferous ether: pessimists, full stop, and she stresses that the equations went over into special relativity basically untouched
- Mendel's factors: realists, even though what a gene is kept changing underneath the word
- Newtonian gravity as instantaneous action at a distance: split again, pessimists about the mechanism, realists about the law surviving as a limiting case
- Bohr's electron orbits: pessimists
- Boltzmann's atoms: realists
- the vital force / entelechy business: pessimists

She also has a slogan she repeats constantly, that she's a pessimist about mechanisms and an optimist about structures — and then the ether ruling flatly contradicts it, which is the thing I cant reconcile and probably what my response paper should be about.

Next week's case is the wave function. Will ψ in 2200 look like the atoms or like the caloric? The wrinkle her earlier cases never tested: the live interpretations (Everett, Bohm, collapse theories, the ψ-is-just-bookkeeping views) agree on every prediction but disagree about what ψ even is, whereas by the time atoms were settled everybody roughly agreed what they were.

So: predict her verdict. Then give me the candidate rules that fit ALL of the above equally well. I've got structure-preservation, entity realism / manipulability, and the unconceived-alternatives line, but I'm pretty sure there's at least one more doing real work here, maybe something keyed on whether a posit could be dropped without losing any predictions. For each rule say what it implies about ψ and exactly where the rules come apart. Then actually commit — which one is hers, and why the ether ruling points that way rather than against it — and tell me which further case would settle it. I was going to propose ψ for a single lab system versus the universal wave function, but pick something better if you can.
