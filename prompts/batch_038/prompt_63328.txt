Our hospital ethics committee blew up last week and I still can't tell who won, so maybe you can.

A pediatrician wants the charity fund to cover growth hormone for a 10-year-old who's in the 3rd percentile for height with no diagnosable endocrine problem. A philosopher who sits on the committee said that's enhancement, not treatment — there's no disease here, just a statistically unusual height — and covering it takes money away from kids who are actually sick. The pediatrician said "statistically unusual" is exactly what a disease is on the standard naturalist account of health: subnormal functioning of a part relative to the species design, so if that account classifies it as treatment, it's treatment. The philosopher said that account can't be doing the real work, because the reference values drift — kids today are taller than their great-grandparents on the same genes — and its not plausible that a whole generation got healthier as it got richer, so "normal functioning" is tracking something social, not biological. The pediatrician conceded the drift point but said it doesn't matter for this kid: what's at stake isn't the number on the growth chart, it's that short men get teased, passed over for jobs, and assumed less competent, and medicine's job is protecting people's fair range of opportunities, which this measurably narrows. The philosopher said that proves too much — if you fund height because tall men earn more, you have to fund braces, tutoring, and skin lightening wherever colorism pays off, and a principle that can't exclude any of those isn't a principle about medicine, it's a principle about redistributing advantage, which is tax policy's job and not the pharmacy budget's. The pediatrician then said fine, draw the line at whether there's an actual bodily deficit to correct, and the philosopher said he'd just thrown away his own argument to save the case. Neither budged.

Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right at each step, not just who wins overall? And please mark which parts of this are settled among people who actually work on the treatment/enhancement distinction versus which parts are still live — I don't want to walk into the next meeting treating a contested view as the consensus.
