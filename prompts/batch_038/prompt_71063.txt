I chair the zoning variance board of a mid-sized city, and I have been trying for some time to put a name on a pattern I keep finding in our own files and then recognizing elsewhere. I would rather describe it carefully and let you tell me whether there is an established concept for it than guess at a term myself.

The clearest instance is our own. The code sets a minimum side setback of eight feet. In one year we grant a variance for a porch at seven feet four, on the grounds of an unusual lot shape. The following year an applicant asks for a detached garage at six feet ten, and what carries the board is that we have already allowed seven feet four in comparable circumstances, and that a difference of six inches is not a meaningful one. A year after that comes a shed at six feet, then a shed with an attached covered walkway, which functions as a structure at five feet. Taken one at a time, I would still defend every decision in that sequence: the increments were small, the reasoning was the reasoning we had accepted the year before, and refusing any single application after granting the previous one would have looked arbitrary, even unfair, to the people in the room. But if you compare the file as it now stands to the code as written, we are administering something like a five-foot standard that nobody ever proposed, that no member of this board would have described as our standard when we joined, and that no meeting minutes anywhere record us adopting.

I have since seen the same shape twice outside zoning. A friend who does trust and safety work at a platform describes enforcement teams that are trained on examples of previously removed content, so each incoming cohort calibrates itself against the last cohort's decisions rather than against the published policy; the operative category ends up considerably wider than the text, with no revision ever announced. And an academic department I know has watched its sense of what a competitive hire looks like slide a little in every search, each committee measuring candidates against the previous hire rather than against any written description of the job.

What I am after is the following, and I should say at the outset that a bare label would not be much use to me.

First: does this configuration have an established name, in philosophy or in any neighbouring literature — law, organizational sociology, the measurement and assessment field, wherever it may live? If there are several partial terms, I would like to know each of them and what part of the phenomenon it actually covers.

Second: how does it differ from the things I would reach for instinctively? I can already hear that it resembles a slippery slope, but nobody here predicted or feared a descent and there was no argument of the form "if we allow this we will end up allowing that," so that does not seem right. It also resembles the heap puzzle, in that the trouble lives in small steps, but our steps do not turn on the extension of a vague word so much as on which earlier case we are comparing to. And it is clearly not the same as moving the goalposts, which I take to be deliberate and adversarial. I would like the differences stated sharply rather than by way of a list of near neighbours.

Third, and this is the part I most want pressed on: in each of my cases every individual decision preserved a kind of local consistency — this application is like the one we granted last year — and it is the record as a whole that has become inconsistent with the rule. Is there a recognized account of that structure, of a standard that is silently defined by the most recent decision rather than by the original rule? Related to it: does the diagnosis depend on the vagueness of words like unusual and comparable, or would the same erosion occur under a perfectly sharp rule administered by a procedure that only ever looks at the immediately preceding case? I genuinely cannot tell which of those two is the source of the problem, and I suspect they have been distinguished somewhere.

Fourth: I want the edges. There is a version of this that looks like a virtue — incremental development through precedent, where each decision is bound by the last and the resulting body of practice is more refined than any code could be drafted in advance. Why is the same structure a pathology in my board and an achievement there? Tell me what would have to change for my case to be the good kind, and give me a case that sits right on the boundary and could be called either way.

Finally, if no single term covers the whole thing, propose one, define it so that it excludes the near neighbours above, and tell me what would count as a clear instance of it and what would not.
