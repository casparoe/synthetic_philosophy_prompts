I have an argument I built for a reading group on updating with conditionals, and it lands somewhere I cannot accept. The set-up is van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin case: Judy is dropped in a region divided into Red and Blue territory and, crosswise, into Second Company and Headquarters sectors; she starts with equal credence in each of the four quadrants. A radio operator she trusts then tells her that if she is in Red territory, the odds are three to one that she is in the Headquarters sector.

Here is the argument, as I wrote it out.

(1) A report ought to move your credence in a proposition only if what the report says bears on whether that proposition is true.
(2) What this message says is a claim about a ratio of probabilities, and that claim stands whether or not Judy is in fact in Red territory; so it does not bear on the question whether she is.
(3) Therefore her credence that she is in Red territory is left exactly where it was — which is the standard answer to the case, so everything up to here seems safe.
(4) But the very same reasoning applies to the other events in play: the message stands whether or not she is in Headquarters, and whether or not she is in both Red territory and Headquarters, so it bears on neither of those questions either, and her credences in them are left where they were too.
(5) The credence in the conjunction is the credence in the conditional times the credence in the antecedent, so if neither factor moves, the conjunction cannot move.
(6) Yet the whole effect the message is supposed to have is to move that conditional, from one-half to three-quarters.
(7) So no message of this form can have any effect whatever: conditional testimony turns out to be unabsorbable, and the Judy Benjamin problem is not a curiosity at the edge of Bayesianism but an instance of a general impossibility of learning anything from a report about a conditional probability.

I want to keep (3), since that is the position I am defending in the group, and I certainly want to deny (7). Tell me which step to give up, why that step looked innocent when I wrote it down, and what the correct principle is that yields (3) without yielding (4) through (7) — including what has to be assumed about the radio operator, and about how he chose what to report, for that principle to be licensed here.

Please stay under 450 words, and open with a single sentence naming the step you reject.
