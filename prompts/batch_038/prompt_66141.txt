**Charity Does Not Survive a Filter**

**1. The guarantee.** Davidson's radical interpreter has, by stipulation, no dictionary, no antecedently shared language, and no direct access to the speaker's mind. What she has is a pattern: which sentences the speaker holds true, and in what circumstances. From that she is to construct a joint assignment of meanings and beliefs. The constraint that makes the construction possible is charity — the interpreter must, so far as she can, maximize the truth or at least the intelligibility of what the speaker takes to be true, because massive agreement is not a discovery she makes after an assignment is in place but the background against which any assignment can be made at all. Davidson then presses the point into an epistemology. Consider an interpreter who is omniscient about the world, including the full causal history of the speaker's utterances, and who interprets charitably. Such an interpreter, he argues, will find the speaker largely coherent and largely in agreement with her; and since her beliefs are all true, the speaker's must be largely true as well. Coherence is thereby made to yield correspondence. That is the anti-skeptical payoff: massive error about the world cannot be coherently attributed, and so cannot be coherently doubted.

**2. A condition that is doing the work.** I want to isolate something that both the constructive procedure and the omniscient-interpreter argument require, and that Davidson never names. Call it *evidential transparency*: an interpreter I is evidentially transparent with respect to a speaker S just in case (i) I can identify which sentences S holds true, and when, without first settling what those sentences mean, and (ii) the events and objects that cause S to hold them true are in principle available to I's own observation. In the paradigmatic setting — two speakers in a shared environment, one of them ostending a rabbit — both clauses hold trivially. My thesis is that the veridicality guarantee is delivered not by charity as such but by charity operating under transparency, and that the two can come apart.

This is not Quine's indeterminacy point. Indeterminacy says that many assignments fit the evidence equally well. My point is different in kind: in the cases I have in mind, the evidence itself has already been shaped by an assignment the interpreter cannot audit.

**3. The filtered case.** Take a chain S → M → I, where M is a mediator: a bilingual go-between who is also the only person reporting the circumstances of the utterances; a scribal and retranslation tradition standing between a historian and her sources; a machine-translation pipeline; a system whose training corpus consists largely of the recorded outputs of another system. In each case I never witnesses the circumstances of S's hold-true attitudes. Everything I has is M's testimony, and M's testimony is already an interpretive product, produced under some assignment of meaning to S's speech that M made and did not announce.

**4. Why the guarantee fails — and why it is not a matter of degree.** The obvious response is that this is mere error accumulation: if M is charitable to S and I is charitable to M, and each stage is largely truthful, then I's picture of S is largely truthful too, with a somewhat wider margin. I want to resist this. The difference between the transparent and the filtered case is modal rather than quantitative. Under transparency, error is self-announcing: the mechanism by which charity guarantees truth is precisely that a systematic misassignment would produce a visible mismatch between what the speaker holds true and what the interpreter observes. In the filtered case there is no such mismatch to be had, because the only thing I observes is M's output, and M's output is exactly what a biased assignment would also produce. Bias here is not improbable; it is undetectable in principle. Davidson's argument, on this reading, is a detectability result dressed as a truth result: it shows that error would be caught, not that error cannot arise, and where there is only one channel, catching is not on offer.

The same point can be put in the vocabulary of triangulation. On Davidson's later account, what fixes the object of a thought is that two creatures react to a common cause and to each other's reactions; the intersection of the two lines to the world is what pins the referent down. In the filtered case I has only one line. Everything arrives along the M-channel, so the intersection that was supposed to fix the referent is never formed. What looks like a second speaker is in fact an extension of the first.

**5. Consequence.** Charity is locally veridical. It secures the truth of the beliefs of those whose circumstances I can watch, and it is silent about the beliefs of those whose circumstances I cannot. That narrows the anti-skeptical result considerably. It refutes the skeptic about my present interlocutor and the table between us; it does not refute the skeptic about testimony, about the past, or about minds at a distance. Since most of what any of us believes about the world arrives filtered, the guarantee covers a smaller share of our cognitive life than the rhetoric of the omniscient interpreter suggests.

**6. Objections I have to face.**

(a) *Category error.* I is not interpreting S at all, only M; the thesis is untouched. My reply is that this concedes the narrowing argued for in §5.

(b) *Collapse.* If I has any independent contact with S's world, the case is not filtered, and triangulation applies to the whole network at once. Agreed — but then the interesting cases are precisely those with no independent contact, and there is a continuum here, so Davidson owes an account of how much independent contact is enough for the guarantee to switch back on.

(c) *This is the familiar modal objection in new clothes* — the charge that the omniscient-interpreter argument equivocates over the scope of a necessity operator, or slides between the interpreter's and the speaker's standards of truth. I do not think so: my objection does not depend on the omniscient interpreter and applies equally to the finite constructive procedure. But I am not confident.

(d) *The dangerous one.* Transparency may be re-describable in a way that makes it transitive. Since M is herself a speaker in a world I shares with her, transparency holds between I and M; and if transparency is transitive along a chain of speakers, the guarantee has simply been applied twice and I have shown nothing. My only reply is that transitivity is exactly what §4 denies: what fails is clause (ii), observational access to the causes of the further speaker's attitudes, and observational access does not transmit through a channel.

(e) *Eligibility.* One might supplement charity with a preference for interpretations that assign more natural properties, on the grounds that charity alone leaves too many candidates. That does not help here. Eligibility is a relation between a language and the world it is used in, and in the filtered case I's eligibility judgments concern M's language, not S's. If anything, a filter gives I further reason to prefer natural readings of M's reports, which is orthogonal to the question of what S meant.

**7. Loose ends.** Should the filter be understood as a person, a practice, or an artifact, or does the argument need all three? And is "undetectable in principle" too strong — might I detect bias by exploiting internal tensions across M's whole output, that is, by treating M's corpus as triangulating against itself?
