Me and my brother got into this at 2am about whether people should have kids and I ended up writing down a chain of reasoning I can't stop, even though I don't believe where it lands. Here's what I've got:

1. Everybody — including people who think having kids is one of the best things you can do — agrees it would be wrong to deliberately bring into the world a child you knew would suffer horribly and die young.
2. The only thing wrong with it, from that kid's own side of things, is that they'd be worse off existing than never existing.
3. So for some lives, never existing really is better than existing. Not as a figure of speech either, because that's what makes (1) true.
4. "Better off" and "worse off" are comparisons on a single scale: how good a situation is for the person it happens to.
5. If never-existing can come out ahead of an existing life on that scale, then it can be compared against *any* existing life. There's no third category of "these two just don't compare."
6. Never existing contains no pain, no loss, no death, and it's the same no matter who you are.
7. Every life anybody actually lives contains some pain, some loss, and ends in death.
8. The good parts of a life belong to the person living them, and that person only exists because somebody chose to make them — so those goods can't be counted as reasons *to* make them. This is the part everyone seems to already accept without noticing: nobody thinks you're doing someone a favour by not having a child who would've been happy.
9. So the bads in a life count against creating it, and the goods don't count for it.
10. Which means no life can be better than never existing. At absolute best it's a tie.
11. So having a child is never a benefit and is sometimes a harm, and people should stop — me and my brother included, and the species should just fizzle out over a couple of generations.

(11) is where I want off but I can't find the exit. (1) and (2) feel completely unshakeable to me and I don't want to give them up, and I also don't want to end up at the other exit, which looks like "more happy kids is always better, so go forth and multiply," because that's obviously not how people actually think about it.

So: where does this break? Point at the specific step, tell me why it seemed fine when I wrote it down, and tell me what I owe once I drop it — like if I reject it do I lose the ability to say anything at all about kids who'd suffer, or do I lose the thing in (8) that seemed so obvious? And is there any middle position left here, or does the whole thing really collapse into "either have as many kids as you can or have none"?
