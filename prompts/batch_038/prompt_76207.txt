Compose a joke about the dispute over original versus derived intentionality. The humor should turn on Dennett's two-bitser — a physical device whose states are about something only because an interpreter treats them as such — and on his deflationary conclusion that the same goes for the states inside a head, so that the chain of borrowed aboutness never seems to reach a first lender.

Constraints. Give it two characters holding opposing views, so that the punchline arises from the disagreement rather than from a definition being restated wittily. The Searlean must be allowed the claim Searle actually makes, that syntax is not sufficient for semantics: he may lose the exchange, but not by being made ignorant of his own position. Rule out the standard nerd-joke formats — no lightbulb, no knock-knock, and nothing walks into a bar — and no pun that would still work if the technical terms were swapped for those of some other controversy. Keep it under about eight lines. Close with one or two sentences identifying what a listener must already accept for the punchline to land.
