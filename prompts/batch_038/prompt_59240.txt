I serve on the animal-welfare standards panel of a national research funder. Our policy tiers species by neurocognitive complexity, operationalized for the past two decades as encephalization quotient; tier determines required environmental enrichment, analgesia standards, and whether distress must be weighted at parity with equivalent human distress in the harm-benefit analysis. A petition asks us to replace EQ with absolute counts of pallial neurons. Its central claim is that EQ is largely an artifact of allometry — it measures brain mass against expected brain mass for a given body size, and so penalizes small-bodied animals for reasons unrelated to their mental lives — whereas birds pack far more neurons per gram of neural tissue than mammals do, leaving corvids and parrots below our top-tier boundary despite well-documented tool manufacture, episodic-like memory, and social transmission. Adopting the metric would promote several parrot and corvid species and demote some large-bodied mammals, and it would leave cephalopods unclassifiable, since a large share of an octopus's neural tissue sits in the arm ganglia rather than in any central brain. Draft the panel's decision memo — adopt, reject, or adopt in modified form — with reasoning the petitioners could actually respond to, a clear statement of which tier assignments would change and whether the evidence supports changing them now, and a treatment of what a neuron count is a proxy for versus what it is silent about. Close with the strongest consideration on the side you recommend against, and what would have to be true for it to prevail.
