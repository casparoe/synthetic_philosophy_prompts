The view: the aggregation problem is a pseudo-problem. Contractualism never owed an answer to "save the one or the many?" as a summing question, because justification is addressed to each person separately and complaints just don't aggregrate across persons — so wherever the numbers look like they matter, something else is doing the work (the strength of individual complaints, a weighted lottery among people whose claims are equal, each of the many having a complaint against you specifically).

What would count against that? I want the exposure map, not a defence: which cases it can't describe, which results about choosing among principles (cycles?) it stands or falls with, whether letting in weighted lotteries is a covert way of smuggling aggregation back through the back door, and whether the deflationary move risks anything at all or has just been qualified until it cant fail.
