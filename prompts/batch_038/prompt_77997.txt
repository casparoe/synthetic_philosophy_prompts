Working title for my chapter is something like "The Comprehension Didn't Vanish, It Relocated." The claim: when Dennett talks about the strange inversions in Darwin and Turing -- competence without comprehension -- the inversion never actually removes understanding, it just moves it somewhere we weren't looking. Into the designer, the trainer, the fitness function, the corpus, the whole lineage. On my view there is no such thing as comprehension-free competence, only comprehension that isn't sitting where we expected to find it sitting.

Tell me what would count against this. I want the arguments, cases, and results that would genuinely threaten the thesis -- not "but the Chinese Room" as a slogan, I mean the versions with teeth -- sorted from fatal down to merely awkward. For each one, say what my view is actually committed to and which piece I'd have to give up, and whether a defender of the relocation thesis would concede the point or explain it away. I'm most worried that the thesis doesn't risk anything: can you name a case of competence with no comprehension anywhere upstream -- not in the designers, not in the data, not in the selection criterion, not in the history -- and if you can't, isn't mine just an unfalsifiable redescription? If it is, say so plainly and then tell me whether that's a real defect here or whether it's fine for a claim that's really about how to carve up explanations rather than a bet about the world. Fold that into whichever item it belongs to, don't give it its own slot.

Also rank these three against each other, since they're the live candidates I keep coming back to: an AlphaZero-style self-play run where the only human input is the rules of the game and the resulting competence can't be traced to anything the designers understood; a directed-evolution result where the selection criterion is a black-box instrument nobody can articulate; and a trained model doing something its developers demonstrably didn't understand and could not have produced by hand.

What would move me most personally is a case where deleting the human contribution doesn't degrade the competence at all -- that's the cleanest test of whether the comprehension was ever doing any work in the first place.

Format: no intro, no summary, no closing remarks -- just exactly five numbered defeaters, each a sentence or two, ordered from most to least damaging.
