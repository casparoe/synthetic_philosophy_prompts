A piece of AI-adjacent writing I keep encountering — most recently in a comment thread I found unusually persuasive — maps the standard pipeline for building a deployed model onto Dennett's fourfold taxonomy of minds. The correspondence it proposes is this. The training run itself is the Darwinian stage: gradient descent is blind variation and retention under a fixed objective, so the model's dispositions are selected rather than designed, and the lab has no more foresight into the resulting competences than a hillside has. Fine-tuning on human feedback is the Skinnerian stage: the system acquires operant dispositions from consequences inside its own history, dispositions it cannot articulate. Test-time reasoning — chain-of-thought, search over candidate solutions, self-verification — is the Popperian stage: conjectures are generated and eliminated internally, so the bad hypotheses perish instead of the hypothesis-maker. And tool use, retrieval, code execution, and the pretraining corpus itself constitute the Gregorian stage: the system imports competence from external artifacts and from accumulated human output, exercising abilities that are nowhere located inside it.

The author then draws a conclusion I want pressure-tested: that the familiar objection to large models — all that competence without any comprehension — is dissolved rather than answered by the mapping, on the grounds that Gregorian creatures never had comprehension to begin with. On this picture a human expert's understanding is itself largely imported from the surrounding culture, so demanding internal understanding of a model is a category error, and the interesting question is only whether the imported scaffolding is reliable.

Please audit the analogy rather than the conclusion first. Walk me through each of the four pairings and say what is actually doing the work in each; I want the mapping stated explicitly, feature by feature, before any verdict. Then separate the disanalogies that threaten the argument from the ones that are merely cosmetic. I have three of my own and would like them adjudicated: that the taxonomy is supposed to be cumulative and nested within a single continuous organism with genuine survival stakes, whereas a model's weights are frozen before it is deployed, so the "Skinnerian" history belongs to something that no longer exists; that all four stages in the model case run through the same medium, namely text, which collapses distinctions the taxonomy exists to mark; and that the referent slides quietly between the model, the optimizer, and the laboratory, each of which sits at a different rung. Finally: does the conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, and if the analogy is doing load-bearing work that it cannot support, is there a better one? I would rather be told the mapping is only good for two of the four stages than be handed a defense of all of it.
