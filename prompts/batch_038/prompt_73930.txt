I'm six months into my third novel and I've started keeping a private file of the metaphysical positions I think I hold, because my brother-in-law is a philosophy postdoc and he keeps needling me that novelists smuggle metaphysics in without paying for it. He's not wrong. I've been living inside Borges for a couple of years now — the forking-paths story, Funes, the refutation-of-time essay, the thing on Kafka's precursors — and somewhere along the line I absorbed a whole set of claims about time that I've never once checked against each other. So here's the file. I want an audit, not a lecture, and I want it from someone who's willing to tell me a piece of it has to go.

(1) Succession isn't real. There's no flow, no moving now, no fact about which moment is the present one. What we call time passing is a habit of the mind — and if the self is just a bundle of perceptions, as Hume had it, then one perception repeated is enough to break the whole idea of a sequence. I got this from Borges's refutation essay and I actually believe it, or I believe I believe it. (2) So there's no persisting self either. The person who drafted chapter two in March isn't the person cutting it now; nobody is both. The river is a metaphor we've mistaken for a thing. (3) And yet I hold that narrative form is the privileged medium for thinking about time, precisely because a novel happens in time — the reader's — and is about time — the world's — and I think form is how a book knows things, not decoration. I'm not being ironic about this and I don't want it cashed out as a useful fiction. I think the temporal unfolding of a novel is genuinely epistemic, the way an argument is. (4) The shape of my book is forking paths, and I mean that metaphysically, not as a game: every divergent series is real, they coexist, and at four joints in the manuscript I let the reader stand inside the fork from the inside. I think the true structure isn't one line but a growing web of diverging, converging and parallel series, and I think my book is truer to reality than a straight-line realist novel is. (5) I'm a realist about my characters in the sense that they resist me. They have standing dispositions I discover rather than decree — in April I found out that one of them lies under pressure, and I hadn't decided that, and the discovery rather than my outline is my evidence she's there. (6) Forgetting is a cognitive virtue. Funes's problem wasn't that he remembered too much, it was that he couldn't reach general ideas; thinking is discarding. I run my prose on that principle — compression isn't style, it's a truth claim. (7) But memory is where the ethics of this book lives. A narrator who conveniently forgets is doing something bad, not just being unreliable in an entertaining way, and the second half is about the duty not to let the past get smoothed over. I also accept the precursors move: a later book really does change what an earlier one meant, and I take that as a literal claim about literary history rather than a figure of speech.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which ones only look like they do? I'm fairly sure at least one pair in there is a fake tension, and I'd rather you say so than flag everything. Please rank the real ones by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and for each say what has to give and what the cheapest fix costs. One thing I've already thought of, so deal with it rather than springing it on me as the answer: go block-universe, all moments statically equally there, the whole web laid out at once — the eternity picture Borges plays with in his history of eternity. Tell me whether that actually rescues the set or just moves the problem somewhere else, because my instinct is that it quietly guts (3) and I don't know if that instinct is right. I genuinely don't know which of these I'd sacrifice and I don't want you guessing at my priorities; just price out the repairs. No reading lists or citations, please — I can't chase them and they'd be useless to me anyway. Last thing: tell me how confident you are in the audit as a whole, and name the single point in it you're least sure of, since I'd like to know which of your objections you'd retract first if I pushed back.
