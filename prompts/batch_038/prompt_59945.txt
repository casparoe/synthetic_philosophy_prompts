I've been arguing epistemology on rationalist-adjacent forums for the better part of a decade, and I've accumulated a position on introspection I've never written down in one place. Before I post it, I'd like an audit of whether it hangs together. Here is the set:

(1) I accept Williamson's anti-luminosity argument — the dawn-to-noon series, with a margin-for-error premise doing the work — and so hold that no non-trivial condition is such that, whenever it obtains, one is in a position to know that it obtains.

(2) I take that margin-for-error premise to be a structural fact about knowledge, not a contingent limitation of human sensitivity that better attention or better instruments could remove.

(3) Still, I treat self-ascriptions of my own present conscious states as the closest thing we have to a fixed point: I can't be right that there is a pain and wrong about whose pain it is, and I measure my other knowledge against these.

(4) I reject KK for beings like us, and I don't think that failure is a defect in us.

(5) But I hold a principle of my own that I've come to call the remediation clause: whenever you fail to know that you know p, some further step — more evidence, more time, more careful attention — would have closed the gap. The failure of KK is always in-principle repairable, even if never repaired.

(6) I accept the knowledge norm in both of its usual places: assert only what you know, and use p as a premise in practical reasoning only if you know p.

(7) And yet I defend what I'd call practical luminosity: feeling cold, I am entitled to reason from the fact that I feel cold and to put the sweater on, notwithstanding (1).

Tell me which of these genuinely conflict and which only appear to, tracing what each commitment actually entails rather than what it sounds like. Rank the real conflicts by severity instead of presenting them as equally bad. I suspect (2) and (5) cannot both stand, and that (6) against (7) is merely verbal — but do not take my word for where the trouble is; if the worst conflict lies elsewhere, say so, and say what the cheapest fix to it costs me.

Please answer in American English, in four titled sections in this order: **The inventory** (one line per commitment on what it commits me to beyond its surface, ~150 words); **The audit** (each real conflict, ordered by severity, with the argument spelled out and what would have to be given up, ~600 words); **Apparent conflicts that dissolve** (which pairs I have worried about needlessly and why, ~200 words); **The cheapest repair** (the minimal revision that leaves me a stable package, what it costs me elsewhere, and how confident you are in your severity ranking, including the single point in your own answer you are least sure of, ~200 words).
