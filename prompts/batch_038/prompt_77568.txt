So here's the thing I've been chewing on since about 1am. I own a small commercial cleaning company, nine people on payroll, in a mid-size Michigan city. One property management firm has been our biggest account for going on nine years — a three-story office building plus two satellite suites, roughly 41% of our revenue, and the contract runs through March 31. Last Thursday their facilities guy Rick was chatting with my supervisor Yolanda down at the loading dock and let slip that the building sold last month and the new owners plan to bring janitorial in-house when our term is up. Nothing is public yet, no announcement, no non-renewal notice in the mail, and as far as Rick knows I have no idea — Yolanda told me over lunch and I haven't said a word to anyone at the building except my brother-in-law Dan, who does our bookkeeping on weekends.

Dan and I argued about this for like three hours at his kitchen table and I still can't get clean on it. His claim is that the whole relationship collapses backwards, starting today. He says: look at the last month of the contract, the one ending March 31 — by then nobody has a future to protect, so I'd be a sucker to keep paying for the extra buffing and the weekend detail, and I'd coast; and they'd figure that out, so they'd start treating us like we're already gone, holding back the key cards and the elevator access and paying our invoices slow; and if they know that I know that, then February is effectively the last month, so I should coast in February too, which makes January the real last month, and so on until you land on this week. Conclusion: cut the crew hours now, bill every change order at the top of the schedule, milk the remaining five months, and don't feel bad about it because the math says the good-faith version of me is the one getting played. He kept saying it "unravels" and I could feel there was a step in there I don't buy, but every time I tried to point at it he'd restate the chain and it sounded airtight again. He took some negotiation class years ago and he is insufferable about it.

What I can't tell is whether I'm just being sentimental or whether his argument is actually broken somewhere. So: walk me through it properly. Where exactly does the chain go wrong, if it does — and be specific about which link, because "reputation matters" feels like a slogan and not an answer. Does it matter that Rick doesn't know that I know, and does it matter that the March date is a rumor rather than a notice, and that the sale could still fall apart or the new owners could keep us on? Does it matter that three of my other four accounts came to me through Rick's referrals over the years, or that Yolanda has been with me eleven years and can read my mood from how I park the van? I also want you to steelman Dan, because if there's a real version of what he's saying I need to know what it is and what it would take for it to apply. And then tell me what to actually do in the next two weeks, including whether I should be the one to bring it up with Rick and what I'd gain or loose by doing that. Please write in American English. At the very end, after everything else, give me one last line that begins with Verdict: followed by exactly one of DAN IS RIGHT, DAN IS WRONG, or DAN IS HALF RIGHT, and nothing else on that line.
