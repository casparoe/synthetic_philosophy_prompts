I am teaching a seminar on Ramsey's contention that discounting the welfare of later generations is ethically indefensible and reflects nothing but a weakness of the imagination, and I want a case built to order rather than another rehearsal of the standard arguments.

Construct a single thought experiment, of no more than three hundred words, set in an ordinary human-scaled institution — a charitable foundation, a municipal water board, a university's endowment committee — which must choose between a policy whose benefits accrue to the present generation and one whose benefits arrive some sixty years hence. Populate it with two decision-makers facing exactly the same two options. The first discounts the future not at all and regards herself as impartial between generations; the second discounts her own future heavily and concedes, on her own account, that this is a defect of imagination. The specification to be met is that the second nevertheless commits substantially more to the distant future than the first.

Constraints: the two must agree on every empirical fact in the scenario, including the entire consumption path of the future; neither may be misinformed, confused, or reasoning fallaciously; neither may have preferences that are exotic in kind, since both evaluate outcomes by summing discounted utility of consumption; and the case may not turn on personal identity, on a changing population, or on any probability, risk, or catastrophe. A scenario that stipulates who chooses what, instead of generating the choices from premises you have stated, fails.

After the scenario, show the arithmetic for each decision-maker: the effective rate at which she discounts the far future, and which of her premises produces it. Then name the single parameter doing the work, say what becomes of the two verdicts if that parameter is set to a neutral value, and close with the strongest reply available to a defender of Ramsey's contention — one which explains why the impatient agent's choice does not, in the end, vindicate her impatience.
