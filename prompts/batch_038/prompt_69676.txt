I am drafting the specification section of a call for papers for a special issue on panpsychism, and our editorial group needs to settle in advance on what will count as an answer to the combination problem. I want the requirements list before I read a single submission. The purpose is deliberately narrow, and I would like it kept in view throughout: the spec has to let a competent but non-specialist editor decide whether a proposed panpsychist account has solved the combination problem or has merely relocated it. Requirements that matter for choosing between panpsychist accounts rather than for clearing that bar should be marked as such, not omitted.

Write the desiderata any adequate solution must meet, and for each give the phenomenon, case, or consideration that generates it — something an editor could point at — rather than a label alone. At minimum, the list should cover:

1. The introspective and phenomenological data a solution must reproduce: why my experiential field presents as bounded and single rather than as an aggregate, and what the commissurotomy literature does and does not show about the contingency of that boundedness.
2. The split between combining subjects and combining phenomenal qualities, and whether an account may take one as primitive while claiming to solve the other — including what it owes us if it does.
3. A constraint I will call the relocation test: the account may not explain binding by positing a further relation whose own operation is a brute fact of exactly the kind that micro-experience was introduced to avoid.
4. A constraint I will call the selection constraint: if binding is said to track some physical or dynamical unity in the substrate, the account must say what makes that particular unity the binding-relevant one, on pain of the combination problem reappearing as an arbitrariness problem about boundaries.
5. The requirement that the account retain whatever explanatory advantage over emergentist dualism motivated the panpsychist turn in the first place, and not spend it in the solution.
6. The constraint that micro-experience is not directly observable, together with what a solution must therefore say about what could confirm or disconfirm it.

Add any requirement you think is missing, and correct any of the above you think I have mis-stated or run together — say which, and how.

Then answer three further questions. First, sort the requirements into non-negotiable ones and ones that are really the preferences of a particular camp (for instance, whether the demand that binding be non-brute is a genuine constraint or a smuggled physicalist scruple about brute facts). Second — this is the part I most want — identify any subset of the requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, and argue that it cannot be; if the list itself turns out to be a negative result about panpsychism, I would rather learn that from the specification than from a hundred referee reports. Third, name the single requirement that excludes the most candidates, and say why it has that much leverage.

Finally, apply the list briefly to three positions, describing each in your own terms: constitutive panpsychism that posits a fundamental phenomenal bonding law; cosmopsychism on which one macro-subject is fundamental and bounded subjects are derived from it; and a flat view that denies any binding fact at all and holds that a subject just is a relatively unified complex of micro-qualities. Two or three sentences each, against the requirements, with the failure mode if there is one.

Please use the following sections, in this order, with roughly these lengths:

- Purpose restated — about 80 words, in your own terms, so I can see whether the framing survived.
- Requirements — numbered, each with its motivating case, about 700 words in total.
- Non-negotiable versus camp preference — about 200 words.
- Joint unsatisfiability — about 250 words.
- The requirement with the most leverage — about 100 words.
- Scorecard — about 200 words; a small table is fine.
