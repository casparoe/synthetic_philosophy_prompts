I am drafting a position paper on the status of the continuum hypothesis for a philosophy-of-mathematics workshop, and before circulating it I would like a rigorous consistency audit of the commitments I intend to defend. I list them in no order of importance. I have not decided which of them I would surrender if forced, and I would prefer that you not guess at my attachments: treat every item as equally negotiable and tell me which of them cannot all survive.

The commitments.

(1) Truth determinacy. There is a single intended universe of sets, and every sentence of its language, CH included, has a determinate truth value that does not depend on what is provable from any particular axiom system. Independence results establish limits on proof, not indeterminacy in the subject matter, and I regard deflationary readings of them as confused.

(2) Anti-pluralism. The multiverse picture is at most a useful instrument for talking about models of a theory. I do not accept that there are many equally legitimate set-theoretic universes, and I do not accept that acknowledging a plurality of models dissolves the question of what is true in the intended universe.

(3) Extrinsic justification. Axioms beyond ZFC are justified by their consequences — by how much they settle, systematize, and unify — rather than by any direct intuitive evidence about the iterative conception. I have no use for the requirement that a new axiom be self-evidently true in order to be admissible.

(4) Large cardinals as paradigm. Large cardinal axioms are the best-justified extensions of ZFC precisely in the sense of (3): they resolve questions about definable determinacy and the regularity properties of sets of reals that ZFC leaves open, and they organize the rest of the subject into a coherent hierarchy.

(5) The Lévy–Solovay datum is a clue rather than an embarrassment. Since forcing of size below a large cardinal leaves its largeness intact, and cannot in the relevant cases manufacture such a cardinal from below, every large cardinal hypothesis is compatible with CH and with its negation. I read that silence as evidence that the structure of the continuum is a different subject matter from the large cardinal hierarchy — a lower floor of the building, so to speak — so that large cardinals' indifference to CH is exactly what a realist should have expected.

(6) Resistance to the continuum-specific axioms. Forcing axioms such as PFA and Martin's Maximum, and Woodin's ℙmax axiom (*), strike me as ad hoc: they look engineered for the questions they answer, and the fact that they entail 2^ℵ₀ = ℵ₂ does not seem to me evidence in their favor.

(7) Optimism about a settlement. CH is false, and I expect it eventually to be settled by continuing the program described in (4) — by axioms of the same general kind, justified in the same way.

(8) Uncharacterizability accepted. I concede that the intended universe cannot be pinned down by any categorical first-order characterization available to us, and I am untroubled by this: uniqueness does not require internal definability.

What I want from you.

- Identify which pairs or clusters genuinely conflict and which only appear to. I expect some apparent tensions to evaporate on inspection, and I would rather you singled those out than listed them alongside the real ones.
- For each genuine conflict, state what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me — not rhetorically, but in terms of which of the remaining commitments would then be left unsupported.
- Rank the conflicts by severity instead of presenting an undifferentiated list. If one is fatal in the sense that no repair short of abandoning the project is available, say so plainly.
- Tell me whether a single revision repairs the most, and whether, after that revision, the position is still recognizably the one I set out to defend — a determinacy realist who expects CH to be answered — or has silently become something else.
- If (5) admits a reading on which it supports rather than undermines (7), state that reading and say whether it is available to someone who also holds (3) and (6).

Please do not give me a general survey of the debate among platonists, formalists, and multiversists; I know the map. I want the audit of this particular package, with the reasoning shown at each step.
