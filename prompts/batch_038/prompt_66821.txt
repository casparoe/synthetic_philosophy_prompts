ok so some context: i'm one of five members of the zoning board of appeals in a mid-sized city (~180k people), and there's a case on our docket for the 14th that has split the board badly and I want to circulate something in writing beforehand so we're not just shouting at each other in the room.

The facts. Applicant owns a corner lot with a big 1920s single-family house, zoned R-1. She wants to convert the house into four units and build a two-story, six-unit building in the back half of the lot facing the alley — ten units total, with no new parking beyond the existing driveway. Our comprehensive plan says this is exactly what we want more of, and council passed an ordinance last year allowing two- and four-unit conversions by right in R-1, except within 500 feet of a property on the local historic register, where you need a conditional use permit instead. Her lot is 380 feet from one, so she needs the CUP, and she's also asking for a variance from the parking minimum of one space per unit, arguing the lot can't fit the required stalls without removing two mature street trees that a different section of the code protects.

The neighborhood association brought 40 people and a petition. Their objections, roughly in order of how much airtime they got: the back building would be visible from two front porches and would cut afternoon sun off one of them; parking will spill onto the street; ten units means more kids and more strain on the elementary school; and it changes the character of the block. Our code lets the board grant a CUP only on a finding that the use is "compatible with the surrounding area and will not materially alter the essential character of the neighborhood" — with no defintion of either term, no findings template, and no weighting of the factors. We just vote.

Two colleagues want to deny: a petition that size means the board has no business overriding the neighborhood, a denial gets appealed but the applicant can come back with something smaller, and an approval becomes the precedent for every corner lot inside the historic buffer. Two want to approve with conditions — cap it at eight units, require three stalls, cap the alley building at 24 feet. I keep feeling both sides are arguing past the actual issue, which is whose interests count as the neighborhood's: the people in the room own their homes and have been there twenty years, and the people who'd live in those ten units aren't there and never will be, because we meet at 2pm on a Tuesday. (The applicant's attorney also opens her cover letter by invoking the right to the city, which appears nowhere in our code, and I honestly don't know what a board does with a sentence like that.)

So: draft the written opinion I'll send the other four members. I need a real bottom line — approve, deny, or approve with named conditions, and if conditions, which ones and why those rather than the eight-unit cap — plus findings I could actually put in the record under the compatibility standard, and a time frame for whatever you're recommending (our rules give us 60 days from the hearing to issue findings or the application is deemed approved). Take the deny side's precedent worry seriously instead of waving at it, and tell me how the opinion should deal with the fact that the standard we're applying is close to unfalsifiable, i.e. any five people can find anything compatible or incompatible if they want to. And if you think the not-in-the-room problem is real but isn't something this board can fix on this record, say that plainly and say where it does belong — council, the plan, the notice rules, whatever.

700 to 1000 words, plain english, something four tired volunteers will actually read.
