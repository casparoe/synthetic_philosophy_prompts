I am at work on a novel whose protagonist directs the whole grants program of a small private foundation, and whose method — announced once, early, and never revisited — is to rank candidate causes by importance, tractability, and neglectedness. My editor has asked whether her record actually hangs together. In the manuscript to date she has ruled on five proposals:

- Funded: stockpiling and clinically preparing a broad-spectrum antiviral against a virus family that has never caused a human pandemic — small probability, catastrophic payoff, no other funder interested, and a concrete procurement path.
- Funded: a cheap sensor network for the early detection of a crop pathogen that has not crossed into the region but would destroy its staple harvest if it did.
- Funded: a small team attempting a speculative approach to a stubborn disease, with perhaps a one-in-twenty chance of a decisive result.
- Declined: scaling a well-evidenced, moderately beneficial health intervention in a district where a larger donor has already committed to paying for the entire scale-up.
- Declined: a diffuse awareness campaign on an issue she herself calls urgent, with no measurable mechanism and a dozen other funders already at work.

The climax turns on a sixth proposal: a permanent clinic-and-registry operation for a chronic condition — proven methods, substantial but steady benefits, no other funder presently involved, and an applicant who tells her plainly that a government program launching in two years will cover it if she declines, so that her money buys only two years of earlier treatment.

Which way should she rule if she is to remain consistent with herself, and which way do you predict she will rule? What decision rule do the five verdicts actually express — it need not be the three-factor one I gave her — what other rules fit them equally well, and how would each come out on the sixth proposal? What seventh proposal could I introduce that would force the surviving rules apart? And where her record genuinely permits more than one answer, tell me which commitment she would be adopting with each option, since that is exactly what my readers will take to be her character.
