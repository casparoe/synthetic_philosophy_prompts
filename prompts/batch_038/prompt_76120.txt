I'm in the last year of a philosophy degree and our metaphysics seminar has just spent three weeks on the unity of the proposition and Bradley's regress. For our revision session I want to build a sorting instrument: a decision tree that places a classmate at a position solely on the basis of how they answer concrete puzzles, so that nobody can navigate it by reciting a label they already like.

Five diagnostic questions, each posing a case with two or three options. I want these cases used: (i) the contrast between the mere collection {Faith, loyalty} and the claim that Faith is loyal — what exactly, if anything, is the extra ingredient; (ii) "Brutus stabs Caesar" against "Caesar stabs Brutus" — must the tie itself carry order and direction, and if so is it thereby a further item of the same kind as what it tied; (iii) a false proposition — does its unity require anything to obtain, and what becomes of it if not; (iv) the regress asked head-on: when the tie is added to the relata, does it need a tie of its own, and is the resulting series vicious, benign, or never started; (v) whether whatever solves unity could be had by someone who insists propositions are mind-independent. Every question must be answerable by someone who has never encountered the phrase "Bradley's regress."

Seven to nine leaves. They must include: the denial that propositions have constituents at all; unity taken as primitive or brute; unity explained by a predicative constituent of a fundamentally different logical type from what it applies to; unity grounded in an act of predication, mental or linguistic; unity by way of a fact-like structure whose tie is admittedly unexplained but claimed non-vicious; the dissolutionist line that only a sentence put to use says anything; and the pessimist verdict that the regress is vicious and so there are no propositions. Flag any pair of leaves your five questions cannot separate.

Return a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- `diagnostic_questions`: array of `{id, case, options}`, where `case` is the puzzle as it would be read aloud to a classmate and `options` is an array of `{key, text}`.
- `scoring_key`: array of `{answer_pattern, leaf_id, determinacy}`, where `answer_pattern` is a string like "Q1a Q2c Q3b Q4c Q5a" and `determinacy` is either "determinate" or "underdetermined" (used when a pattern leaves two leaves equally open).
- `leaves`: array of `{leaf_id, position_name, position_in_one_sentence, response_to_the_regress, mind_dependence, most_awkward_commitment, proponent}`. `response_to_the_regress` says how the view blocks, absorbs, or concedes it; `mind_dependence` is "realist", "mind-dependent", or "compatible with either"; `most_awkward_commitment` is one sentence on what the view is least able to say; `proponent` is a philosopher's name only where you are confident of the attribution, otherwise `null` — do not guess.
- `empty_cells`: array of `{answer_pattern, why_empty, explanation}`, where `why_empty` is "incoherent", "unattractive", or "unexplored", for patterns your questions permit but no leaf occupies.
- `inseparable_pairs`: array of `{leaf_a, leaf_b, why_the_tree_fails, question_that_would_separate_them}`.
