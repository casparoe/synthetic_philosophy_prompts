I direct the behaviour and pastoral programme at a state secondary school serving a deprived area, and my senior team is split between two accounts of what we are actually doing — accounts that correspond closely to the old disagreement between Mencius and Xunzi about human nature.

The first holds that pupils already possess the responses we want — distress at another's suffering, shame at their own conduct, deference, a sense of what is owed to whom — in crude and partial form; that our task is to nourish whatever shows itself and to help the pupil carry a response that fires in one situation across into the situations where it does not; and that an apparent absence of feeling is almost always the product of damage and neglect rather than of what the pupil arrived with. The second holds that the raw material is self-regarding and disorderly, that decent conduct is an artificial construction built from outside through rules, routines, exemplary models, correction and long repetition, and that expecting it to emerge if we merely draw it out is sentimentality.

Write a triage procedure my team could run on an individual incident: the questions to work through in sequence, what would settle each of them, which intervention each answer licenses, and the point at which the form should be set aside and the case referred to a human being because what remains concerns what we are trying to produce rather than what this pupil is like. It must be usable in twenty minutes by staff with no philosophical training, and it must mark every step at which the procedure is soliciting a judgement while pretending to collect a fact.

Then run it on three cases: a fifteen-year-old showing no trace of remorse after a serious assault; a pupil who is fiercely loyal and quick to outrage on behalf of friends but brutal to anyone outside that circle; and a pupil whose profile genuinely limits the relevant responsiveness, so that there may be very little to nourish. For the third, give me the procedure's verdict even if it prescribes the regime my staff find most distasteful.

Close with what the procedure cannot do: where the two accounts license the same practice, so that the disagreement ceases to be operational, and where it must be settled by something other than evidence. Draw on the reasoning of both positions, but write in the vocabulary of a school, with no citations or reading list. Please write in British English.
