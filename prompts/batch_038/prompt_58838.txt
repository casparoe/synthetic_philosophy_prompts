**THE AUDIENCE PROBLEM (draft, section headings are placeholders)**

*1. The usual setup.* Acausal trade is supposed to be a deal between two agents who can't signal each other or affect each other in any way, but who can each work out, by reasoning alone, roughly what the other one wants and roughly what the other one will do. There's no way to enforce the deal and no way to punish a cheater later, so the only thing holding it together is that each side reasons: if I do the thing that helps them, then somebody relevantly like me, over there, does the thing that helps me. Neither side is being generous. Both sides are just being consistent, and consistency happens to line up.

*2. The part I think gets skipped.* Everybody discusses how accurate the mutual prediction has to be. Nobody in anything I've read discusses how confident you have to be that there's anyone on the other end at all. That's a separate issue and I think a worse one. I could know in complete detail exactly what kind of agent my partner would be and still assign a really low probability that any such agent exists. And here's the strange part: that probability is the one feature of the trade that can never get better. In an ordinary deal you can gather evidence — watch them play a few rounds, check references, ask around, offer a small test transaction first. Here there's no possible observation that would count for or against the partner existing, because everything I could ever observe is on my side of the gap and they are by definition on the other side. So their existence works like a hypothesis I can only ever attach a prior to and never update. That turns the trade from a bargain into a bet on a prior, and a prior isn't something two parties can negotiate over, which is kind of the whole point of a trade.

*3. Granularity.* Second problem, maybe smaller. For the reasoning in section 1 to work, each side has to represent the other's *dispositions*, not just the other's goals. But any representation is a simplification, and the stuff that gets simplified away is disproportionately the stuff that determines what an agent does in unusual situations. So there's a squeeze: a model of you simple enough to be reliably run is a model that leaves out your weirdness, and your weirdness is exactly what settles whether you'd honor a deal that has no enforcement mechanism. If that's right then acausal trade should work best between agents who are similar in a shallow, easy-to-model way and wildly different underneath, which is close to the opposite of the "we trade with our copies" story people usually tell.

*4. An objection I'm not sure I survive.* A kid in my club said section 2 proves too much: if no observation can bear on whether the partner exists, then no fact about me can be evidence about the partner either, so the whole trade collapses and not just my point about priors. My answer was that he's conflating two things — the partner's existence is a claim about how the world is arranged, while the partner's behavior is a claim about logical structure, and I can learn about logical structure just by thinking carefully about what kind of reasoning I'm doing right now. I genuinely can't tell if that's a real distinction or if I'm just moving the problem to a nicer room.
