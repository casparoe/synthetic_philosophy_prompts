hey, I want a satirical piece and I have a pretty specific form in mind: the owner's manual / user guide for a commercially sold, fully automated nuclear retaliation system. Give it a bland product name and an invented manufacturer (make up a "customer assurance" team, a warranty department, maybe a quote from their head of product marketing) — the target here is a style of strategic discourse, not any real state or person, so keep it that way.

About 700-800 words, structured like a real manual: Intended Use, What's in the Box, Initial Setup, Operating Modes, Upgrade Tiers, Troubleshooting, Safety Warnings, Warranty and Limitations of Liability.

The important bit: the humour has to come from the deterrence theory being implemented *faithfully*, not from the machine malfunctioning or going rogue. No skynet gag. Every design choice the manual cheerfully explains should be the correct answer to a real credibility problem:

- it's fail-deadly rather than fail-safe, because being able to switch it off is itself a vulnerability someone could exploit, so the absence of an override is advertised as the flagship safety feature
- it retaliates on warning instead of on confirmation, because decision time is a scarce resource and waiting for verification means handing the adversary a first strike
- some of its responses are deliberately randomized or partially delegated, on the logic that a fully predictable retaliation schedule is a fully deterrence-proof one for anyone willing to do the arithmetic
- the tiers should enact the escalation/commitment stuff — base model, a "flexible response" add-on that lets you dial yields, and a premium package whose whole selling point is that there is genuinely no way to recall it once committed, including a footnote about what happens to the guarantee if your command authority is destroyed first

And the troubleshooting section is where I want the reductio to bite: it should include an entry for the case where retaliation was successfully executed but there's no surviving adversary leadership left to be deterred by the demonstration, with a helpful workaround and a cross-reference to the warranty. Similar entries for accidental launch attributable to a solar flare, and for a user who wants to *not* retaliate.

Deadpan throughout, no winking, no narrator asides, no "in a world where..." framing — the manual should sincerely believe it is being helpful and consumer-friendly. A deterrence scholar should be able to laugh and point at exactly which paradox each section is enacting (the rationality of irrationality, second-strike survivability, the problem of threatening something you'd never actually want to do after the fact, hair-trigger alert as a stability problem). Someone who's never read any of that literature should still finish it feeling deeply uneasy. If you want to nod at Schelling or Kahn do it in passing and paraphrase — no quotes, I don't want anything invented and put in quotation marks as if it were real.
