For eleven years I designed the sensor suites for a class of cheap, dumb, extremely numerous inspection drones, and last month I was laid off when the line was acquired by a firm that intends to let the existing fleet reproduce itself, with no designers left in the loop. I read philosophy of mind seriously as an undergraduate and never quite lost the habit, and this has reopened a problem I can't stop turning over; I've started writing fiction to work through it, and I'd like your help with one piece.

Write a short story of roughly 2,000 to 2,500 words about a self-replicating fleet of machines whose internal states get their meaning from what they were built to detect, and about what becomes of that meaning once the builder is gone. Keep the machines recognizably descended from the ones I worked on: unglamorous, repairable, replaceable, and far below any threshold of intelligence. Build in three pressures. First, one unit's detector, built to register a particular kind of metal fatigue, now fires just as reliably at a harmless mineral bloom that appeared after the atmosphere changed; nothing ever selected it for the new target, and it has never once been wrong, so whether it knows what it is looking at depends on its history rather than on its record. Second, a variant arises that nobody designed and nothing has yet selected for; it is by some margin the best unit in the fleet, and on the historical account its signals mean nothing at all. Third, after an accident one machine is reassembled part for part into something functionally identical that has no lineage behind it, and the colony cannot tell it apart from the outside.

Let the story argue nothing in dialogue: have the characters act on these commitments and make the reader do the reasoning. Give me a protagonist facing a decision on which the historical account of meaning and the purely correlational account score in opposite directions, and let that decision cost them something real. Do not use the words teleosemantics, proper function, or disjunction problem anywhere in the text, and do not have anyone explain the view. End on the decision rather than on an account of it.
