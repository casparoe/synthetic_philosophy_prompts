I serve as technical secretary to the Certification Panel of a national metrology institute. We issue certificates for opal-glass colour reference tiles. What makes our work philosophically awkward is that a certificate never reports only what we measured: it entitles every customer to treat the stated property as holding for each tile of the certified production line, including tiles not yet made. We are, in effect, in the business of licensing projections from examined samples to unexamined populations, and over fourteen years the panel has built up a body of rulings about which property statements may be certified as drafted. I must circulate a written recommendation before the next sitting, and I want it argued from our own record rather than from the general literature, which several members would find unhelpfully abstract.

Our rulings, as minuted:

1. Docket 11-04. Certified: "Every tile of the 14B melt line, measured and unmeasured, has CIE chromaticity coordinates x = 0.310, y = 0.316, within ±0.002, under illuminant D65." Forty-one tiles measured, all within range; melt recipe and annealing schedule documented and unchanged for a decade. Endorsed unanimously.

2. Docket 15-09. An applicant sought a certificate phrased in terms of a predicate it had coined for the docket, "W-2035": a tile is W-2035 if, measured before 1 January 2035, its x-coordinate is 0.310, and if, measured on or after that date, its x-coordinate is 0.348. Every tile then measured satisfied W-2035, so the evidential basis was formally identical to Docket 11-04. Refused. The minute records that, for tiles not yet examined, the extension of the predicate is fixed in part by when we get round to examining them. One member, who is given to it, calls this our own grue docket.

3. Docket 17-02. Tiles for assay had been selected by the applicant's in-line quick-check colorimeter, which rejects out-of-band tiles before they reach the sampling pool. Refused as drafted: the sample's membership had been produced by the very property being generalized. Certified on resubmission, once the draw was taken upstream of the screen.

4. Docket 19-06. Certified: "Every tile drawn from melts prepared after the June 2021 change of colourant supplier exhibits the nickel-related absorption feature near 420 nm; tiles from later melts will too." The predicate contains a date term. Endorsed, on the minuted ground that the date marks a change in how the tiles are made, not a change in how we look at them.

5. Docket 22-01. Certified a statement employing "metameric-under-D65", a predicate coined by the applicant eleven days before the sitting, with no projection history whatsoever. Endorsed: although newly named, the quantity is specifiable wholly in terms of apparatus long entrenched in our practice (standard observer, illuminant D65), and there is an accepted account of why the match persists across production runs.

6. Docket 23-04. Refused a statement asserting a property of tiles in storage when the only measurements available had been taken after an accelerated UV-ageing regime that the panel judged not to be a known monotone function of real-time storage.

The docket now before us is 25-03. The applicant manufactures tiles incorporating a fluorescent brightener, intended to calibrate colorimeters whose D65 implementation includes the UV component. The certificate sought reads: "Every tile produced under licence to specification T-9, whether or not it has been measured, lies within chromaticity band W when measured under D65 with the UV component included."

Disclosed facts: sixty tiles measured, all within band W, every one of them measured between two and five days after annealing. The melt recipe, brightener loading and annealing schedule are documented, and our chemists are satisfied there is a sound mechanistic account of why tiles leaving the line should fall in band W. However, the brightener photodegrades along a well-characterised curve: a tile measured more than roughly six months after annealing, or after cumulative UV exposure in service, falls outside band W. The applicant's own logistics data put the interval between annealing and measurement at a calibration laboratory between four and fourteen months. The sixty tiles came from the first three annealing runs, and the applicant's line-side quick-check screen, which rejects out-of-band tiles before dispatch, was operating throughout. The predicate as drafted contains no date term and no disjunction, and "chromaticity within band W" is among the most entrenched quantities in our practice. The applicant's representative argues that the six-month matter belongs to shelf-life labelling, not to the truth of the sentence we are asked to certify; one member argues that this is Docket 15-09 with the date term quietly deleted.

Please draft the recommendation I will circulate. It should:

(a) state a clear verdict — certify as drafted, certify subject to specified amendments, or refuse — and, if amendments, supply the exact wording the panel should adopt;

(b) identify which of our six rulings is the closest analog to 25-03, saying where the analogy holds and where it strains, and how far the sampling problem from Docket 17-02 bears on this docket;

(c) set out the candidate general principles that are each consistent with all six rulings. Members' remarks in Dockets 15-09 and 19-06 point in different directions, and I suspect at least three distinct principles fit the record. For each, state the verdict it delivers on 25-03;

(d) give your best reasoned guess as to which principle the panel is in fact running, and state what the panel would be committing itself to by deciding 25-03 either way, including the strongest point available to the verdict you do not recommend;

(e) propose one further docket, described concretely enough that the panel could rule on it, whose outcome would discriminate among the principles identified in (c).

Treat this as a document the panel could act on: roughly 900 to 1200 words, no survey of the new riddle of induction, and no bibliography — we know the classics, and what we lack is a decision.
