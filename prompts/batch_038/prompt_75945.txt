I own a duplex and my basement flooded pretty badly last spring, and now I'm in a dispute with the developer who built next door about whose fault it was. Both sides hired engineers, and the reports are like 60 pages each — hydrology models, rainfall return periods, contour maps, culvert capacities, I dont really know. My lawyer's whole approach is basically: let the two experts tear into each other's reports and the truth will shake out, because if one side is wrong the other side will find it and I'll be able to watch the error get exposed. We've done three rounds of this so far and honestly both sides look devastating every time. Each one points at some specific number in the other's report and explains in airtight-sounding terms why it's wrong, and then the other side explains why that criticism rests on a bad assumption, also in airtight-sounding terms. I'm not tracking who's right anymore, I'm just tracking who sounds better. One of their rebuttals was a chain of maybe a dozen steps about flow rates and pipe sizing where every individual step seemed reasonable to me but I couldn't hold the whole thing in my head, and I couldn't have said afterwards what it was actually arguing.

The moment that stuck with me: the two reports disagreed about a 1-in-100-year rainfall figure, one used something like 6.2 inches and the other 7.4, and they went back and fourth about it across maybe 40 pages of combined rebuttals. Then I paid a friend of mine who teaches statistics to spend a weekend on it, and she came back and said the lower number came from fitting a distribution to a rainfall record that stops in 1998, so it just doesn't include the last 25 years of storms at all. Took her most of a Saturday. Neither rebuttal ever said that, even though it's exactly the kind of thing that would have ended the argument in one sentence.

So is my lawyer's confidence justified, or am I just paying for two increasingly polished stories? I want to know when the let-them-attack-each-other setup reliably gets an outsider like me to the truth and when it doesn't, and whether there's something about the type of claim involved that makes it the difference — like, which disputes are the ones where a smart layperson can actually be moved by the exchange and which ones are just unfalsifiable to me no matter how good the rebuttals are. And then concretely: what should I do with my remaining budget before mediation, given that paying my friend's rate to check everything would cost more than the claim is worth? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning — no preamble, and don't restate my situation back at me.
