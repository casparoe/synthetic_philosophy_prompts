I don't have a precise story about where the threshold sits, but I'm confident its there.
2. There's no duty to bring happy people into existence, but there is a duty to prevent suffering — the asymmetry intuition, which I accept pretty much wholesale.
3. A quick, painless end to everything would be a better outcome than a future containing trillions of blissful lives plus a small remainder of people dying slowly and in pain. I actually bite the bullet here; I don't want you to tell me its repugnant, I want to know what it costs me.
4. Reducing the suffering of people who already exist always matters more than adding more happy people, no matter how many you add.
5. Wild-animal suffering is among the worst things going on. If we could cheaply reduce it — say by editing predators so they don't torment prey, or whatever mechanism you like — we'd be obliged to.
6. I have agent-relative constraints: I may not kill an innocent person even to prevent five comparable killings, and these constraints aren't just useful rules of thumb or an epistemic hedge about my own unreliable calculations. They're real.
