For the last fourteen months I have sat in an informal reading group of eight: three engineers, two biologists, one physicist, one historian of science, one lawyer. We took our vocabulary from Dennett. Roughly as we use it, an explanation is a **crane** when it lifts order or design upward from a base that could plausibly have generated it; a **skyhook** when it imports from above the very order it was supposed to account for; and guilty of **greedy reductionism** when it flattens a pattern that is genuinely present and genuinely calls for an account. Those glosses are ours, not canonical, and part of my problem is that we have never agreed on the decision procedure behind them.

We keep a ledger. Each entry records the group's label and, where we bothered, the reason:

1. A proposed pathway from self-organising prebiotic chemistry to the first self-replicating molecules, every step demonstrated in the laboratory — **crane**.
2. The bacterial flagellum accounted for by the foresight of a designer — **skyhook**.
3. A bird's flight explained by what the bird is trying to do, together with the aerodynamics of the wing and the neurology of the steering — **crane**.
4. The same flight explained by the bird's wanting to reach the tree, and nothing further — **skyhook**.
5. Altruism explained by group selection, with no account of how cheaters within the group are suppressed — recorded as a **provisional crane**, on the note that the shape of the explanation is right and only the evidence is missing.
6. The claim that moral experience is a fiction the brain secretes in order to coordinate behaviour, with nothing left to explain at the level of norms — **greedy reductionism**.
7. Dreams explained as brainstem noise plus the cortex's habit of confabulating a narrative, where the account predicts which sorts of narrative get confabulated — **crane**.
8. The low-entropy initial state of the universe as the explanation of the arrow of time — **crane, five votes to two**. The dissenters' recorded reason: nothing builds that initial condition up out of anything more basic, so it has the shape of a skyhook even though it does real work.
9. The applicability of mathematics explained by the brain's tracking mind-independent abstract objects — **skyhook**.
10. The consistency limits of a distributed data store derived from a proved impossibility theorem, which predicts precisely which guarantee must be surrendered in each configuration — **crane**, though two members abstained on the ground that a theorem is not an explanation at all. (This entry is mine.)
11. A small inventory of innate linguistic parameters, set by experience, as the explanation of grammar acquisition — **crane, with a warning**: the warning being that the parameters are themselves derived from nothing below, so the account may be a skyhook in waiting.

Next month's candidate entry comes from my own field, and it is the reason I am asking you rather than the group. A researcher I work alongside has published an account of why large trained models generalise compositionally to combinations they were never shown. Her claim is that gradient descent has installed a small number of general-purpose routines in the weights; she identifies them directly in a scaled-down model where the internals can be inspected, and her account predicts in advance which classes of unseen combination will succeed and which will fail. Three independent replications have since confirmed those predictions. She also holds that when people say the model "understands" the task, there is nothing further to explain: the phrase records our reaction to the routines, not an additional fact about the system. Her critics in the subfield object that she has not explained why the routines compose with one another to yield generalisation across an unbounded family of cases rather than a patchwork; she replies that composition is exactly what the identified routines do, and that the objection assumes the very thing she has already accounted for.
