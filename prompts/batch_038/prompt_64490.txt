I am drafting a position paper on the metaphysics of the quantum state and want the package audited before I commit to it in print. Here is where I have landed:

1. Wave-function realism: the quantum state is a concrete physical object, and it — rather than particles or fields in spacetime — is the whole of fundamental reality; the three-dimensional world of detectors, tables, and laboratory notes is derivative.
2. I am nonetheless drawn to the nomological reading, on which the quantum state plays the role of a law, structuring or generating the lower-level three-dimensional world rather than being another inhabitant of it.
3. I take the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph result to have settled that the quantum state cannot be a mere state of knowledge: it must be ontic.
4. I am an Everettian — no collapse, and the probabilities in the Born rule are self-locating uncertainty about which branch I find myself on.
5. I regard preparation independence as a substantive physical assumption rather than a harmless idealization, and I am generally sympathetic to retrocausal and superdeterministic escapes from no-go theorems.
6. In Wigner's-friend cases I want to say that the friend's recorded outcome is a fact about the world simpliciter, not a fact merely relative to the friend.
7. I am uneasy that positing a high-dimensional configuration space makes my ontology gratuitously profligate, and I would like to be able to insist that I have added nothing beyond standard quantum mechanics.

Please tell me which of these genuinely pull against each other and which only look as though they do. Order the real tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each say what specifically has to give, what the cheapest revision is, and what that revision costs me elsewhere. Then identify the single change that would clean up the most. I am not looking for a survey of the options: commit to a verdict about which of these I should abandon and argue for it.
