I am preparing a short talk for a reading group on decision theory and would like a consistency check on the position I plan to present before I commit to it publicly. Here is what I hold about geometric rationality:

(1) What makes a stake rational is its effect on the long-run growth rate of the wealth of the one agent who actually lives through the sequence of outcomes — the growth rate, not the expectation of wealth.

(2) Expected utility theory gets this wrong in principle rather than merely in its choice of utility function, because an expectation is an average over an ensemble of parallel copies of the decision-maker, and those copies do not exist; only the time average describes what happens to a real agent.

(3) It follows that Kelly betting is not simply expected utility maximization with a logarithmic utility function. The two frameworks differ in kind, and the fact that they coincide in the textbook repeated-betting setup is an artifact of that setup rather than evidence of equivalence.

(4) A genuinely one-off bet raises no new question: one should stake the Kelly fraction, because a single bet is just the limiting case of the same bet repeated indefinitely.

(5) The fraction one ought to stake on a given bet is fixed by that bet's own odds and win probability; it does not depend on what else is in one's portfolio or on how the sequence of bets happens to be grouped.

(6) Full Kelly is the correct criterion, and yet I would never bet full Kelly myself, because the drawdowns are intolerable and my estimates of the odds are unreliable.

Please audit this set: which commitments pull against one another, and how badly? For each genuine tension, identify the pair, say what would have to be abandoned or reworded, and say what the revision costs the rest of the position. Where a pair only appears contradictory and dissolves under inspection, say so explicitly and explain why, and do not count it among the tensions. Rank the findings from most to least serious, and where two problems share a single root, treat them as one rather than padding the list.

Format: exactly five numbered items, each of one or two sentences, in order of severity, with no introduction and no concluding summary.
