I have constructed an argument for a seminar that starts from Sen's result — that a minimal degree of liberalism cannot be combined with the Pareto principle and an unrestricted domain — and arrives at a conclusion I do not want, namely that individual rights as such are incoherent.

(1) A social decision rule is acceptable only if, for every profile of individual preferences, it yields a complete and transitive social ordering.
(2) The rule must be Pareto-consistent: whenever everyone ranks x above y, society ranks x above y.
(3) The rule must respect a minimal liberalism: at least two individuals each have at least one pair of social states over which their own ranking is decisive.
(4) By Sen's theorem, (1), (2) and (3) cannot be jointly satisfied.
(5) The paradox does not depend on which pair of states is protected. For any pair over which someone might plausibly claim decisiveness, one can construct a profile in which the meddlesome rankings of that pair by everyone else, chained together through Pareto steps, wreck transitivity; and the construction can be run again for the second claimant's pair.
(6) Hence for every possible assignment of protected spheres, a Pareto-consistent rule that respects those spheres fails (1).
(7) A right that no one could hold without making collective rationality impossible is not a right anyone can hold.
(8) Therefore talk of individual rights must be eliminable in favour of something else — weighted welfare, constraints on procedures, or nothing at all.

I suspect (5), but I cannot articulate what is wrong with it, and I am not convinced that repairing it would rescue (3): I also have it in mind that one can design protected spheres broad enough that any Pareto-consistent rule respecting them is dictatorial rather than intransitive, which would deliver the spirit of (6) by a different route. Tell me which step to give up and why it looked innocent. Then price the alternatives — what is lost by restricting the admissible profiles, by dropping the Pareto condition, by treating rights as trumps over a completed social ranking rather than as constraints on how that ranking is generated, and by accepting intransitivity as the standing cost of keeping rights? I would rather keep (3) than (1), but I want to know what that preference is worth.
