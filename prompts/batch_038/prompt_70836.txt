I write a weekly column on the science of ideas for a general-interest magazine, and this month I am covering the long-running quarrel inside the cognitive and evolutionary study of religion. For the piece I have commissioned an adversarial collaboration between the two camps, and I need you to draft the joint statement they could actually put their names to.

**Camp A — the adaptationists.** Religious belief and practice are not an accident of the mind's wiring but functionally organized. Supernatural monitoring and punishment, hard-to-fake costly ritual, and doctrines that bind unrelated individuals into cooperative coalitions conferred group-level advantages, and cultural group selection is the mechanism by which such traits spread and were retained.

**Camp B — the byproduct theorists.** Religious cognition is a spandrel. Hyperactive agency detection, a teleological bias, intuitive mind–body dualism, and a developed theory of mind generate religious concepts for free; concepts that depart from intuitive ontological expectations only minimally are the most memorable and most transmissible; religions are cultural constructions that parasitize these biases, and no adaptive history is required to explain any of it.

In my reporting I have found the two sides talk past each other along three axes in particular: whether "religion" names a single natural kind at all, rather than a heterogeneous family of practices bundled by modern European vocabulary; whether "function" and "adaptation" are being applied to genes, to culturally transmitted traits, or merely to the present-day consequences of a practice; and whether group-level benefits are causes of religious traits or downstream effects that then stabilize them.

Requirements for the draft:

- Every agreed claim must be substantive — something a partisan outside the collaboration would find informative rather than a platitude. If a claim can only be signed under a qualification, record the qualification rather than blurring the claim.
- Where the parties can jointly sign a claim about which explanatory question is being asked — mechanism, individual development, current function, evolutionary history — include it, and say explicitly which question each camp thinks the other is silently answering.
- Each disagreement must be stated three times: in wording Camp A would accept as a fair statement of its own position, in wording Camp B would accept for its own, and in a shared neutral formulation. Do not slant one camp's wording into the other's vocabulary.
- At least two cruxes must be ones each camp accepts as capable of telling *against its own* view; for those, state precisely what result would force revision and how much of the core thesis would have to go.
- Cruxes must be phrased as questions that could in principle be settled by evidence or argument, and each side's prediction must be recorded in advance, not left to be written after the fact.
- No citations, no reading lists, no attributions to named scholars or studies — I will add sources myself and I do not want guesses dressed as references. Where a claim would be much stronger with a specific number (an effect size, a prevalence, a date, a sequence in the historical record), do not invent one: write "[figure to be supplied]" and state what that figure would have to show.
- Do not adjudicate between the camps, do not write a diagnosis of why the debate is stuck, and do not let either side concede the other's core thesis. This is a document both could sign, with the residual disagreement made exact.

Format: begin with a preamble of no more than 150 words of plain prose that both camps could sign and that I could run as an epigraph. Then give everything else as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- `camps` — array of two objects: `label`, `core_thesis_one_sentence`.
- `agreed_claims` — array of objects: `id`, `claim`, `why_informative`, `qualifications`.
- `disagreements` — array of objects: `id`, `topic`, `camp_a_wording`, `camp_b_wording`, `neutral_formulation`, `kind` (one of `empirical`, `methodological`, `definitional`, `verbal`).
- `cruxes` — array of objects: `id`, `question`, `linked_disagreement_id`, `test_or_evidence`, `camp_a_prediction`, `camp_b_prediction`, `result_that_would_move_camp_a`, `result_that_would_move_camp_b`, `how_it_would_be_settled`.
- `verbal_or_definitional_disputes` — array of objects: `term`, `how_each_side_uses_it`, `what_would_resolve_it`.
- `residual_after_collaboration` — string: what genuinely remains in dispute once everything above is settled, and why neither side should expect that remainder to dissolve.

Aim for six to eight agreed claims, five to seven disagreements, and four to six cruxes, with every `id` cross-referenced consistently.
