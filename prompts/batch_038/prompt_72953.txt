I've been going down a rabbit hole on the statue and the clay thing and I think I've confused myself. I'm not a philosopher, I just read stuff on my own, and what I want to build is a decision tree that sorts someone into a position on what the statue actually is — but built out of cases and answer-combinations rather than out of a list of names, so that a person can answer every node without having ever heard of constitution, sortals, or four-dimensionalism.

Can you write it? Maybe 8 to 12 nodes, each one a question about a concrete situation, with the named positions at the leaves. Here's the cases I keep comming back to and want used: the squeeze question (flatten the statue, does the lump survive?); a knot tied in a rope, where you untie it and the rope is fine but the knot is gone; a snowman that melts into a puddle; a sourdough starter that gets fed and half thrown out every day; and my grandma's quilt where every single patch has been replaced one at a time over forty years.

Leaves I want to see: the statue and the lump are two different things in the same place; they're just one thing; the statue is a four-dimensional worm so the squeeze question never arises; there's no statue at all, just bits arranged statue-like; and there's no lump either. Then flag any two leaves your tree can't really separate, and for any combination of answers that nobody defends, say why — is it incoherent, just unattractive, or does nobody happen to be there yet?
