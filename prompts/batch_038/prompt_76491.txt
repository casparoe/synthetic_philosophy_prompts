so I'm writing the seminar paper for my metaphysics class on dispositions and I've gotten myself into a bit of a hole that I'd like you to help me think out of by just writing the thing — well, writing a version of it I can argue with. Here's the background as I understand it: the simple conditional analysis says something is disposed to M when S just in case, were it S, it would M. Martin's electro-fink is supposed to kill that (a wire that's live, hooked up to a device that would kill its conductivity the instant something touched it — so it's disposed to conduct but wouldn't). Then there are masks or antidotes, where the disposition stays put but something blocks the manifestation from showing up, like packing a fragile glass in foam so it wouldn't break when struck. And then Lewis's fix, roughly: x is disposed to M when S iff x has some intrinsic property that would be a cause of M given S, and among the closest worlds where x is S there's one where x still has that intrinsic property and M's. That clause about still having the basis is what's supposed to make room for finks without making the analysis vacuous.

My problem, which is the actual thing I want the essay to be about, is that all of these cases seem to quietly assume the manifestation is an *event* that happens pretty much right after the stimulus. But loads of dispositions manifest as processes with a duration — a sugar cube dissolving, iron rusting, a poison doing its damage in a body, a material burning rather than flashing. And once manifestations are protracted, there's a whole continuum of moments at which you could interfere, and I honestly can't tell whether the fink/mask distinction survives it. Example I keep turning over: drop the cube in water at t0, and at t2 minutes, when it's maybe 40% gone, a machine coats the remaining crystal in something inert (or the water gets swapped for a saturated solution). Is the cube still soluble? If you say the coating *removed* the disposition, that's fink-ish, but the removal happens after the stimulus, not as a pre-emptive switch, and half the manifestation already occurred. If you say the coating merely *blocked* the rest of the manifestation, that's mask-ish, but then "blocking" and "removing" start to look like the same interference described at different times, and the distinction stops tracking anything in the world. Worse, I think it might bite Lewis's fix specifically: his clause is about whether x still has the intrinsic basis in the relevant nearby worlds, which makes sense as a snapshot question at the moment of stimulus, but for a manifestation that's a process the question of whether the basis is "retained" is itself smeared out over time — retained at t2? at t5? through the whole dissolution? So maybe the cases where interference is most realistic are exactly the cases where the intrinsic-character clause goes indeterminate. A friend of mine thinks this is all a verbal dispute and that you can just index manifestations to temporal stages of the object and the distinction comes right back, and that finks and masks were never about timing anyway but about whether the interfering change is inside or outside the object. I don't buy it but I can't pin down why.

The question to settle is whether protracted manifestations show that the fink/mask distinction is a genuine joint in nature or an artifact of treating every manifestation as an instantaneous event — and, if it's an artifact, whether the damage lands harder on the simple conditional analysis or on Lewis's reformed one.
