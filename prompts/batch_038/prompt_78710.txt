I sit on the Behavioural Insights Review Panel of a fictional English unitary council, which must authorise every proposed nudge before it is piloted. Our terms of reference permit only interventions that are "transparent on reflection and compatible with the autonomous formation of the recipient's own preferences", but the panel has never defined either phrase, and we meet next Thursday.

The proposal comes from the schools catering service. From the autumn term, every pupil in Year 4 will be served the "green plate" — a vegetable-first main with water in place of flavoured milk — unless they opt out. Opting out requires a paper form signed by the pupil and a parent, handed to the catering officer in person within ten school days, and re-authorised each term. The letter home to parents sets out, in plain English, that the default and the effort required to leave it were chosen precisely because defaults work. That disclosure sits below a paragraph inviting parents to keep their child in the scheme so their class does not lose points in the school's behaviour competition, alongside a photograph of the headteacher. Success will be evaluated by the proportion of pupils still on the green plate at week twelve. The bid also pre-commits to a revision trigger: if opt-out exceeds five per cent at any school by week four, the letter will be rewritten to reduce it.

A parent group has objected that the proposal breaches both limbs of our terms of reference. I voted to approve at first reading and would rather not have been right for the wrong reasons.

Please draft the panel's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — of roughly 800 words. It should give a clear bottom line, any conditions specific enough to be operationalised by Monday, and an implementation time frame; and it should put the strongest version of the case against my preferred outcome rather than dismissing it. Close with a short annex marking which of the empirical or normative premises the decision turns on are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who know the literature, so that we are not relying on a false consensus. Invent no sources; I need no bibliography or citations.
