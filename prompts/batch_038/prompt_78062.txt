Context first: I get that the second law isn't really a *law*, it's statistics. Ice cubes melt because there are zillions more ways for the water to be spread-out-and-warm than to be a neat cold cube, so if you take the actual positions and speeds of all the molecules and ask "what is this arrangement most likely to do next," the answer is "melt." Fine, I'm on board. But then I tried running the exact same reasoning backwards and it blew up in my face. Here's the version I wrote down:

(1) The fundamental laws don't distinguish a direction of time. Any history of particles they permit, they also permit played in reverse.

(2) The only thing statistical mechanics adds on top of that is a counting rule: of all the microstates compatible with the macrostate you happen to find yourself in, the overwhelmingly typical ones are the ones that behave normally. And a macrostate is just a coarse-grained description — total energy, volume, the fact that there's a cube-shaped lump of cold stuff in the glass — so it contains nothing at all that says which way is past.

(3) So run the counting rule on right now. I'm sitting here with a half-melted ice cube. The typical microstate compatible with that is one where the entropy in this room was HIGHER a minute ago and will be higher a minute from now. A little dip. Not the tail end of a long downhill slide starting from a cube somebody froze an hour ago.

(4) Which means my own theory tells me the most likely story about this glass is that the ice spontaneously assembled itself out of warm water a few seconds ago, and that everything I seem to remember about the freezer is part of the same coincidence. Scale that up and it says my memories, the rocks, the light arriving from stars, all of it, are more likely manufactured than real.

(5) But every scrap of evidence I could possibly offer for statistical mechanics — thermometer readings, the fact that coffee cools down, the leftover radiation from the early universe — is itself one of those manufactured scraps.

(6) So the theory predicts that I can't trust the evidence that would confirm it. And a theory that predicts its own evidence is fake can't be confirmed by any evidence whatsoever.

(7) Therefore I shouldn't believe statistical mechanics. But if I don't believe it, step (2) was never licensed in the first place, and now I also have no account of why ice cubes melt. I'm stuck in a loop and I've somehow talked myself out of the past existing.

I obviously don't want to accept (7). The problem is I can't find the bad step, and that's the part that's bugging me.
