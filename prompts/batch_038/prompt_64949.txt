stuff I want folded in somewhere appropriate (dont just echo my bullets back at me as the list): why people keep following a convention when no one's watching and there's no live coordination problem at that moment — 3am, empty road, still driving on the right; how a convention gets off the ground from nothing when there's no pre-existing focal point to grab onto; why some conventions stay locked in even though everyone involved would prefer a different one and could switch, and the fact that the collapse when it comes can be extremely fast (QWERTY, and footbinding, which lasted centuries and then went away in basically a generation — I think any account owes us both halves of that); how a convention differs from a norm with actual moral weight behind it, and how either differs from a mere habit, a fashion, or a rule someone laid down on purpose; the fact that breaking a convention seems to license resentment and blame even in pure coordination cases where the defector cost nobody anything; and the fact that real people demonstrably don't have anything like common knowledge of what everyone else believes — a lot of the time they have flatly false beliefs about everyone else's beliefs and expectations and the convention runs anyway, which is the pluralistic ignorance business.
