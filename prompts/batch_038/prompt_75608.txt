We keep arguing in my group about whether imprecise credences -- sets or intervals of probabilities rather than a single one -- are ever the right tool, and I want the spec before we start arguing about particular proposals. So: write the desiderata for an account of rational belief under imprecise probability that is meant to be actually usable, say by a body that publishes confidence statements about low-evidence, high-stakes questions.

Give me the motivating case under each requirement. At a minimum cover: how a set of priors should respond to evidence (the urn whose composition you know nothing about -- after one draw, should your interval for the next draws colour narrow at all?); dilation, where learning something makes your interval wider rather than narrower; inertia, where you stay stuck at [0,1] no matter how much data comes in; what rule picks acts and what constraints that rule has to meet (something sure-thing-like, no dominated choices, consistency across time); whether the model has to be scoreable by proper scoring rules and what breaks if it isn't; and what it owes the people who have to report and defend the numbers publicly.

Sections, in this order: The list (numbered, one motivating case each -- call this half the response); Negotiable vs non-negotiable (a sentence per item, saying which camp wants which and why); Impossibilities (the part I care most about: which subsets of the desiderata can't all be satisfied, with a real argument rather than a shrug); Contenders (three sentences max on maximality, E-admissibility and gamma-maximin). A table is fine anywhere it helps.
