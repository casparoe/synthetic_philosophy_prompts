I am convening a graduate seminar on the direction of time, and one session is devoted to the question of where temporal asymmetry comes from at all. Before that session I should like my students to have a comparative assessment of the candidate answers in front of them, and I would be grateful if you would supply it.

Consider the following seven accounts of why the world exhibits a temporal asymmetry. Unless an account explicitly denies it, take the underlying microphysical laws to be time-reversal invariant.

(A) The Past Hypothesis: the arrow rests on a brute, unexplained boundary condition — the very early universe was in a state of extraordinarily low entropy — together with a statistical postulate to the effect that such a macrostate is realised by a typical microstate.

(B) Cosmological-entropic: the arrow rests on the extreme homogeneity of the early matter distribution, which, given gravity, is very far from equilibrium; the growth of structure (clustering, stars, black holes, radiation) is therefore the entropy-increasing process, and expansion merely sustains the departure from equilibrium.

(C) Two-headed: there is no global arrow. Entropy increases away from a central low-entropy region in both temporal directions, and the inhabitants of either wing call "the past" whichever direction entropy decreases in.

(D) Derivation without a special past: the second law follows from Hamiltonian dynamics, Liouville's theorem and a coarse-graining, since coarse-grained entropy is non-decreasing for essentially any initial condition; no privileged boundary condition is required.

(E) Interventionist: the asymmetry lies not in the physics but in our epistemic and agential situation — we can influence the future and not the past — and this asymmetry of records and manipulability generates the thermodynamic, causal and psychological arrows alike.

(F) Collapse-based: irreversibility is fundamental. A genuinely time-asymmetric process in state reduction breaks the symmetry, and the thermodynamic arrow is downstream of it.

(G) Microphysical: the CP-violating, and hence (given CPT invariance) T-violating, behaviour of neutral kaons and similar processes supplies a fundamental arrow which could in principle be amplified to the macroscopic level.

For each account, assign two numbers between 0 and 1. The first, coverage, is the fraction of the family of familiar arrows — thermodynamic, radiative (retarded rather than advanced waves), cosmological, psychological (memory of the past but not of the future), causal and counterfactual, and measurement — that the account genuinely derives rather than relabels or restates. The second, smuggled, is the fraction of the asymmetry in question that the account simply posits as an unexplained primitive, or covertly presupposes in the very way it is articulated. Then rank the seven by net yield, which I stipulate to be coverage minus smuggled, and say how you break any ties.

Three further requirements. First, the ranking must be discriminating: at least one of these accounts cannot earn the coverage score its proponents claim, and I want you to identify it and explain precisely what is missing from the derivation, rather than merely noting a difficulty in principle. Second, you may judge that two entries are not really distinct accounts; where you do, say which of the pair you have scored and why the other adds nothing. Third, for whichever account you place first, state in a sentence what would have to be true for it to lose that place, and name the account that would then take it.

Begin with the ranking, in order, with both scores attached to each entry: that is your bottom line, and it should occupy the first sentence or two of your response. Give the reasoning account by account thereafter. No preamble, and please do not restate the question back to me.
