I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I have a feature on cross-cultural moral psychology coming due that I would rather get right than merely plausible. Before I commit to the spine of the argument, I want to test it against someone who knows the literature properly, so I am setting out the reasoning as I currently hold it and asking you to push on it.

Here is the version I have been working from. Henrich and his collaborators established that the subjects underlying nearly all published psychology are Western, educated, industrialized, rich and democratic, and that those subjects turn out to be genuine psychological outliers rather than a convenient stand-in for the species. The behavioural experiments run in small-scale societies seem to me to make the same point about morality in particular: in the ultimatum and dictator games, offers and rejection rates differ dramatically from one community to another, some groups punish unfairness expensively while others let it pass without protest, and the magnitude of those differences tracks variables like market integration and religious adherence rather than anything you would call biological. From that I have been concluding that fairness is a local cultural construction with no stable cross-cultural core, which in turn seems to knock the supports out from under the idea that there is a single correct morality that our judgements can be right or wrong about, and to imply that psychology should stop talking about human nature altogether, since the only generalisations it has actually produced are generalisations about one peculiar slice of humanity.

There is one loose thread I cannot tie off, which is that the instruments used to demonstrate all this variation — the games, the vignettes, the translated survey scales — are themselves imported from the very populations being declared atypical, and I am not sure how the evidence avoids proving more than I want it to.

What I would like is an assessment of that chain: does each link hold, and where does it break? In particular, what do the cross-cultural game results actually license about the uniformity or otherwise of fairness intuitions, as opposed to what they license about standard self-interest models; what does the WEIRD critique imply about universals in moral psychology as distinct from universals in the specific findings psychologists have reported; and what, if anything, follows for moral objectivity from descriptive variation of this kind? And since I do have to write something, tell me what the defensible version of my claim would sound like and what I should say in its place.

Please answer in continuous prose, the way you would write to a colleague in an email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold or italic emphasis, and no tables.
