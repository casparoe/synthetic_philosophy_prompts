In my formal epistemology seminar we have just worked through the accuracy-based argument for probabilism: any incoherent credence function is accuracy-dominated by some coherent one, so a non-probabilistic agent is guaranteed to fare worse, by the lights of the chosen scoring rule, no matter which world turns out to be actual. Our problem set asks us to probe how much of that argument is carried by the constraint that the scoring rule be proper, and I would like a concrete counterexample rather than a general discussion of the constraint. Please define an explicit numerical scoring rule on the smallest algebra that will do the job — a single contingent proposition and its negation — such that some incoherent credence function is never accuracy-dominated under it and in fact scores strictly better than every coherent alternative in every possible world; show the arithmetic that establishes this; and then identify which standard conditions on accuracy measures your rule violates, together with an assessment of whether a defender of the dominance argument can rule it out without simply assuming probabilism from the start.
