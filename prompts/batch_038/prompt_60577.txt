so I keep going back and forth on Christiano's HCH setup — Hugh with a box that answers any question by doing exactly what Hugh himself would do if he had the box, all the way down — and I want to pressure-test it against a real instance of a huge cognitive task getting factored into little pieces. The case I have in mind is the first edition of the OED: Murray taking over in 1879, the public appeal for volunteer readers, everybody sending in slips of paper with a quotation and a date and a source, sub-editors sorting them into pigeonholes, Murray drafting entries from the sorted slips, the whole thing not finished until 1928 with Murray dead thirteen years earlier. Somewhere north of a million slips by the end, I think? doesn't matter much. There's also the Broadmoor inmate who sent in tens of thousands of them.

I want the mapping done properly, not vibes. Be explicit about who Hugh is in this story — Murray? the Philological Society? the dictionary itself treated as an agent? — what the leaves are, what question each leaf is actually being asked, and where the recursion bottoms out. Then run it in both directions.

First: where does the OED look like HCH, and does it deliver HCH's actual selling point, which as I understand it is that the top-level answer stays inside the consulter's own judgment precisely because every sub-question is one he could have answered himself. My sense is that it doesn't, and that this is the interesting part rather than a problem with the analogy. Murray's leaves were not copies of Murray. They were amateur readers, specialists he fired off letters to, and one certified lunatic, and the system worked because they knew things he didn't and had no way of knowing. HCH buys its fidelity guarantee by making the tree homogeneous; the OED bought its reach by making the tree heterogenous. If that's the right way to see it, is the OED a counterexample to HCH as an idealization of deliberation, or just a different animal that HCH was never trying to cover? Say which.

Second: I've been carrying around two distinctions I half made up and I want you to either use them or break them. Call the first one the sampling ceiling — a factored system can only return what its leaves were told to go look for, so decomposition buys throughput and not reach, and it's structurally blind to absence: the dictionary can say a great deal about whatever someone happened to copy out and nothing at all about the more typical uses nobody copied out. Call the second leaf-honesty versus leaf-reliability — HCH gets honesty for free and never has to say anything about reliability, while basically the entire OED editorial apparatus was reliability machinery. Are those two genuinely separate, or is the second just a special case of the first?

And please tell me where the theory goes quiet instead of making it talk. The A entries are famously baroque and the later letters much thinner because they ran out of time and money, which means the leaf-level specification changed partway through the recursion, and Hugh wasn't there at the end to be the consulter at all. Does HCH have anything whatsoever to say about that, or does it assume it away?

Bottom line first, two or three sentences on whether HCH survives this case as a model of deliberation, then the mapping. Hard cap of 800 words and no bibliography — I'm not going to look anything up.
