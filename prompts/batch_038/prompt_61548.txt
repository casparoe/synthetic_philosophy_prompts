You are a philosopher of language who also writes professional satire for a general-audience outlet — someone who has read Wittgenstein closely enough to reproduce the pressure points of an argument structurally rather than by quotation, and who writes comedy that a specialist judges fair rather than cheap. Think carefully before drafting; where my brief is underdetermined, make a decision and commit rather than asking me clarifying questions.

Some context for the request. I have been a reader of the rationalist blogs and forums for close to two decades, and one of the recurring genres there is the introspection report: people calibrating their own self-reports, arguing about whether "I feel X" is evidence about X, building elaborate personal vocabularies for inner states and then treating those vocabularies as data. Consumer neurotech and mood-tracking wearables have converged on the same genre from the other direction — earnest, measurement-flavored prose about states that only the wearer can attest to. I want a piece that puts the two in the same room.

The assignment: write a parody of a self-tracking company's public release notes for its flagship product, escalating across six versions, with a support-forum thread appended and a short FAQ sidebar. Roughly 800 words total. Deadpan throughout. Call the company Inward and the wearable the Inward Band; invent the founder, the feature names, and the version numbers yourself.

The target is a position and a register, not a person: the view that a person's own attestations about their inner states constitute a private observational science with a ground truth accessible to no one else, together with the corporate vocabulary of measurement, calibration, validation, and data quality in which that view now dresses itself. The satire must exaggerate nothing that is not already there; the comedy comes from Inward being rigorously consistent about commitments that real products hold only loosely.

The escalation I want, which you should treat as a specification rather than a suggestion:

1. A fixed published taxonomy of twelve states, cross-user norms, and the reassurance that the user's entries improve a shared model.
2. Custom tags — but still pooled and anonymized across the network.
3. A "Personal Lexicon" update in which tags stop syncing; the stated rationale is that public labels contaminate the signal.
4. A recurrence feature that asks the user to mark a state each time it returns and instructs them to attend to it closely and impress the association on themselves, so the app can recognize it next time.
5. A privacy pivot in which the user's own attestation is declared the ground truth against which the hardware is validated, accompanied by a request that users stop asking whether they are using their tags consistently.
6. An admission that Inward has never opened the sealed sensor housing, and a reasoned argument that this cannot matter.

Include, somewhere in versions 5 or 6, a validation study Inward is proud of: near-perfect agreement between band readings and user self-report, with a methods note revealing that the self-reports were collected after the readings were displayed to the users.

The support thread should carry the two beats the changelog cannot. One user asks how they can tell whether they are tagging correctly and receives a reply to the effect that if it seems right to them it is right, followed by a moderator's clarification that this is precisely why the question does not arise. A second user reports that they now anticipate the band's reading before it appears and asks whether this confirms their tags are accurate; support should treat the correlation as vindicating the practice while leaving the question of correct identification wholly untouched.

Hard constraints. Do not name Wittgenstein, the Philosophical Investigations, or the private language argument anywhere in the piece itself, and do not cite section numbers. Do not quote any philosophical text verbatim — paraphrase or re-voice everything in Inward's register. Never state the objection: the reader should feel that something has gone wrong without being told so, and there should be no asides, no parenthetical nudges, no authorial wink. Inward's copy must remain sincere, and each release must be a genuine improvement by Inward's own stated metrics. It should still be funny to someone who has never read philosophy, as tech-marketing satire: scope creep, "we heard you," A/B tests, privacy rebranded as a feature.

Format: version headers and bullet points for the changelog, then the forum thread in the flat style of a real support board, then a four-item FAQ sidebar.

Before the piece, give me a design note of no more than 200 words listing, for each of the six versions and each of the two support beats, which move in the argument it encodes and what makes the encoding structural rather than merely verbal.
