Next Thursday I've got 75 minutes in my Moral Status seminar — twelve second-years who have just done Singer and Regan and mostly decided Regan won — and I have to teach Nozick on animals. Don't explain Nozick to me; plan how I should explain him.

What I want them leaving with is the odd double structure: the line he draws between being utilitarians about animals and Kantians about people, plus rights coming in degrees tracking capacities, so animals are still under a constraint but a weaker one than persons. Tell me what has to be in place before that isn't heard as just speciesism with extra steps — side constraints? the seperateness of persons? ends-in-themselves? — and what depends on what, including whether I can afford to open on animal cases at all or whether that poisons it.

Then give me the one case that carries the session. My instict is a pig-versus-person case where the human interest is trivial, but I worry it hides the aggregation problem entirely, so say what the case has to have and what it has to lack, and replace mine if it's the wrong instrument.

Then the misconceptions, each with the exact point in the sequence where I head it off: that being utilitarian about animals lets factory farming through whenever the numbers work out; that weighting rights by capacity is speciesism by another name; that he's Regan with hedging; that where the line falls is a question for ethology to answer. Say whether the utility monster is worth five minutes here or should be refused out loud, and what else I leave out and announce.

Finally, one closing question that separates the students who got the structure from the ones who learned the slogan.
