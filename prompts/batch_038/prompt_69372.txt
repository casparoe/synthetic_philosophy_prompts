I am preparing a graduate pragmatics seminar on the status of the Cooperative Principle, and I need a purpose-built case rather than another textbook illustration. Please construct a thought experiment to the following specification.

**Purpose.** The case is meant to press a question about what kind of posit the Cooperative Principle is: a psychological one, concerning what the speaker intends her hearer to recover, or a normative and attributive one, concerning what a speaker who has chosen these words in these circumstances can be held to have meant. A case that settles this by fiat — by reporting the speaker's intentions in the setup, or by having a character announce the implicated content — fails however vivid it is. The implicature must be generated by the machinery, not stipulated.

**Specification.** Build a single exchange with all of the following properties:

(a) It occurs inside an institution whose official purpose is the exchange of information — a deposition, an audit interview, a regulatory inspection, a disciplinary hearing — so that it is not a game, a market negotiation, or any setting where the parties have agreed in advance that nothing is owed.

(b) One participant has openly, and with mutual knowledge, opted out of being informative: she has announced, or is operating under a publicly known standing instruction, that she will answer only what is asked, volunteer nothing, and correct nothing. Both parties know this, and know that the other knows it.

(c) She says only what she believes to be true. No lying, no misleading by assertion, no equivocation on the literal content of her words; the maxim of Quality is not the lever.

(d) She has no intention whatever regarding the content the hearer goes on to recover. She is not insinuating, not managing an impression, not preserving deniability for later use; she is mechanically discharging a rule in which she has no personal stake. The absence of that intention must be visible in the setup rather than asserted.

(e) The hearer nonetheless arrives at a specific, determinate proposition — not a vague sense that something is being withheld, but a content she would afterwards report as what the other party was implying, and would be prepared to act on.

(f) That content is false, or at any rate unsupported: the minimal answer had an innocent explanation.

**Deliverables,** in this order:

1. The scenario: one paragraph of about 150 words, including the question asked and the answer given, plus enough of the institutional setting for (b) and (d) to be independently visible.

2. The derivation. Write out the hearer's reasoning step by step in the standard Gricean recipe — the presumed observance of the principle and the maxims, the apparent shortfall in the contribution, the background assumption required to repair it, the conclusion — and mark exactly the step at which the recipe runs out of licensed premises given (b).

3. The diagnostics. Apply Grice's characteristic marks of conversational implicature (cancellability, nondetachability from the form of words, calculability, nonconventionality, indeterminacy) to the recovered content, with a verdict and a one-line reason for each. Flag any test that comes out differently here than it would in an ordinary scalar or flouting case, and say why.

4. The two readings. State what the case amounts to on the psychological reading and on the normative reading, which you think it favors, and what turns on the choice — including whether the principle's own relativization to the accepted purpose or direction of the talk exchange settles the matter, and if so at which level that purpose is to be read: the institution's, or the individual participant's adopted strategy.

5. The repair. Show how an account that presumes only that the utterance is worth the hearer's processing effort, rather than that the speaker is being cooperative, handles the same exchange: does it derive the same content, a different one, or none?

6. The load-bearing feature. Identify the single feature of the case doing the work, and say what happens when it is removed — in particular, what the case collapses into if the public opt-out in (b) is deleted, or if the speaker in (d) is given the insinuating intention she lacks.

7. The defense. Give the strongest response available to someone who wants to keep the case inside a Gricean framework, including the option of saying that what the hearer has is not an implicature but strategic reasoning about a rule-follower, and state what that response costs.

No citations, bibliography, or reading list; I want the argument, not the references. Roughly 900 words in total, with the scenario kept short and the analysis doing the work.
