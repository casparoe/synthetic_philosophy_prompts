A couple weeks back at a meetup two people got into a long argument about a proposal that was in front of some permitting body out where one of them lives — a company had petitioned to run a small field trial of a gene drive against an invasive mosquito on an island where its been devastating the native forest birds. I mostly listened, and I don't remember the specifics of the actual proposal, which I don't think matters for the argument anyway. Roughly:

(1) Dana said it should be denied. A gene drive can't be recalled once it's out, so you're taking a bet where the downside is permanent and the upside is just "fewer mosquitoes," and precaution says you don't take bets like that.

(2) Ray said that was selective. Letting the invasive mosquito keep going is also permanent — some of those bird species are down to a few hundred individuals and will be gone within a decade. So precaution applied consistently forbids the denial just as hard as it forbids the release, which means precaution isn't actually doing the work Dana thinks it is. Something else is.

(3) Dana said the real difference is burden of proof. The island ecosystem has millions of years of testing behind it; the drive has a few years of lab work and some modeling. When one side has that kind of track record and the other has none, the one with none has to prove safety, not the reverse.

(4) Ray said "millions of years of testing" was a tell, because that same ecosystem currently contains a recently arrived mosquito that's doing extremely well by its own lights — being tested by evolution isn't a safety certificate, it just means the risks are familiar ones. And he said Dana had quietly slid between two different claims: precaution as a rule about what to do under uncertainty, and precaution as a rule about who has to convince whom. Those come apart, and only one of them would actually decide the vote.

They both dug in and it never resolved. Can you go through (1)–(4) and rate each move — clearly right, partly right, clearly wrong — with a line of justification each, then say who's more right overall and what neither of them brought up? I've read the standard line that precaution is somehow self-undermining, but I couldn't tell whether that's what was actually happening here or whether it's a different problem.
