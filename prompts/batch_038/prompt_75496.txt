So I'm putting together a proposal for a one-year fellowship and I've got maybe two thirds of an idea that I need turned into an actual document.

The idea: everybody in the Hilbert's hotel literature knows the standard moves. The hotel is full with infinitely many guests, a new guest shows up, everyone shifts up a room, and now you supposedly have a contradiction between "the hotel was full" and "the hotel has a room free." The people who treat this as a reductio against actual infinities lean hard on two things at once — the impossibility of completing an infinite by successive addition, and the complaint that the hotel lets you add guests without the collection getting any bigger. I think those are two very different arguments that have gotten braided together, and the braiding is doing most of the load-bearing work. Traversability is about ordinal structure and about whether a process can be finished; the "you can't add to an infinite" complaint is cardinal — and ℵ₀ + ℵ₀ = ℵ₀ is a theorem of the very set theory everyone in this debate already accepts, so as it stands that complaint just looks like a refusal to do the arithmatic.

My suspicion is that once you strip out the traversability stuff and the arithmetic-denial, there's a third residue worth taking seriously: the hotel seems to license an increase in a non-cardinal sense (there are more guests, in the sense that matters to the manager's ledger, which grows without bound even though occupancy stays in bijection with the rooms) while the cardinality sits still. Maybe the right way to cash that out isn't with cardinality at all but with some notion of "how many have been added" that isn't cardinal-valued. Whether that third argument can stand on its own feet, or whether it collapses back into one of the other two, is what I'd want to spend the year on. There's also a side question I keep circling and don't know what to do with: does anything change if the hotel has uncountably many rooms instead of countably many, since the reductio people usually state the thing for the countable case and I'm not sure the ledger intuition survives going up.

Turn that into the proposal. I need: a sharp statement of the question — not "can there be actual infinities," something with a determinate answer that I could get wrong; why the existing positions don't settle it (I can gesture at the reductio crowd, at the standard set-theoretic reply, and at the finitists and intuitionists who reject the framework the whole debate runs on, but you need to say what's left over after those three have spoken); the method month by month, including where I'd have to actually work through the mathematics rather than wave at it; what a positive result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and how you'd handle the side question about the uncountable case — fold it in, defer it, or tell me to drop it.

And be honest about the two ways I think this could go wrong, because I'd rather the proposal meet them than have a referee find them: (i) it turns out to be a philosophy-of-mathematics question with no consequences for the cosmological and metaphysical debate it grew out of, and (ii) a competent set theorist reads it and says the "non-cardinal increase" just is the familiar gap between cardinality and order type, so there's no third notion to find and I've reinvented ℵ₀ + ℵ₀ versus ω + ω. Say up front what I'd do in each case — is there a version of the project that survives (ii), or should I be building an exit ramp into it?

No bibliography and no lit review by citation, I'll add all that myself; just name positions and arguments where you need them. And at the end, tell me what you'd cut if the fellowship turns out to be six months instead of twelve.
