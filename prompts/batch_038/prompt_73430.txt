Write a short story of roughly 1,000 to 1,500 words that dramatizes a philosophical problem instead of explaining it.

Setting: a small walled city whose economy is audited four times a year by an unglamorous office. The office measures how closely the actual distribution of holdings matches an officially approved pattern, and whenever the measured distribution drifts past its tolerance, it corrects the drift by a single general adjustment. For decades these adjustments have been painless and everyone considers the system fair. Then a puppeteer begins performing in a side street, charging one copper to watch, and her audiences grow week after week.

Requirements:

- The transactions that disturb the pattern must be entirely voluntary and above board — no theft, fraud, coercion, or exploitation — so that the only thing objectionable about them is what they add up to.
- Build the plot around the city's discovery that it has exactly two options: prohibit such exchanges in advance, or keep intervening after the fact to undo their cumulative effect. Show that each option imposes a cost on someone in particular, paid in person and in detail.
- Tell it from the point of view of the auditor who must draft the recommendation. The case for preserving the pattern should be genuinely felt by this character, not a straw man; likewise the case for leaving the transactions alone.
- Include one scene in which the auditor tries a gentler, narrower fix and watches it fail for reasons that follow from the situation rather than from anyone's malice.
- No character may state the moral aloud, and no philosopher, book, or theory may be named. The argument should be legible only through what happens.
- End on the auditor's decision and one concrete, immediate consequence of it.
