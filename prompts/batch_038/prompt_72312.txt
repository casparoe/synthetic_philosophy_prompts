Two notions are routinely run together in discussions of natural kinds, although they originate in very different projects: Wittgenstein's family resemblance, introduced by way of games, and Richard Boyd's homeostatic property cluster (HPC) account of kinds. Your task is to determine how much these notions actually have in common.

Begin by separating them. A family-resemblance grouping is characterized by a network of overlapping and crisscrossing similarities among its members, with no single feature common to all of them; the account is descriptive and makes no demand about why the similarities hold together. The HPC account also posits a cluster of properties that co-occur without a shared essence, but it adds an underlying causal mechanism — a homeostatic process — that maintains the co-occurrence and thereby makes the cluster reliable enough to support induction and explanation. Set out as precisely as you can what philosophical work is performed by this second component. Is the appeal to a mechanism a substantive addition, one that changes which groupings qualify as kinds and explains why some resemblance clusters earn their keep while others are arbitrary? Or is it largely a metaphysical gloss on the bare fact that a given resemblance cluster has proven reliable, such that a family-resemblance picture supplemented by ordinary claims about reliability would deliver the same verdicts? Where the two accounts genuinely diverge, argue for one of them; do not settle for a symmetrical reconciliation.

Then test the divergence against a case built to strain it. Suppose a large video-streaming platform, without anyone designing a taxonomy, ends up with a stable category of titles that reliably hold subscribers past a fixed point in the opening episode. The category has no defining feature: its members share only overlapping properties of pacing, episode length, casting, genre convention, and scoring. Yet the category is actively maintained, because the platform's own engagement-driven recommendations and renewal decisions feed back into which such titles get commissioned, reproducing the property cluster year after year. The category also pays off epistemically: membership licenses accurate predictions about completion rates, and the platform can raise completion rates by steering production toward the cluster.

Address three questions about this case. First, does it qualify as a kind under the HPC account, and does it qualify under a purely family-resemblance picture? Second, if the two accounts agree here, does that indicate the mechanism clause is doing no independent work in cases of this shape — and if they disagree, which verdict is the correct one and what turns on it? Third, the mechanism maintaining this cluster is partly constituted by the classifier's own behavior, so the grouping is reflexive in a way that paradigmatic chemical or biological kinds are not. Consider whether this reflexivity defeats the HPC account's claim to be picking out something mind-independent, or whether it merely relocates a worry that also applies to ordinary biological kinds, where our classificatory practices influence which populations persist.

Conclude by stating which of the two notions, if either, supplies what scientific realism needs from the concept of a natural kind, and what it must give up in order to do so.
