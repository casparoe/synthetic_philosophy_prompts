so, fourth year here, and in three weeks I'm presenting at a climate ethics workshop. the room is mostly normative ethicists who do policy-adjacent work — they've all encountered the nonidentity problem in passing, basically none of them are population ethics specialists — plus two economists who build integrated assessment models and are openly allergic to what they call metaphysical framing, and one Kantian who thinks aggregation is the original sin.

my thesis: the nonidentity problem isn't the deep structural scandal it gets treated as in climate ethics, it's a scope problem. sketch of the argument so you can see where the joints are:

1. for any realistic mitigation decision the implementation horizon is short enough — call it under sixty years — that a large share of the affected population exists in both the actual branch and the counterfactual branch, because the policy doesn't shift their conception timing at all.
2. for that subgroup it's a plain same-people case: counterfactual comparison works, the harm is specifiable, you don't need any impersonal principle to get to a verdict.
3. the genuinely identity-shifting effects only bite past that horizon, and by then the branch-shared harms have already done the normative work.
4. therefore the person-affecting restriction gets you the right answer on near-term climate policy, and nonidentity is a residual puzzle about the deep future, not evidence our moral thinking is broken.

I already know step 2 and step 4 are in tension — if counterfactual comparison is what's load-bearing in 2, why does it go silent in 4 instead of just getting vaguer. I have an answer but it isn't great and I don't want to be ambushed by it.

what I want is a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense of the paper. give me the eight or so objections this particular room will actually raise, ordered by how much damage each would do, phrased the way the person raising it would phrase it — so the economists sound like economists worried about double counting and discount rates, not like philosophers. for each one: a reply I can deliver standing up in about ninety seconds, an honest note on where that reply is softest, and what I should concede out loud before they have to drag it out of me. then separately, tell me which one is the question I'm praying nobody asks. my guess is that its the arbitrariness of the sixty year cutoff, but I may be wrong about that and I'd rather you tell me the real one.
