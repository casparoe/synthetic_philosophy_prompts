I am in the third year of a PhD in philosophy of biology, co-supervised by an aquaculture health and welfare group, and I am applying for a twelve-month interdisciplinary fellowship with a panel deadline next month. I should like you to turn my half-formed idea into a two-page proposal, roughly 1,200 words, in the register a research council panel would expect.

The idea is this. Welfare assessment in aquaculture runs on indicators: cortisol-type stress physiology, opercular rate, avoidance learning, analgesic self-administration, preference tests, and, for decapods, behaviours such as rubbing, guarding and threat displays. Those indicators acquire their evidential force inside a network — a background account of what welfare consists in, calibration assumptions linking the measure to that account, and a body of validation work carried out in particular species, overwhelmingly teleosts and a handful of mammals. Practice and policy then extrapolate the indicators to taxa such as penaeid shrimp, lobsters and crabs, whose nervous systems are organised around a ventral nerve cord with segmental ganglia rather than anything like the centrally integrated architecture of the teleosts in which the indicators were developed. Statutory recognition has run ahead of that epistemic work: the Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 brings decapod crustaceans and cephalopod molluscs within its scope, and Swiss rules require crustaceans to be stunned before being killed. The extrapolation is already load-bearing.

My suspicion is that the interesting question is not which properties make an animal sentient — that debate is well developed and, I think, largely orthogonal here — but what licenses the transfer of a measurement from one taxon to another. I want to frame it in the vocabulary of the psychometric tradition: an indicator has construct validity only relative to a nomological network, and in aquaculture welfare science that network is built in one place and applied in another. I have three failure modes in mind. Please treat these as my own coinages and stipulated labels rather than established terms. First, mechanism divergence: the same behaviour is produced by non-homologous machinery, so the inference from behaviour to affective state does not travel, and a measure may be locally valid while being globally misleading. Second, benchmark bias: validation work in teleosts silently sets the standard of what a competent indicator looks like, so candidate measures that would do real work in decapods are scored as inferior rather than as different in kind. Third, and most serious, double-counting: substantially the same evidence base is used both to establish that decapods are sentient and to calibrate the indicators that measure how well or badly their lives go, so the two inferences are not independent, and a regulatory practice that treats them as independent looks self-confirming.

Please write the proposal so that it contains: a precise statement of the research question, phrased such that it has a determinate answer rather than collapsing into general scepticism about cross-species inference; an account of why the existing positions leave it open — property-based criteria for sentience, calls for further species-specific validation studies, and precautionary decision-theoretic treatments that deliberately bracket the epistemology — together with an argument for why each of those is the wrong framing to fund; the method, stage by stage across the twelve months, saying concretely what I would do at each stage (analysis of validation practice as it is actually reported, structured comparison of two or three indicator–taxon pairs, formulation of explicit transfer criteria, and a test of those criteria against cases where they ought to bite and cases where they ought not); what a successful result looks like both as a philosophical claim and as something a welfare scientist or regulator could use, and what would count as the project failing — I have in mind, for instance, that the transfer criteria emerge either so strict that no cross-taxon inference is ever licensed or so permissive that they exclude nothing; and an honest assessment of scope, saying what must be cut to fit twelve months and what would make the project unmanageable.

Two constraints on form. Do not include a bibliography or reference list; refer to positions, arguments and frameworks by description and I will supply citations myself. And do not let the proposal rest on empirical findings I would need to verify: it is a philosophy project, and the panel will expect to be told why philosophy rather than further ethology makes progress on this question.

Finally, a request about your own stance. Because I shall have to defend this framing in interview, tell me plainly where you think it is most vulnerable: whether the double-counting charge survives scrutiny or dissolves once the two bodies of evidence are properly separated; whether the measurement-theoretic vocabulary genuinely illuminates the problem or merely relabels it; and which single consideration, argument or line of reasoning would most likely change your mind about whether this question deserves funding at all. If you think a neighbouring question should be substituted, state it and argue for the substitution rather than simply flagging it.
