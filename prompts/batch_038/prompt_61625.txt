I have been asked to put my position on long-horizon decision-making in writing for a workshop where the others will attack it, and I would like a consistency check before I circulate it. Here is what I hold. (1) Wealth dynamics are multiplicative, and for such processes the growth rate an agent actually experiences through time comes apart from the average taken across outcomes; maximising an expectation is therefore a category error rather than a difference of taste. I take this from the ergodicity argument associated with Ole Peters, and I regard it as a mathematical fact about the dynamics, not a normative commitment I could trade away. (2) For repeated bets I stake the growth-rate-maximising fraction, in practice half of it, because I never know the true odds; I do not think that discount is a surreptitious return to expected utility. (3) I reject expected utility theory outright: there is no utility function over wealth whose expectation anyone ought to maximise. (4) When colleagues call Kelly exotic I tell them it is nothing of the sort, that it is simply logarithmic utility, and I offer that as a point in my favour rather than a concession. (5) A bet with negative expected log growth should be refused even when it is offered exactly once, at a small stake, with a large positive expected monetary payoff; repetition is not what does the work. (6) I nonetheless evaluate my charitable giving by expected value rather than growth rate, giving a fixed share of income each year and choosing between charities on expected lives saved per pound, on the grounds that this money is spent rather than compounded. (7) I have also written that expected-value reasoning is the right frame for an institution holding a diversified portfolio of many independent opportunities, and I stand by that.

Tell me which of these genuinely pull against each other and which only look as though they do, ranking the real tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and commit yourself on the single revision that would clean up the most and what it would cost me. I am not asking for a survey of the literature or for references. Please answer in continuous prose, as you would in a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
