Our bioethics reading group does a thing where each person circulates a one-pager on where they actually stand before the session, and mine is on suffering and biotech. I'd like a check on whether the list below is jointly tenable before I send it, becuase I accumulated these over a few weeks and I have a feeling I've been collecting things that each sound reasonable on their own.

1. What makes a pain bad is what it feels like, and nothing else. Not what it's for, not what it teaches you, not how it fits into your life story.
2. So an episode of agony that nobody ever learns about, in somebody who forgets it straight after and gets nothing out of it, is exactly as bad as the identical agony that eventually produces something wonderful. Same amount of bad.
3. I'm broadly with the abolitionists: suffering is the thing to phase out with biotech rather than manage indefinitely, and the place to aim is the population-level hedonic set point, since people adapt back to baseline after almost any individual fix.
4. I accept something like the standard asymmetry — the absence of pain counts as good even when there's nobody around to enjoy that absence, whereas the absence of pleasure is only bad when there's somebody it deprives.
5. And at the extreme there's no exchange rate between the two: a life of unbroken torment isn't redeemed by any quantity of bliss stacked on top of it.
6. But I don't buy the world-destruction business. A world of unbroken bliss containing one stubbed toe is not worse than no world at all, and I won't say that it is.
7. I think the you-cannot-survive-without-pain objection is overrated. Not stepping on a hot stove needs information plus some aversive pull; it doesn't need agony, and arguably doesn't need felt badness at all, since you could engineer the pull without the suffering.
8. Even so, I think it would be wrong to deliberately create a creature incapable of pain, even one we could guarantee would be perfectly protected and never injured once.
9. On timing I'm a cautious conservative: no germline work until we understand a lot more, even though an enormous amount of suffering is happening right now.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture on the state of the debate. Go through and say which of these genuinely pull against each other and how badly, and which pairs only look like they conflict. For each real tension, say what has to be given up or reinterpreted to fix it and roughly what that costs me — including whether the fix leaves me holding a position anyone actually holds, or just relocates the problem. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and finish by naming the single revision that would clean up the most. I dont know where the cracks are and I'm not attached to any particular item, so don't strain to preserve all nine.
