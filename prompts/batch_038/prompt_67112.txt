I run a meetup in my town, eight to ten of us around a table every other week arguing about god and stuff, and two of the guys have been at each other for close to three years now and it's stopped being productive. Here's the thing: they're on the same side. Both are christians, both think you can argue from the universe to god. They just each think the other one's argument is garbage and is the main reason atheists win.

The first one, call him D, only cares about the beginning version. Universe started, whatever starts to exist has a cause, so there's a cause, and he thinks the cause has to be a mind because of something about it being outside time and having to choose to make the thing. He says the other argument is hopeless because it rests on that big principle that everything has an explanation, nobody can establish that principle, so you've built the house on sand. He also leans on some cosmology theorem about universes that expand still needing a beginning.

The second one, call him R, thinks the beginning stuff is a trap. He says even if the universe had been going forever you can still ask why there's anything at all rather than nothing, and that D has tied the whole case to science results that could get walked back in ten years — he says D reads way more into that theorem than it says. He also thinks D's claim that you can't have an actual infinite is just an assertion nobody outside his camp signs onto.

I've been trying to referee and I'm bad at it. What I want is the document they should have produced together and never will: a joint statement, like an adversarial collaboration, I heard that term somewhere, that both of them could actually put their names to.

Give me:

1. A numbered list of claims both could sign. Not mush, real claims. I think there's more overlap than they realise, including stuff about what either argument can and can't get you even if it works, and about what the cosmology does and doesn't establish.

2. A table of where they still disagree, with each disagreement stated twice — once in wording D would accept as a fair statement of his own view, and once in wording R would accept for his. If a row turns out to be an arguement about what a word means rather than about the world, say so right in the row and say which word.

3. The cruxes: the handful of sub-questions where settling it would actually move at least one of them. For each one, the argument or thought experiment or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, and each side's prediction, written down in advance like you're locking it in before anyone sees the answer.

My own guess, push back on it if it's dumb: half their fight is about how much of that everything-needs-an-explanation principle you really need. I have a feeling there's a weak version, just about the dependent kind of thing and not about literally everything, that R would accept and D would still hate, and I want that handled properly instead of hand-waved. I also suspect the did-it-begin question and the does-it-have-an-explanation question aren't as separable as they both act like.

Definitely include as cruxes whether the beginning premise is needed at all, and whether a cause that's outside time can be a person who decides to do something.

No book lists and no philosopher names for me to go look up, I won't, and half the group wouldn't either. If you need a technical term, give it to me in one plain sentence first. Keep it to something I could read out loud in about twenty minutes.

Last thing: tell me where you think this document fails. Which of the agreed claims you'd bet one of them refuses to sign, which crux you suspect is fake, and what would change your mind about the whole framing — including if you actually think one of these two arguments is dead and no honest joint statement can pretend otherwise. Be straight with me, I can take it.
