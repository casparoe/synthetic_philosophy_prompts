My half-formed idea: Dennett's ladder of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian and Gregorian creatures gets taught as four levels of increasing cleverness, but I dont think the rungs are actually sorted by one criterion. The first three each introduce a new selection mechanism — genes picking behavior, reinforcement picking it within a lifetime, then an internal model that lets a creature try out options without acting on them. The Gregorian rung is defined by something else entirely: where the design work lives, out in culturally inherited artifacts rather than inside the animal. Sort the tower strictly by selection mechanism and the Gregorian level seems to vanish — it just looks like Popperian creatures with a much richer model — while a real gap opens up underneath it for animals that build and revise external scaffolds but cant inherit them. Sort it by where the design lives instead and the Skinnerian and Popperian levels collapse into each other. So maybe there are two seperate axes smuggled into one ladder, and maybe that's what makes the taxonomy feel both obvious and shaky at the same time.
