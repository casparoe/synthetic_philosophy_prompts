I've been reading the anthropics threads on the rationalist blogs and forums for maybe fifteen years now — doomsday argument, sleeping beauty, halfers vs thirders, the whole SSA/SIA business — and I've come to think SSA vs SIA is the cleanest example I know of a argument that produces exactly zero convergence no matter how much ink goes into it. Both camps are extremely smart, both accept Bayesian conditionalization, both accept the same physics, and after a couple of decades nobody has converted anybody. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Not a survey of the positions, and not a verdict on who's right.

Here's the thing I keep going back to. I made up a test case that I think strips out the usual escape routes. Two cosmological theories, T1 and T2, identical in every physical respect inside our horizon and identical about every observation we could in principle ever make. They differ only in a global parameter that controls how long inflation runs in regions we could never see or verify anything about, and T2 implies roughly a million times more regions like ours, each populated by observers with experiences and evidence exactly like ours — so the total observer population, and the fraction of it that has your evidence, both scale up by the same million. The standard complaint about the presumptuous philosopher, that SIA lets you settle cosmology from your armchair because the numbers involved are absurdly huge, doesn't get any purchase here: a million is big but not ridiculous, and none of the extra observers are ones we could ever meet. When I ran this in a thread it did what these threads always do. The SIA people said you should now be about a million to one on T2. The SSA people said no update at all, since the reference class is the same in both theories and your evidence has the same likelihood within it. A third group said the question isn't even well posed until you specify a measure over the multiverse. Nobody moved an inch and honestly I don't think any of them were being stupid.

My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the fight isn't about probability at all. Both sides conditionalize correctly on their total evidence; they disagree about what their total evidence is — specifically, whether the fact that an observer with these experiences exists is itself a datum to condition on, or a presupposition that gets factored out and carries no evidential weight. On that reading SSA and SIA are one and the same rule applied to two different accounts of first-person evidence, and no cleverer decision theory or betting argument can separate them, because the disagreement sits upstream of the calculus. Alternatives I can see: its largely verbal, about 'observer' or 'reference class'; or there's a hidden physics/metaphysics question doing the work, namely whether a physical theory even determines an observer count; or they're bedrock intuitions about self-location that neither side can argue for, and the impasse is just what bedrock looks like from the inside.

So which is it? Argue for the diagnosis using what the two camps actually say and do in cases like mine, and use the halfer/thirder fight as a cross-check, since I suspect that's the same fault line in a much smaller setting where people think there's a right answer. Then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a new species of thought experiment, a reframing of the question — or whether the honest expectation is that this never resolves and why not. And if my test case is malformed, or I've misstated what one of the camps is committed to, lead with that, because a diagnosis of a strawman isn't worth anything to me.

One last ask: please keep straight which parts of all this are settled among people who genuinely work on it versus which parts are live disputes where informed people disagree. I'm sure there are several of the second kind. Flag them as contested rather than sliding your preferred view — or mine — into the seat marked consensus.
