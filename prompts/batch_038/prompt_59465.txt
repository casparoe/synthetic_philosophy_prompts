I'm second year on a philosophy PhD, working on consciousness, and our little reading group has got itself properly stuck. My supervisor is some flavour of Russellian monist and he keeps saying the synchronic version of the combination problem has been made precise enough to be answered — micro-experiences plus a fundamental phenomenal bonding relation, on the rough analogy of how nucleons bind into a nucleus, and then you have your macro-subject — and that we should stop banging on about it. I don't believe him, but I've realised I can't articulate why in a way that isn't just an incredulous stare, which is the one objection everybody already agrees is worthless. What I think is actually going on is that the interesting version of the problem isn't synchronic at all. It's about time. The bonding relation, as it gets described, binds *contemporaneous* micro-subjects into a macro-subject at a time — but my experience right now isn't a string of instantaneous snapshots stitched together by memory. It has temporal shape built into it: I hear three notes as a phrase, I see the fan as turning rather than as a rapid series of static blades. So somewhere in there is a diachronic combination problem that nobody has to face if the case is set up right, and I want it set up right.

So here's what I need from you: build me that thought experiment, to order. Constraints. The scenario has to be entirely mundane — somebody hearing the tail of a short tune as a single phrase, or watching a ceiling fan, or catching a ball — no surgery, no devices, no telepathy, no exotic physics; the underlying physical story should just be the story of a normal awake human brain doing something unremarkable. The scenario itself gets one paragraph and no more. It has to work *inside* constitutive panpsychism's own commitments, i.e. microphenomenal properties as the categorical bases for the dispositional/structural facts, plus a bonding relation doing the composing — don't smuggle in extra machinery the view hasn't signed up for, and especially don't solve the problem by quietly assuming from the start that the bonding relation already has a diachronic form. If diachronic bonding is needed, run that as a second, explicitly labelled version of the case and tell me what it costs. Hold the physical-functional description completely fixed across the views so that the only thing changing is the compositional story. Then do the check: say what constitutive panpsychism with synchronic-only bonding gives, what it gives with diachronic bonding, and what priority cosmopsychism gives — the view on which the whole, cosmic subject is fundamental and my bounded subject is some kind of limitation or de-combination of it rather than a composition of smaller ones. State which of these can actually account for the temporal character of the experience and which can't, and derive the verdicts from their own commitments rather than from me asserting them. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens if I pull it — specifically, if micro-subjects are allowed to *persist* over time rather than being momentary, so the very same micro-subject is present at both ends of the phrase. Does the case collapse into the ordinary synchronic problem, or does it survive? And then the honest bit: give me my supervisor's strongest attack on the case, not a strawman, and say whether that attack costs him anything he'd rather keep.

Two more things. First, spell out what the case is *for* and what would count as cheating — I want to be able to see where a bad version of this design would just be stipulating the verdicts in fancy dress. Second, I know everyone immediately reaches for the specious present and for retention/protention talk from the phenomenology tradition, and I know that's a live resource here; but say up front whether invoking it helps the constitutive panpsychist or just pushes the problem down a level, because if micro-experiences themselves have temporal thickness then each of them has its own diachronic combination problem, and I need to know whether that's a vicious regress or a legitimate stopping point. Please write the whole thing in British English — I'm at a UK department and I'll be circulating it to the group. Also keep it tight: these people have a habit of writing three pages of scene-setting before they get anywhere, and I'd like to model the opposite.
