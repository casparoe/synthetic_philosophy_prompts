Our Chinese philosophy seminar keeps rehashing Mencius vs Xunzi on human nature and nobody budges. The textual facts arent in dispute: Mencius points to the sprouts — the involuntary alarm you feel at a child about to tumble into a well, plus shame, deference and a sense of what's fitting — and says *that's* what nature is. Xunzi says if you actually let people follow their inborn desires you get grabbing, strife and the collapse of any shared order, so ritual and teachers have to straighten us the way warped wood gets straightened on a bending frame. Both think anyone can end up a sage, and both prescribe roughly the same cultivation program. So what kind of disagreement is this? My hypothesis is that it's mostly verbal: they slice up "nature" differently, Mencius keeping only what's distinctively human and spontaneous, Xunzi counting everything you have before training, so that once the definitions are pinned down they'd agree about most of the psychology. Is that right, or am I flattening a real clash — e.g. over where ritual comes from, or whether raw desire can be transmuted into moral motivation without something arriving from outside? Argue it from what each side actually says, then tell me what would count as progress here: a distinction, a rephrased question, some evidence, or nothing at all. Hard cap: 450 words.
