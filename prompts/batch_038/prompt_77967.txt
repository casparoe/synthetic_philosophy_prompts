I've read Meditations on Moloch more times than I'd care to admit, and the thing that's never sat right with me is that basically every multipolar trap example that gets passed around — the AI speed race, antibiotic overprescribing, overfishing, ad spending, exams — is doing the exact same structural work. There's a real payoff to defecting, everyone takes it, done. Which makes the whole genre feel less like a deep puzzle and more like a prisoner's dilemma with extra steps and better prose.

So here's what I actually want. Give me an example of a race to the bottom where there is no payoff to defecting. Specifically, a setting where:

- the good outcome is a strict Nash equilibrium — if everyone's doing it, no single actor gains anything at all by switching to the bad behavior, and
- the population ends up stuck in the bad outcome anyway, and
- the entire reason is noisy observation. Nobody's being short-termist, nobody's got a weird discount rate, nobody secretly prefers the bad thing, there's no enforcement cost and no principal-agent problem. Each actor just can't tell what the others are doing, or can't tell what state of the world they're all jointly in, and that measurement error by itself is enough to tip everyone into the worse equlibrium and hold them there.

Ideally it's a concrete-ish real domain rather than a toy matrix, and it is not one of the usual suspects I listed above. Then walk me thru the mechanism step by step: what exactly is being observed badly, what each actor infers from their degraded reading, why the individually sensible response to that inference is the collectively awful one, and — this is the part I most want — what the minimal fix to the observation channel looks like and why nobody can just buy it.

And if you think the whole request is incoherent and I'm asking for something impossible, say so and argue it. I half suspect that I am, and I'd much rather get the argument than a made-up example that quietly violates one of the constraints while sounding confident.
