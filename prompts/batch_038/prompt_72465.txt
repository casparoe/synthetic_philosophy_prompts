Please compose a sestina — six six-line stanzas followed by the customary three-line envoi — on the dispute over whether what the intentional stance ascribes (beliefs, wants, intentions) is genuinely there in the world or is only a convenient way of talking.

Use the strict end-word permutation of the form. If the line-endings of the first stanza are numbered 1 2 3 4 5 6, the succeeding stanzas run 6 1 5 2 4 3, then 3 6 4 1 2 5, then 5 3 2 6 1 4, then 4 5 1 3 6 2, then 2 4 6 5 3 1, and the envoi takes all six words two to a line (one standard pairing is 6 with 2, 1 with 4, 5 with 3). Two properties of this scheme should carry argumentative weight rather than serve as decoration. First, no new end-word is ever introduced: the poem's whole terminal vocabulary is fixed in its first six lines and thereafter merely rearranged, so it becomes unavoidable to ask whether the later stanzas discover something in that material or only impose an order on it. Second, the permutation is a single closed cycle — each of the six words occupies each of the six line-positions exactly once across the poem, and the opening order would return in a seventh stanza the form does not permit. Make that withheld seventh stanza felt.

Choose your six end-words so that two belong naturally to the vocabulary of matter and law, two to that of function and construction, and two to that of belief and desire, and so that at least one of them (something with the flexibility of *state*, *position*, *level*, or *map*) is equally at home in all three. The rotation must then drag words across registers, so that a term introduced as a piece of hardware ends up doing psychological work, and vice versa.

The object of the poem should be a machine playing chess, and the occasion a trivial physical mishap — heat, a sagging current, a joint worked loose — that destroys something the machine believed about the position it was in. Let the poem work through the three ways of predicting such a machine: by ascribing beliefs and rationality, by appeal to what it was built to do, and by appeal to its physical constitution. A reader who knows this material should be able to say which stanzas are operating at which level, and should recognize what the mishap is meant to show: that the physical account of the event is complete and localizes the fault, yet contains nothing that could be called the loss of a belief, while the belief that vanished cannot be dismissed as mere shorthand, since the machine's play changed.

The envoi should enact compression — six words, three lines, nothing added, nothing left out — and leave the reader holding the question of whether such a compressed description is a discovery about the machine or a convenience of the describer. Do not resolve it by fiat; do not name the three stances, do not name any philosopher, and do not quote. Aim for accuracy of substance: a philosopher of mind should find the poem right, and a reader who has never encountered the argument should find it a good poem.
