You are, for purposes of this exercise, a senior philosopher who works at the intersection of moral responsibility and criminal law theory and who has sat through several hundred law-school workshop question periods; I want that ear, not a survey of the literature. Think carefully before drafting, and begin with a short paragraph identifying what an audience of this particular composition treats as substantive and what it treats as philosophical decoration — then produce the prep sheet itself. No citations, references, or reading lists: I want your reconstruction of the objections in their own voices, since a bibliography would be of no use to me in the room.

The setting: in three weeks I present a paper at a criminal law theory workshop at a law school, a series co-sponsored with the philosophy department but dominated by the law side. Expect roughly ten criminal law and evidence scholars (two former federal prosecutors, one career public defender), a legal historian of urban policing, two punishment theorists who are broadly retributivist, and a scattering of students. Most will have read the paper. Almost none work on blame as such; they regard standing as a term of art with a settled legal life — Article III standing, third-party standing, unclean hands in equity, selective-prosecution claims — and their first instinct is to ask whether I am using the word in anything like that sense or merely borrowing its prestige. They are impatient with second-personal vocabulary, they press hard on any claim that cannot be operationalized or adjudicated, and they want to know what, if anything, follows for the criminal process.

The thesis is deliberately provocative, and I need the objections targeted at it rather than at the general idea that hypocrites are badly placed to criticize. My claim is that standing to blame, in the public norm-affirming register rather than the register of private resentment, is not a property of the blamer alone and not exhausted by the blamer's relation to the blamed; it is partly a property of the enforcing community's record with respect to the norm invoked. The argument runs: (1) blame in that register speaks for a shared practice of holding one another to a norm, and so borrows a mandate the practice confers; (2) the mandate is conferred only where the norm has been administered as a roughly even-handed instrument of accountability, so a norm that has functioned for a sustained period as a tool of asymmetric subordination — enforced hard against one group, winked at or ignored in another — no longer supports the claim; (3) because the mandate is held jointly rather than severally, it can be depleted by what other members do with the norm, and personal blamelessness does not restore it, since one cannot invoke an authority one's community has already spent; (4) therefore a member of that community who has never violated the norm, never enforced it, and indeed protested its asymmetric use still lacks standing to blame members of the targeted group for violating it, so long as the asymmetry is unacknowledged and unrepaired. I add two qualifications the paper concedes are load-bearing: the deficit is indexed to a specific norm and a specific group rather than global, and it is repairable by acknowledgment and redress, though I am vague about who must do the repairing. I also lean on a metaphor of my own devising, that the community's authority to hold to account is a kind of normative commons susceptible to depletion, and I distinguish throughout between blame being fitting and blame being in a position to be voiced, insisting the wrongdoer remains fully blameworthy.

Deliverable: about eight objections ordered by the damage each would do if I fumbled it, rather than by the order in which they will arrive, each phrased the way its likely raiser would phrase it — say which sort of person in the room is asking — and each followed by a reply I could actually deliver aloud in two minutes. For every reply, tell me where it is soft: where it quietly assumes a boundary I have not earned the right to draw, where it retreats into the private-resentment register in a way that would look evasive, or where it buys consistency at the price of making the view extensionally empty. Flag separately what I should concede in the first ten minutes to buy credibility, and which single reply has to land for the paper to survive the afternoon. Finally, give me the question I am hoping nobody asks — the one that would force me either into a claim about victims' standing or into admitting that, on my own premises, essentially no one in a society like ours has standing to blame anyone for much — together with the least bad answer available to me, and an honest assessment of whether the least bad answer is bad enough that I should soften the thesis before the paper is written up.
