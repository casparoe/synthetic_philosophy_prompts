Okay, this has been rattling around in my head for a couple years and I finally decided to ask someone who might actually know the literature. I do the scheduling and back-office stuff for a small chain of dental clinics — not glamorous — and there's a practice we do that I've never been able to trace to a source. Every Friday afternoon we pull next week's appointment list and re-verify each patient's insurance coverage one at a time, manually, in the portal, even though the software now flags coverage problems automatically at booking. When I asked about it my first month, two different coworkers separately told me that my predecessor (who retired in 2019 and I can't get hold of) had said there'd been a rough week back in 2009 and this was what kept it from happening again. Nobody currently on staff was here in 2009. And here's the part I want a word for: people aren't doing it mindlessly. When I push, they give me what sounds like a completely sensible reason — eleven people do this every week without fail, so it must be load-bearing, or it would have been dropped long ago. And honestly that reasoning isn't stupid, it's correct given what they can actually see. But eleven people doing it isn't eleven observations. It's one person's bad week, copied eleven times, and the copies now count for more then the original ever did.

I keep running into the same shape everywhere once I started looking. My brother's band has a hard rule about never taking a certain type of gig, which every member defends by pointing at how firmly the whole local scene agrees it's a bad idea; when he actually dug into it, the agreement traces back to one promoter who stiffed one band around 2014, and nobody who repeats the warning was around for it. Same thing on forums — some claim treated as thoroughly settled because everyone says so, and if you scroll back through the archives everyone says so because everyone else says so, and the number of people who independently checked anything is maybe two. A friend of mine who reads a lot of rationality blogs kept saying "herding" at me but I don't think that's quite it, because herding is about people not thinking.

So: is there an established term for this, in whatever field owns it? Let me be clear about what I'm NOT asking about, because I think the near-misses are what's tripping me up. It isn't blind habit or ritual — I know that old story about the woman who cuts the ends off the roast because her mother did and her grandmother did, that's not this, because in my case people can and do offer a real epistemic justification. It isn't a situation where everyone publicly goes along while privately thinking it's dumb either, since here everyone genuinely believes it, me aside. And it doesn't feel like peer pressure or conformity, since the mechanism I'm describing is people reasoning perfectly well from inputs that only look like eleven pieces of evidence when they're really one. What I want is the concept, if it exists, for when a group's overwhelming agreement is actually a single stale observation that got passed along and is now re-read as fresh independent confirmation by every newcomer — the belief is rational, the evidence is real and visible, but it contains only one datum, and the person who had the datum is gone and can't be cross-examined. What are the closest established concepts, and for each one, exactly where does it stop fitting my case? And if nothing actually lands, can you name it and define it tightly enough that I could use it to argue the Friday thing deserves a real audit instead of being treated as a settled eleven-vote consensus?
