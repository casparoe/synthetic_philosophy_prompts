I'm building a "what do you actually believe about heritability?" self-sorting quiz for a stats-literate general audience — people who can handle a variance ratio but have never touched the philosophy of biology literature. I want 8 to 10 items, each one a concrete scenario or a claim to agree/disagree with, and never one that names a camp or uses a loaded phrase like "genes for" — the point is to sort people by what they'd say, not by what they call themselves. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the underlying stances: the causal-contribution reading, the "it's only a description of variance in this population in this environment" reading, the genotype-by-environment-interaction view that estimates don't travel across settings, the twin-and-adoption-study-confound skeptics, and whatever else you think belongs on the list.

Some nodes I want in there for sure:
- a batch of genetically identical seedlings split between a rich and a deprived environment, asking what the reader expects to happen to the heritability estimate;
- a claim that a high h² for some trait in one population licenses expecting the same group ranking to show up in a different population;
- a set of polygenic scores that predict well in one ancestry group and poorly in another — what does the reader think that shows about the score;
- an item on whether "missing heritability" is a puzzle at all.

Lay out the logical space first: which combinations of answers the items make available, which ones nobody actually holds and why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexamined), and flag any two positions your items can't really tell apart. Also if any of my four nodes is a bad diagnostic, say so and swap it.
