I'm one of five members of the electoral commission in a mid-sized country — we keep the national roll and we decide the contested cases about who belongs on it. Its been about twelve years of decisions now, roughly in order:

- A man who left at age nine and has lived abroad 46 years, never paid tax here, holds another passport: kept on the national roll, votes by mail.
- Lawful permanent residents with 12+ years here: added to the municipal roll only; their challenge to get on the national roll turned down.
- People who live just over the border and commute in five days a week, 18 years running, paying full income tax here: kept off both rolls.
- Citizens' children born abroad: on the roll if a parent registered them; the third generation born abroad, never resident here: struck off.
- Adults under full legal gaurdianship: put back on the roll, reversing an old exclusion.
- Prisoners: off the roll during the sentence, back on automatically at release; people on probation never came off at all.
- Sixteen- and seventeen-year-olds: refused, but allowed to pre-register so they go on automatically at 18.
- Foreign diplomats resident here, exempt from our taxes and largely from our courts: refused even the municipal roll.

Next month's case. Something like 40,000 people were admitted under a 15-year guest-worker program: they live here the whole time with their families, pay full taxes and social contributions, are subject to all our law including criminal law, and there's no route to citizenship — the visas are non-renewable and at year 15 they have to leave for good. The first cohort is at year 13. Their home country takes the vote away from anyone abroad more than five years, so right now they vote nowhere. A lot of them have kids born here who are citizens under our birthright rule and will stay. They're only asking for the municipal roll.

What will we decide, and what should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Be honest with me that more than one rule probably fits those eight verdicts. Finally, name the hypothetical case you'd want brought in front of us next — the one that would actually force out which rule we're running.
