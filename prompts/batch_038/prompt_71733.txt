I'm a third-year political theory PhD student and I'm the grad rep on my college's arts funding board — small liberal arts place, 1,800 students, town of about 12k. Every spring we split a $30k pot. This year two proposals are fighting over basically all of it:

1. A professional troupe wants $24k to run a four-show season in our black box. Students can audition for small parts, tickets are free.
2. A student group wants $22k for a series of open-air, no-audition events — food, borrowed PA, permits, street performance, and one big end-of-year thing where anybody can get up on the platform. No headliners, deliberately.

The numbers from last year are awkward: the season sold out every night, and the open-stage series averaged about 14 people, all of whom were already in eachothers friend groups.

The troupe's artistic director made a claim in her pitch that I can't stop thinking about — that watching difficult plays together teaches empathy and makes students better citizens. That's more or less the whole justification for public arts money everywhere.

Meanwhile I've been living in Rousseau's 1758 letter to d'Alembert about whether Geneva should let a theater open, and honestly the arguments are uncomfortably good: plays only flatter whatever morals a place already has; the tears you cry at a tragedy are a cheap substitute for actually doing anything; actors are people hired to perform a self they don't have; and his counterproposal was open-air festivals where the crowd is itself the show.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. Give me a concrete recommendation on the money — split it, fund one, fund neither, your call, but commit — with reasons the artistic director could actually answer back. And take the strongest case against the Rousseau line seriously: his worry was tuned for a small Calvinist republic, we're not Geneva, and "participation" usually just means the confident people dominate. Two things I especially want: whether the director's citizen-making claim is even the kind of thing a board like ours could check, and what our rubric would have to say if we funded the festival side — right now "excellence" is a thing you can only judge from thier seat in the dark.
