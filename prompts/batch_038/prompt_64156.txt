Third year in economic history, comparative track, and I keep hitting the same dialectical move in the divergence literature and I can't find a name for it anywhere.

It goes like this. Someone offers a factor for why industrialization got going in Britain and not in the Yangzi delta — coal, say, or wages high enough to make labor-saving machinery worth building, or European demographic restraint. Somebody else shows the same factor was present on the Chinese side too at roughly comparable levels around 1750: they had coal (and then the argument shifts to where the coal sat and how deep it was), they had commercialized agriculture and fairly integrated markets, and on the restraint case the revisionist population work made Chinese fertility control look pretty responsive to economic conditions as well. And the original claim is treated as dead. Fine so far. But the response is never to drop the factor — it's to demote it from "the cause" to "one condition among several that had to line up," and add a second factor that's claimed to be uniquely European. When the second one also turns up on both sides, same move again: demote and add. Three or four rounds in you have an account that is a conjunction of six or seven conditions, not one of which distinguishes the two cases, but whose joint presence supposedly does the work.

That last part is what I want a handle on. The "they had it too" objection only bites if the explanans is supposed to be a difference-maker. Once the account is a conjunction assembled out of the failures of its own components, no single component has to be a difference-maker anymore, so the test that generated the account no longer applies to it. The account has become immune to the very standard it was built under, and its read as refinement rather than as a loss — nobody says out loud that the standard changed, they just keep publishing.

I know the comparative-methods people have machinery for small-N inference and I know there's a philosophy literature on contrastive explanation, but I haven't found either one addressing this particular slide, and I suspect it isn't quite what either of them is about. So: is there an established term for it? If yes, what does that term actually pick out, and does my description match it or is it a cousin that gets conflated with it in practice? If no, coin one, define it properly, and give me boundary cases — especially how it differs from a legitimate conjunctural explanation that was specified up front and independently, and whether there's any principled way to tell "the conjunction was there all along and the debate finally uncovered it" from "the conjunction is just sediment built out of defeated factors." I don't want a bare label, I want the edges of the thing and what falls outside them.

Tell me how confident you are in the carving you end up with, and single out the one claim in your answer you're least sure of. And lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble, and don't restate my question back at me.
