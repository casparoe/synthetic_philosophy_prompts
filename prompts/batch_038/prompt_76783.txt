Okay so I've been going back and forth with a friend about Hofstadter's "Person Paper" — the satirical letter-to-the-editor bit where he swaps in racial analogues for the gendered words people think are harmless, to expose that "man" is quietly doing the job of the default human being. I think the piece is rhetorically brilliant, but I've got a nagging worry that the whole thing rests on a premise I don't actually buy.

Here's the worry. The argument only gets its grip if you think the words we use feed back into how we think — i.e. if people keep hearing "chairman" and "mankind" and generic "he," they'll start to really picture a man, and that in turn entrenches the assumption that offices, and humanity in general, are male by default. But that's just the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis wearing a nice suit, isn't it? And my understanding is that strong linguistic determinism is basically dead: the evidence for language reshaping thought in any interesting way is thin, and people can understand a generic "he" perfectly well without it changing a single one of their beliefs about who can hold a job. So if the causal story fails, doesn't the Person Paper collapse into an aesthetic preference — Hofstadter just finds certain words icky — dressed up as an argument about respect?

I'd like you to tell me where I'm going wrong, becuase I suspect I am somewhere. Is the piece even making a claim about how language affects cognition, or is it doing something else entirely — something about what a term presupposes or conventionally signals, rather than what it causes people to think? And if it's the latter, why does the racial substitution still sting so much, given that swapping in a racial analogue doesn't change anyone's beliefs either? Also can you sort out what's doing the work in the marked/unmarked asymmetry he leans on — why "chairperson" strikes a lot of people as a clumsy political imposition, while... hmm, actually I'm not sure there's a clean racial counterpart to that, which might itself be a crack in the analogy. I keep flip flopping on whether that crack matters or whether I'm just looking for a way to dismiss the piece without conceding its point.
