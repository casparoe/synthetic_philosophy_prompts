My opening move: last cycle you scored my cause area 9 / 8 / 2 = 144 and funded it, but you scored a rival application 5 / 5 / 9 = 225 and gave them twice the money, and you also knocked out a 10 / 10 / 1 application at exactly your cutoff line. Here's the problem. Those are ordinals. Nothing about a 9 means nine times as much as a 1 in any unit that survives contact with arithmetic, so the product isn't invariant under relabeling — and the relabeling I have in mind is one your own theory should like. If the reason neglectedness matters at all is diminishing marginal returns to dollars already committed to a space, and that relationship is even roughly logarithmic, then the honest encoding of your own 1-10 ratings is something like 1 + ln(score), which is perfectly monotone, so nobody's information is lost. Rescore on that and my 9/8/2 becomes 9 × 8 × 1.69 ≈ 122, and the rival's 5/5/9 becomes 25 × 3.20 ≈ 80. Your ranking flips, and it flips toward the answer I already believed. And please don't say "garbage in garbage out, we'd rescore with better data" — assume every number is exactly as scored and exactly as well-anchored as you claim. Tell me why the product of these three numbers is the right rule anyway. Then tell me the second thing: your neglectedness axis is doing two jobs that point in opposite directions, one economic (more resources in, less marginal return out) and one evidential (nobody smart is working on this, which you treat as evidence you found something), and in the meetings I've sat in you reach for whichever one rescues the score you already wanted. Which is it, and what happens to the 10/10/1 application under each?
