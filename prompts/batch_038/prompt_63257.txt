The thing I can't get straight is the irreversibility claim: the idea that once a technical possibility exists it will sooner or later be realized somewhere by someone, so "un-inventing" isn't on the table even in principle. That claim is doing a lot of work but I can't tell what kind of claim it is. Read one way it's about knowledge — you can't un-know how to build a thing, so any prohibition is at best local and temporary. Read another way it's about game structure — even if everyone could agree simultaneously to forgo it, the payoff to being the first defector is so large that no stable agreement holds, which makes the irreversibility a fact about incentives rather than about physics or memory. And there may be a third: a claim about coupling, that technology and social organization have fused into something symbiotic so you can't hold one fixed while changing the other, which would mean "giving up technology" isn't even a coherent description of a possible world rather than a merely hard one.
