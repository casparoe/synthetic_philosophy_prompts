my opening move: I want to know which claim you're actually making, because they differ enormously in strength. Either talk of experiences during sleep is empty — nonsense, no fact of the matter to get right or wrong — or it's meaningful but unverifiable from where any of us stand. If it's nonsense, then it had better be nonsense in the neighbouring cases too, and it isn't. There's the old story of the man who dreamt the blade of a guillotine came down on his neck and woke to find the headboard of his bed had fallen on it; there are patients under general anaesthesia who afterwards report things said over them in the theatre which the staff then confirm. Nobody could check any of that from the inside either, and still the reports sometimes line up with what was really going on while the person was out. If instead you mean only that we couldn't tell, then you've left the experiences themselves perfectly intact and your conclusion shrinks to a remark about evidence — far less than "there are no dream-experiences."
