I lead a small team that builds and maintains large enterprise knowledge graphs: OWL ontologies, description-logic reasoners, SPARQL query answering. My daily vocabulary is signatures and expressivity profiles, TBoxes and ABoxes, materialization versus query rewriting, the open-world assumption, inconsistency repair, and reasoning time budgets. I have no training in philosophy, though I have read enough epistemic logic to run into what is called the problem of logical omniscience: if you represent what an agent knows as the set of worlds compatible with its knowledge — so that the agent knows exactly those sentences true in all of them — then the agent automatically knows every logical truth and everything entailed by anything it knows, which is plainly false of any actual agent.

I have formed a view about this problem and I want it stress-tested, not indulged. My view: the problem is an artifact of representing a knowledge state with a semantic closure operator and then expressing surprise that the operator closes. Where I work there is no mystery at all. We have two implementations — materialize the closure into the store, or leave the axioms alone and answer queries on demand against them — and choosing between them is a performance decision under a budget, not a philosophical puzzle. Nobody builds a system that knows all theorems; we build systems that know the assertions plus whatever the reasoner derives inside its time slice. So my thesis is that epistemic logic should simply define knowledge as closure under a resource-bounded reasoner and move on, and that the literature is manufacturing depth where there is only an engineering tradeoff.

Please do the following three things.

First, translate the debate into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Take the closure conditions that generate the problem, then the main families of proposed repairs — the awareness-style or syntactic accounts that restrict knowledge to formulas built from the agent's conceptual repertoire, impossible-worlds accounts that allow "worlds" at which logic itself fails, accounts that define knowledge relative to what a bounded reasoner can derive from an explicit representation, and the strategy of weakening the modal logic so that the necessitation rule is given up — and tell me, for each, what it corresponds to in ontology engineering. I want to know which of these I am already performing without noticing: whether restricting an agent's signature is the awareness move, whether inconsistency-tolerant query answering over an unsatisfiable ontology is the impossible-worlds move, whether the materialization-versus-rewriting choice is the bounded-reasoner move, and whether the fact that an empty ontology still entails every tautology is the necessitation problem wearing a different hat.

Second, and this is what I actually care about, give me the residue report. For each translation, state whether it is clean, whether it needed distortion, and precisely what you had to fudge. Then identify what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. I have four candidate residues; evaluate them rather than echoing them back to me. (a) My reasoners are sound by construction, so I have no way to represent an agent that concludes something not entailed — is that a fatal omission for the bounded-reasoner proposal, or merely a difference in subject matter? (b) Entailment in my setting is always relative to a TBox someone wrote, and I have no internal distinction between "entailed" and "true of the domain," so factivity appears to evaporate under translation — does it, or can it be recovered? (c) In my practice an inconsistent ontology is a defect to be diagnosed and repaired, whereas in the epistemic literature inconsistent belief is the phenomenon to be modeled; did I smuggle that normative stance into my account of what knowledge is? (d) I have read it claimed that genuine unawareness cannot be modeled within a partitional state space without inconsistency, so that one must abandon state spaces altogether to get it; I cannot see what that has to do with restricting a signature, and I want to know whether my signature move escapes that result or simply hides it.

Third, give me a verdict on my dissolution thesis and commit to it — do not lay out the options and leave me to pick. If you think the problem really is nothing more than an engineering tradeoff and the philosophical literature has been misled, say so and say why. If you think my translation drops something that matters — that closure under a bounded reasoner is not a cheaper closure operator but a different relation, and that the difference is substantive — say that instead, name exactly what is lost, and state what would have to be true for my account to succeed anyway. Either way, tell me which of the four candidate residues does the most damage and rank the others.
