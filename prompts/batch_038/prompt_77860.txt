In three weeks I give the closing talk at a workshop on evaluation in machine translation, and what I need is a Q&A prep sheet, not a fresh defense of my view.

My thesis is a restated version of Hofstadter's shallowness charge that deliberately does not lean on error rates, poetry, or untranslatable wordplay. I propose what I call a foreclosure test: a rendering of a source passage manifests understanding of that passage only if the translator can, on request and without starting the job over, say which readings the source genuinely permitted at its decision points, which of them the rendering ruled out, and why the retained one was preferred. My claim is that current large models fail this test not because they choose badly at those points but because the choice is never represented as a choice — a sample is drawn, the alternatives persist only as probability mass, and nothing in the system holds what was given up. Corollary I intend to defend: metrics that score outputs alone cannot distinguish this failure from success, so evaluation should probe the foreclosed alternatives rather than the rendering.

Audience: mostly NLP researchers who build and benchmark translation systems and treat human adequacy and fluency judgments as the gold standard; two psycholinguists who work on bilingual lexical access; one literary translator. I am the only philosopher in the room. They have heard the Chinese Room dozens of times and regard it as either answered or beside the point, several do interpretability work, and they are allergic to armchair intuition pumps.

Give me the eight objections this audience will actually raise, ordered by how much damage each would do, each phrased in their idiom — decoding strategy, sampling temperature, calibration, probes, activation patching, verbalized confidence, metric correlation with human judgment. For each, a reply I can deliver in two minutes and a blunt note on where that reply is soft. Then tell me what to concede in the opening five minutes to buy credibility, which single reply has to land for the thesis to survive the session, and the question I should be praying nobody asks.
