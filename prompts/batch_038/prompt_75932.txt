I am assembling teaching materials for a graduate seminar on the philosophy of probability, and I need a purpose-built case on the reference class problem: a case in which the two standard prescriptions for choosing a reference class issue different probabilities for one and the same single event, and in which the disagreement cannot be dissolved by the moves the seminar has already covered.

Please construct a scenario to the following specifications.

**Setting.** A realistic risk decision about a single, identified case — one structure, one patient, one loan, one shipment; your choice. No anthropic or self-locating reasoning, no cosmology, no quantum measurement, no lotteries, no science fiction. The numbers may be invented, but present them as invented: nothing in the case should read like a report of actual empirical findings.

**The data.** Give a compact frequency table covering at least three candidate reference classes to which the case belongs, all defined by ordinary, projectible properties — no disjunctive, gerrymandered, or grue-like predicates anywhere in the case. Sample sizes must be large enough that no statistician could dismiss any of the frequencies as noise, and the frequencies should be treated as known rather than as estimates carrying wide error bars. Two further conditions: the candidate classes must cross-cut rather than nest, so that no one of them refines all the others; and no class in the table may be homogeneous with respect to the outcome, so that narrowing until the frequency stabilizes has no stopping point anywhere inside the case. Let the table be closed, in the sense that no data exist for any class narrower than the narrowest one listed.

**The divergence.** Two rules must select different classes, and hence different probabilities: (i) the broadest-class rule in the Reichenbach tradition, which directs us to the widest class containing the case for which reliable statistics are available; and (ii) the rule of maximal specificity in the Hempel–Salmon tradition, which directs us to narrow as far as the data show statistical relevance. State each rule in one sentence as you are applying it, and flag it if you think either is standardly formulated differently from the gloss I have given. The two probabilities must straddle a decision threshold that is derived from utilities you state in the scenario, rather than chosen after the fact to make one class come out the winner.

**Foreclosed resolutions.** The case must not be resolvable by any of the following, and you should show why not: appealing to a causal model (stipulate that the decision-maker has none and that the relevant mechanisms are unknown); averaging or otherwise weighting the candidate classes; retreating to the outside view, since both candidates already are outside views; invoking the agent's stakes, since the stakes are what fix the threshold; and dismissing one class as an artificial or gerrymandered partition, since every property in play is plain.

**Deliverables, in this order.** (1) The scenario: at most two short paragraphs plus the frequency table. (2) The demonstration: for each of the two rules, the class it selects, the probability it issues, and the steps by which it gets from the rule to that probability. (3) The diagnosis: which single feature of the case is doing the work — I suspect it is the absence of any homogeneous class, but say whether you agree — and what happens if that feature is removed, for instance by adding one further property at which the frequencies do stabilize. In the same section, answer the objection that the divergence is a mere artifact of the table running out, so that the two rules would agree if only more data were collected. (4) The opposition: the strongest available deflationary response, from someone who holds that a single-case probability is just a degree of belief conditional on total evidence and that there is no reference class to choose; make that response as good as it can be, then say whether it genuinely escapes the case or merely relocates it.

Keep the whole thing under about 900 words. Begin with a single sentence stating whether the specifications can be met jointly and which feature of the case does the work; then give the scenario and the demonstration, with no preamble and no restating of the request.
