I'm deep into a novel whose narrator is the opinion editor of a big American paper in the summer of 2020, and to write her I keep going back to the Times/Cotton episode. Everything said about it lands in one of two bins — censorship or accountability — and neither helps me. So treat the episode as an instance of punishment and run that analogy honestly.

The facts I'm working from: the paper invited a sitting senator to argue for sending federal troops into cities where there was rioting; within a day a lot of staff were saying publicly that running it put Black colleagues in danger; the paper appended a note concending the piece fell below its standards; and the opinion editor, who had defended it at first, was gone within four days. Years later he published a long essay claiming the publisher and the executive editor had privately backed the decision and let him carry it alone.

Go slot by slot — offense, offender, prosecutor, adjudicator, sanction, the community in whose name it's imposed, the reintegration stage — and tell me which are empty, doubled up, or held by the wrong party. Then: could retribution, deterrence, or the expressive/communicative account justify what happened, and does his comfortable comeback afterwards break those accounts or is it just what the expressive one predicts? Be honest about where the analogy stops being useful rather than merely imperfect: does calling this punishment already convict the staff, who on another description were only employees telling their boss a document made their own work unsafe? And what does the case say back to the theory — is social sanction punishment only in the denunciatory sense, or does the accountabilty vocabulary import conditions (proportionality, a hearing, a route back) that nobody in these episodes will actually honor?
