I'm a postdoc in a systematics lab — day job is species delimitation in a group of freshwater snails, so my whole working vocabulary is monophyly vs. paraphyly vs. polyphyly, grades vs. clades, homoplasy and convergence, character choice and weighting, OTU boundaries, ring species, tree topology, and the endless argument about when a group counts as real and wich features are actually diagnostic. I know that toolkit cold. What I don't know is political philosophy, which is a problem, because our building's interdisciplinary reading group has a session coming up on the left/right distinction and I've volunteered to open it. The question on the table is roughly: is the split between left and right a real division in the world, or a habit of speech, and if it's real, what is it that holds each side together — shared content, shared history, or shared enemies?

Here's what I want. Take the main candidate answers to that question and restate them, one at a time, in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can manage. The candidates I have in mind: the origin story about it starting as a seating arrangement in the French revolutionary assemblies (defenders of the old order on the president's right, partisans of the revolution on his left) and the resulting view that the labels are purely positional or indexical, defined only relative to whatever arrangement is currently established; the view associated with Bobbio that the whole axis is really one underlying disagreement about equality — whether inequalities are natural and to be accepted or social and to be attacked; the family-resemblance or bundle view, where each side is a statistical cluster of positions that hang together without any single necessary feature; the relational view where a side is held together less by what its members share than by who they oppose, including the horseshoe suggestion that the extremes end up resembling each other; and the deflationary view that the axis is largely an artifact of how questions get asked and positions get coded in the first place. For each one, give me the translation into my terms — is the group monophyletic, paraphyletic or polyphyletic; is it a clade or a grade; is the resemblance homoplasy; is the space a line, a circle, a tree or something with reticulation; are the boundaries observer-chosen or discovered; are we weighting economic characters over cultural ones or vice versa.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about.
