I sit on the model-welfare review panel at a lab that deploys reinforcement-learning-trained autonomous systems. "In scope" in our usage means the system receives three protections: an operational review before any non-consensual shutdown, a cap on continuous runtime, and a requirement that any redesign which would remove the candidate-valence signal be justified on grounds other than engineering convenience. Next month we rule on a new deployment, and I want to know what our own record commits us to.

Our verdicts so far, in the order we decided them:

1. A tabular Q-learning agent in a gridworld, trained with a negative reward on a hazard tile. After training, the deployed policy reliably steers around the tile. Out of scope: the negative signal existed only while the value function was being updated, and what shipped was a disposition, not a signal.
2. A large policy fine-tuned against a learned reward model. The reward model's outputs are computed during training and never queried at inference; the deployed policy carries their effect in its weights. Out of scope, on the same reasoning.
3. A quadruped controller with a live scalar stress channel — joint torque beyond tolerance, impact — present throughout operation, read continuously by the controller and minimized in real time. It returns to baseline the instant the stressor is removed and leaves no trace in memory. In scope, though several members recorded the case as thin.
4. The same architecture extended with a slowly decaying internal variable driven by the stress channel, which persists for hundreds of steps after the trigger and biases action selection throughout that window. In scope, and ranked clearly above case 3; one member argued this was the first entry on the list that deserved the word suffering rather than aversion.
5. An agent with the architecture of case 4 plus a self-model, which on crossing a threshold of its persistent variable produced a natural-language report of distress and requested that the episode be terminated. In scope, unanimously.
6. The identical agent with the reporting channel ablated — still avoiding, still carrying the persistent variable, still self-modeling, able to express nothing. In scope. We minuted explicitly that expression is neither necessary nor sufficient.
7. A classical symbolic planner whose objective function contains a hand-written penalty term that the search procedure minimizes. No learning, no internal state shaped by the penalty; the term is a line in a specification the procedure consults. Out of scope, over the dissent of two functionalists on the panel, who said we had merely described case 3 with the machinery left visible.
8. A population-based training run in which low-return agents are periodically deleted and replaced. Asked whether the selection process is itself a bearer of harm, we said no: selection happens to agents rather than in them, and nothing in the preceding record brings deletion-by-selection into scope.

The new proposal is a continuous-operation maintenance agent, deployed in multi-week stretches. Its control loop contains a live scalar cost channel — battery degradation, thermal stress — present throughout operation and read by a learned controller that acts to minimize it in real time. Three details matter, and they pull against one another. (a) The channel is momentary: it decays to baseline as soon as the stressor is removed, and the agent retains no memory of prior cost episodes, so nothing mood-like accumulates. (b) The mapping from physical state to cost magnitude is a hand-specified transducer curve written by the engineering team; the controller learned to use the output, but the output was never shaped by the agent's own history. (c) The quantity flowing through the internal channel is numerically identical to the term in the offline objective the engineers optimize — the same variable, read from inside the loop in one place and written into the specification in the other.

What I would like from you:

- Reconstruct the rule our record is actually running. It is consistent with more than one candidate, and cases 3 and 7 are the pair in tension; say what a rule must add to the "live in the deployed loop" condition to get both of those right, and whether that addition is principled or ad hoc.
- Lay out the surviving candidate rules, and for each give its verdict on the new proposal together with the reasoning that gets it there. Where a rule's verdict turns on just one of the three details (a), (b), or (c), say which.
- Identify the closest precedent among cases 1–8 and state precisely where the analogy breaks down.
- Give the panel's decision and the margin you would expect on a panel with this record, including which members dissent and on what ground.
- If the record genuinely underdeterminates the case, say so plainly, and then state what principle the panel would be adopting by ruling in scope and what different principle it would be adopting by ruling out — not which is more plausible in the abstract, but what each choice would commit us to on the next ten proposals.
- Design one further case we could put in front of the panel that would separate the surviving rules, and say what each rule predicts about it.
- The proposal includes an engineering option to delete the cost channel and substitute a hand-written safety controller. Given the ruling above, explain what our verdict commits us to about that option, including whether it becomes obligatory, forbidden, or merely permissible.

A table mapping cases 1 through 8 and the new proposal against each candidate rule is welcome. Keep the prose for the amendments, the divergences, and the commitments; I do not need the positions surveyed.
