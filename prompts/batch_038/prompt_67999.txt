Our department runs this small internal seed-grant scheme — eight months, a little travel money, no fieldwork budget, and it's explicitly meant for interdisciplinary pairs. I'm the philosophy half; my partner is a health economist who costs disability support programs. Applications are due in three weeks and I need a 3-4 page proposal, and I basically only have the seed of an idea so far.

The seed: the "equality of what?" debate is framed as a fight about the right metric — welfare, resources, capabilities. The capability side's big selling point is that it takes conversion seriously: the same bundle of goods yields different functionings for different people (the wheelchair user, someone living somewhere cold, a pregnant person, someone with a fast metabolism). But as far as I can tell everybody in that debate treats a person's conversion rate as a standing feature of them that you just plug in. My hunch is that the politically interesting variable isn't the *level* of someone's conversion rate but how *robust* it is — how much of a range of circumstances they can move through before their resources stop converting into the functionings they were converting a month ago. Two people can have effectively identical capability sets today and wildly different exposure: one of them loses the functioning the moment a supplier changes, a landlord sells the building, a bus route gets cut, or a family member takes a job; the other is basically shockproof. If that's right, the metric question might be less fundamental than it looks, because a robustness-sensitive view ranks some distributions differently than both resourcism and capability-egalitarianism do — it cares about things that show up in neither a resource index nor a snapshot capability set. And it might also give you a way around adaptive preferences, since people adjust their expectations to their circumstances but that doesn't make their circumstances any less fragile.

Turn this into the proposal. I want:

- the question stated precisely, so it has a determinate answer — not "does robustness matter?" but something like whether robustness is a fourth metric, a refinement internal to the capability metric, or a weighting principle for comparing capability sets, and what would tell those apart.
- why the existing positions don't settle it, and where each of them can absorb the idea vs. where it genuinely bites.
- what we'd actually do, month by month, given eight months and no data collection. Be clear about what the economist contributes and whether the thing needs real case material or can run entirely on worked examples — and argue for that choice over the obvious alternative of just operationalizing something and regressing it.
- what a successful result looks like, and what would count as failure. The panel will definately ask how we'd know if the idea is trivial (just the capability view relabeled) or false, so I want that answered in the proposal, up front.
- an honest scope note. I suspect trying to cover both the disability cases and the climate/disaster cases is too much for eight months and one of them has to go — say which and why.
