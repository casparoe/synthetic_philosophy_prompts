I'm putting together the take-home for my upper-level seminar on the epistemology of the past — mostly third-years plus a couple of grad students, 48 hours, 900 words, marked out of 25. I want one essay question built around the usual trio (the Exodus, the Trojan War, King Arthur), but the catch is that it must not be answerable by reciting how much legend has piled onto each one, or by intoning that absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence. That's what nearly all of them will do if the question is phrased loosely, and I've read it far too many times.

So: make the question force them to state, for at least two of the three cases, what the minimal historical claim would be — the thinnest proposition that has to be true for the tradition to have a kernel — and then say what non-question-begging evidence could confirm or kill it, i.e. evidence that doesn't just assume the tradition is reliable where it's convenient. The conceptual crux I want them hitting is that the real trouble isn't only transmission over the centuries; it's that you can't fix the threshold for a historical kernel without either making it so thin it'd be true no matter what, or so thick it just restates the legend.

Deliverables: the question as students would see it, a model answer of about 900 words, and a marking scheme worth 25 that says what separates a strong answer from a merely competent one by naming the specific moves. Please also list three or four mistakes that should cost marks and say why each is a mistake rather than just a difference of scholarly opinion — I have views about a couple (silence in the Egyptian record isn't a neutral gap given what we know about New Kingdom administration in the Levant; the Arthur and Exodus problems aren't the same shape of problem; "we have archaeology, so Troy is different" needs defending, since a mound is not an event), but don't feel obliged to share them.

I won't be able to check any of this online, so close with a short note: how confident are you in the model answer's substantive claims, and which single claim in it are you least sure of?
