I sit on the governance board of a worker co-op — about 200 members, we do appliance repair across three cities. Last year we got every tech to write up a diagnostic note on each job, and a couple of us trained a model on roughly 40k of those writeups plus the parts and pricing data. Its good. Good enough that a new hire with six months under her belt now closes jobs at the rate a twelve-year member used to, and an outside competitor has already made us an offer to licence it.

One of the older members — call him R — has filed a grievance under our bylaws, and the board has to rule at next month's meeting. His claim, roughly: the model is his own past labour, congealed, now standing over against him as something that makes his craft learnable by anyone and his seniority worth nothing. He points out he technically owns a two-hundredth of it and says that's precisely not the point. He says he's being exploited — by himself and by the membership collectively — and that the notes were collected under a norm of trust ("write it up so the next person can learn") that nobody ever said meant "write it up so it can replace you." He wants the model deleted, or at minimum he wants the external licence refused.

Half the board's line is that exploitation in Marx's sense requires a class of non-owners pocketing surplus, and we're a co-op where the surplus comes back to the people who made it, so the grievance is confused or even incoherent. The other half thinks he's put his finger on something real that the ownership form doesn't touch at all.

Draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. I need an actual bottom line: pick from (i) dismiss, (ii) licence externally with a per-note royalty to contributors, (iii) keep the model internal only, (iv) shelve or delete it, or some hybrid you can defend — and give me a decision rule the board can apply to the next model we build, not just this one. Also say what we tell the outside bidder, who's asked for an answer within 60 days. Two things I specifically want worked through: whether "self-exploitation" in a member-owned firm is a coherent charge or just a way of registering that R lost a bargain he didn't know he was making, and whether the early manuscripts on estranged labour — the bit where objectification of labour isn't yet the same thing as alienation from it — does any independent work here or is decoration on an ordinary complaint about the terms of a deal. And give me the strongest thing R could say back against whatever you recommend, in a form we can't just wave off.
