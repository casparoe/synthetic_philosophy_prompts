I accept pragmatic encroachment, but in a nonstandard form. On my view, whether S knows that p depends neither on the practical stakes S actually faces nor on the stakes salient to whoever attributes knowledge, but on the stakes S would face after idealized deliberation about her own situation: the consequences she would recognize as hers were she fully informed about her circumstances and instrumentally rational about them. The idealization is meant to absorb the usual objections — knowledge attribution that drifts with the subject's mood, or the high-stakes subject who hasn't noticed she is in high stakes — without giving up the core claim that knowledge is sensitive to practical context.

Tell me what would count against this. I want the arguments, cases, and theoretical results sorted by how decisive they would be: those drawn from within the encroachment debate, those concerning idealized desire formation generally, and those concerning how stakes are supposed to enter a threshold on credence at all. Which would a committed proponent of the view concede as genuinely damaging, and which could she defensibly dismiss? Address in particular whether the idealization quietly collapses the view into stakes-insensitivity, or into a form of contextualism, since reflective deliberation may filter out most of the variation in stakes that the theory was built to track. Note what the view predicts that its rivals do not, and give me an honest verdict on whether what it risks is fatal or merely a cost worth paying.
