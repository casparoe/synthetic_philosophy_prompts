Four proposals are commonly offered as resolutions of Bertrand's chord paradox: (1) the symmetry requirement — adopt the unique measure invariant under the transformations the problem leaves undetermined; (2) the physical-mechanism answer — the problem has no answer at all until a randomizing device is specified, and different devices yield different answers, each correct for its device; (3) the partition rule — indifference may be applied only to the coarsest partition available, never to a refinement of one; (4) the subjectivist retreat — there is no uniquely correct prior, and indifference is a policy for an ignorant agent rather than a claim about the world.

Rank these four, not by how well they handle chords, but by how well they handle a bounded case in which no improper measure and no order-of-limits escape is available: a factory makes cubes whose edge length lies somewhere in [0, 1] cm and nothing further is known, so uniform over edge length, uniform over face area, and uniform over volume are all live candidates. For proposal (1), state explicitly which of the three the invariance requirement actually selects, or whether it selects none of them.

Use tiers: A = the same resources dissolve the cube case outright; B = survives, but only with an ingredient the chord case never demanded; C = relocates the problem instead of solving it; D = silent or self-undermining. Give one or two sentences of justification per placement, and end with a single number in [0, 1] for how much of the principle of indifference you think survives all of this as a rule a working statistician could follow.
