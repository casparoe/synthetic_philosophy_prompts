A research group has spent two years arguing past itself about a single body of records, and I want the document its members should have produced. Please write in American English. Everything you need is stipulated below; do not supply a bibliography, a list of real archives this one resembles, or references to the scholarly literature, and do not attempt to identify the corpus with any actual collection — it is a composite constructed for this exercise, and I want the reasoning to be self-contained.

**The stipulated corpus (an estate in a slaveholding colony, 1831–1848):**

1. An overseer's annual account book. Enslaved people appear as "head," entered in a single column alongside livestock and appraised values, sometimes by number only, sometimes by given name without surname. The pages for 1839 and 1840 are not missing by decay: the stubs remain, and the cut is clean, indicating excision at some later date by an unknown hand. In the 1843 pages there are marginal notes in a second hand, some of which appear to correct figures in the first.
2. A mission baptismal register covering the same estate and years. It gives names, godparents, and ages, frequently qualified as judged as nearly as could be determined; three entries in 1843 carry no age at all. It also distinguishes infants from those marked adult, instructed.
3. A bundle of depositions from a single 1843 case. Testimony was taken down by a clerk in the third person, rendered from a creole the clerk did not speak, through an interpreter who was himself a salaried employee of the estate.
4. For 1843 the account book gives 61 head. The register gives 74 names, of which 9 are annotated as belonging to a neighboring estate, leaving 65 attributable to this one. The deposition bundle names three individuals who appear in neither of the other two sources.
5. Nothing self-authored by the estate's enslaved population survives from the period: no letters, no transcribed songs, no narrative. The nearest first-person account from the region postdates emancipation and concerns a different estate.

**The stipulated vocabulary both camps have agreed to use, provisionally:**

- A silence is *constitutive* when the record's own categories could not have held the missing content — the form has no slot for it — and *indexical* when the content was recordable in that form and either was withheld, or was recorded somewhere that no longer survives.
- The *symmetric legibility test*: an inference from a source is admissible only if the same inference pattern, applied to a record produced by the dominant party, would be granted the same weight. No asymmetric charity in either direction.
- *Backfilling*: estimating the content of a gap from population-level regularities observed in comparable estates, rather than from the source itself.

**The two camps:**

- **Camp A (reconstructive):** the silences here are structured by power, and structure is itself evidence. Careful conjecture, explicitly labeled as conjecture and stated in probabilistic terms, is not merely permissible but obligatory, because a scrupulous refusal to fill the gaps simply ratifies the archive's own selection of who counted.
- **Camp B (conservative):** conjecture is licensed only where a source's own categories leave room for it. Backfilling imports exactly the frame the archive imposed, and the honest product is often a documented lacuna rather than a plausible reconstruction. Camp B has already conceded, in the second year, that at least some silences in this corpus are indexical rather than constitutive, and that conceding this does not by itself license any particular reconstruction.

**Deliverable — the joint statement both camps can put their names to, in four sections:**

**I. Agreed claims.** A numbered list of at least twelve, each tagged [C] for claims about this corpus, [M] for claims about method or terminology, or [E] for claims about the ethics and politics of conjecture. At least four must be claims one camp initially resisted, with a one-line note of what moved it. The claims must be non-vacuous: if either camp could sign a sentence without it constraining any actual practice, cut it.

**II. Disagreements table.** Columns: (i) the disagreement; (ii) Camp A's formulation of it, in wording Camp A would accept as its own; (iii) Camp B's formulation, likewise; (iv) a classification as substantive, partly verbal, or wholly verbal; (v) where verbal, which stipulated term is doing the work, and a single neutral sentence both camps would sign in place of the two rival formulations. Include at least these candidates: whether the four-person discrepancy between 65 and 61 in 1843 is evidence of unrecorded persons, of divergent counting conventions, or of loss; whether the clean excision of 1839–1840 tells us anything about the excised content as opposed to the exciser; whether the interpreter's position as an estate employee discredits his mediation or merely locates it; and whether the second hand in the 1843 margins can be treated as a distinct witness.

**III. Cruxes.** For each: the exact question whose settlement would move at least one camp; the specific item, comparison, or piece of arithmetic within the stipulated corpus that both camps agree bears on it; each camp's prediction, written down in advance and in a form that could be scored rather than merely admired; and what each camp agrees would falsify its own prediction. Address explicitly whether the constitutive/indexical dichotomy is itself a crux — that is, whether the two camps should replace it with a three-way distinction covering cases where a slot exists but every available filler is drawn from the dominant party's taxonomy — and if so, say which of the corpus's silences the dichotomy currently misclassifies.

**IV. Joint methods paragraph.** Roughly three hundred words, drafted as it would actually appear in the monograph both camps agree to co-author: the labeling convention for conjecture, the rule governing backfilling, and a candid statement of which portions of the two-year dispute the camps now believe were about words rather than about the past.

Throughout, aim for formulations that are neither slanted toward one camp nor so hedged that disagreement becomes invisible; where you judge that a dispute is verbal, say so plainly and show which term is responsible.
