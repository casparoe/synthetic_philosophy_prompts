been arguing Lakatos vs Feyerabend with a friend for the third time this year and I still can't say clearly what bugs me about the progressive/degenerating distinction. rather than another comment thread, work it out as a poem for me.

Write a Petrarchan sonnet in the manner of Milton's sonnet on his blindness — the one that ends "They also serve who only stand and wait." So abbaabba up top, cdecde below, heavy enjambment, and the sense running straight over the break between lines 8 and 9 instead of stopping for it.

The octave should be someone living inside a research programme that stopped predicting anything new: anomalies arriving, each one soaked up by another tweak to the protective belt, the hard core untouched becasue the negative heuristic makes it untouchable. Make the machinery actually visible rather than name-dropped — someone who knows Lakatos should be able to point at which lines are belt and which are core, and feel how ad hoc the patches are, how they only ever reproduce facts that were already on the table.

The enjambment over the turn is where my problem lives: there's no clean moment when a programme announces itself as degenerating, and if you can always rationalize one more auxiliary hypothesis, the criterion never tells you to stop now. So the sestet should do the Miltonic move for methodology — waiting isn't obviously irrational, young programmes catch up, the patchers are sometimes the ones who turn out right — but keep the tension open. don't let it secretly take Feyerabend's side.

skip Ptolemy and the epicycles, everyone reaches for those. 14 lines, no title, american english please.
