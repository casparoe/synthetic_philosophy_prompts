Build me a thought experiment for a just war seminar. I want a case where a Walzer-style traditionalist (moral equality of combatants, noncombatant immunity) has to say a particular killing is impermissible, while a McMahan-style revisionist, for whom liability tracks moral responsibility for an unjust threat, has to say its permissible — the reverse of the usual complaint that revisionism is more demanding on soldiers. The point of the exercise is to find out whether traditionalism's in bello permissiveness does any real work or just falls out of who gets labeled a combatant, so the case has to turn on that rather than on stipulation.

Constraints: contemporary and realistic, no sci-fi, no omniscient narrator. You cant just tell us which side has the just cause — the injustice has to show up in the facts you give. One paragraph for the scenario. And the person killed has to be someone each theory gives a clear, principled verdict about, not a gray-zone case where both sides can duck.

Then walk it through: each view's verdict and the reasoning behind it, which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if you remove that feature (does it collapse back into the standard disagreement?), and how a traditionalist would attack the case.
