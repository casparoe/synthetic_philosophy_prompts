Assume the students had Dennett and Dretske in lecture and have seen polysemanticity and superposition discussed. Here's the question as it was set, worth 20 points:

"Interpretability researchers say things like: a certain direction in a model's activation space represents the Golden Gate Bridge, or that models encode concepts linearly. Some philosophers think this is harmless shorthand; others think it commits the researchers to a realist metaphysics. Say what a realist about neural-network representations has to claim over and above what an instrumentalist will happily say, and then say whether the causal-intervention evidence (activation patching, steering vectors) settles the question. About 400 words."

And the answer:

"The instrumentalist says representation talk is a convenient way of predicting what a model does, and nothing more: the claim that the model represents the Golden Gate Bridge isn't false, it just isn't about anything in the network. The realist says it is about something — that there's a genuine, mind-independent item inside the model that has that content. So the disagreement is metaphysical, about what would make the ascriptions true, not about which predictions come out right; both sides agree the model will go on about the bridge once you amplify the direction.

That means no experiment could decide between them in principle, since every intervention result is equally consistent with both. In practice, though, the causal evidence pushes hard toward realism. Patching takes a component from one run, inserts it into another, and shows the behaviour changes in exactly the way you'd expect if that component carried that content. Steering vectors go further: add the direction and the whole output distribution shifts toward the concept. This isn't predictive convenience, because the researcher didn't know beforehand which behaviour would change — the intervention pins down the content and the behaviour then follows. A direction that, when amplified, makes a model produce concept-relevant behaviour is thereby a representation of that concept. Content just is reliable causal role, which is roughly Dretske's indicator account: a signal represents what its tokenings reliably bring about.

The strongest evidence is that the directions get cleaner in the right basis. Individual neurons are polysemantic — one unit responds both to cars and to cat faces — which alone is a reason for scepticism. But superposition says that's because there are more features than neurons, so they have to overlap, and sparse autoencoders recover directions that are far more monosemantic than any neuron. Turning up directions that are cleanly single-concepted and causally effective out of a search over an enormous space can't be luck. So the real features are the SAE latents, and neurons are just the wrong grain.

Conclusion: the interventions don't force realism, but they leave instrumentalism conceding everything interesting."
