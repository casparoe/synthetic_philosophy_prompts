Write me a joke about the gap between determinism and predictability in chaotic systems. The audience is physicists and applied mathematicians, so the humor has to turn on the structure of the situation rather than on the stock imagery: no butterflies flapping their wings, no meteorologists who are really just reading horoscopes, no Laplace's demon shrugging. The vein I find most promising is quantitative. Because error grows exponentially, extending a forecast horizon by a fixed amount always costs the same multiplicative factor in the precision of your initial conditions — a millionfold improvement in instrumentation buys only about twenty error-doubling times, and every subsequent factor of ten buys the same modest increment. A punchline that lands because the listener silently does that arithmetic, and discovers that perfect determinism is compatible with a hard ceiling on knowledge that no budget of better sensors will lift, is the effect I am after.

Give me one joke and commit to it: the version you think is actually funniest, not a menu of three with a note that it depends on taste. Follow it with three or four sentences saying what the punchline technically rests on, and where the joke takes a liberty with the material — for instance, whether shadowing undercuts the premise that a computed trajectory is simply lost past the horizon, whether exponential divergence describes all trajectories or only typical ones over finite spans, and whether conflating an epistemic limit with a metaphysical one smuggles in more than the mathematics licenses.
