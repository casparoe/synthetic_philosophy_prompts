Act like a political philosopher who has also done measurement/methodology work — I want rigor, not vibes. Brainstorm a bit first if it helps, then give me a verdict move by move.

Two of my flatmates got into it over which Scandinavian country is more liberal, Norway or Sweden, and asked me to keep score, which I'm terrible at. Lise is Norwegain, Anders is Swedish. Roughly how it went:

(1) Anders opened with Sweden: school vouchers and for-profit independent schools, a pension where you pick your own funds, no wealth tax — while the Norwegian state owns a big chunk of the listed companies on the Oslo exchange and its private schools are regulated so hard they can't really be businesses. On economic freedom Sweden wins, end of story.

(2) Lise said that's a policy shopping list, not a concept. Freedom is what you can actually do: Norwegians are richer per head, more equal, and a single parent in Bergen has more real options than one in Malmö. Call it effective freedom.

(3) Anders said she was just describing a good society and relabeling it liberal — on that standard any rich competent state is maximally free and the word stops doing work. He offered a way to settle it: list the choice points in an ordinary life (which school, which doctor, which pension fund, whether you can buy wine at a corner shop at 9pm) and score each country by what share the state has already decided for you.

(4) Lise said that metric is backwards. It counts you unfree when the state's default is exactly what you'd have picked anyway, and it can't tell apart a state that decides one big thing badly from one that decides a hundred things well and lets you opt out of most. Her replacement: measure how much your life would change if the decision had gone the other way — or if your political enemies ran it. Unfreedom is sensitivity to who decides, not the number of things decided.

(5) Anders said fine, but two stable democracies come out identical on that test, so it can't rank them, and if no measure can rank them then there's no fact about which is more liberal — just an index choice dressed up as a discovery.

(6) Lise said no: her test doesn't make them identical, it shows they were both measuring the wrong thing, and the honest answer might be "neither — they're differently free."

Go through (1)–(6) and say who's right at each step, not just who wins overall. Then tell me what, if anything, would actually settle the original question, and what neither of them noticed.
