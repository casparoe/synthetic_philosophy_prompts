Two people at my LW-ish meetup got into it about panpsychism last week and I still can't tell who was winning, so I'm offloading it on you. Dev holds a constitutive panpsychist line; Nadia thinks the combination problem just finishes the view. Roughly how it went:

(1) Nadia: the view says my unified experience is composed out of little experiences had by the parts of my brain. But subjects don't stack. There's no conceivable mechanism by which a bunch of separate first-person perspectives add up to one first-person perspective. And if you retreat to "the parts aren't really subjects, just proto-experiential properties," you've given up what made the view attractive and you're back at brute emergence — the exact thing you accused physicalists of.

(2) Dev: the physicalist's problem is structurally the same and strictly worse. They need dead matter to produce experience full stop; I only need simple experiences to compose into complex ones. Same shape, less distance to cross, and I'm at least starting from the right kind of stuff.

(3) Nadia: only if you're allowed to treat subject-composition as brute. If brute emergence is on the table, panpsychism buys you nothing over physicalism but nicer vocabulary; if it isn't, you owe the account and you don't have it. Pick one.

(4) Dev: fine, so flip it. Take the cosmos as the one fundamental subject and treat individual minds as partly walled-off regions of it, the way one mind can host dissociated parts. No composition needed.

(5) Nadia: you've moved the problem to the other end. Now you owe a story of how one subject splits into genuinely private ones with their own boundaries, and you've thrown out the original selling point — that the intrinsic nature of microphysical stuff is experiential — since a whole cosmos doesn't hand you a neat microscopic intrinsic nature.

(6) Dev: also your whole line assumes there's a sharp fact about whether subjects compose. If unity of consciousness comes in degrees, "one subject or many" is the wrong question.

(7) Nadia: degrees-of-unity talk restates the puzzle, it doesn't answer it. You still haven't said why there's any unity at all where the parts each have their own.

More important to me though: flag what neither of them raised that someone who actually works on this would say the argument turns on.
