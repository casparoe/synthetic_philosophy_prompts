I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of biology, and I'm drafting a chapter that reads the typogenetics material in Gödel, Escher, Bach as a formal result rather than a heuristic. My claim is this: because Hofstadter's toy genetics makes a strand's encoding of an enzyme the analogue of a theorem's admitting a proof, and because a strand can encode an enzyme that recognizes the very strand that encoded it, self-replication is itself a diagonal construction — so any replicator faces a Gödelian limit, in the sense that there are truths about its own structure it cannot derive from within. I take the genetic code to play the role of Gödel numbering, and I assume on that basis that the diagonal lemma carries over to the enzyme–strand correspondence. What further premises would I need to make that carry-over rigorous, and where in the correspondence is the requirement that the system represent enough arithmetic satisfied?
