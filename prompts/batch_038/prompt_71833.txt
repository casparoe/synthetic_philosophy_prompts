I serve on the Public Safety Oversight Commission of a mid-sized American city, a civilian body empowered to renew or terminate the funding of our Office of Violence Prevention every three years. The authorizing ordinance states only that the Office shall be continued if it "demonstrably reduces violence in the city." It nowhere defines violence, and it nowhere specifies a baseline or a denominator. The renewal vote is in June.

The facts before us are as follows. Since the Office stood up four years ago, the city's resident population has fallen from roughly 318,000 to 254,000, largely out-migration after the assembly plant closed. Homicides fell from 79 to 68 over the same period. Non-fatal shootings rose from 141 to 173; the trauma surgeons attribute part of that rise to faster response times and better survival of gunshot wounds, which moves incidents from the homicide column into the non-fatal column. Calls to the domestic-violence hotline roughly tripled, and the Office counts this as success on the ground that its public campaign persuaded people who had never reported anything to report. In the two neighborhoods where the Office concentrated its outreach, reported aggravated assaults fell by nearly half; in three adjacent neighborhoods receiving no outreach, they rose by about a fifth. One commissioner wants the ledger to include the 1,400 households displaced by the redevelopment the council approved alongside the Office's creation, on the grounds that displacement of that kind is itself a form of violence; two others reply that this stretches the term until nothing the Commission says can be shown false. For what it is worth, the Office filed two reports last year covering the same period — one to the state, using resident population as the denominator and concluding that violence had fallen, and one to a private funder, using an estimated daytime population that counts commuters and concluding that violence had risen — and the state auditor declined to say that either was mistaken.

Draft the memorandum I will circulate to the Commission in advance of the vote. It should reach a recommendation — renew, renew with conditions, or do not renew — and it should propose a counting rule the Commission could adopt as standing policy: what counts as violence, which denominator to use while the population is moving, and how to treat measures that rise for reasons that may themselves be improvements. I want the memorandum to state plainly whether the question of whether violence decreased here has a determinate answer that our choice of measure merely tracks, or whether the answer is fixed by the choice; if it is fixed by the choice, it should explain what makes one choice non-arbitrary rather than merely convenient. Finally, please give the strongest honest case for the position I expect to lose on, and identify where my own recommendation is most exposed.
