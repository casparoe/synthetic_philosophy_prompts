I have been teaching myself Solomonoff induction, and two things I keep reading pull against each other. On one hand, the choice of reference machine is said to be arbitrary but harmless: Kolmogorov complexities in two different universal languages differ by at most an additive constant, so the corresponding priors differ by at most a multiplicative one, and a fixed factor of that kind is eventually swamped as evidence accumulates. On the other hand, uncomputability is presented as the genuine obstacle — not a limitation of hardware that faster machines could lift, but something structural. Yet a computable approximation to the prior also stands at some definite distance from the true prior, and I cannot see why a fixed gap should be benign in the first case and fatal in the second. Could you explain what actually distinguishes the two situations — and, in particular, whether "off by a constant" is even the right way to characterise the difference between the true prior and the closest thing a computer can reach?
