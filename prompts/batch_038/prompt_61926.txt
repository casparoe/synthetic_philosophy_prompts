Hey — I run a little physics-nerd reading group at work, about 8 of us, all engineers, nobody's taken a philosophy class. I volunteered to write down my actual positions on determinism and predictability so everyone can attack them, and before I circulate it I'd like a sanity check on whether the set hangs together. Here's what I've got:

1. The world is deterministic. The complete physical state at time t plus the laws fixes the state at every later time, full stop. I don't think quantum randomness is real randomness — I lean toward the branching/many-worlds reading, so it's deterministic all the way down.

2. I take Laplace's demon literally. If something knew the exact state and the exact laws it could just compute the future. That's an engineering problem, not a metaphysical one. And since a deterministic law means same input, same output, I figure that if the present state is finitely describable, the future is computable from it.

3. Chaotic systems are genuinely unpredictable past a short horizon, and this is not about my hardware being bad. The sensitivity is in the system: more precision buys you a couple more steps, not a longer story, so no amount of cleverness fixes it.

4. Which is why I think chaos is evidence the world ISN'T deterministic. If a system's own present doesn't fix its future — if two states you can't tell apart end up in completely different places — then there's no fact about what happens next, and that's just what non-determinism means.

5. Day job: same seed, same binary, same thread ordering, bit-identical output every single time. I treat that as determinism at the machine level, and as evidence my model isn't throwing anything away — the float errors are small, so what they cost is small.

6. I don't think infinitely precise real-numbered states are physically real. Any finite chunk of the world holds finitely many bits, so the textbook's exactly-specified initial condition with an infinite decimal expansion doesn't exist out there. It's an idealization, like a frictionless plane.

Which of these actually contradict each other, and which only look like they do? I suspect I'm double counting somewhere but I cant see where. Rank the real ones by severity rather than listing everything as equally bad, and for each say which commitment has to go or get weakened and what the cheapest edit is.
