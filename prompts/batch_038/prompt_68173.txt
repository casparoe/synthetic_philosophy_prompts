so me and my friend went around in circles for like three hours last night about this and I still don't know who's right. He reckons my whole package is incoherent and he keeps hammering on the no-self-but-also-rebirth thing like it's a gotcha, and I keep saying that's the least interesting objection on the table. Anyway, I wrote my views down so someone could audit them without me having to defend them out loud over beer. Here's the list:

1. There's no self over and above the causal stream of mental and physical events. Persons are like the chariot — perfectly fine to name and reason about, but not an extra thing sitting on top of the parts.

2. Personal identity over time consists in nothing but psychological connectedness and continuity, it comes in degrees, and — this is the part I actually care about — it isn't what matters. What matters is the R-type continuity stuff (the Parfit business), and I don't care much what causes it.

3. Case verdicts. Fission: neither survivor is me and I'm genuinely unbothered by that. The scan-copy-destroy teletransporter: I'd step in, and I'd say the person who shows up on Mars is me, tho I don't think there's any further fact that settles it either way and I don't feel the need for one.

4. Blame and punishment require literal numerical identity with the agent. R isn't enough, degrees aren't enough — either you're the one who did it or you're not. I don't believe in degrees of responsibility.

5. At the same time I do think selfhood comes in degrees in a different sense: a stream whose motives mostly endorse each other, where there's less internal noise (call it what meditation practice is supposed to build), is more of an agent than a scattered one. And I think that unity is part of what makes their own future matter to them — not just however many memory and intention links happen to be there.

6. The causal stream continues after the body dies, no soul or transmigrating entity involved, just more of the same kind of causal continuation, like a flame lighting another flame. And the being who inherits the results of the deeds is the one who did them, so there's no injustice in any of it.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture on Buddhist philosophy and not a survey of who says what. Go through the list and tell me which of these actually pull against each other and which pairs only look like they do — I'm fairly confident at least one of the "obvious" contradictions in here isn't a real problem, and I'd like to know which one and why it dissolves. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them flat, and for each say what would have to go or be weakened and what that revision costs me. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most, and whether the package survives if I make it. And wherever two of my commitments hand out incompatible verdicts about an actual scenario, build the scenario out concretely instead of just waving at it — that's exactly what my friend kept doing and it's why we got nowhere.
