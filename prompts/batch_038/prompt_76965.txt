so im a backend engineer, no philosophy background at all, and on friday im doing one of those 20-minute internal talks about device attestation — basically why you cant let a thing vouch for itself, self-signed certs, a service reporting on its own health, that sort of thing. while i was hunting for an opening i went down a rabbit hole and landed on G.E. Moore's proof of an external world, the one where he holds up his two hands, announcing each of them in turn as he does it, and concludes that two human hands exist and therefore that there's a world out there independent of his mind. and the standard complaint about it feels exactly like my talk: the only way he gets the hand premise is through his eyes, and whether his eyes can be trusted is precisely the thing thats supposedly up for grabs, so the argument sort of funds itself.

i want to open with a villanelle. 19 lines, two refrains, and heres the structural bit: refrain one is the hand gesture (the premise), refrain two is the world being there anyway (the conclusion). they should take turns all the way through until the closing couplet, where they finally land side by side. someone who actually knows the argument should be able to point at those last two lines and say why they end up together; my colleagues, who have never heard of Moore and wouldnt care if they had, should just get a good poem.

a few constraints:

- youre allowed to let each refrain drift by a word or two each time it comes back, and id like you to actually use that drift — the premise coming back subtly different under repetition is part of the point.
- dont use the words circular, begs the question, presuppose or assume anywhere in it. the form has to carry that on its own.
- it must not come out as saying the argument is invalid or that Moore fluffed the logic, because thats the bit people get wrong and i dont want to teach my team something false. as far as i can tell its a perfectly valid argument and the real fight is whether someone who doubts the conclusion can be said to know the premise in the first place — with one camp saying perception just hands it over regardless and the other saying the warrant never gets across. if you can hold that disagreement open in the couplet instead of settling it, do that.
- one image from my world only — a cert, a checksum, a health check, whatever fits — used once and lightly. if it starts reading like a jokey tech poem ill bin it.
- british english throughout please, and just give me the poem, no explanation of your choices afterwards unless theres something you think ive got badly wrong.
