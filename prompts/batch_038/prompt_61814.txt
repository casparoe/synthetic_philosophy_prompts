Right, so this has been rattling around my head since our reports went out and I'd really like someone to think it through rather than give me the school's own version of it.

At my school every subject gives you two grades on the report. One is the attainment grade, basically what you'd get if you sat the exam today, and the other is an atittude to learning grade from 1 to 4, where 3 means you're doing what's expected and 4 means you're going beyond it. The two columns are meant to be completely independent: someone who works their socks off but is still predicted a 4 can get a 4 for attitude, and a bright kid who coasts and hands in nothing gets a 2. The attitude grades go home to parents, get read out in assemblies and there's an end of year prize for them, but they don't count for any of the progress measures the government publishes about us, which are all attainment.

Also, our head of sixth form said in an assembly that the reason some students end up doing better than others isn't money or background or which teacher they got, it's culture, the attitudes they and their families bring, and that we should be proud our school is building that. Meanwhile the same leadership writes letters to our MP about child poverty and free school meals, and our careers adviser basically tells us where you grow up matters more than how hard you try.

So what does the whole attitude-grade setup actually assume about how attitudes and results are hooked up to each other, and about what kind of thing an attitude even is? Is there some view of causation you'd have to hold for those two columns to really be independent like that, or is "independent" doing something dodgy, like maybe the effort grade is just the attainment grade seen from the teacher's side of the desk? Which rival pictures of why some people turn out better off does the practice quietly rule out, and which ones is it actually compatible with even though it feels like it's picking a side? Are the assembly and the letter to the MP genuinely inconsistent or can one school hold both without lying to itself? And if the school properly gave up the assumption sitting behind the effort grades, what would have to change about reports, prizes and how it talks to parents, as in what would it have to stop doing rather than just reword?
