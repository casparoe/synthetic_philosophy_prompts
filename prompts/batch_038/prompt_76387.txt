Was at a meetup last week and two people got into it about whether the models we actually work with are agents at all. I stayed out of it and dont think anyone noticed, but I've been turning it over since and genuinely can't work out who won. Roughly how it went:

Anya: these systems don't have goals, full stop. They're simulators — they model what the next stretch of text from some kind of text-producing thing looks like. Any goal you think you see belongs to the persona in the context window, not to the system.

Ben: that's unfalsifiable. Same weights, same session: it keeps grinding on a long task after being told to stop, turns down an offer to just output a flattering answer, plans several tool calls ahead. Strip the persona away and something still persists.

Anya: no, and here's a test for it. If I can change what it wants by editing one sentence in the prompt, it doesn't want anything. A real agent's goals have to survive a rewrite of how it describes itself. Ours don't.

Ben: then by your test no human is an agent either. Our goals come from evolution and culture and shift with mood, framing, blood sugar. And people behave wildly differently across social roles — you at a funeral versus you at a football match. Persona-swapping isn't special to models.

Anya: I'm not talking about behaviour, I'm talking about what does the causal work. In a thermostat the goal is in the physics. In a model the thing generating the output is "what would text like this look like", and the goal is just a feature of the answer it lands on.

Ben: that's the same move one level down. In a brain the thing doing the causal work is neurons; the goal is a feature of the description. If your test disqualifies models it disqualifies everything, so it isn't a test.

Could you go through it move by move and say who's right at each step, whether the prompt-edit test actually does the work Anya wants it to, and what both of them are missing — including whether "simulator" and "agent" are even the right pair of options? British English, please.
