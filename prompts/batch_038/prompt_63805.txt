Been going down a rabbit hole on whether generations are actually real kinds or just a story we tell — Boomers, Gen X, Millennials, Gen Z. The people who insist they're real always point at the survey data: ask people today about anxiety, work, dating, or home ownership and the 24-year-olds and the 40-year-olds give strikingly different answers, and they've been giving them for a while now. So the generational differences must be doing something real, right? My own intuition is that the only serious objection is that the birth-year cutoffs are arbitary (who decided that somebody born in December is a Millennial and somebody born a month later is Gen Z?). Is that basically it, or am I missing a stronger objection — and if so, how much of the debate is actually about ontology versus about how the evidence gets collected? Answer in American English please.
