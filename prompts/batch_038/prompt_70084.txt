In the last of the objections he canvasses against his main thesis in the 1950 *Mind* paper, Turing takes up extra-sensory perception. He reports that the statistical case for telepathy at any rate strikes him as overwhelming, and he considers how the imitation game would have to be run if telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition and psychokinesis were admitted.
(a) Explain what goes wrong in the imitation game if ESP is real. Identify whose position in the game is affected and how, and say why Turing's response is to alter the conditions under which the game is played rather than to revise or abandon his thesis. (6 marks)
(b) Some readers hold that by granting ESP Turing concedes a dualist or otherwise anti-mechanist view of mind. Assess this claim. (5 marks)
(c) Present-day evaluation of large language models faces the problem that a model may have encountered the items on its benchmark during training. Are the standard remedies for this problem structurally analogous to Turing's proposed remedy for the imitation game? Say where the analogy holds and where it breaks. (4 marks)

And here is the student's answer, transcribed as submitted:

Turing devotes the last of his contra-indications to extra-sensory perception, and he is unusual among the objectors he replies to in taking it seriously rather than waving it away: he refers to Rhine's card-guessing experiments and says that the statistical evidence for telepathy at least seems to him overwhelming. He works through four phenomena — telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition and psychokinesis — and concludes that the imitation game would have to be run under altered conditions if any of them were real: the participants would be housed in a room designed to exclude telepathic influence, so that the witness cannot pick up the interrogator's thoughts and thereby obtain information the machine has no access to.

(a) The difficulty is one of fairness. The game is meant to compare two sources of answers on a common informational footing. Telepathy destroys that footing: the witness can acquire the interrogator's intentions directly and so appear far more perceptive than it is, while the machine, whose every state is the outcome of physical causation inside a cabinet, is left with whatever it is told. Shielding the room restores the footing, which is why Turing's response is to modify the procedure and not the thesis. Nothing about telepathy shows that a machine cannot reason; it shows only that a particular experimental arrangement would measure the wrong thing.

(b) It does not follow, though it is tempting to say so, that Turing thereby becomes a dualist. He grants ESP as a matter of evidence, not as a premise in an argument about the nature of mind, and his remedy — a screen around the room — is an engineering remedy of exactly the kind he prefers elsewhere in the paper. Readers tend to group him with the argument from consciousness at this point, since both raise the question whether thinking is a purely physical process, and if telepathy establishes that some mental activity is not explicable in terms of matter in motion, then the mechanistic picture Turing wants is incomplete rather than false. That is why, of all the objections, he takes this one to bite deepest: it is the only one on which the evidence might force him to give ground. But it is also the only one he takes to be capable of being designed around, and designing around it is precisely what he proposes to do. His openness to this material is sometimes explained by his involvement with psychical research circles in Cambridge, which would have given him a prior sympathy for the phenomena.

(c) The parallel with contemporary evaluation is exact in structure. When a language model has seen its benchmark during training, the model is the party holding the illicit channel, just as the witness was the party holding the illicit channel in Turing's game, and held-out test sets are the telepathy-proof room of machine learning: they let you compare model and human under conditions in which neither has been given the answers in advance. Both fixes operate by controlling the environment in which the comparison is made rather than by redefining what counts as thinking, and in both cases the underlying capacity that worried us — apparent insight in one, apparent competence in the other — is left untouched and is supposed to reveal itself once the leakage is stopped. The cost in both cases is that the test becomes harder to administer and easier to dispute.
