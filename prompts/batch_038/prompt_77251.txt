so im the humor columnist for my school paper (junior, we do one all-fake-documents issue a semester, last time i did a product recall notice for the water fountain by the gym) and next month i want to do a cease-and-desist letter from a law firm. i have the bit already worked out, i just need you to actually write it.

the bit: the math club here plays Hex at lunch, the connection game where you try to link your two opposite sides. one kid — invent a name, dont use a real one — is convinced his opponent stole his second-player winning strategy and has talked his parents into retaining a firm. the letter demands the opponent return the strategy, stop using it, and destroy all copies and derivative works. the joke for anyone who knows the material is that the strategy-stealing argument says the second player cant have a guaranteed win in a symmetric game where an extra move can never hurt you, because the first player could just make a throwaway move and then play the second player's own strategy back at him. so the firm is demanding the return of something that provably does not exist, and they are doing it in totally straight-faced legalese.

what i need:

- the letter itself, around 750-850 words, laid out like a real demand letter: invented firm name plus a letterhead gag, date, a Re: line, numbered paragraphs, a cc, a signature block, and an attached Schedule A itemizing the allegedly stolen moves. Schedule A should be like five lines long and honestly funnier than the body of the letter.
- deadpan from start to finish. nobody winks, nobody says "obviously this is absurd," no author's note explaining the math at the end. the reader is supposed to work it out.
- the laughs have to come from the game theory being correct, not from the lawyers being idiots. specifically i want: (a) one paragraph that lays out the copying/stealing maneuver properly enough that my math teacher nods along instead of wincing, (b) the firm demanding the recipient produce the winning first move while somewhere else conceding that their own client cant produce it either, since the argument only establishes that such a strategy exists without saying what it is, (c) a paragraph where the firm tries to dodge the whole thing by arguing the game in question isnt symmetric, or that an extra piece is actually a liability under their client's house rules — the zugzwang style escape hatch — and the way that argument collapses should be technically right, not just goofy, (d) at least one demand that is literally impossible to comply with, like an order to cease and desist from moving first.
- the target should be the practice of aggressive ownership claims over things that cant be owned or excluded, and the habit of treating "a proof shows X exists" as "here is X." dont target a real person, a real firm, or a real case, and dont quote any real document or real textbook — everything invented.
- it should work on two levels: someone whos never taken combinatorics still laughs because the surface joke is "lawyers suing over a thing that cant exist," and someone who has taken it laughs at the extra stuff.
- no citations, no bibliography, no reading list, no footnotes about who invented the argument.

oh and if you can end with a settlement offer that turns out to be worse for my client than just losing the game, that would be perfect.
