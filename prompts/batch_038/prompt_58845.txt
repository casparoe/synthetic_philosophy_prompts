Last week a friend of mine — an economic historian, though he would describe himself as a recovering macroeconomist — and I spent most of a Saturday night arguing about whether history is locked in. He began by insisting that the persistence of an incumbent standard is evidence that it is the efficient one; I replied that this begged the question, because on his account there is no observation that could count against the incumbent's optimality. Somewhere around midnight the argument slid sideways into Gould's tape-replaying thought experiment and the convergence literature in evolutionary biology, and it was only when we were both trying to reconstruct the other's position that we realized we had been answering entirely different questions, and that neither of us could say which. I have spent the week since drafting an essay out of that argument, for a workshop I am presenting at in about six weeks. The draft is below. I would like substantive comments on it rather than encouragement.

---

**Draft: Two Robustnesses — Canalization, Lock-In, and the Question Behind the Question About the Tape**

**§1. Why the standing dispute is under-specified.** The usual gloss on path dependence is counterfactual: an outcome is path-dependent if it would not have obtained had the process taken a different branch early on, and it is locked in if it cannot now be dislodged even by agents who would prefer an alternative. Increasing-returns models supply the mechanism — early advantages compound through coordination, training networks, complementary investment, and switching costs — which is the line of argument Arthur developed in the late 1980s around competing technologies and lock-in by historical events, and which the political-science taxonomies that followed generalized into a list of mechanisms including threshold effects, unintended consequences, and reactive sequences. Two camps then fight: those who read the survival of an incumbent as evidence of its merits, and those who read it as evidence of an accident that hardened. I want to argue that the fight is under-specified in two distinct ways, and that both are routinely collapsed.

**§2. First collapse: there are two robustnesses, not one.** Waddington's canalization is a claim about return dynamics: a process is canalized to the extent that it departs from and returns to the same endpoint under perturbation. Lock-in, as the term is used in the economics of standards, is a claim about accessibility: as the process proceeds, the set of outcomes reachable by interventions the relevant agents could actually undertake shrinks. These are different properties and they come apart in both directions.

(a) A process can be strongly canalized with respect to the perturbations it actually meets while having had a large reachable set at the outset. Development is buffered against variation in temperature, maternal nutrition, and a great deal of genetic variation, yet homeotic-scale perturbations reach endpoints nothing like the normal one. Canalization is graded by the size of the disturbance, so a canalization claim is empty without a perturbation class.

(b) A process can be locked in — no alternative now reachable at any price the relevant agents would pay — while being highly non-canalized in its present state. An order sustained purely by the cost of coordinating an exit is not robust to a coordinating shock: a single large buyer switching, or a state mandate, and it collapses at once, even though it was, from inside the system, unavoidable. Lock-in here is the absence of affordable exits, not the presence of restoring forces.

Increasing returns delivers (a) for perturbations below the switching-cost threshold, and delivers (b) only relative to a specified set of agents and prices. My claim is that the efficiency camp answers historical claims by trading on the difference: they show that the incumbent would have been returned to after small disturbances, and infer that it was never an accident. That inference requires the two robustnesses to be one property.

**§3. Second collapse: three counterfactuals are being confused.** I distinguish C1, the perturbation counterfactual (same process, same initial conditions, a small disturbance at time t — does it return?); C2, the branch counterfactual (the same world up to a branching point, an alternative branch taken there, everything downstream re-solved under the same constraints); and C3, the deep re-run (different initial conditions, same generating structure — the tape played again). Increasing-returns models are built to answer C1 and, with effort, C2 for a nearby branch. Nearly all public assertions about contingency are made at C3. C3 requires a modal premise about the space of possible processes, not a dynamical model of one process.

The two famous standoffs sit at different levels. In the keyboard case, the economists' rejoinder — that the case for the alternative layout was assembled largely by its own proponents, and that any real advantage would have to be net of retraining an entire typist labour force and the complementary investments built around the incumbent — is a claim about which interventions are inside the budget, i.e. about C1 and C2 under an agent-relative intervention set. The Gould–Conway Morris standoff is at C3 and concerns the size of a constrained space of viable forms: the repeated independent arrival at camera-type eyes in vertebrates and cephalopods is evidence that certain adaptive solutions are reachable from many starting points, which is a robustness result at C3 and says nothing about which lineage realizes a solution or when. One can hold pervasive convergence at the level of solutions together with radical contingency at the level of lineages without contradiction. The parallel in economics is exact, and I think it is the reason my friend and I could not get traction: the survival of an incumbent standard is perfectly compatible with the claim that which standard it would have been is an accident.

**§4. Outcome-grain as a second parameter.** Both robustness and contingency vary with how finely outcomes are individuated. Eyes, camera-type eyes, camera-type eyes with the particular inverted wiring vertebrates have, and eyes in this clade at this time are four outcomes with very different contingency profiles. Likewise in the standards case: a QWERTY-family layout, the layout actually standardized, and the specific manufacturer decisions that produced it. A path-dependence claim is therefore doubly indexed, to an intervention set and to an outcome-grain. Disputes look intractable because the parties vary both indices without noticing.

**§5. The worry I cannot yet answer.** If every such claim is indexed to an intervention set and a grain, then the thesis that history is locked in is either false — take a grain fine enough or an intervention set generous enough — or true — take a coarse grain or a small set. I want to resist this deflation by arguing that some indices are privileged: the intervention set should be the one the actual agents in the system could undertake given their resources and information, and the grain should track equivalence classes that are stable across interventions in that set. But the second clause looks circular, since the grain is selected to make the claim come out as intended, and the first clause makes lock-in agent-relative in a way that seems to surrender exactly the objective sense of the term the debate was about. I have no fix for this.

**§6. What I intend to do with it.** A formal sketch: represent the process on a state space, define reachability relative to an intervention set with a cost bound, and show that canalization and lock-in correspond to distinct properties of that reachability relation — return after departure versus shrinkage of the cost-bounded reachable set. Then two worked cases, keyboard standardization and the vertebrate eye. If the sketch cannot separate the two properties non-trivially, the essay reduces to a plea for precision, which is not worth six thousand words.

**What I would like from you.** Please comment in numbered points keyed to sections. I am not asking for a bibliography or for references I should add; I will handle sourcing myself, and I would rather you work from the argument as stated.

1. §2 is the load-bearing wall, so attack it first. Is the canalization/lock-in distinction a genuine distinction between two properties, or is it a single feature of a basin structure reported in two coordinates — depth of the basin on one hand, number and size of the alternatives on the other? If it is the latter, show me exactly where the example in (b) fails, since that is the case I most need to survive.

2. Do C1, C2, and C3 remain distinct, or does C2 collapse into C3 as the branch point is moved far enough back? A modeller would object that increasing-returns frameworks can simply be re-parameterized to answer deeper counterfactuals, so that my claim about what they license is a claim about practice rather than principle. Tell me whether that objection defeats §3 or only weakens it.

3. Accuracy, on the material rather than the argument. Is my description of the keyboard dispute fair to both sides — in particular, is it right to characterize the economists' point as being about the intervention budget rather than about efficiency as such? Is the convergence example I use one that the convergence camp actually pressed, and is Gould's position properly located at C3 rather than C2? And does importing canalization this way distort it, given that Waddington's notion is bound up with buffering against genetic as well as environmental variation and with the genetic-assimilation story that followed?

4. §5 is the weakest part and I know it. Either tell me the deflation worry is fatal and describe what weaker claim is still worth making, or give me a route to privileged indices that is not circular. I am also open to being told that the agent-relativity is a feature rather than a bug — but then I need to be able to say why it does not drain the thesis of interest.

5. Blunt assessment of whether the essay is interesting at all, or whether it states a distinction a specialist in this literature would consider obvious and already made. If it is the latter, tell me what would have to be true of it for it not to be the latter.

6. Structure. Which section should go, and should the formal sketch in §6 be moved to the front and made the spine of the paper instead of a promise at the end?

Please do not rewrite the essay for me. Objections, gaps, and a verdict on each numbered point are what I am after.
