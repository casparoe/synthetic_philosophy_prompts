I help run the appeals panel for a mid-size social VR world — persistent user-built stuff, an in-world currency you can cash out for real money, and account-bound companion creatures that pick up memories and habits from you over time. Five of us vote on incident reports: is this a real harm that warrants sanctions and compensation, or not. Our record over roughly two years:

- A user's house, built up over two years, deleted by somebody exploiting a bug. We called it real harm: permanent ban, and we paid the victim out of the offender's escrow. Land and structures aren't in our backup system, so it was just gone.
- Somebody wore an exact copy of another user's one-of-a-kind jacket design. The original was untouched and had no cash value. We said no harm — the copier had to stop wearing it, but no sanction and no payout.
- A duplication exploit flooded the currency and tanked prices for everyone. Real harm to the whole userbase; rollback plus bans.
- A user's memorial plaque for her brother, who died in real life, got defaced. Real harm, and we escalated it above an ordinary vandalism report.
- A player spent about $400 on crates and pulled nothing but commons. No harm — he'd accepted the odds.
- A player tricked a user-run shop's NPC clerk into handing over goods. We said the harm ran to the shopkeeper, not to the NPC, and made it good.
- A player's account-bound mount, worthless in cash, was killed in a duel and auto-respawned 30 seconds later at full status. No harm, no action, nothing lost.
- Abuse delivered in-world by voice got treated as more serious than the same words posted on our forums.

Now here's the case we can't agree on, and its the first one like it. User A used an exploit to delete User B's companion creature — account-bound, zero cash value, 14 months of accumulated memories and learned behavior. Our backup feature, rolled out three weeks ago, caught it: within an hour, while B was offline, the system spun up a fresh instance from the last snapshot, bit-for-bit identical, memories intact. B never noticed, says she doesn't feel harmed at all, but wants A banned. The logs are unambiguous that the original instance's process was terminated and a different one started from the snapshot. A says no harm no foul, and honestly two of us are sympathetic.

Which way should we vote if we want to stay consistent with our own record? What I want from you: the candidate rules that genuinely fit all eight precedents above (I'm pretty sure more than one does), what each of them says about this case, your best guess at which rule we've actually been running and why you'd bet on it over the others, and — since backups mean we'll keep getting these — one made-up incident we could rule on that would cleanly seperate the rival rules, plus what each rule would say about it.
