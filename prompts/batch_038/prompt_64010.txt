I have been reading about panpsychism in blogs, forums, and comment threads for the better part of two decades now — the constitutive-versus-cosmopsychist split, Goff's phenomenal bonding proposal, the twin objections that subjects do not add up and that a sparse palette of micro-qualities cannot yield the diversity of what we actually experience — and I have come to think the combination problem is badly served by its usual medium. Analytic metaphysics papers are the least vivid format imaginable for a difficulty that is ultimately about what it would be like to be a great many things at once. I would like to stage it in a different register.

Please write a satirical legal document, 800–1,000 words, in the form of a class-action complaint: caption, jurisdictional statement, numbered paragraphs, a short procedural history, an exhibit list, and a prayer for relief. The forum should be invented — a chancery court with jurisdiction over phenomenal aggregation, or something of that sort — and every case, party, statute, and precedent should be invented as well. Do not cite real papers or quote real philosophers; the piece must be self-contained.

The plaintiff class consists of the micromental properties and microsubjects instantiated in the brain of one unremarkable adult, say a regional logistics manager. The named defendant is the manager's own unified conscious subject, sued on behalf of everyone he is made of. Develop three causes of action: quantum meruit, on the theory that the class has conferred the entirety of the defendant's phenomenology and received none of his in return; breach of an implied phenomenal bonding relation; and a count seeking a declaration on whether the class can be certified at all.

That last count is where the piece should do its real work. Let the certification fight carry the metaphysics. Numerosity is trivially satisfied, but commonality fails because the class members cannot identify a single phenomenal property they all share; typicality and adequacy of representation collapse once the only viable class representative turns out to be the defendant himself; and notice to absent class members cannot be given to entities who are not, on the plaintiff's own theory, anyone yet. The defendant's answer should be a fair and internally consistent statement of constitutive panpsychism — he does not deny that the class members are real and fundamental, only that they are separate from him — and should plead one alternative defense in which the plaintiffs are in fact modes of a single cosmic subject, so that the true defendant is the universe and venue is improper. Let that alternative defense rebound on him: if unity descends from above rather than assembling from below, he cannot explain why he, of all the nested unities, is the one being served.

Add a third-party defendant, a consultancy that sells a numerical index of phenomenal integration and is ordered to produce an accounting of where the unity is stored; its production should consist of one number and a cover letter. The prayer for relief should ask the court to appoint a receiver empowered to bind the class into a single subject, and the complaint should concede, in one dry sentence rather than a confession, that no such receiver can be appointed without presupposing the very subject whose existence is in dispute.

Tone: deadpan throughout, no asides, no author's note explaining the joke. A reader who has never opened a panpsychism paper should be able to follow the shape of the dispute from the legal boilerplate alone; a reader who has should recognize every joke as a real argument in disguise, and should see that the case is unwinnable for much the reason William James thought mind-stuff theories were unwinnable. I intend to post this where actual panpsychists will read it, so the target is the hardness of the problem and the rhetoric that papers over it — never the view's holders, and never the view itself.
