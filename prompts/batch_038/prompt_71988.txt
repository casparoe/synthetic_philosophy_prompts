My partner is a systems neuroscientist, and for the better part of a decade our running disagreement has concerned his habit of explaining a cognitive capacity by pointing straight at a circuit and calling the explanation complete. Last month he read the crane and skyhook material in Darwin's Dangerous Idea and announced, with real satisfaction, that he had never once appealed to a skyhook in his life. I do not think that gets him off the hook, and I have decided that the most affectionate and most precise way to say so is at his laboratory's tenth-anniversary dinner next month, where I have been asked to give a short toast.

Write me the joke that toast should be built around. It has to turn on the actual content of the distinction rather than on a generic materialist-versus-dualist gag: my target is the reductionist who prides himself on never invoking a skyhook while declining to name the crane that would have to do the lifting. I have a coinage of my own for this — a promissory crane, meaning a lifter that is announced, credited with the explanatory work, and never specified — and I would be glad for the joke to hang on that. Alternatively it could hang on the related point that greedy reductionism is not excessive scepticism about higher levels but a refusal to pay for the intermediate ones.

Please give me one joke rather than a shortlist, and commit to it: tell me which of those two targets you chose, and why it makes for a better punchline than the other. Keep it to something I can deliver standing up, three sentences at most. Afterwards, add a brief note on where the joke takes liberties — anything it overstates or flattens about the crane/skyhook distinction, and whether it lets my partner off more or less easily than the distinction itself would.
