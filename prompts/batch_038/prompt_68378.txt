Some context. I am a software engineer on a distributed-systems team, with no formal training in philosophy, though I have been reading about the hole argument on and off for a few months, mostly because I wanted to understand what people mean when they say that general relativity forces you to give up the idea that spacetime points are things in their own right. At a team offsite I got talking to a physicist whose group works adjacent to ours, and he waved the whole problem away with an analogy I have since heard repeated on a couple of podcasts and in one blog post. His version ran roughly like this: diffeomorphism invariance is a gauge symmetry, and a gauge symmetry is like alpha-renaming in a program — you can rename every variable, or even relabel which node in a cluster holds which shard, without changing the state of the system. So two solutions of the field equations related by a diffeomorphism are the same physical situation written in different notation, exactly the way `swap(x, y)` and `swap(y, x)` are the same function. On that basis he concluded that the hole argument is a pseudo-problem: a naming bug dressed up as metaphysics, and the substantivalist is simply someone who has confused the labels for the data. A friend of mine who has started a PhD in philosophy disagrees, and says the analogy is quietly doing the philosopher's work for them instead of settling the dispute, but when I asked her to be precise about where it breaks she gave me a rather vague answer about active versus passive transformations.

So here is what I would like: a full audit of the "diffeomorphism invariance is just refactoring" analogy.
