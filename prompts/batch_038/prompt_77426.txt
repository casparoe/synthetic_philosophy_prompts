ok so two things are colliding in my life this semester. I'm a senior double-majoring in environmental studies and philosophy, and I'm in a seminar on the ethics of eating — we've done the standard Singer material, and last week we got to crop deaths and wild animal suffering, which honestly wrecked the class, there's like three committed vegans in the room and it got heated. Seperate from that, I hold the student seat on our university's Dining Advisory Board, which is eight people (dining services staff, a chef, two faculty, three students) that has to sign off on the protein lineup for the two all-you-can-eat halls every semester. Nobody on it has any philosophy training except me, so I've apparently become the "welfare person," and this spring there are three competing proposals and the board wants one method for ranking them so we stop going in circles. Proposal A: kill the beef station entirely and replace it with chicken plus plant-based, on the grounds that beef is the worst for climate and cows are big. Proposal B: keep chicken but switch from commodity birds to a certified higher-welfare one, which roughly triples the cost and means we shrink portions. Proposal C: swap commodity beef for pasture-raised beef from a farm about forty miles away, pitched by the sustainability office as a way to cut the field animals killed in growing feed, since grass-fed cattle don't eat much grain. My gut going in was "obviously A, then B, then C" and I'm nervous that an actual procedure will spit out something else, especially on C.

What I want from you is the procedure itself, not an essay about whether I should be vegetarian. It needs to be something the board could run on a proposal in an afternoon using only ballpark numbers that are publicly floating around, by people with zero philosophy background: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a no or an "insufficient data," and where it hands the decision back to a human because it's really a values question wearing an arithmetic costume. It has to explicitly deal with: how many animals are involved per unit of protein, which is the step everyone skips when they compare one cow to one chicken; whether a farmed animal's life is net positive or net negative, and what the ranking looks like under each answer, because the board will never agree on that; the crop-death objection, and whether pasture beef actually escapes it or just relocates the harm somewhere else (land conversion, wildlife pushed off by grazing, the fact that the animals killed to make grain are wild and may have had lives worth living or not); and whether our purchase moves anything at all, given that our contracts are locked a year in advance and we're a rounding error in the national market. I also want you to flag every place where the procedure is smuggling a contested assumption in as if it were a number, and to be upfront about what it structurally can't handle — I already suspect it's going to be useless on seafood, which is next year's fight.

Then run it on all three proposals and give me the ranking it actually produces with the reasons, including — especially — if it puts C above A or B, since that's the version of this I'm going to have to defend to my seminar classmates. And if the honest bottom line is that the procedure can't rank them and the board just has to vote on a value question after doing some bookkeeping, say that, and tell me exactly which question is the one that breaks it, because I'd much rather walk in with that than hand eight people a spreadsheet that's pretending.
