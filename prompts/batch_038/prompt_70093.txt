My friend and I argued for three hours last night about whether "cause" picks out a single relation, and I now want to test my own verdicts against each other rather than argue with him again. Here is what I say when the cases are put to me. Suzy's rock arrives first and shatters the bottle; Billy's would have shattered it a moment later: Suzy caused the shattering, Billy did not, and I feel no pull at all from the fact that the bottle would have shattered anyway. An assassin poisons a man's coffee; his bodyguard, who would have administered an antidote, is shot dead by the assassin's partner: the partner's shot caused the death, even though all it did was remove a prevention. A boulder rolls off a cliff, a hiker sees it and ducks and survives: the rolling caused the ducking and the ducking caused the survival, yet I refuse to say the rolling caused the survival. A gardener who simply fails to water his plants causes them to die; a stranger on another continent who equally fails to water them causes nothing — so I want omissions to be causes only where a role, a norm, or an expectation is in play. Finally, the case I constructed myself, and the one my friend calls a cheat: a lamp is wired to a relay B, and B's position is itself set by a master switch A, so that there is no way to move A without moving B. I judge that flipping A caused the lamp to light, and that B caused nothing, since B's position was never up for grabs independently of A — while conceding that no intervention on A leaves B alone. Is there a principle, or a compact set of them, that delivers all six verdicts? I want it stated precisely enough to be applied mechanically: which case motivates each clause, whether the principle is ultimately about counterfactual dependence, about invariance under interventions, or about both at once, and what it says about cases I have not raised — in particular a cause that raises the probability of an effect without ensuring it, and the choice between describing a cause coarsely (the boulder was heavy) and finely (it weighed exactly two hundred kilograms). And then tell me honestly which of my verdicts the principle cannot keep, and whether my friend is right that the relay case is one I should simply withdraw.
