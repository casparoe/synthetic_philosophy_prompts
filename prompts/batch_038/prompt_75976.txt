Three hours of arguing with a friend last night about state apologies and we didnt budge an inch, so I'm looking for someone who isn't either of us to referee the structure of it.

We started from the Germany/Namibia case — Germany eventually called what was done to the Herero and Nama a genocide, then after years of negotiation offered about 1.1 billion euros framed as development aid rather than as reparations, with no admission of legal responsibility, and a lot of Herero and Nama groups rejected it, partly saying they'd been kept out of the talks. That's the shape of thing we were using.

His side: an apology is a speech act that has to come from whoever did the wrong. A chancellor who wasn't born yet can't feel remorse about 1904, so what he's doing at the lectern is performance, and it's worse than useless because it lets the people who actually inherited the benefits swap a few careful sentences for money. He also has a standing point I couldn't answer — even if a state could apologize, what entitles me, a citizen who did nothing, to apologize on its behalf or to take on shame for it?

My side: the state is the kind of thing that persists, the same entity through complete turnover of personnel, like a company that still has to honour contracts its founders signed — so it can acknowledge what was done and be liable to make repair without any individual feeling guilty. Guilt and liability are seperate. He said I'm just relocating the mystery: I've invented a corporate agent with obligations but no psychology, and apology without remorse is a contradiction, not a distinction.

My hypothesis about why we went in circles, which I want you to attack rather than nod at: 'apology' smuggles in a requirement of felt remorse that only a thing with a psychology can satisfy, so the whole fight is really about whether that requirement can travel to a corporate agent — and on top of that, he keeps answering "should I feel bad about what my country did?" while I keep answering "can the government do something?", and those look like one question only because the thing that delivers the apology happens to be a man at a lectern.

Is that right, or is the disagreement substantive in some other way — about what persists when a regime changes (is today's Germany the same agent as imperial Germany, or does a revolution, or a total constitutional replacement, reset the ledger?), about whether the injustice is ongoing rather than finished so the inheritance problem never gets off the ground, or about who an apology is even addressed to — the victims, or the apologizing nation's self-image? And could one of us be making a hidden empirical bet, that official apologies change how the receiving group is treated and how it sees itself, that neither of us has said out loud?

Give me the diagnosis, argued from what each side actually says and what each is committed to, and then tell me what would count as progress: a distinction that dissolves it, a settled answer about whether a group can have anything like remorse, an empirical finding about effects on recipients, a change of question — or your honest view that this one just runs and runs because both sides are right about different things. No survey of the field and no reading list, please. I want the structure of the standoff.
