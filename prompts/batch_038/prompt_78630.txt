I've been writing songs for like fifteen years and I've finally admitted they're all the same song wearing different clothes. Someone handed me Hofstadter's chapter on variations on a theme in Metamagical Themas -- the knob-twiddling picture, where a style is a pile of knobs and creating means knowing which ones to turn, including finding ones nobody knew were there -- and I'm pretty convinced I only ever touch the same two. I run a monthly songwriting circle, six of us, all amateurs, and I want to hand out a sorting quiz at the next meeting: eight or nine questions, each a concrete case (a four-bar melody your bored of, a lyric that won't land, a chord loop that goes nowhere), with two or three options that are all perfectly sensible things to do, plus a scoring key that sorts us by what we actually reach for -- turning knobs inside a fixed style, mapping the whole space a style makes available, rewriting the rules the style runs on, or shrugging that it's all recombination anyway. Nobody should be able to tell from the wording which camp an option belongs to, and please no question that just asks whether you think you're creative. Name each leaf, and if it's a position somebody actually defends, say whose in a few words. Add a leaf for the person who only ever fiddles with meta-knobs and never finishes a song, note any combination of answers that's possible but that nobody lands in, and flag any two leaves your questions can't really separate. American English please.
