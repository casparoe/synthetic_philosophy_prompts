I've been circling this material for years — I came in through the old Kalam threads on the forums, drifted into foundations, and ended up earning my living checking programs in a dependent type theory that is a moral cousin of ZFC — and a local meetup has asked me to give a talk on where I've actually landed on the infinite. A friend who read my outline said my position amounts to the cosmological argument with the theology deleted and several extra steps added, which I found annoying mainly because I couldn't immediately show it was wrong. So before I write the thing up I want my commitments audited as a set, not explained back to me. Here they are, as plainly as I can state them:

(1) I'm a potentialist about the set-theoretic hierarchy, in what I take to be the Aristotelian line updated for modern set theory: individual sets are completed objects, but the cumulative hierarchy never is. There is no determinate totality of all sets, and I don't think talk of all the ordinals picks out anything that could be surveyed as a whole from anywhere.

(2) Hilbert's Hotel does real argumentative work for me, and I don't think it's just a cute paradox. A fully occupied hotel with infinitely many rooms that can absorb one more guest, or infinitely many coachloads of them, purely by reshuffling — and in which evacuating infinitely many guests can leave either a single occupied room or infinitely many, depending on how you do it — describes something that could not happen anywhere in a world of concrete things. I read that as evidence that actual infinities cannot be instantiated. To be explicit about one limit: I do not buy the traversal argument. A beginningless past requires nobody to have completed an infinite task, since every moment is only finitely many steps from the present, and I think that objection fails on its own terms. I don't need it.

(3) Cantor's diagonal argument, and the theorem that the power set of a set is strictly larger than the set, are proofs rather than sophisms, and I take them to establish that there are genuinely different sizes of infinity — not merely that certain functions fail to exist.

(4) For me ZFC is true in the ordinary sense, not a profitable fiction. I'm not a fictionalist about the theorems of analysis: they are true of something, and that something is not a story we tell to make predictions convenient.

(5) I'm broadly Quinean about how mathematics acquires an ontology at all — we owe commitment to whatever our best physical theories ineliminably quantify over — but I also suspect physics may not need the infinite in the end. Spacetime might be discrete somewhere near the Planck scale, and I lean toward treating the continuum as a calculational idealization we simply haven't yet been forced to abandon.

(6) Banach–Tarski doesn't trouble me in the slightest. It's a theorem about mathematical points, and a gold sphere is not a collection of dimensionless points, so nothing about physically dividing it follows.

(7) Arithmetic is determinate and classical. Every sentence of first-order arithmetic has a definite truth value independently of anyone's capacity to establish it, and I am not an intuitionist: I don't want to surrender excluded middle, in mathematics or anywhere else.

Please go through this as a package and tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, ordered by severity, and for each, what would have to go and what the loss costs — I want exact pairs named and the mechanism of the conflict stated, not a general mood about infinity being difficult. Equally important: tell me which tensions are only apparent. I already suspect two, and I can't tell whether my suspicions are right or whether I'm flattering myself — (1) and (4) look like a flat contradiction, since a never-completed universe doesn't obviously leave room for a theory to be true of anything, and (1) and (3) look like a case of my smuggling back the very completed totalities I've just denied. If those worries dissolve, say precisely why rather than just reassuring me; if they don't, say what breaks. Where a tension has a repair rather than requiring a surrender, name the repair and its price — I'd rather buy consistency expensively than discover later that I was holding two incompatible pictures in the same head. Flag any commitment that turns out to be doing no work at all in the rest of the package, and finish by telling me which single revision would clean up the most, even if that turns out to be one I'm obviously fond of. I'm not asking for a survey of the positions, a history of the debate, or a reading list; I know roughly where to look. I want the audit of my own view.
