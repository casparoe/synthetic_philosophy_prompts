I'm on my college's admissions-integrity panel and we've got an ugly case. A rejected applicant used our records-access policy to read her letters of recommendation and complained about one that said, in full, that she never missed a class, was unfailingly courteous, and turned in beautifully formatted problem sets. Its obviously meant to be read as "nothing upstairs," and the committee did read it that way. The applicant points out that the writer supervised a paper she co-authored that year and never mentioned it. His response is that every sentence in the letter is true and he never undertook to be comprehensive. Our rule only bars "false or misleading statements in support of an application" — and the letter does count as in support of her, technically.

Draft the panel's decision memo: does the letter violate the rule, and what should we actually do — including whether anything happens to her application? I want exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, no preamble and no sign-off, with the bottom line up front in item 1. Build the writer's defense as strongly as you honestly can before saying whether it survives, and be explicit about whether a meaning that's conveyed but never written down is the kind of thing our rule can even reach.
