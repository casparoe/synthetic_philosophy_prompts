I'm helping one of my doctoral students put together a two-page proposal for a dissertation-year fellowship, and I'd like you to draft it. Take on the role of a philosopher of art who works on the aesthetics–politics interface and who is also at home in early twentieth-century Black political thought — and think this through carefully rather than reaching for the standard summary.

Her half-formed idea concerns Du Bois's insistence that art just is propaganda, alongside his equally insistent demand for high artistic quality and his resentment at being judged by white standards. People writing on this tend to do one of three things: say he's an instrumentalist who subordinates art to racial uplift, say he's torn between two commitments he can't reconcile, or try to dissolve the tension by pointing out that on his view propaganda has to be true to be any good. My sense is all three readings miss something becuase the word is doing triple duty, and she should split it apart: a thesis about content (the work asserts or presupposes political claims), a thesis about design (the work is deliberately engineered to move a specified audience toward a belief or an action), and a thesis about function (the work does political work in its social setting whether or not anyone intended that). Du Bois could then coherently endorse the first, endorse the second only conditionally, and treat the third as description rather than aspiration. Layer on top of this that his propaganda is audiance-relative — what's called for when addressing Black readers isn't what's called for when addressing white readers — and his complaints about quality start to look like complaints about efficacy rather than bids for artistic autonomy.

Turn that into an actual proposal: the question stated so sharply that it could have a determinate answer; why none of the three readings above settles it; the method, i.e. what she would concretely do month by month across twelve months; what a successful result looks like and what would count as the project failing; and an honest note about where the scope is too ambitious for a fellowship year. No bibliography, no literature review by citation — she'll add references herself. And please don't put anything in quotation marks as though quoting Du Bois; I'll check the wording against the texts myself.

Before the proposal, give me a few lines of brainstorming on which of the three theses is most likely to turn out load-bearing and which is most likely to collapse under pressure. Then write the document.
