Our phil-of-language seminar does the Davidson bit as a prediction game — she posts "field notes" and publishes her ruling on whether the interpreter has actually pinned down what the speaker believes, as opposed to just what they'd say. Record so far:

- utterance paired with a visible event every time, systematic translation, speaker mostly right about their surroundings: real beliefs, determinate
- speaker coherently and successfully wrong about a whole domain (all sickness traces to one spirit): attribute the false beliefs, dont bend the translation to make them come out true
- two manuals fitting every disposition to assent, one taking a term for the animal, one for any undetached part of it: no fact of the matter, the dispute doesnt arise
- creature whose words track regularities in its own inputs but which has never touched anything outside its tank: beliefs alright, but about the inputs, not about what the words look like they're about
- speaker so erratic no assignment makes them come out mostly rational: not a failure of the method — nothing there to be right or wrong about
