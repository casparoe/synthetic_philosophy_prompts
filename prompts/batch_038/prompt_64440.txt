My sister asked me in June whether she should take the job in Denver. I said yes, take it, its obviously the right move — and I meant it, I'd turned it over for days. But I didn't have any special knowledge, no inside info about the company or the city or anything, I just thought it suited her. Six weeks in she was miserable, she's back home now, and she says I steered her into it.

When I said I only told her what I honestly believed, she said that wasn't the point. She said I knew she was going to act on what I told her, and that if it had gone well I'd have gotten away with it — which to her means I got lucky rather than did right by her. I keep wanting to answer that if it had gone well she wouldn't have blinked, so how can the exact same sentence be fine then and not fine now? But I can tell that reply is dodging something.

So: did I actually wrong her by saying it, and does the fact that it turned out badly have anything to do with it? And does it change things that in my family we're always telling each other what to do with our lives and nobody treats that as a big deal?
