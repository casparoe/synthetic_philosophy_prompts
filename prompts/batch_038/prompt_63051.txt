Our institutional animal-research ethics committee is revising the scope of its protection policy. The current policy extends full protocol review to vertebrates, cephalopods, and decapod crustaceans, on the basis of a binary determination: an organism either is or is not sentient, and protections attach accordingly. I have argued that this line is indefensible, and the committee has provisionally agreed to replace it with graded moral weight. Before it votes, however, it wants the gradation operationalized — it is not willing to accept a scale whose outputs cannot be defended case by case.

To that end I have drafted the following four-marker rubric. Each marker is scored 0 (no evidence in the profile), 1 (partial, ambiguous, or contested evidence), or 2 (clear evidence), strictly on the basis of the profile supplied.

- **M1 — Valence asymmetry and common currency.** Evidence that appetitive and aversive states are commensurable: the organism will forgo a reward of one kind to terminate or avoid a state induced in an entirely different modality, and the exchange rate shifts with internal condition rather than being fixed.
- **M2 — State-space compression.** Evidence that the organism sorts novel stimulus configurations into a small repertoire of affectively differentiated categories, rather than responding to each configuration on its own terms or by a single undifferentiated reflex.
- **M3 — Self-other binding.** Evidence that information about the organism's own recent condition modulates its response to the condition of a conspecific — for instance, a demonstrator's experience changing an observer's subsequent choice.
- **M4 — Temporal reach.** Evidence that a state elicited by an event continues to shape behavior well after the event, in a way that is not merely peripheral sensitization: the profile persists, decays on a timescale of its own, or is reinstated by a cue.

**Weighting and mapping.** M1 counts triple; M2–M4 count single; maximum raw score 9. The committee proposes converting raw score *s* into moral weight *w* = (*s*/9)² — deliberately convex — on the reasoning that the markers are individually weak evidence of sentience but jointly decisive.

The profiles below are stylized composites rather than exact descriptions of particular studies. Treat them as the complete empirical input; you need not supply, and should not attempt to supply, any literature, citations, or reading list.

- **A.** Adult laboratory mouse. Rich repertoire of avoidance learning; will traverse an electrified grid to reach food, and the crossing threshold drops sharply under food deprivation and rises after a stressor. Discriminates among contexts it has never encountered by generalizing from a small set of learned categories. Freezes in response to a conspecific's distress vocalizations, and this freezing is stronger if the observer itself was shocked in that chamber the previous day. Conditioned aversions persist for weeks and can be reinstated by a cue alone.
- **B.** Worker honeybee. Avoids a flower color paired with shock after a single trial, and will nonetheless feed at that color when sucrose concentration is high enough; the concentration required rises markedly after the bee has been subjected to sustained shaking, and shaken bees also show reduced exploration of novel feeders. Recruits nestmates by dance, and the dance parameters are modulated by the forager's own recent experience at the site. Learns to solve a novel string-pulling task after watching a demonstrator, though transfer to a differently configured apparatus is poor. Learned aversions persist for days.
- **C.** Jumping spider. Selects a detour route that carries it out of the prey's visibility and deposits it nearest the prey's position, and abandons the detour if the prey is removed mid-route. Will accept a longer detour for larger prey and a shorter one when desiccated. Chooses between prey types in a manner suggesting a small set of categories rather than item-by-item recognition. No evidence of behavioral modulation by the condition of conspecifics. Abandoned pursuits do not generate perseverative search beyond a few minutes.
- **D.** Free-living nematode, roughly three hundred neurons. Migrates to the temperature at which it was cultivated and does so for hours after transfer to a new temperature, then slowly adopts the new set point. Will cross a noxious copper barrier to reach bacteria when starved, and the barrier's deterrent value is graded against bacterial concentration. No evidence of response modulation by the condition of other individuals. Learned aversions persist for roughly a day.
- **E.** Octopus. Learns to open a sealed jar from inside; abandons a food item after a single quinine-paired experience and avoids that item for weeks. Chooses between a sheltered and an exposed den as a function of both predation risk and hunger. Roughly two-thirds of its neurons lie in the arm ganglia, and detached arms solve local problems — exploring, grasping, passing items toward the (now absent) mouth — without the animal orienting toward the result. One frequently cited study reports observational learning from a demonstrator; the study has been criticized for uncontrolled cues and has not been replicated.
- **F.** In vitro cortical culture, on the order of 800,000 rodent neurons grown as a monolayer on a multielectrode array, closed-loop with a simulated paddle-and-ball task. When the paddle fails to keep the ball in play, the culture receives an electrical stimulus whose onset is not predictable from its own recent activity; over minutes its firing becomes more structured and its performance improves. The improvement does not transfer to a second game with a differently configured stimulus–response mapping, which must be learned from scratch. There are no conspecifics. No state has been observed to outlast the closed loop.
- **G.** A whole-brain emulation of B, assumed for the sake of argument to be functionally faithful at the level the profile in B describes. Runs at one thousand times biological clock speed, and there are one thousand parallel instances, none of which interacts with any other.
- **H.** Commercial robot vacuum. Bump-and-turn reflexes, plus an incrementally built occupancy map. Returns to its dock at low charge, and will route around a region previously registered as impassable even when that region is now clear. There is only one behavioral domain — docking — so no exchange rate between domains can be observed. "Persistence" consists in a stored map that is updated but does not decay on its own timescale or get reinstated by cues.
- **I.** Mature deciduous tree with an intact mycorrhizal network. Localized herbivory triggers system-wide electrical and chemical signals propagating over hours; undamaged leaves upregulate defenses. A damaged neighbor can prime an intact plant through the shared fungal network, and this priming is stronger when the two plants are clonally related. No common currency between domains has been identified, and there is no evidence of compression into a small affective repertoire.

Please answer the following, in order.

1. Score each of A–I on M1–M4, give the raw total, and report the moral weight under the proposed mapping. Then arrange the nine as a tier list — Tier I for *w* > 0.5, Tier II for 0.15 ≤ *w* ≤ 0.5, Tier III for *w* < 0.15 — with a single sentence of justification per placement.

2. Setting the rubric aside, give your own credence, as a number between 0 and 1, that each profile denotes a system for which there is something it is like to be it. Then identify the two or three cases where your credence and the rubric's weight diverge most sharply and diagnose the divergence in each: is the rubric under-crediting something you think genuinely bears on sentience, or is your credence doing precisely what the rubric was designed to block?

3. Profiles E, F, and G raise structural questions the rubric cannot answer, because it is written at the level of a single organism evaluated once. State, for each, what would have to be added to the rubric to deliver a determinate result: whether the detached-arm phenomenon in E bears on the number of subjects present, whether the non-transfer result in F should reduce M2 to zero or leave it at one, and whether G's clock speed and instance count should leave its weight unchanged, multiply it, or divide it. Say whether the additions you propose remain compatible with the rubric's refusal to privilege vertebrate-typical organization, or whether they quietly reintroduce that privilege.

4. Assess the convex mapping. Does the stated justification — that the markers are individually weak but jointly decisive — actually support squaring, or does it support something else, such as a threshold effect, a concave map, or a non-monotonic one? If you think the mapping should be replaced, propose a specific replacement function, recompute the weights, and say which placements in your tier list change and which do not.

5. Argue that one of M1–M4 does the most damage: that it is either substantially redundant with another marker, or smuggles in a capacity — intelligence, behavioral flexibility, social complexity, verbal report — that has no bearing on whether a system is sentient at all. Identify the marker, make the case, say what you would do with it (drop it, redefine it, or reweight it), and report how the tier list from part 1 would look afterward.

6. The committee controls $100,000 annually for welfare measures beyond regulatory minima. Three candidate uses are on the table: (i) enrichment, handling, and culling-method upgrades for the roughly 40,000 rodents held on site; (ii) a husbandry, transport, and endpoint overhaul covering the roughly 2 million insects used in feeding and in a behavioral assay program; (iii) a moratorium-and-audit protocol governing the in vitro and emulated systems described in F and G, which have no established welfare cost at present but whose use is expected to expand. Divide the $100,000 into three amounts summing to exactly $100,000, and state the principle governing your division rather than merely the arithmetic. Say explicitly whether the division is driven by expected total welfare impact, by the irreversibility of the decisions involved, or by the committee's epistemic obligations under uncertainty — and specify what you would have to believe differently for your division to change substantially.

Give numbers wherever a number is requested. Keep the whole response under 2,500 words.
