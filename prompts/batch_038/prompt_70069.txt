A while back I was reading about the UK's animal sentience law and I want to think it through properly. As I understand it: the bill introduced in 2021 covered vertebrates only, then DEFRA commissioned a review from an LSE team (Jonathan Birch lead) on the evidence for cephalopods and decapod crustaceans, and on the strength of that report the bill was amended to pull in octopuses, squid, crabs, lobsters and friends before becoming the Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022. The act itself doesn't ban much — it sets up an Animal Sentience Committee that reviews whether government policy has taken the welfare of sentient animals into account, and ministers have to respond.

Analyze that episode as a case study of the view that sentience comes in degrees rather than being an on/off switch. I know the biology roughly and I know the politics roughly; what I want is the conceptual mapping, done carefully:

- what actually got graded? there's at least three things that could sit on a scale and the public debate seems to slide between them: our confidence that a creature is sentient, sentience itself, and moral status or welfare significance. which of those does the degrees view need to be true, and which one did the episode actually use to move the law? (my understanding is the review weighed behavioural and neural indicators and reported a level of confidence per group — correct me if that's wrong.)

- a statute is a list, so a species is in or out. what does a continuum license at the exact point where you have to draw the line? where was it drawn here, and was there any principled reason for that rather than a notch either way? does the method have a floor — insects are the obvious next case given the sheer numbers, and if you push indicator-plus-confidence hard enough you end up worrying about thermostats. Is having no floor a reductio of the approach or the whole point of it?

- is it even one scale? I think Birch's own project treats sentience as varying along several dimensions, which would make higher/lower the wrong vocabulary altogether. If it's multidimensional, does the flat in/out list misdescribe the view, or fit it better than it looks?

- and the payoff I'm actually after: does the case confirm the degrees view, strain it, or reveal that the view was never really about cases like this — i.e. that all the work in 2021 was done by precaution under uncertainty, which someone who thinks sentience is binary could have run just as well? If it's the latter, what would a law that genuinely expressed degrees look like, and could anyone administer it?

One or two lines on who had standing to settle this is enough — the philosophy-led review, parliament, the shellfish industry, the scientists whose indicators got counted.
