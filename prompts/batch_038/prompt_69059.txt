The setup. I own a payments service. We take roughly 40 million requests a day. Any request that reaches the payment handler is supposed to get an idempotency key attached, so that a retry doesn't double-charge a customer. Call that hypothesis H: every request that reaches the payment handler gets an idempotency key.

Our logging pipeline is nice and uniform, which is what got me into trouble. Every request, whatever route it's on, emits one line with two boolean fields: `reached_payment` and `got_key`. So a health check, a search query, an auth token refresh, a static asset — they all show up as `reached_payment=false, got_key=false`. About 97% of our daily log volume is lines like that.

Here's the argument I gave a colleague, and he said it was obviously broken but couldn't tell me where:

1. Nicod's criterion: seeing an A that is B confirms 'all A are B', and seeing an A that isn't B disconfirms it.
2. 'All A are B' is logically equivalent to 'all non-B are non-A', and logically equivalent hypotheses have to be confirmed by the same observations — otherwise whether a log line counts as evidence would depend on how I happened to phrase the alert rule.
3. So seeing a thing that isn't B and isn't A confirms 'all A are B'.
4. The contrapositive of H is: every request that did not get an idempotency key is a request that did not reach the payment handler.
5. So each of those ~39 million `false, false` lines in a day's logs confirms H. And I checked, they genuinely are independent observations — separate connections, seperate pods, no shared state between them, so I'm not double-counting one event a million times.
6. If each observation confirms H, then more observations confirm it more. Confirmation accumulates.
7. So one day of logs raises the probability of H by tens of millions of increments, which means H is as good as certain: an idempotency bug simply cannot survive our log volume.
8. But that's ridiculous. H could be false tomorrow morning. I could merge a PR that breaks keying on one payment route and take the whole edifice down with it, and it'd be a bug I shipped myself while the logs were cheering.
9. Therefore 1–3 are broken. Irrelevant observations confirm nothing whatsoever, the raven business is a trick of notation, and confirmation is not logic-shaped.
