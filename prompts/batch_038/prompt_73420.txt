Our prof has a move he uses on basically every metaethics question, and it's Quine's web: moral claims sit in the same fabric as physics, just way out from the observational edge, and since the analytic/synthetic line is gone there's no principled place to cut where science stops and values start. The conclusion he wants is that the is/ought gap and the charge that moral claims aren't truth-apt are just hangovers from the dogmas he refuted, and that moral disagreement gets adjudicated holistically, the same way theoretical disagreement does. When a student asked what plays the role of recalitrant experience for something like 'cruelty for fun is wrong,' he said: suffering, social breakdown, the practical wreckage of getting it wrong. Another student said that's just empirical sociology doing the work, with the moral claim itself never actually touched by any possible evidence — and the prof said fine, but that's true of the deepest parts of physics too, so it's a diffrence of degree, not kind.

Can you audit the analogy for me? Give the correspondence table (what's the periphery here, what's the interior, what counts as a tug on the edge, what plays the role of predictive success), then sort the disanalogies into ones that genuinely threaten his conclusion and ones that are harmless. I'd also like to know whether the web picture, pressed hard, cuts the other way — something about deep posits being earned by how well they organize experience. And if it can't carry the weight, what analogy would?
