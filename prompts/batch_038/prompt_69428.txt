Our department's graduate seminar is hosting a one-hour talk by a researcher who has spent several years on the embedded agency problem set — the view that an agent is physically part of the world it models, so no model of that world can be complete, and that self-models in particular are reflexively entangled with the behaviour they describe. Her thesis is that once agency is understood this way, the free will debate dissolves rather than gets settled: neither libertarian freedom nor hard determinism is even well posed for an embedded reasoner, because both assume the agent stands outside its own causal story.

Please draft the eight questions I should put to her, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. I want the questions to engage the actual machinery of her position rather than invite her to repeat the framing. Specifically, work in:

- the circularity worry: if her self-model informs her deliberation, and her deliberation changes what the self-model should say, what stops the whole structure from being self-undermining, and how she distinguishes a principled fixpoint from a convenient vagueness;
- whether "incompleteness in principle" is doing real philosophical work or is just bounded computation dressed up — every implementation faces resource limits, and I want her pushed on why a finite memory budget counts as a discovery about the self rather than a platitude about engineering;
- the asymmetry between prediction and decision: if her embedded agent could in principle build a self-model accurate enough to forecast its own choice, does she forbid that in principle or only in practice, and what either answer costs her;
- whether the account licenses fatalism or blocks it, given that on her picture the agent's reasoning is itself among the causes of what it does;
- and, last, the payoff question: what does her dissolution forbid that a standard physicalist compatibilism already permits, and what claim of theirs would she be committed to giving up.

No warm-up questions about what drew her to the topic. Assume she has read the surrounding literature closely and will recognize a soft question immediately.
