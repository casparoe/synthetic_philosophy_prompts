Been reading the rationalist corner of the internet for like fifteen years now — sequences, the Goodharts law threads, all of it — and Jurassic Park gets invoked as a cautionary parable constantly, usually in a lazy "the hubris!" way. I'm putting together a 20 minute talk for a mostly non-technical meetup group on specification gaming, and I want to use the movie (and Crichton's novel, which is way more explicit about the mechanics) as the spine of it. But I don't want to just paste a story onto a framework and call it analysis. So I want a real reading, one that's willing to say where the lens doesn't fit.

The specific thing I want to stress: that paper by Manheim and Garrabrant sorting Goodhart effects into four buckets — regressional (you select on an imperfect proxy so you also select the noise), extremal (the proxy-goal relationship held in the range you observed it in and you've now pushed out of that range), causal (the regulator's own intervention breaks the link), and adversarial (someone with different goals games your metric). Which of the park's failures actually lands in which bucket? My intuition is that people lump everything under one label when there are at least three structurally different things going on in there.

Cases I care about: in the book, the tally system was built so operators could type in the expected number of animals, which is why nobody noticed they were breeding — the instrument was designed to return the answer they wanted. Then the breeding controls themselves, all-female chromosomes plus the lysine dependency and the irradiated gonadal tissue, versus the fact that they filled genome gaps with frog DNA, which let some animals change sex. Also those Poisson distribution graphs Arnold shows off, which Malcolm reads as evidence of a normal wild population rather than a controlled one. And Nedry's backdoor, which masked the fence status in the control room so Arnold kept trusting the screens while the fences were dead. Is sabotaging the instrument even a Goodhart effect? Nedry isn't optimizing the park's proxy, he's optimizing embryo theft, and it feels to me like the taxonomy might need a fifth slot for "the measurement channel itself is compromised." Tell me if I'm wrong about that.

Second question, and this is the one I actually care about: is Malcolm's whole bit about life finding a way a Goodhart claim at all, or is it a different thesis about uncontrollability in complex systems that the story conflates with the measurement failures? And is Malcolm right for the right reasons, or is he a broken clock — would his chaos spiel have explained literally any way the park fell apart, including one with no metric in it? If he's unfalsifiable, I need to know before I build a talk around him.

Finally, be honest about where the reading strains. Grant's arc with the kids and the big T. rex awe moments have nothing to do with proxies, and the novel is more cynical about corporate incentives than the movie is. Say what the work gives the debate that an argument in a paper can't — I suspect it's something about who got to pick the metric and why nobody had standing to check it — and where a critic should just admit the movie is a monster movie and stop.
