Reading group meets in a pub, half of us do philosophy of mind, and every session ends with somebody telling a terrible Kim joke. Write me a better one. It has to actually turn on the content of the exclusion argument, not just namecheck it: the nothing-left-to-do step (if the physical realiser is already doing all the causal work, then the higher-level property either just is that realiser or it's an extra cause piled on top), plus Kim's complaint that the nonreductive route only buys you mental causation at the price of rampant systematic overdetermination, which he thinks no one should swallow. Bonus if the same joke works on the strong emergence / downward causation crowd too, since that's the version of the argument our biologist keeps reinventing. Two rules: dont make it a pun on the word reduction, and no walks-into-a-bar or knock-knock format. Best case it's funny from both directions — somebody who thinks exclusion is decisive and somebody who thinks it just assumes what it needs to prove should each get something out of the punchline. Keep it short enough to actually say out loud over a pint.
