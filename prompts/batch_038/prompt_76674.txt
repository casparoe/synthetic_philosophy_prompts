The topic was whether the pessimistic meta-induction actually works. You know the argument — Laudan's thing, every past scientific theory posited stuff that turned out not to exist (phlogiston, caloric, the luminiferous ether), those theories still made accurate predictions, so we should expect the unobservable posits of our *current* theories to be false too, and scientific realism is dead on arrival. Maya was arguing the pessimistic induction works, Dev was arguing it doesn't. Here's roughly how it went, in order:

1. **Dev:** The induction base is garbage. Phlogiston theory couldn't explain why metals *gain* weight when you heat them in air. Caloric got killed by measurable stuff — Joule showing you can convert work into a definite amount of heat, so heat isn't a conserved fluid. The ether was posited to carry light waves and then Michelson-Morley couldn't find it. So the theories realists get embarrassed about weren't empirically successful in the first place. And the ones that *were* successful kept most of their content — Newton's predictions survive inside relativity as a limiting case, oxygen is still oxygen. Run the induction over genuinely successful theories and it points at approximate truth, not at falsehood.

2. **Maya:** That's completely question-begging, and it's the exact move the pessimist gets to forbid. To say phlogiston theory "wasn't successful" you have to already assume phlogiston didn't exist — which is the thing under debate. And to say Newton "survives" in relativity you need our current theories as the yardstick. The whole point of the meta-induction is that you can't grade the past by the present, because the present is what's on trial.

3. **Dev:** No, I can grade it ontology-neutrally. There's a sense of "successful" that doesn't care what exists: did the theory let you predict measurable quantities to high precision, and did the successor preserve those predictions? Newton misses Mercury's orbit and nails basically everything else to a lot of digits; Einstein keeps nearly all of that and adds Mercury. So I never needed to claim phlogiston is unreal to claim phlogiston theory failed. I only said its predictions broke down.

4. **Maya:** Fine, but look what you've bought yourself. So your continuity result either includes ontology, in which case it's question-begging, or excludes it, in which case it saves nothing a realist actually cares about. So you're computing a failure rate from a sample that was selected for failure.
