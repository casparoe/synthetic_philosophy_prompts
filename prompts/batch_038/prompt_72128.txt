So I've bascially bought the multiple drafts picture — there's no single place or instant where things come together for a subject, and any ordering someone reports is itself just another draft, not a readout of some earlier fact about their experience. But I want to know what would actually count against this.

Suppose a lab reported that subjects' order judgements for three near-simultaneous flashes were completely immune to anything that happened afterwards: no probe, no later question, no mask, no instruction could shift them, and the reports tracked one localized neural event with millisecond precision. Is that a genuine refutation, or does the view just absorb it by saying fine, that's a case where a draft happened to be stable?

More generally, lay out the candidate defeaters for me — which would show the position false, which merely force a qualification, and which a defender should happily concede as awkward. And be straight about whether it's been insulated into unfalsifiability by treating every report as a possible revision, and if it has, whether that's a real cost here or whether the view was never making a timing claim in the first place.
