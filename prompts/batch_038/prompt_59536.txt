Third year, decision theory, and my second chapter is basically one long case study in transformative experience. Before I hand it in I want to war-game what my supervisor will do with it, because she has a weirdly consistent record of calling these things in seminar and she never hedges.

Her calls over the past two years, as best I've reconstructed them:

- Deciding whether to have a first child: blocked. No amount of babysitting, memoirs, or interrogating your sister gets you the utilities you'd need.
- Deciding whether to enlist, having read everything and interviewed twenty veterans: still blocked. She was emphatic that testimony does no work at all here.
- Elective back surgery where the recovery is unlike anything you've felt and nothing about you changes as a person: blocked. You cant weigh six weeks of that against a straight spine.
- Starting a PhD in your 40s knowing it will reshape your friendships, your ambitions, the way you talk: not blocked. "You know the shape of it."
- A two-week trial in a role before you sign anything, with a clean walk-away: not blocked.
- A year-long secondment you can quit at any time: not blocked.
- Moving cities, trying a fruit you've never eaten, first time in a glider: not blocked, just ordinary uncertainty, do the math.

Now my case, which is the one I'm nervous about.

Ines is 34 and has been offered a place in a program that permanently and one-way rewires what she cares about. Two features make it unusual rather than a vanilla conversion. First, admission requires a two-week dry run: a neural replay that makes her, for the duration, exactly the person she'd become — values included, not just sensations — with a guarantee that she reverts on day fifteen keeping full first-person memory of the whole thing. She did the dry run in March. She can tell you what it was like to be post-Ines, and she can tell you that post-Ines was glad. Second, the outcome data is public: 98% of completers endorse the choice from where they now stand, and the 2% who don't are contactable, but the program's own researchers describe their reports as untranslatable into terms a pre-procedure person can use.

What I keep circling: the dry run hands her everything the enlistment case was missing. It isn't testimony, it isn't imagination, she was there. But it was borrowed — she had the endorsement without having the values that make it an endorsement, and now she's back to being someone who can't feel them. She also says that since March she finds she can't take the 2% seriously anymore, which is either good news or evidence that the dry run has already compromised her as a judge of her own future. And the real thing is irreversible.

So: blocked or not blocked? I want the mechanics rather than just the label. Which two or three rules are equally consistent with all seven of her calls — phenomenal ignorance, self-change, reversibility, endorsement-on-reflection, some hybrid of two of those — and what does each one say about Ines? Then commit to a single prediction, tell me which of her past cases is the closest analog and why, and say what she'd have to add or drop to get there.

And since I'm picking the discussion case for the next department meeting: design me one she can't get through without either breaking her pattern or revealing she's been running something much narrower than anyone thinks. Ideally it splits whatever rules my Ines case leaves tied.

Also flag it if you think any of her seven calls are already in tension — I have a suspicion the 40s-PhD one and the enlistment one are doing different work than she realises.
