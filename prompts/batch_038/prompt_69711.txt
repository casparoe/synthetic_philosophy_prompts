I'm the junior editor on a one-volume reference encyclopedia of political movements — think a beefed-up dictionary of modern ideologies, one entry per movement, and every entry gets a running-head tag: Left, Right, or Syncretic. The senior editor makes all the tagging calls and she's extremely protective of them, so before I propose a tag on the next entry I want to work out what rule she's actually applying.

Her rulings so far, with the bit of each movement that matters:

- Bolsheviks, and the 1945 Labour government in Britain — public ownership of the major industries, plus a program of radical levelling: Left.
- Anarcho-syndicalists, e.g. the Spanish CNT — production run by workers' collectives, and they wanted the state abolished entirely: Left.
- The Diggers of 1649 — dug up and farmed common land collectively, wanted the enclosures pulled down and the commons put back to common use: Left.
- Marxist national-liberation movements, Vietnam and Algeria — the whole program framed as the liberation of one particular people, and openly impatient with universalist, cosmopolitan talk: Left.
- The Manchester free traders — laissez-faire and low tariffs; their pitch was that free exchange lifted every nation on earth and would end wars, and they held that whatever inequality a free market threw up wasn't a wrong to be corrected: Right.
- Anarcho-capitalists — private property, no state at all, and a theory written for any rational agent anywhere: Right.
- Postwar Christian Democrats — built out quite generous welfare states, but defended the family, the churches, and an organic social order with natural differences of station: Right.
- Thatcherism — privatization, plus a deliberate demolition of the postwar settlement she'd inherited: Right.
- Peronism — the only Syncretic tag in the whole volume. Her note says it nationalized and redistributed like a left movement while organizing everything around one man and one nation.

The next entry is the school choice movement: public money attached to the child rather than to the school, so a family can carry it to whichever school they pick, private and religious ones included. Supporters argue that a poor kid trapped in a failing school is being denied the one thing that sets the terms of their whole life, and that the current arrangement effectively sells an education bundled with a house price. Opponents argue it hollows out the common schools that everyone else has to keep working at, and hands public money to institutions that get to select on faith and ability. Both camps are sincere, and both claim the egalitarian high ground.

So what tag will she put on it? Tell me which rule you think she's running, and also which other candidate rules fit everything above just as well — I can think of at least a couple that look live until you actually check them against the list, and I want to know which ones are dead on arrival and what killed them. Then say which of her precedents is my closest analog if I have to defend a proposed tag to her in the meeting, and be honest about what principle the volume commits itself to whichever way this one goes, since a reference work's tag scheme is basically an implicit theory of what the left/right distinction is.

Also: name one further movement, real or invented, that would tell me whether her tag tracks what a movement wants to happen or how it wants to get there, because I suspect thats the actual fork in the road, and Peronism is the only place she's ever admitted the two coming apart. And then step back for a sec — does her record look like evidence that there's a single property all left-wing movements share, or a handful of properties that just happen to have travelled together since the French Revolution, or no real property at all, just a name for whichever coalition is currently facing off against the other one? Whichever you land on, tell me what in the list above is doing the work. And dont fob me off with "Syncretic, obviously" for the new entry unless you can say what makes Peronism genuinely different from it.
