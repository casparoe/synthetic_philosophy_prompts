I am a third-year philosophy PhD student. For the past year I have been embedded in a vision lab, partly to learn the methods and partly because my dissertation concerns whether perceptual experience can be altered by what the perceiver believes. Last week two postdocs in the lab, Nadia and Ezra, got into an argument during a lab meeting about a dataset Nadia had just collected. I was present, took notes, and did not intervene; I am reporting it to you as a bystander, and I do not want you to guess which side I prefer, because I genuinely could not tell. I would like your adjudication.

The design, as Nadia described it:

The stimulus is a bistable sphere defined by orthographically projected moving dots — the classic structure-from-motion display, in which the direction of rotation is ambiguous and reverses spontaneously every few seconds. Before each trial, a written word appears for 300 ms, either the word naming clockwise rotation or the word naming counterclockwise rotation. There are two kinds of block. In Informative blocks the word correctly predicts the eventual rotation direction on about 75% of trials. In Sham blocks the word is drawn at random, and the running contingency is displayed continuously on screen so that participants can see for themselves that it sits at chance. There are three dependent measures: (1) the participant's report of the direction they first saw; (2) the mean dominance duration of the cued direction during a five-second presentation; and (3) a point-of-subjective-equality measure on a two-interval task using mixtures of clockwise and counterclockwise dot motions, scored for bias, with equal payoffs in both intervals and no accuracy incentive either way. In a fourth condition the word is masked so that participants perform at chance on a separate cue-identification test.

The results: in Informative blocks, all three measures shift with the cue, which everyone expected. In Sham blocks, measure (1) shifts by about four percentage points above chance and is significant; measure (2) shows no shift at all; measure (3) shows a significant shift of roughly six percent of the full range. The Sham-block effects survive a post-experiment debrief in which participants confirm they understood the cue carried no information, and they survive per-trial accuracy payment. In the masked-cue condition, the shift on measure (3) is about the same size as in the Sham condition.

Here is the exchange, in seven moves, as nearly as I can reconstruct it.

1. Nadia: This is cognitive penetration. Measure (3) is not a report about whether the cue was followed; it is a bias in the point of subjective equality under equal payoffs, in participants who know the cue is uninformative. There is no strategy available that would produce it and no compliance incentive that would explain it. The perceptual system is not informationally encapsulated, and the encapsulation thesis is empirically false for this case.

2. Ezra: A shifted point of subjective equality is precisely what a shifted decision criterion predicts. In a two-interval task with near-ambiguous mixtures, an observer whose prior over responses leans one way will show a bias with no change whatever in the visual representation. And measure (2) is the tell: dominance durations in bistable displays track the strength of the underlying perceptual representation, so if the representation had changed, rivalry dynamics should have changed too. They didn't. The pattern is what you would expect if the locus of the effect is at decision, not in the percept.

3. Nadia: Then explain the masked condition. Participants in that condition cannot identify the cue above chance, so the cue cannot make either response label more accessible or more available to them — there is no content they could comply with, no label they could favor. Yet the bias is the same size. A cue that nobody can report still shifts a fine-grained visual judgment, which places the effect upstream of report and upstream of response selection.

4. Ezra: That result cuts against you, not for you. A masked cue that cannot be identified is not a belief, an expectation, or a thought — it is a subliminal prime, and subliminal priming is a bottom-up associative process. If your effect is carried by the masked condition, you have shown only that the visual system is sensitive to recently presented stimuli, which nobody has ever denied. And in the Sham condition, where the word is actually read, you have something that looks like compliance or attention. So you have two unrelated mechanisms, not one phenomenon, and the design gives you no way to unify them.

5. Nadia: Grant that the masked route is associative and set it aside. The Sham effect remains, and it remains after we made the cue's uninformative status explicit, let participants verify it on screen, and paid them for accuracy. A rational observer should assign that cue zero weight, so on any inferential account of perception — perception as the system's best estimate given evidence and prior — the bias should have vanished. It did not. That is penetration that no inference licenses, which is exactly why it matters.

6. Ezra: It is irrational only on your account of what the observer is doing. Setting order effects aside — your Sham blocks followed your Informative ones, and a slowly learned prior need not have caught up — there is a simpler story: on near-ambiguous mixtures, going along with the cue costs the participant essentially nothing in accuracy. A response-level bias appears exactly where it is free and disappears where it is costly, and that is what you found. Cost-sensitivity is a signature of the decision stage, not of the perceptual stage.

7. Nadia: If the effect lives at the decision stage, it should be largest where the participant has the most latitude to be strategic, and smallest where the response is tightly constrained by a graded perceptual judgment. Yours predicts the reverse of what I observed. And on your final point: if a word naming a global direction of motion can direct attention to that direction, and attention directed in that way changes what is seen, then the encapsulation thesis has been defined so as to be unfalsifiable — which is a reason to abandon the thesis, not a reason to dismiss my data.
