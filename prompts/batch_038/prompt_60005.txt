ok so here's where I've landed on the problem of old evidence, and I want you to attack it rather than explain it back to me.

My view: nothing is ever confirmed by evidence the theory was built around, full stop. The Mercury perihelion case only looks like a puzzle because we picture a single probability function being handed general relativity and the anomoly in the same instant. Real inquiry has an order to it, and what earns a theory credit isn't that it raises the probability of some datum — it's that the datum was use-novel, that the theory got constructed without access to it. So the right measure of support compares the actual history of the theorist's work against a counterfactual history where the data had been sitting on the table the whole time. Support is a property of the process, not of a conditional. Old evidence doesn't confirm, it never did, and conditionalization doesn't need repairing so much as replacing with something that can represent the order of inquiry.

What would count against this? I want the cases and arguments that actually bite, ranked by how much damage they do. A few I can see coming, tell me if they're as bad as I think:

- are there situations where a theory tailored to the data still obviously gains support from fitting it — say a fit that's extremely tight with very few free parameters — and does my view have to just deny that?
- same data, two different scientists: one knew the perihelion numbers while building the theory, one didn't. My view seems forced to say the theory is better supported for one of them than the other, and I'm not sure I can live with that. How much of the account survives if I try to relativize to a community or a research program instead of the individual, and does that just move the problem?
- the one that worries me most: that use-novelty is a crude proxy for something purely statistical, a theory tuned to the data has spent its free parameters on it, so the whole "order of inquiry" business collapses into a Bayesian account with better bookkeeping rather than replacing it. How far does that collapse actually go — is there any residue my view explains that the statistical story doesn't?

Also: what does my position predict that a straight conditionalization-repair view doesn't predict? Is there anything I'd notice if it turned out to be false, or have I hedged it into something that can't be contradicted — and if I have, is that a bigger deal here than it would be for some metaphysical thesis, given that this is supposed to be a theory about evidence and support? Finally, which of these would a committed use-novelty person concede as genuinely counting against them and which would they wave off, and where do you think I should actually move?
