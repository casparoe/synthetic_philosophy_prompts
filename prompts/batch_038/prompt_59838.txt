I am assembling a set of constructed cases for a seminar on emotion in the arts, and this one is meant to test whether the three standard families of solution to the paradox of tragedy can be told apart by an ordinary psychological profile rather than by argument. Since that fixes what counts as cheating, let me be explicit: the scenario must *generate* the verdicts from the accounts themselves, not assert them. No stipulation may name an account or deploy its technical vocabulary, and no stipulation may state a conclusion — you may not write that the viewer's sadness is "full-blooded" rather than "quasi," nor that her pleasure is "in the sadness itself" as a theoretical label. Everything must be presented as concrete, checkable fact about one person and one film, from which a reader holding only the glosses below can derive each verdict independently.

The three accounts, so that nothing outside them is needed:

1. **Hume's conversion account** (his essay "Of Tragedy"): the painful passion in an audience is not merely outweighed by a contrary pleasure but *converted* into pleasure by the force of the execution — the eloquence, artistry, and beauty of the work. What the spectator enjoys is the art, with the pain supplying the material that is transposed.
2. **Walton's make-believe account** (1978): what a spectator feels toward a fiction is a quasi-emotion — a state with much of the phenomenology and physiology of sadness, but caused by and deployed within a game of make-believe, and lacking the beliefs and behavioral dispositions of real sadness. On this view no one is genuinely saddened, so no one is both saddened and pleased.
3. **Feagin's meta-response account** ("The Pleasures of Tragedy," 1983): the spectator has direct responses to what is depicted, which are painful, and meta-responses to those direct responses, which can be pleasant — pleasure taken in her own sympathetic capacity. The pleasure of tragedy is the metapleasure, not the direct pain.

Now build me a single case meeting all of the following specifications.

**Specifications for the scenario**

1. The film is a low-budget fictional dramatization of a real event, but both the film and the event must be invented: name no actual disaster, artwork, director, or performer. The viewer is an ordinary person with an ordinary life and a specific, unexotic reason for returning to the film once a year. No science fiction, no neuroscience, no pharmacology, no artificial intelligence; the only permitted manipulation of the situation is an alternative edit of the film.
2. Give facts that make plain she grieves for people she takes to have existed and suffered, and that the film is the occasion of her grief rather than its object. Show this through what she says, what she does in the days before and after, and what she would refuse to accept as a substitute. Do not label the state.
3. Do **not** stipulate that the film lacks aesthetic merit. Describe its execution concretely — delivery, camerawork, sound, pacing, dialogue — so that whether a Humean has the raw material she needs remains a live, arguable question. If the film is flat, make it flat in a particular way that a defender of the conversion account might or might not be able to claim as eloquence.
4. Supply her reports and dispositions concerning her own tears: what she says about crying, what she does while doing it, what she would accept and what she would refuse if offered. These must bear hard on the meta-response account without ever stating that no meta-response occurs.
5. Include the isolating intervention: two cuts of the film that differ only in the final stretch — in one, the aftermath is shown at length; in the other, those minutes are replaced by an equally drab but emotionally inert coda. Her pleasure tracks the difference; her judgment of the quality of the filmmaking does not. Describe both cuts and her reports on each.
6. Close off the surrogate sources of pleasure by fact rather than by exclusion: make evident that the pleasure cannot be relief, curiosity-satisfaction, the pleasure of understanding, social pleasure, sensory pleasure, or catharsis-as-lightening — for instance, she watches alone, knows precisely what will happen, has watched it a dozen times, feels no lighter afterward, and would decline, at no cost to herself, an offer to skip the final stretch every year.
7. The scenario itself must be at most two paragraphs. The analysis may run as long as it needs, under the headings below.

**Deliverables**

A. *Verdicts.* For each of the three accounts: what it predicts in her case, which features of the case are doing the blocking work, and whether the block is decisive or merely expensive — say what the defender retains and what she has to give up.

B. *The load-bearing stipulation.* Name the single feature whose removal lets at least one of the three accounts back in. Remove it explicitly, then say what changes for each of the three.

C. *Coherence.* Are the specifications jointly satisfiable? If any of them renders the psychology impossible, self-undermining, or question-begging against one account, identify it and give the minimal repair that preserves the design's purpose.

D. *Yield.* Does the case accomplish anything that an argument does not? State whether it is a genuine counterexample to any of the three accounts or a vivid restatement of the paradox in narrative form, and if the latter, say which stipulations are carrying the illusion of a counterexample.

E. *The fourth option.* If all three accounts fail here, say what the case points toward instead — a mixed state in which the pleasure and the sadness are not two episodes, an evaluative rather than a felt attitude toward one's own grief, or something further — and what that costs. In particular, address whether it can explain why the pleasure *requires* the sadness rather than merely coexisting with it.

Answer from general knowledge. Do not recommend readings, and do not cite studies, articles, or artworks beyond the three accounts glossed above; the case should be fully adjudicable by someone who knows nothing of the literature beyond those glosses.
