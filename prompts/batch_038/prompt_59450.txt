I was in the kitchen of my hall of residence last night while two second-years who take far too much philosophy — Rosie and Dan — got into a proper argument about vagueness that ran for well over an hour and ended with both of them refusing to speak to each other's points. It started from something completely mundane: our buildings contract says the communal lawn has to be kept 'tidy', the warden had fined one of the flats, and Dan said the word is vague so there's no fact of the matter whether the fine was deserved, while Rosie said obviously there is, there's a sharp line somewhere, we just can't find it. Rosie is an epistemicist, Dan's a supervaluationist, and once that came out it went fully technical.

Here's roughly the order it happened in. (1) Dan: if 'tidy' has any sharp cut-off at all, then there's some exact number of leaves such that 4,000 on the lawn is tidy and 4,001 isn't, and that's absurd, because nothing in how we actually use the word could possibly settle it at that leaf rather than one earlier or one later — so there's no line, and claims about borderline cases are neither true nor false. (2) Rosie: fine, but you still have to say 'either the lawn is tidy or it isn't' is true, since that's an instance of excluded middle and your whole selling point is keeping classical logic — so you've got a disjunction you call true when neither of the two ways it could be true is true, which isn't saving the logic, it's a conjuring trick. And her favourite bit: you only get to keep the gap at the first level, because ask whether it's definitely definitely tidy and you're straight back in trouble, so either borderline-ness is itself vague and you need precisifications of precisifications all the way up, or it isn't and you've got exactly the sharp boundary you objected to, just one storey higher. (3) Dan said he'd grant the higher-order worry is awkward for the naive version but that you handle it by iterating, and that Rosie's own view is worse anyway: she's committed to a meaning that could never be discovered even by an ideal investigator who knew every fact about our usage, and a theory of meaning that makes the meanings permanently unknowable hasn't explained anything, it's just hung a veil over the thing it was supposed to account for. (4) Rosie said that's not a cost, it's a prediction, because there are independent reasons — something about safety and margins for error, she didn't spell it out — why we couldn't know where the line is even if there is one, and that being unable to locate a boundary isn't evidence against its being there, any more than our being unable to count the blades of grass shows there isn't a number of them. (5) Dan: so then your view and mine agree about everything anyone could ever check, and differ only over an unknowable extra fact, which means it isn't a real disagreement, it's my theory with a metaphysical decoration bolted on. Rosie said no, they genuinely differ, though when pressed she could only say it was about bivalence, and Dan said bivalence failing changes nothing you could ever detect either. (6) His parting shot, which she didn't really answer: when a boundary really is sharp — 'bins out by 8am', 'eighteen or over' — that's because somebody decided it; wherever nobody's decided anything there's no line; so sharpness needs an author, and 'tidy' has no author. She said that our usage *is* the decision, just spread across millions of speakers and uncoordinated, which he called a metaphor doing the work of an argument.

Could you go through it move by move and say who's actually right at each of the six steps rather than just who won the night — a rating for each, with the reason? I'd particularly like to know whether the higher-order point in (2) really does force Dan either into an infinite stack of precisifications or into a sharp boundary one level up, whether (3) lands on Rosie or whether she's got an answer to it, and whether (5) is right that the two views make no detectable difference — because if there is somewhere they'd come apart in practice, I want to know where you'd go looking for it, in terms of what someone could say or bet on or work out. And if there's something both of them were missing that would have defused the whole thing twenty minutes earlier, tell me what it is.
