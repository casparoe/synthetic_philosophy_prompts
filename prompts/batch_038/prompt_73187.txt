ok so I'm putting together a little zine about direct democracy and I want a parody piece for it. The genre I want sent up is the Swiss federal voting booklet — that red pamphlet that lands in every household before an Abstimmung, with the Federal Council's bloodless explanation of what you're voting on, the initiative committee's own allotted paragraph, the counter-proposal, and the tie-break question. I want you to invent an initiative and write the booklet entry for it, in that exact register.

The initiative: let's call it the Schlusspunkt-Initiative (Final Word Initiative). It amends the constitution to abolish the right of popular initiative altogether. The joke is that its reasoning is the purest, most unembarrassed version of what initiative proponents actually say all the time — the people should have the last word, parliament keeps watering things down in committee, the instrument has been captured by well-funded parties and used as a campaign device rather than a way of governing. Take all of that completely straight and run it to the end: if the people are soverign, then the sovereign decision is the decision to stop deciding.

Format and structure — please use these sections, in this order:

1. **Die Vorlage auf einen Blick** (~80 words): the boxed overview. What's being voted on, the recommendation of Bundesrat and Parlament, whether there's a counter-proposal, whether there's a Stichfrage.
2. **Text der Initiative** (~80 words): the constitutional article as it would actually be worded, including one transitional provision that is quietly doing something legally horrible.
3. **Die Argumente des Bundesrates** (~300 words): the government's explanation. Crucially it must be internally honest — it cannot declare the initiative invalid, because the grounds on which parliament may do that (unity of form, unity of subject matter, peremptory norms of international law) don't obviously cover abolishing the initiative right. So it has to recommend rejection on other grounds while audibly not wanting to say what those grounds are. Include a fiscal/cost estimate that is technically correct and completely absurd.
4. **Stellungnahme des Initiativkomitees** (~120 words): their own paragraph, which should be genuinely persuasive in the way these paragraphs are.
5. **Direkter Gegenentwurf und Stichfrage** (~120 words): parliament trying to out-democracy the democrats, and a tie-break question that cannot actually be answered.

Then one more section after the piece:

6. **What I was actually satirising** (~200 words): name the two or three features of the machinery you exaggerated, and say why a specialist would recognise each as a real feature rather than something you invented.

Some ground rules. Keep the whole thing in English but leave the official headings and the technical terms (Stichfrage, Ständemehr, Gegenentwurf) in German, glossing them only where a reader would otherwise be lost. The double majority of people and cantons should come up, and someone should be counting on the half-cantons. Deadpan throughout — the text should never say or imply that any of this is ridiculous, no winking, no explanatory footnotes; the reader has to feel it. Don't quote or reproduce wording from any real booklet, real initiative or real campaign — invent all of it. And keep real politicians and real committees out of it; the target is the practice and the discourse, not anybody.
