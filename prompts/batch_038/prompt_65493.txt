the quantity we call "user preference" in our rankers isn't a preference in any sense that can bear weight. I went and read revealed preference theory to work out what we'd be committing to if we claimed our model recovers what users actually want, and it turns out that observed choice only licenses an ordering when the choices satisfy consistency axioms — the weak and strong axioms, plus the contraction/expansion consistency conditions on choice functions. Our logged data violates those systematically and on purpose, because changing the menu is literally the job. A recommender is a machine for making the choice set non-stationary, so choices-relative-to-our-ranking can't reveal an ordering, which means the ranker isn't measuring a latent thing at all, it's measuring a thing we co-produce. and even where the axioms do hold, consistency is silent on the difference between wanting something, being committed to something, and having adapted to wanting something because we've been feeding you a narrow menu for three years.
