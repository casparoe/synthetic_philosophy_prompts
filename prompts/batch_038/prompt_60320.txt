Got handed this at a reading group and I can't shake it — it looks like it does way more damage then anyone in the room intended, including to a view I was pretty sympathetic to.

(1) Right after a measurement you don't know which of your successors you'll be, so your credence in an outcome should just be the fraction of successors who observe it.
(2) Applied to a spin prepared with unequal amplitudes, that gives half/half for any two-outcome measurement — you count branches.
(3) Branch counting contradicts the Born rule, so the Everettian has to reject it.
(4) Her reason: branch number isn't well defined. The decomposition into branches is only approximate, and any branch can be re-sliced into as many sub-branches as you like, so "how many successors" has no answer.
(5) But decoherence is ubiquitous, so exactly the same goes for a coin landing heads or whether it rains tomorrow — those are branching events with no well-defined successor count either.
(6) So an Everettian has no grounding for credences about anything, not just about quantum measurements.
(7) A theory that can't underwrite my 1/2 credence in heads is refuted by the fact that I rationally have such credences and they track frequencies.
(8) Therefore (1) is false, and the self-locating route to the Born rule goes down with it.

My gut says (5) is where it derails, but I can't explain why without handing the Everettian back the branch counting she's trying to get rid of. Which step would you reject, what made it look innocent in the first place, and what does giving it up cost? And is there any way to fix it that doesn't just quietly reinstall the weighting as a primitive?
