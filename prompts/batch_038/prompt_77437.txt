The case. A coin lives in a box. Every day at noon it is tossed inside the box, unobserved, and the result is posted at midnight. This world also contains a device I'll call the Auditor, which reads my brain continuously. The stipulation that makes it a case: the best system for this world — the one that is simplest and strongest, that does everything chance is supposed to do — assigns the chance of today's noon toss landing heads like this. At every moment t before the toss, chance_t(heads) = 1 if my credence at t that today's toss lands heads is below 1/2, and 0 otherwise. After the toss it behaves however you like. Its a real objective chance in your sense, not a prediction the Auditor makes about me, and the reason my credence is an input is just that my credence is physically realized in my head and my head is part of the mosaic — so Humean supervenience looks like it licenses exactly this. And don't escape by having the Auditor take one snapshot at 9am and then freeze the chance for the day: I've stipulated it's live at every moment, which is anyway how time-indexed chance is supposed to work.

My opening move. There is no credence that satisfies the Principal Principle in this world. If my credence is below 1/2 then the chance is 1, and I know that, since I know the chance function and I know what my own credence is, so PP tells me to be at 1 — contradiction. If my credence is 1/2 or above, the chance is 0 and PP tells me to be at 0 — contradiction again. The dynamic version is worse than the static one: say I start at 0.6. Chance is 0, PP says go to 0, but the instant I'm at 0 the chance is 1, PP says go back to 1, and I'm chasing my own tail in a two-cycle forever. So one of these has to give — PP isn't a requirement of rationality, or this world has no chances, or PP needs a restriction that isn't the admissibility move. And the admissibility move looks unavailable here, because what's poisoning the well isn't inadmissible information about the outcome; at 9am I know nothing about the toss. It's information about the chance hypothesis itself, which is precisely what PP tells me to defer to. If your answer is going to be that introspective knowledge of my own credence counts as inadmissible, say so plainly, and then tell me how PP ever gets a grip on anything, since applying it seems to require knowing which chance hypothesis you're conditioning on.
