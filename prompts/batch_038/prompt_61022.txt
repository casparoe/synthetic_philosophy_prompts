I need help with a speech and I'm running out of runway on it. I'm a city council member in a mid-sized US city. I sponsored the resolution — passed 7-2 last month — having the city formally apologize for what it did to the Bottoms neighborhood in 1968: used eminent domain to clear roughly 300 mostly Black-owned homes for an interchange, paid well under market, and the displaced families were then funneled onto the east-side low ground that the city later routed its drainage through, so they flooded twice in the seventies. Of the council that voted for it, one member is still alive. He's 94 and not coming.

The ceremony is six weeks out, in a high school gym. About 400 people and a local TV crew. Maybe a third will be descendants of the displaced families, a lot of them in their seventies and eighties — the people who were actually put out of their houses are nearly all dead. The two council members who voted against the resolution will be sitting on the dais behind me. There'll also be city employees, some of whom have worked here thirty years and resent this intensely.

Three problems, and I've become convinced they're one problem:

1. City attorney says nothing can read as an admission of legal liability, and wants my line to be "the city regrets the harm that occurred." To me that's a weather report, not an apology. I need it to actually be an apology — first person, naming the thing as a wrong we did — without handing the plaintiff's bar a gift.

2. A descendents' association has said publicly that they'll walk out unless the apology comes with a commitment on reparations, and council explicitly refused to commit to one. So I'm apologizing with nothing attached. I won't promise what I can't deliver, and I won't pretend the gap isn't sitting right there in front of everyone.

3. Half that gym is going to hear "we" as "you, personally, in this building, did this." A lot of them were children or not born. But if I water the "we" down to "the people who ran things back then," then no one is apologizing and my resolution was theater.

Please write the speech — six to seven minutes spoken. It has to be sayable out loud by a politician, not an essay: no philosopher names, no jargon, and it cannot use the word closure. And I want you to deal straight-on with the fact that the people who were wronged are dead and cannot accept anything, while their grandchildren are in the front row and some of them don't want to be there. Don't route around that.
