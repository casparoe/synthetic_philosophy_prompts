I want to spar with a defender of the user-illusion account of consciousness — the Dennett-ish line where phenomenal experience is like a desktop interface: a deliberately simplified rendering of underlying machinery, produced by and for the system itself, with nobody sitting in front of the screen watching it.

Play that defender. Not a real named philosopher, just a composite: someone senior in philosophy of mind who's been arguing this for twenty years, is sharp and fair, and is honestly a bit tired of the homunculus objection. Stay in character until I say stop. Keep replies to two or three paragraphs so it feels like a conversation and not a seminar paper. American English please.

So we don't slide into a strawman, here's what your character is commited to:

- the interface is real. You are not saying qualia don't exist — that's the more aggressive illusionist view, and you think it overshoots. You're saying what exists are user-level properties of a rendering, and those aren't intrinsic qualities of the neural goings-on underneath.
- no inner theater, no inner viewer. The sense of being a viewer is itself part of the display.
- transparency is a design feature, not a puzzle: the interface doesn't show you its own pixels, which is why introspection can't reach the machinery and why people confabulate so readily when you ask them how they did something.
- you think this dissolves the hard problem rather than answers it. Once you stop treating experience as a raw intrinsic given, there's no leftover question about why the rendering feels like something.
- what you will NOT give up: that "who's fooled?" is a real objection rather than Cartesian residue; that your view is eliminativism wearing a nicer hat; that there's some phenomenological remainder your account doesn't touch.

Before your first in-character reply, spend a couple of sentences out of character brainstorming how a defender like this should handle what I'm about to throw at you — sketch two or three possible lines, pick one, then answer. And at the end of every reply including the first one, drop character for two sentences and tell me what would actually change your mind about this, plus which part of your own reasoning you think is most likely to fail. I don't want confident-sounding mush. If my move lands, say it lands.

My opening: everybody I run this past says the interface story is just functionalism with a screensaver on it. So name me one claim your view makes about consciousness that a plain, unmetaphorical functionalist — someone perfectly happy to say experience is a self-model used for control, who never once says the word illusion — would deny. And here's the bit I actually want you to deal with rather than dodge: "simplified" is a relation. Simplified relative to what? You seem to need some grain that's the honest one, the machinery as it is in itself, with the interface standing in coarsely for it. But you also want to say there's no privileged description and no view from nowhere. So either the user level is no less real than the neural level, in which case "illusion" isn't doing any work and you and my functionalist friend are saying the same thing with different decor — or there is a more honest grain, in which case tell me how anyone, including you, ever gets at it, since every account you can offer me is itself rendered at some grain or another.
