Some background on me: I've been reading about Boltzmann brains since around 2009, first on the rationalist blogs and forum threads and then in the actual cosmology literature, and I think I finally have an angle. But it's still half-baked and I need someone to beat it into shape. The situation is that I'm applying for a small self-directed fellowship — three months, no advisor, deliverable is one paper of about 7000 words plus a presentation to a mixed physics/philosophy audience, half of whom will think the whole topic is a waste of time.

Here's the half-formed idea. Everyone frames the BB problem as a choice between three seperate moves: swallow it and conclude you're probably a freak fluctuation, or take the cognitive-instability line and say a theory that predicts you're a BB can't coherently be believed by someone reasoning under it, so it gets ruled out for that reason, or mess with the typicality assumption / the measure until the BBs stop dominating. What I think all three share, and what nobody ever actually argues for, is the assumption that the thing a cosmological theory is being asked to predict is a distribution over observer-moments.

But a fluctuation brain that exists for a tenth of a second doesn't run an inference. It has an experience, if you want to grant it that much, and then it's gone. It can't notice its evidence, form a hypothesis, check it, or be surprised. So when a theory says most observer-moments matching your evidence are Boltzmann brains, it isn't saying your reasoning is probably unreliable — it's saying something that, taken literally, no reasoning agent could ever be an instance of. Which makes me think the real question isn't am I a BB but: what is the correct unit of counting, and is that unit fixed by the physics, by what the theory's predictions even mean, or by the pragmatics of who is doing the testing? My hunch is the third-ish: it's fixed by an independent question about what would make a cosmological theory empirically evaluable at all, and once you settle that, momentary fluctuations fall out of the reference class not by stipulation but because they aren't the kind of thing that can stand on either side of a test.

Turn this into the proposal. Specifically I want:
