So there's a thing that bugs me about cliodynamics — the Turchin school, secular cycles, all that — and it's less about whether they're right than about what they have to already believe before they can even start.

The empirical practice goes roughly like this. You can't measure "political instability" in 1500 or 1780 directly, so you build a composite index out of proxies — real wages of unskilled laborers, the number of advanced degrees relative to the jobs that absorb them, top wealth shares, public debt — and you separately code qualitative historical claims into numbers (how many riots, strikes, political murders this year) so the whole thing becomes a time series you can fit a dynamical model to. Then you point at the fit, note that the index was climbing well before the 2020s, and call it a successful forecast.

What theory of measurement and of explanation does that workflow presuppose?

- When you average four proxies into one number and treat it as a thing that rises and falls over centuries, what are you committed to about that number — that there's a latent variable behind the proxies which is the *same* variable in 1780 England, 1850 China and 2020 America, or only that the proxies happen to covary? Is there a middle position, and what would license it?
- The proxies go in as causes and the instability counts come out as the effect, but the proxy series are assembled, interpolated and rebased with hindsight about which years turned out to be unstable. Is there a coherent account of confirmation on which that's fine, or does the practice assume something about evidence it can't actually deliver?
- What does it presuppose about history itself — that there are recurring process types across polities that share no institutions, language, or record-keeping habits, as opposed to each sequence of events being a one-off that can only be narrated? Which rival view of explanation is being quietly ruled out when someone says "we can now test this"?
- They insist they aren't determinists: the spark is contingent, only the fuel load is predictable. What does that split require of the underlying metaphysics, and is it really available given how the models are fitted and scored?

Then be honest about which of these commitments the practice strictly needs and which are just metaphysical gloss that happens to sit well with it — my hunch is that several quite different theories of explanation license the exact same index-building, in which case the interesting question is what the practice is *silent* on rather than what it assumes. Finish with what would actually have to change in the workflow — not the rhetoric, the coding decisions, the functional form of the model, what counts as a pass — if they adopted the rival view where these indices are interpretive constructs rather than measurements.
