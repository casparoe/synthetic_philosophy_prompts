Me and one other company are the only two realistic players for a thing — instant cross-border payroll for small contractors. Our CEO wants to ship in 8 months instead of the 18 we planned, and his argument has come up in like three separate meetings now. It goes: if the other guys get there first, then whatever they ship, we can ship a few months later — and we'd still have all the options they used plus the ones they didn't think of. So the first mover can never wind up worse off than the second mover, which means first is at least as good as second, and probably strictly better. His line was "an extra option can't hurt you, so moving first dominates."

Something in my gut says theres a hole in this, but everyone who agrees with him is smart and I don't want to be the guy who just vibes against a math argument. Some stuff I've noticed that may or may not matter: shipping early means we're the ones the regulator looks at first and the ones who set the precedent; the 8-month version will be rough and our early customers have long memories; we'd burn most of the compliance team now and not have them later when we need them; and honestly the other company might not do what we'd have done at all.

So: is his reasoning actually valid, or is it a fallacy wearing a theorem's clothes? Please commit — I don't want "it depends on your risk appetite" or a both-sides list I have to adjudicate myself. Either the dominance claim holds and my unease is a bias I should get over, or it doesn't, and you should tell me the exact step where it breaks and why it breaks.
