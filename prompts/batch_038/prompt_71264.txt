I run root cause reviews at a mid-size hospital group — whenever something goes wrong clinically we convene a review, and our official line is "blameless," justified on the grounds that blaming people makes them hide what actually happened. A collegue says Galen Strawson's Basic Argument hands us a much stronger justification: nobody could ever deserve blame or credit in the first place, determinism or no determinism, because to deserve it you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, and that would mean creating yourself. Assume she's right about that. What are the most important implications for how we should actually run these reviews — including for praise when an outcome is good, for the retraining and remediation we substitute for blame, for whether "blameless" is even the right word for what we're doing, and for what we're permitted to say to a patient or family who got hurt? Give me exactly five implications, numbered, one or two sentences each, and make at least one of them a place where the argument doesn't get us as far as we'd like or actively cuts against our practice. No introduction and no conclusion.
