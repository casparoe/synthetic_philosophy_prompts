my dept is running a "settle it properly" workshop and we've picked pain asymbolia as the case. the two camps we've got are (a) a hedonist about pain's badness — pain is bad insofar as, and because, it feels bad, and what makes it bad is the felt unpleasantness itself — and (b) someone who thinks an experience is a pain at least partly in virtue of its action-guiding, imperative-ish content (protect this body part, stop this), so that the badness of pain rides on that motivational component rather than on a free-floating quale. asymbolia — people who report little or no distress at stimuli they'd normally find agonising, classically after certain brain lesions — is obviously the shared battleground, and both sides have been claiming it for years.

write the joint statement the collaboration should end up producing. what i want:

1. a numbered list of claims both camps can honestly sign. make them substantive, not placeholders — about what the syndrome actually involves, about how much weight patient self-report can bear, about which bits are empirical and which are conceptual, about how heterogeneous the reported cases are.
2. a disagreements table: the exact wording of each live disagreement, with each side's position phrased the way that side would accept for itself, plus a note on whether settling it would actually move anyone.
3. the cruxes — the sub-questions where an answer would force at least one camp to give something up.
4. for each crux, a concrete test or thought case both sides agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down before the fact. at least one should be a real experiment you could imagine running (forced choice, avoidance vs reward tradeoff, analgesic self-administration, whatever fits), and for each say something about feasibility and ethics, since these patients are rare and vulnerable.
5. an explicit call on whether any part of the fight is merely verbal — my hunch is that "is the asymbolic still in pain" is partly a fight over the word, but don't just take my word for it — and say which part.
6. what each side agrees would count against their own view.

keep the whole thing neutral in tone: neither camp gets to write the other's position as a strawman, and don't sneak in a verdict about who's right.

at the end, give me your confidence on the main claims you make, and single out the one point you're least sure of. also tell me which of the empirical details you're confident the literature actually supports versus which ones you're extrapolating or reconstructing — i'm not asking for citations, i just want to know where you're on solid ground and where you're filling gaps.
