Retired soft-matter physicist here — thirty-eight years of phase transitions and driven systems, and now that I've stopped going into the lab I've fallen into philosophy of mind, which my wife reckons is worse than the mid-life motorbike (I didn't actualy buy one). The thing that started it was the Aunt Hillary business in *Gödel, Escher, Bach*: an ant colony that supposedly has a mind, with its thoughts carried by the overall traffic and distribution of ants rather than by any individual ant, plus the reductionist pushback in the same dialogue that only the ants are real and the colony-level talk is a convenience. I've also come across Block's thought experiment about a billion citizens implementing a brain's functional organisation, and something like Kim's complaint that a multiply realisable property can't do any genuine causal work.

What I want is that position restated in the vocabulary I actually think in — coarse-graining, order parameters and their slow dynamics, timescale separation, hydrodynamic modes, universality classes, effective descriptions, boundary conditions — followed by a residue report. I'm completely fluent in that vocabulary and a beginner in the other one, so don't explain my own subject back to me, and don't be polite about what fails to translate.

One honesty condition. In my field I get the degrees of freedom, the couplings and the system boundary for free, because I declare them before I start; a colony comes with none of that. If your translation quietly supplies them, flag it rather than presenting it as a finding, and don't let the physics notion do unearned work and then report the philosophy as inexpressible.

Run these five through, one numbered item each, in this order:

1. The claim that the colony literally has beliefs and thoughts, realised in the collective pattern.
2. Multiple realisation, tested against a case of my own. Variant A: swap each ant, one at a time, for a microrobot reproducing that ant's local response function exactly, but all of them driven off one common external clock so that local noise vanishes. Variant B: leave the ants alone but strip out every local interaction and have each one respond only to a single colony-wide broadcast field. My suspicion is that if the coarse-grained dynamics agree, A, B and the real colony sit in one universality class, which would make "same class, same mind" license rubbish. Which variant keeps the mind, and is multiple realisation just universality-class membership?
3. The reductionist/nihilist reply, and Kim's worry about causal work.
4. The tempo point — colony thoughts running orders of magnitude slower than ours — and whether slowness has any counterpart beyond timescale separation.
5. Individuation: is there one system here or two, given budding, supercolonies and workers drifting between neighbouring nests?

Format: exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each (I'll forgive you three on item 2), no introduction and no conclusion. End each item with a verdict tag — clean, distorted, or no counterpart — plus a clause saying whether the residue is a hole in my physics vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was vaguer than it looked. British English throughout, please.
