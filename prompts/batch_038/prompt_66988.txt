Our philosophy society had a debate last night about whether the universe needs a beginning, and the guy arguing "yes" built basically everything on Hilbert's Hotel. But the line he actually kept returning to — the one he insisted was a contradiction rather than just a curiosity — was Euclid's common notion that "the whole is greater than the part." His move: in the infinite hotel the even-numbered rooms are a proper part of all the rooms, yet there are exactly as many of them, so the principle is violated, so an actually infinite collection of concrete things can't exist. Not just can't be pictured — can't exist. He had a second bit I found more interesting: if infinitely many guests check out you can be left with a full hotel or an empty one depending on which ones leave, so on his account "how many guests there are" simply isn't a property the hotel has at all. My roommate came away convinced. I came away sure he's equivocating somewhere but unable to say where.

Can you give me the whole menu of precise claims that one sentence could be making? Read as a claim about parthood or containment, read as a claim about cardinality / "how many", read as a claim about measure or size in some other sense, plus any other readings you think are live — and for each, say whether it's a platitude, a substantive thesis, a theorem, or flatly false, and whether infinite collections really violate it or only look like they do. I vaguely remember dedekind turning the failure of this very principle into the definition of an infinite set, so work that in too: does it mean the speaker is right that the principle gets denied, or is he sliding from "here's an axiom we refuse to adopt" to "here's a contradiction"? Also flag whether the principle is supposed to behave differently for a potential infinite than an actual one, since I think thats lurking in his argument somewhere.

Then separately: which of those readings would he need to get from the part/whole business all the way to "the past can't be an infinite series of events"? As far as I can tell those are miles apart, and I want to know if there's any reading that closes the gap. Please assess the subtraction point on its own terms as well — that one might actually be doing real work, and I'd rather not dismiss it by accident.
