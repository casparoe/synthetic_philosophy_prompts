Okay so I'm in a moral responsibility seminar this semester and I've talked myself into a slightly weird position about standing to blame, and I want you to stress-test it rather than explain the literature back to me (we've done several weeks of that and I have a reading list already, please don't add to it).

Here's the position. When someone blames another for X while being guilty of X themselves and having done nothing to adress their own guilt, they lose standing to blame. Fine, not controversial. My twist is about the mechanism. I want to say it isn't unfairness to the person being blamed, and it isn't anything about the blamer's relationship to them or their authority or role. I want to say it's sincerity: standing is to blame what knowledge is to assertion. Blame presents itself as coming from someone who actually endorses the norm it invokes; the hypocrite's unaddressed violation is on-the-record evidence that they don't, so the blame fails by its own lights the way an assertion by someone who doesn't know what they're asserting fails. No victim needed — the defect is in the performance, not in how the target is treated. On top of that I want standing to be binary rather than graded, and I want it to apply to total strangers, to judges handing down sentences, and to purely written blame where nobody involved feels any resentment at all.

So: what would count against this? I want the stuff that should actually move me, not the standard tu quoque points. In particular please cover — but don't limit yourself to — cases that would force me off binary standing and onto degrees; the bootstrapping worry, i.e. if confessing and reforming restores standing, does that show sincerity was never the real issue and something about the relationship or the target was doing the work all along; whether "nothing done to adress it" is quietly smuggling in a relational or fairness element through the back door; and the overgeneration worry, since on a strict sincerity requirement almost nobody with any unconfessed fault could ever blame anyone for anything, which might make the concept useless rather than illuminating. Also tell me which of these a committed defender of my view would themselves concede as counting against them, and which ones they'd wave off as misdescribing what blame is for.
