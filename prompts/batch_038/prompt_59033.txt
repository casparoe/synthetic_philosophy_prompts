ok so i run a small ethics-and-econ reading group and our last session of term is on repugnant markets, and I want something satirical to hand out. Here's the conceit: an op-ed of about 750 words for the business pages, written by a market designer who has just launched a pilot program — call it the Surge Allocation Exchange or whatever you like — which prices emergency goods after a disaster rather than letting people queue for them. Make the whole guy up; don't put any of this in a real economist's mouth.

He's sincere, not a cartoon villain in his own eyes, and mostly he's exasperated that nobody will engage with his design. The comedy should come from him answering each objection with better mechanism design, and every fix making things worse: means-tested discounts so the poor aren't excluded, sealed anonymous bidding so nobody has to see who they were outbid by, a three-year installment plan so a family can finance a generator, a "repugnance offset" where the exchange donates a slice of clearing fees to a food bank, and then the same machinery quietly getting pointed at dialysis slots and eventually at the last seat on the evacuation helicopter. Somewhere near the end he should pitch a vote market as the obvious next product line and deal with the argument that if you can't trust people to sell their votes sensibly you shouldn't trust them to cast them either.

The thing that matters most to me: he has to engage the real arguments, not strawmen them. The point that a queue is also a price, just paid in hours and elbows and who you know. The crowding-out worry from the old gift-relationship literature on paying blood donors, including that the field evidence on paying donors genuinely cuts both ways, which he should cite as vindication rather than as a complication. The difference between saying a market coerces the poor and saying it corrupts or degrades the good being traded — he should think the second one is confused sentimentality, and say why, in terms that are technically defensible. And the idea that repugnance is itself a kind of market failure, one that no clearing price fixes, which he should treat as a bug in the population rather than in the design.

Include one invented quote from a bioethicist who objects, and his rebuttal to it should be simultaneously correct and monstrous. Deadpan throughout: no winking, no breaking character, no closing paragraph that spells out the moral. A reader who knows the literature should laugh and also feel slightly sick, and a reader who doesn't should still be able to follow and recoil on their own.

Also please no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — just continuous prose, the way an actual newspaper column reads. Sorry for all the constraints, i just want it to feel like a real op-ed someone could have written.
