Two things I believe pull against each other and I want the second one stress-tested. (1) I think demandingness objections to consequentialism are mostly a dodge — "that's too much to ask" isn't a reason it's false. (2) I also think someone who genuinely maxed out moral goodness would be living a worse life than the rest of us: the tastes, obsessions, jokes and particular loves that make a person worth knowing all get squeezed out by the moral project.

What would have to be true for (2) to be wrong? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Specifically: does the view survive the move of re-describing any non-moral good as itself morally valuable — if every hobby can be moralized, can (2) ever lose, and is that a problem or just what a view about ideals looks like? Is it an ethical claim at all or a snobbish aesthetic distaste for kind people? Does it lean on a scarcity of hours that a well organized saint simply wouldn't face? And what does it predict that a plain eudaimonist or a plain utilitarian wouldn't — anything about artists, or about merely decent people, that I should find uncomfortable?

Finish with which of these a defender of (2) would themselves concede as a real cost versus swat away, and how exposed the view actually is. Dont soften it for me.
