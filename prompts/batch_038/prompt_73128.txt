I am a software engineer working on the control software for a fleet of autonomous haulage vehicles at a large open-pit mine. I have no formal training in philosophy, so please define technical terms as you use them rather than assuming I know them — but write for an intelligent adult, not for a student.

Here is the situation, and then what I want from you.

Our safety architecture contains a constraint we treat as inviolable. The planner may never initiate a maneuver that it predicts will injure a person, even when initiating one would prevent a larger number of injuries caused by something else. The constraint is stated in terms of what our vehicle does, not in terms of what happens overall. Concretely: if a runaway loaded truck is about to roll into a crew of nine, and the only available intervention is for our vehicle to deliberately strike a parked light truck that our perception stack says is occupied by one person, we do not intervene. We have simulated the alternative configuration extensively; stipulate, for the purposes of your essay, that the prediction of who gets hurt in either branch is reliable and that the numbers are as given.

Over four years of fleet operation we can estimate the aggregate cost of the constraint — a running total of predicted injuries and fatalities that our vehicles declined to cause in order to prevent more of them. That figure appears in a quarterly safety report. Nobody on the team disputes its accuracy, and nobody has proposed changing the constraint.

I have read enough to know this is treated as a paradox for deontology: a rule apparently justified by the importance of people not being harmed requires that more people be harmed, and the defense that suggests itself — that the point is to keep one's own hands clean — sounds less like morality than like a preference about one's own moral record. But I want the essay built on two features of our case that I do not see handled in the versions of the problem I have encountered.

First, the constrained party has no perspective of its own. The usual defenses I have run across appeal to something about the agent: integrity, the special standing of what one does as opposed to what one merely allows, a demand that each person be treated in a way that could be justified to them. Our vehicle is not an agent in any sense those arguments need. It is executing a decision made years in advance, in a design review, by people who will never be present at the moment of choice and who chose the constraint precisely because they knew its aggregate cost. When a machine obeys a restriction on my behalf, whose hands are being kept clean, and does the answer make the restriction more defensible or less?

Second, the aggregate is not hidden. In an individual case, the cost of honoring a restriction is one tragedy among an uncountable number of things that happen to people; in our fleet it is a measured, recurring total that we publish. Does turning that total into a number change whether the restriction can be justified, or does it merely make an argument that was already available more rhetorically vivid?

Please write an essay of roughly fifteen hundred to two thousand words that takes up the dispute and settles it. Lay out the strongest case that restrictions of this kind are indefensible — that they are either covert consequentialism or dressed-up self-interest — and the strongest case that the charge misfires, that the restriction is aimed at something other than minimizing harm and is therefore untouched by the arithmetic. Then reach a verdict: is our constraint defensible as designed, or should we replace it, and if we should replace it, say what it should be replaced with, in terms an engineering team could actually implement. I want you to commit to a position and defend it with reasons, not to conclude by observing that the matter is difficult and that reasonable people differ. If your verdict rests on an empirical assumption about the case that I have not stipulated — the reliability of the prediction, the frequency of such situations, who bears liability — state the assumption explicitly and say whether the verdict survives if the assumption is weakened. I am not asking for references, a bibliography, or a reading list; I cannot check them and they would not help me.
