Sorting out my gut verdicts on Bell-type cases and I want to know whether there's a principle underneath them, or whether some of them have to go.

- Standard spacelike-seperated polarizer setup, correlations violate the inequality: not a causal influence, in my book. Nothing I do over here changes what you see over there.
- Same joint statistics, but produced by a shared cable that lets Alice's dial physically recalibrate Bob's detector at sublight speed: obviously a real causal connection, even though it's slower than light and totally controllable.
- PR-box style correlations, no-signalling but saturating the algebraic maximum of the inequality: I want to call these *more* nonlocal than the quantum ones, and I can't articulate why, since neither lets you send a message.
- Bohmian mechanics: genuinely nonlocal, no hedging.
- Everett: locality saved. If you pushed me to point at the local mechanism I'd be stuck, but the verdict feels right.
- Superdeterminism, where λ and the dial settings share a common cause in the deep past: I reject it as cheating — not so much because it's false as because it seems to make the experiment meaningless.
- Retrocausality, where the later settings help fix λ at the source: I'm fine with this, which embarrases me, because formally it looks like the previous bullet with the arrow turned around.
- A toy theory where Alice's outcome is correlated with Bob's setting but not the reverse: a real influence, asymmetric and ugly, and I dislike it more than the symmetric quantum case.

Is there one criterion — screening-off, controllability, something about what's in the dynamics versus what's bookkeeping, a distinction between influence and dependence — that delivers all eight? I doubt it, so tell me which verdicts it forces me to drop and which clause of the criterion is doing the work in each of the ones it keeps. Then apply it to the one I keep flipping on: a theory where λ is set by the measurement settings but only through variables nobody could ever manipulate. Is that retrocausality, is it superdeterminism, or is it a third thing my list doesn't have a slot for? Also give me the criterion's verdict on a case I haven't listed, ideally one where you think my intuitions would break rather than one where they'd be flattered. And say where your own reasoning here is most likely to go wrong — which consideration would make you abandon the criterion you end up defending for a different one.
