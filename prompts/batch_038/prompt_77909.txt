Analyze the Oscar Pistorius eligibility dispute through the therapy/enhancement distinction. The facts, briefly: in January 2008 the IAAF ruled that his carbon-fibre running blades were a banned technical device because they gave him an advantage over able-bodied competitors, resting that finding on a biomechanical study it had commissioned; in May, the Court of Arbitration for Sport overturned the ban, holding that the study had measured him at steady speed only and therefore did not establish a net advantage across a race. I know that outline, and I know the standard philosophical accounts of the distinction. What I want is the mapping.

Under Daniels's normal-functioning account and under Boorse's statistical account, are the blades therapy, enhancement, or neither — and does the verdict change with the baseline chosen: Pistorius's own counterfactual function, the able-bodied distribution, the Paralympic field, or whatever performance level an open final requires? Say explicitly which baseline each ruling was tacitly using.

Then two questions the literature mostly skips. First, CAS's decision turned on who bore the burden of proof about advantage, not on whether the device restored function or augmented it; does the therapy/enhancement distinction have any analogue of a burden-of-proof rule, and would supplying one repair it? Second, the IAAF's operative concept was advantage rather than health, and the blades were constitutive of the athlete rather than an addition to an intact body — does the distinction have anything to say about competition eligibility at all, or did both sides reach for it because nothing better was on hand? Finish by judging whether the episode confirms the distinction, exposes it as question-begging, or shows that it was never addressed to cases of this shape.
