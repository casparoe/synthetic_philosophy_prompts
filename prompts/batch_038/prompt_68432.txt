Not a philosopher — I do data work — but a friend got me hooked on the Sleeping Beauty coin puzzle a few years back and I've been reading around the edges of it since. There's a pattern I keep running into in these arguments and I can't find a word for it anywhere.

The pattern: two people agree on every fact in the setup. The coin's fair, they both know the whole schedule of what happens to whom, neither one has seen or heard anything the other hasn't. And they still get different numbers. When you dig in, it turns out they aren't disagreeing about probability at all — they're disagreeing about *what the things being counted are*. One of them is counting situations, the other is counting chances-to-be-in-a-situation. And crucially neither thinks they're doing anything unusual: each says "obviously it's a half" or "obviously it's a third" and genuinely cannot see what the other one is up to.

It happens in Sleeping Beauty — the half camp is counting two possible worlds, the third camp is counting three possible wakings. It happens in the two-children thing too: someone says a family has two kids and at least one is a boy, and half the internet says a third and half says a half, and the actual fight is about whether you're counting families or counting the ways that remark could have come out of someone's mouth.

So — does this have a name? I know people say the Sleeping Beauty fight is about "self-locating" belief, and that's obviously part of it, but the two-children version has nothing self-locating in it, nobody's asking when or where or who they are, and the disagreement has exactly the same shape. Which makes the centered-content story feel like it covers some cases it shouldn't and misses others it should. I've also seen Bertrand and the chord of the circle brought up, but there the problem statement really does fail to fix a way of picking at random, and here I don't buy that — my gut says one of the two answers is just correct and the setup is rich enough to determine which.

What I'm after: the nearest established concepts, how what I'm describing differs from each of them, and whether this is one phenomenon or two that happen to look alike from here. And if there's no term for it, coin one — but give me the edges, not just the label. Like what would a case be that looks exactly like this but isn't? I want to be able to spot the thing in the wild, in arguments that have nothing to do with coins or sleeping.
