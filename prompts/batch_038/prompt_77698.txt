And Dev's clincher is that the entire point of the concept is to get design without needing a designer, so insisting on a reasoner is just insisting on a skyhook by another name.

My side: 'reason' is doing two jobs and the slide between them is carrying all the weight. There's the reason-why sense — an explanatory, functional story about how a trait or a circuit got there — and the reason-for sense, something that counts in favor of doing something, that can be weighed, that an agent can be wrong about and can act against. Only the second one is normative and only the second one can justify. And the second one needs a subject, because "counts in favor" is implicitly counts in favor *for someone*. So free-floating rationale is a perfectly good label for reason-why and an empty one for reason-for, and Dev is spending the credibility of the first to buy the second.

Here's the part that isn't standard, I think, and I want you to test it rather than nod at it. I work adjacent to ML, so I said the strongest case for my side is mechanistic interpretability. I say: look at what "found it" has to mean — a rationale only becomes a reason once it's some person's reason, which is the precise moment it stops floating. Which would suggest the concept was never about reasons without reasoners but about reasons ahead of their reasoners, latent reasons waiting on uptake. That's a much weaker claim, and I suspect not even a coherent one, and if that's right Dev's whole case deflates.
