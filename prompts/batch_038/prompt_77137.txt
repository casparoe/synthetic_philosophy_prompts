I'm a graduate student in a philosophy-of-language seminar, and I've been put in charge of the discussion session with our department's visiting speaker. She defends minimalism about truth: everything there is to say about truth is captured by the schema stating that a sentence is true just in case things are as the sentence says they are, together with the claim that the truth predicate earns its keep only as a device for endorsing or generalizing over sentences we cannot conveniently quote. Her stock reply to objections is that they smuggle in the substantive notion of truth she has rejected. Draft eight to ten questions for the session, each paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. I want at least one on the Liar and on what fixes which sentences fail to express propositions; one on why truth should govern belief and assertion if it is nothing over and above disquotation; one on whether the schema explains the success of our best scientific theories or merely restates it; one on how she is entitled to the infinitely many instances she has never considered, including in languages she does not speak; and one on whether she can coherently say that her own thesis is true — and, if she answers that she simply asserts it without calling it true, what is left of her disagreement with a correspondence theorist.
