Just spent most of an evening arguing with a friend about the hole argument — he did physics before he did philosophy, which somehow makes it worse — and I've come away more confused then when I started. He thinks he's got a knockdown argument that there are no individuals anywhere, not just no manifold points. I'm pretty sure he can't be right, but I couldnt find the step where it breaks. Can you?

He starts from the ordinary hole set-up and then does an extra move I couldn't block. Here it is in my words (he'd quibble with 6, I know):

1. If a transformation of a theory's models leaves the equations intact and leaves every empirical prediction intact, then the models on either side of it are two descriptions of one and the same physical situation.
2. A smooth map of the manifold onto itself, dragging every field along with it, is exactly that kind of transformation for general relativity: the dragged-along model still solves Einstein's equations and gives the same readings for every pointer, every coincidence of worldlines, all of it.
3. So take a model M and a dragged twin M*, where the drag is the identity everywhere outside some bounded region H but shuffles things nontrivially inside it. M and M* describe the same physical situation. He also insists you don't need the whole-spacetime version: give him the same initial data on a Cauchy surface and he can produce two extensions that agree on and before that surface and differ only inside a hole to its future.
4. Now pick a point p inside H. M assigns p one metric value there; M* assigns p a different one. So we have one and the same situation described as giving p two incompatible properties.
5. Therefore there is no fact about p, over and above the fields, that could make one of those two descriptions correct rather than the other. A manifold point isn't the sort of thing that carries properties in its own right — it is individuated only by the field pattern it happens to sit under.
6. Generalise. Nothing about this turned on the points being spacetime points. It turns on there being a reshuffling that leaves the theory unable to tell the candidates apart, and on nothing further being available to settle which is which. So: if a theory's own resources cannot distinguish two candidates, there is no further fact about which one is which.
7. Two electrons. Permute the labels and the state comes back to itself up to a sign. The theory cannot tell them apart, so by 6 there is no fact about which electron is which. I can live with that, honestly.
8. Same for people. Take the world exactly as fundamental physics describes it: patterns of fields, particles, whatever. Nothing in that description says "and this pattern is me." Now permute two exactly similar people — or take his favourite toy, a universe containing nothing but two exactly similar iron spheres a mile apart. The description is left untouched, so by 6 there is no fact about which is which, and hence no fact about which one I am.
9. So no one is any particular someone. There are no thisnesses anywhere, and "I'm me and not you" is exactly like "this point is p and not q" — a bit of surplus bookkeeping the physics never needed and never used.

Here's my bind. I want to keep 1 and 2, because I don't want to say the dragged model is a genuinely different world; that seems to be the one thing the hole argument actually teaches you, and giving it up looks like accepting his version of the indeterminism. I also want to keep 5 — the bit about the manifold points seems solid and I don't see how to fight it. But 9 I absolutely cannot swallow, and everything between 5 and 9 looks innocuous to me: 6 reads like 5 with the specificity drained out, 7 I'm happy with, and 8 looks like 7 with different nouns.

His line is that the only exit is to deny 1 or 2 and become a substantivalist who eats the indeterminism. I don't believe that's the only exit but I couldn't articulate another one at the time.

So: which step do you reject, what made it look fine on the way in, and what does rejecting it actually cost me — including whether it costs me anything from the list I said I wanted to keep? If more than one step is available to reject, tell me which rejection is cheapest and why. British English, please.
