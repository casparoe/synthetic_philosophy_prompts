I've been doing RL engineering for a couple of years and last week I was digging through our own training code looking for a bug, and I ended up falling into something that I can't stop thinking about. Three things we do routinely, all pretty standard across the field as far as I can tell. (1) Everyone tells you not to use negative rewards in an episodic task, because the agent figures out it can stop the bleeding by ending the episode early, so you add a constant to the whole reward function to push it non-negative — and the reason that's considered harmless is that in the usual discounted setup adding a constant just shifts every state value by the same amount, so the ordering of policies doesn't change at all. The zero point is treated as a totally free choice, a convention. (2) The classic Atari work, if I remember it right, clipped the game's reward down to −1, 0, or +1 so one set of hyperparameters would transfer across dozens of games — a score jump of 10,000 and a score jump of 1 look identical to the learner. And we do the same kind of thing constantly now: whitening advantages, dividing returns by their std, squashing rewards through a tanh. (3) There's also that standard result about shaping — if you add a term computed as the discounted difference of a potential function over the next and current state, the set of optimal policies is provably unchanged, which is why people add dense shaping terms to speed up learning without worrying they've changed the task.

Here's what bugs me: in the same repo, in the same design docs, written by some of the same people, we say the agent "prefers" this, "avoids" that, is "averse" to terminating, is "punished" for illegal moves, is "seeking reward", and then in the safety and welfare conversations around RL people argue in dead seriousness about whether reward signals could be valenced, whether such a system could suffer, whether a training run has a welfare profile. Those two registers seem to be making contradictory assumptions about what the number is. If the reward is a hedonic magnitude, then the zero point obviously isn't arbitrary — adding +5 to everything would be the difference between a run whose experience is net bad and one whose is net good, and yet the math says nothing behaviorally detectable changes. And clipping would be erasing intensity, so two runs with identical optimal policies could have wildly different experiential totals. And the shaping theorem would be saying that you can pump a huge amount of extra "feeling" into a stream while leaving the behavior provably untouched.

So: can you reconstruct the implicit theory of the reward signal that our actual engineering practice presupposes? I want to know what kind of quantity the practice treats it as — there's presumably a name for the difference between "only the ordering matters", "differences matter but the zero is free", "ratios matter too", and "there's a non-arbitrary zero that marks the difference between good and bad" and I don't know the vocabulary. Tell me which of those each of the three practices above commits us to, whether it commits us strictly or just fits comfortably with it (i.e. is there an engineering reason for each choice that has nothing to do with any theory, so the practice is underdetermined), and whether the clipping/normalizing practice and the shaping practice are actually consistent with each other or only with a view on which the signal carries no magnitude at all. Then say which rival accounts of reward the practice quietly rules out, and whether the mismatch with the way we talk ("punished", "prefers", "averse") is a real inconsistency or just loose speech borrowing from folk psychology. Finally: if a lab decided to take reward-as-valence seriously as a working assumption rather than a metaphor, what would concretely have to change — the choice of baseline, the discount factor, the clipping, the shaping terms, whether negative-reward-plus-early-termination counts as something we'd want to avoid doing to a system, whether any of it would need review? I'm interested in what's forced by the assumption versus what just seems prudent under it. Sorry for the wall of text, I couldn't make it shorter.
