Basically there's one organization that campaigns to get fish farms and seafood processors to stun fish before slaughter, and another that campaigns to get restaurants and grocery chains to stop selling farmed shrimp. Dev wanted to pick one, Kayla wanted to pick the other one, and it turned into an hour-long thing. Here's roughly how it went:

(1) Kayla said go with the fish-stunning campaign because the science is way more settled — fish have backbones, there's decades of work on fish responding to painkillers and avoiding spots where they got zapped, whereas with shrimp you're mostly guessing. Dev said that's exactly backwards: way more individual shrimp get eaten than fish, you're talking hundreds of shrimp per person per year versus a fraction of one salmon, and most shrimp just get dumped on a deck to suffocate or tossed into an ice slurry, which nobody disputes would be an awful way to go if they can feel anything at all. (2) Kayla said you're multiplying an enormous number of animals by a probability nobody actually knows, and that move can rescue anything — plug in 5% for shrimp and suddenly shrimp dominate the whole calculation no matter what the evidence says. Dev said sure, that's what expected value is *for*, and besides the UK passed a law a couple years ago that legally counts crabs and lobsters as sentient, so 5% is way too low and its more like informed people put it above 50%. (3) Kayla said at least stunning does something real and checkable — you can verify a fish is unconscious before it's cut, which is about the cleanest welfare measure there is. Dev said that's precisely why the industry might do it on its own eventually for product-quality reasons, since less thrashing means better flesh, so a campaign there is paying to push a door that's already open. (4) Kayla said and what's your alternative, lecturing people to stop ordering shrimp, which only reaches people who already care about animals. Dev said her alternative is that a humane-certified shrimp label might make people MORE comfortable eating shrimp, which grows the industry and grows total suffering, so at least her side bets on fewer animals being born in the first place. (5) Kayla said that's a total guess with zero evidence behind it. Dev said neither does Kayla have any evidence that a stunning campaign ever gets adopted at scale, so they're both betting on unmeasurable downstream effects — except hers reduces numbers.
