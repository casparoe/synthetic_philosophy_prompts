I'm moderating a departmental Q&A with a metaphysician who defends counterpart theory over transworld identity, though in a deliberately stripped-down form: she treats the apparatus of worldmates and counterpart relations as a translation device for quantified modal logic that stays neutral on what makes de re modal claims true. Draft six questions that test whether that deflationary version can still do the work the view is normally hired to do, and don't spend any of them on getting her to restate the position.

Include at least one question each on:

- the problem of accidental intrinsics — if the theory is only a formalism, does it dissolve the problem, or does dissolving it require a substantive claim about how an individual's intrinsic properties are world-bound;
- the familiar complaint that the theory answers the wrong question, since a truth about some other individual in another world is not a truth about what Humphrey himself might have done;
- the possibility of several equally close counterparts within a single world, and the resulting indeterminacy in de re modal truth;
- whether counterpart theory can preserve the necessity of identity without reintroducing the cross-world identity talk it forbids.

Choose the remaining two yourself, but they should press on what her neutralism costs her — for instance, whether counterpart theory and transworld identity are still rival views at all once the former disclaims a metaphysics, or whether context-sensitive similarity standards leave anything ruled out.

For each question, add a single-sentence follow-up aimed at the reply she is most likely to give. Open with the question you judge hardest for her to answer, and keep the entire set, follow-ups included, under 500 words.
