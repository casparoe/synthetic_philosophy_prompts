Consider the following fully stipulated system, which I shall call the Logged Duplicate. Nothing about its history has to be inferred from its present structure: every relevant interval is on the record.

A haploid bacterial population is grown in a chemostat for 2,400 generations with a single limiting carbon source. At generation 12 a duplication event produces two adjacent copies of a permease gene, P-a and P-b, initially identical in sequence and expression. From generation 12 to generation 1,600, P-a alone saturates uptake under these conditions; substitutions accumulating in P-b's coding sequence are selectively neutral with respect to transport, and by generation 1,600 P-b's product has no permease activity whatsoever. A clean deletion of P-b constructed in the generation-1,600 background has no measurable effect on growth rate. At generation 1,613 the experimenters switch the medium, once, to include a constant low concentration of a mildly toxic compound, T. Degradation fragments of P-b's truncated product weakly bind and sequester T; deletion of P-b after the switch costs the cells roughly two percent of their growth rate. From generation 1,613 onward P-b is under weak positive selection, with logged mean selection coefficients of about 0.004 per generation, and the population is sequenced every fifty generations, so the lineage, the environments, and the fitness contributions are known without gaps. At generation 2,397 a promoter mutation arises in P-b that raises its expression approximately twenty-fold and increases sequestration correspondingly; at generation 2,400 it is still segregating at low frequency, though rising.

Two accounts of function are in play. On the causal-role account, in the spirit of Cummins, the functions of a trait are those of its effects that contribute to some specified capacity of the containing system. On the etiological or selected-effect account, in the spirit of Wright, Millikan, and Neander, the function of a trait is the effect for which it was selected — the effect that figures in the explanation of the trait's presence in the lineage.

Please argue for the following thesis: in a system whose selection history is documented as completely as the Logged Duplicate's, the etiological account is not a substantive rival to the causal-role account but a diachronic restatement of it. Every function the etiological account ascribes at generation 2,400 is also ascribed by the causal-role account relativized to the appropriate stretch of the system's history, and every function it withholds is withheld for reasons that are themselves causal-role reasons about past contributions to fitness. It follows that the considerations ordinarily advanced in the etiological account's favour — above all, the claim that only it can make sense of malfunction and of the normativity attaching to function talk — do no independent work in a case of this kind.

Please organize your response under the following headings, in this order, and at approximately these lengths:

**The stipulations the argument uses** (about 150 words). Identify which features of the Logged Duplicate your argument actually depends upon and which are incidental, and state explicitly what you are assuming about how each account treats the interval from generation 12 to generation 1,600 as against the interval from generation 1,613 to generation 2,400.

**The argument** (about 700 words). Numbered premises and intermediate conclusions leading to the thesis, arranged so that a reader who rejects the thesis can locate precisely which premise to attack.

**The promoter mutation** (about 300 words). This is the only point in the case at which the two accounts look as though they must part company. Set out both branches: what the etiological account says about the generation-2,397 allele if rising frequency under weak positive selection is already sufficient for a selected effect, and what it says if a longer history is required. Argue that each branch supports the thesis, or else concede the point and assess the damage.

**The strongest objection, and my reply** (about 350 words). State the objection you consider most likely to defeat the argument — not a convenient one — and reply. If part of the objection survives your reply, say what residue remains and how much of the thesis it costs you.

**What would falsify the thesis** (about 150 words). Name one alteration to the stipulations, or one further fact about this case, that would show the etiological account carrying content the causal-role account cannot replicate, and explain why it would have that effect.

Argue from the stipulations and from the two characterizations of the accounts given above; do not appeal to the authority of any philosopher, to the state of the literature, or to references of any kind.
