Douglass started out a Garrisonian — he took the Constitution to be a pro-slavery compact between the free and slave states, so the honest abolitionist had to refuse to vote, refuse office, and work for disunion. 2. He flipped around 1851 after reading Spooner and Gerrit Smith, and from then on argued that the framers kept the words slave and slavery out of the text precisely so the document would be anti-slavery, since what the framers meant is what the Constitution says. 3. His positive case was that the Constitution nowhere grants property in man, so the three-fifths, slave-trade, and fugitive clauses have no legal force at all, and the federal government had plenty of power — over the territories, the District, the courts, and the guarantee against domestic violence — to end slavery by itself. 4. Which is why the Fourth of July speech of 1852 is basically a celebration of the founding: after the break he dropped the hypocrisy charge and stopped drawing any line between the Constitution and the government operating under it. 5.
