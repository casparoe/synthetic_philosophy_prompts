"Moore noticed that some sentences cannot be true. 'It is raining, but I do not believe that it is raining' is a flat contradiction — the two halves cannot both hold, and that is why nobody could ever say it sincerely. The reason is that to assert something is already to claim to believe it, so the speaker contradicts himself in the very act of speaking. This is the deepest fact about self-knowledge. Your beliefs are transparent to you: you answer the question 'do I believe it's raining?' by attending to the weather rather than to yourself, and so you cannot be mistaken about what you believe. Moore's paradox is the proof. Nor does the absurdity depend on tense or on who is speaking — 'it rained yesterday, but I didn't believe it was' sounds unobjectionable only because by the time you say it you have acquired the belief you lacked, so the contradiction has been dissolved. And the paradox settles an old question about self-deception: strictly speaking, holding a belief and its denial at once is not possible."
