Analyze the Great Leap Forward famine of 1959–61 through Amartya Sen's entitlement framework, then tell me where that framework runs out of road in a command economy. Take the outline as settled: procurement quotas were set from wildly inflated field-level reports; grain kept leaving the worst-hit provinces for export and foreign aid while local granaries stayed sealed, and in Xinyang people starved within sight of full storehouses; peasants were pinned to the communes by household registration and by militia checkpoints on the roads out; the one moment of internal criticism, Peng Dehuai's letter at Lushan in the summer of 1959, was answered by his purge and an anti-right-deviation campaign that redoubled procurement; relief arrived only with the policy reversal of 1961–62.

I want three things. First, a ledger that sorts the missing calories into three buckets — never produced, produced and taken, present locally but inaccessible to people entitled to nothing — with a verdict on which bucket does the real causal work, and on whether the second and third are genuinely distinct categories or two descriptions of one political fact. Second, Sen set against the supply-side rival: the argument that abolishing the right to withdraw from the collective in the autumn of 1958 converted it from a repeated game into a one-shot game and thereby collapsed productivity. Say what observations could discriminate between a famine caused by incentives and a famine caused by entitlements, given that both stories appeal to the same state. Third, and most important: Sen holds that famine is prevented by democratic accountability and a free press, and the Great Leap had neither, which makes the episode look like a confirmation rather than a test. Say what in it could have falsified that claim; whether calling it a political crime rather than a natural disaster turns on causation at all, or on agency and intent; and whether the entitlement vocabulary is doing analytical work here or simply redescribing the fact that the state took the grain.
