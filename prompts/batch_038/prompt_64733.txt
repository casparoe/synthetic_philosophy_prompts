For a session of my graduate seminar on personal identity, I have constructed a variant of Parfit's teletransporter, which I shall call the Diluting Transporter. A traveler enters, her physical state is recorded, the original body is destroyed, and an exact duplicate is produced on Mars, waking with all of the traveler's memories, intentions, and traits intact. The single modification is prospective: from the moment of arrival onward, every psychological connection the duplicate newly acquires — each fresh memory, intention, and commitment — decays at ten times the ordinary rate, whereas the connections she inherits from the traveler decay normally. She is fully competent moment to moment, and retains what she learns on Mars for a few weeks, but a year after arrival her psychology is as it was on arrival: she recalls her life on Earth in complete detail and has kept nothing of her year on Mars.

Please argue that this case shows Relation R is not sufficient for what matters in survival — the duplicate stands in Relation R, indeed in robust psychological connectedness, to the traveler and to her own earlier Martian stages, yet something we plainly care about is missing, which we might call the capacity to accumulate — and further argue that Parfit's reductionist cannot make room for this further requirement without reinstating the very idea of a subject whose life hangs together as a single unfolding whole that Relation R was introduced to replace.

Present your argument as exactly five numbered steps, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion.
