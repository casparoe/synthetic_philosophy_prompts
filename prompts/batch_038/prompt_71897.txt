I'm a commercial litigator. My working life is restitution and proprietary claims: unjust enrichment, tracing through clean and mixed funds, constructive trusts, knowing receipt, the bona fide purchaser for value without notice, the change-of-position defense, and limitation. I have no philosophy training and no interest in being lectured at in that idiom. Take the moral case for reparations for historical injustice — concretely, a claim by the descendants of enslaved people against an institution whose endowment was substantially built from a slave-trading fortune, since commingled with other funds and partly spent on buildings, salaries, and reinvestment — and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use. Tell me who the claimant is, who the defendant is, what the res is, what counts as the enrichment, and which of my doctrines would be doing the work at each step.

Then give me the residue report. Which elements of the moral claim survive translation into tracing and unjust enrichment without distortion? Which can only be stated by bending the doctrine — and say precisely what you bent to make them fit? And which have no expression at all in a framework organized around identifiable assets, transfers between particular parties, and defenses designed to protect innocent recipients? Where the case for reparations divides into a backward-looking ground (rectifying what was taken) and a forward-looking one (remedying present disadvantage), say which of the two my vocabulary can carry and what becomes of the other.

Finally, give me your diagnosis of the residue: does it show that restitution law is missing something it could in principle be extended to capture — a claim held by a group rather than by an identifiable claimant, an enrichment that persists after dissipation — or does it suggest that the moral claim was less determinate than its advocates assume? Mark the places where those two readings come apart rather than choosing one for me. And please separate what is settled, in doctrine and in the philosophical literature alike, from what is genuinely contested among people who work on these questions; don't hand me a live dispute dressed as consensus.
