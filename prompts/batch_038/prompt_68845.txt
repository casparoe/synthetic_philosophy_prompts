My department is trying to end a long standoff between two colleagues. One holds that a slur's derogatory force is part of what a slur sentence says, so that such sentences are false in every application and their falsity is what carries the wrong. The other holds that the derogation is non-displaceable expressive content: it projects through negation, conditionals, and indirect report, and it survives explicit attempts to cancel it. Please produce the document their adversarial collaboration should have yielded.

Begin with a numbered list of claims both can sign — concerning what a slur does and does not share with its neutral counterpart, what competent speakers know without being told, and why a slur behaves differently from an ordinary false insult. None of these may be so thin that a third colleague, who denies that slurs carry derogatory content at all and treats them as merely prohibited words, could sign them equally.

Then give the surviving disagreements as a table with these columns: the point at issue; the truth-conditionalist's statement of it, in wording that side accepts as its own; the expressivist's statement of it, likewise; a concrete case both grant is probative — denying a slur sentence, reporting one in indirect speech, in-group reclamation, a speaker who disavows the ideology while still using the word; and each side's prediction about that case, recorded before it is examined.

Close by identifying any part of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal, and say what feature of the two formulations shows it.
