Ok so I need some honest feedback on an essay I finally wrote. I have no philosophy training at all — I've just been reading on my own for years — so please be blunt.

Background: way back I read a Dennett book from the library where he sorts minds into four levels: Darwinian creatures, Skinnerian creatures, Popperian creatures, Gregorian creatures. As I remember it: Darwinian ones are pure genetic design with no learning, the genes pick the behavior and the ones that got it wrong are gone. Skinnerian ones can learn from consequences, trial and error, so the individual picks up new behavior within its own lifetime. Popperian ones have some kind of internal model of the world so they can run an action through in their head before doing it — the point being the guess gets killed instead of the animal. Gregorian ones add outside stuff: language, writing, numbers, calendars, tools for thinking, so the machinery of the mind is partly out in the world and gets passed between people. And they stack — a Gregorian creature is all four at once. Tell me straight off if I've already mangled that, because the rest of the essay leans on it.

My essay is three moves:

1. I claim the Skinnerian/Popperian split is the same distinction reinforcement-learning people make between model-free and model-based learning. Model-free caches values for actions straight from reward, no world model — that's the Skinnerian rung. Model-based learns transitions and plans over them internally, so it can evaluate before acting — that's the Popperian rung. My argument is that this matters because it turns the ladder from a story about the history of life into a hierarchy of control architectures, which would explain why it feels so intuitive and also why it doesn't line up cleanly with species.

2. From that I argue the rungs are per-system, not per-animal. An animal can be model-based about predators and completely model-free about taste aversion. Same with people: I can plan a budget and then be incapable of not checking my phone. So maybe the right question is never which rung an animal is on but which of its subsystems are on which rung.

3. The one I'm attached to most: Gregorian status isn't stable, it can be lost. External tools of thought have to be learned and maintained, and an environment can be built that strips them away without touching the organism at all. Feeds and variable-reward interfaces are Skinner-shaped — they route around the internal model and hit the reward system directly — so somebody can be fully Gregorian at work and functionally Skinnerian for three hours a night. I call it de-Gregorianization. And I claim it's asymmetric: you can't upgrade a creature just by handing it tools it can't use, but you can downgrade a creature that already has them by changing what thier environment rewards. Which would make the ladder as much about environments as about organisms.

Smaller point I'm unsure about: I think the fourth level is named after Richard Gregory, who in a book called Mind in Science split abilities into aptotes, the ones you get from evolution, and intools, the ones you acquire. If that's the right reading, then Gregorian-ness is defined by a kind of learning rather than by artifacts, which pushes the level way back before writing — maybe to birdsong dialects, or chimps passing on nut-cracking. Or am I stretching it?
