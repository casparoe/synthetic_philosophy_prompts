Been covering science and ideas for a general interest outlet for about twelve years, and if I'm honest the thing I know cold isn't philosophy, it's newsroom practice: the ethics code, what counts as original reporting versus aggregation, when a story has to run as a new piece versus getting quietly updated in place, how we credit bylines on things a ghost actually wrote, and the fact that we'll fire somebody for plagiarism even when every stolen sentence turned out to be true. So I want to run the art-forgery debate through that machine and see what falls out.

Here's the ask. Take the position that a genuinely perfect forgery — indistinguishable from the original to an expert eye, materials plausible, no one deceived — is aesthetically worse and not just commercially worse. And take the restoration argument, the one that says a fresco where conservators have replaced most of the original paint isn't the damaged work repaired but a different work that resembles it. Translate both, as faithfully as you can, into my vocabulary: plagiarism, fabrication, churnalism, the sourcing chain you'd need to defend a piece to a lawyer, corrections and updates policy, credit and bylines, what an editor signs off on. Don't smuggle aesthetic concepts in to make the mapping look tidier than it is; where you have to bend my terms, say you're bending them.

Then give me the residue report, because that's the part I actually want. My hunch, which may be flattening something, is that plagiarism-when-it's-true and forgery-when-it's-indistinguishable are structurally the same puzzle: identical content, different history, and we punish the history. Is that right, or does one of them have a feature the other lacks? Which claims only survive with distortion? And where does newsroom talk just run out — I'm especially curious whether it can express "a workshop copy signed by the master is a lesser work even though nobody was misled about anything," because we do that constantly with staff-written pieces under a columnist's name and nobody thinks there's a problem. Finally, tell me which way the residue cuts: is my framework missing a category the art people have genuinely identified, or is their debate importing market and provenance intuitions and dressing them up as aesthetics? I need to know before I pitch this.
