So I started reading about the replication crisis, and everyone seems to explain it as a misbehavior problem: p-hacking, HARKing, publication bias, tiny samples, people running 20 analyses and reporting the one that worked. Fine, that's real. But I think there's a quieter problem underneath that I can't stop poking at. "Replication" only means anything if the first study licenses a prediction about what a second study would find. Whereas a Bayesian posterior predictive distribution actually looks like the object you'd want here. So my question, rough version: is a big chunk of the replication crisis really a crisis about the predictive content of inference — i.e. did frequentist methods never support the thing that replication quietly assumes they do?
