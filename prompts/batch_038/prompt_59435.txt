I work on ML (mostly RL) and lately I've been reading the proposals that say you shouldn't try to write down what humans value directly — you should instead specify a process, something like "take whatever people would endorse after a long stretch of reflection, with better models of themselves and of each other," and let the value fall out of the process. I keep running into the same wall from three directions and I can't tell whether it's one wall or three. Does this have a name?

1. The ideal conditions get described as something like full information plus clear thinking plus no confusion. But someone pointed out to me that knowing everything relevant about, say, the person you love could change how you feel about them in ways that have nothing to do with getting closer to the right answer — the extra knowledge can eat the very thing it was supposed to reveal. So you can't just crank the idealization up and assume more of it means better.

2. Different idealizations give different answers. More information vs. more patience vs. more impartiality vs. vividly imagining you might be anyone — they don't line up, and there's no obvious sense in which one is "more ideal" than another. When I ask which one is the right idealization, the answer I get is some version of "the ones that produce bad answers weren't really idealizations," which you can only check once you already know the answer.

3. Other proposals say: deliberate until your views stop moving, and take wherever they settle. But where they settle seems to depend on where they started, so the supposedly ideal answer is indexed to the actual messy starting point. When I suggest averaging over starting points instead, I get told you'd have to weight them somehow — and with what?

The common shape, as far as I can tell, is that the procedure only gets pinned down by reference to the output it was supposed to generate. So any candidate procedure can be dismissed by waving at a rival one, and there's no neutral ground from which to rank the rivals.

I know a few things that sound adjacent — circular definitions, infinite regresses, the old worry that you need a standard to judge standards and then another standard to judge that standard — but none of them seem to be quite it. Circularity isn't right because each individual step looks perfectly fine on its own. And I don't think it's just "the answer depends how you frame the question," since the whole selling point of these proposals was that they were supposed to dissolve framing disputes rather than inherit them.

Please don't stop at a label, the carving is what I actually want. Specifically: (a) state what the phenomenon is precisely enough that I could recognize a new instance of it in the wild; (b) go through the nearest established concepts and say how each one differs from what I described, including which fits best and exactly how far the fit stretches before it breaks; (c) tell me whether this is really one phenomenon or three that I've conflated, and if three, whether they share a structure worth naming anyway; (d) if nothing established covers it, coin a term and give me a definition plus boundary cases — things that look like instances but aren't, and things that don't look like instances but are.

Treat yourself as the expert here: someone who has read both the metaethics work on idealized or fully-informed accounts of what's good and the more recent alignment-flavored work on processes-that-output-values. Think carefully, and start with some brainstorming — I'd rather see five half-right candidate concepts with the reason each one falls short than one confident answer. No bibliography, no citations, I can't look anything up right now; explain any technical term in your own words as you go.
