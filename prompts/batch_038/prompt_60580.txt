I'm a nurse practitioner on a hospice palliative care team, 14 years in, and I've been reading value theory badly on the side, mostly at 2am. There's a practice I'm inside of every week that I can't figure out how to describe philosophically, and I want someone to tell me what it actually commits us to.

Two things we do, in the same building, under the same health system.

**One.** We assess suffering on a number. Patient says 0–10, we chart it, we titrate to it. And there's an informal but very real line — call it an 8 — where the goal of care changes kind, not just degree. Below it we're managing: find the dose that touches the symptom while keeping the person awake and able to talk to their family. Above it, and once we've agreed the symptom is refractory, meaning nothing left to try that they could tolerate, the goal becomes abolishing the experience. We sedate to unconsciousness and keep them there until they die. Last month it was a man with delirium who was terrified and pulling at his line and we'd genuinely run out of other options. Nobody on my team said anything like "the remaining good of his conscious life is outweighed by the suffering." We don't weigh it. We say the suffering at that point can't be weighed against anything, it just gets switched off along with everything else, and we treat that as obviously the better outcome.

**Two.** The same health system, for entirely different decisions — which drugs to fund, which programs to expand — uses quality-adjusted life years. Under that arithmetic a year lived in severe pain is worth less than a healthy year but its still worth something positive, and you compare it on one single scale against costs and against other people's years. Our health economists will cheerfully produce numbers *below zero* for the worst health states, i.e. states worse than being dead, and then as far as I can tell they usually clip them at some floor so the maths doesn't blow up. So there's a threshold in the system too, it's just sitting somewhere else and nobody seems to notice it's there.

So: what theory of value does practice one presuppose? Is it committed to something like suffering above a threshold having no finite exchange rate against anything good — the lexical-priority kind of view I've read fragments of and don't really understand — or can you get practice one out of an ordinary cardinal scale plus some assumption about how much confidence we're allowed in our own reports? I keep circling back to an epistemic story instead: we can see pain, we can't really see whether someone's remaining conscious experience is net worth having, so the line is about who gets to judge rather than about how bad it is. That would explain why the definition of refractory varies so wildly between teams and countries. But I don't know if it can carry the weight, because we don't act like it's a judgment call under uncertainty — we act like there's a fact of the matter on the other side of that line.

And then there's the tension with practice two. Are those actually inconsistent, or is one a rule of thumb serving the other, or are they answering different questions entirely?

Please don't give me a lecture on the doctrine of double effect. I know that's the official justification, I think it's a bad fit for what we actually do and say, but tell me if you disagree and say why.

Format — please use exactly these sections, in this order:

- **What the practice commits us to** — ~200 words, the strongest single reading, stated as a claim about value and not about intentions
- **Weaker readings** — ~250 words total: other views that would equally license the practice, and how you'd tell them apart from each other using things that are observable on the ward
- **The epistemic story** — ~200 words: how far it gets and exactly where it runs out
- **The QALY tension** — ~200 words: inconsistency, rule of thumb, or different questions
- **Where it's underdetermined** — ~150 words: what the practice is simply silent on
- **Settled vs contested** — ~150 words

On that last one especially: I want to know which parts of this are actually settled among people who work on it and which are live disputes, and please don't dress up a contested position as the consensus. If the honest answer is that nobody has argued about this exact configuration much, say that instead of manufacturing a literature.
