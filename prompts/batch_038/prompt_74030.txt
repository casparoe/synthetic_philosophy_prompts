I run a first-year grad seminar on Everettian quantum mechanics and I'm writing the take-home for the probability unit. I want the question to turn on something one of my students pitched at me after class: he insists branch counting *does* deliver the Born rule, provided you stop pretending a measurement gives you a tidy finite set of branches. His argument is that decoherence actually spawns a continuum of them, so you have to regulate — count only the branches distinguishable at some fixed resolution — and then take a limit, and he says the squared amplitudes fall out at the end. I want candidates to diagnose exactly where that either collapses back into the naive counting problem or smuggles the Born rule into the choice of regulator, and I want them to say whether the incoherence problem is the same difficulty or a different one (too many of them treat it as one issue and hand-wave both at once).

Deliverables: the question itself, sized for about 45 minutes and worth 15 marks; a model answer around 500 words; and a marking scheme keyed to the specific moves that earn each chunk of credit, plus the mistakes that should cost marks — especially the ones that sound sophisticated but aren't.
