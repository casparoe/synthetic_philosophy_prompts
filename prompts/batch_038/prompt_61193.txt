Ok so — I'm assembling some material for my grad seminar in philosophy of mind, and I want a piece that can only be written by someone who actually understands the multiple realizability debate rather than someone who's skimmed a summary of it. You're a working philosopher of mind who also writes comedy; treat the philosophy as load-bearing and let the jokes fall out of getting it exactly right. Please think carefully before writing.

Here's the commission. Write a satirical bureaucratic case file — about 900 words, American English — from an invented regulatory agency, something like a "Bureau of Realization Claims," whose job is to adjudicate applications from systems (or their lawyers) seeking certified entitlement to a psychological predicate. Make the predicate deliberately undramatic: not "consciousness," but something like *mild annoyance at being interrupted*, so the absurdity is in the paperwork rather than in the stakes. Format it as a real form would be: a header, a notice to applicants, several adjudicated applications with dispositions, and a sidebar about appeals. Deadpan throughout, no winking, no explanatory asides, no footnotes that decode the jokes.

The target of the satire is a *move*, not a person: the habit of dropping "well, it's multiply realizable" into a discussion as if it settled the matter, and the equally reflexive habit of assuming that any functional characterization of a mental state must therefore be both wildly over-inclusive and wildly under-inclusive. Everything invented — the bureau, the applicants, the staff. Real philosophers may be alluded to as sources of arguments, but don't put any of them on stage as characters.

A few beats I'd like the file to contain, because these are the in-the-weeds bits:

- The application form should force the applicant to tick one of three distinct boxes — same property, different materials but the *same* mechanism; same property via genuinely *different* mechanisms; or full medium-independence, where even the inputs and outputs are realized differently — and most applicants should turn out to have ticked the wrong one. The comedy is that nearly everyone claiming a dramatic realization difference has only got a cosmetic one, like a waiter's corkscrew versus a winged one.
- One application should be *denied* on a grain-mismatch ground: the bureau notes that the applicant proved sameness of psychological state with a coarse-grained description and difference of physical state with a fine-grained one, and that this is not evidence of anything.
- One application should be *granted* to something that obviously shouldn't qualify — a logistics network, a highway system, whatever you like — because the bureau's implementation criterion is so permissive that anything with the right causal organization qualifies, and the clerk's memo should note the resulting worry that under a sufficiently loose mapping every open system realizes every abstract automaton, including the office copier.
- One case should involve a system whose pain-role is filled by a state that produces entirely the wrong behavior — intense focus instead of wincing — and the committee should get tangled in the question of *which population's* folk theory defines the role, and therefore whether the applicant is a member of the relevant population at all.
- One applicant should be an astronomically large lookup table with a pre-recorded response to every possible input sequence, and the file should record the bureau's failed attempt to find anyone home inside it, including a note that they decomposed the system into subsystems and then into sub-subsystems and eventually just stopped writing.
- There should be a separate, permanently unfunded annex dealing with what-it's-like-ness, whose forms are all out of stock, and a standing disclaimer that certification of a functional profile is certification of a functional profile only.

Before the piece, give me a short plan — five or eight bullets, one per beat — saying which real argument or objection each bit is meant to dramatize, so I can check you've actually got the targets lined up.
