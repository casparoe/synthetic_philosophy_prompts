Our team is about to standardize the language in which we state safety claims about deployed model systems, and I want the specification written down before we start arguing about methods. The question underneath the standards document is this: when we assert that a system is guaranteed to satisfy some alignment-relevant property P — refusing a class of requests, not pursuing a class of instrumental subgoals, reporting its reasons faithfully, not taking actions that would irreversibly remove human oversight — what exactly is being claimed, and what would make an account of such claims adequate?

The case that prompted this: a system that violates P on roughly one in a hundred thousand draws from our internal red-team distribution, but for which an outside evaluator found a family of inputs — a particular framing device, rendered in a language the system saw little of during training — on which violation is close to certain. Both numbers are true, they differ by orders of magnitude, and the two camps on our team each insist that their number is the one that matters.

So: write the desiderata for an account of alignment guarantees. I do not want a survey of techniques (formal verification, adversarial training, red-teaming, interpretability-based auditing, debate) and I do not want a scorecard of existing proposals, though you should note where a given requirement bears on a technique. For each condition, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Then take a definite position on each of the following.

1. Which conditions are non-negotiable for anything that deserves the word guarantee, and which are the preferences of a camp — in particular, whether universal quantification over adversarially chosen inputs is constitutive of guaranteehood, or whether a high-probability claim relative to a stated deployment distribution can be a genuine guarantee of a different kind. I want a ruling with reasons, not both sides.

2. Which distinctions an account may not collapse. Candidates I have in mind: a guarantee about the trained artifact versus about the deployed system, which is wrapped in scaffolding, given tools, and adapted by its operator; worst-case quantification over all inputs versus over a domain the adversary is assumed to inhabit; guarantees about observable behavior versus about dispositions or reasons; a snapshot guarantee versus one that must survive continued training, downstream fine-tuning, and prompt-level pressure; a guarantee that holds in virtue of how the system was produced versus one that holds in virtue of what it now is; and a guarantee whose scope is fixed in advance versus one whose scope is settled afterward by the people reporting it. Add what I have missed, and tell me which of these are not genuinely distinct.

3. Which results an account must be consistent with. At minimum: that no nontrivial semantic property is decidable for programs in general, while a fixed, finite artifact over a bounded and discretized input domain is exhaustively checkable in principle however intractable in practice; that finite testing never establishes a universal claim; that a certificate valid on a bounded region licenses nothing outside it; and that optimizing against a proxy erodes the relation between proxy and target. For each, say whether it constrains the account of what a guarantee is or only the practice of producing one, since I suspect the two are routinely conflated.

4. Whether some subset of these conditions is jointly unsatisfiable. My own suspicion is that adversarial-input quantification, certification rather than estimation, and applicability to properties we can state only in natural language cannot all be had at once, and that most disputes in this area are unacknowledged choices about which of the three to abandon. Confirm or refute this; if you refute it, identify the subset that genuinely is unsatisfiable.

Finally: which single condition does the most to exclude candidate accounts, and — the part I most want a view on — whether an account offering only distributional assurance within a stated envelope should be permitted to call itself a guarantee at all. Commit to an answer and defend it. I would rather have a position I can argue against than a taxonomy I have to adjudicate myself.
