Fourth-year PhD student here (philosophy of psychiatry), and I've somehow ended up as the early-career seat on a professional body's nomenclature working group. We vote on Tuesday and the chair has asked each of us to circulate a short written opinion beforehand. I need you to draft mine.

The case: a proposal to add a brand-new category, "anticipatory dysregulation disorder," for patients whose symptoms are organised around dread of a specific, datable, foreseeable event — a scheduled surgery, a custody hearing, a redundancy notice that's already landed but hasn't yet taken effect. The selling point is that it's forward-facing, where adjustment disorder is reactive to something that already happened; right now these people get coded as GAD, shoehorned into adjustment disorder, or coded as nothing at all.

Two things make this a mess rather than a routine vote.

First, the pilot numbers. Three years of intake data from eleven sites: prevalence ranged from about 4% to about 19%, and the spread tracked almost perfectly with whether the site's intake form listed the category as an option — not with anything about the patient population. Which is the looping worry in its purest form: the label changes the people it gets applied to, clinicians start looking for it, patients start narrating their distress in its terms, and then the prevalence-and-coherence data you'd use to validate the category are partly artefacts of having published it in the first place.

Second, a leak. The consultation draft went up on a patient forum about eight months ago and the term is now in general circulation online, with self-diagnosis checklists that don't match our criteria. Round-two intake rates jumped at several sites that had changed nothing on their end. One of my colleagues argues this settles the question — the term is loose whether we adopt it or not, so the only real choice is between our criteria and the folk version. I want the memo to say whether he's right, because I genuinely don't know.

The committee is split into two camps and both have something. The recognition-first people say you can't treat or study what you can't name, that "wait for clean data" means waiting forever, and that in the meantime these patients are being handed a CBT protocol for generalised anxiety that the pilot suggests fits them badly and that some of them simply can't access funded care without a code. The validity-first people say adoption would manufacture the very epidemic that justifies it, and that looping is asymmetric — a category that turns out to be bad is far harder to retract than one that was never adopted, especially once it's carrying insurance eligibility and people's self-understanding.

Where I've landed, provisionally: recognise it for treatment-access purposes only, hold the criteria in a sealed registry rather than publishing them to clinicians, stage the rollout so a matched set of non-adopting sites stays comparable, and pre-commit to a sunset review with a decision rule written down now, in advance, while nobody's invested. But I don't want you to just defend that — I want you to pressure-test it, especially the sealed-criteria part, which strikes me as possibly incoherent (how does a clinician apply a category they're not allowed to see the criteria for, and doesn't sealing it wreck informed consent for the people getting labelled?). If it doesn't survive, recommend something else.

So: draft the memo, roughly 900 words. Bottom line up front — an actual motion the committee could vote on, not a mood. Then the reasoning. Then an honest section on the strongest consideration for whichever side you end up against, stated in terms that side would recognise rather than as a strawman. Then implementation mechanics: what the staged rollout looks like, what would count as evidence at the sunset review, and who gets to say the trigger has been met. No bibliography, no citations, no name-dropping of philosophers — half the committee are clinicians and it would just read as padding. Also please don't hedge into "it depends on your values"; they know it depends, that's why they're voting.
