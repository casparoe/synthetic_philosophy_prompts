I'm drafting a paper for a workshop on institutional responsibility, and the question I actually have to answer is when a loose, unorganized group — members who share no communication channel and may not know of one another individually — can be treated as a single agent that acted. Before I go any further into the literature on joint action, I want the specification: what must any account of acting together deliver to be usable for that purpose? Give me the requirements — the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to classify correctly, the distinctions it may not collapse (acting together versus acting in parallel toward the same end; participating versus merely enabling; the group's intention versus each member's intention about the group), and the developmental and comparative findings it must not contradict — with the motivating case or consideration behind each. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are really the doctrinal commitments of particular camps: I especially want to know whether mutual belief and meshing subplans are debts every adequate theory owes or the signature theses of shared-intention accounts, and whether a joint commitment that gives co-participants standing to rebuke is a genuine requirement or a claim internal to one view. The case driving this: a dispatch app notifies eleven independent couriers that an oxygen cylinder has to be carried four flights up to a clinic whose lift has failed; each accepts separately, with no way to message the others; they arrive over the course of twenty minutes, each taking a grip wherever there is room and falling into step with whoever is nearest; three leave early, and the cylinder is dropped when the rest can no longer hold it. Did the eleven act together, and is an early departure a breach owed to the others or simply a change of mind? Say whether your list forces a verdict here and which requirement does the work. Finally, flag any pair of requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and name the single requirement that eliminates the most candidates among shared-intention accounts, joint-commitment accounts, accounts that treat we-intentions as primitive individual attitudes, and the deflationary view that there is no distinct phenomenon to explain.
