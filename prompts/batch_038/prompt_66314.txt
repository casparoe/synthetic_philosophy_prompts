My bank's app has this feature where every card purchase comes with an estimated CO2 number — fill up the car with 40 litres and it tells me that transaction was about 90kg, buy a beef burger and its about 5kg. End of year it gives me a total and a little chart comparing me to "people like me". The same bank publishes an annual financed-emissions figure in its sustainability report, where it takes each company it lends to, works out its stake as a fraction of that company's enterprise value including debt, and claims that fraction of the company's whole emissions as its own. So for a coal miner where it holds a sliver of the debt it might book 2% of the mine's output, but for the petrol station where I bought my 40 litres it books 100% of the emissions from that fuel as mine.

What theory of causal contribution to a collective harm does each of these two practices presuppose, and is it the same theory? I keep trying to work out whether the per-purchase number is claiming my purchase counterfactually made those emissions happen — surely it didn't, if I hadn't bought the fuel the tanker would just have been slightly less empty and someone else would have driven off with it — or whether its doing something else entirely, like handing me a share of a total regardless of whether my act made any difference to it. And if it's a shares view, why does the shares view assign me the whole of the pump's output but only 2% of the mine's, when my causal leverage over the mine looks bigger, not smaller: my bank's loan is plausibly part of what keeps the mine operating, and my 40 litres keeps nothing operating at all.

Does either practice quietly rule out the standard objection that individual acts make no difference to harms like this? A footprint display seems to just assume that debate away, but I can't tell whether it's actually committed to disagreeing with the objection or whether it'd be perfectly compatible with the objection being true.

So: tell me which parts each practice strictly presupposes versus which parts it merely happens to sit comfortably with. Is there some view of responsibility under which both numbers come out exactly right, and some other view under which both should be zero? And if you swapped the implicit theory for a strictly counterfactual one, what would the two displays actually look like — still usable, or does one of them collapse into a wall of zeros with a few enormous numbers scattered through it?
