so my friend and I got into it last night, three hours of arguement about Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind, and I want to write the thing up properly but I cant get past one bit and I'd like a second opinion.

Background so you know where I'm coming from: Dennett's Consciousness Explained, specifically the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction he builds out of the colour phi demonstrations. Colour phi: flash a red light here and a green light a few degrees away, well under a second apart, and people report one spot travelling across and changing colour somewhere along the path. Weird, because at the moment of the colour change the second light hasn't happened yet. Two escapes. Stalinesque: the brain waits, assembles the whole moving-and-changing-colour show, and only then delivers it, so the subject never consciously has anything false. Orwellian: the subject really does see red all the way across, and then the memory record gets edited afterwards so the report comes out as a mid-path colour change. Dennett's kicker is that these two are the same story about the same brain events, differing only over whether some stage counts as pre- or post-experiential, hence indistinguishable in principle — and he thinks the dilemma only looks real if you assume a finish line in the head, a cartesian theatre where things become conscious at an instant.

My friend says the film is a theatre movie. Her version: Lacuna is a studio, Joel is the audiance, the procedure is a Stalinesque edit made before delivery, and the long stretch where Joel is awake and fighting while his memories go proves there's an inner viewer. My version: the erased content isn't destroyed, it just stops being reportable — he wakes on the train crying for reasons he can't state, skips work, ends up in Montauk anyway, which is basically the multiple-drafts claim that what makes something conscious is that it gets written down and leaves consequences behind, not that it plays on a screen. And the tapes, where patients hear their own pre-procedure testimony with zero memory of recording it, look to me like the film running the Orwellian/Stalinesque experiment on itself: the old written record survives the revision, which is the one thing the colour phi subject never gets.

So write me the reading. Take the movie and the philosophy seriously in both directions — don't turn the film into an illustration of Dennett and don't bend the distinction to make it fit.
