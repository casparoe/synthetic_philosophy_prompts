My mom's a school psychologist and she runs the team at my school that decides which kids get sent for an official diagnosis and which don't. I've heard enough of her cases at dinner that I want to see if you can predict her. Track record: (1) sophomore, panic attacks basically every week, but straight As and a big friend group — she wouldn't refer him, said the label would just hand him an identity he didn't need. (2) kid with almost identical symptoms whose grades had collapsed and who'd stopped showing up — referred, diagnosed, accommodations. (3) boy who went flat and stopped talking after his parents split — she stalled six months and did counseling instead, no label, and he came out of it. (4) girl who'd already diagnosed herself with ADHD off TikTok and wanted the paperwork — mom did the referral, but first sat the whole family down about how a category tends to grow into the kid. (5) boy with the same attention problems, and it was clearly hurting his work, parents asking for the exact same thing — but they could pay out of pocket for a therapist and a tutor, so she talked them out of it. She keeps bringing up some philosopher, Hacking? and his thing about looping effects, that people reshape themselves to fit the categories you put them in.

New case, and she has to decide next week: a junior in my chem class. Academically fine, Bs and As, makes the swim team. But she's been cutting, and her parents can't get insurance to cover ongoing therapy without a diagnosis code. She also already describes herself entirely in diagnosis language.

So: what does my mom call on this one? More than the verdict, I want the rule she's actually running, because I think the impairment-threshold rule, the looping-effects rule, the only-label-if-the-label-is-what-unlocks-treatment rule, and the wait-and-see rule all fit her five cases pretty much equally well and they don't agree about the junior. Give me your best guess with reasons, then name one further case that would tell thier rules apart. Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and don't restate the question back at me.
