I am a junior and I chair the student council's governance committee at my high school. For three years our elections have been uncontested: the same cluster of friends from the debate team runs, nobody challenges them, and turnout is around 12 percent. The dean of students has offered to fund whichever selection model we recommend, so we have to choose among (1) continuing annual elections for all fifteen seats, (2) filling all fifteen by lot from a pool of every student who has not opted out, and (3) filling ten seats by lot and keeping five elected at large.

Please draft the memo I will circulate before our Thursday meeting. It must state which option we are recommending, and it must settle two questions that split the committee. First, the elected five are the only members anyone can actually remove at the end of a term, so should real authority — in option (3), the sole power to approve spending — be reserved to them, or does a lottery seat confer standing of a different kind rather than none? Second, drawing lots gives every student the same chance of being selected, which sounds fair, yet we expect that in practice the students who opt out will be disproportionately the ones who would have run anyway, so the draw may reproduce the current pattern without the election that at least pretended to authorize it. Say plainly whether sortition is a positive improvement or merely a stopgap for a failed election, and give the strongest reason someone on our committee could still vote against your recommendation.
