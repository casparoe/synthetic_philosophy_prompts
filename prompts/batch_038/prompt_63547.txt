I'm putting together a piece about the old Gödel, Escher, Bach picture — Hofstadter's careenium and simmballs, the idea that symbols at the top level are what actually steer the wetware underneath — and I need someone to audit the analogy my main source keeps leaning on.

He's a neuroscientist and a big GEB fan, and his move is always the same: a symbol level in a brain is just like software on a computer. The software is only a pattern implemented in silicon, sure, but it's causally real, it drives the hardware, and nobody finds that spooky; so a simmball pushing neurons around is exactly as unspooky, and anyone still worried about "downward causation" has obviously never written code. He also has an add-on he thinks is the clincher: the silicon doesn't care what the software means, only what its syntax is, so meaning drops out of the causal story entirely — it's just a nice way of talking about the symbol-shuffling.

A philosopher I also interviewed says the whole thing begs the question. Software can drive hardware only because a chip designer and a compiler built the layering and police it, so that a given pattern at the top reliably maps onto a given configuration of voltages; in a brain there's no compiler and no designer, and the "symbol level" and the "neuron level" are the same undifferentiated soup, so the analogy assumes the clean stratification it was supposed to explain.

Can you sort this out for me? I want the correspondence table spelled out — what plays silicon, what plays the instruction set, the compiler, the programmer, the voltage levels — and then the disanalogies, separated into the ones that actually bite on his conclusion and the ones that are just decorative. Then tell me straight: does "downward causation is no mystery" survive, does the meaning-drops-out add-on survive independently of it, and if the computer analogy is the wrong one, what should a GEB person have reached for instead — and what does the better analogy license that this one doesn't? Take a side and give me your reasons rather than a menu of options; bottom line in the very first sentence, then the argument, no preamble and no restating my question back to me.
