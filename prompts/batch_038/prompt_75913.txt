I run an internal workshop for our infra team — about 18 engineers, 75 minutes, and nobodies taken a philosophy class including me. I want a game that makes them feel the difference between sizing up a project from its own details versus sizing it up from the track record of similar projects, and why the second one keeps winning even when you're certain this time is different.

Two things I care about most. First, the track record shouldn't come from me as a handout — it should get generated by the room, so that partway through people realize they *are* the reference class they've been waving off. Second, I want one round where they get to pick which class of past projects counts as the relevant one, and they notice that picking the class is the exact same kind of judgment they were just told not to trust — you can gerrymanderd your way to any base rate you want. And I want at least one round where the base rate is genuinely the wrong guide and following it makes you lose, so the takeaway isn't just "trust the spreadsheet."

Give me rules, a scoring scheme that doesn't need real money, materials, a timeline, and a debrief that maps specific rounds onto the ideas. Also: what predictably goes wrong the first time you run something like this, and which piece of it the game can't show so I have to just say it out loud.
