I'm in a small reading group attached to our lab — mostly people who work on machine learning, one or two who've read a bit of philosophy of mind, and a couple of us who are trying to figure out whether any of the consciousness stuff is worth taking seriously. One of the regulars (I'll call him D) has this analogy he reaches for constantly, and he used it again last week to shut down two separate conversations at once. It goes like this: phenomenal consciousness is like the check-engine light on your car's dashboard. The light doesn't turn the wheels, it isn't part of the causal business of the car going anywhere, it's an inner display that happens to light up while the engine does all the work. From that he draws two conclusions he's quite attached to. First: you can't learn anything at all about whether the light is on from how the car drives, so behavioral tests for consciousness in machines are worthless — a system could show zero trace of it and still have the light on, and equally a system could be built to imitate every trace and have nothing there. Second: introspective reports aren't evidence either, because whatever makes me say "there's something it's like" is engine-side machinery, so the saying would have happened regardless. He's very relaxed about all of this — his line is that it's a question with no detectable consequences, so we should stop arguing about it and go back to work.

Someone else in the group (S) says the whole thing is self-undermining: if the light really caused nothing, you couldn't even find out it was on, so D's own claim is unfalsifiable mush, and she brought up the old locomotive whistle version of the same picture — the whistle doesn't help pull the train — as another case of an analogy that eats its own user. My hunch is that she's circling something with an actual name, the paradox of phenominal judgment or whatever it's called, but I don't think she's stated it correctly, and honestly I'm not sure D's version is as broken as she thinks, because in the car case the light and my noticing-the-light might both be downstream of the same sensor, which could explain the match without the light doing anything.
