Just got out of a three hour arguement with my roommate about whether voting should be made compulsory. Every time I pushed on something — the safe-seat problem, or that a vote you're fined into giving isn't really expressing anything — he'd come back with "well, if you don't vote you can't complain," said like its a trump card, and I kept failing to say why that felt slippery.

So: what is that sentence actually claiming? I want the menu of precise principles someone could be expressing with it — is it about forfeiting standing to object, about being complicit in whatever the electorate does, about fairness to the people who turned up, about having done the homework to have an opinion, about something else entirely? For each candidate say whether it's a platitude everyone already accepts, a substantive claim that might be true, or just false, and roughly what would break it.

Then two things. First, which of those readings, if any, would actually support making voting compulsory rather than just scolding non-voters — I'm pretty sure he was sliding between them all night and I want to name the slide. Second, run the best-sounding readings against the people who have no vote to give: the undocumented guy who's lived on our street for nine years, my 17 year old sister. I suspect that's where they come apart and I want to know which ones survive it.
