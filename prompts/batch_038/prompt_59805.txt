I am a third-year doctoral student in philosophy of physics; my dissertation concerns how cosmological models are tested by arguments about who or what is observing them, and for the past year I have been cataloguing my supervisor's verdicts on a family of thought experiments about fluctuated observers. Her verdicts, recorded after each seminar, are these.

1. An eternally inflating de Sitter vacuum with no decay channel, whose horizon temperature fluctuates observers into existence at a constant rate forever: unacceptable.
2. The same universe, except that the vacuum decays into a big crunch after a finite time, so that fluctuation-production is truncated while ordinary observers still outnumber the fluctuated ones: acceptable.
3. A universe in which randomly fluctuated configurations vastly outnumber ordinary observers, but in which none of these configurations is an observer in any useful sense, each being a momentary, internally incoherent jumble that supports no ordered experience, retains nothing, and cannot carry out an inference: acceptable.
4. A universe in which the fluctuated observers are internally coherent, retain stable memories of a past that never occurred, are incapable of detecting that this is so, and outnumber ordinary observers: unacceptable.
5. A universe like ours in which ordinary observers eventually build hardware on which vastly more minds run than were ever born, these minds inheriting the physics of their builders and arriving at the same cosmological conclusions: acceptable.

Next week I must present a sixth case and defend a prediction about how she will rule on it. The case is this. A cosmology that matches ours through the hot early universe, structure formation, and the rise of ordinary observers, but whose dynamics also include, in the emptied far future, a lawful process that repeatedly nucleates fully formed observers having no causal contact whatever with that earlier history. The nucleation law is written so that the records these observers carry are accurate: their memories of the early universe, of structure formation, and of the ordinary observers are true, and their capacity for inference is entirely unimpaired. They outnumber ordinary observers by an enormous factor, and, reasoning impeccably from records they did not earn, they arrive at and accept the cosmology that entails their own existence.

Please do the following. State the candidate principles that jointly account for verdicts 1 through 5, each in a form I could apply to an arbitrary cosmological model rather than to these five alone. Say which of the five verdicts does the most work in narrowing the field and which does the least, and whether anything in the record already tips the balance between the survivors or whether the record is genuinely neutral. Give each surviving principle's ruling on the sixth case, noting where they agree and where they part company. Commit to a single prediction of her verdict on the sixth case, with the reason you would give her for preferring it. Finally, describe one further case I could put to her after the sixth that would decide between the principles still standing once her ruling on the sixth is known, and say which way each of them would go on it.

Answer in exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion.
