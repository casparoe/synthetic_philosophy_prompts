Consider the safe-Pareto-improvement program in game theory: agents who lack probabilistic beliefs about how a game will be played may nevertheless be able to agree, in advance, on a transformed proxy game whose outcome is guaranteed to be at least as good for each of them as the outcome of the default game, and strictly better for at least one. Existing arguments for such a guarantee typically lean either on qualitative assumptions about outcome correspondence — isomorphic games get played isomorphically, dominated strategies get eliminated — or on genuine precommitment devices.

Now stipulate a variant, which I will call the transparency-only case. Two agents are about to play a mixed-motive two-player game. Each is a fully transparent program that can read the other's complete source code, and each reasons about that code in a functional-decision-theory style; neither can make any binding commitment before play, and neither holds any probabilistic belief about the other's behavior, only qualitative reasoning about what the other's code implies. Remove every outcome-correspondence assumption.

Give me your credence — an actual number — that in this setting transparent reasoning alone still guarantees a safe Pareto improvement relative to what the same two agents would have played had they been mutually opaque. Then decompose that number, with one line of justification for each term and the arithmetic that combines them: (a) that a Pareto-improving target outcome exists in the game at all; (b) that the baseline of what they would have played had they been opaque is even well defined for agents holding no probabilistic beliefs about play; (c) that transparency singles out one improvement rather than leaving the choice among several candidate Pareto improvements indeterminate; (d) that each agent's visible willingness to negotiate is not itself an exploitable opening once the other can read it; and (e) that what survives is certainty-level safety rather than merely an improvement in expected utility.

Finally, identify which term your number is most sensitive to, and describe the argument or constructed case that would move it most. Please write your answer in American English.
