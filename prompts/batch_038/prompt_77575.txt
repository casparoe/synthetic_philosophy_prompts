I write an ideas-and-science column, mostly economics and social science, i.e. whatever people with models are currently fighting about. I'm working on a piece about the endless war over whether macro explanations need microfoundations, and I've now talked to something like a dozen people across both camps. Here's the thing: at some point nearly all of them reach for the same line — the whole is greater than the sum of its parts. The holists drop it like a trump card, usually right after I ask them what their preferred explanation actually adds. A couple of the individualists say it too, then insist it's perfectly compatible with everything else they believe. My editor wants one graf that settles whether the slogan is doing any work at all, and I can't write it until I know how many distinct claims are hiding inside a single sentence.

So: lay out the readings as a menu I can keep next to me on the next call. For each one give me the precise claim, a verdict — trivial/platitude, substantive and maybe true, or flat-out false — and, most important, whether it would actually get the holist what they want. Does it defeat methodological individualism, or does it just sound like it does? My hunch is that several readings are true but beside the point, and that the one reading that would genuinely matter is the one nobody wants to say out loud in print. Tell me if that hunch is right and what that reading has to commit to: supervenience failing, some version of downward causation, the best explanation not living at the level of the parts, aggregation being impossible in principle, something else?

Also, for each reading, give me the sort of case where it's clearly satisfied, including from outside the social sciences — I want to be able to say whether anyone has ever produced an actual result that vindicates the strong one, or whether it's always about to be vindicated.

And when someone drops the slogan mid-interview specifically to end the line of questioning, which reading are they most likely smuggling in, and what's one follow-up that forces them to pick? Give me two or three of those, phrased so they don't come off as gotchas. Finally: if some of this is a live disagreement among people who know the literature, rather than two sides just talking past each other, say so plainly. I don't want to file "it's all semantics" if it isnt.
