I chair the research ethics board at a vet-med research institute, and I need the decision memo for Thursday's meeting. Here's the file thats splitting us.

A PI wants to run a chronic pain study in macaques using an implanted neural decoder hooked to a touchscreen symbol board — the animals get trained to select symbols, and the lab claims the decoded output tracks nociceptive and affective states. Fine so far, and possibly interesting. The problem is the protocol's own ethics section: it sorts subjects into a "communicative" tier and a "non-communicative" tier depending on whether the animal reaches a performance criterea on the board, and the non-communicative animals get a much lighter post-operative analgesia assessment schedule — behavioral scoring at longer intervals, no mandatory re-evaluation. The stated justification is explicitly Cartesian. Roughly: a creature that never produces language gives us no positive reason to attribute thought to it, so the default is to treat its behavior as machinery until proven otherwise, and the burden of proof sits on anyone who wants to ascribe felt pain rather than on anyone who wants to discount it.

Half my board reads that as welfare discounting dressed up in a citation. The other half — including our attending veterinarian — thinks the PI has a real methodological point: species-specific grimace scales and behavioral pain scoring are genuinely unreliable and heavily anthropomorphic, and a decoder might in some cases be better evidence than a human reading a monkey's face.

Write the board's decision memo: approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Some requirements:

- Engage the Cartesian argument on its own terms instead of just calling it speciesist. I specifically want the memo to work through whether the language criterion, if you actually grant it, licenses the inference the PI wants from it — including what Descartes himself concedes about animals and the passions in the correspondence (the letters to Newcastle and to More are the relevant ones), and whether a machine-mediated output could ever satisfy that criterion or would just be a fancier reinforced response that a Cartesian could dismiss along with a trained parrot.
- Make the memo address the gap between "no report is available" and "there is nothing to report," and say which side of that gap the protocol is actually on.
- Give the strongest honest case for the losing side, not a strawman. If you approve with conditions, the conditions have to be things an administrator could actually check and enforce.
- 700–900 words, plain prose, no footnotes, no bibliography, and don't put anything in quotation marks attributed to Descartes — paraphrase, since I can't verify wording on short notice.
- American English throughout.
- Very last line, on its own, exactly in this form: Verdict: [APPROVE or REJECT or APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS] — followed by one sentence naming the single most important reason.
