Subjects briefly shown a grid of letters can report more items from a row cued after the display has gone than they could have reported from the whole grid — evidence usually taken to show a rich but rapidly fading store that outlives the stimulus. Block argues that this shows phenomenal consciousness overflowing access consciousness: at a given moment one's seeing has more phenomenal character than one can report, reason about, or deliberately use.

Give me your credence that the inference is correct — that the surplus in such cases is genuinely phenomenal rather than a pre-conscious buffer — together with the decomposition. I would like a rough probability and a line of justification for each of at least the following: (a) the data are best read as showing a store richer than anything accessible; (b) 'phenomenal' here does not covertly reduce to a weaker form of access (fragile, dispositional, or merely delayed), so that the two notions genuinely come apart; (c) a global workspace theorist cannot absorb the result by distinguishing broadcast sufficient for report from broadcast sufficient for use; and (d) phenomenal character wholly cut off from any functional or cognitive role is a coherent notion at all. Combine these into a single number, then tell me which term that number is most sensitive to and what evidence would move that term furthest.
