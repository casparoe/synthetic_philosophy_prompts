Me and my friends play a Nomic-style rules game: everything is a rule, including rules about how to change rules, and amendments pass by simple majority unless some rule says otherwise. I've been logging which moves I'd accept as legal, and I want to know whether there's a real principle behind my verdicts or whether I'm just contradicting myself.

1. Rule 301 says no rule may be repealed. Majority vote repeals 301, then a majority repeals the scoring rule. I accept both moves.
2. Rule 302 says Rule 302 may never be repealed, amended, or suspended, and any attempt to do so is void. Unanimous vote repeals it. I reject that one — 302 still stands. On paper 1 and 2 look like the same move to me, but they feel different, and I can't say why.
3. Rule 303 says any rule adopted after this one is void. Later we adopt a rule repealing 303. I accept the repeal.
4. Rule 205 sets the amendment threshold at two-thirds. Under that threshold we adopt Rule 306, which drops the threshold to simple majority. I accept 306, and I accept majority amendments after it, even though 306 could never have passed under its own rule.
5. Someone adopts a rule that clearly didn't get the votes it needed, and for six sessions everybody plays under it. Then a player challenges it. I'd say the rule was void all along, but every move made under it still counts.
6. A player invokes a rule that was validly repealed two sessions ago and nobody noticed at the time. I'd say the invocation is void but the tally it changed stands anyway.

My guess at the principle: a move is legal iff the rules in force at the moment of the move permit it, where "in force" means roughly "nobody has sucessfully challenged it yet." Test that against 1–6 and repair it or throw it out. What I really want is the tightest principle you can state that saves the most verdicts, what it says about a rule that entrenches its own entrenchment clause (one saying the clause forbidding its repeal may not itself be repealed), which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, and a couple of cases I haven't thought of where it lands somewhere surprising. American English, please.
