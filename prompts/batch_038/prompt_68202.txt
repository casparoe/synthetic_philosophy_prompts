I have been assembling, over a number of years, a list of verdicts about which of a system's symbols are grounded and which are merely verbal, and I have reached the stage where I can no longer tell whether the list hangs together. I should like an account — one principle, or a small ordered set of them — that reproduces the verdicts, together with a candid statement of what must be abandoned if it cannot.

The cases, in roughly the order I came to them.

1. A robot that acquires a predicate for a kind of object purely by handling objects: it reaches, misses, adjusts, and its internal state settles onto the category through its own error correction. Grounded. This is my paradigm, and I will not give it up.

2. A text-trained system with no body and no sensors, which has encountered everything ever written about cups and can nonetheless tell me that a cup with a hairline crack will not hold soup. Not grounded, I think — though I confess I cannot name the capacity it lacks, since the inference it performs is one I would have credited to the robot in (1).

3. A congenitally blind geometer who uses 'scarlet' fluently, reasons correctly about reflectance spectra, and can tell a colleague from measured reflectances which of two swatches will look warmer to the sighted. I want to say her term is grounded, or at least that she understands it. Note the difficulty: her route to the term is the same route as the system's in (2) — other people's words about a property she has never met. Cases (2) and (3) are the pair I cannot reconcile.

4. An astronomer's symbol for sodium in the atmosphere of a particular exoplanet. She has never perceived it and never will; she has an instrument trace and a thorough understanding of what would make that trace misleading. Grounded. A technician who runs the same instrument, obtains the same trace, and cannot say what would count against it: not grounded. Identical causal history, opposite verdicts.

5. A robot whose 'red' has, as a matter of fact, been fired by red things throughout its life through an accident of wiring, and which will behave indistinguishably from a properly wired robot for the remainder of its operating life. Not grounded. Its intrinsic duplicate, wired so that it would track redness in circumstances neither will ever encounter, is grounded. The difference looks modal rather than actual.

6. A child's 'unicorn' seems to me grounded, being assembled from materials she has met. A term for a purely doctrinal entity — a scholastic's 'quiddity', say — learned only from other words that were themselves learned only from other words, is not. Grounding can be transmitted, but the transmission has to terminate somewhere.

7. A clerk who manipulates unfamiliar symbols by following rules he does not understand: his own language remains grounded while the borrowed one does not. Grounding attaches to individual symbols, not to whole systems, and 'is this system grounded?' strikes me as a malformed question, since a system may have a grounded vocabulary and an ungrounded one at the same time.

8. A research group: three scientists and their text-trained model, whose outputs are checked, corrected, and used to direct instruments that act on the world. The group's symbols seem grounded to me. But I do not think grounding descends to the model merely because the model is a part of the group; its own symbols still strike me as ungrounded within that arrangement. I am not certain that (7) and (8) are consistent.

My tentative gloss, which I expect you to break: a symbol is grounded just in case its application is answerable to something other than further symbols — that is, when there is some channel by which the world, or an agent who is herself answerable to the world, can tell the user that the symbol has been misapplied.

Three things, please. First, the principle or principles, stated precisely enough to be applied to a case neither of us has yet met, with the case from my list that motivates each clause. Second, the verdicts they deliver on questions I have not settled: is a text-trained system grounded when its misapplications are corrected in real time by human annotators who are themselves perceptually competent? Is a robot grounded whose sensors were calibrated once by an engineer and never checked again? Is grounding a matter of degree, and if so, along which dimension does it admit of degrees? Third, and most important: tell me which of my verdicts your account can keep and which it overturns, and whether the more elaborate principle that saves all of them is worth its complexity. I expect (4) and (5) to constrain whatever you say about (2) and (3) in ways I have not anticipated. Please do not assume that I want the verdict that flatters my attachment to (3).
