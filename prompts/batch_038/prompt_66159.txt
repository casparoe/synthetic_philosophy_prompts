My department is running an adversarial collaboration between two non-classical camps and im the one who has to draft the joint statement, so help me out.

Camp D is a dialetheist: the liar sentence is both true and false, so logic has to be paraconsistent, because otherwise a true contradiction drags every sentence along with it. Camp G is a paracomplete "gaps" theorist: the liar sentence is neither true nor false, classical logic stays intact for everything that does get a value, and D is just relocating the problem rather than solving it. They've agreed in principle to sign one document; they have not agreed on a word of it.

Here's what I need. First, a numbered section of claims both camps can genuinely sign — including what exactly turns out to be inconsistent (some naive truth principle plus classical logic, presumably — make them be precise about which), which inference rules each of them surrenders, and what each one owes by way of an account of why ordinary mathematics survives their revision. Second, a disagreements table, with each row worded so that the side whose position it is would accept the wording as their own. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions where an answer would actually move at least one of them, not just restate the standoff.

For every crux, give the case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down before the arguing starts. I'd like at least one crux on the strengthened liar and the determinacy/only-true operator, one on the Curry-style self-referential conditional — the one that gets you to an arbitrary conclusion through conditional proof and detachment without ever needing a contradiction to explode — and one on whether losing disjunctive syllogism costs D more than losing excluded middle costs G. Also have them pre-commit to a classification table over a fixed list of sentences: plain liar, strengthened liar, truth-teller, Curry sentence, and a liar-disjunction along the lines of "this sentence is false or 2+2=5," with each camp recording true-only / false-only / both / neither in advance.

Two things on the honesty front. Flag any place where you've slipped something into the agreed section that only one camp would really sign — that's the failure mode I'm most worried about here. And close with a short note separating what's actually settled among logicians who work on this from what's still a live fight, so I don't end up circulating a contested view dressed up as the consensus.
