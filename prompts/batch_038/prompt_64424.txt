Our department's crossover reading group meets Thursday and roughly half the room has never opened a semantics paper. Topic is slurs — what they mean and what people do with them. Before anybody presents anything I want to hand out a short "which view do you actually hold?" quiz, because I've watched people announce themselves as expressivists or whatever and then answer every diagnostic case like a straight-up descriptivist.

So: eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three options, plus a scoring key. Every question has to be answerable by someone who's never heard the debate — if a question only makes sense once you know what a conventional implicature is, cut it. Keep the actual words blanked out with a placeholder (say ___); nobody needs them printed in order to answer.

Cases I want in there, plus whatever else you think earns a slot:

1. Negation. Someone says "it's not the case that Kai is a ___." What, if anything, is conveyed about Kai, and what about the speaker?
2. Indirect report. Ana tells Bo "Kai is a ___." Bo later tells Cara what Ana said, using the word. Who slurred, if anybody?
3. Quotation. A linguistics article prints the word in order to discuss it. Is the author doing anything wrong — the same wrong as the bigot, a smaller version of it, or nothing?
4. The clean-hands speaker. Something with no attitudes at all — a chatbot trained mostly on 1920s novels, or a person who learned the word only from texts in a language nobody speaks anymore — asserts "Kai is a ___," and Kai has every descriptive property the word tracks. Is the assertion true? Was anything wrong done? I want this one to be a real fork, because I think some positions are forced into saying true-and-nothing-wrong, and I want to know which ones and whether they can live with it.
5. Reclamation. Same word, used among friends who are all in the target group. Difference in content, difference in who may deploy it, difference in what it's for — or no difference at all, just a social illusion?
6. A new coinage. A community deliberately invents a slur for itself; outsiders pick it up and start using it to hurt. When does it become a slur — at the coining, at the uptake, or at the first hateful use?
7. For three or four words you pick, is there a neutral counterpart that means the same thing minus the poison? At least one of them should be a case where the honest answer is no.

Now the key. My hunch, and push back if you think it's wrong: the literature is answering two different questions at once and calling the results rival theories. One axis is what the word contributes to the truth conditions of a sentence containing it. The other is what a speaker does in uttering it. So I'd like the scoring key to come out as a grid rather than a list — place each answer pattern into a cell and put the live positions in the cells they belong to. I want at least these represented: the line that puts the derogatory content in what is said; the line that puts it in a non-displaceable expressive side-channel; the line that says the word is simply taboo and every utterance of it does the damage regardless of the speaker's attitudes; the line that makes the content depend on a community's derogatory practice rather than on the individual speaker's head; and whatever hybrids the pattern space actually supports. Name the leaves however the literature names them if there's a standard name, but no bibliography and no reading list, I'll fill that in myself.

Two more things. First, some answer patterns won't correspond to anybody's actual view. Don't smudge them into the nearest leaf — list them seperately and say for each whether the cell is empty because the view is incoherent, because it's coherent but nobody wants it, or because nobody has bothered to occupy it yet. Second, tell me which pairs of positions my cases can't separate at all, meaning where two theories give identical answers to everything above. And if question 4 turns out to be unanswerable without already taking a side on whether truth is even the right thing to ask about here, say so in the key instead of writing a question that pretends otherwise.
