prepping the last session of a translation workshop, the one on Le Ton beau de Marot, and i want to hand out a short poem that makes the book's argument by performing it instead of stating it. so: no naming Hofstadter or Marot anywhere in the verse, no title, and no explanatory note afterwards.

here's the shape i want. a cascade of four stanzas, all rendering one tiny bit of content — someone telling the person they love that the good light outside isn't going to last and they should come stand in it now — each stanza under a tighter straitjacket than the one before. first loose and prosy, second in a strict meter, third in that same meter plus a locked rhyme scheme, fourth in monosyllables only. 16–20 lines total.

the point isn't a difficulty flex, it's that the content has to come out sharper at the end rather than thinner, since the whole claim is that form isn't packaging you peel off to get at the message. stanza four should feel like the same poem rebuilt out of completely different material — equivalents for effects, not for words — and it should hit hardest of the four while sharing not a single word with stanza one. bonus if its rhymes pick stanza one's sounds back up, because that loop is what i want the class to hear out loud.
