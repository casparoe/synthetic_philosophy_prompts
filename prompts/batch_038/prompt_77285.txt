I am circulating a short statement of my own position in advance of a graduate reading group on the philosophical reception of the incompleteness theorems. The organizer has asked each participant to set out their commitments in numbered form so that the session can be spent attacking the structure of the packages rather than on exposition, and I would like a consistency audit of mine before I send it out. I have tried to write each item so that it is something I could defend in front of people who know the actual proofs; where I have been careless, or where an item is ambiguous in a way that turns out to matter, please say so directly rather than working around it.

Here is the package. (1) Mechanism is a live hypothesis for me in this sense: my brain is a finite physical system, and I accept that a complete physical description of it would in principle settle, for any arithmetical sentence, whether I would eventually come to accept it; I see no need for nonphysical ingredients anywhere in the story. (2) I reject Penrose's positive proposals about microtubules and quantum-gravitational state reduction as unsupported speculation, and I would not want any anti-mechanist sympathies of mine to depend on exotic physics. (3) I do accept the soundness-based form of the argument: for any formal system F, if F is sound and I can come to know that F is sound, then I can see that F's Gödel sentence is true, so no such F captures everything a competent mathematician can come to know about arithmetic. (4) I take the usual objection to the crude version of the Lucas argument — that we have no means of knowing our own consistency, and so cannot apply the second incompleteness theorem to ourselves — to be decisive against that version. (5) I hold that the theorems are statements about provability in recursively axiomatized theories containing enough arithmetic; I use "true but unprovable" only as shorthand for true in the standard model of arithmetic yet not derivable in the theory, and I am content to talk that way without any Platonist metaphysics of the standard model. (6) I accept Gentzen's proof as a genuine consistency proof for first-order Peano arithmetic, and I do not think it is in tension with the second incompleteness theorem. (7) Methodologically, I hold that a consistency proof earns its keep only if the means it deploys are finitistically acceptable in Hilbert's sense — reasoning about finite symbolic configurations whose own reliability is not nearly as much in question as that of the theory being certified. (8) I accept a closure principle about my own commitments: whenever I come to accept a system F as sound, I thereby accept its consistency statement and the local reflection principle for F, and I regard my arithmetical commitments as closed under this operation without bound. (9) I do not think the significance of incompleteness rests on any self-referential artifact, since there are natural number-theoretic and combinatorial statements — Goodstein's theorem, the Paris–Harrington theorem — that first-order Peano arithmetic cannot prove.

What I want back is an audit rather than a survey of the literature. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — naming the item or items that cannot survive alongside the rest — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question. Rank the tensions you find by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and for each say explicitly whether it is a genuine incompatibility or only an apparent one that dissolves once the claims are stated precisely; where one dissolves, say what the dissolving move is. For the genuine ones, identify the least costly revision and what that revision would force me to give up elsewhere in the package, and end by telling me which single change would clean up the most. I have no prior attachment to any of the nine items, and I would rather be told that the whole thing is unstable than be reassured.
