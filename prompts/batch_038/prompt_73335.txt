ok so i had a two hour argument with my friend last night about German data protection and I don't think either of us won, help me figure out who's right.

He's American and his whole case is that the Germans don't have privacy, they have paperwork. He points out that if you move apartments in Germany you're legally required to register your new address with the town within two weeks, your landlord has to sign a form certifying that you actually moved in, and if you screw it up you get fined. So there is no living unregistered in Germany — the state knows exactly where every person sleeps, and it makes you actively tell it. Meanwhile in the US nobody is required to tell the government where they live, and yet the credit bureaus, the data brokers, your employer, your phone company and anyone with a subpoena all know where you live, what you buy and who you sleep with. So he says: the German rules don't reduce how much is known about you at all, they just change who has to ask and how politely. It's not a theory of privacy, it's a theory of state manners.

My counter was that the German thing is about linkage, not disclosure. The constitutional court invented that right to informational self-determination in the 1983 census case precisely because it was scared of the state pooling registers into a picture of a person, and German law still bans the obvious moves: no single searchable national population database, one agency can't just query another's files, and data collected for one purpose can't be reused for a different one. Then he said fine, and they're dismantling it anyway, because now everybodys permanent tax number is being turned into the master key that ties the separate registers together. Which honestly I couldn't answer.
