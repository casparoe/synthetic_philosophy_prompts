I have been assembling a position in accuracy-first epistemology — the epistemic utility theory program on which norms for credence are derived from the promotion of accuracy — and before I present it I would like a consistency audit of the package as a whole. I am not looking for a tutorial on the framework; assume I know the standard arguments. What I want is an assessment of whether these commitments can be held together, and if not, what the least costly revision is.

Here is the package:

1. Accuracy monism. The only fundamental source of epistemic value for a credence is its accuracy, understood as closeness to the truth. Any candidate epistemic norm that cannot be derived from the promotion of accuracy — coherence, evidential support, reliability, whatever it may be — is either derivable after all, or is not an epistemic norm but a pragmatic or aesthetic preference wearing epistemic clothes.

2. The two central norms are established, not merely rationalized, by accuracy-theoretic arguments rather than by betting considerations: a credence function that violates the probability axioms is accuracy-dominated, in that some alternative is closer to the truth at every possible world; and plans to conditionalize maximize expected accuracy as assessed from the agent's current credences.

3. The choice among additive, strictly proper scoring rules — quadratic, logarithmic, spherical — is not settled by epistemic reasons. I choose among them on pragmatic grounds, and I hold that this choice makes no difference to the epistemological upshot: whatever the framework delivers, it delivers under any such measure.

4. Where the evidence is genuinely thin or ambiguous, the rationally required doxastic state is imprecise: a non-singleton set of probability functions rather than one. My paradigm is an urn about whose composition I know nothing at all. I do not think imprecision is merely permitted here; I think a single sharp number is an unsupported sharpening, and that adopting one is a failure to fit one's credal state to one's evidence.

5. I also hold a chance-credence constraint in its own right: where my evidence specifies that the objective chance of a proposition is x, my credence ought to be x. My reason for treating this as independent of accuracy is that a chance-matching credence can turn out badly inaccurate at the actual world, so accuracy alone cannot be what recommends it.

6. Epistemic norms must be followable by bounded agents like me. I cannot enumerate the propositions I have opinions about, still less close that collection under consequence, and I cannot carry out conditionalizations on complex bodies of evidence. A theory whose verdicts no finite agent could in principle comply with has not, in my view, issued norms — only ideals.

7. My doxastic states are partial. I have credences only in propositions I have actually entertained, and I deny that I am epistemically obliged to have an opinion about every member of a fixed algebra or about the consequences of what I believe.

8. I am uneasy about the framework's sensitivity to how the problem is represented. I take it there are results to the effect that conditionalizing on the evidence proposition is not in general the expected-accuracy-maximizing plan, and that the maximizing plan instead conditionalizes on the proposition that one has learned it. If the deliverables shift with how finely the algebra is carved, and accuracy itself cannot adjudicate the carving because the algebra is not scored, then something has gone wrong.

Please work through this set and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how badly, ordered by severity rather than listed as equally troubling. For each genuine tension, state precisely where the collision occurs — what each commitment entails and which premise does the work — and identify the cheapest revision, together with what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. Separately, identify the pairs that only appear to be in tension and say why they dissolve; if a commitment of mine survives but for a bad reason, tell me that too. Say where the framework is simply silent rather than pressing it to deliver a verdict it cannot support, flag any place where I have misdescribed an argument, and finish by naming the single change that would clean up the most. No references or suggested reading, please — I want the analysis, not a bibliography.
