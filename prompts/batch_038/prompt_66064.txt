Been going back and forth with a friend over the crane/skyhook distinction and how it gets deployed on consciousness. His move is the vitalism analogy: every time people insisted life needed some extra ingredient, what they were really doing was mistaking their own failure of imagination for a hole in the theory, and the hole always got filled by ordinary mechanism — so the residual intuition that there's an explanatory gap left over after you've accounted for all the functions is just a demand for a skyhook, and the whole hard problem debate is that mistake replaying itself.

Can you audit the analogy rather than take a side? I want the explicit mapping — what plays élan vital, what plays the functional story about metabolism and self-reproduction that made mechanism credible before anyone had found all the chemistry, what plays the eventual discovery that there was nothing left over — and then the disanalogies sorted into the ones that actually bite for the use he's making of it versus the ones that are just noise. I suspect it quietly assumes the two sides already agree on what the explanandum is, and that the target has to be a process rather than a standing property, but i can't tell whether that's a real problem for him or something he can shrug off. Then: does "your intuition is a skyhook demand" survive once those are on the table, is there a better analogy for whatever's going on, and does the crane/skyhook distinction earn its keep here at all or is it mostly doing rhetorical work by pre-labeling the opponent's target as forbidden?
