A working group I sit on is drafting accountability doctrine for a city's autonomous shuttle fleet, and working through cases has made clear that my own verdicts do not line up. Here they are.

(a) A driverless municipal shuttle swerves to avoid a child who darts into the road and kills a cyclist. Nothing malfunctioned, no engineer wrote any rule concerning cyclists, and the passenger had no controls. I feel strongly that someone should answer for this, and that it is not the passenger.

(b) A triage model flags a patient as low risk. The on-call clinician is required to review every flag and has ample time to do so; she waves this one through and the patient dies. The clinician is plainly answerable and the vendor plainly is not — even though the vendor is what produced the erroneous flag.

(c) Identical system, identical review requirement, but the clinician faces two hundred flags a shift and spends eight seconds on each. Now I do not think she is answerable — and I am unwilling to shift it to the vendor, which leaves no one.

(d) A firm's trading algorithm does something no one designed or anticipated, wrecking third parties. I hold the firm answerable even though no individual in it did anything wrong.

(e) Our fleet's models are retrained nightly on pooled driving data and the training corpus is overwritten, so nobody can reconstruct why the shuttle behaved as it did in (a). This makes me want to hold the engineer who signed the safety case more answerable, not less.

(f) A platform's moderation system buries a legitimate political post; a human appeals process reverses it eleven days later. Here I feel no one is answerable — only that a service failed.

(d) and (f) are the pair I cannot reconcile: both involve a corporate agent whose system did something undesigned. Is there a principle that delivers all of these verdicts, or nearly all? State it precisely enough that I can see which clause each case turns on; say what it implies about two cases I have not listed — one where the oversight function is itself automated, and one where the deployer profited from a harm it could not have foreseen; and say plainly which of my verdicts it forces me to give up. Under 450 words.
