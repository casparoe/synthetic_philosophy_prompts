I am at work on a literary novel whose narrator is the regional compliance officer for a European extractives group operating in an unnamed Central Asian republic, and Chapter Eleven opens with a document she has written herself. I want to get that document right, and I should be grateful if you would draft it.

Please write a mock corporate Gifts, Hospitality and Conflicts of Interest declaration form together with its guidance notes, of roughly 750 to 900 words, throughout in British English, laid out as numbered sections with tick-boxes, declared-value thresholds, a compliance helpline in the footer, a version number, and a signature block attesting to the accuracy of the declarant's answers.

The target of the satire is not any real company, country or person, but the confident universalism of the compliance genre itself: the presumption that the line between a gift and a bribe becomes self-evident once a monetary ceiling has been fixed, and that a definition of corruption as "the abuse of entrusted power for private gain" travels unaltered into a setting where power is not entrusted by an impersonal office at all but is constituted by networks of kinship and client obligation, and where the "private" cannot be prised loose from the claims of one's household, one's cousins and one's clients.

The humour must be generated entirely by the form's sincere attempt to make those distinctions operable. I would like the document to contain:

- a de minimis threshold stated in a hard currency and then converted, the local equivalent arriving at a figure that quietly renders the category unusable;
- a section on timing — before the decision, during it, or after it — which demonstrates, without noticing that it does so, that an identical object changes moral species purely by date;
- a test of secrecy (was the gift declared? was the meeting minuted? who else was present?) that collapses into a test of bookkeeping rather than of intent, so that the well-documented offence is clean and the undocumented kindness is a breach;
- a carve-out for facilitation payments, or for customary hospitality, which contradicts in plain sight the zero-tolerance clause two pages earlier;
- guidance notes that describe, in compliance idiom and without naming any theorist, the obligation to give, the obligation to receive and the obligation to reciprocate — including the difficulty that a refused gift is itself an affront, and must therefore be reported as an incident;
- and a merit-based recruitment clause whose conscientious implementation would dissolve the patronage networks on which the company's entire local workforce, and its informal licence to operate, depend.

The register should stay in-house and deadpan from first word to last: no asides, no winking, no footnote conceding the point, and at no stage an explicit statement of the objection. The author must appear to believe every line she has written, and to believe that the form solves the problem. An anthropologist of the state should recognise the argument being made and judge it fair; a general reader should simply find the document funny and faintly sinister.

Invent all wording, worked examples, thresholds, currency figures and the titles of annexes. Do not reproduce the text of any real policy document, and do not include citations, a bibliography, or references to identifiable scholars, firms, states or officials.
