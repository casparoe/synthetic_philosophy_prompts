I have spent a decade in the calibration-obsessed corner of the internet — Brier scores on forecasting platforms, arguments about proper scoring rules, the standing insistence that a credence earns its keep only through long-run accuracy. That sensibility eventually pushed me into the formal literature, and I have now settled into something like accuracy-first epistemology as my house view. Let me state the version I actually hold, because I want the attack aimed at it rather than at a caricature.

My commitments are these. First, accuracy — the closeness of one's credences to the truth — is the sole fundamental epistemic good, and every other epistemic norm is derivative of it. Second, the inaccuracy of a credal state is the additive sum of the inaccuracies of its component credences, each graded by a strictly proper scoring rule, and I accept the arguments that plausible constraints on such rules single out the quadratic family. Third, probabilism and conditionalization follow from dominance and expected-accuracy considerations: incoherent credences are accuracy-dominated by coherent alternatives, and conditionalizing maximizes expected accuracy by the agent's own lights. Fourth, and deliberately, I do not claim the framework settles the value of knowledge over mere true belief, adjudicates disagreement, or tells us which propositions one ought to have opinions about; I treat those as out of scope.

I know the objections exist in outline: that the choice of scoring rule does unearned work; that the constraints forcing the quadratic measure then generate verdicts of their own that look unacceptable, including an updating rule critics have charged with being flatly irrational; that dominance results shift depending on how finely propositions are individuated; that infinite or continuous setups may leave no accuracy-maximizing credence function at all; that imprecise or set-valued credences may resist domination; and the broader charge that grounding epistemic norms in the promotion of a good is epistemic consequentialism, which trades accuracy across propositions in a way that ignores the separateness of propositions. What I want is not a reprise of those slogans but a map of my exposure.

So: what would have to be true for the position as I stated it to be false? Work through the pressure points and tell me, for each, (a) exactly what result, case, or intuition would count as a defeater rather than an irritation, (b) whether that defeater is live — established, or at least seriously contested by people who accept the framework's basic machinery — or merely logically possible, and (c) whether an accuracy-first theorist would concede it as counting against the view or explain it away, and on what grounds. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and say where you think the real fault line runs.

Then take up two questions I cannot resolve myself. Does this framework risk anything at all, or has it been insulated by scoping disclaimers like my fourth commitment into a position compatible with any outcome whatsoever — and if it is that insulated, is that a defect in a normative theory of this kind, or precisely what such a theory is supposed to be? And what does accuracy-first epistemology predict or rule out that its rivals — pragmatic betting-based vindications, knowledge-first accounts, imprecise Bayesianism — leave open? If the honest answer is nothing, say so. You will not be able to check sources, and I do not want a reference list or a bibliography; I know the shape of the literature and want your reasoning and your verdicts instead.
