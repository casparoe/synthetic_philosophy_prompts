Two weeks into a quals paper on how you're supposed to revise your credences when what you learn is a conditional probability instead of a plain fact, and I've realised I don't actually know what would count as a good answer. So before I read one more proposal: write me the spec.

The case I keep circling (a cousin of the Judy Benjamin one, but my numbers). An island is split into three zones — A, B, C — mutually exclusive and exhuastive, and Judy starts out with 1/3 on each. She's dropped into one of them blindfolded. A radio message then says: if you're in A, the chance you're in B is 3/4; and, if you're in B, the chance you're in C is 1/2. What should her probabilities be now, and what would make an answer correct?

Give me the list of requirements any rule for updating on that kind of report has to meet, each with the case or consideration behind it rather than just the requirement stated. And adjudicate these rather than reciting them:

- whether the probability of the antecedent is allowed to move at all when everything you learned was conditional on it — the obvious Jeffrey-style treatments make it move, and I can't tell if that's a bug or the whole point;
- what has to happen in the limit where the reported conditional probability is 1, and why that shouldn't just collapse into ordinary conditioning;
- whether it matters if the two reports arrive together or one after the other, and what a rule that gets different answers in the two orders has to say for itself;
- what happens when a source hands you conditional reports that no probability function can jointly satisfy;
- whether a repeated report from the same source should change anything the second time;
- whether the report is a constraint on Judy's credences or evidence about the world from a possibly unreliable speaker, because those seem to need completely different machinery and I dont know which the question is even asking for;
- whether the thing being updated is a sentence with its own probability or just a number attached to a pair of ordinary propositions — if it's the first, I gather there's a collapse result waiting.

Say what the list is a list *for*: I want a norm of rational revision, not a model of what people actually do, though if some requirement only makes sense as psychology, flag it. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are one camp's preferences, and if some subset can't all be satisfied at once, show exactly where it breaks — that's the part I want most. Then tell me which single requirement does the most to rule proposals out. Skip the scorecard of named views; one line each at most if you can't help yourself.
