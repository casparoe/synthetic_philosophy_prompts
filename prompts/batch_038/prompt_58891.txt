so I've got no philosophy training at all, I manage a builders' merchant, but I've fallen way too deep into this whole question of whether two people are actually disagreeing about something or just using their words differently. the little test I came up with on my own: when me and someone are properly stuck, we agree to bin the contested word completely and invent a brand new one instead, which we both define super precisely on the spot, then we carry on having the conversation using only that. if the argument evaporates, it was only ever about words. if it keeps going under the new word, there was something real underneath it the whole time.

the case thats bugging me is this one. picture two people who agree on literally every plain fact about a worktop — there's a mug, a spoon, and a bowl of sugar sitting on it, and they agree what each one's made of, where each one is, whether the mug's got a chip in it, all of it. then one of them says "so there's three things on there" and the other says "no there's way more than three, the sugar is a few thousand grains and the mug's got a handle and there's air inside it" and neither will give an inch. there's no extra fact either one is appealing to that the other denies. feels like exactly the sort of argument my test is supposed to kill, but I don't think it would.
