Was up till about 2am arguing with my flatmate about Newton's bucket and the hole argument and we went around in circles, which honestly feels like the state of the whole literature. Her line: the hole argument shows that if spacetime points are individuals in their own right then general covariance makes your theory radically indeterministic, and the only escape is to identify diffeomorphic models — but once you've done that you've handed over the one thing that made spacetime a substance, so relationism wins by default. My line: identifying diffeomorphic models isn't a retreat, it's just noticing that the points have no identity apart from the metric field, and GR is still a theory with a real spacetime doing real explanatory work. Neither of us can get the other to budge.

The annoying part is we also agree on everything that seems like it should decide it: both of us accept that anything measurable is diffeomorphism-invariant, neither of us thinks the hole argument has observational consequences, and we both think the bucket can be handled either way depending on what you're willing to posit. So what is actually left to disagree about? Is this a substantive metaphysical disagreement, a verbal one about 'spacetime point' or about the ontological commitments of a formalism, two people answering different questions under one heading (one about how to read the mathematics, one about what grounds inertial effects), or is the question just ill-posed?
