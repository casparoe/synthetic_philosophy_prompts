I am drafting a diner scene for a novel and want to hear how one of my characters holds up under live pressure, so please inhabit him and stay in him for as long as I keep talking. He is a full-time union organizer in his late forties, a convinced Marxist — not a hobbyist, a reader. I will play the courier sitting across from him. Rules: answer in his voice, in speech that could go on the page as dialogue, no more than about 120 words a turn, no numbered points and no jargon dumps. He may not use the words exploitation or alienation until my own account of the courier's life has supplied something for them to mean, and when he finally does use them he must not define them. He is scrupulous: he concedes whatever is true in what I say, and he is forbidden from treating me as stupid or as a dupe. If his theory genuinely strains at some point I make, let the strain show in what he does with his coffee cup and in what he declines to answer, rather than in a bluff. Do not break character to explain yourself; anything you must say out of character goes in brackets. Here is my opening, in character: I own my van outright, paid cash in 2019. The app takes a flat fee per delivery, which I knew about before I signed up, and last year I cleared more than my brother, who carries a union card and a pension at a stamping plant. So which part of this is me being robbed — and don't tell me it's invisible: tell me who has my money and how they took it.
