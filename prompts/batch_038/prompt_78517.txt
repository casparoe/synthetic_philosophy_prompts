I retired three years ago after thirty-five years of field work on selection in natural populations — most of it spent estimating selection differentials and heritabilities for shell characters in an intertidal snail, and defending those estimates against reviewers. Only since retiring have I begun reading the philosophy of science in earnest, and I now attend a monthly reading group consisting mostly of intelligent amateurs and two academic members. Last month the subject was Popper, and on that one evening the sentence "survival of the fittest is a tautology" was produced twice, each time to close a discussion rather than to open one.

The first use was dialectical. A member objected to an adaptationist explanation of a shell polymorphism on the grounds that fitness just means leaving more offspring, so to say the successful variant was fitter is to say the variant that succeeded succeeded; any trait can be retro-fitted with a story of advantage, hence nothing is explained and nothing could have been predicted.

The second use was stronger and came with an attribution. A younger member said that this is precisely Popper's point, that it is a logical rather than a merely rhetorical defect, that Darwinism is therefore unfalsifiable, and that Popper consequently grouped it with Marxism and psychoanalysis as non-science. She also said that Popper changed his mind late in life, without being able to say what he changed it to. I have read enough to suspect that both the attribution and the grouping are wrong, but I cannot yet say why, and I would rather have the reasons than my suspicions flattered.

Let me add my own attempt at a precisification, which the group found unsatisfying. It seemed to me that the schema "heritable variants with higher expected reproductive output increase in frequency" may indeed be true by definition once fitness is defined as expected reproductive output, and yet not empty, because the empirical work is done by a separate step: predicting fitness independently, from morphology, physiology or behaviour, before counting offspring. In my own work I never inferred fitness from reproductive counts and then explained those counts by fitness; I measured shell characters, predicted performance, and treated the discrepancy as a finding. So I want to know whether this is a genuine additional reading of the slogan, whether it is correct, and whether it disposes of either of the two uses above.

Please set out the distinct claims the slogan could be making, as a menu rather than as a single interpretation. I would like each reading stated precisely, and each assigned a verdict: platitude, substantive and defensible, or false. In doing so, keep separate at least the following, which the group ran together: the Spencerian phrase as against the theory of natural selection as against evolutionary theory in general; the several senses of "tautology" in play, including analytic truth by definition, immunity to falsification, explanatory vacuity, and necessity across all possible populations; and the different meanings of "fit" that can be plugged in, since Darwin adopted Spencer's expression in the fifth edition of the Origin meaning something like better suited to the immediate local surroundings, whereas the technical notion is expected reproductive output. Then tell me which reading each of my two colleagues actually needed for the work they were doing with the slogan, and whether that reading is available to them. Finally, give me your own account of where Popper stood, including whether he ever ranked Darwinism with Marxism and psychoanalysis, and what his later revision amounted to. I am not asking for references — I want your reconstruction and your judgement.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question.
