Been stuck on Bradley's regress for weeks and I want an essay that actually picks a side.

Quick setup so we're talking about the same thing: if propositions have constituents, then to get from the pile {Caesar, crossing-the-Rubicon} to the proposition that Caesar crosses the Rubicon, you need some hook-up relation to bind the constituents together. But then that hook-up relation is just one more item on the list, so you need a second relation to bind it to Caesar and to crossing, and a third to bind that one, and so on forever — and the pile never turns into a truth-bearing unity no matter how many relations you stack on top.

Now here's a proposal I want you to take seriously rather than swat away. Call it the self-tying view. The hook-up relation R is weird in one specific way: R ties itself to its own relata. The very same relation that binds Caesar to crossing also binds itself to Caesar and to crossing, all in one go. So there's no second relation to go looking for — the regress terminates at step zero instead of running off to infinity.

Write me an essay on whether thats a real solution or just the problem wearing a disguise. It needs to cover at least:

- Can R's tying itself to the relata fail? If it can't, what sort of necessity is that, and does it sneak in exactly the "must hold" we were trying to explain in the first place? If it can fail, then there's a difference between R-tied-to-Caesar-and-crossing on the one hand and R plus Caesar plus crossing on the other, and we need a seperate binder for that difference.
- How is self-tying any different from the Fregean line that relations are unsaturated or incomplete, so no extra glue is required? Same move with better PR, or does it actually buy something the unsaturatedness picture doesn't?
- Does it generalise? Suppose I care not only about propositions but about facts, and about plain predication — how anything at all gets to be true of anything. Does self-tying cover that too, or is it a patch that only happens to fit propositions?
- Can we even say what self-tying is without restarting the regress one level up? Every attempt to describe R's self-tie seems to need a relation between R and the tie.

Then reach a verdict and defend it: is Bradley's regress genuinely vicious? And if you think the self-tying view collapses, tell me what should be given up instead — constituentism about propositions, the demand for a hook-up relation, or the whole idea that propositional unity is something needing an explanation. No "both sides have a point" ending please.

Don't bother with references or a reading list, I've got the literature already, I just want the argument.
