I have spent fifteen years as a process-safety engineer: chairing HAZOPs, filling in LOPA worksheets, sizing relief devices, arguing about which instrumented functions deserve which integrity level, and defending the position that a written procedure is not an independent protection layer. That vocabulary is how I reason about the ways systems fail, and it is the only one I can actually think in. I can follow a payoff matrix if someone draws one for me, but game-theoretic language is not native to me, and I have noticed that when I read about Moloch — the thesis that competitive dynamics compel every participant, however well disposed, to degrade safety, honesty, or the commons, and that any participant who refuses is displaced by one who does not — I nod along without genuinely understanding.

So translate it into my terms. Specifically, take the pessimistic argument about voluntary restraint: the claim that an informal industry-wide commitment to slow down fails even when every participant would prefer it and even when compliance is fully observable, because a defector gains an advantage that cannot be clawed back and because a new entrant who never signed inherits the race at full speed. Restate that argument claim by claim using the apparatus I work in — hazard versus unsafe condition, initiating events, independent protection layers and the independence assumptions LOPA requires before their failure probabilities may be multiplied, engineered versus administrative layers, common-cause and common-mode failure, integrity levels, prevention versus mitigation, residual risk and the question of who is entitled to accept it, ALARP, normalization of deviance, and the distinction between a scenario that has been prevented and one whose consequences have merely been contained. Anchor the whole translation in a single running example of a competitive race that you construct.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually want. For each significant claim, state whether it (i) translates cleanly, (ii) translates only with a distortion that you must name, or (iii) has no counterpart in my framework at all. Two disciplines apply. First, no smuggling: if a claim comes out better or worse supported once phrased in my terms only because those terms carry an assumption the original argument never made — that layers can be credited, that somebody owns the system, that the failure mode is unwanted by anyone — flag it as an import rather than presenting it as a finding. Second, be explicit wherever the translation flatters the pessimism or deflates it, and say which of the two it is doing.

Close on the most consequential gap you identified, and give me a verdict: does that gap mark a concept process safety ought to have and lacks, or a step in the Moloch argument that was never earned? Tell me how confident you are in the mapping overall, and single out the one item in the residue report you are least sure of. Hard limit: 900 words.
