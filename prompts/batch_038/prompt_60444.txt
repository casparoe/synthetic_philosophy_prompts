So I've basically signed on to Popper's line in The Poverty of Historicism: no laws of historical development, only trends, and trends are existence-dependent and bound to particular times and places, so anything that deserves to be called forecasting in history has to be conditional prediction built on ordinary underlying laws. What would count against that? I want the things that should actually move me, ranked from fatal to merely embarassing.

A few pressure points I already suspect, which I'd rather you take head-on than wave off:

- The reflexivity move (the Oedipus effect) looks like an objection to prophesying out loud, not to there being laws. Couldn't a science of history be perfectly true and simply kept quiet, or applied only to populations that never learn the prediction?
- The trend/law distinction is doing an enormous amount of work here. What would a genuine law of succession look like if I saw one, and is the difference ever anything more than a redescription available to the Popperian afterwards?
- The argument that we can't predict our own future knowledge: does the unpredictability of particular discoveries really carry over to their aggregate social effects, which is all the historicist needs?
- The empirical side. There's now a field that fits dynamical models to long-run history — structural-demographic models, multi-generational cycles of instability, a big comparative databank of world history — and one of its founders put a short correspondence piece in Nature in 2010 saying the coming decade would be one of growing instability in the US and western Europe. Say that kind of thing keeps landing. Is it a tendency or a law of succession, and what's the difference in practice?

Then tell me what an actual refutation would have to look like — the test I should pre-commit to, including what makes a prediction novel, registered in advance, and specific enough that it can't be reinterpreted afterwards. Personally the thing most likely to move me is a case where the historical model called something that no rival framework called, and got the timing roughly right. Which of these would a committed Popperian concede cuts against them, and which would they say misses the point entirely? And please be willing to conclude the position is well insulated and tell me whether that's a virtue or a symptom.
