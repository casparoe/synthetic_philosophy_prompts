My partner does investor relations, and last night she wrote the following on the back of a reciept to convince me that "lying vs. misleading" is a fake distinction. I'm pretty sure she's wrong but I keep loosing the thread somewhere in the middle.

Her setup: an analyst asks her point blank, "were there any customer cancellations in Q3?" She answers, "we closed the quarter with more customers than we opened it." Literally true — one big logo signed in September — but three mid-sized accounts left in July, and the analyst walks away thinking churn was zero. Compare the flat-out lie: "no cancellations in Q3."

Her argument:

(1) What makes lying wrong, when it is wrong, is that you deliberately plant a false belief in someone who is relying on you to be straight with them. Nothing else is doing the work — not the words, not the grammar.
(2) You can deliberately plant exactly that false belief with literally true words: the Q3 answer does it.
(3) Across the two cases the analyst's false belief is the same, her reliance is the same, the intent is the same, and the downstream harm is the same. The only thing that differs is the truth value of the sentence uttered.
(4) A difference makes a moral difference only if it makes a difference to how things go for somebody. The truth value of a sentence, all by itself, doesn't.
(5) So there is no moral difference between the lie and the Q3 answer. Misleading is just lying with extra steps.
(6) And the same reasoning runs to anything where you can foresee the hearer drawing a false inference and you say nothing to stop it — including simply not volunteering the information in the first place.
(7) But ordinary conversation is built out of inferences speakers don't guarantee and information they don't volunteer. "I've eaten" doesn't say when. "The meeting went fine" doesn't say fine in every respect. Nobody discloses everything they know.
(8) So if (5) and (6) are right, basically every ordinary utterance is a lie-grade wrong.
(9) That can't be right, so (1) has to go — lying really is worse in kind, not just in degree.

Here's my problem. I want to keep (1) and (2). The Q3 answer seems to me genuinely bad, and I don't want to get pushed into the position that literal truth launders a deliberate deception.
