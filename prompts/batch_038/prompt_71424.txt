So my building-heritage nerd roommate and I got into it the other night about Notre-Dame and I realized neither of us could actually settle anything with the metaphysics we're supposed to have learned, and I want to see if you can do better. The facts, in case any of them matter: the fire was 15 April 2019, it took the spire and most of the roof, the stone shell and the vaults largely held, the rose windows and the organs came through. The spire that fell wasn't medieval — the original one from the 1220s was taken down at the end of the 18th century and the building sat without a spire for decades until Viollet-le-Duc's replacement went up in 1859 — while the roof timbers that burned were largely 13th-century oak. Also, four days before the fire the sixteen apostle and evangelist statues at the base of the spire had been lifted off by crane and sent away for restoration, so during the blaze they were sitting in a workshop somewhere and the cathedral had parts that were nowhere near it; they went back onto the new spire afterwards. Macron set a five-year deadline within a couple of days and floated an international competition for a contemporary spire, and then a year or so later, after a heritage commission weighed in, that got dropped and the decision came down to rebuild the spire and roof exactly as they had been, new oak and all. There was no Christmas Mass in 2019, the first time since 1803, and it reopened in December 2024 with the new spire finished at the end of 2023.

What I want is that whole sequence run through the endurantism/perdurantism debate — not a summary of the debate, I know it, wholly present at each moment versus spacetime worm with temporal parts. Do the actual mapping. Start with how many objects there even were on each view, because I think that's where it gets interesting: there's the physical structure, and there's the cathedral in the sense of the archbishop's seat, which never stopped being a cathedral for one single day and whose status has nothing to do with the condition of the timbers, and there's the monument in the sense heritage people mean it, the thing that's on the UNESCO list and gets discussed in terms of authenticity and integrity and minimal intervention. Are those one object under three descriptions or three objects with different persistence conditions, and do endurantism and perdurantism even agree on the count? Then: under perdurantism, was anything destroyed on 15 April? A worm doesn't obviously get destroyed by acquiring a ragged end and then some new temporal parts, so where does the sense that something was lost live in that ontolgy — can the theory locate it, or does it have to say the loss was really about a different worm entirely? And under endurantism, what makes the 2024 structure the same wholly present object as the 2018 one when essentially none of the burnt roof matter is left — spatiotemporal continuity, functional role, the intentions of the restorers, something else — and is there any principled reason to prefer one of those over the others here rather than just picking? Bonus points if you can say what each view does with the statues being off-site during the fire.

The bit I most want pressed on: the decision had to choose a moment to restore to, and it chose 1859 rather than the 1220s or the spireless 1840s. So rebuilding it as it was turned out never to be a discovery about the object's identity, it was a choice about which slice counts as the one to be identical to. Can either theory say that choice was metaphysically correct rather than merely conventional, or are both just silent — and if both are silent, does the silence count against the whole debate, or does it only show that the debate is operating at a level this kind of case never reaches? Relatedly, nobody in the real argument ever asked whether it was numerically the same building. They said authentic, identical, faithful, as it was. Tell me what those words are actually tracking and whether either theory has a home for them or has to treat them as confused. And then run it backwards: does the Notre-Dame episode do anything at all for or against endurantism or perdurantism — a confirmation, an anomaly, a case neither side anticipated — or is the honest verdict that a real restoration controversy is just the wrong kind of evidence for a metaphysical dispute, and if so, what would the right kind of evidence look like?
