I'm a policy analyst for a state legislature that's about to take up a universal education savings account, and both sides keep reaching for the same book — Hirschman's Exit, Voice, and Loyalty. The choice people say exit is what makes voice credible, that the option to leave is the only thing that forces a school system to listen to parents. The other side says exit is a luxury of the articulate and that it hollows out the institution for whoever can't leave. I've read the book and I don't trust either camp's version of it, so I want to settle it by running the framework on the one American case where exit got pushed all the way to the wall: Prince Edward County, Virginia.

Facts, so nobody has to guess at them. In 1959, after a federal court ordered the county to desegregate, the board of supervisors just declined to appropriate any money for the public schools, and they stayed shut for five years. The county and state handed out tuition grants to any student, race-neutral on paper, for private nonsectarian schooling; a private academy opened that September and within a few years essentially every white child in the county was in it. There were no private schools for Black children, so the grants were worth nothing to them, and Black kids in the county went without formal schooling from 1959 to 1963. A private foundation offered to open a school for Black children and the community turned it down — partly, in the Supreme Court's telling, because they wanted to keep fighting for desegregated public schools. In 1963, with federal and state help, classes finally started up again in the county's own empty school buildings. The Court ordered the schools reopened and funded in 1964 and, unusually, directed the supervisors to levy taxes to pay for it.

What I want:

First, do the mapping and be picky about it. Who or what is "the organization" here — the school system, the county, the state, the segregated order as a whole? The answer changes everything downstream and I want to see you notice that rather than pick one silently. Who exits, from what, and what counts as voice addressed to whom? Flag that the exits were not individual responses to perceived decline: the institution was killed first and exit was made available afterward, at public expense, largely by the people doing the exiting.

Second, run the framework. Hirschman's own illustration is a public school where quality-conscious parents pull their kids into private schools, the school loses precisely the parents who would have complained, and it locks into decline — he even floats that a tight monopoly might be better than allowing that exit. Is Prince Edward County that example with the dial turned to eleven, or is it structurally different because the exiters were causing the decline rather than fleeing it? Where does loyalty show up — I think the foundation refusal is a textbook loyalty datum, holding exit at bay for political reasons, and I want to know whether you agree or whether that's a misuse of the category. Does the theory have any slot at all for exit that isn't a recovery mechanism, exit whose object is the organization's death? And is a suit to a federal court — an outside sovereign with power over the organization — "voice" in his sense, or something the framework has no category for?

Third, turn it around on the theory. Is the five-year closure a confirmation (the articulate leave, the institution collapses, the stayers lose voice entirely), an anomaly, or a case he never contemplated because his examples are firms and parties that need their members to stay? Be honest about whether the seesaw — easier exit means less voice — even gets tested here or just gets assumed.

Finally, the verdict I actually need: can Prince Edward County carry any evidential weight in an argument about universal ESAs today, or is it so saturated with race and captured local government that citing it is just rhetorical theft? If it can carry weight, name exactly which mechanism transfers to a normal district and which one doesn't, and say what would have to be true about our bill for the bad mechanism to be live.

No history lesson — I know the story — and don't hedge everything into mush. Where the theory is silent, say it's silent instead of making it talk.
