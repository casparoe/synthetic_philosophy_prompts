I chair the medical policy committee at a regional insurer, about 400k members. Roughly a dozen coverage requests a month turn on the same question in different clothes: is this treatment or enhancement? Our contract defines medical necessity as care that restores or maintains function toward normal for age and sex, and I want one standing procedure our physician reviewers can run — they have no philosophy background and about 30 minutes a case. Right now they basically pattern-match to whatever the manufacturer's brochure claims, and the decisions dont survive appeal.

So write the procedure. Ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it terminates in "not covered," and where it explicitly hands the call to a human because the issue is about values rather than function. Tell me which two or three distinctions have to survive the simplification and which ones I can drop. And be straight about which steps are judgment calls wearing a procedure's costume — especially the age-and-sex-adjusted baseline, which seems to make "restoring a 68-year-old to 30-year-old levels" a bookkeeping question rather than a medical one.

Then run it on three: testosterone for that 68-year-old whose level is normal for his age but low for a 30-year-old's; a cochlear implant for a prelingually deaf four-year-old whose Deaf parents are refusing it; and modafinil for a long-haul trucker with no sleep disorder. I expect at least one answer to be one I'll hate. Close with what the procedure can't do and where it will misfire predictably.
