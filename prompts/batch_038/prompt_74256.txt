Okay, I'm not a philosopher and I've never taken a class in any of this — I'm a pharmacy tech at a chain store outside Columbus and I got sucked into this question about a year ago when my son got diagnosed with ADHD. He's on methylphenidate now and it genuinely changed his life, he went from failing everything and crying over homework to being an ordinary eleven year old. Around the same time one of the techs I work with started ordering modafinil off a website because she picks up extra doubles. No diagnosis, nothing, she just wants to be awake. And I realized I think the first one is obviously medicine and the second one is obviously cheating, and I could not for the life of me say why, because they are the same kind of thing going into the same kind of brain. I've been arguing with my husband about it ever since and I've written down my gut calls on a bunch of related cases, and what I want from you is the principle, if there is one, that they actually add up to — or an honest verdict that they add up to nothing and I just have a pile of vibes in a lab coat.

Here's the list. My kid's methylphenidate: completely fine, that's treatment. My coworker's modafinil: not fine, that's an edge. But my coworker also drinks three Monsters a shift and I don't think that's an edge at all, and I cannot locate the difference between the energy drink and the pill except that one comes with a label you can read. Fluoride in the city water: totally fine, even though nobody drinking it is sick and its doing something to everybody's body without asking. But if the same city handed out a little tablet at the dentist's office that did the exact same thing to the exact same teeth, and healthy kids had to get it prescribed — that one makes me squirm, and I can't say why, because the chemistry is identical and the only thing that changed is how it gets into you. LASIK: fine, feels like medicine to me, even though glasses already solved the problem perfectly well for basically no money. An implant that gave somebody sharper-than-20/20 vision: I flipflop on this, some days fine, some days deeply wrong. Cochlear implant for a Deaf adult who wants one: fine. Same implant for a Deaf toddler whose parents want one: I said not fine for years and now I'm not so sure. Growth hormone for a kid who's healthy but 4'11" and getting eaten alive by his classmates: not fine, that's his parents buying him an advantage. But if the endocrinologist says he's right at the bottom, borderline deficient, suddenly it's fine — and the line between those two cases is a quarter of an inch on a growth chart. Beta blocker for the cellist down the hall who gets the shakes before auditions: fine. Same pill for a surgeon with no tremor diagnosis who just wants steadier hands through an eleven hour case: I said not fine, but his stakes are higher and the drug isn't making him better than he already is, so I suspect I have that one backwards.

The half-baked theory I've got, which you should attack rather than be polite about: it's treatment if you're putting someone back to normal and enhancement if you're taking them past normal, and everything on my list that I call cheating is the past-normal stuff. Trouble is I don't think "normal" is doing any real work there — it feels like the answer hiding inside the question — and it doesn't tell me whether a Deaf kid who has never heard anything is below normal or just differently normal. There's also a second thing underneath my list that I only noticed recently: half the cases I call cheating are only bad because if everyone did them, nobody would be better off. The height one, the focus one. If every kid in the class is on the pill, the kid who isn't is the handicapped one, and my son is going to grow up in a world where he has to take something just to stay even. So maybe what actually bothers me isn't the drug at all but the arms race, and my whole treatment-versus-enhancement framing is me mislabeling a fairness problem as a medical one. I'd like you to test that hard.
