The trouble is that everybody in the literature seems to be grading proposed answers against a different set of criteria, and half of those criteria are just the grader's own conclusion wearing a disguise: whoever insists that meaning has to be publicly checkable has already decided the diarist loses, and whoever insists the argument can't be allowed to touch public language at all has decided he wins. So I don't want another reading of the text off you. I want the spec. Give me the list of requirements that any account of what fixes the meaning of a sign — and in particular any proposed diagnosis of what goes wrong, or what goes right, in the 'S' diary from §256 onward — has to meet to count as adequate. For this list, fix the purpose as follows: the account must be statable to somebody who isn't already a Wittgensteinian, so 'form of life', 'grammar' and 'criteria' can't do any unexplained work, and it must be capable of actually settling whether the diarist case is a genuine reductio or a bit of theatre. For each requirement I want a short handle, the case or consideration behind it — i.e. the concrete situation that breaks an account which lacks it, drawn from the private ostension, the whatever-seems-right-is-right move, the split between memory scepticism and meaning scepticism, the last speaker of a dying language, the manometer, the fact that an uncracked code isn't thereby private — and a tag saying whether it's non-negotiable, a preference of one camp, or something you'd strike off the list altogether. If there's room at the end of that section, two or three lines each on how the standard diagnoses do against the list, but keep it short, the list is the point.
