i teach a grad seminar on the foundations of consumer theory and i want something to open the last session with: a sestina on revealed preference.

the six end-words should be the operative vocabulary — something like choice, preference, utility, consistent, index, want, though pick your own six as long as each one can be read both as an ordinary word and as a term of art. the point is that the form has to do the arguement: the retrograde rotation never lets in a seventh term, so the poem should make a reader feel the charge that the theory only shuffles the choice data it started with and then presents the shuffle as an explanation. somewhere in the middle stanzas i want a violation of transitivity to happen as an event, a person actually choosing in a circle and getting pumped, rather than just being named — but it should be recognizable as the consistency condition the theory needs, not a random bit of irrationality. the envoi carries what the program's defenders think they genuinely bought with all that rearranging, and it shouldn't collapse into a slogan on either side: not "it's a tautology in a nice hat" and not "it saved economics from psychology."

two asks. first, keep the economics right — someone who works on this should recognize the terrain (operationalism, the as-if gloss on prediction, whether preference talk earns its keep once utility is defined only by what gets chosen) instead of just hearing a rhyme scheme with hard words at the ends. second, after the poem, add a short note that seperates what's actually settled here from what's still live among people who know the literature, and flags anywhere the poem leans on a contested reading or smooths over a disagreement in order to land the form.
