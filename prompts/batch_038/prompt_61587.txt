can you write me a product recall notice — the boring official kind, with affected batch numbers, a "what you should do" section, and a toll-free line — for a company that sells installable character traits, where one production run of their Courage 4.1 module turned out defective. 550–650 words.

the humor has to come from the company trying and failing to say what actually went wrong. some units stop manifesting courage right when the triggering situation shows up, because a third-party "humility patch" yanked the disposition out beforehand; other units still have it, fully intact, but something in the environment is blocking it from expressing itself. the warranty covers one failure mode and not the other, or mabe the reverse, and legal keeps softening the distinction because spelling it out means admitting a trait isn't just a matter of what its bearer does.

please include: a section telling customers how to check whether theirs is affected, where the procedure is quietly useless — the only way to test a disposition is to trigger it, and triggering it under controlled conditions changes the very conditions the test was supposed to hold fixed; a short FAQ in which someone asks point blank "so is my unit brave or not" and gets four paragraphs of hedging; and a closing note where the company adds a proviso about "normal circumstances" that makes the whole warranty unfalsifiable, with everyone pretending not to notice that they've just done it.

deadpan corporate voice throughout, no winking, and never name the philosophical problem or gesture at it in so many words — a reader who works on dispositions should recognize exactly what's being dodged, and a reader who doesn't should still get the joke about a company covering its ass. no citations, no real philosophers, no reading list. just the fine print.
