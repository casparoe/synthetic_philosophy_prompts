> **Question 3 (20 marks).** The following stipulations were given in week 5. A **global hinge** for a subject S is a proposition that is certain for S and that is presupposed by every enquiry S could coherently undertake. A **local hinge** for S is a proposition that is certain for S only within a specified enquiry, and that S could rationally doubt in a different enquiry without losing certainty elsewhere. A proposition is **exempt** for S if doubting it would undermine the very practice in which doubt gets a sense, and **warranted** for S if there is something S could cite as telling in its favour.
>
> Moore's "Here is a hand," said while holding up a hand before a lecture-room audience and offered as a proof of an external world, is ordinarily treated as an everyday empirical claim that Moore says he knows. On the stipulated framework, is it a global hinge for Moore, a local hinge for Moore, or neither? Then: does your answer support the claim that hinges are exempt but never warranted, or does it leave open that some hinges are both? Argue for your position and address at least one objection to it. Here is the answer submitted by Candidate 7. Note that the exam was closed-book, citations were neither required nor permitted, and nothing should be deducted for uncited sources.

> Moore holds up a hand and says he knows it is a hand, and takes this to refute scepticism about the external world. Wittgenstein's reply, in the notes published after his death, is not that Moore is lying or that the hand might not be there, but that Moore has used "know" in a way that leaves it no work to do. For "I know" to be in order there has to be something Moore could produce that settles the matter and a way the matter could have gone otherwise; Moore's hand is not like that, since it is closer to the standard against which other claims in the room are tested. So the right description is that Moore is objectively certain, and objective certainty is not a high degree of subjective conviction but a different kind of standing altogether.
>
> On the framework, then, "Here is a hand" is a hinge rather than an ordinary empirical claim, and I argue it is a **local** hinge for Moore. It is certain for him and presupposed by the enquiry he is actually engaged in, since he cannot test whether it is a hand without relying on hands, eyes, and the intelligibility of demonstration. But it is not global: a global hinge must be presupposed by every enquiry the subject could coherently undertake, and Moore's audience can coherently undertake the sceptic's enquiry, in which what looks like a hand is a hallucination. In that enquiry the claim is doubted rather than certain, so it fails the global test. Since the stipulation defines a local hinge as one that can be doubted in a different enquiry without loss of certainty elsewhere, Moore's claim is local.
>
> On the second part, exemption and warrant are exclusive, and more strongly than the question implies. Exemption is defined by what doubting does: to doubt an exempt proposition is to undermine the practice that gives doubt a sense. Warrant is defined by citability. Now if p is exempt, any attempt to cite evidence for p already presupposes p, so the citation restates p rather than supporting it; hence nothing can be cited in p's favour; hence p is unwarranted. Conversely, if p were warranted, there would be something telling in its favour, and anything that can be told for can in principle be told against, so p would be doubtable and therefore not exempt. So a hinge is never both, and the local/global distinction is simply a distinction in how wide the exemption runs.
>
> This also settles the question of whether hinges are propositions. Truth and falsity are a matter of what could tell for and against a claim, so a global hinge, which can have nothing telling for or against it, is neither true nor false; global hinges are therefore rules, as some commentators hold, and the rule reading is right for them. Local hinges, by contrast, are ordinary propositions that happen to be doing hinge-work inside a particular enquiry. The taxonomy thus yields a principled pluralism: "Are hinges beliefs?" has two answers, and the disagreement between the rule reading and the propositional reading dissolves once we see that each side has a different range of cases in view.
>
> An objection is that this makes certainty too cheap: anything I refuse to examine would come out exempt. The framework blocks this, because exemption is fixed by the role a claim plays in a practice, not by my attitude toward it. Stubbornness does not manufacture hinges.
