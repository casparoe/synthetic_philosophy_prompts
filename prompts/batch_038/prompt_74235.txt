Last session of my philosophy-of-AI seminar is the frame problem and I want to walk in and hand out a poem instead of a lecture. It has to be a sestina: thirty-nine lines, six stanzas of six plus the three-line envoi, the standard rotation of end-words, no rhyme. And I want to fix the end-words myself, because the whole point is that the vocabulary is closed and the poem isnt allowed to reach outside it. They are: persist, load, wait, shoot, frame, Fred. Inflect them however you like (persisted, persists, frames, framing) but no substitutions — if you find yourself needing a word that isn't on that list, that failure is the poem's actual subject, not a licence to cheat.

The permutation has to carry the argument, not decorate it. First stanza: the naive picture, where you just write down what each action changes and assume the rest takes care of itself. Second stanza: the bill arrives, one frame axiom per fluent per action, forever, and the poem should feel the tedium of it. Third stanza can be the effects you didn't list because they follow from the ones you did. Then I want the anomalous model somewhere around stanza four — the gun has somehow gotten itself unloaded during the wait, Fred wanders off alive, and nothing in the premises licensed any of it. Don't apologize for it and don't explain it away; just let it sit there the way minimizing change did. The envoi has to use all six words at once and I'd like it to be about what's still missing once the list is exhausted, the conditions nobody thought to write down in the first place.

Half the class has never met the situation calculus, so it needs to read as a poem first and foremost; the two or three who know the literature should be able to spot every stage anyway, including our formal methods colleague who will be sitting in the back and will absolutely notice if I've botched it. I've taught this material for twenty years and I still cant explain why that second model bothers me as much as it does — maybe the form can. No jokes about robots, or at least none of the obvious ones.
