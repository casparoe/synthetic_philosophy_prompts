I have to hand in a one-page statement of where I've got to for an epistemology seminar on Tuesday, and everybody elses will be sharper than mine, so I'd rather find the holes myself first. Here's the package:

1. The anti-luminosity argument (Williamson's, the feeling-cold one) is sound. You can be moving along the series, actually be cold, and still not know you're cold. I accept the margin-for-error premise that does the real work in it, not just the conclusion.

2. And I don't think that's a quirk of temperature phenomenology. Roughly: no non-trivial condition is luminous. If a condition can obtain in a case where you can't tell it obtains, that tells you something general about knowledge, not something special about cold.

3. KK, stated weakly. If I know p, I'm in a position to know that I know p. I'm not claiming I'd ever actually form that second-order belief, just that nothing stands in the way of it.

4. The reason knowledge needs margins is something in the safety family: to know p, p has to hold in the nearby cases where I form the belief the same way. And I don't think this requirement changes shape or gets more lenient when the thing known is itself a fact about what I know. Same rule at every order.

5. My evidence is exactly what I know. No more, no less.

6. Justification is internally accessible: whether a belief is justified for me depends only on my own mental states, and I can find out about those states by reflecting.

7. Not everything needs a margin, though. I take it I can know that I believe that p with no risk of error whatsoever, and I can know that 7 is prime with no risk of error whatsoever. Margins only bite where the content is the sort of thing that could easily have been otherwise.

8. Knowledge isn't closed under known implication. Working out that q follows from p doesn't by itself put me in a position to know q.

For what it's worth my one vague unease is about 8 — I've never been able to tell whether denying closure like that is compatible with the rest of this, and nobody has ever said straight out. Everything else feels solid to me, I just cant see the whole thing from the outside.
