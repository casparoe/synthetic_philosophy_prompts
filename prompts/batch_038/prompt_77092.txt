I am preparing seminar material on higher-order theories of consciousness, and I want to test the theories against a case I have constructed rather than against the stock objections.

The case, which I will call the Displaced Report: Nora's visual system is functioning normally, and she is looking at a red square on a white page. It produces a first-order visual state V that accurately carries information about that red square. However, the only higher-order representation Nora has as of V is mismatched: it presents V as being a state as of a green triangle. Assume that this higher-order representation is caused by V and by nothing else, that it is neither inferential nor observational in any sense that would disqualify it on your preferred account, that Nora has no other route of access to V, and that she is in every other respect a normally functioning, non-confabulating subject. Asked what she sees, she sincerely answers: a green triangle.

For each of the five positions listed below, do three things. First, state the verdict the position is committed to: what it is like for Nora, if anything, and whether her report is a mistake about her own experience. Second, write the single most awkward sentence that a sincere and competent defender of the position would be obliged to utter in defending that verdict — the sentence that defender would least like to hear read aloud at a conference. Third, assign a score from 0 to 10 for how well the position handles the case, where 10 means the verdict is one its defender would happily advertise as a virtue of the view, and 0 means the case forces the position into something its defender would treat as a reductio of it.

The positions:
1. Rosenthal's actualist higher-order thought theory;
2. Carruthers' dispositionalist higher-order thought theory;
3. a higher-order perception or inner-sense account of Lycan's kind;
4. Kriegel's self-representationalism;
5. a first-order representationalist account of Tye's or Dretske's kind, included only as a control.

Then give the five scores as a strict ordering, breaking any ties and saying what breaks them.

Two working constraints. Work from your own understanding of these theories: I want no references, no bibliography, no page numbers, and I would rather you reason from the structure of each view than hedge by gesturing at the literature. Where a position comes in more than one variety — Rosenthal's own formulation has shifted over the years, and Carruthers' account is embedded in a broader scepticism about phenomenal consciousness as it is usually conceived — choose the version most favourable to the position, and say explicitly that you are doing so.

Close with a paragraph on two things. If any two of the five positions turn out to be committed to the same verdict while differing only in their explanation of it, say which ones, and say whether that undercuts the Displaced Report as a test of anything. And say what would change your mind: identify the single consideration that, if you came to believe it, would most reorder your scores, and point to the place where your own reasoning above is most likely to have gone wrong — in particular, whether you have silently assumed that phenomenal character must track the content of the higher-order representation, or the content of the first-order state, and which of the two you have smuggled in without arguing for it.
