For two years I've run all my numbers through an AI — I'm a small-scale market gardener and it does my margins, my rotation planning, my tax stuff. Every answer it's given me has checked out against reality. But it was down for three days last month and I couldn't do a single thing by hand, not even break-even on a flat of seedlings. So: at what point did I stop understanding my own business?

Before I read any more about cognitive offloading or epistemic dependence or whatever it gets called, I want the spec, not the theories. Write out what any account of "outsourcing costs you understanding" has to deliver: the cases it has to get right (my blackout weekend, the community that's forgotten how to find its way around because everyone follows satnav, and the person who understands *more* because the tool explains as it goes), the distinctions it can't collapse (understanding vs. getting the right answer vs. being reliable vs. being in charge of your own thinking), and the thing it can't just wave away — if you're happy calling the notebook in someone's pocket part of their mind, why isn't the chatbot part of mine? Give the consideration behind each item, and tell me which of these are genuine requirements and which are really just one camp's prejudices dressed up. Flag any pair you think can't both be satisfied.

My own candidate criterion is what I call the blackout test: unplug the tool for a week, see what's left. Tell me whether that earns a place on the list or whether it begs the question. Then one line each on how "if the answers are right nothing is lost," "outsourcing always costs you," and something in between score against the finished list.

Last thing — say how confident you are in the list overall, and single out the one item you're least sure belongs on it.
