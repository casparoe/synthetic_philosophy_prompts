I run a monthly philosophy meetup — mostly ex-colleagues from a job I quit two years ago, plus a couple of people who originally came for the free wine and never left — and next month's session is Strawson's Basic Argument, the one that says you can't be truly morally responsible for an action unless you were responsible for the character it sprang from, and since nothing can be causa sui you can never get that regress started, so nobody is ever truly responsible, determinism or no determinism. The problem is that half the group has already announced "yeah, obviously, no free will," and the other half keeps saying "it's too fast" without being able to say *where*. So I want to build a decision tree that sorts them by what they actually believe instead of by which label they shout. The branch nodes shouldn't be "are you a determinist?" — that's the wrong first question, the argument is supposed to go through either way — but the individual pressure points: whether responsibility for an act requires responsibility for the character it came from; whether responsibility for your character requires having *chosen* it or only having *shaped* it; whether an endless chain of self-shaping choices could still terminate in something good enough even if it never bottoms out in a self-caused self; whether the regress is vicious or merely infinite; whether the word "true" in "truly responsible" is smuggling in an impossible standard. Every node needs to be phrased as a case or a plain yes/no claim someone can answer cold, so please don't ask anyone whether they're a compatibilist.

I have three test cases I want used as nodes — feel free to add more, but keep these three in. (1) The Clinic: at 25, Mara books a procedure that dampens her quick temper because she's sick of hurting the people she loves; the desire to book it grows out of guilt she never chose; is she more responsible, less responsible, or exactly as responsible for what she does afterwards as before? (2) The Slow Version: identical, except the temper is worn down over thirty years of ordinary choices rather than one procedure, and I want the tree to force a verdict on whether that difference matters at all or is just cosmetic. (3) The Author: someone who shapes their own second-order desires with no third-order court of appeal anywhere above them — ask what, if anything, is missing from that picture that "real" responsibility supposedly needs, and make the answer options concrete enough to be contentious. The leaves should be named positions: Strawson's own skepticism, Frankfurt-style hierarchical compatibilism, reasons-responsiveness views of the Fischer-and-Ravizza type, agent-causal libertarianism, event-causal libertarianism with something like Kane's self-forming actions, hard determinism, illusionism about free will, and the deflationary reply that the argument proves too much and is really a sorites in disguise — plus any leaf you think I've forgotten.

Two more asks. First, flag every pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate: I suspect hard determinism and Strawson's skepticism will nearly collapse into each other since one looks like a special case of the other, and I'd rather the tree said so out loud than quietly fudged a branch. Second, wherever a combination of answers is logically available but nobody seems to hold it — say, someone who thinks self-shaping is enough for responsibility but also thinks the regress has to terminate somewhere — mark the cell empty and give me a one-line diagnosis of why: incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. Then close with a short paragraph telling me which single node, if I only have ten minutes before the wine shows up, would sort the room best, and why. Please start straight in with the tree itself, first node and all, with no preamble and no rephrasing of what I asked for, and don't tell me which positions you think are right.
