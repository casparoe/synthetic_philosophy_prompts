I work in institutional research at a public university and there's a thing in our annual equity report I've never been able to make sense of, philosophically. Figured I'd ask.

Every year we publish three sets of numbers on graduate admissions, broken out by gender. (1) The overall admit rate for the whole university. (2) The admit rate within each department. (3) A "standardized" overall rate, where we take each department's admit rate and weight it by a fixed reference mix of applications — basically the departmental composition from a base year, held constant so the headline number doesn't move just because engineering got bigger.

Most years, some departments have gaps pointing the opposite direction from the aggregate. Simpson's paradox, I know, I've read the pop explanations. What I dont get is that the report publishes all three numbers side by side with no note about which one is the finding, and different offices cite different ones in different meetings. And the standardized number isn't stable in the way it's presented as being: the reference mix gets rebased every five years, and when it does, the trend line for the past decade visibly shifts, sometimes enough to change which years count as improvements. That never gets written up as a change in what's being measured. It's treated as the same quantity, measured better.

Layered on top is a second practice. When a department actually gets in trouble over it's numbers, the university's own explanation is almost never about that department's admit rate. It's about decisions made above it — how many lines went to which division, whether we chose to grow a field, whether a department was funded at all. So the institution holds itself answerable for the aggregate while explaining that aggregate entirely by the shape of the partition, and treats the cells as somebody else's business.

So: what view of probability, or of measurement, or of what a statistic is even about, does this whole apparatus presuppose? I'm especially interested in:

- what publishing (1) and (2) unannotated assumes about whether "the admit rate" names one quantity whose apparent value depends on the level of aggregation as an artifact, or two genuinely different quantities that both exist;
- what the standardized number assumes — that composition is a nuisance to be stripped away? that there's a version of the university's behavior invariant to who applies where? Is there such a thing, and does the practice need there to be?
- whether rebasing every five years is consistent with the claim that it's the same quantity across time, or whether the report is silently trading one quantity for another and calling it progress;
- whether the second practice is coherent: under what account of being answerable for an outcome is a within-department reversal exculpatory for the departments but not for the university?

Reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as you can, and tell me which rival positions it quietly rules out. Then say what would have to change in the report if we replaced it with the view that the right level of aggregation is fixed by causal structure, and separately with the view that it's fixed by whatever decision the number is going to feed — I gather those come apart here and I'd like to see exactly where. Also keep clear about what the practice strictly presupposes versus what it just happens to sit comfortably with; my suspicion is that a lot of this is underdetermined and that several incompatible theories would license the exact same PDF.

No citations needed, I'll chase down sources myself. But do mark as you go which parts of your reconstruction are settled and which are live arguments among people who actually work on this — I'd rather not walk away thinking there's a consensus where there isn't one.
