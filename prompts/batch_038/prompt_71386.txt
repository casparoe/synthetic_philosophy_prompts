Okay so I know about Buridan's ass, and I don't want a recap of the ass — I'm after something narrower that I keep running into and have no name for.

The situation: two options, and the person genuinely can't rank them. Not "hasn't thought hard enough" — they've thought about it for weeks and everything relevant cancels out. So they settle it with a coin. Here's the bit I actually care about. The instant the coin lands they feel a preference. Not a shrug-and-go preference, a real pull toward whichever one it picked, and a minute later they'll tell you they always leaned that way, they just needed to confirm it. But the coin was fair and they'd have felt the identical pull toward the other one if it had come up tails. So the procedure didn't find a preference that was already sitting there, and it didn't just break the tie mechanically either — it seems to have made the preference, and then made it feel like a discovery.

Same shape, different setting: a committee is deadlocked and votes to draw lots, everyone agrees in advance to be bound, the lot falls, and within a day most of them are privately relieved it went that way and can list reasons why the drawn option was the better one all along.

Does this have an established name? I can see its in the neighborhood of post-decision rationalizing, and of people rating what they picked higher once they've picked it, but neither seems to get at the three things that strike me as doing the work: (a) there was nothing there beforehand to be revealed, (b) it would have gone exactly the same way in the other direction, and (c) it looks like it has to be self-administered — when someone else flips the coin and hands you the result, I don't think you get the pull at all, you just get resignation.

If there's no term for it, make one up and give me a real definition with the edges drawn: what counts as a case, what's nearby but doesn't count, and how you'd tell them apart in practice. The label isn't the point, the carving is — I don't want just a name back.
