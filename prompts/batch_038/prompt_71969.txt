I am the TA for an upper-level philosophy of physics course on irreversibility and the arrow of time, and I need to grade the following final-exam answer. I would like a grade plus the specific comments that justify it.

**Question:** Explain why the time-reversal invariance of the underlying microdynamics makes the Second Law problematic, and what further assumptions are needed to recover an entropy increase at all. Then say what the Past Hypothesis adds, and assess whether it merely relocates the problem it was meant to solve.

**Student's answer:** "Because Hamiltonian dynamics is time-reversal invariant, for every solution in which entropy rises there is a time-reversed solution in which it falls, so the dynamics alone cannot privilege either temporal direction. Liouville's theorem makes this vivid: phase-space volume is preserved, so the fine-grained Gibbs entropy is constant along a trajectory, and any increase in coarse-grained entropy belongs to the description we impose rather than to the microphysics. What Boltzmann supplied in response is a probabilistic version of the H-theorem: after Loschmidt's recurrence objection he conceded that entropy decrease is possible but overwhelmingly improbable, so the Second Law holds only statistically — a claim now confirmed by fluctuation experiments on small systems over short times, in which transient violations are routinely measured. Recovering an increase even statistically requires one further assumption about the initial microstate, namely that it is typical of the low-entropy macrostate the universe began in. The Past Hypothesis supplies the other half: the early universe was in a very special macrostate, smooth and nearly uniform rather than clumped into black holes, and hence of very low entropy once gravitation is counted. On this account the Past Hypothesis is not an unexplained posit, because cosmic inflation now explains it: a brief period of exponential expansion smooths out any inhomogeneity, so the special initial condition falls out of the dynamics instead of being assumed. Nor is it ad hoc, since its consequences are exactly what we observe. Finally, the proposal is continuous with Boltzmann's own thinking: his fluctuation hypothesis likewise holds that our region of the universe sits in an improbably low-entropy state, so the Past Hypothesis is best read as a modern restatement of it rather than as a rival to it."
