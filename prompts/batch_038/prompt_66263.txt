I teach the philosophy of well-being, though my doctoral training was in behavioural neuroscience, and that is still the vocabulary I reach for first: incentive salience, the wanting/liking dissociation, hedonic hotspots, reward prediction error, alliesthesia, consummatory affective reactions as the operational measure of hedonic impact. Please restate the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — the difficulty of saying what the pleasure of a cold drink, of a proved theorem, and of a remembered afternoon have in common — together with the principal answers to it (a single common felt quality; an occurrent or dispositional desire that the experience continue; an attitudinal analysis on which pleasure is a matter of being pleased that something is so; and the deflationary option that the pleasures form no natural kind but only a family resemblance) in that neuroscientific vocabulary, family by family, as faithfully as the material allows. Then supply the residue report: which theses translate without loss, which can be rendered only by letting the target framework's own commitments do substantive work — most obviously, by treating the framework's measure of hedonic impact as an analysis of pleasure rather than as evidence about it — and which admit of no rendering at all. Two probes in particular. First, alliesthesia: the hedonic sign of a fixed stimulus flips with the subject's internal state; does this datum describe heterogeneity in the pleasures, or only in their causes? Second, the attitudinal case: a parent's pleasure in her daughter's recovery has no consummatory reaction, no hotspot activity, and no prediction error I can locate; say where it goes and what is lost in sending it there. Close with your verdict on the residue as a whole — a limitation of the target vocabulary, or evidence that the felt-quality thesis was never a substantive claim.
