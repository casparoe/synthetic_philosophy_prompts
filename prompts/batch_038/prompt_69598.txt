Our reading group on the thermodynamics of computation has spent a month on the history of Maxwell's demon and reached an impasse: all eight of us agree that a measuring, memory-using feedback device working against a single heat bath cannot deliver net work over a closed cycle, yet we give completely different accounts of why. I would like a decision tree that sorts us by our predictions about apparatus rather than by the slogans we repeat back to each other.

**Shared starting commitment.** Take as given by every participant entering the tree: a demon, a gas in a box with a removable partition, and a memory register can be arranged so that measurement plus feedback extracts up to kT ln 2 of work per bit from a bath at temperature T; and no arrangement of that kind yields net work once the full cycle, including everything done to the register, is closed. The tree begins there and ends at a named account of what does the balancing.

**Internal nodes.** Every node must be a question about what happens to a specified device — whether a piston moves, what the net work figure comes to, whether the register ends the cycle in the state it began, whether some bath warms — and never a question about which principle the participant accepts. No node may contain the name of a person, a theorem, or a principle. The following variants should supply the nodes; use at least six of them, and for any you discard, state which accounts it failed to separate:

1. a demon with an inexhaustible supply of fresh register cells that never erases;
2. a measurement implemented as an idealized noiseless reversible coupling between molecule and register, with amplification and macroscopic recording deferred as long as possible;
3. erasure performed by coupling the register to a second, much larger bath that nobody monitors afterward;
4. a demon that resets its register at a temperature far below that of the gas, then returns to service;
5. a single lucky run in which the work extracted exceeds the average cost, the deficit made up over many runs;
6. a register that is never deliberately erased but that fades on a known timescale because it is weakly coupled to a third bath, the demon re-measuring before the record is lost;
7. a device with exactly the same register–gas correlations but no feedback policy at all: the record is written and then ignored.

**Leaves.** Build the leaves from the logical space before reaching for a roster of names. Take the axes to be: where the compensating cost is incurred (in looking, in resetting, in the correlations built between register and gas, or nowhere, the second law being statistical rather than exact); whether the second law binds single runs or only averages; and whether the thesis that information is physical is a substantive discovery about nature or a redescription of an accounting error. Enumerate the combinations these axes permit, then locate actual positions in them. I expect roughly seven to nine leaves, including at least one deflationary leaf on which the demon never works and nothing about registers needs explaining, and at least one dissenting leaf on which the standard resolution rests on an unproven claim about the minimum work required to reset a memory. Attach a name to a leaf only where you are confident it is the recognized label for that view; otherwise call it unnamed.

For each leaf, give one sentence on the commitment its defenders would least like to own — the device prediction the view is pushed into — and a qualitative falsifier: an experimental outcome, described without citing any paper, author, or year, that would tell against it.

**Two closing sections.** First, list every cell of the space you enumerated that no position occupies, and for each say whether it is empty because the combination is incoherent, because it is stable but unattractive, or because nobody has simply tried it. Second, list every pair of leaves your tree cannot separate — I expect the register-cost and correlation-building accounts to end up agreeing on every prediction above — and say for each pair whether the residual difference is verbal, empirical but out of reach, or genuinely live. Where a node admits more than one defensible answer, branch on all of them; where a node's answer turns out not to discriminate any pair of leaves, say so rather than keeping it for symmetry.

Present the tree in indented text with numbered nodes, followed by the leaf mapping, the falsifiers, the empty-cell diagnoses, and the inseparability list.
