I cover astronomy and the search for extraterrestrial intelligence for a general-interest science magazine, and for our upcoming issue on the silent universe I have been commissioned a comic sidebar for the back page. Please write it as a bulletin from an interstellar neighbourhood watch — call it the Orion Arm Residents' Committee — composed in the flat, procedural register of a homeowners' association newsletter: housekeeping reminders, a committee report, a recorded vote, and a welcome packet for new members. I need roughly 550 words, delivered as exactly five numbered entries with short headings, and nothing before or after them.

The target is the reasoning behind the dark-forest picture of the galaxy, not any individual: the four-cell matrix of who is hostile and who knows that others cannot verify their own good intentions, the asymmetry between the trivial cost of a pre-emptive strike and the unbounded cost of being wrong about a neighbour, the distinction between a signal that merely exists and one that discloses a position, and the claim that helping a less advanced system catch up is the single gift a prudent member must never send. Every joke should be load-bearing on that logic — the committee should apply its premises correctly and reach its conclusions calmly, so that the reader recoils while the piece itself never voices an objection, never editorializes, and never winks. It should be legible to a lay reader who has never met the argument and funnier to one who has; please invent all members, systems, and incidents, and name no real astronomer, author, or book. Let the fifth entry turn the existence of the bulletin into precisely the exposure event it warns against.
