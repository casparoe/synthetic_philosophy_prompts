Before I work through the literature on Maxwell's demon, I want the specification rather than the proposals. Fix the purpose: the account must convince someone who already accepts statistical mechanics and wants the second law to remain a substantive empirical claim, not a stipulation about how we count states.

Write the desiderata any adequate resolution must meet. Which features of the setups does it have to reproduce — a demon sorting fast molecules at a trapdoor, and a one-molecule engine whose measure-and-expand cycle apparently converts heat from a single bath into work? Which distinctions may it not collapse: measurement against erasure, correlation against extractable work, information-theoretic against thermodynamic entropy, a lucky single run against the cycle average? Which formal constraints must it respect — the reduction in the minimum work cost of a process allowed by mutual information about the system, and the fluctuation relations governing feedback-controlled dynamics? And which bookkeeping questions must it answer outright: over what boundary is the entropy total taken, and why may the demon's memory be assumed to need resetting if the device is to operate as a repeating cycle? For each item, give the consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark it non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp.

Then tell me whether any pair is jointly unsatisfiable. I most want an assessment of whether a resolution can hold all three of: that erasure carries a minimum heat cost per bit, that this cost is what closes the demon's books, and that the second law is thereby vindicated non-circularly — against the standing objection that the erasure cost is itself derived using idealized processes whose legitimacy is what is in question. Close with a few sentences on how the live approaches fare: the erasure-based account, the epistemic dissolution, the fluctuation-theorem and resource-theoretic treatments, and the outright dissent. Name the single desideratum that does the most separating.
