I'm applying for a 12-week summer fellowship at an AI safety org and I need a proposal. My idea is half-formed but I think it's onto something. The standard line is that debate breaks down against obfuscated arguments — arguments a judge can't tell are unsound. But I think "obfuscated" is being put in the wrong slot: it's treated as a property of the argument, when really whether an argument is checkable depends on the judge and on how much checking effort the judge actually has. So obfuscation should be a three-place relation: argument, judge, budget.

And the consequence I keep coming back to is this: an argument can be obfuscated relative to a budget even when every individual claim in it is trivially easy to check, just because there are enough of them. A model doesn't have to say anything clever and undetectable. It just has to spread its errors across so many individually-cheap claims that the judge's budget runs out first. That turns the question from "can the judge detect the falsehood" into a resource-allocation problem, and I suspect it predicts that debate degrades gracefully on some tasks and falls off a cliff on others, depending on how many independently-checkable atoms a good argument in that domain has to contain.

Turn this into a real proposal. State the question so it has a determinant answer. Explain why the existing accounts of debate's limits — the ones about whether an honest model can win, about judges being too weak, about whether a model's stated reasoning is faithful — don't already settle it; I want the gap to be precise, not just "nobody has thought about this hard enough." Say what I'd actually do over the twelve weeks and why this conceptual route rather than the obvious alternative of just putting two models in a room and measuring win rates. Say what a useful deliverable looks like at the end. My hunch is that the payoff is a measure — call it claim density, or something better — that lets you predict in advance which tasks debate will hold up on, and I'd like that either spelled out properly or knocked down.

Be honest about what would count as the project failing. My main fear is that the whole account collapses into "the judge has to be smart enough" and buys nothing, so engage with that head on rather than hoping nobody asks.
