Consider the thesis that Leibniz's mature ethics survives intact if we delete his metaphysics. Concretely: a reconstructer keeps the three grades of justice — strict right, equity, piety — keeps justice defined as the benevolent love of the wise, keeps love analysed as taking delight in the happiness of another, and keeps harmony as maximal variety under maximal order, but drops monads, pre-established harmony, and the claim that this is the best of all possible worlds. Give me your credence that what remains is recognisably Leibniz's ethics rather than a shell, where the test for survival is that it yields the same substantive moral conclusions and the same motivational story, not merely the same vocabulary.

Decompose rather than assert. Assign a probability and one sentence of justification to each term you think does the work — among them, whether the third grade can be stated at all without a divine monarch presiding over a universal commonwealth of rational beings; whether his anti-voluntarist insistence that justice rests in reason rather than in the will of a superior requires God as the measure of the good; and whether the promised coincidence of virtue with the agent's own happiness and perfection survives once nothing guarantees a harmony of interests. Then say how those terms combine into your number, which single term the result is most sensitive to, and what argument or textual discovery would move it furthest.

Lead with the bottom line — your number — in the first sentence, and give the decomposition after it, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
