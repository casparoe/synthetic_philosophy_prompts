I'm putting together a 90 minute workshop for a mixed crowd — mostly first year grad students in social ontology, plus a handful from the evolutionary game theory group who've never read any philosophy — and I want to hand them a decision tree they can actually run on a real world regularity. Deliverable: a flowchart that starts at "here is a pattern of behaviour almost everyone in some group follows" and ends at a named position in the debate about conventions and norms. Lewis's account is the anchor — the one where a regularity is conventional because everyone would keep doing it given that everyone else does, they all know that, and some other regularity would have served about as well — but I don't want Lewis exegesis. I want it to sort actual cases, including the ones his account handles badly.

Every node should be a question you can answer by watching behaviour and asking people what they'd do, with no philosophy required. So things like: is there an alternative the whole group would switch to together if it could? would you still do this in an empty room, with nobody watching and nobody else around to coordinate with? do uninvolved bystanders punish people who break it — and are they *angry* at them, or just mildly inconvenienced? could a creature pick this up by trial and error without ever representing the alternative as an alternative? yes / no / not sure at each node, and tell me wherever the order of the nodes actually matters.

Cases it has to handle, and I will check the leaves against these: driving on the right; the QWERTY layout; the US never going metric; saying "bless you" after a sneeze; outrage at a line jumper coming from people nowhere near the front of the line; white moving first in chess; a coat on a chair holding a seat in a library; you-all / youse / you guys filling the hole English left when it lost the singular-plural you; and greeting gestures, where the French two kisses and the American handshake are each fine but you cannot mix them mid greeting.

Leaves should carry the standard name if one exists, otherwise a descriptive name you invent, plus one sentence on what the position is committed to. At least eight of them. I also want at least one cell of the space that nobody occupies — arbitrary alternatives plus intrinsic third party punishment plus no common knowledge anywhere in sight — and your diagnosis of whether that cell is empty because it's unstable, because it's incoherent, or because nobody has bothered looking. Finally, flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate. My guess is that'll bite for a regularity that evolved by pure reinforcement in a population of non reasoners versus one that philosophers rationalise in terms of common knowledge, and I'd rather you say so at the branch point than pretend the tree settles it.
