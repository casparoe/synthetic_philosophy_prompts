Been going in circles arguing with a friend about the two-envelope problem, so I finally wrote down where I actually stand. Before I defend this again I want to know whether the package holds together.

1. Before I open the envelope I'm exactly as likely to be holding the smaller amount as the larger. That comes straight from the setup — one of the two envelopes gets handed to me at random — so it doesn't lean on any assumption about how the amounts were picked.
2. Opening it and seeing some definite number A doesn't change that. I had no information beforehand, so the amount I see can't be evidence about whether it's the small one or the big one. Conditional on seeing A, its 50/50 that the other envelope holds 2A rather than A/2, and that's supposed to hold no matter what A turns out to be.
3. Credences have to be proper probability functions: normalized, countably additive. I'm not comfortable with improper priors, and I don't like declaring an infinite expectation "undefined" and then carrying on as if nothing happened.
4. There's no fact of the matter about the correct prior over the possible amounts — any prior is a matter of taste — so the puzzle can't be dissolved by picking a cleverer prior than your opponent did.
5. The switching argument, the one that says you gain a quarter of A, has to be blocked somewhere, and where I block it is at the step of taking expectations with unbounded payoffs. Expectation talk just doesn't apply when the sums dont converge.
6. As a general policy: when expected-value reasoning hands me a verdict I think is absurd, I treat that as a reason to give up expected-value maximization as the criterion, not as a reason to go hunting around for a flawed premise.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I don't want six complaints of equal weight — rank the genuine ones by severity, and for each say what has to be surrendered and what that costs me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. No need to point me at papers or readings, I just want the reasoning worked through.
