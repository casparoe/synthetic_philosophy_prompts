So here's a thing that's been eating at me for a week and I genuinely can't tell whether it's a real problem or a fake one I've talked myself into. Four of us are about to sign a lease on a place with four bedrooms that are wildly unequal: one has its own bathroom and a bay window, one is basically a hallway with a door, and that one faces the street where the bus idles at 6am. We're all agreed the rent should be split unequally and nobody wants to just haggle, so I proposed what felt to me like the obvious move — everyone privately writes down a number, 0 to 10, for "how much better would your life be in this room than in a mediocre room," then we assign rooms to maximize the sum and set the rents roughly in proportion to the numbers. It worked beautifully for about ten minutes. Then D said the thing that stuck: that this is fake precision, because my 7 and her 7 are not the same kind of object, there's no exchange rate between two people's inner scales, so adding them up is meaningless and whatever allocation pops out is just aesthetics with a spreadsheet attached. I wanted to tell her she was being pedantic and I couldn't actually come up with an answer, which annoyed me, because I work in global health and my whole job is ranking interventions by cost per QALY, which seems at least as bold as our little sheet. Also when my sister had a kidney stone the same week I had a nasty flu, I had zero difficulty knowing which of us was worse off.

The messier part is that our numbers came out exactly the way D's objection predicts they would. One housemate has been pretty depressed for a couple of years and gave every room a 2 or 3 — she said "nothing would make me much happier right now" — while E gives 9s to everything and has been openly thrilled about the whole process. Another one cares about natural light in a way that reads to me as expensive taste, and the fourth is by far the poorest of us, which means a dollar hurts him more, except our method never asked anyone about dollars, it asked about rooms. So the procedure I built has quietly shifted value toward the enthusiast and away from the person least able to register gains, and I can't work out whether that's a bug in my scheme or just a fact about the world I'm supposed to swallow. I also have a half-memory from years of reading in these parts that economics as a field formally gave up on this whole business in the 1930s and declared it unscientific, and then some Hungarian guy later proved a theorem that smuggled it back in through the back door of a veil-of-ignorance style argument — I'm confident I'm mangling that, but if so please tell me what the actual result does and doesn't buy you, because it feels relevant to whether D gets to win.

What I'm after is not "communicate openly" advice. I want to know whether D is right in principle, whether there's a version of this that isn't self-deception, and what we should actually do about the lease: go back to real-money bidding, split by square footage, keep the numbers but transform them somehow, or something else entirely that doesn't just collapse into "whoever is most expressive wins." And if the answer really is that we can't compare across people, why does it feel trivially easy with my sister's kidney stone — is my intuition there tracking something, or is it the same error wearing a disguise?
