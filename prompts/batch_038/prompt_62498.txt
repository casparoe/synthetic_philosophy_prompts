my mum died in april and since then i've been reading Douglas Hofstadter's book about his wife Carol — the bit where he says people are patterns, and that his own copy of her was still running inside his head, so she wasn't entirely gone, and that this is something more than just having memories of her. i've been trying to get the idea across to my brother, who thinks it's mystical rubbish dressed up in maths.

the analogy i keep reaching for is a folk song. the song isn't any particular performance — it gets handed from singer to singer, half of them sing it wrong, nobody owns it, and it's still going long after every fiddle that first played it has rotted. so mum was like the tune and me and my sisters are like people who learnt it by ear and each hold a slightly different version.

can you audit that for me? i want the correspondences laid out (tune, singers, the singing-it-wrong part, the fact nobody ever wrote it down), then where it actually breaks, and what it's quietly assuming people have that they don't. two things bother me specifically. one: a tune drifts as it gets passed on, and after a few generations it's a diffrent tune — so is the copy in my head still her, or just a cousin of her? two: the song picture makes mum into something public, with pieces scattered round the family and the neighbours, but what i actually want it to say is that her pattern in me isn't interchangeable with her pattern in someone who barely knew her. can the analogy deliver that, or does it cut right against it? and does my brother's "it's just memories" objection survive once you've been honest about the breaks? if the song's the wrong picture altogether, tell me what a better one would be.
