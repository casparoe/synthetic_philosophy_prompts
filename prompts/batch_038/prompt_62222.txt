In my school's ethics club we have been arguing over the choice between one person tortured for fifty years and a googol of people each getting a single dust speck in the eye. Our most senior member insists the whole case is just Pascal's mugging in costume: in both, an enormous number is supposed to overwhelm a harm we can vividly picture, and in both the mature response is to refuse to be bullied by the arithmetic, since neither our priors nor our sympathies scale that far. He uses this to defend choosing the specks. Please audit the analogy for me. I would like an explicit correspondence table — what in the mugging plays the role of the googol of specks, of the mugger's promise, of the tiny probability, of the five dollars, and of a demand that can be escalated without limit — then the disanalogies, sorted into those that actually bear on his conclusion and those that are merely decorative, and finally a verdict on whether choosing the specks survives once they are all on the table. If the analogy turns out to be doing bad work, tell me what comparison would capture the dust-speck case better.
