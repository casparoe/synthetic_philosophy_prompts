Here's my opening move, and I want you to take it seriously instead of swatting it. Suppose your side wins outright: bequests above a generous threshold are taxed at 100%, the revenue is recycled as a flat capital grant to everyone on their 25th birthday, and gift taxes are tightened so families can't just front-run the death. My worry is that parents don't respond to that by giving up on their kids — they respond by switching channels. Cash is the *most* taxable channel and arguably not the one doing the heavy lifting. The real transmission is reading to the kid at night, knowing which school to fight for, having a friend who'll take a phone call about an internship, and above all buying the education and enrichment that never shows up as a bequest at all, because it gets consumed years before anyone dies. So your policy equalizes starting capital while leaving the actual mechanism of advantage untouched — and it may make things worse, because it pushes families out of the one channel you can see and into the ones you can't. Its a visible fairness purchased at the price of an invisible, widening gap, and the grant ends up a rounding error next to a bedtime story. Why isn't that a reason to think the whole project has misidentified what an inheritance even is?
