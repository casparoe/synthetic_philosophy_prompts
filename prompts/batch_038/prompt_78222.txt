I am a postdoc in a philosophy-of-psychology group, and for as long as I have been here my supervisor has acted as the gatekeeper on a single question: which experimental findings count as perception being genuinely penetrated by cognition, and which are really attention, perceptual learning, or post-perceptual judgement wearing a disguise. Her settled verdicts on the standard cases are these.

Accepted as penetration:
- the saturation effect in which a heart-shaped figure makes a patch of physically identical colour look more saturated than an irregular figure, where participants match rather than describe;
- the cross-category colour discrimination advantage that vanishes when participants hold a concurrent verbal interference task;
- the tendency for greyscale fruit to be matched as slightly more saturated in its typical colour than it in fact is;
- effects of an explicitly taught, verbally reportable object category on discrimination thresholds.

Rejected:
- the mid-century finding that children from poorer backgrounds judge coins as larger, on the ground that nobody established what those children took themselves to be asked;
- the hill-steepness-and-backpack-weight line, as a change in how people use a rating scale rather than in what they see;
- the audiovisual speech illusion in which a dubbed syllable changes what is heard, because the penetrating factor there is another sensory signal, not anything cognitive;
- an effect in which an informative cue shifts apparent brightness, on the ground that this is attention doing what attention always does;
- a study reporting that a feared object is seen sooner, in which signal-detection analysis turned up a criterion shift and no change in sensitivity.

We now have a candidate result of our own. Over five days participants learn arbitrary names for two families of visually similar novel objects. On day six, in a pure appearance-matching task with no verbal report at any point, the learned names shift the matched colour of task-irrelevant instances of those objects; the shift shows up in sensitivity, not in criterion; it survives a concurrent spatial-attention load; and it is abolished by concurrent verbal interference. Predict her verdict. More importantly, set out the rules that would account for her record equally well — at least one keyed to what kind of state does the penetrating, at least one keyed to where in the processing stream the effect lands, and at least one keyed to how the effect was measured — and say what each of them implies here. Then give me your best single prediction with the reasons for preferring that rule over its rivals, and name the one further study that would separate the rules that currently agree with her record, explaining what pattern of results each rule expects from it. Finally, tell me whether the design should be changed so that the result is interpretable even on the harshest reading of her standards. Please write your answer in British English.
