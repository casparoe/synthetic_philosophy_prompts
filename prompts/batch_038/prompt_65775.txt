I'm building a taxonomy of closed epistemic environments and before I commit to it I want the spec, not the candidates. What does a theory of epistemic enclosure actually have to deliver to count as adequate? Give me the requirements list — the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions its not allowed to collapse (say, between exclusion by mere omission and exclusion by cultivated distrust of outside sources), the things we know about how belief actually spreads that it can't contradict — and behind each one, the motivating case or consideration. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just what some camp happens to want.

Two spots I want real pressure on. First, the baseline problem: every finite community leaves voices out, so a theory has to say what makes selective closure a defect rather than just a location — and if it can't appeal to the falsity of the enclosed beliefs without begging the question, tell me what else it can appeal to. Second, my draft adds a third kind alongside bubbles and echo chambers, call them damping chambers: members hear plenty of dissent, may even concede it, but nothing is load-bearing, so no belief ever propagates into action. Exposure doesn't pop them and counter-testimony doesn't dent them either. Does an adequate theory need a category like that, or is it a distinction with no explanatory payoff? And be blunt about wether some subset of your requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — if the list itself is a result, that's what I most want to see. Skip the scorecard against existing theories, a line each at most.
