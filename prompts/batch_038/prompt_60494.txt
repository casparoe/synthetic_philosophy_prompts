I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar session on updating under uncertain evidence, and I need a thought experiment built to a precise specification. The purpose of the case is to make a formal feature of Jeffrey conditionalization visible to students who have only just met it: successive updates on different partitions are known not to commute, and I want them to see that this is not a curiosity confined to the algebra but something an agent can in principle act upon. A case that merely announces the divergence, or that is rigged by stipulating numbers until the point comes out right, defeats the purpose; the divergence has to fall out of a scenario that is independently intelligible.

Background to state in the handout itself: the agent has a fixed prior over a hypothesis H and over each of two two-cell partitions, {A, ¬A} and {B, ¬B}. Ambiguous evidence bearing on the first moves her to P(A) = q, and she updates by Jeffrey conditionalization, P′(H) = q·P(H|A) + (1−q)·P(H|¬A), with the conditionals held fixed; likewise for the second partition.

The scenario. A single realistic professional setting — a clinician, an analyst, an editor, an assessor, whatever fits — in which the agent controls the order in which she obtains two pieces of evidence, and each piece, once obtained, yields a genuinely inconclusive result rather than a determination. The final credence in H must differ between the two orders by enough to flip a decision she actually faces. And she must be able to foresee this in advance: before taking in either piece of evidence, she knows from prior experience with both roughly how each will move her, well enough to predict which order leaves her with the more comfortable posterior and to choose accordingly.

Constraints.
1. The inconclusiveness must not be curable by trying harder. If the agent could resolve either piece of evidence into certainty at reasonable cost, the case degenerates into a lesson about gathering better evidence.
2. No reinterpretation mid-case: the agent may not change her mind about what either piece of evidence means, and her conditional probabilities may not shift for any reason other than the updates themselves.
3. Give the arithmetic for both orders with numbers a student can follow by hand in two or three minutes.
4. Tell me whether the exploit can be built with both updates satisfying the rigidity condition, or whether it requires rigidity to fail somewhere. If it requires failure, say so plainly, and build the case so that the agent could not have detected the failure beforehand.
5. Confront the obvious objection to the foresight requirement: an agent who can predict her own future shifts seems either to already possess the evidence, in which case there is nothing to update on, or to run against whatever constraint reflection-style considerations place on predictions about one's own future credences. Either design around this or report that it cannot be designed around.

Analysis to follow the scenario.
(a) Both orders worked through, with the resulting credences and the decision each supports.
(b) An identification of the structural feature doing the work — what must hold of the relationship between the two partitions, and of H's conditional probabilities, for order to matter — together with a check that removing that feature collapses the case into order-independence.
(c) A verdict on what the case shows: whether an agent's being able to select an evidence order for its downstream effect indicts Jeffrey conditionalization as a norm of rationality, or shows only that the norm is incomplete and needs a supplementary requirement — for instance one barring order-sensitive sequences, or one directing the agent to update in a single step on the partition generated by the two experiences together. If the second, say what that requirement prescribes for an agent who genuinely cannot entertain the joint partition.
(d) The strongest reply available to a defender of the standard view — most plausibly, that the two experiences are one piece of evidence taken in two instalments, so that no genuine second update occurs — and an assessment of whether that reply is available in this particular case.

I will supply the readings myself, so refer to positions and results descriptively rather than by citation. Format: the scenario in two or three paragraphs, the calculations set out cleanly, and the discussion sections brief enough to fit on a two-page handout.
