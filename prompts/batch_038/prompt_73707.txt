So this blew up in my raid groups discord last week and I sat through the whole thing and still can't tell who won.

Background, since none of it works without the facts: our guild had a shared stash — a "guild bank" — where members deposited rare gear and crafting materials on the understanding that anyone who put something in could take it back out later. One of the officers, call him M, had permissions to move items between the stash and his own characters. He used them to empty the entire stash onto an alt, quit the guild that same night, and sold the lot through a third-party site for something like 900 euros. Our raid leader K had maybe 400 hours of farming sitting in there. We reported it and the company's reply was that the transfer was permitted by the game's own systems so they wouldn't act, even though selling items for real money does break the terms of service.

Then the argument. (1) Dan said it's theft, full stop — M took stuff that wasn't his, K really lost it, and real money moved; that it happened inside a game is irrelevant, like saying a wire transfer isn't real because it's just numbers on a screen. (2) Priya said that's a category error: K never owned anything, he held a revocable permission under a contract with the company, and the company could delete the whole stash tomorrow without owing anybody anything, so there was no property there to steal. (3) Dan: fine, then the 900 euros isn't real money either, it's just numbers in the company's ledger — but obviously it is, and it went through actual banks. (4) Priya conceded the euros are real but said that makes it a terms-of-service violation and a market problem: M wronged the company and the player economy, not K, whose "loss" is only the loss of a permission he was never entitled to. (5) Dan said that's absurd, because 400 hours is 400 hours — time is the one resource you genuinely spend, so at minimum that was taken from K. (6) Priya said effort doesn't create ownership (people sink years into bad relationships and nobody robbed them), and in any case those hours only count as a loss if the stash was worth something, which is the question at issue.

Dan also threw in what he called the grief test: if K's reaction would have been indistinguisable from his reaction to someone clearing out a storage unit full of bike parts, that shows the stash was the same kind of thing. Priya said that begs the question, since grief tracks what you believe you had, not what you had.

Rate each of the six moves — clearly mistaken, partly right, clearly right — with a line on each, and say where the grief test actually lands. Then tell me what both of them are missing: is this ultimately a fight about whether virtual objects are real, or a fight about which legal and moral categories to apply to them, and could those come apart, so that one of them is right about the metaphysics and still loses the argument?
