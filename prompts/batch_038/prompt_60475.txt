I'm a backend engineer with zero philosophy background, so bear with me on this one.

For about three years I've been the volunteer archivist for a ~20-year-old open source project — mailing lists, issue trackers, git metadata, all of it. We're putting together an official history page for the anniversary, and there's a fight about the fork.

The story everyone who was around tells, told in almost identical words by different people: the original maintainers sold out, the fork happened because of that betrayal, and the people who left were the ones who kept the project's values alive. But the archived threads from the time look... different. The folks now cast as the villains were pushing for something close to what the community says it always wanted, and the actual break seems to have happened months earlier, over something pretty mundane, and then got a moral narrative retrofitted onto it afterwards.

I raised this on a call and one of the old-timers — very much on the winning side of the current governance — shut it down with "history is written by the victors." Which struck me as a strange thing for him to say, since he's the victor, and the archive isn't something he wrote, its just what got logged whether anyone wanted it logged or not.

So my question is about the sentence itself. What are the distinct precise claims that could be hiding in "history is written by the victors"? I can vaguely see a few — something like "the documents that survive are the ones the winners kept" vs. "the winners get to decide what counts as having happened" vs. something much stronger like "there's no fact of the matter apart from the telling" — but I can't tell which are true, which are just uninteresting, and which one he was actually deploying.

Take them one at a time and give me a verdict on each: platitude, live and defensible, or false, and why. Then tell me which of them, if any, would support overriding a timestamped contemporaneous record with a community's remembered story — and say whether it matters that some of that record is testimony people wrote to persuade (list posts, written for an audience) versus material nobody composed as a narrative at all (commit dates, revert chains, who had merge rights when). Also: does the slogan cut the other way in my situation, i.e. does it predict the lore is the victors' history rather than the archive being so?

Please don't just hand me a menu and make me choose — commit to a position. Tell me which reading you think is right, what it implies for what goes on the history page, and where you're reasoning this out yourself versus reporting something historians have actually settled. I'd also like your honest read on how much confidence the timestamped stuff deserves, since I'm the one who has to write the thing and get yelled at.
