so — 51, took the buyout after the heart scare, and I'm facing maybe thirty years of afternoons with nothing in them, which has forced me to actually write down what I believe instead of gesturing at it. I've been reading Schlick on the meaning of life and think I've mostly agreed with him, but I want a consistency check on the package before I make decisions on it. Here's the package:

(1) a purpose isn't a meaning. every purpose is a means to some further purpose, and if life's meaning were a purpose you'd be finished with it the moment you reached it — so I reject the demand that my life be justified by an outcome.
(2) meaning sits in activity enjoyed for its own sake, i.e. play. my test for whether something counts as play — call it the sealed room test — is whether I'd keep doing it knowing that nobody would ever see it, use it, or be affected by it.
(3) the fullest version of that is what Schlick ties to youth. I don't mean literally young, but I do think the capacity mostly goes by forty and mostly doesn't come back.
(4) all the same, a life that leaves no trace is a lesser life. I want to finish the book. I'd be embarrased by thirty years of agreeable afternoons that produced nothing.
(5) the transience thing doesn't move me: that everything gets forgotten eventually is no reason to think nothing matters now. how long people remember an episode has nothing to do with how much it was worth.
(6) and a verdict. my friend gave up the cello — she was very good — for a teaching job and a calmer life, and she's happier than she was. I still think she made her life less meaningful. so enjoyment can't be sufficient.

my nagging suspicion is that (5) and (4) don't fit, but I might be chasing a non-problem. rank the tensions by severity, say for each what would have to go and what the cheapest repair costs, mark the pairs that only look like they clash, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. don't summarize Schlick back at me.
