I sit on the board of a small nonprofit that sponsors refugee families — we cover rent and fund a caseworker for a family's first year after they arrive. We can afford maybe a dozen next year, and at the last meeting one member derailed the whole discussion with an argument I still can't answer. Pick any number, he said: twelve, fifteen, forty. The difference between the twelfth and thirteenth family is morally nothing — one more kid gets a bed instead of staying in a camp. So every possible number has an arbitrary cutoff, and an arbitrary cutoff can't be the right one. Since "whoever shows up" isn't an option on our budget, the only non-arbitrary number is zero: we should quit sponsoring and give the money to advocacy instead. Two people nodded. Then ten minutes later the same board approved a points system for ranking *which* twelve families we'd take, weighting medical need, and nobody batted an eye. I want to say his argument is bad and also that the ranking decision doesn't contradict it, and I can't hold both of those at once. Is the arguement any good? And does the fact that we can defend a ranking of the families tell us anything about whether the total number can be defended too?
