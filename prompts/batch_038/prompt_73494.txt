You are a philosopher of cognitive science with substantial experience serving on comparative-cognition methods panels. Think carefully before writing, and spend a few paragraphs brainstorming candidate reconstructions of the implicit theory before settling on one.

Consider the following widely accepted procedure. A laboratory first habituates an animal to a mirror until novelty responses extinguish. It then checks whether the animal uses the mirror to explore normally invisible parts of its own body, and whether it moves to see how the reflection moves when it does. Only after that baseline is established does the experimenter anesthetize the animal, apply an odorless, non-tactile dye mark to a site it cannot see without the mirror — a brow ridge, an ear — plus a control mark of the same substance in a location the animal can feel but not see, or a visually salient but odorless control. On waking, the animal is scored for mark-directed touching, inspection, or investigation mediated by the mirror. Passing is treated by the literature, and by the panels that fund and gate-keep it, as evidence of self-recognition, and hence of a self, consciousness, or a theory of mind; failing is treated as evidence of the absence of those things.

What theory of mind does this battery presuppose? Reconstruct it explicitly rather than naming a school, and separate what the procedure strictly requires from what it merely happens to fit well with — in particular, is it committed to the layered design taxonomy that divides creatures into Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian kinds, or could an embodied or sensorimotor account of selfhood license the very same staging? Identify at least one rival theory of mind that the procedure quietly rules out before any data are collected, and explain how it does so. Then confront an apparent inconsistency in the practice: it certifies an animal's escape from mere conditioning by means of a graded series of conditioning checks, and it treats a threshold crossing as a discrete verdict about a continuous capacity. Say what the field must say to resolve that tension, or concede it.

Finally, suppose we replaced the presupposed theory with one on which there is no threshold and selfhood consists in an organism's ongoing self-specification through its own activity. What would have to change about this procedure — its staging, its scoring, its choice of species — and what would happen to the current roster of passing animals?
