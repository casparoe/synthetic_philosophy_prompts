Background as I understand it: the interventionist analyzes "X causes Y" as there being an ideal intervention on X that changes the value of Y, with all the other variables in your set held fixed at their actual values. And then there's this result that keeps getting gestured at in seminars that I've never managed to get a clean statement of, roughly: if your variable set is rich enough and there's no latent common cause messing things up, then whenever X is a cause of Y in the graph-theoretic sense, Y counterfactually depends on X under an ideal intervention on X. So the two analyses can't actually come apart.

Which is why I went into quals last month assuming the interventionist/counterfactual fight is basically over — the interventionists didn't lose so much as get absorbed — and that what's left is bookkeeping about how fine-grained your variables should be.
