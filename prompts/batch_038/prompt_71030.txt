The idea. Nyāya defends testimony as a knowledge-source in its own right, and it also holds that a cognition's veridicality is not self-certifying: you find out a cognition was true by way of a seperate cognition, paradigmatically by acting on it and the action succeeding. Mīmāṃsā denies this — validity is both produced and grasped intrinsically, and it's invalidity that comes from outside. So far this is just the textbook svataḥ vs parataḥ prāmāṇya debate. My claim is that the textbook framing hides what's actually interesting, which is that Nyāya has a bootstrapping problem of exactly the kind contemporary epistemologists press against reliabilism.

Two worries I want the proposal built around:

1. If successful activity is the mark of truth, what makes that relation a tracking relation rather than a mere correlation? Nyāya wants a realist notion of truth plus a pragmatic criterion, but I can't see a principle reason the criterion should be truth-conducive that doesn't quietly re-assume the reliability of the causal complex (the guṇas, the excellences in the cause) that it was supposed to be testing.

2. Sharper for testimony: for content you can't act on — scriptural claims about dharma, karma, liberation, God — the test is simply unavailable. So everything rides on the speaker being trustworthy, and trustworthiness is itself established largely by further testimony plus observation of the speaker's conduct, which we only interpret against a testimonial background. So Nyāya looks like it either bootstraps or defers forever.

The question I want stated precisely: what structural condition does a validation procedure have to meet for the bootstrapping objection to get a grip on it, and does Nyāya's parataḥ account satisfy or violate that condition in the domain where pragmatic testing is unavailable?

Please give me: the question stated so it has a determinate answer; why the existing positions (both externalist and internalist readings of Nyāya, plus the standard Kumārila side of the debate) leave it open rather than settling it; the method — I'm thinking an explicit reconstruction of the validation procedure as a defeater-sensitivity condition, checked against how reliabilists respond to bootstrapping — and an argument for why that rather than the obvious alternatives, i.e. pure philology on one side, or just declaring Nyāya a process reliabilist and importing the modern debate wholesale on the other; a month-by-month plan for nine months; what a positive result would look like and what would count as failure; and a candid scope note, including how much Sanskrit reading is realistically required and what I should cut if they only fund six months.

One thing: take seriously the possibility that the honest failure mode is that bootstrapping never bites here, because Nyāya never offers the pragmatic test as a justification at all but only as a diagnostic. If that's where the project lands, say so in the proposal rather than papering over it.
