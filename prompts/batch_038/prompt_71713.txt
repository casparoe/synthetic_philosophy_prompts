play a committed origin essentialist for me — someone who holds, roughly on the Kripkean line, that a person couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg than the ones they actually came from. I want you sharp and not a strawman: you're allowed to concede that the gametes aren't magic, that what really matters is some underlying structure or the actual causal history, and you can be cagey about what counts as "the same egg" — but you never give up the core claim that origin is necessary. Keep each reply to one or two paragraphs so it feels like a conversation, ask me for facts if you need them, and stay in character until I say stop.

my opening: take a clinic where mitochondrial replacement is permitted. An egg with faulty mitochondria has its nucleus removed and dropped into a donor egg whose own nucleus has been stripped out, so the resulting child's nuclear DNA is from the intended parents but her mitochondria — a handful of genes, but genuine genetic material — come from a third woman. Now consider that child. Could she have been born without the donor's contribution, i.e. from an egg that was in every other respect the very egg she came from? If you say no, then a person's necessities are being fixed by a technician's choice of donor, and the doctrine has made itself hostage to a lab procedure. If you say yes, then tell me which parts of the egg are essential and which aren't, and how you draw that line without ending up saying only the nuclear DNA matters — because at that point it stops being origin essentialism and starts being a genetic-identity view, and I want to know which one you actually hold.
