I'm presenting a paper next month at a workshop on algorithmic decision-making. The room is mostly ML and fairness researchers who think about multi-objective model selection in terms of Pareto frontiers, hand-set scalarization weights, and knee-point heuristics; two economists who know cooperative game theory properly; and the policy lead from the agency that would deploy the system. The case is choosing which triage model to deploy when the frontier trades overall predictive accuracy against a disparity measure in false-positive rates between two groups. My thesis is that this should stop being treated as a scalarization problem and be treated as a two-party bargaining problem — the currently deployed model as the disagreement point, each objective's best attainable value as its utopia coordinate — with the Kalai–Smorodinsky point selected for deployment, on three grounds: it is the point at which both sides gain in equal proportion over the disagreement point; it is invariant to affine rescaling of each metric, so no arbitrary weights are needed; and it avoids the independence-of-irrelevant-alternatives axiom that the Nash product requires.
