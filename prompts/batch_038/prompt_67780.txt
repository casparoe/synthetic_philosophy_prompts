I am a novelist, and a university philosophy department has offered me a six-month research residency on condition that I submit a proposal for the intellectual work I intend to do before drafting. The novel imagines a jurisdiction that permits a consenting person to serve another's sentence, and its plot is built on David Lewis's argument that penal substitution is incoherent: a fine, a debt, or a compensatory burden can be shifted onto a substitute, but punishment cannot, because punishment is owed by the offender rather than merely suffered. My half-formed idea is that both that argument and the theological replies to it treat punishment as one thing, when it may be three separable things — who may be made to suffer, who may be censured, and whose debt is discharged — and that pulling these apart would show which substitutions are genuinely incoherent and which are only distasteful.

Please turn this into a proposal: the research question stated precisely enough to have a determinate answer; why the existing positions on both sides leave it open; the method, month by month, and why that method rather than a survey of atonement theory or of comparative sentencing law; what a result would look like, and what would count as the project failing; and an honest assessment of what might make it too large for six months. Open with the research question in a single sentence, then the proposal proper. I will supply citations myself, so include no bibliography. Nine hundred words maximum.
