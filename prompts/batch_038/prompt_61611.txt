The view I've landed on, stated as bluntly as I can: process supervision isn't a distinct species of supervision from outcome supervision. The headline result in this area is that you can auto-label a reasoning step by rolling out a bunch of completions from it and checking what fraction reach a correct final answer — and the thing you get from that is just a value-function estimate, a discounted outcome signal. So the "process" reward isn't new information entering the system; it's outcome information reorganized for computational convenience, a credit-assignment / varience-reduction device. On that reading, the stronger claims people make — that process superivision supervises *how* a model thinks rather than what it produces, that it's intrinsically more robust to reward hacking, that it lets you align a model to tasks where you can't check the answers — are overclaimed, because the entire label supply is still downstream of an outcome check.
