We've just covered the accuracy-dominance argument for Probabilism in my formal epistemology seminar: any credence function that violates the probability axioms is such that some probability function beats it in Brier accuracy at every possible world. I take the upshot to be that an incoherent agent is simply guaranteed to be less accurate than a coherent one, whatever the world turns out to be like. That leaves me with a practical question for the problem set. Working through a case about the three possible outcomes of a committee vote, I ended up assigning 0.01, 0.99, and 0.01 — sloppily, since they add to 1.01. Dividing each by the total seems like the natural repair, and I assumed the rescaled triple is precisely the coherent function the theorem guarantees, more accurate than my original at every one of the three worlds. Is that assumption correct? And more generally, when I catch myself in violation of the axioms, is rescaling by the sum just what the theorem is telling me to do?
