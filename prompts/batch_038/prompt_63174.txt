My roommate and his brother argued for like two hours on Saturday about that old thought experiment where you have to pick between one person being tortured for 50 years and a dust speck in the eye of some gigantic number of different people. I'd never thought about it before and I couldnt stop listening. Roughly how it went:

(1) The brother said obviously the specks win if there's enough of them — each speck is a real bad thing, so if you pile up enough of them you eventually pass the torture, and anybody who denies that is just squeamish about arithmetic.

(2) My roommate said the arithmetic is fake, because nobody ever experiences the total. Each of those people feels one speck and goes on with their day. The 50 years is felt by one person continuously. So you're comparing a number that only exists on paper with an experience that actually happens somewhere.

(3) The brother said okay, but then on your rule it's exactly as bad to give a trillion people a speck as to give one person a speck, and you've lost the ability to object to anything that happens to lots of people a little bit at a time. Also, you clearly do compare inside a single life — you'd take the speck over an hour in the dentist's chair — so you already accept that small bads and big bads sit on one scale. What changes when you cross over to somebody else's life? Nothing you can state.

(4) My roommate said what changes is that maybe it isn't one scale. Bads might come in tiers, and no amount of speck-tier stuff ever adds up into torture-tier stuff, the same way no amount of money adds up to being loved. He said his brother just assumes one long ruler and then claims victory from the assumption.

(5) The brother said the tiers view is unlivable, because it needs a sharp cutoff somewhere between an annoyance and an atrocity, and there isn't one — one extra speck can't possibly turn mild irritation into torture, so keep adding specks and you either accept a magic line you can't point to, or you accept that the sum gets there.

(6) My roommate said that's not a problem, you don't need a line to know which side of a comparison you're on, and demanding one is the mistake. Then he said he was done arguing.
