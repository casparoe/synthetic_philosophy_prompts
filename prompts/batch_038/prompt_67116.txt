Been going back and forth on the "what makes every a an a" question, and I want to know what my own gut verdicts add up to. Here they are.

- A Times 'a' (double-storey, with the little hook) and a geometric sans 'a' (basically an o with a tail): pretty different shapes, both obviously a's.
- A display face where the 'a' is squashed, the counter filled in, the stroke weight all over the place: still an 'a', though I have to squint.
- My grandmother's handwriting near the end, shaky, the bowl never quite closes: still an 'a', no doubt about it.
- A Cyrillic 'а' typed on a Russian keyboard and rendered in the exact same font, pixel-identical to the Latin one: I want to say that is NOT the letter 'a'. Different letter wearing its face.
- An ambigram that reads as both 'a' and '4': I'd say it genuinely is an 'a' (and genuinely a 4). But the exact same outline showing up in a crack in the pavement isn't an 'a' at all.
- A smudged scan where nobody, human or OCR, can tell wether it's an 'a' or an 'o', but the typeset original was definitely an 'a': I say it's an 'a', badly reproduced. Yet a hand-lettered mark by a scribe that every reader since has taken for an 'o', with decent evidence he started out writing an 'a': I say that one is an 'o'. Same setup — shape uninformative, original intention known or guessable — opposite verdicts, and I can't reconcile them.

My rough stab at a principle: it's an 'a' if a competent reader of the relevant tradition would take it for one. But that wrecks the Cyrillic case (any competent reader would) and probably the smudge too.

So what principle actually fits these, or fits most of them? State it precisely, clause by clause, and say which case each clause is there to handle — including whether you need separate clauses for identity and for legibility. Then tell me what it delivers for a forger who traces someone else's 'a' perfectly: same outline, no first-hand intention behind it. And be straight about which of my verdicts it has to throw out, and why that one rather than another.

Say how confident you are in what you land on, and single out the part you're least sure of. Under 400 words.
