I'm no philosopher, just someone who's argued about this with friends for years. Where I've landed: a government doesn't need elections to be legitimate. If it actually delivers — people get richer, the streets are safe, the trains run, kids outlive their parents — then it's earned the right to rule. And consent is a fiction anyway, nobody ever signed anything, we all just got born somewhere.

But I've added a condition that I think people making this argument usually skip: the rulers have to genuinely be on the hook. If delivery stops, they have to actually lose power, not just re-define what counts as delivery. Otherwise the whole thing is circular.

So what would count against this? Give me the arguments, the real cases, and the stuff I'm probably not seeing, sorted by how much they'd force me to give up versus how much I could just shrug off. Also tell me what my view is commited to that's embarassing — the places where "they're doing a good job, so they get to stay" gives a verdict I'd hate. And be straight with me about whether my on-the-hook condition rescues the idea or just makes it so that no government anywhere could ever meet it.
