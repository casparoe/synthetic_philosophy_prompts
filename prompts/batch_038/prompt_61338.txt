I am a junior, and I have debated policy and public forum for three years, so the vocabulary I actually think in is links and internal links, uniqueness, impacts and impact calculus, turns, solvency, framework, and the kritik as the residual slot for arguments that refuse to be reduced to consequences. My school board is considering a requirement that every freshman attend six live performances each year and write reflective essays on them, sold as character education, and my coach has assigned me the opposition case for a public forum night before the vote. I have read Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert on the theater once, in translation, and I have no background in eighteenth-century philosophy, so I would like you to do the conceptual work rather than assume I can supply it.

Please restate Rousseau's case against the theater in my vocabulary, argument by argument, as faithfully as you can. I mean specifically his claim that plays cannot reform morals but only intensify the ones a city already has; his account of tragic pity as an emotion that leaves the spectator feeling generous at no cost and pleased with his own sensitivity; his reading of Molière's Misanthrope as a comedy that makes honesty ridiculous and rewards the man who knows how to get along; his objection to the actors' trade as a form of selling one's person; his worry about what a taste for spectacle does to a small, hardworking, mutually surveilling community; and his alternative of open-air public gatherings in which the spectators are themselves the performers. For each of these, tell me whether it can be run as a link with a genuine impact, as a turn, as a framework argument, as a kritik, or as nothing at all, and whether I would be foolish to run it in front of a judge who does not already share Rousseau's premises.

Write the whole thing as continuous prose, the way you would write a letter or a long email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
