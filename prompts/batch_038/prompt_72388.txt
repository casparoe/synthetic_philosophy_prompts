Been going down a rabbit hole on Dutch books and I can't tell what norm I actually endorse, because my gut gives different answers in different cases. Here they are:

1. A bookie puts three bets on the table at once, each one looks fine to me separately, and together they cost me $200 no matter how things turn out. Obviously refuse, and I think my odds were genuinely incoherent.

2. The same three bets offered one at a time over three days, and I'd take each one as it comes up. Somehow this feels much less bad, even though I lose the same $200 with certainty. Though if I were asked today to sign a contract binding me to all three, I'd refuse to sign — which makes me wonder whose odds are the problem.

3. A bet, settled tomorrow, that pays $1000 if I believe then that it rained today. Offered at 5:1 against. I refuse, and I think it's cheating: the bookie isn't betting on the weather, he's betting on my future opinions.

4. Same setup but it pays on whether I'll have eaten lunch by 2pm, and I know perfectly well I skip lunch when I'm busy. Here I'd take the bet and I don't think there's anything cheating about it.

5. Even-odds coin flip for $10, offered every morning forever. Take it every time, no question.

6. Same coin flip, but $100,000 against my entire savings. Refuse, and I don't think that makes me incoherent, my utility for money just isn't linear.

7. Someone offers me 1000:1 against a hairy arithmetic statement I can't check by hand. I refuse, but I don't feel incoherent, just finite.

Is there a single principle — something in the neighborhood of "your betting dispositions must not guarantee you a loss" — that delivers all seven? I'm pretty sure 3 vs 4 is the bad pair, and maybe 1 vs 2 too, and that whatever clause rescues 3 seems to rescue a lot more than I want it to. So: state the principle clause by clause, saying which case forces each clause; tell me what it says about a case I didn't list, like a bet that pays on what I'll believe after someone has paid me to change my belief; and be straight with me about which of my verdicts it simply can't keep.
