Our metaphysics reading group is doing a session on origin essentialism — could you have come from a different sperm and egg, and if not, how much slack is there — and I want to hand out a "which view do you actually hold?" quiz beforehand so people commit to answers before they see the labels. Everyone's read Kripke's argument, so no question should just ask "is your origin essential to you?"

Give me nine questions, each a concrete scenario with two or three answer options. Scenarios should include: your mother's egg with a different sperm; that sperm with a different egg; both swapped; the same two gametes but conception delayed five years; the same zygote gestated by someone else; a lab-built embryo from entirely different gametes but with exactly your genome; you split into twins at the two-cell stage; a clone grown from one of your skin cells; and one you pick that you think does the most diagnostic work. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto strict gamete-pair essentialism, zygote essentialism, the asymmetric view (one gamete fixed, the other not), the graded view where small departures from your actual origin are possible but large ones aren't, and flat denial that identity tracks origin at all.

Also flag any pair of positions the quiz can't actually seperate — my hunch is zygote and gamete-pair essentialism agree everywhere except the lab-embryo case — and for each leaf give one commitment its own defenders find hardest to live with. Finally, name one combination of answers that's logically available but that no view occupies, and say whether that's because it's incoherent or just unclaimed territory. No reading list, no citations, just the instrument.
