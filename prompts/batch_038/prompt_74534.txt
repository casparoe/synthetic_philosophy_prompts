One guy on it, who's clearly been reading the honor/dignity/victimhood-culture sociology, says no: dignity cultures supposedly teach you to shrug insults off, victimhood cultures treat them as harms requiring third-party intervention, and adopting the policy would be importing the second one. His clincher was that this is literally what we were all raised on, and he quoted "Sticks and stones may break my bones but words will never hurt me" as if that settles what dignity culture is commited to. I'm pretty sure the rhyme can't bear that weight but I can't say why cleanly.

Then tell me which reading(s) his argument actually needs to get from "dignity culture teaches this rhyme" to "so a policy on insults is a cultural shift," and whether that reading is one the rhyme plausibly carries, given that in honor cultures reputation is exactly the thing an insult destroys.
