I'm working on a seminar paper in a history-of-science course, and my argument depends on how modular Kepler's astrology really is. I have been treating it as three separable layers: (a) the harmonic theory of aspects — that the operative configurations are simply those divisions of the circle that the soul can construct geometrically, which is why Kepler adds the quintile and biquintile to the traditional aspects while stripping away everything in the received art with no geometric warrant (the unequal house divisions, the fixed stars, the Arab lots and mansions); (b) the account of how any of this could work — no physical influx at all, but the Earth's formative faculty and each individual soul at birth perceiving and retaining the celestial configuration much as one recognizes a tune; and (c) the practice — the nativities, the annual prognostications he was paid to cast, the meteorological and medical predictions.

Please rank these three layers by how much of each can be lifted out of the astrology and still bear weight in his astronomy, assigning each a score from 0 (nothing survives the transfer) to 1 (it does essentially the same work there), and justify each score in a single sentence. Present the result as a three-row ranked list — layer, score, justification — and close with a one-sentence verdict on whether the ranking supports my supervisor's position that the harmonic machinery is fully detachable from astrology, or my own suspicion that layer (b) is what makes the harmony theory astronomical rather than merely musical. Hard limit: 300 words for the entire response.
