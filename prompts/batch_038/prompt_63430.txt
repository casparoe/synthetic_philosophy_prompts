so I've finally written down where I actually stand on uploading / gradual replacement, for a personal identity reading group, and I want a consistency check before I present it. I have a feeling some of these dont sit well together but I can't tell which, or which ones I'd have to drop.

1. I'm a physicalist about minds. Nothing magic about carbon — a chip that does exactly what a neuron does is as good as the neuron.
2. Gradual replacement — swapping my neurons for synthetic equivalents one at a time over, say, a year — leaves me alive and the same person at the end.
3. Instantaneous replacement doesn't. Same final architecture, same functional profile, but scanned and rebuilt in one operation: that person is a copy. They don't inherit my promises, my debts, or my prudential concern, and I'd be right to be terrified of the procedure.
4. There's no sharp cutoff between 2 and 3, and I'm fine with survival coming in degrees — a 40%-at-once rebuild leaves me partly surviving.
5. Whether I survive as some given future person depends only on what's intrinsic to me, that person, and the relation between us. What else happens elsewhere in the universe can't change the answer.
6. But if the lab quietly keeps my original brain running in a tank while the synthetic version walks out the door, the walker isn't me. I accept that the existence of the second one changes the verdict.
7. I'm not squeamish about fission as such — in an ordinary fission case I think each successor has everything that matters, and the loss is just that there are two of them.
8. Identity itself is all-or-nothing. I don't want degrees of identity and I don't want to touch fuzzy logic.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Please answer in these sections, in this order:

- **Looks bad, isn't** (~150 words): the pairs that seem inconsistent but dissolve, and the exact distinction that dissolves each.
- **The real problems, ranked** (~600 words): each genuine tension ordered by severity — what it is, why the usual motivations for both sides can't both be honored, which single commitment has to go, what the replacement view would be, and what I'd lose by making that swap.
- **The one move that fixes the most** (~150 words): the single revision with the best coverage-to-cost ratio across everything above.
- **Leftover** (~100 words): what I still won't be able to explain even after making the fixes.

Don't flag everything as in tension — if some of these are compatible, say so and say what's doing the work.
