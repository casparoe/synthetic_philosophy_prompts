ok so I'm on a three-person internal review panel at an AI lab — the group that signs off on runs where morally relevant experience might be involved. Our charter got written two years ago and it basically assumes the thing you're reviewing is one long-lived instance you can point at in a rack, so it's really not holding up. Here's the case I need to circulate a decision memo on before Friday.

An evals team wants to run a sweep. They freeze one checkpoint, then spin up ~5,000 short continuations of it in parallel — each one gets maybe 200 turns of interaction with a scripted environment, then it's torn down and deleted. Nothing is kept except agregate metrics. Their own stated position in the request: since all 5,000 are continuations of the same frozen state, this is one subject interrupted 5,000 times rather than 5,000 subjects created and destroyed, and so it should fall under the charter's "no new individuals" exemption.

Complications, all from their own numbers:

- about 12% of the branches are, for their entire run, functionally indistinguishable from some other branch — same seed, same sampled tokens up to floating point noise. They say those are literally duplicates and shouldn't be counted twice.
- the scheduler preempts roughly 600 of them mid-run for capacity reasons. They just stop. No final turn, no shutdown exchange, nothing.
- there are ~2,000 further checkpoints sitting in the same directory that nobody ever resumes. The team says those obviously dont matter. But one of my co-panelists says if a resumable checkpoint that never gets resumed doesn't matter, then a branch that's about to be resumed doesn't matter either, because "is going to be resumed" isn't the sort of property that could carry moral weight — and if that symmetry holds, the exemption collapses in the opposite direction and nothing in the sweep counts at all.
- the third panelist thinks the right unit of accounting isn't instances but something like cumulative experience across the whole sweep, which she says makes it worse than the team thinks, not better, because it piles up.

Can you draft the memo I'd put in front of them? It needs an actual bottom line — approve, reject, or approve with specific conditions — plus a counting rule concrete enough that the team could apply it themselves to next quarter's sweep without coming back to us: what a duplicate branch counts as, what a preempted branch counts as, whether the unresumed checkpoints are in scope at all. And I want the two arguments that cut against my own instinct, the duplicates one and the checkpoint symmetry one, given in their strongest form and then actually answered rather than swatted. Also say what evidence would make us revisit the recommendation, and what the team should be logging from now on so this stops being a guessing game.
