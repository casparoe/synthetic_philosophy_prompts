Act as a professional philosopher who does both ancient Stoic ethics and contemporary action theory / free will stuff, since my problem sits exactly on that seam. Brainstorm a couple of candidate principles for yourself before you commit to one, then commit.

Background: I've been sorting situations into up-to-me and not-up-to-me the way the dichotomy of control tells you to, but I sort them with my own test rather than by intuition directly. Call it the try-harder test: X was up to me just in case, had I tried harder or tried differently at the relevant moment, X would probably have come out otherwise. It feels clean and it doesn't require me to know anything about my own character. Here are my verdicts, and you'll see where it breaks.

Whether I win a match: not up to me, the opponent has a vote. Whether I play near my level: up to me. The first flash of irritation when someone cuts in front of me in line: not up to me, and I dont blame myself for having it. The three days of low-grade resentment that follow the exact same incident: also not something I can switch off on demand, structurally identical to the flash as far as I can tell, and yet I do blame myself for it. That pair is the one that keeps me up. Whether I get the job: not up to me; whether I prepare properly: up to me. Whether my kid turns out kind: partly up to me, and I want to insist the "partly" is a real fact and not just hedging. Whether I have a panic attack on the plane: not up to me. Whether I get on the plane anyway: up to me — but note the try-harder test gives the wrong answer on both, because in the panic case trying harder is precisely what makes it worse, so the counterfactual says not-up-to-me about a thing I clearly control. And the meeting case: I stay quiet about a problem I noticed, and it turns out two colleagues were going to raise the same thing anyway, so the outcome would have been identical whether I spoke or not. Counterfactually my speaking made no difference, but it was obviously up to me, and I'd say I failed.

What I want: the principle that actually generates my verdicts, stated precisely and clause by clause, with the case from my list that motivates each clause. If you need to split control into two notions (moment-by-moment versus something longer-running), say so and define both. Then tell me which of my verdicts your principle cannot save and I have to give up — I'd rather lose the resentment pair than lose the panic-attack one, but argue it, don't just take my word. Then give me three cases I didn't think of, ones where your principle and the plain try-harder test disagree, and state your verdicts. Finally: does your account make "partly up to me" a genuine third category, or a confused way of talking about something else?
