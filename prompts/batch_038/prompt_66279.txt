I'm a software engineer at a mid-sized firm, and for three years I've built and maintained the scoring spreadsheet behind our company foundation's grants round: roughly £350,000 a year, about forty applications, three of us on the panel, none of us trained in philosophy or economics. The spreadsheet asks each applicant for an estimated cost per unit of outcome plus a confidence range, and ranks on that. It has produced a bias I can no longer argue my colleagues out of by intuition alone: organisations with clean published numbers sail through, while applicants whose work is advocacy, organising or policy change score near zero simply because they cannot produce a figure — and one applicant this round has written to say that supplying a number would mean inventing one. The panel has split into two camps, and I need something that settles applications rather than arguments. One camp says we should concentrate the money on whatever scores highest, since spreading it thinly is just buying ourselves a good feeling; the other says our contribution only counts where other funders will not go, which on their reasoning points at precisely the unmeasurable applicants.

Please write a decision procedure our panel could actually run: the questions to put to an application, in order; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; where the procedure stops at a no; and where it must hand the decision back to a human being because what remains is a question of values rather than of fact. It has to fit inside twenty minutes for triage and about two hours for each shortlisted application, and it must state explicitly what to do when an applicant supplies no number — defaulting to zero is what got us into this. Be candid about which steps are judgement calls in procedural costume. Then run it on three applications from the current round: a global health charity with published trial data and a firm cost-per-outcome figure; a regional tenants' union campaigning to end no-fault evictions, which declines to estimate its effects; and a local food bank with immaculate records of parcels distributed, which most of us volunteer at and which I suspect your procedure will turn down — if it does, say so plainly rather than softening it. I have no philosophy background and no time to chase references, so please don't send a reading list; write it so that it stands on its own. British English, please.
