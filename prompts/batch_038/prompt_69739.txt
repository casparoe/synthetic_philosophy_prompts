I am a novelist, three books behind me and a fourth in draft, and I have run into a structural problem that my own professional vocabulary cannot seem to state. The book has two strands. The first is narrated throughout in the historical present, in the second person, following a woman through a single year. The second runs backward, from her death to her seventh birthday, in chapters of equal length, and the two strands converge in a closing chapter that belongs to both at once. My readers have been telling me for months that the forward strand feels open and the backward strand feels fated, and I have no way of describing that difference except by borrowing from a discipline I read as an amateur. I have been working through the philosophy of time, and I have absorbed the dispute between presentism and eternalism at what I hope is a reasonably careful level: I understand that the presentist says only the present moment and the things in it exist, while the eternalist says past, present, and future are alike real and that tense is a matter of where one stands rather than of what there is. I also understand the standard lines of attack in both directions — that presentism owes an account of what makes claims about the past true if there is nothing past to ground them; that eternalism struggles to explain the felt asymmetry between relief and dread, the point Prior pressed with his example of being glad an ordeal has finished rather than glad merely that the ordeal lies earlier than one's utterance; and that presentism, once the quantifier in it is pinned down, threatens to collapse into either a triviality or a falsehood.

What I want from you is a translation, done claim by claim, into the vocabulary I actually work in: story time against discourse time; anachrony, and the specific figures of retrospection and anticipation within a plot; order, duration, and frequency; focalization as strictly distinguished from voice, that is, the separation of who speaks from who perceives; free indirect style; the aspectual choice between the historical present and the simple past; scene against summary; and the position the text constructs for its reader. Tell me what the presentist thesis comes to in those terms, what the eternalist thesis comes to, and what each of the objections I listed comes to when stated entirely inside craft talk. Please be strict about it. Do not quietly import metaphysical assumptions and hand them back to me as translation, and do not merely relabel the philosophers' terms with narratological ones and call the substitution a mapping — if the two vocabularies share a word but not a concept, say so. I am not asking for sources, a bibliography, or a reading list; I am asking for the analysis itself, and for it to be yours.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I am actually after. Which of these claims translate cleanly, which translate only at the cost of a distortion you should name, and which have no expression whatever in my vocabulary? Two places in particular need pressure. First, does anything in this debate genuinely correspond to focalization, or is that a category mistake, since focalization governs access to information rather than the ontological status of events? Second, the analogy everyone reaches for is that a finished manuscript is a small block universe, every event equally there on the page, the reader merely moving along it; does that analogy survive contact with the actual arguments, or does it fail precisely where it looks strongest — in, say, the difference between an event being fixed in the order of a text and an event being real in the order of the world? Finally, tell me which way the residue cuts. Does its presence show that the craft vocabulary I have been relying on is impoverished, missing a distinction I should go and acquire, or does it suggest that the metaphysicians are arguing in a register where the words have quietly stopped doing work, so that the disagreement is thinner than its literature implies? Treat me as someone who would rather be told that a structural intuition of mine is confused in some specifiable way than be reassured.
