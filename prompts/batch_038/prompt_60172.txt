I'm putting a philosophy pub quiz together for our department and I want to slip one proper joke in between rounds. Write me a short joke about Berkeley's master argument — the bit where he challenges you to conceive of a tree existing unconceived, and then points out that if you've succeeded, the tree was in your mind after all. The trouble is that every Berkeley joke I've ever heard just does the tree-falls-in-the-forest routine, which isnt even where that argument lives, and I'd like ours to be a cut above. So the punchline should turn on the actual content: the scope slip between the reading on which Berkeley is trivially right (of course whatever anyone conceives is conceived) and the reading he needs to get idealism out of it, so that the joke only lands for someone who can tell those two apart and knows which one does the work. Two or three lines is plenty — a setup with Berkeley and a confident refuter, then the turn, maybe a tag if you've got one. No headings, no bullet points, no bold and no tables, please: just continuous prose, the way you'd write it in an email. And in British English.
