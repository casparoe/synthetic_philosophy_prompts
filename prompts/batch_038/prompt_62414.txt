I cover science and ideas for a national magazine, and I am reporting a feature on low-impact AI: the proposal to constrain a capable agent by penalizing the side effects of its actions rather than by writing down everything we care about. Before I report the controversy, I want the standards straight. So the question is what an impact measure has to deliver before anyone should treat it as a safety rationale rather than a research program.

Take this deployment as fixed, since it is the one my piece centers on. A public water utility has handed a learning agent operational control of a mid-sized distribution network — pumps, valves, chemical dosing, storage — under a deliberately narrow objective: hold pressure and turbidity inside a band. Nobody at the utility can articulate its full set of values, which is the entire reason for the low-impact approach in the first place. The network is not a closed system: pipes corrode, filters foul, algae bloom, storms arrive, and staff make mistakes, so inaction is never stasis. Contractors and operators act inside the plant every day and can be induced to do things. The agent will receive upgrades that expand what it is physically able to do. And every verdict the measure reaches about a particular action may later have to be defended before a regulator, a city council, or a reporter like me.

Write the desiderata: a numbered list of requirements any measure meant for that job must meet. For each one, state the requirement precisely; give the case or consideration that motivates it, inventing cases as needed but making them specific and saying which family of measures each is aimed at; and mark it either non-negotiable for this purpose or a preference of one camp. I want both the requirements that already appear in the technical discussion and the ones that only become visible in a deployment like this — auditability by non-specialists, the agent routing an irreversible change through a human so the accounting shifts while the outcome does not, the aggregation of many individually trivial actions into a transformation, the fact that irreversibility and seriousness come apart in both directions, and the fact that a penalty defined relative to the agent's own capacities stops meaning the same thing when those capacities change.

Then answer three questions. Which single requirement eliminates the most candidate approaches, and why. Whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable — specifically, whether value-agnosticism can coexist with getting the verdicts right in cases where two physically comparable changes differ only in what somebody cares about — and if it cannot, what the smallest honest value input would be. And which distinctions the field blurs that a general-audience piece must keep apart: an agent that causes little impact is not the same thing as an agent scored as low-impact, and I suspect there are others.

You may score the main families of measures against the list in a sentence or two apiece at the end, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases. No citations and no bibliography; I am doing the reporting.
