A colleague of mine reviews novels for a small magazine, and each review ends with two distinct things: a letter grade for the writing, and a separate line telling readers whether to spend money on the book. Her record so far:

- A stylistically dazzling novel whose narration frames its protagonist's cruelty as enviable and rewards the reader for sharing his contempt: D, with the note that the framing was a failure of craft rather than a matter of taste.
- A volume of poems about gardens and grief by a living poet later convicted of cheating an elderly relative out of her savings: A, and buy it — the poems, she wrote, have nothing to do with him.
- A lyrical novel assembled largely from the recorded testimony of survivors of a real massacre, with no authorial framing, published posthumously with royalties going to a charity: A, and buy it — the lushness is what makes the book unbearable, and unbearable is what it ought to be.
- An affectionate, funny novel by a living comedian credibly accused of assault, where the book itself never touches the accusation: A for the prose, but don't buy it, since there is nothing to be gained by paying him.
- A clumsy novel that spends three hundred pages endorsing a view she considers vile: D, and the clumsiness and the vileness, she wrote, are the same defect.

Next month's assignment: a brilliant novel by a much-honored living author who has recently and credibly been accused of the very thing his narrator confesses to. The book's whole achievement is that it makes you sympathize with the narrator, and my colleague believes that removing the sympathy would leave nothing behind. The author keeps the royalties; the victims' families have asked readers not to buy it.

Predict her grade and her buying advice. Lay out the rules that fit all five precedents — I can find at least three, and they don't clearly separate until this book — say which she is most likely running and why, name the closest analog among the five, and specify the further case that would settle which rule she holds. Finally: if she wants her verdicts to hang together, what principle would she be adopting by grading it high, and what by grading it low?
