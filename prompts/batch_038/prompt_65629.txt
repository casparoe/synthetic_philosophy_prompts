I have no academic background in philosophy and have been working through Wittgenstein's remarks on certainty on my own, and there is one point where I keep going around in circles.

The propositions he calls hinges — that here is a hand, and the rest of the things Moore claimed to know — are supposed to sit outside the space of evidence. They are not anything one can find out, and doubt about them is not genuine doubt but a failure to play the game at all, since they are what make testing and doubting intelligible in the first place. But he also insists the picture is not static: the river-bed of thought is part hard rock and part sand, and the channel shifts, so that what once served as a hinge can turn into an ordinary empirical claim, and the reverse.

That second part is what I cannot make sense of. If hinge status can change, then something must drive the change, and whatever drives it looks like a rational assessment of how things are — precisely the thing hinges were meant to be exempt from. If instead nothing rational drives it, then hinge status is just causal drift or the say-so of one's community, and I cannot see why trusting my own hinges is any better grounded than trusting a mood.

Please explain what sort of process is supposed to move a proposition into or out of the hinge set, and how it avoids collapsing in either of those two directions. I would also like a view on whether hinge status is relative to a person, to a community, or to the world itself; and on the case that worries me most, whether a sceptic's argument could itself dislodge a hinge, since if it could, the hinges do not seem to be doing the anti-sceptical work people expect of them.
