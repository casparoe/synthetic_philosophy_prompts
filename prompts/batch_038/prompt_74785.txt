so i run a tiny podcast out of my department — roughly four hundred downloads an episode, and half of those are my mom — and next month's guest is a philosopher of mind whose whole shtick is a thought experiment she borrowed from Hofstadter and made her own. Picture a sealed skull, she calls it a careenium, packed with little spheres she calls simmballs; each simmball has a word or a fragment of a sentence on it, and they careen around, collide, and stick together into chains. Her twist is that some of them are magnetised in a rigged way: a clump carrying the two premises of a valid inference preferentially drags over a loose simmball marked with the conclusion and knocks aside any marked with a contradiction. Once the thing is churning, she says, the honest causal story is told at the symbolic level — the symbols push the substrate around, not the other way round.

She's emphatic that this isn't dualism and isn't panpsychism either. Her actual criterion is the part I want to press: a level is causally real just when intervening at that level is more reliable than intervening at the level below it. Full stop, no metaphysics added.

Write me eight or nine questions I can ask her, and make them turn on what she's actually committed to rather than on general free-will chat. At minimum I want:

- one on exclusion, i.e. if every collision in the careenium is already fixed by the physics of magnetised spheres, what is left over for the premise-clump to do that isn't just a redescribing of it
- one on whether her interventionist criterion makes causal reality depend on who's doing the intervening and with what tools, and what happens to temperature and pressure in a gas on that criterion — is the gas's temperature causally real, and if yes, is that a feature or a bug
- one on the gap between a simmball's geometry and its meaning: what would it take for the *content* rather than the magnetic shape to be doing the work, and how would she tell the two apart from inside the careenium
- one on whether the rigged magnets are smuggling in exactly the semantics she claims to be discovering — someone built the thing to mean something, so is the downward causation hers or the system's
- one asking whether this is a mechanism or a metaphor with good production values
- one that makes her say what observation would convince her the symbolic level was causally inert after all

For each question give me the follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. She's quick and she has a move for everything — usually some version of "you're asking at the wrong level" — so build the follow-ups to cut off the retreat rather than just re-asking. Keep the wording short enough that I can say it out loud without sounding like I'm reading a seminar paper, and don't give me anything in the shape of "what first drew you to this picture."
