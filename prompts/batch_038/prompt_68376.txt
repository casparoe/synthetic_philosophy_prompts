I'm about to accept a job at a four-year-old software company, where I'd be roughly the sixtieth person they have ever hired. A friend of mine who does statistics for a living tried to talk me out of it, and I haven't been able to answer him. His claim is that my place in the hiring order is, for all I can tell, a random draw from the total number of people the company will ever employ, and that this alone licenses a forecast: the company will probably hire about as many more people as it has already hired, with a substantial chance it stops hiring within a decade. He insists this is arithmetic rather than opinion. Then he said the same reasoning applies to our species — some hundred billion people have lived, so a comparable number more is what we should expect, none of them further off than a few millennia — and that this ought to change how I think about having children.

I have facts he seems to dismiss: I have seen the company's books and its new funding; they hired thirty people last year against twelve the year before; and my accepting the job alters the very sequence I am supposedly sampling from. Do these defeat his argument, or is he right that they are beside the point? And if the reasoning fails, does it fail in the same way for the company as for humanity — or does the gap between them tell me something about what the argument was really relying on?
