I am drafting the opening chapter of a dissertation prospectus on accuracy-first epistemology, and before committing to any technical machinery I want to be clear about what the research program is actually being asked to deliver. Part of my difficulty is that the label seems to cover three claims that are routinely run together: a statistical claim that certain scoring rules reward well-calibrated degrees of belief; a value-theoretic claim that accuracy is the fundamental, perhaps the only, source of distinctively epistemic value; and a normative claim that familiar coherence requirements — probabilism, conditionalization, and the rest — are derivative, holding only insofar as they are accuracy-conducive. My committee has asked me to fix the standards of adequacy first, so that we can tell whether disagreements in the literature are substantive or merely reflect which of these three claims a given author is defending. So please write the desiderata: the requirements any accuracy-first account must meet to count as a theory of epistemic rationality rather than a collection of representation theorems, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, and each marked either as non-negotiable or as a demand peculiar to one camp.

Treat at least the following as candidates, add whatever I have missed, and — this matters as much as the additions — tell me which of my candidates are not genuine desiderata at all but prejudices of a particular side. (i) Coverage: the framework must deliver comparative verdicts over every credal state it claims to regulate, including grossly incoherent ones and ones no real agent entertains, since a dominance argument only bites if the offending state is outperformed across all the possibilities the agent takes seriously. (ii) Measure-independence: the normative upshot must not be an artifact of the scoring rule selected; if probabilism follows from a Brier-like measure but fails under some other proper measure, the theorem is a fact about measures rather than about rationality, and the account owes a non-circular justification of whatever constraints it imposes on admissible measures. (iii) Aggregation: it must take a position on whether the accuracy of a whole credal state is the sum of the accuracies of its components, given that credences in a proposition, its negation, and their conjunction can each look acceptable in isolation while the package is clearly worse. (iv) Granularity: verdicts should not flip when the algebra of propositions is refined or coarsened, as when the same state of mind is scored against a three-way partition and then against a nine-way refinement of it. (v) Anti-luck: accuracy is standardly a function of credence and truth value alone, so a wild hunch that happens to be confident and right scores exactly as well as knowledge; the program must say why that is not a counterexample to the claim that accuracy is the whole of epistemic value. (vi) Guidance: dominance arguments must recommend states an agent can actually adopt, which forces the program to confront credences about necessary truths, about propositions too complex to have been considered, and about the very scoring rule in dispute. (vii) Evidence: it must explain why deferring to one's total evidence is accuracy-conducive without smuggling in a norm that is not itself about accuracy, given that in any particular world the evidentially reckless agent may turn out better calibrated than the careful one. (viii) Anti-consequentialism: it must answer the charge that maximizing expected accuracy licenses trading a badly inaccurate credence on one proposition for gains elsewhere, and that this misconstrues the relation between an inquirer and each individual truth. (ix) Full belief: it must either accommodate the connection between high credence and outright belief or explain why the program is entitled to remain silent about it.

On top of the list, I want three things. First, identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidate theories, and say what kind of view it knocks out. Second, identify any subset that is jointly unsatisfiable — my own suspicion is that measure-independence, additivity, granularity-invariance, and the extensional conception of accuracy cannot all be had at once, and if that is right the list is itself a result rather than a shopping list — but I want the argument sketched, not the conclusion asserted, and I want to be told if the tension really lies elsewhere. Third, and briefly, say how three broad lines of response fare against the list: the permissive line that admits any proper scoring rule and lives with measure-relative verdicts; the axiomatic line that imposes conditions on measures to narrow the field and re-derives the theorems for the whole resulting family; and the pluralist line that treats accuracy as one pro tanto epistemic consideration among several. If any of those three labels mischaracterizes a real position, correct the label rather than the position. A numbered list or a table is fine. Please keep the weight on the requirements and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring, and do not include references, a reading list, or attributions — I have the bibliography in hand and want the specification, not the survey.
