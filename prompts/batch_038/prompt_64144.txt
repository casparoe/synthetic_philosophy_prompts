My half-formed idea, which my supervisor thinks is a trap because everything worth saying about it has already been said: basically everyone frames Kepler and astrology as a yes/no question, did the guy who wrote down the laws of planetary motion really believe in it or was he just casting horoscopes to keep the landlord off his back, and the standard answer is "sort of both," which tells you nothing. My suspicion is that the interesting thing isn't what he believed but what he counted as a license to believe it. From what I've read he seems to run something like a mechanism test: an astrological claim is admissible only if he can name a channel for it — light, the sun's approach and recession giving you seasons, the moon working on moisture, geometric or harmonic proportions between planetary rays which he insists are located down at the recieving end rather than up in the sky — and inadmissible otherwise, no matter how often it seems to come true.
