I have no formal training in philosophy — I left school at sixteen — but I've spent years in arguments with believers and with former believers, and there is a pattern I keep running into that I can't put a name to.

Someone raises a case of apparently pointless suffering: a child dying slowly of cancer, a fawn burned in a forest fire that takes days to die. The religious answer is that we are in no position to judge what an all-knowing being would have reason to permit, so calling the case pointless proves nothing. Then the same person says, days later and with complete confidence, that God allowed a particular marriage to fail, or a particular illness, in order to teach someone something, or that a disaster was a warning to a town. The humility only ever runs in one direction. It blocks the objector's negative claim and never touches the believer's own positive claims about what God is up to in this or that event.

When I've pressed people on this, they don't say they were guessing. They say the positive claims come from somewhere else — scripture, the teaching of their community, something they take to have happened to them — so they are reporting, not speculating. Which leaves me unsure whether I've caught a genuine inconsistency or stumbled on something subtler.

Is there an established concept for this? The folk labels I know — special pleading, having it both ways, moving the goalposts — don't seem to fit, because the standards aren't being changed mid-argument; two standards are kept on separate shelves and whichever is needed gets used, and the second shelf may be independent rather than a dodge. I'd like to know what the nearest established ideas are, precisely how what I've described departs from each of them, and whether the appeal to revelation rescues the practice or only relocates the difficulty. If nothing in the literature covers it, say so plainly and then name and define it — with real edges, including whether the same pattern turns up in non-religious arguments. I'm after the carving, not the label.
