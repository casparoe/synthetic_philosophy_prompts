I write monitoring software for a shrimp farm — mostly Pacific whiteleg shrimp, and a second site with tilapia tanks that we also cover. Our cameras spit out per-tank behavioral features: feeding response latency, how much of the stock is hanging near the surface, swimming path weirdness, how hard they scatter when a shadow crosses the tank. The product team wants all of that collapsed into a single "welfare score," 0 to 100, on a dashboard, and eventually into rules like "harvest this tank early" or "these are the stunning parameters for this batch." I'm a backend engineer, zero philosophy training, and the dozen-ish papers I've read all gesture at "stress" and "suffering" without ever saying what would have to be true for for a number like ours to actually be tracking something.

So before I write the product spec, I want the spec of the theory. Give me the desiderata: what any account of welfare for farmed fish and decapods has to deliver before a score like ours is even a candidate for measuring it. For each requirement, I want the case or consideration behind it — why it's on the list, not just its name.

Things I'd want the list to speak to, though you should reorganize, cut, or add as you see fit:

- whether the account has to take a stand on what welfare *is* (feels good to the animal / gets what it wants / functions well) or whether it can stay officially neutral and still license a cardinal number;
- the gap between "this animal is mounting a stress response" and "this animal is faring badly," and what would actually close it;
- what it owes us for animals with no cortex and a distributed nervous system, without just quietly importing mammal-shaped assumptions;
- cross-species comparability — our dashboard puts shrimp and tilapia on the same 0–100 scale, is that ever defensible, and if not what should it be doing instead;
- the case where behavior and physiology point in opposite directions, especially an animal that looks fine under chronic bad conditions because it's stopped responding;
- aggregation inside a single tank: one number for ~30k animals that aren't interchangeable;
- and whether robustness-under-misuse counts — a farm tech on a tablet at 4am is going to treat the number as an instruction, not a hint. Is that a genuine desideratum on the theory, or an engineering problem I should keep off the philosophy list?

Then give me your take on three things: which of these are non-negotiable versus which are really just the stated preferences of one camp; which single requirement knocks out the most popular approaches; and which pair you think can't be jointly satisfied. I have a suspicion it's the "hand us a cardinal number" one colliding with whatever honesty requirement you end up with, but tell me if the real collision is somewhere else. Score the existing views in two or three sentences at most — the criteria are the deliverable, not the scoreboard.

Hard limit: 700 words. American English. And no bibliography, no citation list, no "see also" — I won't be able to look any of it up.
