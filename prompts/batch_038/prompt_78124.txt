Just spent three hours arguing with my friend Dana about tipping and I'm still wound up, so bear with me. Her view: local conventions like tipping, queuing, and not crossing against the light bind you only if you've somehow signed on -- you're a guest, guests keep the house rules, end of story. My view: that's not why they bind at all. What's wrong with cutting the line or stiffing the server isn't rudeness toward your hosts, its free-riding on a scheme that hundreds of strangers are quietly keeping alive so everyone's ordinary plans work out. And underneath that is the real disagreement: does a convention have to have some particular person you wrong in order to generate an obligation? Dana says jaywalking at 3 a.m. on a totally empty street proves it doesn't. I say the empty street is a special case that proves the rule.

Here's my problem. Thursday I'm giving the opening remarks -- about seven minutes -- at a one-day workshop on everyday obligation. The room is maybe half experimental economists who think norms are just equilibria and don't want to be moralized at, half moral philosophers who want nothing else, plus my department chair and two people from student affairs, because the university is drafting a handbook page for incoming international students spelling out which local customs they're expected to follow. The chair has already hinted she'd like the remarks to be warm about it. If I'm right, that handbook is roughly defensible. If Dana's right, it's either condescending or toothless, and I'd be praising something I think is a mistake.

So write the remarks. They have to land for all three factions without turning into a metaethics lecture, and they have to actually take a position on whether a visitor owes anything to a local convention. Don't split the difference and tell the room that reasonable people differ -- pick my side, pick Dana's, or pick a third thing entirely, but commit and build the reasons into the speech itself. Afterward, give me two or three sentences outside the speech saying which side you chose and why, including where you think I got the argument wrong if you think I did.
