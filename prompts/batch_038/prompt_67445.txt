I'm a control systems engineer. I think in feedback loops, controllability and observability, actuator authority, exogenous disturbances, closed-loop response, cost functionals and setpoints; my knowledge of Stoicism stops at the slogans. Take the position at the heart of the modern Stoic revival — that some things are up to us and others are not, that wellbeing consists in directing one's concern exclusively at the first class, and that the payoff is tranquillity rather than better regulation of outcomes — and restate it in my working vocabulary, item by item. Include the practice of undertaking a project with the mental proviso that its result may be taken away by circumstances, and the revisionists who think the dichotomy is too crude and insert a third class of things only partly up to us. Then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly, what maps only if I let you stretch an engineering term, and what has no counterpart at all. Do not treat a mapping as successful because it sounds apt, and do not substitute engineering assumptions for Stoic ones and present the result as translation. Finally, tell me which way the residue cuts — does it expose a distinction the Stoics need and lack, or does it show that my framework cannot even state the question they are asking?
