So my reading group has been going in circles on quantum suicide for three weeks and I've come out the other side with an argument I cant get out of. Assume I'm sympathetic to Everett — grant the whole package: every nonzero-amplitude outcome happens on some branch, and my experience is always located on one of them.

(1) At any moment, my observations come from exactly one branch: the one where there's a me having them.
(2) A rational Everettian sets credences by branch weight, conditionalised on there being an observation for me to have.
(3) Suppose a machine flips a quantum coin 100 times and, if any flip comes up tails, painlessly destroys me; if all 100 come up heads, it does nothing at all.
(4) On every branch where I'm still around to check the readout, the readout says 100 heads. The tails branches contain no me for me to be.
(5) So by (2), my credence in observing 100 heads, given that I observe anything, is 1 — even though that branch carries weight 2^-100.
(6) Nothing in that reasoning cared about the number 100. So it goes through for a million flips, and it goes through for the ordinary lethal stuff too: the aneurysm that doesn't rupture, the biopsy that comes back clean, the truck that misses by a foot.
(7) Therefore it's rational for me to be certain that the world will keep producing visible, statistically impossible conspiracies in my favour for as long as I live, no matter how low-weight each one is.
(8) But a belief-forming rule that hands me certainty about events of arbitrarily small weight isn't tracking evidence at all. If (7) is right, no sequence of observations could ever confirm the Born rule, because I'd expect law-defying luck whatever the weights said.
(9) So (2) is false: branch weight can't serve as a guide to credence. Which, as far as I can see, removes the only thing that ever connected the theory to evidence.

I don't want to accept (9). If I have to rank, (2) is the premise I'd most like to keep — I think the whole Everettian project needs weight to do double duty as both what I should believe and what I should care about, and I'd rather not split those apart.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it actually cost? I want the price, not just the name of the move. Two follow-ups. Half my group says (4) is where it breaks; the other half says (4) is fine and something slides further down — I can't tell who's right and I don't want to just pick the diagnosis that lets me keep (2). And second: does anything change if the mechanism is one that leaves no branch containing a functioning me at all, like standing at ground zero of something that vaporises you outright? My instinct is that case kills the arguement, but I can't say why, and if it does kill it I'm worried (6) was smuggling something in that I now have to go back and make explicit.
