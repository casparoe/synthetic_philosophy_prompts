"Carnap held that metaphysical sentences are neither true nor false but empty of sense, and he was perfectly consistent about applying this to morals and politics too: to call a society unjust is not, for him, to say anything at all, it is to express an attitude, not very different from booing. That left only one genuinely political question, the empirical question of which means serve which ends, and it is why Carnap became an enthusiast for central planning — once you accept that values can't be argued about, you leave the ends to whoever happens to hold them and let scientifically trained administrators work out the means. The same attitude runs through his philosophy of logic. His principle of tolerance holds that there is no fact of the matter about which logical language is right, so we are free to adopt whatever framework we like; choosing a framework is a matter of taste in just the way that choosing an ultimate value is, and Carnap's pluralism about logic and his liberalism about politics are therefore one doctrine stated twice. He was personally a fairly conventional social democrat, but he saw where his premises led, and he sketched a rationally organised society in which political disagreement would be settled the way engineering disagreements are settled, by calculation. Those who accuse logical empiricism of being technocratic or politically quietist are, on this point, simply taking Carnap at his word."
