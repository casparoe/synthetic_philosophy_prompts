> In the ontological models framework, a preparation procedure P gets associated with a probability distribution μ_P(λ) over a space Λ of ontic states λ, and a measurement outcome k gets a response function ξ(k|λ); the model reproduces quantum mechanics when integrating ξ(k|λ)μ_P(λ) over Λ gives the Born rule probability. Harrigan and Spekkens call a model ψ-ontic when any two distinct pure states have distributions with disjoint supports, so that the ontic state fixes which pure state was prepared, and ψ-epistemic when some pair of distinct pure states has overlapping distributions, so that a single λ is compatible with more than one pure state and the quantum state functions as information about λ rather than as a physical property of the system. This is a classification of pure states only, mixed states aren't covered.
>
> The PBR argument takes two systems prepared independently, each in either |0⟩ or |+⟩, so there are four possible joint pure states. If the model is ψ-epistemic then there's a nonzero chance that λ₁ falls in the overlap region of the |0⟩ and |+⟩ distributions, and likewise for λ₂, which means the joint ontic state is compatible with all four of those product states at once. The two systems are then brought together and measured jointly. Since the four product states are mutually orthogonal, there's a measurement that identifies which one was prepared, and whichever outcome that measurement returns has probability zero for at least one of the four — including one the ontic state is supposed to be compatible with. That's the contradiction, so the overlap has to vanish and distinct pure states are ψ-ontic.
>
> The assumption doing the work is preparation independence: independently prepared systems have a product distribution over their joint ontic state. That isn't Bell's locality condition, it constrains how preparations combine rather than what happens at spacelike separation, but it has a similar flavor, and giving it up means letting the ontic state of one system depend on how another, separately prepared system was set up, which is hard to motivate physically.
>
> So I think the claim is basically right, with one qualification. ψ-ontic means ψ is part of the ontology instead of a state of knowledge, and in the concrete theories that cashes out as a real field: in Bohmian mechanics the universal wave function is a physical field on configuration space guiding the particle configuration, so Bohmians should read PBR as a vindication of what they already assumed. Everett is ψ-ontic for the same reason, and so are collapse theories. The qualification is that ψ-ontology doesn't say the field lives in ordinary 3-space.
>
> Where PBR really bites is on the epistemic interpretations. QBism says the quantum state is an agent's personal degrees of belief about their own future experiences, and Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics says a state is only ever a state relative to some system; on both views different agents can assign different pure states to the same situation. PBR shows that's incompatible with the Born rule, so both are refuted, or else forced to deny there's any agent-independent ontic state at all, which isn't a physical theory anymore. Spekkens' toy model is a different case: it's the cleanest illustration of what ψ-epistemic means, with epistemic states as sets of ontic states restricted by a knowledge balance principle, but its not a model of quantum mechanics (it can't reproduce Bell-violating correlations), so PBR doesn't apply to it and it isn't a counterexample. What the toy model does show is that the ψ-epistemic style of model building is coherent — you just can't get all of quantum mechanics that way while keeping preparation independence.
