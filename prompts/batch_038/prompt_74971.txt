I run a small metaphysics of science seminar and I'm sick of the same two moves getting trotted out separately — the cosmic-coincidence complaint against the best systems analysis, and the uninstantiated-law complaint in the other direction. I want one case that drives both wedges in at once and that can't be brushed off with either stock reply. It's going out as a take-home, so stipulating the verdicts instead of generating them counts as cheating: every verdict has to fall out of what each theory independently says.

Build me a finite world — a mosaic small enough that you can state every fact in it in a couple paragraphs — plus one counterfactual variant of it where a handful of those facts are different. No probablities or chances anywhere, no infinities, no ideal observers. And nothing may turn on which vocabulary the mosaic gets described in or on gerrymandered predicates: I want the case to bite before Lewis's eligibility/naturalness machinery is switched on. The anti-Humean side should be a plain necessitation/governance view, not a powers view.

Inside that single world pair I want two regularities:

- R1, true throughout the actual mosaic, such that the best systems analysis has to count it as a law while the necessitation view has to refuse it — and the refusal can't just be "the world's too small to tell," it has to follow from what the necessitation relation is supposed to do.
- R2, such that in the variant mosaic the necessitation view still counts it as a law while the best systems analysis no longer does.

For each, actually run the comparison: name the rival candidate systems and show how the simplicity/strength tradeoff comes out, rather than just announcing a verdict.

Give me these sections in this order: "The world" (both mosaics, ~200 words), "R1" (~250), "R2" (~250), "What's doing the work" (~150 words on the single feature that generates both disagreements, and what happens to each verdict if you delete it or scale the world up), and "Best Humean reply" (~150 words on the strongest thing a Lewisian can say about R1 without buying back any of the naturalness apparatus I banned).
