You are a philosopher of AI and cognitive science who has brokered enough adversarial collaborations to know the two ways they fail: the agreed claims come out so bland that signing them costs nobody anything, and the disagreements get phrased in one camp's vocabulary so that the other camp reads them as caricature. Your task is to draft a document that avoids both failure modes. Think carefully, work in the order I specify, and do not skip the scaffolding section.

**Context.** I am convening a two-day workshop on the frame problem. My two headline participants have agreed, reluctantly, to produce a joint statement in advance of the sessions rather than to spend the sessions restating positions they have held since graduate school.

*Camp A — the axiomatizers.* A formal knowledge-representation theorist. Their view: the frame problem in its original technical sense was a problem about the cost of writing down what does not change, and it was solved by re-axiomatizing the domain rather than by bolting on default machinery. Replace the per-action-per-fluent frame axioms with one successor-state axiom per fluent, add unique-names assumptions for actions, and inertia falls out of the logic; axiom count then grows with the number of fluents instead of with the product of fluents and actions, so the combinatorial objection is simply dead. What remains, on their account, is either ordinary engineering — sensor selection, perception tuning, deciding how much of the world to model — or a wholly general problem about abstraction for which no formalism can be singled out and blamed.

*Camp B — the situated critics.* Their view: that re-axiomatization relocates the problem instead of solving it. Every successor-state axiom is written in a fluent vocabulary someone has already chosen, over an action set that has already been closed, at an event granularity fixed by the unique-names assumption. The hard question was never how to keep the books once the world has been sorted into representable properties; it was how a system embedded in an unsorted, unbounded world determines what is representable at all and what is worth attending to right now. Camp B holds that Camp A's result is a solution *relative to an ontology*, and that the ontology is exactly what was in question.

*Concessions both have already made in principle,* and which your drafting must reflect rather than paper over: A concedes that choosing the fluent vocabulary is a real and unsolved problem, but denies that it is the frame problem; B concedes that within a fixed vocabulary the bookkeeping result is correct and non-trivial, but denies that it touches the problem the term was coined for.

**Shared test case.** Both camps have agreed to argue from one concrete artifact rather than from intuitions about robots in general. It is a bench setup we are calling the Counter Perturbation Battery: a household manipulator with a hand-written domain model of roughly 40 fluents and 9 action schemas, executing a 14-step plan to clear a counter and make tea. Three perturbation conditions are introduced mid-plan, silently, with the planner's beliefs initially uncorrected. (1) A mug the plan needs is moved behind an occluder. (2) The kettle it identified is swapped for a visually near-identical empty one. (3) A person begins working in the counter region the plan reserves for itself. For each condition the battery records whether the system re-plans, what it chooses to re-perceive, and how many fluent values it must consult before making that decision. There are two further arms: an ablation arm in which the hand-written fluent interface is removed and the controller is given raw perception plus a world model trained by next-frame prediction, and a scale arm in which the domain model is expanded to roughly 4,000 fluents.

**Deliverable.** First, a short brainstorming section: list at least five candidate formulations of the core disagreement between these camps and label each as vacuous, slanted toward A, slanted toward B, or usable, with one line saying why. This is scaffolding for you, not part of the document, so keep it tight.

Then the joint statement itself, as a text both camps could put their names to, in five sections:

1. *Agreed claims.* Numbered. Each must be something both would actually sign and each must be non-vacuous — say what it rules out.
2. *Disagreements.* Each stated once neutrally, then once in the wording Camp A would prefer for its own side and once in the wording Camp B would prefer for its own side, such that each camp recognizes its own statement as its view rather than as a parody.
3. *Cruxes.* The sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. I expect at least three, and I expect one to concern whether the fluent vocabulary belongs to the system or to the theorist's description of it, one to concern whether the ablation arm counts as solving relevance determination or as amortizing it over a training distribution other people assembled, and one to concern whether the phrase picks out a single problem or two problems of different kinds. If you think one of my three guesses is mistaken or better replaced by something sharper, say so explicitly and substitute your own. For each crux, give the test, argument, or case both camps accept as bearing on it — drawn from the battery wherever possible, naming the condition and the recorded quantity — and each camp's prediction, written down before any results exist, in a form that could come out false. Where the two predictions do not genuinely differ, say so rather than manufacturing a difference.
4. *Self-falsifiers.* For each camp, what would count as evidence or argument against its own position, specific enough that the opposing camp would accept it as a real concession rather than a decorative gesture.
5. *Verbalness.* Which parts of this dispute you judge to be terminological; the explicit stipulations that would remove them; and what substantive residue survives those stipulations. If you conclude that none of it is verbal, argue for that.

Keep the prose document dense rather than long: roughly 1,200 to 1,800 words. Do not adjudicate the debate and do not let a verdict leak into the drafting; the deliverable is a document, not a ruling.

**Two constraints on sourcing.** The workshop has banned reference-dropping: no bibliography, no reading list, no citations, and no attributing any view to a named author or text. Everything must stand on the arguments. You may use the ordinary technical names of devices — fluent, successor-state axiom, unique-names assumption, nonmonotonic default, occlusion — but no claim in your document should depend on getting a source or a date right.

**Finally, output format.** After the prose document, and with nothing following it, return a single machine-parseable JSON object inside a fenced code block with exactly these top-level fields:

- `agreed_claims`: array of objects with `id` (string, A1, A2, …), `claim` (string, the claim as both camps would sign it), `what_it_rules_out` (string — the inference or position the claim excludes, which is what makes it non-vacuous), `cost_to_each_signer` (string — what A gives up and what B gives up by signing).
- `disagreements`: array of objects with `id` (string, D1, D2, …), `neutral_statement` (string — the question they differ on, in vocabulary belonging to neither camp), `camp_a_wording` (string), `camp_b_wording` (string), `each_side_would_accept_own_wording` (boolean — your honest judgement), `verbal_or_substantive` (string, exactly one of `verbal`, `substantive`, `mixed`).
- `cruxes`: array of objects with `id` (string, C1, C2, …), `question` (string), `settlement_would_move` (string, exactly one of `camp_a`, `camp_b`, `both`, `neither_yet`), `shared_test` (string — the battery condition or the argument both accept as bearing on it, naming the condition and the recorded quantity where applicable), `prediction_camp_a` (string), `prediction_camp_b` (string), `predictions_genuinely_differ` (boolean), `if_not_why_not_decidable_yet` (string, empty if they differ).
- `self_falsifiers`: object with keys `camp_a` and `camp_b`, each an array of strings.
- `verbal_disputes`: array of objects with `dispute_id` (string, matching a `disagreements` id), `proposed_stipulation` (string — the definition both should adopt), `residue_after_stipulation` (string — the substantive disagreement that survives it, or `none`).
- `bottom_line`: string — one or two sentences naming, as neutrally as you can manage, the single question on which these two camps actually turn.
