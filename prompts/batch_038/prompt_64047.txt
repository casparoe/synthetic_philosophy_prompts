I co-run a reading group that's about half ML people and half philosophy-of-social-science people, and for like three years the same argument comes up every time the fairness impossibility results get mentioned — the Chouldechova one and the Kleinberg-Mullainathan-Raghavan one — and nobody ever moves an inch. One side says the theorem is genuinely deep: when base rates differ, calibration and equal error rates can't coexist, so any deployed classifier is making a value tradeoff whether it admits it or not, and pretending otherwise is bad faith. The other side says it's a base-rate observation dressed up in theorem clothes, that you could sketch the counterexample on a napkin with two groups and any unequal prevalence and no machinery at all, and that the impossibility framing does real damage because it hands policymakers a reason to think nothing can be done. They wave the same equations at each other. Then there's a third group who say both are confused, because fairness isn't a property of a score but of a sociotechnical system, and I cant tell whether that's a way out of the stalemate or just a fourth position that doesn't engage with the first two.

My own hunch, which nobody finds convincing: the camps aren't talking about the same object. One is making a claim about score functions and the other about decision rules, and the force of the theorem depends on which you mean — a calibrated score that gets group-specific thresholds applied to it is a different animal from a calibrated binary decision, and once you separate those the argument maybe dissolves into a dispute about what "the algorithm" refers to, which sounds verbal to me. But maybe it isn't verbal; maybe the real fault line is normative, about whether base rate differences are the kind of fact a model should be allowed to encode, and the math is just where both sides park it because it's more respectable to fight there.

So: diagnose this, rather than telling me both sides have a point. Verbal dispute, hidden normative disagreement, two questions under one heading, or is one side simply wrong about what the result says? Also tell me how confident you are in the diagnosis and single out the point you're least sure of.
