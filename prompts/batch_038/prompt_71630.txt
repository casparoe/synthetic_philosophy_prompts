We just finished the careenium stretch of GEB in my reading group — the vat where simmballs float around clumping and colliding, and the collisions mirror the inferences between the symbols they stand for — and the group split clean in half and hasn't budged in three sessions. Camp A says obviously the simmballs push the careenium around, that's just what it is for the vat to have thoughts. Camp B says that's incoherent: every collision is already fixed by the fluid dynamics, so the symbol-level story is at best a handy shorthand for physics we can't compute. Neither side ever actually engages the others argument, they just re-run their own and declare victory.

I tried a variant and it backfired. Two vats, identical careenium, identical laws, identical trajectories from start to finish — except in one the simmball shapes were set by a designer so the chemistry works out as long division, and in the other they were set by random sloshing. Same microphysics, different symbol facts. Camp B said I'd proved their point for them; camp A said I'd changed the subject.

So what kind of disagreement is this, really? My working guess is that it isn't metaphysical at all — it's a fight over the word 'cause', or over what an explanation is required to do, with a smuggled-in question about whether either vat has content. Is that right? Give me a diagnosis grounded in what each side actually says, plus what would count as progress — a distinction, a result about exclusion, a change of question — or why none is coming.
