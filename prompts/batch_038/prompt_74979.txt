My colleague Priya is leaving our department after nine years to take up a post in Auckland, and several of us from the Tuesday reading group are assembling a farewell card. The group spent last term working through the personal identity section of Parfit's Reasons and Persons, so the card has become, inevitably, a vehicle for teletransporter jokes — which means the bar for mine is regrettably high. I would like something better than the usual gags about a man who wakes up on Mars and learns he was destroyed in London, or about a queue of indistinguishable replicas each insisting he is the original.

Could you write an original joke, then, framed as an ordinary farewell-card message, whose humour turns specifically on the distinction Parfit draws between identity and what matters in survival? Ideally the punchline would exploit the fact that Relation R — psychological connectedness and continuity with the right kind of cause — can obtain between people who are not one and the same person, can hold in degrees, and can have more than one candidate satisfying it without any further fact settling which of them is Priya. A reader who has never opened the book should still find the surface funny, or at least charmingly strange, while someone who has should feel the joke land precisely where the argument lives.

Format: put the joke first, with nothing before it, and keep the card text to a maximum of seventy words. After a blank line, add at most two sentences identifying the particular feature of Parfit's view that carries the punchline. Please do not include any references, bibliography, or suggested readings — I have no way of looking anything up at the moment, so I would rather the note stand entirely on its own.
