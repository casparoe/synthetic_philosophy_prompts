ok so I'm writing up my position for a practical ethics seminar and I want to stress-test it before I say any of it out loud. Here's the package, roughly, in the order it came to me:

1. Consequentialism is a criterion of rightness and not a heuristic. What makes an act right is the value of its actual consequences.

2. The cluelessness worry bites against that criterion itself. You know the shape of it: an ordinary act's effects ramify indefinitely down the causal chain and no finite agent ever gets any grip on them. I'm not moved by the standard deflection that this is only a problem for action-guidance while the criterion stays untouched. My thought is that if the standard of rightness is one that no agent could ever get even approximate purchase on, that's information about the standard, not just about us.

3. Given that, when I can't say anything at all about the far-downstream effects of an act, I don't let them move the needle in either direction. They drop out of the calculation rather than sitting in it as one huge fuzzy error term.

4. So in practice I deliberate over the reasonably foreseeable near-term consequences, and I think that's still being a consequentialist, not a quiet retreat into some other theory.

5. I'm completely unbothered by the thought that this undercuts acting on medium-range aggregate forecasts — emissions pathways out to 2050, epidemic modeling, that kind of thing. That seems to me like ordinary statistical prediction and I don't see it colliding with 2 or 3.

6. But I also find the astronomical-value arguments genuinely compelling: the far future might contain such a vast amount of potential value that even a very small probability of shifting it ought to swamp anything achievable in the near term.

7. And none of this makes me a moral sceptic. I still think we can know that some acts are wrong — casually cruel is worse than not casually cruel, that sort of thing — and I don't think 2 or 3 threaten that.

So: does this hang together, or am I talking out of both sides of my mouth? I'd like the tensions ranked by how bad they actually are rather than dumped in a list as if they were all equally serious. For each real one, say which commitment has to go or get weakened and what I lose by weakening it — I want the cost, not just the diagnosis. And please also tell me where I'm worrying about nothing: if a pair only looks like a tension and dissolves once you spell out what each claim is actually doing, say so and say why, becuase I suspect at least one of these is fine and I can't tell which.
