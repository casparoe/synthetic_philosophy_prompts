I TA a philosophy-of-math seminar where everyone's had one term of set theory, knows what ZF is, and has never thought about set-theoretic epistemology. For the handout I want a "where do you actually stand on the axiom of choice?" decison tree. Build it out of cases and claims they could answer without knowing any of the labels: does a vector space with no basis bother you, would you give up Banach-Tarski to make every set of reals measurable, is "a countable union of countable sets is countable" something that needs proving at all, does it matter that taking choice internally in a topos forces excluded middle. One question per node, one named position per leaf, and I want these leaves at a minimum: the ZFC realist who thinks the iterative conception settles it, the maximize-the-universe version of that same realism, Solovay-style ZF+DC with everything measurable, determinacy, the Quinean/NF-style rejection, Brouwerian intuitionism (be careful here — they don't just reject choice, they keep something choice-like for choice sequences), topos-theoretic relativism, and a multiverse/deflationary "no fact of the matter" leaf. Add anything I've left out.

Then two extra bits. For every combination of answers that no leaf can occupy, say why its empty — incoherent (like wanting the well-ordering principle without choice), unattractive, or just unexplored. And flag the pairs of leaves your questions honestly can't tell apart, rather than pretending they can. Aim for 12-15 nodes and something I can fit on one page.
