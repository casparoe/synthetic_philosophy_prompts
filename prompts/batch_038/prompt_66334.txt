our ethics reading group has a move somebody always makes when we hit dueling or abolition: the norms didn't die because anyone got persuaded, they died because they stopped being useful — same way a technology gets dropped when something better comes along. a norm is a tool doing a job, and when the tool stops paying you swap it out. it's supposed to settle the arguments-vs-interests question in favour of interests, and against the honor-code people.

can you audit it? i want the correspondence table — what's the job, what's the tool, who's the user doing the swapping, what counts as the better replacement — and then the disanalogies, sorted into the ones that actually bite on that conclusion and the ones that are just decoration. two things i'm stuck on. first, with a technology somebody notices it works worse and picks otherwise, and that noticing is itself a cognitive act, so i worry the analogy doesnt separate interests from argument so much as relabel one as the other. second, the people who gave up dueling were the ones using it, whereas the people who ended slavery were mostly not the ones it served — does the analogy have to treat those as the same kind of thing, or does that split it in two? tell me whether my worries hold or whether it has resources to answer them, and what a better analogy for how dueling actually ended would look like.
