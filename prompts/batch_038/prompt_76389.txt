I am a junior at a public high school, and our administration has asked three students to serve on a committee reviewing how disciplinary appeals are decided at our school. The current arrangement is a three-person panel of teachers reaching a verdict by majority vote. The proposal under consideration would replace it with an eleven-person panel — seven teachers and four students — also deciding by majority. The strongest argument offered for the larger panel at the first meeting was a version of the Condorcet jury theorem: if each member is individually more likely than not to reach the correct verdict, then the panel's majority verdict is likelier to be correct than an individual member's judgment, and the larger the panel gets, the closer its reliability approaches certainty. Several of the adults present treated this as settling the matter. I am not convinced, and I would like to be able to say precisely what I find unconvincing rather than merely registering skepticism.

So, before I draft the committee's recommendation, I want a specification. Write the desiderata: the requirements a mathematical result must meet before it may be cited as an epistemic justification for enlarging a decision-making body like ours. For each requirement, state the consideration that motivates it, and where it helps, the concrete case showing what goes wrong if the requirement is dropped.

A few matters I would like the list to address — though please tell me where I have mistakenly treated a camp's preference as a genuine requirement, or listed something twice under different names, and add what I have missed:

- Whether a requirement concerns the result's assumptions, its conclusion, or the step from the conclusion to what a committee is licensed to believe and do. In the meeting these three were run together constantly, and I suspect much of the disagreement was about different ones.
- Where the premise that each member is more likely than not to be right is supposed to come from, and whether estimating it from the panel's own past verdicts is legitimate or circular.
- Whether the result must hold for a panel of exactly eleven people, or whether an asymptotic claim about panels growing without bound suffices for our purpose.
- Our members do not decide in isolation. They read the same incident report, several have worked together for a decade, and the four students may simply defer to the teachers. What independence assumption the result needs, and whether it survives that degree of overlap.
- Our procedure is staged: the panel votes separately on three questions of fact — whether the student committed the act, whether the act violated a rule the student had been given, and whether the student understood that it did — and the penalty follows mechanically from the combination of answers. Must a justification of majority rule say something about that structure, or is it only about a single yes-or-no question?
- The costs are asymmetric: wrongly expelling a student is far worse than wrongly clearing one. Can a reliability result that treats the two errors alike license a decision about panel size?
- Whether members vote for the verdict they believe correct or for the verdict they expect to prevail.
- Whether there must be a fact of the matter about which verdict is correct, prior to and independent of the panel's deciding it, for this whole framework to apply at all.

Then tell me which single requirement, if I insist on it, eliminates the largest number of otherwise attractive results. Flag any pair of requirements you believe cannot both be satisfied, and in each such case say whether that means the project of justifying the larger panel fails outright, or whether one of the two was never a real requirement to begin with. Finally, and briefly, indicate how the plain theorem and two or three of the principal ways of repairing it fare against the list — a few sentences each, since the list itself is what I need for the write-up.

Please lay the reasoning out in full rather than directing me to sources; I want to be able to check every step on my own. A table is acceptable for the requirements, provided the motivating case for each is included.
