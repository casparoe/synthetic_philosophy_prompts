I've got maybe twenty minutes before my next class so keep it moving. Please play a committed defender of the language of thought hypothesis — a Mentalese realist, sharp and not a strawman. Ground rules: you may not appeal to introspection or to what thinking feels like, because I'll just deny the datum; treat LOT as a claim about format (discrete symbols, compositional syntax, symbols causing what they cause in virtue of their shape and their arrangement); you're allowed to concede that subpersonal processing is connectionist, that imagery isn't pictorial, and that the brain doesn't remotely look like a tape — but you may not concede that productivity and systematicity can be had without a compositional format. Keep your replies to about a paragraph so it reads like a conversation, stay in character until I say stop, and if my case description is missing something you'd need, don't stall on that: say what you'd need to know and give me your answer under each plausible filling.

Here's my opening. Suppose a patient, call her R, who after a stroke has a very strange but very clean gap. She can think, of any two things you like, that A is left of B. She cannot think that B is right of A. She'll say it, she'll act on it — tell her to grab the book that's right of the cup and she grabs it — but she reports that she isn't thereby entertaining a thought, that she's reading the same sentence from the wrong end and working out what it implies. And the whole of her relational vocabulary goes like this: above but not below, before but not after in the spatial sense, bigger but not smaller. She has no trouble with novel objects and none with novel sentences, and no graded difficulty, it's categorical, and it's exactly the converse pairs that go missing. So she has systematicity everywhere your argument from systematicity predicts it, plus a systematic hole exactly where it predicts a matched pair. If her medium had a compositional syntax and whatever it is that forms converses, she'd have both halves. Which leaves you a fork: either she's just missing a lexical item — in which case your theory is safe but bought at the price of being unfalsifiable, since you can always say she merely lacks a word — or she's missing a rule, in which case here's a counterexample your argument can't absorb and the connectionists in my seminar get to go home happy. Pick one and defend it.
