I've followed the responsibility-gap debate for years now — the argument that when an autonomous vehicle or weapon system kills someone, no agent is left who can coherently be blamed — but the pattern I keep running into sits on the other side of that argument, and I don't have a word for it.

The standard answer to the gap objection is to build more accountability machinery: a named human supervisor, an audit log, a signed safety case, an ethics review board, an officer formally designated as accountable. Then, incident by incident, that machinery gets cited not to locate blame but to release each participant from it. The supervisor notes that the log shows she escalated within the specified window; the engineer notes that the safety case was reviewed and approved; the reviewer notes that she worked the checklist without deviation; the executive notes that the process he installed functioned exactly as designed. Every appeal is legitimate on its own terms. Together they leave a death fully processed and entirely unowned, and the gap has not closed — it has been relocated into the apparatus that was supposed to close it.

I know the neighbouring concepts, and none of them seem to be it. A moral crumple zone absorbs blame onto one designated human so the technical system escapes; here the procedure absorbs it, not the person. The normalization of deviance describes standards quietly drifting; here the standards are being satisfied, and that is precisely the trouble. Decoupling, as institutional sociology uses the term, implies that the formal structure is window-dressing; here it is sincere, competently run, and doing what it was built for.

So: is there an established term for a compliance regime whose blame-blocking effect grows with how faithfully it is followed? If there is, give it to me and say where my cases fit it and where they strain it. If there isn't, coin one, define it tightly enough that a given case could be tested against the definition, and mark its boundary cases — above all whether such a regime can ever be benign, since some of these procedures do reduce harm even when they assign it to nobody.

I want a committed answer rather than a survey of candidates: tell me which single term or coinage you would defend and why, and flag anywhere you think I have over-described the pattern or built the phenomenon out of my own intuitions about it. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning — no preamble, and no restatement of my question.
