I am about to circulate a short statement of my own view on knowledge for a graduate seminar, and I would like a rigorous consistency audit of the package before I do. The view is a tracking account of the Nozickian kind, with two modifications of my own. My commitments are these:

1. **Analysis.** S knows that p only if p is true, S believes that p, and two subjunctive conditions hold relative to the method M by which S came to believe p: *variation* — if p were false, S would not have believed p via M; and *adherence* — in nearby worlds in which p is true and S is in a position to employ M, S believes p via M. I take these four conditions to be jointly necessary and jointly sufficient.

2. **Constraint on methods.** M may not be individuated by reference to the content of the belief it produces, nor by reference to the truth of that content. Vision, memory, testimony, random-sample polling, and deductive proof are admissible methods; seeing that there is a barn, or inferring from a sample that happens to be representative, are not. My reason is that otherwise sensitivity can be purchased for any belief whatever by describing M narrowly enough, and the analysis stops saying anything. The tracking view already relativizes the subjunctive conditions to method; my constraint is meant to say how M may legitimately be chosen, so I regard it as a repair rather than a departure.

3. **Closure.** I deny that knowledge is closed under known competent deduction. Specifically, I accept all of the following: I know that I have hands; I know that if I have hands then I am not a handless brain in a vat; I can and do competently deduce from these that I am not a handless brain in a vat; and nevertheless I do not know that I am not a handless brain in a vat. I hold this conjunction to be a substantive discovery about knowledge rather than a symptom of indexicality in the verb.

4. **Inductive knowledge.** The account is supposed to cover ordinary statistical and inductive knowledge without special pleading. Two cases I am attached to: a pollster who samples 2,000 likely voters, finds the incumbent ahead 51 to 49, and concludes the incumbent will win — and the incumbent does win, 50.9 to 49.1; and a trialist who concludes from a large randomized trial that a drug lowers five-year mortality from 12.4 percent to 11.6 percent. I hold that both of them know.

5. **Uniformity.** The same four conditions govern knowledge of necessary truths, with no supplementary clause. That variation is vacuous when p is necessary strikes me as no objection at all: a condition that is trivially satisfied is still satisfied. I also hold that a mathematician who believes a theorem she has personally checked knows it.

6. **Invariantism.** The truth conditions of knowledge attributions do not shift with the attributor's context, and I reject pragmatic encroachment: what is at stake for S has no bearing on whether S knows.

7. **The barn verdict.** In fake-barn county, Henry, looking at the one real barn, does not know that it is a barn, and my account delivers that verdict straight from (1) and (2), with vision as the method and no further machinery. This is the case I most want the view to get right.

What I want from you: identify which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, and rank them by severity rather than treating every friction as equally damaging. For each genuine tension, give the argument that generates it, say which commitment has to give, and describe the cheapest available revision together with what it costs — including whether it endangers (7). Then identify the pairs that only look like tensions and explain why they dissolve. Finally, tell me whether any single revision would repair the largest portion of the damage, or whether no single change suffices.

Two colleagues have already predicted problems, and I think both are wrong. One says (2) contradicts (1) on the grounds that vision and memory are themselves content-involving descriptions, so every method specification smuggles in content. The other says (3) is stable only if I quietly adopt contextualism. Tell me whether they are right; I would rather be corrected than reassured.

Please do not pad the audit — I want discrimination, not a catalogue, and no bibliography; give me the arguments rather than the literature. Where your verdict depends on a contestable way of evaluating the relevant counterfactuals or on a particular reading of adherence, say so explicitly. State how confident you are in each of your principal verdicts, and single out the one point in the whole audit on which you are least sure.
