Twelve years in an amateur theatre company in a town of about 8,000 people — I've been in something like twenty shows and im directing this spring. Over the holidays a friend who's doing a philosophy PhD put me onto Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert on the theatre, and I haven't been the same since, because it reads like he watched our company from the wings. We're doing A Midsummer Night's Dream. So: can you read that play, and especially the Mechanicals and their Pyramus and Thisbe, through the Letter?

His charges seem to fit uncomfortably well — that theatre doesn't purge the passions so much as feed them and reinforce whatever's already in a community, that it trades entirely on amour-propre, that it hands you a feeling of pity you get credit for without ever having to act on it. Does the play-within-a-play dramatize that? Bottom's crew are precisely the sort of ordinary people Rousseau's festival would hand a stage to, except here the court spends the whole performance laughing at them and Bottom is delighted to be laughed at. So is Shakespeare showing the corruption thesis at work, or is the Mechanicals' play something Rousseau's categories can't hold — closer to the festival he wants than to the theatre he's attacking? And what do you do with the ending: Puck sweeps the whole thing away as a dream, which either concedes his point that the pleasure was only ever in appearance or refuses his whole frame. I'd like you to pick a side there and defend it.

Be honest both ways, including where his argument is simply wrong about plays rather than just uncomfortable, and end by saying what the play adds to the debate that his argument alone doesn't get at. Not looking for a roast of my hobby or a defence of it — I just want to know whether to keep doing this or whether Rousseau is right that its a waste of everyone's evenings.
