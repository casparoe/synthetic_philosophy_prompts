I keep running into the same move in charity emails, political campaigns and sales training and I can't find a word for it: the speaker openly announces the device they're about to use on you, and the announcement is what does the work. A fundraiser that opens "this email is built around one child's photo because numbers don't move people — so here's a photo, and here's why you should distrust it." A candidate who says the other side is going to run attack ads about his divorce and honestly they're not wrong. A sales coach teaching you to say out loud "I'm about to anchor you with a big number, bear with me." Each time the target's suspicion gets neutralized by being acknowledged, so the technique lands better then it would have if it stayed hidden. I know about the inoculation stuff, where warning people about a manipulation makes them resist it — this looks like the reverse of that. Is there an established concept for the disclosure itself being the manipulation? The label isn't really the point, I want to know where the edges are. Answer as exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion: the first four should each be a nearby concept and say precisely how my case differs from it, and the fifth should be your verdict — either the real name, or, if there isn't one, a coined term with a definition and the boundary cases it excludes.
