I have spent about a decade building backend systems organized around event streams — ingest, partition, fan out — and my working vocabulary is the vocabulary of that job: append-only logs, partitions and their per-partition ordering, consumers and consumer groups, offsets, snapshots, compaction, vector clocks, read-your-writes versus eventual consistency, backfill, and the ordinary engineering distinction between an event that happened and an event that some particular reader has seen. The system I own now ingests telemetry from a delivery fleet whose vehicles report out of order, so two downstream services can hold genuinely different and equally defensible answers about where a given truck is right now, and there is no component whose job is to reconcile them into one official answer. I have no formal philosophy training beyond what I have picked up in the margins of other people's work, but I recently read through Dennett's attack on the Cartesian theater and his proposed alternative, the multiple drafts model — the 1991 book and the 1992 paper he wrote with Kinsbourne — together with the apparent-motion material he leans on, in which a dot seems to change color partway along a path it never actually travelled. I could not shake the feeling that the whole dispute is an architecture argument being conducted in a language I do not have, and that I would grasp it much better if someone would put it in mine.

So please restate the position in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can manage. I want the theater rendered as a system design: what exactly plays the role of the place where it all comes together, and why a single consumer that joins every stream is objectionable in the specific way Dennett thinks it is rather than merely inefficient or hard to scale. I want the multiple drafts model rendered as well: many parallel processors, none of them privileged, and the further claim that there is no single moment at which some draft stops being provisional and becomes the published version. Then the harder pieces. Translate his two styles of post-hoc revision into log terms — my guess is that one of them amounts to rewriting history so that later readers encounter a consistent record, and the other to emitting an output for which there was never a corresponding input event — and tell me whether that guess is right or whether I have already distorted it. Translate content fixation: when does something count as settled rather than still in flight, and does my notion of an offset or a snapshot boundary carry the same weight as his? Translate probing, where a system is asked a question by someone who was not already subscribed to the answer. And translate the most radical claim, that in some cases there is no determinate fact about the temporal order in which two contents became available — which I would express by saying that we have no global clock, only per-partition order and vector timestamps that are sometimes simply incomparable.

The part I actually care about is the residue report. For each item above, tell me whether it translates cleanly, whether it translates only with a distortion I should be warned about, or whether there is nothing at all in my vocabulary to receive it; and where there is a gap, say whether that gap is a limit of the engineering picture or a sign that the philosophical claim was less clear than it looked. I ask because of a disagreement on my team. After I described the theater to a colleague, his view was that Dennett is simply redescribing eventual consistency — that serious engineers abandoned any single point where it all comes together years ago, so the theater is refuted by practice rather than by argument, and whatever philosophers continue to worry about past that point is a failure to let go of the old picture. I want to know whether that conclusion survives your translation or whether it was being carried by it all along. In particular, I suspect my instinct to reach for an append-only log with a well-defined global order may be quietly reinstalling the very thing Dennett is trying to deny, so that the translation looks tidy only because I have smuggled the theater back in as a source of truth. Please keep the translation honest on that score: wherever you have to stretch my terms, mark it as a stretch instead of presenting it as what I meant.
