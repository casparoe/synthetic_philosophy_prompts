I'm the student rep on my university's lab safety panel — I'm taking a philosophy of AI course this term so everyone assumes I know what I'm doing, which I don't. Next week we vote on a request from the chem dept to run an autonomous tidying robot in the second-year teaching labs overnight. The vendor's design is: the robot carries a list of about 12,000 objects and situations it's forbidden to touch or move, and anything not on the list it's allowed to bin or relocate; when it's unsure it just stops where it is and waits for a human. The list was built from last year's lab inventory, so plenty of current equipment isn't on it at all. Two professors on the panel think this is ordinary engineering and we should approve it with a trial period, one thinks it's a disaster waiting to happen because no such list can ever be complete, and I keep feeling like this is the frame problem wearing a lab coat — though I genuinely cant tell whether that's a real objection or a philosophy student showing off.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. Take a position — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — and make the bottom line something the panel could actually act on: what the conditions are, who checks them, over what period. Engage seriously with the strongest case for the side you're arguing against, including whether "stop and wait" really escapes the problem or just relocates it, since standing still in a doorway has consequences too. And be honest about how much of my frame-problem intuition survives contact with the fact that people build working robots all the time.
