I have come to hold a fairly specific version of constitutive panpsychism, and I want to know what would count against it. The view, stated as precisely as I can: microphysical entities have intrinsic natures that are simple phenomenal qualities; these are joined by a phenomenal bonding relation that is not classical mereological composition; and out of bonded micro-subjects there arises a macro-subject — a person such as myself — numerically distinct from the bonded subjects that ground it. I take this to be the cheapest way of reconciling two things: that physics characterises matter only structurally and relationally, and that there is something it is like to be anyone. Please engage with it straight rather than dismissively, and sort the defeaters by how exposed they leave me, from fatal to merely awkward. In particular I want to know which of the combination problems — the unity of the subject, the palette of qualities available to a human being, the structure of experience — my version genuinely answers as opposed to relocating one level up; what work the bonding relation is doing that mereological composition was not already doing, and whether, if the answer is 'none', my position is simply panpsychism with an extra primitive attached; what a cosmopsychist would say I have already conceded in positing that relation; and whether my micro-phenomenal properties explain anything that a rival account — intrinsic natures that are protophenomenal, or non-experiential all the way down — could not explain just as well, since that is what the view has over its rivals and I cannot at present point to it. Then, because physics is causally closed and these qualities are supposed to do no causal work of their own, give me an honest verdict on whether the view risks anything at all, or whether it has been qualified into a position that no observation could ever tell against — and if so, whether that is fatal for a thesis of this kind or merely the price of its being a claim about intrinsic natures. End by telling me which single objection you would press hardest if I were your student, and what you take the strongest reply to it to be.

Write the whole of this as continuous prose, in the manner of a letter rather than a report: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
