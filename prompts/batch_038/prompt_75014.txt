I'm in a decision theory seminar this term on ambiguity — credal sets instead of single priors — and our professor flat out refuses to say which decision rule for imprecise credences she endorses. She just gives verdicts on the problem-set menus and expects us to reverse-engineer it. I've been keeping a list and I want to predict next week's problem before I walk in.

Her verdicts so far. In every case her credal set is exactly what the problem states, she never sharpens it:

1. Urn, 90 balls: 30 red, the other 60 either black or yellow with the split unknown. Menu: R pays $100 if red, B pays $100 if black. She said R is required and B is ruled out — and she was explicit that this isn't just "personally I'd take R," taking B is a mistake. She added that if you had real evidence about how the urn got filled that pinned the split down, that's a different problem and the verdict could change.

2. A supplier's component passes inspection with probability somewhere in [0.2, 0.8], the spec sheet gives you the interval and nothing else. Menu: A pays $12 for sure, B pays $30 if it passes and $0 if it fails. She said A is required, B ruled out. Her comment was that the gamble's ceiling doesn't even enter into it, what matters is the floor.

3. Same component, same interval. Menu: C pays $25 if it passes and $10 if it fails, D pays $12 for sure. She said C is required and D is ruled out. So she took the ambiguous one there — her point being that it's about floors and not about certainty.

4. Urn, 100 balls, black or white, split completely unknown, so her credal set is every possible split. Menu: E pays $30 if black and $0 if white, F pays $0 if black and $30 if white. She said both are permissible, neither choice is criticisable, there's just no fact about which colour is favoured.

She's also said more than once that you're not allowed to select one probability function out of the set to make the decision well behaved — she calls that "pretending to know what you don't" — and she's totally fine with the set spreading out when you get evidence that's ambiguous about the payoff-relevant state, she thinks that's a feature.

Next week's menu is the same completely-unknown black-or-white urn, but with three options: Plus pays $40 if black and $10 if white; Flip pays $10 if black and $45 if white; Swing pays $8 if black and $60 if white.

So what does she say — which of the three does she rule out, if any, and is anything required?
