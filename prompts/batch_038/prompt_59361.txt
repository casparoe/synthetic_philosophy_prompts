I am assembling a seminar on the taxonomy of liberty, and before I set the reading I want to discipline a judgment I suspect I have been making on aesthetic grounds rather than argumentative ones. The judgment concerns republican freedom.

Let me fix the terrain as I will fix it for the students. On the Berlinian picture, negative liberty is the absence of interference by other agents with what one might otherwise do, and positive liberty is self-direction or self-mastery. Pettit and Skinner maintain that a third thing is available: freedom as non-domination, which is neither. Their case rests on two families of example. First, one can be dominated by an arbitrary power that never in fact interferes, as with the well-treated slave of an indulgent master, so that non-interference is insufficient for freedom. Second, one can be interfered with by a process that is not arbitrary, because it tracks the interests and opinions of those subject to it and they can contest its deliverances, as with the citizen under a law she helped to author, so that interference is not necessary for unfreedom. The rejoinder from negative libertarians of the Carter and Kramer sort is that neither family reveals a new concept, only a neglected parameter: unexercised arbitrary power lowers the probability that one's options will in fact be available, and a probability-weighted measure of negative liberty already ranks the slave below the free person; while the second family shows only that Pettit has imported a normative thesis about which interferences are legitimate into the analysis of a concept that was supposed to be normatively thin.

I propose to settle the matter by a criterion I want treated as binding for the purposes of your answer, since it is meant to be neutral between the parties. Two putative conceptions of liberty are genuinely distinct only if there is some class of logically possible social arrangements on which they yield opposing ordinal rankings of freedom, and no monotone re-parameterisation of one conception's metric reproduces the other's rankings across the whole class — where the admissible re-parameterisations include re-weighting by the probability of interference, substituting a modal or resilience measure for a probabilistic one, restricting or enlarging the set of relevant obstacles, and restricting the set of relevant agents.

Give me your credence that freedom as non-domination satisfies this criterion relative to Berlinian negative liberty. I want a number rather than a survey of the literature, and I want the derivation. Decompose it into the sub-questions on which the number depends, with a probability and one or two sentences of justification for each, and then show how they combine. I would expect at least the following terms, though you may add, merge, or reject any of them with a reason: whether conceptual distinctness is a matter of divergence in verdicts across cases at all, or of divergence in the structure of the property being measured, and how much the criterion above already begs that question; whether the probability-weighting reply genuinely absorbs the slave case, or whether domination carries irreducibly modal content that no probability assignment over interference events can express; whether the arbitrariness clause does independent analytical work, or is a relabelling of a substantive judgment about legitimacy; and whether the two conceptions also diverge in the opposite direction, on arrangements where a pervasive unaccountable power is held by no particular agent and so cannot be attached to any interferer. Say which term your final number is most sensitive to, and what would shift it.

Two closing requests. My own credence is 0.62 and that of a colleague who works on measurement in political theory is 0.30; identify the single term in the decomposition that most plausibly accounts for the gap between us, and say why you assign it there rather than elsewhere. And state what consideration — an argument or a newly constructed case, not further empirical evidence — would move your own number furthest, and in which direction.
