Her index takes a list of standard life-choices — schooling, healthcare, childcare, eldercare, buying a bottle of wine, joining or leaving a church, workplace representation, retirement saving — and counts how many of them a person can make without routing the choice through a state-run or state-mandated channel. She calls the result the permission-free option set, and she reports it comes out higher for Sweden than for Norway.

Write me exactly five interview questions, numbered, no intro and no conclusion, one or two sentences each — each item is the question plus the follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. I want one on whether the index is really just measuring market thickness, and if so what it makes of the US; one on the baseline problem (permission-free relative to *what* — a state that provides nothing?); and one on a Swedish voucher school, where the choice looks private but the money and the admissions rules are public. Fill the other two yourself, but they have to bite on her actual claims, not on Scandinavia in general. Nothing like "what drew you to the Nordics?"
