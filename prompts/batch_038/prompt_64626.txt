I spent forty years in a materials lab and only got into philosophy properly after I retired, so forgive the way I frame things. I've been working through the Frege–Geach problem for expressivism, and I keep thinking of the rival fixes the way I'd think of competing models fitted to the same data set: each one buys us the inference patterns we want, and each one pays for them with new machinery — extra attitude types, attitudes toward attitudes, extra clauses bolted onto the semantics. So I'd like you to score the main candidates on something like a parsimony-adjusted coverage ratio rather than just telling me what each view says.

Take the usual suspects: Blackburn's higher-order attitudes and his logic of commitments, Gibbard's early norm-expressivism, Gibbard's later planning semantics, Schroeder's being-for account, and Ridge's hybrid. For each, first give me what fraction of the embedding cases it actually gets right, counting the cases as: simple conditionals, negation, disjunction, quantifiers over moral sentences, reports of other people's attitudes, and modals — so out of 1.0, splitting the difference where the story is a mess or just never gets told. Then give me a rough number for how much machinery it pays to get that. Then combine those into a single 0–10 score and drop each view into a tier, best to worst. The ranking matters more to me than the individual numbers, so if two are basically tied say so rather then inventing a gap, and tell me if you think one of the five should come off the list altogether for failing on something structural rather than on details.

Write it back to me as continuous prose, like an email — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just work down the list in sentences with the numbers inline. And don't point me at the literature; I'm not going to chase references, I want your judgment.
