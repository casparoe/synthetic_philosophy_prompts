I moderate a fairly big politics forum and there's a move I've watched show up in threads for years that I've never been able to name. Someone from the other side makes a genuinely good point — careful, caveated, actually cites stuff, concedes a thing or two. And the response from our regulars isn't "here's the flaw in the reasoning." It's about the argument's finish. Like: notice how polished that is, nobody writes that well by accident, that's think-tank copy. Or: only someone getting paid would bother being this evenhanded. The competence is the tell. Meanwhile a sloppy, half-incoherent version of the same position gets engaged with more generously, because at least that guy is obviously just some dude being honest about what he thinks.

Second example, different setting. I'm in a reading group, somebody forwards an article that argues against a position everyone in the room holds, and two people say roughly the same thing: the fact that it's so well-organized is itself suspicious, a real person working this out for themselves wouldn't have it that tidy. Nobody checked whether the reasoning held up.

So the pattern is: perceived quality of the argument gets read as evidence of bad faith, it moves your credence down instead of up, and the worse the argument the more sincere it looks.

Does this have a name? I can guess at some neighbours and I'm pretty sure none of them are it. It's not just assuming someone's wrong and then explaining their belief by their motives — that's a much broader habit and it doesn't need the polish premise. It's not the thing where you stop trusting arguments after getting burned by bad ones, because here its triggered by a good one, which is backwards. It isn't the trap where any denial counts as further proof of guilt, and it isn't ordinary partisan motivated reasoning either, since that can run without any claim about how well-written the other side is.

If nothing fits, coin something and define it properly — but I care about the carving way more than the label. Specifically: where does it stop being a failure mode and start being justified suspicion, given that PR firms and astroturf campaigns are real and produce exactly this sheen? What would separate a correct detection from this thing? And does it need an in-group/out-group structure to work, or can you pull it on your own side?
