I'm TA-ing a philosophy of social science seminar and I want to hand out a decision tree called something like "do you actually think generations are real?" The whole point is to sort the students by what they're committed to, not by which label they picked up online, so no node can be answerable by someone who already knows what leaf they're aiming at.

Start from a claim basically everyone in the room accepts — that people born in the same stretch of years score differently on some measures than people born twenty years earlier — and branch from there. I want the age-period-cohort identification problem to be a genuine node and not decoration: is the similarity caused by sharing a historical location, or is it just what falls out once you've averaged over age and period effects? Then things like: are the boundaries discovered or drawn, is a generation a kind of person or a kind of timeslice, does it have to be named and self-recognised before it exists (Mannheim's shared-location-in-history idea is one answer, not the only one), and does a Strauss-and-Howe style cyclic theory need anything extra ontologically over the plain cohort claim.

Leaves I need: eliminativism (generations as sampling artifacts), cohort realism without generations, social-construction realism, and whatever you think belongs in the corners I've missed. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't seperate, and tell me which cells of the space nobody occupies and why — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Eight to twelve nodes, one plain-language question each, jargon confined to the leaf labels.
