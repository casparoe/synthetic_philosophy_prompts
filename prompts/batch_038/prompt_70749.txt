The German partner's press office has pushed back hard, saying that in a German newspaper the adjective reads as market-radical, so the centre will look like a lobbying outfit for deregulation, and that no reader will hear what we intend. One of my collegues has a line of argument I keep coming back to, and I'd like the memo to engage with it head on rather than around it. She thinks the word has three quite different jobs that we're muddling together: it can name a doctrine with a reasonably stable content; it can mark a position relative to wherever the local centre of political gravity happens to sit; and it can function as a term of praise or abuse. Her claim is that in America the positional job has swallowed the doctrinal one, while in Germany and the Netherlands the doctrinal job — in a specifically free-market reading — has swallowed the positional one, so that speakers of the same word are not even disagreeing with each other but answering different questions. She says you can't translate a term like that, only re-index it for each audience, and that a name which has to be re-indexed isn't really a name at all.
