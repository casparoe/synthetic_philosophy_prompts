I have been assembling a dilemma about the eternal recurrence that I now suspect proves too much, and I would like a diagnosis rather than encouragement. The argument, as I currently have it:

1. Suppose, for reductio, that recurrence is a genuine possibility: this life, exactly as I have lived it, recurs innumerable times, down to every detail.
2. Two readings of "exactly as I have lived it" exhaust the options. Either the recurrence is merely qualitative — an indiscernible duplicate of my life, numerically other than the original — or it is numerical, so that one and the same life and one and the same person occur again.
3. If the recurrence is merely qualitative, then it is not *my* life that comes back, only an indistinguishable counterpart of it. On that reading the hypothesis concerns a universe that contains endless repetitions of a life like mine, which is not what the thought experiment is supposed to be about, and the reading is therefore unavailable to anyone using recurrence to ask how I ought to stand toward my own existence.
4. If the recurrence is numerical, then there is no fact detectable even in principle by anyone inside a cycle as to whether this is the first occurrence, the millionth, or the millionth-plus-one: every instrument, memory, record, and observer state recurs identically too, so no observation could distinguish one cycle from another or a single non-recurring universe from an endlessly recurring one.
5. A difference no observation could ever detect, even in principle, is not a difference in the world.
6. So on the numerical reading there is no difference between endless recurrence and a single unrepeated life. The doctrine states something that nothing could make true or false, and the thought experiment it animates loses whatever content gave it force.
7. Therefore recurrence is not a genuine possibility: either the reading that would supply content is unavailable (by 3) or the available reading supplies none (by 4–6).

The stakes, as I see them. The passage in *The Gay Science* where a demon creeps into your loneliest loneliness and tells you that you will have to live this life again and innumerable times more, with nothing new in it, is usually treated as an existential test rather than a cosmological claim, while the notebooks compiled posthumously press it as something closer to a hypothesis about force and finite configurations. Much of the secondary debate is about which of these is primary and whether they can be held together. My argument, if it goes through, forecloses the debate in a way that strikes me as too tidy: it says neither reading can do independent work, because the existential one collapses into the cosmological one and the cosmological one evaporates.

Here is where I need help. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I am most attached to (5), since giving it up seems to leave me committed to a raft of distinctions I would rather not defend — a universe shifted five meters overnight, a global velocity boost, a gauge transformation, a branching that leaves every branch qualitatively unchanged. But I also feel the pull of the worry that (5) is a verificationist principle wearing metaphysical clothing, and if it is, I would like to know what I can say about recurrence without simply changing the subject to epistemology. Please also tell me whether (2) really does exhaust the options — whether there is a third reading on which numerical recurrence is both non-trivial and not merely a duplicate — and, separately, whether the argument has a genuine flaw I have not located or is valid but for a premise I should have priced in advance. Say explicitly which of these two you think it is; do not soften the choice by telling me the argument is suggestive either way.
