Longtime reader of the sequences and the comment threads, not an academic, so bear with me. There's a shape of disagreement I keep hitting in the how-many-minds-are-in-there debates and I don't have a word for it.

Concretely: two people are arguing about a split-brain patient. They agree on every single observable and every scan — what the left hand does, what the mouth reports, which hemisphere got shown the picture, what happens when you cue each side separately. They also aren't smuggling in private definitions; ask them what they mean by "subject of experience" and they give you the same gloss. And yet one says there are two subjects in that skull right now and the other says there's one, and each says the other is mistaken about the world, not about words. The kicker: neither one will accept that it's a fuzzy or borderline case. They don't say the concept runs out here. They each say there's a definite fact and the other guy has it backwards. So you've got a disagreement that no observation could ever settle, between people who are already in full observational agreement, who agree on meanings, and who both refuse the "it's vague" escape hatch — while insisting it's substantive.

Same shape comes up with gradual neuron replacement: everyone agrees on all the functional facts at every step, and they still fight about one stream versus two, and nobody concedes borderlinehood.

So: is there an established concept for that exact configuration? I know the reflexive answer is "it's just a verbal dispute," but that's precisely what both sides deny, and I don't think the denial is empty here. Tell me what the nearest existing ideas are, what each one gets wrong about this case, and which comes closest. Does whatever you land on also cover the replacement version, or is there something special about the consciousness case? And if nothing really fits, coin me a term with an actual definition plus one case that sits just inside the boundary and one just outside. I'm not after a reading list, I'm after the carving — and while you're at it, say whether you think one of these two arguers is making a mistake or whether they're both right that there's a fact of the matter.
