I am a pricing actuary in personal lines — auto and homeowners — with some earlier experience in disability underwriting. I have no philosophy training. I want you to restate luck egalitarianism in the vocabulary I actually work in: exposure, peril, rating factors, adverse selection, moral hazard, deductibles and coverage elections, reserves, community rating versus experience rating, and the category of losses we are permitted to classify on versus those we are forbidden to classify on.

The position to be translated is this: an inequality is unjust when and only when it traces to brute luck rather than to the person's own choices; brute misfortune should be pooled across the group, while the consequences of deliberately assumed risk should stay with the person who took it. Translate that as faithfully as you can, then give me a residue report under three headings: claims that carry over cleanly, claims that carry over only with distortion (say exactly what gets distorted), and claims that have no expression at all in my vocabulary.

Two specific questions I want answered inside that report.
