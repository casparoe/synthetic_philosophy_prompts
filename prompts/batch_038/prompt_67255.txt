so my country just had a referendum and the winning side got 53%, and now the campaign that won keeps saying stuff like "the people have spoken" and that everyone who voted the other way was never really part of the people anyway. I keep hearing academics call that populist and anti-pluralist, but I honestly don't follow what the objection is supposed to be. 53% is a majority, and in a straight up-or-down vote the winning option is by definition the one that beats the alternative head to head — so it's the Condorcet winner. There isn't some other, truer "will of the people" being trampled on, the majority option just is the majority option. And if the winners go on to say that only they speak for that majority, that's an accurate description of who won, not some weird metaphysical claim?

So what exactly are the pluralists objecting to here that isn't just disapointment about the result — is there a real conceptual distinction between "we won a majority of the vote" and "we alone represent the people," or is anti-pluralism basically a fancy term for being on the losing side? btw I'm not asking whether I like the outcome, I voted against it, I just want to know whether the criticism has any content beyond that.
