So I started a new job about two months ago on a team that does evals and behavior work at a mid-size AI lab, and last week someone forwarded around a long magazine piece about the Chinese Room — some anniversary of something — and it blew up in our group chat because half my team is convinced the whole "does the model actually want anything" framing is nonsense. Here's the paragraph that everyone's arguing about, roughly as it appeared:

"Searle's Chinese Room asks you to imagine a man locked in a room who follows a rule book to shuffle Chinese symbols in and out, so that the people outside get fluent answers from someone who understands not one word of the language — which proves that running a program is never by itself enough for genuine understanding. Dennett's comeback is his vending machine: a coin detector wired to register quarters will equally pass the physically identical coin used in whatever country the machine gets shipped off to, and since nothing inside the wiring changes when it moves, nothing inside the machine can make its state mean quarter rather than the other thing. Meaning, in other words, sits in the eye of whoever is using the device — this is Dennett's version of multiple realizability, the same belief built out of neurons or out of silicon, so Searle's appeal to biology is just prejudice. Crucially, Dennett keeps Searle's split between original and derived intentionality but argues that a machine can be promoted from the second to the first once it acquires the right causal history. The upshot is that there's no fact of the matter about what any system, human or artificial, really believes or wants, which is why researchers hunting for 'deception' or 'scheming' in language models are mostly measuring their own interpretive habits rather than anything in the model."

Some of that feels right to me and some of it makes my stomach turn, mostly because I've also come across Dennett insisting that beliefs are real and not just convenient fictions, which looks flatly incompatible with that "no fact of the matter" ending — but maybe I'm misremembering him, or maybe he changed his mind later, and I genuinely can't tell which.
