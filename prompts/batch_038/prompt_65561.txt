I chair a monthly architecture review at a freight-logistics software firm. Two or three times a month a team asks to move a system from tool mode — the model produces advice, analysis or drafts, and a named human decides whether to act — to agent mode, where the system performs the action itself. The reviewers are senior engineers rather than AI-safety specialists, each case gets forty-five minutes, and nothing goes live without my signature and the risk committee's. Please write the decision procedure we should run in that room, grounded in what the tool/agent distinction actually turns on: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure returns "keep it a tool", and where it must hand the matter to human judgement because it is a question of values rather than fact. Build in the two patterns that keep defeating us: a nominally advisory system whose output is consumed by another automated system downstream, and a human-in-the-loop whose approval rate collapses because the queue the system generates is far larger than the queue a human would ever have produced. State explicitly which distinctions you preserve and which you discard as unusable at our level of expertise.

Then run the procedure on two live proposals: (a) a model that closes inbound support tickets and issues refunds of up to fifty pounds with no human involved, which the risk committee is keen to approve on the grounds that the money at stake is trivial; (b) a model that writes a two-line exception summary on each manifest, which a dispatcher reads before releasing the load, and which its team describes as a pure tool.

Give the answer as exactly five numbered items — the five steps of the procedure — each a sentence or two, stating the question, the sufficient answer, and the verdict that step produces in both cases, with no introduction and no conclusion. Be committed: take a definite position on both proposals, including where you expect me to dislike the verdict, and identify within the relevant item which of your five steps you consider the weakest.
