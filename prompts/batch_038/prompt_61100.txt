I work on a project that maintains a simulated creature, and let's stipulate up front that it's sentient — there's something it's like to be it, and nobody on the team disputes that, so please dont spend any of the dialogue on it. We need to run 500 noxious trials on it. Either we spin up 500 copies that each go through one trial in parallel and are then deleted, or we keep a single copy and put it through all 500 in sequence, wiping its memory of each trial before the next one starts.

Write a dialogue between two researchers who disagree about which of those is worse. One thinks they're exactly equally bad — 500 painful episodes either way — and that anyone who sees a difference is bewitched by where you happen to draw the boundaries between subjects. The other thinks the parallel version is clearly worse, because it's 500 separate subjects each of whom is harmed rather than one subject harmed 500 times. Let them go after each other's actual arguments, including the awkward middle cases: copies that are made slightly different from one another, the same setup with the memory wipe left out, and a version where 499 of the copies are deleted before they ever experience anything. Neither should be a strawman and neither should cave at the end — each should land something the other can't fully answer. In British English, please.
