So next month I'm giving a 20-minute paper at a conference session on early complex societies, then the same thing at a department seminar, and I want to walk into the Q&A with it already thought through. Draft me the prep sheet.

My thesis, roughly: from a single well-excavated assemblage you can sometimes get an *institutional* inference but not a *belief* inference, and the asymmetry is definately principled rather than just me being a functionalist about pots. The case is my site — a mid-2nd-millennium hilltop settlement in the highlands, no texts anywhere in the region, ever. Storage jars, ~4,000 diagnostic sherds and 60-odd near-complete profiles. Capacities cluster hard, CV around 4%, around what looks like a fixed unit; the jars are completely undecorated and made from a single non-local clay recipe. No sealings, no bullae, no seal impressions on anything, no counters, no potters' marks. Floors are sealed, arid, no bioturbation, and we have a decent handle on our excavated fraction of the storage terraces. I ran an expected-count calculation: if this were an administered redistribution node of comparable scale to the ones that do have texts, we should have recovered sealings in the dozens, and we got zero.

So I claim (a) tight capacity clustering + one recipe + an actual denominator on the null result points to some authority that measures and reallocates, and (b) the total absence of iconography tells you nothing at all about what these people believed, because symbolic variation isn't task-constrained the way capacity is, and the depositional argument that licenses (a) doesn't license (b). Different machinery, different burden of proof. That second half is the part I think is interesting and I need it to survive contact.
