For the past two years I've been working on what I take to be a clean case of a norm arising from nothing, and I've built a general thesis on top of it. The case: at a distributed software company of roughly four hundred people, a practice settled in over about eighteen months whereby nobody sends non-urgent written messages between Friday evening and Monday morning. There was no policy, no announcement I can find in any archive, and no visible sanction; deviation is rare enough that when it happens people notice and mildly raise an eyebrow. The practice also looks arbitrary in exactly the sense I need: a quiet period from Wednesday night to Thursday morning, or a norm of no-meeting Mondays, would presumably have solved whatever the underlying problem was equally well, and everyone involved is aware that some such regularity exists and aware that everyone else is aware of it. My thesis has three stages: first, a recurrent interaction with more than one workable equilibrium; second, a focal point plus a few early movers fixing one of those equilibria, sustained thereafter by nothing more than each person's preference for general conformity conditional on general conformity, plus mutual awareness of that preference and of the near-universal compliance; third, the regularity acquiring a moralized narration after the fact, with third parties beginning to express disapproval of deviants — disapproval that was never needed to hold the behavior in place, but that gets attached to it because a group that believes its equilibrium is mandatory coordinates more cheaply than one that merely knows it is mutually convenient. On this picture the "ought" of a norm is a gloss laid over a coordination solution.

I want to know what would have to be true for this to be wrong, and I want the answer sorted by how much damage it does. Start with the case: which facts about the company, if they turned out to obtain, would break my description of it — conformity that is really held in place by reputational fear rather than conditional preference, compliance that is only partial and stable at some middling rate with distinct subpopulations, participants who think the rival quiet period genuinely would not have worked, a regularity whose content tracks an independent reason rather than being arbitrary at all, mutual belief that stops short of anything like common knowledge, an origin I've failed to find in some manager's offhand remark. Say which of these I should go looking for and what finding each would license me to conclude. Then go after the general thesis, which I suspect is the weaker link: what mechanisms produce stable, arbitrary, group-wide regularities without any of my stage-two machinery — cultural transmission that biases learners toward whatever is common, prestige-based imitation, dynamics in which everyone conforms while privately disapproving and no one notices the mismatch, enforcement equilibria in which costly third-party punishment does the work that conditional preference was supposed to do, norms handed down by an authority and internalized rather than converged upon. For each, tell me whether it is merely an alternative story that could also fit my data or one that makes claims mine cannot. I also want the asymmetry: what does the Lewisian picture predict that its rivals do not — about what happens to compliance when mutual awareness degrades but sanctions remain, about whether nearby alternative regularities should be discoverable and functionally equivalent, about the order in which disapproval appears relative to the behavior — and, separately, whether my third stage risks anything at all, since it looks as though it can absorb either outcome: disapproval present, the gloss has taken; disapproval absent, the gloss is thin. Tell me plainly whether, after all the qualifications a defender is entitled to make, what is left of my thesis is capable of being false.

For each item, mark it fatal, seriously damaging, or merely awkward, and distinguish what a committed defender of this framework would concede as counting against them from what they would dismiss as a misreading of what the framework is for — including whether they would retreat to the claim that this is a rational reconstruction of a practice rather than an account of the psychology that sustains it, and whether that retreat costs them anything they wanted. If you think the most dangerous single defeater is one I have not listed, name it. I'd be most moved by a class of real case in which the norm's content is arbitrary and compliance is near-universal, yet compliance demonstrably depends on punishment rather than on any conditional preference for general conformity; tell me whether such cases exist in a form I cannot explain away. I don't want sources, a reading list, or named studies — I want the argumentative structure, and where you are leaning on an empirical claim you are not confident about, describe the claim and flag your uncertainty rather than dressing it up. Finally, commit: take a definite position on whether my thesis survives, in what restricted form if any, and why, rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose among them.
