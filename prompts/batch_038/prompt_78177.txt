A number of the accounts I've been reading describe the outbreak of the First World War as overdetermined: the alliance system, the naval and military build-up, imperial rivalry, Balkan nationalism, and the assassination at Sarajevo are each credited with having been capable of producing the war on its own. Granting that, I'm trying to work out two consequences. First, doesn't overdetermination in this sense entail that the war was inevitable — so that the familiar disputes, over whether Berlin deliberately pressed for a continental war in July 1914 or blundered into one, cannot be disputes about causation at all, but only about which of several sufficient causes happened to fire first? Second, if no single factor made the difference to the outcome, in what sense can a historian still claim to have explained why the war occurred rather than not? Is there a way to keep the label while answering these worries, or does it collapse under them?
