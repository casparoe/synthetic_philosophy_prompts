Ok so I have a hunch that might be a project or might just be a bad pun, and I need to find out which before I write up a proposal for a summer stipend (8 weeks, ~5000 word paper at the end). Turn it into the proposal, but flag it if you think the hunch is actually empty.

The hunch: when people say history bends toward justice, I think they're sliding between three claims that have different evidence and different stakes. One is a trend claim — some metric of how people are treated has been going up over a long stretch. Two is a stability claim — the trend isn't a lucky transient, there's some mechanism that pushes back after setbacks, so the direction would have been roughly the same across a range of different histories. Three is a providence claim — the direction holds whether or not anyone keeps working at it, which is the one that licenses relaxation or quietism. My suspicion is that most of the actual fights in this area (the optimists vs the people who say progress talk is a Whig self-congratulation, the secular-providence readings of Hegel or Marx, the objection that believing in progress makes you passive) are all conducted on claim one while the disagreement is really about two and three, and that nobody has said clearly what would count as evidence for the second one.

What I need the proposal to do:

- State the research question so it has a determinate answer. Not "is there moral progress" — something like whether claim two is a distinct claim at all, or whether it collapses into claim one plus a hopeful attitude.
- Explain why the existing framings leave it open. I know there's a body of work on moral progress as measured trend, and a separate body of work arguing that progress isn't even coherent once you take value pluralism seriously. My read is that both of those are arguing about claim one and never touch two or three — but tell me if that's unfair, since I'd be building on it.
- Give the method and, importantly, why it beats the obvious alternative, which is just going and accumulating more historical evidence about whether things got better. My thought is that claim two is really a modal claim — it's about what happens under pertubation — and that you can make it precise with attractor / basin-of-attraction ideas: an attractor claim says the trajectory returns after being knocked off, a transient says it just happened to be going up while we watched. I want the proposal to take that analogy seriously rather than decoratively, which means it also has to say what breaks: history has no repeated trials, no fixed state space, and the metric is itself partly constituted by what people at the time thought counted. If those problems are fatal, say so and tell me what's left.
- Say what a result looks like after 8 weeks and what would count as failure. I'd genuinely rather the proposal name the failure modes than sound confident, and one thing I want in there is a test for whether I've just relabeled vague optimism in dynamical-systems vocabulary.
- Give an honest scope note on what has to be cut, including whether I should drop claim three entirely or whether the project is worthless without it.
