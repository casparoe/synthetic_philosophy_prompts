Here's what she says, laid out as cleanly as I can manage at 1am:

1. It seems obvious that my pain makes me wince, and that the redness of the sunset makes me say "look at that." Experiences seem to push behavior around.
2. But neuroscience says every twitch and every sentence has physical causes sufficient to produce it. If a quale also caused the same wince, the wince would be causally overdetermined, every single time, systematically.
3. Overdetermination like that isn't a live option, so qualia cause nothing. Epiphenomenalism.
4. If qualia cause nothing, they can't cause my beliefs about them. So my belief that I'm in pain is produced entirely by physical goings-on in my head.
5. Now picture my philosophical zombie — physically identical, no experiences at all. She forms the same beliefs by the same physical route and says the same things, only hers are all false.
6. So when my beliefs about my own experience come out true, they come out true as a matter of a standing coincidence, not because anything about my experience had a hand in producing them.
7. The same goes one level up. I came to accept (3) on the basis of conceivability intuitions and introspective attention to what experience is like — and those very processes would produce an identical conviction in the zombie, where the conviction is false.
8. So if epiphenomenalism is true, my belief in it isn't grounded in experience; and if it's false, then I arrived at it by a route that would have delivered it just as confidently had it been false, so it still isn't grounded in experience.
9. Either way, my belief isn't properly connected to what makes it true.
10. Therefore I shouldn't believe epiphenomenalism — and nobody else should either.
