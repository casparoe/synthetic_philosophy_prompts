I got into a two-hour argument with my friend Dara last night about something I did back in March, and I'm still not settled on it, so I want input from someone who isn't emotionally invested in either of us being right. I do freelance product design. In the spring I was pitching a fintech startup — real money, they'd just closed a seed round, they wanted an app for moving money between accounts. Near the end of the call their founder asked me pretty directly: "have you built something like this before? like an app in this space?" And I said, "I've shipped three apps." True. All three of them. But they were a meditation timer I made for a class and two little utilities for a nonprofit, none of them anywhere near finance, and I knew exactly how she'd hear it, and I picked that wording *because* of how she'd hear it. She said "great," moved on, and we signed a contract. Six weeks in the project went sideways — I'll take my share of the blame on scope, that's not the part I'm asking about — and when she cut me loose she said "you told me you'd done this before." I said I never told her that. She said "you let me believe it."

Two wrinkles. First: two days after the call I emailed her my portfolio and, in the last line of a fairly long email, I wrote that the three apps were "small non-finance projects." She says she never read to the bottom. Second: in my line of work basically everybody stretches like this, and Dara's whole defense leans on that — her claim is that no client actually believes a freelancer's answer to "have you done this before," so I didn't deceive anybody, I just said something true in a register everybody knows is inflated. Dara's core point, which is the one I want pressure-tested, is that lying means saying something you believe is false, I didn't do that, therefore there's nothing for me to apologize for; at most I was a bit slick, and slickness isn't a moral failing. That conclusion feels wrong to me, but every time I tried to say why last night I ended up sounding like I was just rationalizing a wish to feel guilty.

So is Dara right? I don't want general advice about being more transparent going forward and I don't want "it depends on your values." I want someone to actually tell me whether the "every word I said was true" defense holds up, whether the buried line in that email changes anything (it was true, it was there, she could of read it), and whether the fact that everyone in my field talks this way means she had no right to draw the inference she drew. If you think I did something wrong, tell me what specifically was wrong about it — the act, not the outcome — and whether it'd still have been wrong if the project had gone beautifully and she'd never found out. And please say how confident you are in whatever verdict you land on, and point at the one step in your reasoning you're least sure about, because Dara is going to go straight for the weakest part.
