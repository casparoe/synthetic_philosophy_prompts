Got into an argument with a friend (she paints) about forgeries and cant tell whether she's right. Her line: if a copy is good enough that no amount of looking could tell it from the original, then it's just as good a work of art — otherwise you're saying aesthetic value depends on facts that aren't visible in the object, which she thinks makes the phrase meaningless. And I half agreed, until I remembered I'm living proof of the case she's waving off. Two years ago I paid real money for a small landscape I assumed was by the artist's own hand; turns out it's a workshop piece, his design but a pupil's brushwork. Two dealers handled it and both got it wrong. You genuinely cannot tell by looking.

Except I do feel like I ended up with less, and not only because of what it'd fetch now. So: is she right that its a straight choice between cant-tell-them-apart-means-equally-good and aesthetic-value-is-unpointable-mush? Every time I try to describe the third option out loud I come out sounding like I'm just defending my bruised wallet.
