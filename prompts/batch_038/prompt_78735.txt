I run a history-of-science reading group — about 14 people, mostly second-years, two hours on a Sunday afternoon — and our next session is Kepler and astrology. Everyone turns up already knowing the punchline (genius astronomer, quietly embarrassed by the horoscopes), and that's useless to me, because Kepler thought he was reforming the art, not smuggling it in. So I want a classification instrument they can actually use during the session. Two deliverables, fitted together.

**First: a "which side of the argument would you be on in 1610?" quiz.** Ten questions, each a concrete situation rather than a belief statement — a patron wants a nativity for his newborn and pays well; a bright new star appears close on the heels of a great conjunction; two planets are sitting 72 degrees apart, an angle the older authorities never counted, and nothing in particular happens; the town expects next year's almanac from you and your salary depends on it; a comet turns up in November; a harvest fails after a heavy eclipse; a patient's humours swing with the Moon. Two or three answer options each, then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto named positions. No question may be anything like "do you think the stars influence us?" — the point is that people who give the same answer to that end up in different leaves.

**Second: the positions, as a tree built out of the logical space rather than a list of names.** The splits I care about: what is supposed to carry the influence (angles between the planets' directions, sectors of the zodiac, or the planets' own natures); where the effect is allowed to land (weather, bodies and humours only, or the soul and particular events too); what justifies the practice (accumulated experience, a priori harmonic or geometric reasoning, scripture and providence, or nothing); whether the practitioner has to believe any of it; and what you do when a prediction flatly fails. Leaves must include at least: unreformed judicial astrology with houses, signs and elections; Kepler's own harmonically-reformed version; sublunary-only influence with no forecasting of events; the providential reading on which the sky is a text of signs rather than a machinery of causes; the natural-philosophical total rejection (nothing reaches us but light and heat); the occult-sympathy and talismanic tradition that treats planets as ensouled beings you can work with; and the pure instrumentalist who casts charts for the fee and believes none of it. Mark any leaf you can't put a historical occupant in as unnamed.

Then three things on top:

- Flag every pair of leaves your questions cannot really separate, and say whether some extra question would seperate them or whether the distinction is just not reachable from what people say about cases.
- List the unoccupied cells in the space — someone who accepts the harmonic aspects but denies they touch the weather; someone who believes in signs but not causes and refuses fees; whoever else falls out of your branches — and diagnose each: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
- Run Kepler himself through the tree using what he did and argued: the new star of 1604 and the conjunction just before it, the nativity he drew up for Wallenstein and came back to years later, the annual prognostications he needed the money from, the extra aspects he introduced and the ones he threw out because geometry couldn't construct the underlying polygon, the comets of 1618, and the 1610 pamphlet where he set himself up as the third party between a physician who rejected the art wholesale and an astrologer who defended it wholesale. Say which leaf each one lands in, and be blunt about the cases where he ends up in two leaves at once or where your tree simply gives the wrong answer about him — that's the part I want the group to fight over.

House rules: British English throughout. No quotation marks around anything purporting to be Kepler's words and no invented Latin titles — paraphrase, and if you're unsure of a date or a title, drop it rather than guess. No bibliography, no reading list, nothing for them to look up afterwards. Scoring key as a table, tree as indented numbered text, and keep it to something I can print on two sides of A4.
