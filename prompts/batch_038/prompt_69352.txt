A friend and I argued for the better part of two hours last night about tranquilism. Let me stipulate the view rather than rely on the label, since it gets used loosely: the only thing that makes a life go better is the absence of craving and the absence of suffering — pleasure, achievement, desire-satisfaction, and meaning have no value in themselves, but only insofar as they remove one of those two. Our disagreement was whether this is one view or two, and we never settled it, because every example we reached for was one in which the two components moved together.

So build me the case that would have settled it. I want a pair of lives — or a single decision problem faced by one person — in which craving and suffering come apart in opposite directions, so that a craving-only tranquilist and a suffering-only tranquilist rank them in reverse orders. Constraints: ordinary and realistic (a career, a marriage, an illness, a retirement; no ascetics, no pharmacology, no neurosurgery, no speculative technology); each life described in a single paragraph; and the decoupling must be substantive rather than a matter of one life having slightly more of both — the craving in the first life has to be genuinely non-aversive, something the person would not trade away and that does not hurt, while the suffering in the second has to genuinely fail to register as anything wanted to stop. If you think that last constraint cannot be met, say so plainly and give me the nearest approximation together with a diagnosis of why it fails; the verdicts must be generated by the two descriptions, not announced.

After the two paragraphs, run the checks. State what the craving-only and the suffering-only versions each say and why. State what the view as I formulated it — the absence of craving *and* suffering — yields for this pair: a ranking, a tie, or incomparability, and what the conjunction is supposed to mean here. Then say what a preference-satisfaction theorist and a hedonist say about the same two lives, so I can see whether the tranquilist split is merely their split in disguise. Finish by naming which feature of the case does the work, what happens to it if that feature is removed, and how a defender of the unified view would attack the pair rather than concede the disjunction.
