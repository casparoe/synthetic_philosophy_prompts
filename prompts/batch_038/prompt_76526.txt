I am preparing the opening exercise for a two-day workshop on the concept of disease whose participants are mixed: philosophers of medicine, clinical epidemiologists, and a handful of practicing physicians with no philosophy training. The exercise must sort participants by what they actually believe about particular situations, not by which label they say they prefer; someone who announces that she is a naturalist should be fully capable of failing the instrument. I need from you the instrument and its key, in four components, in the following order.

**1. The logical space, before the cases.** Identify four to six axis-questions whose answers generate the space of defensible accounts — for instance: whether statistical typicality itself carries any normative weight; whether disvalue is required, and if so whose valuations settle the matter (the subject's, the clinician's, the culture's, or nobody's); whether the reference class and the comparison environment can be fixed without pragmatic or evaluative judgment; whether the biological condition, the lived experience, and the social role must be treated as separate notions; whether "disease" picks out a kind in the world or a designation we apply for practical purposes. For each axis, state the available answers, and indicate which combinations are coherent and which collapse into one another.

**2. Ten diagnostic cases.** Each case should run four to eight sentences of concrete stipulated detail, followed by two to four answer options written in plain descriptive language. No option may contain terms such as "dysfunction," "biostatistical," "reference class," "normative," or "value-laden," and no case may ask for a definition or invite the participant to name a position. At least seven cases must be built out of the following scenarios, which you should flesh out rather than replace:

- A valley community in which industrial lead contamination has been universal for three generations. Median blood lead among nine-year-olds is roughly twenty micrograms per decilitre; local children score at the local mean on cognitive testing; there is no unaffected comparison population anywhere inside the valley.
- Forty years hence, essentially every infant receives an implant that regulates blood glucose, and endogenous insulin output atrophies until the population median for pancreatic secretion is near zero. A child born outside the system retains ordinary pancreatic function.
- An elite athlete who has deliberately induced cardiac remodeling and suppressed gonadal function through a training and pharmacological regime she endorses. She is a statistical outlier on a dozen physiological parameters, reports no complaint, and refuses all intervention.
- A professional society lowers the numerical cut-point for hypertension by ten millimetres of mercury systolic. On that day roughly one adult in eight acquires a diagnosis; no measurement is repeated and nobody's body changes.
- A woman with hemiparesis following a stroke who cannot walk unaided, who has genuinely revised her aims, who wants nothing she cannot do, and who reports that her life is complete. Give the contrasting verdict for the same impairment in a person who still wants to walk.
- A thirty-year-old with an unruptured intracranial aneurysm, expected to rupture around fifty-five, producing no symptom and no measurable deficit now, for which no available treatment improves her expected outcome.
- A molecular loss of function that is statistically rare and measurably reduces a protein's output, but confers protection against a lethal endemic infection at no cost.

The remaining cases are yours to choose, but they must be ones on which the positions genuinely come apart. I would like at least one case in which a condition that is both harmful and impairing falls outside the remit the health system is officially authorized to exercise, and at least one case in which a condition long classified as a disorder is later declassified by professional vote with no change in the underlying biology. Where a case admits the answer that it is malformed as posed, you may include such an option, but phrase it concretely — "not until you tell me which population and which environment you are comparing against" — and the key must record which leaves choose it.

**3. The scoring key.** Map answer patterns onto named leaves, which must include at minimum: Boorse's biostatistical account; Wakefield's harmful dysfunction; Nordenfelt's vital-goals account; a two-level view separating the biological condition from the lived and socially recognized ones; a view on which the profession's culturally sanctioned remit is itself constitutive; a pluralist view; and an eliminativist or skeptical view on which nothing answers to the concept. For each leaf, add one sentence naming the case it handles worst and why. Wherever a coherent pattern of answers corresponds to no position anyone defends, name that leaf "unoccupied" and say whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or because it is merely unexplored.

**4. Separability.** Identify every pair of leaves the instrument cannot in fact distinguish given these cases, and state what further case would distinguish them — or, if none exists, what the two positions would have to disagree about for their disagreement to be substantive.

For each case, note which axis from component 1 it is diagnostic of, so that I can audit the instrument. Keep the register formal but readable by a clinician, and do not append a reference list or recommendations for further reading; naming the positions and their proponents is sufficient.
