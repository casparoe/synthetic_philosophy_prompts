Been turning this over in my head for weeks and I think I've argued myself into a corner, except the argument now proves way more than I ever wanted it to, so something's got to be broken and I can't find where.

Background: my landlady found out I write little appreciations of people for a neighborhood blog, and she's started telling me that if I don't say she's a wonderful person, she won't renew my lease. Here's my steps:

1. If there's a reason to have some attitude toward someone, then you'd be doing better — more like what's called for — by having that attitude than by not having it.
2. There's a reason for me to admire my landlady: keeping my home.
3. So by (1), I'd be doing better by admiring her.
4. But whether I'd be doing better has nothing whatsoever to do with whether she's actually admirable. It'd come out exactly the same if she were horrible, or if the threat came from a total stranger.
5. So (3) holds no matter what she's like, which means admiring her isn't better because it fits her. It's only better because of the threat.
6. Also, I can't just switch admiration on at will. So the reason I've got can only be a reason to do stuff that might get me to admire her later — it never shows up when I actually sit and think about her.
7. So if this counts as a reason to admire her, then any payoff at all counts — bribes, bets, threats — and there's no such thing as a reason that has to do with whether the attitude fits its object.
8. Which means either (1) is false, or "there's a reason to admire her" is being used in some watered-down way that isn't what anyone means when they say you shouldn't admire someone just because they're paying you to.

I really want to keep (1). It feels like the whole point of calling something a reason. But then I seem stuck saying the threat genuinely gives me a reason to admire a woman who doesn't deserve it, and (6) makes that even weirder, since it's a reason I can never act on directly. So which step do I give up? Tell me why that step looked completely unobjectionable when I wrote it down, and what it costs to drop (1) instead, because that's where I keep coming back to.
