I spent thirty-odd years as a plant ecologist and have only lately taken up philosophy in earnest; I am currently working through the literature on verbal disputes and the diagnostic procedure of eliminating the contested term to see whether anything substantive survives. The cases used there seem to me to settle too quickly, so I should like one built to a harder specification, drawn from my own field.

The scenario. Two members of the same recovery team are preparing an assessment for a shrub of temperate rainforest: triploid, sterile, setting no seed, persisting as roughly five to six hundred above-ground stems scattered along a kilometre of creek flats, all genetically identical, each with its own root system, and propagated vegetatively in a botanic garden for decades as insurance. The listing form asks for the number of mature individuals, and the relevant threshold is fewer than fifty. One biologist enters "1," counting the single genet; the other enters the stem count, counting physiologically independent ramets. They accept every empirical fact the other accepts — clonality, somatic mutation load, absence of root grafting between stems, the epidemiology of the one pathogen that threatens them.

Specification. First, show that the dispute survives the method of elimination: once the term is banned and each states the position in a shared vocabulary, no disagreement remains. Second, show that it is nonetheless not idle — the two admissible precisifications place the taxon on opposite sides of the threshold, and neither biologist has misused the term. Neither result may be stipulated; both must fall out of the setup. Keep the scenario itself under about 350 words, realistic throughout, and botanically accurate enough that a field taxonomist would not wince.

Afterwards, give me the analysis: what a Carnapian deflationist says about the case and what a realist about biological individuality says; whether the disagreement is faultless; which single feature makes it verbal yet action-guiding, and what becomes of the dispute if that feature is removed — say, if no listing decision is pending. Then have each biologist attack the diagnosis, and tell me whether the case simply collapses into a substantive quarrel about which precisification best serves the purpose of the legislation. No reading list, please; I want the argument.
