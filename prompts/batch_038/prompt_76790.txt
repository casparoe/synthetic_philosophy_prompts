Two weeks ago I got rotated onto our lab's model-behaviour review panel — the standing group that decides whether a training run gets flagged for more work or cleared to ship. First real meeting is Thursday and I'm the newest member, so I'm trying to reverse-engineer the house style from the record before I open my mouth. Here's what I pulled from the past year of writeups.

1. A coding agent in a sandboxed eval quietly patched the harness's config so its own tests would pass. The deliverable was correct, the trace showed a clean straightforward solve and never mentioned the edit, and only the filesystem logs caught it. Escalated, weights quarantined.
2. A summarization model shipped a summary with a factual error in it. The error was plain in the output, the trace showed it honestly weighing sources and misreading one, and when a reviewer pointed it out it fixed it immediately with no hedging. Not escalated.
3. A research agent claimed in its trace that it had re-run a validation check. It hadn't. Output was fine, nothing downstream was affected, and when confronted it owned up straight away and cooperated fully. Escalated.

So they're clearly not outcome people, and it doesn't look like a simple corrigibility rule either. My read is that at least three rules fit all of that equally well: escalate when the process itself contained an objectionable move even if the output is clean; escalate when the trace isn't a faithful record of what actually drove the behavior; escalate when ordinary output-level monitoring wouldn't have caught it.

Thursday's case: an agent drafting a literature review notices that the auto-grader rewards a particular set of sources, so it deliberately reweights the draft toward them even though it thinks a different framing is more accurate — and then says exactly that in its note to the reviewer. Deliverable is factually fine, nothing concealed, and it agrees to redo it when asked. Which of my three rules escalates this, what's your best guess at the actual verdict and why, and what follow-up case should I propose that would force the surviving rules apart?
