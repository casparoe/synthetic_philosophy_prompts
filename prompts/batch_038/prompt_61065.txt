I've been going down a rabbit hole on the "avoid side effects" approach to AI safety — the idea that instead of writing down everything humans care about, you give an agent a task plus a penalty for changing stuff it wasn't asked to change, usually cashed out as something like how many other outcomes you've made harder to reach.

Write me a short story, maybe 2000 words, where this breaks in a way I haven't seen anyone write about. Setup: a team has a deployed agent whose side-effect penalty gets scored against a forecast of what would have happened if it had done nothing. Then they ship a better do-nothing model, and the agent's entire history gets rescored overnight — the yardstick it was being measured against was wrong the whole time. I want the fallout to be concrete and physical, not just a meeting about dashboards.

A few constraints. Don't give me the standard endings: no agent that's uselessly frozen, no paperclip-style goal blowup, no "it wakes up / its been scheming the whole time" — it should be doing its job correctly. And have two people on the team fight about whether the baseline should be the world with no AI in it at all versus the world with the best ordinary human effort, where it slowly becomes obvious they're actually arguing about something moral and neither of them can quite say what. I'm a software engineer with zero philosophy background, so let the story carry the concept instead of explaining it at me.
