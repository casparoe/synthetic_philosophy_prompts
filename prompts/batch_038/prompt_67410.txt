I'm a postdoc at a research institute whose working group is considering a norm that no member releases results, tools, or data externally without sign-off from the rest of the group. Two senior colleagues argued about it after the meeting, and having read the rationalist material on this for years I still couldn't tell who was winning. The exchange went roughly as follows:

1. N: The case for the norm is the unilateralist's curse. Suppose each of us, acting alone, has some small probability p of wrongly judging an externally consequential action to be safe. With N of us acting independently, the probability that at least one of us acts on such a misjudgment is 1 − (1−p)^N, which approaches 1 as N grows. Individual competence is not a safeguard that scales; the more capable people working in a field, the more likely someone acts on a misjudgment. Only a rule against acting alone fixes that.

2. R: That same formula argues against you. Each of us also has some probability q of wrongly judging a valuable action to be dangerous, and under your norm a single holdout blocks. So the probability that at least one of us wrongly blocks something worthwhile is also 1 − (1−q)^N. You haven't dissolved the curse, you've relocated it from acts to omissions.

3. N: Those aren't symmetric. A wrongly blocked release can be unblocked next month; a wrongly made release can't be unmade. If the two error types differ in magnitude and reversibility, equal error probabilities don't give equal expected costs.

4. R: "Reversible" is carrying all the weight, and it isn't generally true — waiting has costs that aren't refunded, including the possibility that the thing we're sitting on was the intervention that mattered. And your asymmetry assumes the harm of acting is catastrophic while the harm of waiting is a delay. If the benefit forgone is itself catastrophic in scale, the asymmetry inverts and your norm becomes a rule for waiting forever.

5. N: Then restrict it: the norm applies only to actions whose downside is global and irreversible. Inside that domain, the alternative is that the least careful actor anywhere sets the pace for everyone else, and that's what I want to avoid.

6. R: But then your problem isn't unreliable unilateral judgment, it's that one actor's choice binds everyone. Improving the information, incentives, and verification facing that actor deals with it without handing each person at our table a veto over the world.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the six from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a brief justification. In particular, say whether step 2's symmetry reply survives step 3's asymmetry argument, and whether step 5's restriction is stable or collapses back into a position one of them would reject. Then say who is more right overall, and identify any premise the whole exchange turns on that neither of them stated.
