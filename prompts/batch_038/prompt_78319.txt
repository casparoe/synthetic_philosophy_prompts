Been going down a rabbit hole on CEV (coherent extrapolated volition) and the whole idealized-preference move in AI alignment — instead of asking what people actually want, you ask what they'd want if they knew more, thought harder, and were more the people they'd like to be. Everyone treats that as a straight upgrade on raw preference aggregation, but I keep seeing the complaint that it just changes the subject.

The version that stuck with me: treat idealization as an operator on someone's desire set — inform it, rationalize it, make it more impartial, iterate to a fixed point. Nothing guarantees those steps commute. Fully-informed-then-rational might land somewhere different than rational-then-fully-informed, and if the endpoint depends on which path you take through idealization space, then "what you'd really want" isn't a well defined quantity at all, and picking a path looks like smuggling in the theorist's own values. (Same issue with the guy who wants a boat: his idealized self wants to give the money to charity instead. Whose preference is that, really?)

Write me an essay on whether that objection is fatal or survivable. Weigh it against the obvious replies — convergence, the operator being contractive enough in practice, the idea that my actual self pre-authorizes the endpoint — and pick a side; I want a verdict, not a survey. And tell me where you'd draw the line between idealization that's still my volition and idealization that's just someone elses preferences wearing my name.

Bottom line in the first sentence, then argue for it. Hard cap of 700 words.
