Our team is writing a chapter that sorts policy problems into genuine multipolar traps versus everything else, and Dana does all the classifying. Her calls so far:

- elite youth travel soccer, where families pour money into private coaching to chase a fixed pile of college scholarships: trap.
- open-access fishing before quotas, where the skipper who held back just donated his catch to the next boat: trap.
- countries undercutting each other on corporate tax rates to win the same handful of headquarters: trap, and she notes the only cure is a treaty no single country can sign alone.
- banks moving onto a shared instant-payments rail after somebody convened them: not a trap, just a coordination problem with an obvious focal point.
- airlines unbundling fees for bags and seats while one carrier spent years advertising that it didn't charge for bags: not a trap, the carriers win and the flyers pay and any one carrier can step out of the race and sell that as a feature.

New case we have to file: decarbonizing shipping. An owner who orders a green-fuel vessel pays more for the hull and more for the fuel, the charterer books whoever is cheapest that week, so almost nobody moves first — the handful who have are betting some charterer will pay a premium someday. The emissions land mostly on people who will never set foot on a ship, and flag-of-convenience registries mean even a determined government cant reach most of the fleet.
