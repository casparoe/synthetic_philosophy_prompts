My opening: I'm in a sleep study. At 8am tomorrow they tell me the result of a scan -- either I'm clear or there's a finding. Right now I put 0.2 on there being a finding. But at 6am they give me a sedative that reliably makes people sunny; by noon, subjects who got bad news typically rate there own chance of being fine at 0.9. So I'm near-certain that tomorrow at noon I'll assign 0.9 to "I'm fine." Reflection then seems to push me to 0.9 right now, which is absurd -- that 0.9 is what a drugged, wishful brain produces, and my 0.2 is the better estimate. Note the case survives the conditional version too, since I'll land on 0.9 whichever way the news goes. And if your move is that Reflection only binds me to opinions I expect to form by my own reasoning, then tell me what makes an opinion mine in the first place -- on that account tomorrow's 0.9 belongs to somebody else, and Reflection has stopped being a principle about me. Why isn't that fatal?
