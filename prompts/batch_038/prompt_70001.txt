We run a forecasting-ish meetup, and afterwards somebody posted his numbers on two things: 0.7 that a certain lab ships a frontier model this quarter, 0.6 that its safety lead quits, 0.2 that both happen. Those can't all stand — 0.7 and 0.6 force at least 0.3 on the conjunction. Two people in the group chat went back and forth about what that actually shows.

(1) Anika said it shows his credences are irrational, full stop: anyone who knows his numbers can sell him a package of bets that loses him money in every branch, and not being walkable into that is the floor of practical rationality. (2) Theo said the trick only works if you assume he prices bets one at a time and that money-utility is linear over the stakes involved — and Ben himself says he'd never sign a bundle he hadnt priced as a whole, so there's no book to make. (3) Anika said that's a double-edged sword: if bets don't reveal credences, Theo loses every other argument from betting behavior too, including the representation-theorem stuff he's always citing. (4) Theo granted maybe, but said the deeper problem is that guaranteed loss just isn't the same thing as irrationality — a bookie who simply knows more than Ben does can walk him into losing money on bets that every one of which looks good to him, no incoherence anywhere. (5) Anika said that case is different in kind, because the bookie's edge only pays off in the branch that actually obtains, whereas a real Dutch book loses in all of them. (6) Theo said fine, but then the betting story isn't doing any independent work: it dramatizes an axiom violation you already spotted, and if you don't already think coherence is a norm, no amount of imaginary money is going to move you. Neither budged.
