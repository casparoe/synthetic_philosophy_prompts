I have run a reading group on the Sleeping Beauty problem for several years, and I have come to suspect that the halfer/thirder dispute is not the dispute it presents itself as. Let me lay out the state of play as I understand it, then give you a hypothesis of mine that I want you to test rather than endorse.

The positions, as I have them. Elga's thirder argument runs by symmetry across the three centered possibilities open to Beauty on waking — heads-and-Monday, tails-and-Monday, tails-and-Tuesday — each of which she should weight equally, giving 1/3 to heads. Lewis's halfer reply holds that Beauty acquires no new non-self-locating information when she wakes, since she was told the whole protocol in advance, so her credence in heads should stay at the 1/2 she had on Sunday. The double-halfer line, developed by Meacham, holds that both the unconditional credence in heads and the credence in heads conditional on its being Monday are 1/2, which forces giving up something familiar about how credences are supposed to respond to evidence; Cozic reaches a comparable place by replacing conditionalization with an imaging rule, on the grounds that context-sensitive propositions create trouble for conditionalization generally and not merely here. On the decision-theoretic side, Hitchcock argues that a halfer Beauty is exposed to a Dutch book; the standard rejoinder is that evidential decision theory blocks the book; Conitzer argues that it does not. And Groisman's diagnosis is that the question put to Beauty on waking is simply ambiguous, so that 1/2 and 1/3 are correct answers to two different questions — a diagnosis that must swallow the consequence that two necessarily equivalent propositions get different credences.

My hypothesis is this. The reason nothing moves is that the parties are not disagreeing about probability at all. They are disagreeing about what a credence is answerable to: whether credences are disciplined by their role in fixing fair betting prices, or by their role in tracking a chance-governed situation that obtains independently of how many times one finds oneself in it. The betting literature makes the fault line visible without noticing it. If a wager is offered and settled at each awakening, betting at thirds maximizes gain; if one wager is offered once and settled once for the whole experiment, betting at halves does. Each side points at the same decision-theoretic machinery and extracts a different verdict because each has silently fixed a different settlement convention. On this reading the dispute about the number is downstream of a dispute about the job description of a credence under forgetfulness.
