I'm at work on a novel, and the scene I keep failing is the hinge of the third act: the night after their mother's funeral, in her kitchen, Nora — a philosopher of religion who built her career on the argument from divine hiddenness — and her brother Elias, a parish priest, sit down with the box of her papers. In it is a journal spanning nine years in which their mother, who had been raised devout, records praying every night to be given faith and never receiving so much as an intimation.

Please write the scene as a real dialogue, about 700 words, and hold it to the following.

On the philosophy: Elias must concede the journal's sincerity and openness, so the reply that all such nonbelief is really resistance is unavailable to him. So are appeals to the inscrutability of God's reasons and to compensation in the life to come. His line has to be that a presence too unmistakable to be missed would foreclose the free, uncoerced response that love requires — and he must then face Nora's sharpest question, which is why the silence is distributed as it is, since Elias himself reports assurance he never sought while the woman who sought it for nine years got nothing. Nora must press the two moves I think are strongest against him: that a father who withheld himself from a child weeping for him nightly would be judged cruel rather than prudent, so any disanalogy Elias offers must be one that does not reduce their mother's silence to administrative convenience; and that an account of who receives evidence keyed to inner states nobody can audit is indistinguishable from there being no one to distribute it.

On the craft: both are at full strength and neither wins. No speech longer than five lines; let them interrupt and let each correct the other's misstatement of their own view. Underneath the argument, Elias is trying to get Nora to say something she has not said since the diagnosis, and Nora is trying to be left alone. The scene should end on a concession that costs one of them something, and the final line should not be an argument.
