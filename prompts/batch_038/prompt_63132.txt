I run a tiny podcast that's basically just our philosophy depts grad students interviewing each other plus the occasional visitor, 40ish minutes an episode. Next week's guest works on early modern political thought and has a reading of Pascal on justice and force that I find both attractive and slippery, so I want questions that actually get at it rather than the usual so-what-did-he-think-about-the-wager filler.

Her line, roughly as I understand it: Pascal isn't a might-makes-right guy, but he isn't a relativist either. There really is such a thing as justice; what we lost after the Fall is the capacity to reliably pick it out. So force doesn't replace justice as content, it replaces our epistemic access to it — force becomes the only usable criterion available for deciding what to treat as just. That's how she reads the passage where he says that since we can't make what is just strong, we've made what is strong just. And she claims the same structure shows up on the religious side: the advice to go through the motions, kneel, take the holy water, let habit do the work, is compliance without inner assent in exactly the way that obeying the established powers is compliance without belief in their title. Politics and piety are one move applied twice.

Give me eight to ten questions. Nothing generic, no what-drew-you-to-the-seventeenth-century. Specifically I want:

- two or three on the describing-vs-endorsing gap: if force supplies a criterion only because we're blind, is that a justified criterion or a coping strategy wearing a justification's clothes, and where does the duty to obey run out — does her Pascal have any room for resistance, or is he quietist all the way down, including when the regime is the kind that's killing people.
- at least one on the imagination material: the robes and the ermine, the fact that crowds revere a magistrate because of how he's dressed and a king because of the armed men standing near him. If the sense of legitimacy is manufactured out of theater and habit, what's left of the claim that anybody is tracking something real.
- one on the geography remarks — justice bounded by a river, truth on this side of the Pyrenees and error on the other. Does that commit him to a relativism her realist reading has to explain away, and if she explains it away, what work is that sentence doing for him at all.
- one or two on the politics/piety parallel: is it a genuine structural parallel or two things that happen to rhyme. Push the theological problem too — if God authored justice and also gave us whatever natural light we have, in what sense is the blindness total enough that force has to be substituted for it.
- one that makes her name the cost: what does her Pascal have to give up that other readings get to keep.
- and a final one on what in the text would count as evidence against her interpretation.

For each question, also give me one or two sentences on what a genuinely good answer sounds like, the dodge I should refuse to accept, and a single follow-up line that closes that dodge off. Then flag which two questions I should hold until the end because they only bite once she's commited to something earlier in the conversation.
