I'm a philosophy of mind PhD student working on mental causation, and I've been trying to make sense of Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment — the billiard table full of symms whose stable clumps, the symmballs, are meant to be symbols — as his way of posing the question of who shoves whom inside the skull, symbols or substrate. My difficulty is that the setup seems to require something Hofstadter never actually supplies: a rule book determining which symmballs may collide with which, since otherwise the clumps would merely careen about at random and the high-level description would carry no logical structure whatsoever. Yet the moment you posit such a rule book, aren't you saying that something over and above the physical order of symms is steering them — that symbols genuinely shove the substrate, in flat violation of causal closure? Is there a reading of the careenium on which symmballs come by their collision rules without the thought experiment collapsing into interactionist dualism, or does Hofstadter simply have to choose between randomness and shoving?
