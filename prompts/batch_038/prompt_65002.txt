Write a pantoum of five quatrains on the circularity objection to Hume's standard of taste. The two lines that circulate — those that reappear as the first and third lines of each following stanza — should carry the two halves of the circle: that the true judge is recognized by the works he approves, and that the best works are those a true judge would approve. The closing stanza's return of the opening lines should therefore land as the argument's closure rather than as decoration. In between, the poem should work through Hume's actual machinery rather than name-drop it — delicacy of taste, practice, comparison, freedom from prejudice, and good sense — and may draw on the anecdote he takes from Don Quixote, in which Sancho's kinsmen detect a faint taste of leather and of iron in an excellent hogshead of wine and are vindicated when a key on a leathern thong is found at the bottom. Write from the position of a sympathetic reader who wants Hume to be right and watches his own criteria close over themselves: rueful rather than satirical, and legible as a poem to someone who has never read the essay.
