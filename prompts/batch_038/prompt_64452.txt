I am drafting the third chapter of my dissertation in philosophy of physics, and before I commit to its central argument I want the specification that argument presupposes. The argument, roughly, is that a certain picture of deliberation — that deliberating about X presupposes that whether X obtains is not already settled — needs support from an account of objective temporal becoming, and that treating becoming as a merely indexical or perspectorial feature of a block universe cannot supply it. What I want from you is not the argument but the standard it must meet: write the desiderata, the list of requirements any account of objective becoming must deliver to count as adequate, where adequacy is fixed by my purpose — an account good enough to do work in a theory of rational agency, not merely one that satisfies metaphysical taste.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or a shibboleth of a particular camp. I expect the list to cover at least the following territory, but I want you to organize it, prune what is redundant, and tell me what I have overlooked:

- Consistency with special relativity, including a determinate verdict on the relativistic deliberation case: two observers pass one another in relative motion, each carrying a different simultaneity hyperplane through the passing event, while at a distant station, spacelike separated from that event, someone is deciding whether to launch. The account must say whether that decision is already settled, and whether the answer may legitimately be observer-dependent.
- The distinctions it may not collapse: between what is real and what is knowable or accessible; between reality as a monadic property of events and becoming as a binary relation between events and locations or worldlines; and between a fact that is frame-relative and a fact that is merely apparent.
- Results it must be consistent with, and here I want your judgment rather than a summary: Malament's uniqueness theorem, which says that standard simultaneity is the only non-trivial simultaneity relation definable from the causal structure of Minkowski spacetime relative to an observer's worldline, and which is contested at the level of its definability, non-triviality, and causal-symmetry assumptions; and the Stein–Clifton–Hogarth line on becoming relations, usually read as showing that any becoming relation satisfying natural invariance and non-triviality conditions collapses to an everything-or-nothing affair, though some read the same theorems as vindicating Rietdijk and Putnam.
- Two further requirements I want assessed rather than granted. The first is what I will call the verbal-dispute test: the account must be able to state what non-linguistic difference obtains between its world and a bare block world, on pain of the whole debate being terminological. The second is implementability: the posited becoming-structure must be something that local physical processes, including the cognitive processes of the deliberating agent, could in principle be sensitive to, since otherwise it cannot bear on agency, which is the only job I need it to do.

Mark throughout which demands are imposed by the physics, which by the metaphysics, and which by the theory of agency, since I suspect these three sources pull apart. Then identify any subset of the requirements you believe to be jointly unsatisfiable, and for each such impossibility tell me whether it is a theorem, a strong argument resting on contestable premises, or a methodological preference dressed in a theorem's clothing. That assessment matters more to me than the list itself. Finally, say which single requirement excludes the greatest number of candidates. Skip the full scorecard; end with only three lines, naming for presentism, for the growing block, and for the block universe plus indexical becoming which requirement each gives up first, and whether that surrender is principled or ad hoc.
