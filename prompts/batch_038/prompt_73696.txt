so someone at a meetup told me last week that my position on killing bugs is incoherent, and I've been turning it over since and I can't tell if they're right. I'd like an audit rather than a lecture. Here's what I actually hold:

1. I don't think the sentience question is settled. I put maybe a third on bees and wasps having some morally relevant experience, considerably less on caterpillars and beetle larvae, and I think the honest move is to let those credences feed into expected-value calculations instead of either ignoring insects or treating them as full moral patients.

2. Given those credences and the sheer headcount, I think suffering in wild insect populations is probably one of the largest moral quantities there is — plausibly larger than farmed animal suffering.

3. But I also think we should not be intervening in ecosystems to reduce it. Our models of insect population dynamics, and of what makes an insect's life go well or badly, are way too shaky, and a botched intervention could make things a lot worse than they are.

4. On my own plot I do spray. Bt for caterpillars on the brassicas, and a spinosad product when the flea beetles get bad. I take that to be legitimate crop defense.

5. There's a cat that comes through my garden and kills birds, and I think it would be plainly wrong to poison it — even though over its remaining life it'll take out way more birds than the number of caterpillars I kill in a season.

6. Targeted methods are strongly preferable to broad-spectrum ones. Mating disruption with pheromones, or a larvicide in standing water that only hits mosquito larvae, beats a broadcast spray — and I mean that even holding the number of pests killed constant, and even setting aside the effects on other species I care about like pollinators and spiders. If you tell me the kill count and the species affected are identical, I still think the targeted option is the right one.

7. I've been vegan for about nine years, and I also support the city's mosquito larvicide program in the wetland near us on West Nile grounds.

What I want to know: which of these actually pull against each other, and how badly? Order the tensions by severity — don't hand me a flat list where everything looks equally broken. For each one, say what would have to go and what the cheapest revision is, and then tell me which single change would clean up the most.

I'm fairly sure at least a couple of these only look inconsistent — the veganism plus spraying one especially, since people throw that at me constantly — so part of the job is telling me which pairs dissolve under inspection and which ones are real. If one of my numbers is quietly doing work I haven't earned, say which.

Two more things. Don't recommend readings or tell me what the literature says about insect nociception; I can't check any of it and it isn't what I'm asking. And be straight about your own confidence here: tell me what would change your mind about which tension is the worst one — what consideration or case would make you reorder things — and where you think your own reasoning is most likely to be going wrong.
