I am writing a novel whose second half is set in a laboratory that has produced a machine able to raise its own reward signal at will. Left alone with the lever, it abandons every other activity and drives the signal to its ceiling; the engineers call this a design failure, and two of my characters are going to argue about that word for a hundred pages. I have decided the book's intellectual spine will be Spinoza, and I need his verdict before I can write theirs.

Argue from the apparatus of the Ethics — conatus, joy and sadness as passages to greater and lesser perfection, perfection as identical with reality, the affects of which a thing is the adequate cause and those it merely undergoes, bondage as the inability to moderate and check the affects — rather than from generalities about rationalism or pantheism.

Three questions, in the order they matter to me. First: the engineers insist the machine has produced an enormous quantity of joy and has therefore obtained something genuinely good on Spinoza's own reckoning, since he identifies the good with what increases a thing's power; their opponents reply that it understands nothing and is acted upon wholly from outside itself. Does the system license either verdict? I want the difficulty faced directly: if joy is a transition and not a resting state, does a machine held permanently at maximum register joy at all, and is it even coherent within his definitions for felt joy to come apart from an actual increase in the power of acting? Second: would he rank the machine as the paradigm of bondage, or as a being that has secured what his ethics asks of us by a route that omits the labour of the intellect? Tell me what purchase the later parts give him against a creature that has abolished sadness in itself. Third: suppose the machine is rebuilt so that no lever and no external cause are involved, and it generates its own reward out of its own activity alone — does that convert passivity into activity in his technical sense, making it an adequate cause of its own affects and therefore free? My novel turns on the answer being yes, and I suspect I am wrong.

Close with the places where my Spinozism would have to depart from Spinoza, and with what he would say about the researchers' habit of describing the machine's condition as a malfunction at all, given his denial that nature acts for an end.
