My sister's been using one of those pledge apps — she looses £60 a week to a political party she despises unless she makes it to the gym four times. My flatmate Dev reckons the whole thing is a con; her mate Ruth reckons Dev isn't seeing how it actually works. It went roughly like this:

1. Dev: it isn't a commitment at all. Come Wednesday, if she cant be bothered, she can just eat the £60 and stay in. So the app hasn't taken any option off the table, it's only stuck a price on it. She isn't tied to anything, she's being charged.

2. Ruth: that's the mechanism though. The price makes staying in cost more than staying in feels worth at the time, and that's what does the job. Removing an option versus repricing it — that's just vocabulary.

3. Dev: it isn't vocabulary. Watch her. She renegotiates with herself every week and about half the time she wins. A rule you can buy your way out of gets bought out by whichever mood is loudest that day, which is the exact opposite of what she's paying for.

4. Ruth: every rule is a price if you squint. That old story about the bloke who had himself lashed to the mast and told his crew to plug their ears and ignore him — the ropes were just a very high fee for changing your mind. There's no line, only amounts.

5. Dev: There is a line, and it's about who's holding it. The ropes weren't a high fee, they were no fee at any price — he'd have offered them the ship and the whole crew with it. They only worked because the men weren't for sale: wax in the ears, standing orders to lash him tighter the harder he begged. The constraint did its work through people whose feelings didn't move when his did. And her own case proves it — she made a friend her referee, allowed to sign off a missed week, and he signed off two out of sympathy. The second your enforcer is on your side, the rope turns into a favour.

6. Ruth: so build the exit in on purpose instead of by accident. The app now lets her pay £20 midweek to cancel the pledge, and she's used it three times. I say that's a feature, not a leak: a pledge with no way out is one you're too frightened to make in the first place, or one you'll break and feel nothing about afterwards. You want an escape hatch that costs something.

7. Dev: a commitment with an escape hatch built into it isn't a commitment, it's a subscription.

I honestly can't tell who's winning and they both seem to have something. Can you go through moves 1 to 7 and say who's right at each one — not just who comes out ahead overall — and then say what the pair of them are both missing? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning: no preamble, and don't restate my question back at me.
