I am writing the opening chapter of a dissertation in political philosophy on the citizen-soldier ideal, and I come from a country whose defence is provided by an all-volunteer professional force, so the institutions of the three European armed-neutrality states read to me less like policy choices than like encoded theories of political obligation. Three packages in particular. Switzerland: military service is compulsory for able-bodied male citizens, with women serving as volunteers; conscripts keep their personally assigned service rifle at home, and kept a sealed box of ammunition at home as well until that practice was discontinued in 2007; a man found unfit for both military and civilian service instead pays a supplementary tax of roughly three percent of his income, levied until age thirty-seven and for no more than eleven years, with severe disability exempting him outright; every day of service actually performed reduces that year's tax by four percent, until the tax for the year falls to zero; conscientious objectors may perform civilian service of about 340 days, roughly half again as long as ordinary military service; and the electorate has repeatedly reaffirmed the arrangement, defeating a 2013 initiative to abolish conscription by more than seventy percent and, in November 2025, an initiative that would have replaced the male-only draft with a general civic-service obligation for all citizens by more than eighty percent. Finland: universal male conscription with terms of 165, 255 or 347 days depending on the training received; civilian service fixed at twelve months, longer than even the longest military term, with unarmed service at nine or twelve; a person who refuses both military and civilian service is imprisoned for 173 days less any days already served, generally without parole; the group exemption once granted to Jehovah's Witnesses was abolished in 2019; the demilitarized Åland islands are exempt. Sweden: abolished conscription in 2010 in favour of volunteers, then reinstated it in 2017 on a gender-neutral basis, with a formally universal liability of which only a small fraction of each age cohort is ever actually called up for muster and training.

What theory of obligation, of the state's claim on the person, and of the relation between citizen and legitimate violence does each of these practices presuppose, and do the presuppositions cohere — within each package and across the three? Some specific knots I cannot untie. First, the Swiss exemption tax: is it a price, a penalty, or a surrogate? If the duty to defend is inalienable, how can money discharge it at all; if it can be discharged, why is the buyout stigmatized rather than offered to anyone who prefers it, why is it graduated by income, why capped at eleven years, and why does severe incapacity cancel the debt entirely while unwillingness never does? Second, the four-percent credit per day served, which appears to set an official exchange rate between days of service and money and so to presuppose that the burdens are commensurable in some common metric — time, risk, inconvenience, or the political act itself? How does that square with the anti-mercenary line running at least from Machiavelli to Rousseau, on which a citizenry that outsources its own defence thereby loses its liberty? Third, the extra length of civilian service in both Switzerland and Finland, in systems where the objector need only declare his objection rather than prove it to a tribunal: is the surplus a punishment for objecting, a costly screen guaranteeing the sincerity of the objection, or an equalization of burden — and what view of conscience and of fairness does each of those readings carry? Fourth, Finland's use of imprisonment for the total objector: does adding prison to the menu presuppose that once both an armed and an unarmed route are on offer nothing further is owed to conscience, and what does abolishing the Jehovah's Witness exemption presuppose about whether conscientious objection is an individual or a communal status? Fifth, Sweden's universal-but-almost-never-exercised duty: if nearly everyone is exempted in practice, is the duty doing any work, or is its content purely expressive — liability rather than labour — and what conception of political community makes an unexercised obligation worth reinstating after a seven-year volunteer interval? Sixth, the rifle in the hallway: does keeping one's service weapon at home presuppose that the state's monopoly on legitimate force is a monopoly on authorization rather than on possession, with the arms understood to belong ultimately to the citizenry, and how is that compatible with the same militia having been turned on strikers and demonstrators in the twentieth century? Seventh, the male-only scope of the Swiss duty combined with the rout of the universal civic-service initiative: does that combination presuppose that the duty is specifically martial, that bearing arms is constitutive of full citizenship, or nothing more than that the polity will not pay for a general service obligation?

Please excavate the implicit theories rather than argue for or against conscription. For each practice, reconstruct what it strictly presupposes — what would have to be true of the duty, of the person, and of the state for the practice to make sense at all — and separate that carefully from what the practice merely fits comfortably, since I suspect a good deal here is philosophically underdetermined and could be licensed equally by a civic-republican account, a contractarian one, an expressivist one, and a plain public-goods-and-free-riders account. Then tell me where the parts of each country's package pull against one another, if they do, and finish by specifying what would have to change — in law, in fiscal structure, and in the self-understanding of citizenship — if these states replaced the implicit theory with a frankly contractarian one on which defence is a public good purchased from willing professionals and funded out of general revenue. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
