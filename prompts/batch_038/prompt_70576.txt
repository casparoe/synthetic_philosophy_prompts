I'm drafting the conceptual-foundations chapter of my dissertation on analogy-making, and an argument I assembled there now looks as though it destroys the data it was built from. The setting is the Copycat letter-string microdomain: subjects see that abc becomes abd and are asked what becomes of some other string under the same change.

(1) In this microdomain, which description of a target string governs the change is not fixed by the source pair. When the target resists the obvious reading — runs of repeated letters, or a final letter with no successor — subjects apply the change to something else instead, a group rather than a letter, the leftmost element rather than the rightmost, and they diverge from one another.

(2) So the rule that takes abc to abd has no fixed extension: what it requires is settled only in interaction with the string it is applied to. This is why the model has to redistribute activation among neighboring nodes in the slipnet rather than look up a standing definition.

(3) If a concept's application conditions are settled only by the case at hand, the concept has no determinate content of its own.

(4) Two thinkers share a concept only if there is determinate content for them to share.

(5) But running the experiment at all requires experimenter and subjects to share a concept — above all, the concept of making the same change that the instructions invoke.

(6) So if (1) through (4) hold, the subjects never understood the instructions, and what was recorded are not responses to the task the experimenters took themselves to be setting.

(7) Those records are the evidence for (1). Hence the fluidity of concepts is self-undermining: on its own account, the data that motivate it are unintelligible.

(1) is what I most want to keep — the slippage looks like the most robust result in the microdomain. Which step should go, and what does giving it up cost? If your answer is that context-dependence is not indeterminacy, tell me what has to be true of a concept for its application to be fixed jointly by concept and case without thereby becoming indeterminate, and whether (4) survives that repair or collapses along with (3). If instead you think (3) stands and (1) has to go, say how much of the microdomain data you can retain and what replaces it.
