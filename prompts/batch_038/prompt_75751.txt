In my work on the internals of trained networks I have accumulated a set of judgments about which internal states are representations, and they do not sit well together. A transformer trained only on sequences of moves from a board game develops a state from which the current position can be recovered, and perturbing that state changes the move it plays: I say it represents the board. A language model's verb inflection turns on a feature a linear probe reads off as *subject is plural*, and later computation is sensitive to that feature: it represents plurality. From the same model I can also decode the number of words in the prompt, with high accuracy; nothing in its behavior or downstream processing is sensitive to that quantity, and here I balk — reliability of readout is not enough, it does not represent word count. But a model asked directly for the capital of Australia says Sydney, while the same model, given the answer as a cloze passage with the capital blanked, produces Canberra; I want to say it represents the true fact and something blocks its expression in the direct format, which looks like representation without use in the pathway that matters. A classifier's accuracy rides on the background shading of its training images rather than the object: decodable, causally load-bearing, and explanatory of its errors, yet I refuse to call it a representation of shading and instead say the model represents the category badly. Where a behavior is destroyed by ablating a direction that no clean probe can isolate, because the content is smeared across overlapping directions, I still grant representation. And running through all of it: I take these to be facts to be discovered rather than readings imposed by me, except in the shading case, where I suspect I am merely choosing an interpretation I like.

Is there a single account of what makes an internal state a representation that delivers every one of these verdicts? State it precisely, identify which case motivates each clause, and apply it to cases I have not raised — a state that is used but could have been realized in arbitrarily many different ways, and a state carrying information only about the training corpus rather than about the world. Then tell me which of my verdicts the account cannot save, and whether the shading case shows my realism about these matters was never secure to begin with.
