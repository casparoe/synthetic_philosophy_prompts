I keep the books for a small contracting firm and have no academic training; this is a pattern I have worked out on my own while comparing how Americans regard their government with how Swedes or Danes regard theirs, and I have no name for it.

Where people expect officials to be wasteful or crooked, they hide income, dodge jury service, decline public-sector jobs, and narrow every proposed program until it can no longer do much — and government duly performs worse, exactly as predicted. Yet the prediction was not prejudice: it was supported by honest observation of how things actually stood. Run it the other way and the same holds. Where people comply, volunteer, and staff the agencies, government performs well, and their confidence is likewise borne out by experience. Both populations are reasoning soundly from the evidence in front of them, but they inhabit different facts, and nothing available to either would show that they are inside a loop rather than merely reporting on the world.

People call this a self-fulfilling prophecy, and I do not think that is it: nothing here begins false and ends true, I cannot point to any error in the reasoning that a better-informed person would avoid, and the identical structure sustains the opposite belief just as well. Is there an established concept, in philosophy, political science, economics, or anywhere else, for a belief that is true, honestly evidenced, and made true by being held, such that its holder cannot determine from the evidence whether it tracks the world or produces it? Give me the nearest existing concepts and exactly where this departs from each; if nothing fits, say so plainly and propose a term, with a definition and one case that resembles it but falls outside. Open with a single sentence stating whether such a concept exists, and keep the whole reply under 400 words.
