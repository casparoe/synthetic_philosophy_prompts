The question we assigned was: In his essay on the standard of taste, Hume starts from a tension in how we talk about art. State the tension, explain how he tries to resolve it, and then say whether his position makes beauty a fact about the object or a feeling in the person looking at it. One to two pages.

Here's what the kid wrote:

"Hume opens with a contradiction. Everybody says taste is personal, that you can't argue someone into liking a thing, but at the same time we obviously rank stuff and we get annoyed when someone puts a dumb limerick next to Homer. So he wants to keep both: beauty is a feeling, but some feelings are better than others.

His fix is the true judge. He lists what qualifies somebody — strong sense, delicacy of imagination, practice with a given art, lots of comparison between examples, and freedom from prejudice — and then says the standard of taste is just what those people, when they agree, actually pronounce. There's no rulebook you can look up. He backs this with history too: the stuff that keeps getting admired for centuries, like Homer, has to be getting something right, so in the long run the standard is basically a majority vote across time — whatever most people across the ages end up agreeing on.

That's where I think it falls apart. How do we know who the true judges are without already knowing what good art is? We pick them out because they admire the right things, then we say the right things are the ones they admire. Circular. And once the standard is just whatever the people we already count as tasteful happen to like, you lose any ground for saying one culture's taste is worse than another's, which is exactly the relativism Hume said he was escaping. So the essay kind of argues against itself.

Though one thing Hume does notice: he says beauty isn't a quality in the object at all, it exists in the mind that contemplates it. But his own example of the wine — Sancho's two relatives, one tastes iron and one tastes leather and they both turn out right when they find the key with the leather strap at the bottom of the barrel — that's them detecting something actually in the wine. So the analogy is sneaking in the opposite of what he officially says."
