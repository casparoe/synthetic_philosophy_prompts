The question as it appeared:
(a) Dennett sorts creatures into Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian kinds. What capacity marks each step up?
(b) Two robots learn to get around a warehouse. R1 learns purely from the rewards and penalties it receives while actually acting. R2 additionally builds a predictive model of the warehouse and chooses its moves by trying them out inside that model first. Place each in the hierarchy and say why.
(c) Does this hierarchy show that having a mind is a matter of degree, or does it just give us four thresholds to cross instead of one?

The student's answer:
"(a) A Darwinian creature has every competency it will ever have built in by natural selection, so there is no learning. A Skinnerian creature can have its behaviour reshaped by its own history, through reinforcement, so one design gets tuned to whatever environment it lands in. A Popperian creature goes a step further: it carries an internal model of its surroundings and can evaluate candidate actions in advance, so it can find out that a move would be fatal without making it -- the guess dies instead of the guesser. What this adds is that the animal can hold its hypotheses in something like a language, which is what makes real reasoning possible. A Gregorian creature -- the name is from gregarius, flock, so creatures that live in groups -- adds the outside world: it imports tools its own body never grew, above all language, so its thinking is scaffolded by artifacts.
(b) R1 is straightforwardly Skinnerian, since its only selection is by the consequences of acts it actually performs. R2 is harder. It does have a model and does pre-select, but lacking a language it isn't reasoning, so I'd file it as a Skinnerian creature with a good trick rather than a Popperian one. Also, R2's model was written by engineers using mathematics and programming languages, so through them it sits inside the Gregorian circle anyway, which suggests the taxonomy can't grade artifacts at all.
(c) Each level is a distinct evolutionary achievement, so there had to be a first Skinnerian creature and a first Popperian creature. Therefore the hierarchy doesn't give degrees of mentality; it gives four thresholds, and mentality is still all-or-nothing, just crossed four times."
