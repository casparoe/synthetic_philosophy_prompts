our schools AI club did a session on interpretability and two seniors got into an argument that went like 20 minutes past when everyone else left. I was sitting there taking notes so here's roughly how it went, word for word in spirit if not exactly:

1. Dev: when interpretability people find a "feature" — a direction in activation space that fires for dogs — they've found something the network genuinely represents, the way a map genuinely represents a city.
2. Mara: that's a category error. The network isn't representing anything. We look at which inputs make that direction light up, notice it lines up with dogs, and *we* do the representing. Same as saying a cloud represents a rabbit.
3. Dev: but you can push on it. Nudge that direction and the model's dog-behavior changes. Cloud-rabbits don't do that.
4. Mara: that just shows the direction is causally load-bearing. You already picked the description. Physically it's a vector, and "dog" is your gloss on the training data it happens to correlate with. You could just as well call it "four-legged-ish, but only when there's grass in the picture," and nothing in the network tells you which label is the right one.
5. Dev: then your argument rules out there being meaning anywhere, including in my head — and if you'll really bite that bullet, fine, but you can't use it selectively to knock down interpretability while keeping it for people.
6. Mara: I'll bite it. But notice I'm not saying the features are fake. I'm saying some glosses beat others — the ones that let you predict and intervene across lots of contexts win — and "better by our standards" is still us grading our own models. It never turns into the network reporting its own contents.

Can you referee this move by move? Rate each of the six from clearly wrong to clearly right with a line of justification each, then say who came out ahead overall and what both of them are ignoring.
