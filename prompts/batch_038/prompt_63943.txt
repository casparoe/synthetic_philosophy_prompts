I write a weekly column on science and ideas for a general readership, and this autumn I have been asked to lead a seventy-five-minute session at a weekend school for adult learners: about thirty people, no philosophy training, a retired engineer here, a secondary-school teacher there, several of whom signed up because they have read my pieces on deepfakes and unreliable sources. My brief is to get them to see why a certain account of knowledge is attractive and what it costs. I do not want the account explained to me; I want the session planned.

Tell me how the pieces have to be ordered, and why the order is forced rather than merely convenient. In particular: does the counterfactual condition have to be established on its own, in cases where it delivers the intuitively right verdict, before I can afford to show what it does to closure? Or does closure have to appear first, as a thing they already believe, so that the loss registers? I have a whiteboard and no slides.

On the carrying example: I had intended to use the animal in the pen that might be a painted mule. Tell me whether that can bear the whole session or only a fragment of it, and what the example must have and must lack in order to do the work. I would also like to know whether anything from my own beat — a face-recognition system that gets the match right, a climate attribution result, a laboratory finding that would not have survived replication if it were false — can carry the point without letting the room collapse a modal condition into a question of how reliable the instrument is.

Then the misconceptions. My predictions are that they will hear the tracking condition as an ordinary-English claim about being attentive to evidence; that they will conclude the sceptic has simply been answered and we have won; and that at least one of them will say this is all just the Gettier problem with extra steps. Tell me where in the sequence each of those should be headed off rather than corrected afterward, and tell me which ones I have failed to anticipate.

Finally: one question I can put to the room at the end which would show me that the structure has landed rather than the example, and preferably built out of cases they supply; and a candid account of what I should decline to cover at this level — the method-relative repair, the contrast with a safety condition, the contextualist rejoinder — together with how to declare the omission without making it sound as though I am concealing where the view is weakest. Please write your answer to me as continuous prose, in the manner of a letter, with no headings, bullets, bold text or tables; convey the sequence in sentences rather than in lists.
