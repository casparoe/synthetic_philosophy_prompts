So my decision theory reading group is doing a session on imperfect recall next week and one of the others suggested I write down what I actually think about the absent-minded driver case beforehand, so they can attack a fixed target rather than whatever sounds defensible on the day. Fair enough. The setup I'm using, so we're all talking about the same case: a driver is heading home, there are two exits off the highway, taking the first dumps him somewhere bad and is worth 0, taking the second gets him home and is worth 4, and if he misses both he winds up at a hotel at the end of the highway which is worth 1. He's absent-minded enough that when he comes to an exit he can't tell which one it is, or whether he's already passed one — so any plan he can actually carry out amounts to a single rule applied at every exit he comes to. My views: (1) what gets evaluated is the policy he settles on before setting out; the exit-level decisions aren't fresh choices, they're the plan getting executed, and the right question is which plan is worth the most. (2) That said, when he's sitting at an exit he does have a decision to make, and it would be irrational of him to do something that, by his own lights right then, is worse than the alternative — so he should exit or continue according to whichever looks better on his credences at that moment. (3) Those credences should be 1/2 first exit, 1/2 second exit: he can't tell them apart, he doesn't remember passing anything, and I can't see what would tilt him toward one over the other from where he's sitting. (4) That's also why I'm a halfer about Sleeping Beauty — waking up doesn't tell you anything about the coin, and being at an exit doesn't tell you anything about which exit it is, same principle both times. (5) I don't buy updateless or commitment-flavored decision theories; deciding at the exit by asking what pre-drive-me would have wanted to lock in looks like letting the guy with less information steer for the one who actually has his hands on the wheel. (6) He gains nothing by carrying a coin — whatever a randomized rule would get him he could get by just deciding, so the search over plans should really be a search over deterministic ones.

One item I want handled separately, because it's the one the group keeps circling back to: someone ran the numbers using credences that weight the two exits by how likely he is to be at each, got "exit with probability 1/3 at every exit," said that's worth 4/3 from the start of the drive, and then noticed that at the exit itself both options look like they're worth 1.6 to him. He thinks that's incoherent — parts worth more than the whole. I said it's an artifact of how the calculation is set up and not a real problem, and he wasn't satisfied. Tell me whether I'm right to wave it off or whether I'm papering over something.
