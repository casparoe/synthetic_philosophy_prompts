Here's the thing. The line you get in basically every methods text — and in two of my lectures — is that Simpson's paradox isn't cured by randomization, because the odds ratio is non-collapsable: even when your covariate is independent of treatment assignment by design and the conditional odds ratio is identical in every stratum, the crude odds ratio still comes out closer to 1 then the conditional one. And that gets sold as a genuine Simpson reversal showing up in a perfectly randomized trial.

I want to argue the opposite: non-collapsability is not Simpson's paradox. Attenuation toward the null is a different animal from reversal — nobody flips which treatment they'd give over it — and lumping the two together is exactly what makes people think randomization has failed when it hasn't.

Can you build me the strongest version of that argument? Specifically:
