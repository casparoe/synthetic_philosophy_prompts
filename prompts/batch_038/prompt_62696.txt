Take the run on Northern Rock in September 2007 and analyze it as an information cascade -- then tell me honestly whether that's what it was. The facts, since you can't look anything up: Northern Rock was one of the UK's biggest mortgage lenders and funded itself largely by short-term wholesale borrowing and securitisation rather than by deposits; when the interbank market seized up in August 2007 it couldn't roll that funding, and on 13 September it went to the Bank of England for emergency liquidity assistance. The BBC's business editor broke that story that evening. By the next morning there were queues outside branches, the first retail bank run in Britain in well over a century, roughly £2bn came out over a few days, and it only stopped on Monday 17 September when the Chancellor guaranteed all existing deposits in full. Throughout, the Bank, the FSA and the Treasury had been saying publicly that the bank was solvent and viable, and it made no difference. Also worth flagging: at the time the compensation scheme already covered the first £2,000 in full and 90% of the next £33,000, so most of the people physically standing in those queues had very little at stake.

Here's what I want. First the mapping -- who's observing what, what counts as a private signal here, what the action set is, and at what point in the sequence a cascade is supposed to tip. Then run the rivals against the record: Bayesian herding (people inferring insolvency from each other's behaviour), a Diamond-Dybvig style coordination panic (people running because they expect others to run and being early pays), and reputational herding. My hunch is that the deciding detail is this: days of official reassurance about solvency did nothing, while an announcement that changed payoffs rather than information stopped the run overnight. Tell me whether that hunch actually licenses the conclusion, and whether public-signal / global-games reasoning does better, since the thing that moved everybody was one broadcast that everyone knew everyone else had seen.

Then the anomaly: small depositors queueing at all. Is that a problem for the cascade story or exactly what it predicts? Be careful to seperate "she inferred the bank is bust" from "she inferred the payout would be slow, or the guarantor wouldn't come through." And close by saying what the cascade framework -- built for people copying each other where actions don't interact -- just can't express about this case, including whether the media is even in the model.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restating the question back at me.
