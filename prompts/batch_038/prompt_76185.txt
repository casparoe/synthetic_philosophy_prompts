I'm drafting a short policy memo arguing for front-loaded emissions abatement, and I want my treatment of the discounting literature to be airtight. My line is this: Ramsey held that discounting later welfare merely because it is later is ethically indefensible — a failure of imagination rather than a moral judgment; Stern follows him in setting the pure time preference parameter essentially to zero, whereas Nordhaus retains a positive value only as a concession to whatever markets happen to do. The Stern–Nordhaus dispute therefore reduces to ethics, and once one accepts Ramsey's argument the social discount rate falls to zero, so that damages a century from now weigh exactly as much as damages today and the case for aggressive near-term abatement follows automatically. Can you help me sharpen this line of argument, and tell me which objection from the Nordhaus side would be hardest for me to turn aside?
