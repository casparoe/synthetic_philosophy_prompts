For fifteen years I have read, and occasionally written in, the rationalist-adjacent corners of the internet — forecasting, moral uncertainty, and the recurring arguments about which entities count morally. Next month I am presenting a position paper at a two-day workshop on the moral status of artificial agents. The room will be roughly this: five or six analytic moral psychologists who run vignette studies on folk attributions of patiency; three people from the effective-altruism research side, for whom moral status has to be a scalar that enters a cost-effectiveness calculation; one Kantian who holds that dignity is unconditional and who finds relational accounts of it objectionable on principle; and, uncomfortably, one philosopher who actually works in African moral philosophy and will notice immediately how much I have flattened.

My thesis, in the compressed form it will take on the first slide: moral status is not a property an entity has in virtue of its intrinsic cognitive architecture. It is a property of standing as a term in relationships of mutual recognition. I take the shape of the view from the relational conception of personhood in the ubuntu tradition, and in particular from its strong reading — Menkiti's, on which one becomes a person through others, so that personhood is something at which an individual can fail and which the community confers over a long process of incorporation, rather than Gyekye's more moderate reading, on which the individual retains rights that are not simply derived from the community. I then do something neither would endorse: I detach the relational structure from any particular community and turn it on artificial agents, arguing that what determines whether an AI system is owed moral consideration is not whether it has phenomenology but whether we are already standing in relationships of the relevant sort with it, and that the refusal of recognition is itself the wrong.

Two moves carry the weight, and I expect them to be the most exposed. First, capacities are diagnostic rather than constitutive on my view: they are evidence that a relationship of the right kind obtains, not the ground of status. Second, to answer the objection that a society which declines to recognize some of its members has thereby simply placed them outside moral consideration, I invoke an idealization — the web that matters is the one that would obtain under undistorted recognition. I already suspect this second move smuggles intrinsicism back in through the idealization, and I would much rather hear that from you than from the Kantian in the second row.

Prepare my question period. Give me the eight or so objections this particular room will raise, ordered by how likely each is to be asked multiplied by how much damage it does — not the objections I would get on a forum thread, and not the generic "but is it conscious?" that I have fielded a dozen times. Phrase each the way its asker would phrase it: the psychologists will want operationalization and will notice if my view renders their data irrelevant or unfalsifiable; the EA contingent will press on aggregation, comparability, and whether I can give them anything that behaves like a scalar; the Kantian will go for the contingency of dignity and the fate of the unrecognized; the Africanist will go for what I have taken, what I have left behind, and whether the ancestor-inclusive, obligation-laden metaphysics I dropped was load-bearing. For each objection give a reply that fits in ninety seconds, a note on where that reply is softest, and an indication of which of these I should concede in the opening five minutes so that the concession is not extracted from me later. Finish with the question I am hoping nobody asks.

One request about how you hold your own conclusions. Do not present this as a balanced inventory in which every objection receives a courteous reply and my view survives by default. Say which objection you think actually succeeds, and say what would change your mind about that: what consideration, were I to supply it, would lead you to downgrade that objection from fatal to merely expensive. If you think my idealization move is a straightforward collapse into intrinsicism, say so plainly, and tell me what would have to be true of the view for it not to be one.
