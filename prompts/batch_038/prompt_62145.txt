I'm putting together a written statement of my mereological commitments for a material-constitution reading group at my department — we each hand in a page beforehand so that the others have a fixed target to attack — and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is the package, listed in roughly the order I arrived at it rather than in any logical order. (1) I'm a universalist about the special composition question: any plurality of objects whatsoever composes something. My reason is essentially the Lewis–Sider argument from vagueness — a restricted answer would have to draw a line somewhere, and drawing that line would make composition itself vague, which I take to be impossible. (2) I'm an epistemicist about vagueness generally: there is no such thing as a genuinely indeterminate fact; borderline cases have precise boundaries that we simply cannot locate. (3) I reject composition as identity. The fusion is a further thing over and above the plurality, so composition is not innocent in that sense and my universalism earns me no ontological discount. (4) I think ontological disputes are substantive and that quantifier variance is a dodge: whether there are statues has a mind-independent answer, and I think the answer is that there are. (5) I'm a four-dimensionalist about persistence — objects have temporal parts — and I feel no unease whatever about objects whose parts are scattered across decades. (6) And yet I deny scattered objects in the spatial sense: I do not believe there is any object composed of the cup on my desk together with a rock somewhere in the Cydonia region of Mars. (7) In the usual ship-of-Theseus setup, where the original planks are removed, stored, and eventually reassembled into a ship while the continuously repaired vessel stays in the harbour, I judge that the reassembled ship is the original and the repaired one is not — though I'm happy to say, per (1), that both are perfectly real objects.

Some of this friction is probably genuine and some is probably just me using a phrase in two senses at once, and I honestly don't know which is which; nor have I settled in advance which item I'd rather keep, so please don't assume I'm attached to any of them. What I want is an audit rather than a survey: go through the set and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against one another, ordered by severity, and which merely look like they conflict and dissolve once you're careful about what is actually being claimed. For each genuine tension, identify precisely which commitment has to be given up or restated, say what the cheapest such revision looks like, and say what it costs me downstream — I'd rather be told that a fix exists but is expensive than be told the whole package is incoherent. In particular I'd like to know whether the motivation I cite for (1) is still available to someone who also holds (2); whether (5) and (6) can be held together by someone committed to (1) and (4); and whether (7) is a verdict my own principles can actually deliver or one I'm borrowing from a theory of persistence I've supposedly rejected. Finish by telling me which single revision would clean up the most. Please don't append a reading list — I want the reasoning carried through in the reply itself.
