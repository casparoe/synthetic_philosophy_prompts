I'm about 220 pages into my third novel and I've painted myself into a corner that I think only someone who reads philosophy for fun can get me out of. The book is set in a near-future city that has a municipal office — I call it the Bureau of Considered Status — where an overworked claims assessor has to file a numeric moral-weight score for whatever entity is party to a dispute coming across her desk: a beekeeper's hives, a biotech lab's organoid cultures, a smart building's climate controller, a family arguing about a relative in unresponsive wakefulness. The structural conceit is that each chapter is one case file, and the length of the chapter in pages is proportional to the score the assessor gives it. So I need real numbers, and they need to actually differ from each other — if everything comes out at 0.5 the whole book collapses into mush and my editor will have my head.

Here's the list she has to score, and I'd like you to score all of them too: an adult human being (this is the anchor, pinned at 1.0, with bare granite pinned at 0.0); a single worker honeybee considered on its own; an entire honeybee colony treated as one organism; a common octopus; a nematode worm with its roughly 300-neuron nervous system; a clonal stand of quaking aspen all sharing one root system; a pea-sized human cerebral organoid in a dish; a human at eight weeks gestation; an adult human two years into unresponsive wakefulness syndrome; an adult human under surgical general anaesthesia; an awake split-brain patient whose corpus callosum has been severed; a current large language model chatbot; and a learning smart-home thermostat with room sensors and a schedule. (I wanted to include my sourdough starter and was talked out of it.) I don't want one number per item, I want three, because my beta reader — an entomologist, and the person I've been fighting with for six weeks — keeps insisting that I'm collapsing different questions into one. Score each entity on a capacity axis (how rich or intense any felt experience there might be is), a unity axis (whether there is plausibly a single subject there at all, one point of view rather than many or none), and a stakes axis (whether it's the kind of thing that can be harmed or benefited, has welfare interests, regardless of how vivid the inner life is). Then give a composite between 0 and 1 and tell me what weighting you used to get there. Also: run the composite twice, once assuming something like a first-order or global-workspace-ish picture where integrated flexible information processing is the thing that matters, and once assuming a higher-order picture where a creature needs some kind of representation of its own states, and flag the biggest rank swaps between the two passes. And settle the bee argument — tell me who's right about the individual bee versus the colony, with numbers.
