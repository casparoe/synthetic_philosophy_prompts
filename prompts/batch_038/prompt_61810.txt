I want a paired case in resultant moral luck engineered to run in the direction the skeptics find hardest to dismiss: two agents indistinguishable in motive, character, beliefs, intentions, and diligence, where the one whose plan *succeeds* is intuitively more blameworthy — and where that success leaves nobody worse off. That last clause is what the case is for. I want to see whether outcome-sensitivity in blame can survive the total removal of harm, since that decides whether the control principle is really about control or is doing duty as a proxy for damage. So a case in which the successful wrongdoer does more harm, gets caught, or is found out afterward counts as a failure, not as a solution.

Constraints: ordinary-life setting (a firm, a clinic, a lab, a family), nothing science-fictional; the scenario itself in a single paragraph; both agents take equally thorough steps to bring the outcome about; the difference between them may not be traceable to differential negligence, differential evidence, self-deception, or a difference in what they aimed at; and it may not be reducible to legal liability, to the reactions of anyone who knows what happened, or to the agents' own feelings about themselves.

Then do the checking work. Walk through each constraint and show how the case meets it. Give the verdict of a control-principle defender and of a moral-luck defender, plus any third position the case seems to invite (for instance, that blame is equal while the agents' normative situations are not). Identify the one feature doing the work, say what happens to the case if you delete it, and state the skeptic's best attack on the case as rigged. If the constraints cannot all be met at once, say which fails and why rather than quietly loosening it.

Argue from the positions themselves — no citations, no reading lists. American English. Finish with a table using exactly these columns: position on moral luck; verdict on the pair (equal or unequal blame); the claim that generates that verdict; the best resistance available to it; what that resistance costs.
