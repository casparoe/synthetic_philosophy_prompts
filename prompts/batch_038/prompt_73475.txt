Please take on the role of a professional moral philosopher who has spent a career working on how we ought to compare outcomes that involve different numbers of people, and who has also advised real institutions on program evaluation. I would like you to think carefully and at length before answering, rather than reaching for the first reasonable-sounding response. Please begin by brainstorming, in a few sentences each, at least three genuinely different ways of valuing the two outcomes I describe below — including at least one that you yourself think is wrong — and only then work through my questions.

I am seventeen and a student member of the advisory committee that reports to our school board. I have read the agenda materials three times and I still cannot say what I think, so I want your reasoning, not your summary of other people's.

Here is the situation. Our district has run a summer research program for eleven years. It takes twenty-four students, pairs each with a scientist at a university thirty minutes away, and costs sixty thousand dollars. The students produce actual work. Alumni describe it as the thing that decided what they did afterward.

The board now wants to convert it. The replacement costs about the same and would take eight hundred students through week-long workshops and self-guided modules, with no mentors. A consultant evaluated a pilot of something like it in a neighboring state and reported two numbers: added together, the learning gains across the eight hundred participants are roughly six times the total gains of our twenty-four; but the gain to each individual participant is roughly one-fifth of what one of our twenty-four receives. The consultant also confirmed that the eight hundred would not have any summer program at all otherwise — the budget is fixed and there is no version of the world where they get the intensive one.

The conversion is not happening at once. It was phased over three years: first the twenty-four were kept and two hundred workshop seats added by stretching the same staff; then the research track was folded into the larger program as an optional capstone for forty students, with mentor hours per student cut in half; then the final version. At every one of those votes the board approved the next step six to one, and the measured total went up while the measured average went down. The same members who voted yes each time now say they would never have voted for the endpoint directly. Two of them are genuinely baffled by that fact, and so am I.

One member dissents from the whole comparison. She says a real apprenticeship and a week of worksheets are not the same kind of good, that you cannot put them in one column and add them up, and that the consultant's spreadsheet only looks like a measurement because nobody bothered to say what unit it is measuring in.

My questions:

1. Is the final program better or worse than the original one, all things considered? If you think that question is malformed or has no determinate answer, say so and explain why — but do not simply tell me it depends on my values.
2. Does the fact that the summed gains are six times larger settle the matter? What has to be true about those gains, and about the people receiving them, for summing to be a legitimate operation at all?
3. Is the dissenting member right that the two goods are not comparable? If she is right, what actually follows for my vote — and if she is wrong, what is wrong with her reasoning?
4. How did six of us, voting separately, arrive at an outcome none of us would have chosen? Is there a rule we could have applied at each individual stage that would have prevented this, without also ruling out expansions that were genuinely good? I care about this more than about any of the rest.
5. Tell me plainly what I should recommend to the board.

I have no internet access for this project and I am not asking for sources, references, or a reading list — I would not be able to check any of it, and invented titles would be worse than useless to me. Please do not name-drop. If you introduce a technical term, define it in the sentence where it first appears. If there is a standard name in philosophy for the trap I have just described, give it to me in one sentence at the very end, and do not let the label substitute for the reasoning.
