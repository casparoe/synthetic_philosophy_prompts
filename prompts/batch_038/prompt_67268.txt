I sit on the screening panel of a comparative economic history journal. Nothing goes to referees until we have ruled on whether the submitted research question is well posed. Our record so far:

1. Sent to referees: "Why was the water frame put to work in a Cromford mill in 1771 rather than in any Yangzi Delta workshop in the same decade?"
2. Desk-rejected: "Why did the West rise and the East stagnate?"
3. Returned for respecification: "Why did the Ming court abandon the treasure fleets after 1433 while the Portuguese pushed down the West African coast?" Our note said that, as described, the two halves were not rival outcomes of a single kind of situation.
4. Desk-rejected: "Why didn't China have an industrial revolution?"
5. Sent to referees: "Why did the gap between real wages in London and in Beijing widen after 1750 rather than before?"

The submission now before us asks: "Why did Britain, rather than the Yangzi Delta, enter sustained industrial growth first?" The author dates the divergence to roughly 1780–1830, defines the outcome as a rise in output per worker that keeps going once it has started, devotes a long chapter to arguing that the two regions were comparable in 1750 on commercialization, market integration, life expectancy, and rural industry, defends the choice of units by showing that any coarser pairing swamps the comparison, and concedes that there is no sharp line marking when growth becomes self-sustaining. The editor's covering note observes only that the piece is written as an answer to the old question about why industry arose where it did.

Predict our verdict, choosing among sending it to referees, returning it for respecification, and rejecting it. In doing so: lay out at least three different rules, each of which fits all five of our earlier decisions, that nevertheless disagree about this submission, and state what each implies here; say which of our precedents is the closest analog and which one a dissenting member will reach for; and, if the record genuinely underdetermines the case, say what principle we would be adopting by accepting it, what we would be adopting by sending it back, and what further submission would settle which rule we have actually been running.
