A friend and I spent most of a Saturday arguing about the hole argument, and we reached an impasse that I now think can only be settled by building something.

Her position is that the argument, as Earman and Norton pressed it, is a general schema rather than a fact about general relativity. Wherever you have (i) a substratum of individuals that a theory posits but that can be detected only through what they bear, (ii) a transformation permuting those individuals while leaving everything else in place, and (iii) laws indifferent to the permutation, you get the reductio: the substantivalist is committed to a multiplicity of states that the laws and all available data cannot distinguish, and therefore to a radical indeterminism no one should accept. Spacetime is one instance; the schema travels.

My position is that it does not travel. Outside spacetime the substratum is always something we constructed, and everyone knows it is a device of notation — coordinate labels, bound-variable names, accession numbers. The transplanted substantivalism is therefore a straw man nobody occupies, the reductio has no target, and what looks like a general schema is an artifact of the fact that manifold points are the one case where the bare particulars are not of our making.

To settle this I want the strongest case you can build against me: a thought experiment that reproduces the structure of the hole argument in a setting where the substratum is emphatically not a mere notational convenience. I will propose the setting; your job is to make it work, or to show that it cannot be made to work.

The setting. A relational database supporting a hospital's research registry. Each row in the central table carries an opaque surrogate identifier: an integer the storage engine generates at insertion, uses internally to locate, lock, update and garbage-collect the record, and never exposes. By stipulation it appears in no report, no view, no query result available to users, no foreign key, no audit trail, no backup catalogue and no replication stream. Everything anyone can learn about the registry — every attribute value, every constraint, every relation among records, the entire documented history of insertions, updates and deletions — is fixed independently of which identifier bears which row. The system's published specification requires only that identifiers be unique and stable once assigned; it says nothing about which identifier goes with which row, since the allocator's behaviour is an implementation detail permitted to vary between releases.

The hole. One night's bulk import loads four hundred records, and the order in which the loader handed them to the engine was never written down anywhere.

The specifications the case must meet:

1. The scenario itself: one paragraph, no vocabulary borrowed from differential geometry or set theory, no possible-worlds talk and no stipulated haecceities inside the story — an ordinary piece of institutional information technology — and intelligible to a first-year graduate seminar that has had the hole argument explained in words but has never seen a metric.

2. Exhibit a transformation that is a bijection of the substratum onto itself, is the identity on every row outside that night's import, and is non-trivial within it. A global renaming will not do: if your case turns out to be a Leibniz-shift wearing a disguise, it fails, and you should say so rather than dress it up.

3. Show that the transformation leaves invariant everything the specification, the constraints and the totality of accessible evidence jointly determine.

4. State the analogue of the Cauchy problem. Say precisely what counts as complete data outside the hole in this setting, and precisely what remains unsettled once all of it is granted. The indeterminism must come out as a determinate claim about what is and is not fixed, not as a mood.

5. Give both readings. Identifier-substantivalism — the view that the identifiers are the real individuals the registry is about, on the grounds that they are what the engine physically tracks, what survives the wholesale replacement of a record's contents, and what makes this row the same row from one update to the next — must deliver two distinct states of affairs. Identifier-relationism must deliver one. Neither verdict may be stipulated in the setup; both have to fall out of the setting plus the metaphysics, and I want to be shown the exact step at which the metaphysics does the work.

6. Then the honest part. Which single feature of the case is load-bearing, and what happens if it is removed — does the case collapse into an unobjectionable redundancy, and if so, why? And here is the question my friend and I actually care about. An identifier-substantivalist has at least three replies available: that the identifiers are not empirically transcendent at all, since they are physically written somewhere and a sufficiently deep inspection would reveal them; that the indeterminism is real but benign, since no difference it makes could ever be detected; and that the invariance in my setting is an engineered design choice rather than a symmetry the laws force upon us, which is exactly what makes it disanalogous. Take each in turn, say whether it defeats the case as built, and then say whether the same reply, if it succeeds here, would also succeed against the original argument about spacetime points.

7. Close with your verdict on our dispute, and with one structural feature of the hole argument that you think this setting cannot reproduce at all. Say whether that feature is load-bearing in the original.

If you judge that no case satisfying all of 1 through 5 can be built in this setting, or in any setting of the kind I have described, say so plainly, identify which constraint is unsatisfiable, and give the reason. Do not quietly relax a constraint and present the relaxed case as the deliverable; that is the one answer I could not use.
