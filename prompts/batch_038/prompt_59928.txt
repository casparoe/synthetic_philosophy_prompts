I'm on the board of a small land trust and next month I'll hold the deciding vote on whether we lease a strip of our preserve to a solar company. The money would let us buy twice as much forest somewhere else, and thier models say the panels offset a real chunk of carbon. Thing is, the last time we did a trade like this, what actually mattered had nothing to do with the plan — the buyer went under, the parcel got flipped, a county road went through it, and we stumbled into a good outcome nobody predicted. Since then I've noticed the ranking flips whenever I nudge one small assumption, and my confidence tracks whichever story I heard most recently. Half the board says that's exactly why we should only count what we can verify locally: acres, water quality, deer. The other half says if you can't calculate the big stuff you should just maximize the part you can. My worry is a different one. I dont think I'd know which choice was better even ten years out, once everything has played out — the results will be swamped by stuff neither side saw coming. So is there a way to actually decide this, or am I just picking a story either way? And does "go find out more before you vote" even help here?
