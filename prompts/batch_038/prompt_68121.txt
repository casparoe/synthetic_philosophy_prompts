I am running a workshop on the identity of indiscernibles, and two participants have agreed to an adversarial collaboration. Party A holds that Max Black's two-sphere universe — nothing but two iron spheres at some distance from one another, matching in diameter, colour, temperature and every other monadic respect, and standing in exactly the same relations to each other — is a genuine metaphysical possibility, that it therefore refutes the principle, and that we are consequently forced to admit primitive thisness or bare individuation. Party B holds that the principle survives: the spheres are at least weakly discernible, since an irreflexive symmetric relation such as "is at some nonzero distance from" holds between them though of neither to itself; and more fundamentally, a world that can only be specified by a description that already helps itself to two referring terms has not been shown to be possible at all.

Both parties have signed a protocol you must honour throughout. The property inventory excludes thisnesses, indexical properties, and the properties of being identical with or different from a given object, so neither side may win by inflating what counts as a property. And each side must state a verdict, with reasons, on two variants: Black's world as described, and a variant in which the spheres stand in no spatial or other irreflexive relation whatsoever.

Please write the joint statement they would produce, using exactly these sections in this order:

1. Shared ground — a numbered list of six to ten claims both can sign, concerning how the principle is to be formulated, what the grades of discernibility (absolute, relative, weak) are and what each of them buys, and what the thought experiment is and is not meant to establish. One or two sentences per claim.

2. Remaining disagreements — a table with the columns Disagreement | Party A's wording | Party B's wording | Genuine or merely verbal?; four to six rows, each wording phrased so that its own party would accept it unamended.

3. Cruxes and tests — two or three cruxes, that is, sub-questions whose settlement would actually move at least one party. For each, give the argument, thought-experiment variant, or piece of physics (identical particles in symmetric and antisymmetric states are fair game) that both sides agree bears on it, together with each party's prediction recorded in advance. I expect one crux to be whether weak discernibility does any of the work the principle is supposed to do, given that in a world containing exactly two objects mere distinctness is itself an irreflexive symmetric relation. Roughly 250 words per crux.

4. Bottom line — under 150 words on what the collaboration settled, what it left open, and what the rest of the workshop should take away from it.
