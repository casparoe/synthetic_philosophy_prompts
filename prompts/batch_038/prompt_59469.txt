My opening move: you always explained satisficing by the cost of search — the mind stops at good enough because looking further is expensive. Fine. But I build systems now where search is nearly free. I can get a thousand candidate answers for pocket change, and it still lands on mediocre, because the scarce thing isn't generation anymore, its evaluation: the same machinery that proposes the candidates is the one judging them, and it can't reliably tell good from merely plausible. So the bound has slid sideways from the proposer to the critic, and nobody's theory was built for that. Worse — and this is the part I think breaks your framework rather than extending it — the aspiration level isn't set by the agent's own experience of its outcomes at all. It's inherited wholesale from whatever population it was trained on, it doesn't ratchet down when it keeps failing, and the agent never gets to find out, because it's discarded and re-spawned for the next task instead of living with the consequences. In your world an aspiration level was the sediment of a career. Here it's the sediment of somebody else's career. So: is that still bounded rationality, or do you need a new primitive for a decision maker that cannot update its own standards? And if you say the standards were always handed down from the organization, tell me what's left of the individual agent that your theory was about.
