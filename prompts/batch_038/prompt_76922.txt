In three weeks I am presenting a paper at an interdisciplinary workshop on mechanistic explanation. Roughly two-thirds of the room will be philosophers of neuroscience and biology who work squarely in the new-mechanist tradition — mechanisms as complexes of entities and activities, organised hierarchically and producing regular changes — and the remainder will be machine-learning safety researchers who use the word "mechanism" to mean whatever a sparse autoencoder has isolated in a transformer's activation space. Only two people in the room have ever run an activation-patching experiment, and one of them is me. My thesis is that mechanistic interpretability has no principled criterion for when two model implementations, or two versions of the same model, contain "the same mechanism", and that the right criterion is a graded one. The argument I shall give runs: (i) mechanisms are individuated by causal-explanatory role rather than by material realisation, so differences in weights or architecture do not by themselves settle non-identity; (ii) causal role is fixed by the profile of what changes under interventions, but there is no privileged intervention set handed down by nature — the set is selected by the explanandum, that is, by what we are asking the mechanism to do; (iii) it follows that mechanism identity is both graded and interest-relative: two implementations are the same mechanism to the degree that they are substitutable across the interventions relevant to the explanandum in view; and (iv) as a corollary for safety work, a property certified as "the model does X because of mechanism M" transfers across model versions only relative to a declared evaluation interest, and can fail under a different specification without anyone having made an error. I am also willing to bite the bullet that a direction in activation space is not, without more argument, an entity with activities; most of what interpretability calls a feature is better described as a stable causal correlate, and only some of it earns the title of mechanism.

The people I am worried about are specific. One commentator is a philosopher of neuroscience who has written at length on constitutive relevance and will think I have thrown away exactly the machinery that does the work — that mutual manipulability, not bare counterfactual covariance, is what distinguishes a component of a mechanism from something that merely tracks it, and that my account has no way to make that distinction. Another is a philosopher of mind who works on multiple realisability and functionalism and will ask, politely and then less politely, whether I have simply re-described causal-role individuation in mechanistic dress, and what work the mechanism vocabulary is doing beyond that. Two graduate students will press on the coherence of graded identity: identity is transitive, symmetric and all-or-nothing, so am I not really talking about similarity, and if so why not say that. The safety researchers will attack measurability instead: the relevant intervention set is unbounded in practice, and patching is not an intervention in the interventionist sense at all, because it shifts the activation distribution and drags off-path effects along with it, so the counterfactuals I am individuating by are artefacts of the method. Nobody in the room will object to the interpretability examples on empirical grounds, which means I cannot rely on technical detail to carry me past a philosophical objection.

Please prepare my Q&A sheet rather than defend the view for me. Give me the eight or so objections this particular audience will actually raise, ordered by how much damage each would do if it landed cleanly, each phrased the way the person raising it would phrase it — in the idiom of the constitutive-relevance debate, of the multiple realisability debate, or of an empirical practitioner who doubts the measurement — with a reply of about two minutes that I can deliver aloud, and a blunt note on where that reply is softest. Then tell me what I should concede in my opening remarks to take the sting out of the two or three most predictable attacks, what is the one concession that would unravel the whole paper and must therefore be refused, and what the single objection is that I am hoping nobody raises: my guess is that interest-relativity makes the account unable to underwrite the safety conclusion I built it for, but if you think there is a worse one lurking, say so and tell me how I would answer it. I would rather not be sent off to the literature on any of this — no citations, no reading list, nothing I would have to look up afterwards; I need sentences I can say out loud in a room where I will not have another turn to think. A colleague has suggested I simply stop using the word "mechanism" and talk about counterfactual profiles instead, which is not available to me, since the whole point of the paper is that the mechanistic vocabulary earns its keep.
