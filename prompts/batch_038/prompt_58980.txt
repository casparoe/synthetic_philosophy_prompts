So me and my mate have been arguing about the same thing for over a year now and neither of us can budge the other. Neither of us studied philosophy — we just read around it in the evenings — so don't assume we know the jargon, but don't water it down either.

The question: if the universe turns out to be infinite, and so already contains an infinite amount of happiness and an infinite amount of suffering, what's left of moral reasoning?

My position is that this is a problem with adding up infinities naively, not a problem with ethics. Adding one more happy life to an infinite world doesn't make the total any bigger, but that doesn't mean it didn't matter. You can compare worlds in richer ways — limits of finite averages, infinite numbers that differ from each other, or comparing the difference an act makes rather than the totals — and you have to hold onto something like the principle that if an act is better for everyone at every time then it's better overall. Drop that and you've got no ethics left at all.

His position is that all those fixes are arbitrary. The sum of a difference depends what order you add it up in, the average depends how you carve the world into chunks, and picking your infinite numbers is just picking a favourite way of counting. Once the totals are infinite, ranking acts breaks down, and the honest move is to admit that and decide on some other basis — compare only bounded regions, say, or give up on totalling wellbeing altogether.
