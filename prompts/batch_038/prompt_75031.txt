Working on my quals and I've talked myself into an argument I can't get out of, and the conclusion would torpedo my whole thesis topic. It's about inverse reinforcement learning — the setup where an agent watches what people do and tries to back out what they want, instead of being handed a reward function.

(1) An IRL agent's only evidence about a person's preferences is that person's behavior, choices included.
(2) If two hypotheses are equally compatible with all the evidence you have, then as far as you're concerned they aren't two hypotheses.
(3) Given any stretch of behavior, the reading "she does X because she prefers X" and the reading "she does X but really prefers Y — she's misinformed, coerced, weak-willed, discounting the future, whatver" can both be fitted to it, since you can always adjust the auxiliaries: her beliefs, her constraints, how noisy her actions are.
(4) So the agent never has a reason to pick one reading over the other.
(5) So for the agent, "she prefers X" and "she prefers Y but does X" aren't genuinely different possibilities.
(6) But being able to tell those apart is the entire point of inferring a reward rather than just cloning behavior.
(7) So IRL can't get at what people actually want. It's behavior cloning in a normative costume — it can never say that an addict's choice fails to reflect what she values, or that someone's preferences have adapted to a bad situation they'd escape if they could.
