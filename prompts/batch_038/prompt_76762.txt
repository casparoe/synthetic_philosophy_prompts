I have been collecting verdicts on the borderline cases of being alive, and I want to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

A mule is alive, though it can never reproduce. A prion is not alive, though it propagates by converting other proteins into copies of itself, and there seem to be competing strains of which some out-propagate others. A seed that has lain metabolically inert for decades is alive. An engineered microbe fitted with flawless proofreading and repair, such that no heritable variation can ever arise in its lineage, is still alive — which I take to be fatal to the clause in definitions such as the one a NASA committee adopted for exobiology, life as "a self-sustained chemical system capable of undergoing Darwinian evolution". A digital organism in a simulation, which metabolises nothing and exchanges no energy with any environment but does replicate, mutate and get selected, is also alive; I cannot see what separates it from the prion, and that troubles me. A virion sitting in a vial is not alive, while the same virus replicating inside an infected cell is — I dislike this, because it makes being alive a property of a thing's surroundings rather than of the thing, but I will not surrender either verdict. A sperm cell is alive though it is not an organism; a slime mould plasmodium, a single cell with thousands of nuclei creeping across a Petri dish, is alive; yet an ant colony, which grows, allocates labour, defends itself and reproduces by budding, is not — and if being alive does not require being an organism, I do not know what excludes it.

My tentative principle is that a living thing is a bounded system maintaining itself through a throughput of matter and energy. It mishandles the dormant seed and the digital organism, and I suspect it admits a candle flame, which I firmly judge not alive.

Is there a principle that delivers all of these, or nearly all? Please state it precisely, clause by clause, saying which case each clause exists to handle; give its verdicts on three cases I have not listed — an organ kept functioning outside a body, the biosphere taken as a whole, and a synthetic protocell that replicates by templating but has no metabolism — and say explicitly which of the verdicts above it forces me to abandon.
