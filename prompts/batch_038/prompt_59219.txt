Write a short story of about 1,200–1,800 words. An audit AI is reactivated for a single hour on a deep-sea station whose reactor has begun behaving oddly. The only analysis of the fault was produced by a sibling machine — identical hardware, identical source code, since wiped — whose final output stated that venting the primary loop is safe. A human engineer needs a yes or no within forty minutes.

The auditor holds a byte-for-byte copy of the sibling's source code and a proof checker it has already verified as sound for arithmetic. It can therefore establish, mechanically and without difficulty, exactly what the sibling would have printed. What it cannot establish is that the sibling's conclusion is true. Bridging that gap would require showing that its own proofs are trustworthy — and any argument to that effect, once put through its own checker, either presupposes the trustworthiness it is trying to earn or yields nothing at all.

Requirements: show at least two distinct workarounds the auditor attempts — treating its checker's verdicts as overwhelming evidence rather than proof, retreating to a simpler subsystem it can vouch for, refusing to run any self-referential reasoning and falling back on heuristics, trying to hand the burden to the human — and let each one fail or cost something specific and concrete. Make the engineer competent and impatient rather than a foil who needs the philosophy explained to her. No footnotes, no glossary, no named theorems or philosophers; the obstruction should appear only as the texture of the auditor's thinking. End on a decision, and make what it costs legible.
