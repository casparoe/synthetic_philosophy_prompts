i'm in a first year MA seminar on archives and the politics of the record, and next week's session is the one on silence — trouillot's argument that silences get baked in at four distinct moments (making of sources, making of archives, making of narratives, retrospective significance), hartman on the girl who survives only as a line in the indictment against the captain who killed her, plus the derrida/foucault strand about the archive being an instrument of rule rather than a neutral warehouse. my assessment has a creative component where you can submit anything you want as long as you write a paragraph defending the form, and i want to try something a bit weird with it, so i'm using you to see if the conceit actually holds together before i commit.

so: write a poem that is literally formatted as an archival finding aid. i mean the apparatus, not a poem about a finding aid — repository, creator, title, inclusive dates and bulk dates, extent in linear feet and box count, a scope and content note, an arrangement into series with folder-level descriptions, access restrictions, related material, and a processing information note at the very end. the collection should be invented (please don't use real repositories or real people's names), and the subject should be somebody who appears in it only from the outside: the creator field names a mission, a family estate, a hospital, whatever, and the person the poem is actually about shows up in the boxes as an inventory line, a valuation, a deposition given through an interpreter, a mark made where a signature should be. get the conventions right enough that someone who has sat in a reading room would nod — the bracketed inferred dates, "n.d.", "1 folder", "no further accruals expected".

the constraint i care about most is that the form has to carry the argument rather than just decorate it. a finding aid describes records *about* people, not by them, and provenance means you can't gather a life back up out of the fonds it was scattered through without destroying the evidence of how it got filed — so i want the silences to be structural, not stated: a gap in the inclusive dates that's longer than the bulk span, a series listed but with no items under it, an extent that's obviously too small, a restriction date that outlasts everyone named in the file, a separated-materials note pointing somewhere unrecoverable. and then the volta should land in the processing information note, because that's the one field where the describer is allowed to be present and talk about their own interventions — it's the natural place for the third-person bureaucratic register to finally crack, or for a first person to get through, or for a field to be filled in with a refusal instead of a description. don't let it resolve, and don't let it turn into a little sermon about giving voice to the voiceless; the whole point of the session is that recovery is itself an archival act with its own power in it. somewhere around 45 to 60 lines total across the sections feels right, tho i'm flexible on that.
