There's a monthly thing my friends and I do that started, no joke, in a LessWrong comments thread about six years ago and has since metastasized into an actual meetup we call dilemma night. The ringleader — I'll call her N — has the most settled opinions of anyone I've met about which hard cases are real moral dilemmas and which are just hard-but-solvable, and she has never once budged on a case after the first time she ruled on it. Since she's basically the group's ground truth, I want to predict her before I spring a new case on her next month.

Her record so far, in the order she gave them:

- Rescue boat, five drowning strangers on one reef and one on another, time to reach only one group, no relationship to anybody: not a dilemma. The numbers settle it, apparently.
- Surgeon who has to take a crush-injured hiker's leg to save her life, patient consents: not a dilemma. "She's not wronging anybody, she's treating somebody."
- You've promised two different friends you'd help them move on the same Saturday: not a dilemma. "A scheduling problem with feelings — tell them both a week out and there's nothing left over."
- Williams's botanist: twenty people tied up against a wall, you get handed a rifle and the offer that if you shoot one the other nineteen walk free, and if you refuse all twenty die. Real dilemma. What made it real for her was that he's going to be the one who pulled the trigger.
- Sartre's student, his mother who has nobody else in the world versus getting out to England and the Free French: real dilemma. "There's no scale you can put those two on."
- Sophie at the camp, made to pick which of her two children gets sent to one side: real dilemma, and she considers this the paradigm case rather than an extreme one.
- The politician who signs off on torturing a detainee to locate a bomb that will kill hundreds: real dilemma. Her gloss was that the interesting part isnt whether he does it, its what he owes the guy afterwards.

The case I've got for next month is this. T runs enrollment for an expanded-access program for a pediatric disease with basically no other treatment, and there's one slot left. Two kids meet the protocol's criteria; the protocol says ties get broken by a random draw, both families signed paperwork acknowledging that, T ran the draw, and it went to kid A, whose family has already been told they're in and has started making arrangements. Then T learns something the draw structurally couldn't see: kid B's family is in the country on a visa that expires in about ten weeks, so if B misses this slot there's no next round for her and she'll be out of reach of the program entirely, whereas kid A stays eligible for the following cohort in eight months. So T can either let the draw stand, or override it and hand the slot to B, which means phoning A's family and taking back something they were told was theirs. And here's the wrinkle I think N will fixate on — the only reason there's one slot instead of six is that the sponsor's VP quietly capped the drug supply to keep the trial small and tidy for an acquisition pitch, and T knows it.

I've got a twenty dollar bet going with someone else in the group about this. My guess is she says not a real dilemma, because there was a procedure, it was followed, and T's job is to not be the person who second-guesses the draw after the fact. My bet-mate's guess is real, on the grounds that T dirties her hands on a specific identifiable kid either way and that the scarcity was manufactured by a man in a suit for a bonus. I genuinely don't know, which is why I want this done properly rather than as a vibe check:

(a) lay out the candidate principles that would generate all seven of her verdicts, stated precisely enough that they'd actually disagree somewhere, not just relabeled versions of each other; (b) what each of them says about T; (c) your single best guess at N's verdict and why you'd bet on that rule over the others; (d) if more than one rule is still alive after (c), give me one further case, described concretely in the same style, that kills all but one of them, and say which rules it separates; (e) your honest take on whether "genuine dilemma" is even one question for N, or whether she's sliding between something like *there's no fact about what she ought to do* and *she comes out of it dirty either way* — and if those come apart somewhere in her record, point at exactly where. For what it's worth my calibration on predicting N specifically is embarassing, so don't weight my own guess in either direction. I know the literature has labels for a bunch of these positions and I'm not asking for the labels or a reading list; I want to know which rule N is running, not which school she'd join if you made her pick one.
