So I'm on the prize committee of a smallish academic society — nine of us — and every year we pick one winner out of a shortlist of nominated papers. This year the shortlist is four papers (call them A, B, C, D) and the whole thing has turned into a fight about voting rules, which is not what I signed up for. Three members only got through part of the shortlist and handed in ballots with a paper left off entirely. The ballots we actually have:

- 4 members: A > B > D > C
- 3 members: B > C > D > A
- 2 members: C > D > B, with A left off, because they never read it

Under our old rule (plurality, and anything you left off counts as last) A wins, 4 first-place votes to 3 to 2, and nobody voted D first at all. I put the same ballots in a spreadsheet with Borda points and B wins comfortably, and B still wins basically however I handle the missing A. But here's the part I'm stuck on. Run the head-to-heads treating those two incomplete ballots as "A goes last" and D beats everybody — D is the Condorcet winner, and D is also the paper the four A-first members ranked third. Run the same head-to-heads but skip any pairing where someone didn't rank both papers, and now A beats everybody, all three of its matchups 4–3. Same nine ballots, two rules for the hole in the data that both sound reasonable, two different winners.

Two members have gone in opposite directions from this. One says Arrow's theorem shows there's no principled way to aggregate our rankings anyway, so we should quit pretending and let the chair just pick, since at least a person can be held responsible for thier judgment — and he adds, not unfairly, that D winning would mean honoring a paper nobody put first. The other says we should move to approval voting next year because it sidesteps Arrow entirely, Arrow only being about ranked ballots.

Can you draft the memo I'll circulate before our meeting next Tuesday? We announce at the annual meeting in six weeks so whatever we land on has to be executable by then. Bottom line up front, then the reasoning, and written so the people who disagree with me can actually push back on it rather than feeling talked down to. I need it to settle: which rule we use for this year on the ballots already in hand and who wins under it; whether we ask the two members to read A and re-ballot or just hold the deadline; and what standing rule we adopt going forward. On the reasoning side, I specifically want (a) a precise account of what Arrow's theorem does and doesn't cover here — I suspect the missing-entry business means we aren't even inside the setup the theorem is about, but I dont know how to say that without it coming across as dodging the impossibility result; (b) the chair-decides proposal taken at its strongest, not just swatted down with the observation that a chair picking the winner is literally the dictatorship the theorem rules out; and (c) a straight answer on whether the approval-voting person is right that it escapes the problem or whether it just moves the problem somewhere else. And if you think some of what these two have been arguing about for a year is verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why.
