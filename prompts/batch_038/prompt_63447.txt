I sit on a county behavioral health authority board and we vote on the 14th. Here's the mess: a bequest from the 90s earmarks money for our north-zone clinics, and the earmark is non-transferable — we can either keep spending it up north or hand it back to the state, where it evaporates. North zone has a three-week average wait for an intake appointment; south zone has fourteen weeks. Staff has proposed cutting north-zone bequest spending by about 40 percent, which would push north's wait to roughly 13 or 14 weeks and do literally nothing for the south. The entire rationale is closing the gap, and our enabling statute says the authority is supposed to reduce inequities of access between zones. Two board members think refusing the cut means we don't actually care about fairness; the rest of us think spending public money to make people worse of with nobody gaining anything is malpractice.

Draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting. It needs to land on one of three things — approve the cut, reject it, or approve some modified version — with reasons a board member could actually argue back against. Specifically I want it to take a position on whether the reduce-inequities language is about gaps or about where people sit absolutely, and what we should do if the wording won't settle that; to give a real hearing to the thought that the post-cut situation might be better in one way even though it's worse overall, instead of just swatting it; and to say what we should be doing about the fourteen-week wait if we're turning the cut down. Two pages tops, plain English, no references or footnotes — most of these people haven't touched philosophy since undergrad.
