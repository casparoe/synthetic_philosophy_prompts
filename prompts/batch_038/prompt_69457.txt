ok so context: I TA the week of our first-year game theory section where we do iterated elimination of dominated strategies, and it is by far the most glazed-over fifty minutes of the term. Half of them never get strict vs. weak dominance straight, and the thing that absolutely does not land is that a pure strategy can be strictly dominated *only* by a mixture of other strategies even when not one single strategy beats it on its own. Meanwhile my flatmate watches an ungodly number of Big Brother / Love Island-type eviction shows and narrates them to me at volume. I want to merge the two into a handout for the last section of term that they'll actually read instead of scrolling past.

So: write me the official contestant rulebook for a reality competition show — call it something like House of Dominance — in which the contestants are the pure strategies of one player in a two-player game, and the "audience" is the set of choices available to the other player. Deadpan corporate-reality-TV legalese: numbered clauses, a short glossary, a compact FAQ, and a one-paragraph sidebar recapping last week's elimination. Around 800–900 words. Two targets, and neither may be named or winked at: the way intro textbooks present this procedure as a frictionless little machine that tidily solves a game, and the way eviction shows dress up a mechanical rule as drama and contestant welfare.

The jokes have to come from the rules being *correct*, so the handbook needs to encode: (i) nobody goes home unless some other contestant — or some cocktail of them, mixed only from people still in the house — beats them against every single audience configuration, and ties don't count; (ii) the clause the fanbase loses its mind over, where a 50/50 pairing of two individually safe contestants is evicted anyway, because there's a three-contestant / two-configuration case where A and B each survive on their own but their equal mixture loses to C in both configurations — put the actual numbers in the sidebar and make sure they genuinely work, a specialist will check; (iii) an "at-will" legacy clause letting the producers also remove *weakly* dominated contestants, plus the ensuing forum meltdown, because under that clause the order of evictions changes who's left and one particular order has already deleted a couple who were perfectly stable together (you know what I mean, but the show itself must never say the words); (iv) the reassurance the producers bury in the FAQ — under the strict rule the order genuinely doesn't matter and no stable pairing is ever evicted — delivered in the tone of a PR statement that nobody believes but everyone quotes; (v) an equivalance clause the show's lawyers keep invoking without being able to explain it, to the effect that in this house "nobody's favourite" and "strictly dominated" always come out the same. Contestants who make the finale get the word rationalizable tattooed on them, which the glossary should define exactly the way a reality-show glossary would. No payoff matrices beyond that one sidebar, nobody breaks the fourth wall, and a reader who's never taken a game theory course should still come away understanding that these people are being evicted by an algorithm that is technically fair and completely indifferent to them. If you can make the weak-dominance clause feel like the season's villain without ever once saying its unjustified, that's the bullseye.
