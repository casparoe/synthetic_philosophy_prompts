I run a five-person quantitative risk group inside a mid-size insurer that underwrites novel technologies — grid-scale battery installations, cultivated-protein plants, autonomous freight corridors — where the exposure we are pricing has essentially no loss history. For years I have followed the imprecise-probability literature from the outside: the argument that a single sharp number is false precision when the evidence is thin, the counterargument that mushy credences are ruled out by principles about evidential symmetry, and the machinery that grew up around sets of probability functions — lower and upper expectations, the imprecise Dirichlet model for sparse data, dilation, and the various rules for choosing an act when a credal set refuses to rank the options (maximality, Γ-maximin, the Hurwicz criterion, Levi's E-admissibility). What I have never found is anyone describing how to run a meeting with any of it. I want the procedure, not a survey of who holds which position.

Our current house practice is a crude approximation. Each expert supplies a 5th, 50th and 95th percentile for the quantity at issue; we take the median of the medians and the outer envelope of the tails; the result feeds a pricing model that accepts only point estimates, so somewhere downstream — no one is quite sure where — the interval is collapsed to its midpoint. Two consequences follow. The envelope is nobody's belief and is not obviously coherent, and the collapse destroys the very information we were trying to preserve while leaving an audit trail that makes the panel look as though it had been sharp all along.

Please write the decision procedure this group could actually run, in ordered steps, covering at minimum:

1. Elicitation — how to obtain interval- or set-valued beliefs from experts who have never met the formalism, and what to do when one expert is confidently sharp while another will not go narrower than a factor of two hundred.
2. Pooling — a rule for combining them that does not manufacture precision, and the conditions under which the pooled set is a legitimate representation of the panel rather than an artifact.
3. Default width — how wide the set should be as a function of how much data exists, with the width governed by a parameter we can defend on the record rather than tune until the answer looks right.
4. Updating — what happens when new evidence arrives, including the case where conditioning on it widens our interval for the quantity we care about, and what the group should say to a committee that will read a wider interval as the panel having become more confused.
5. Decision — how to get from the set to an underwriting call when the acts are left unranked: which rule, what attitude toward ambiguity that rule bakes in, and where a nominally technical choice of rule is doing substantive normative work.
6. The point-estimate demand — whether the procedure should ever emit a single number, and if so how it is derived, so that the derivation is documented rather than tacit.

For each step, flag the ones that are judgment calls wearing procedural clothing, and say where the procedure must stop and hand the question to the chief underwriter because it concerns risk appetite rather than uncertainty.

Then run it on three cases: a parameter supported by exactly one historical loss event plus a plausible mechanism for many more; a case in which a credible new report dilates our interval instead of narrowing it, with the wording the group would use in the room and in the written submission; and two underwriting acts our set leaves incomparable, with a binding decision required within forty-eight hours, showing what each of the standard rules picks and whether the pick is defensible to a regulator. At least one of these should produce an answer I will not like — I suspect it will be that one of our two flagship numbers should stop being reported at all.

Finally, state what the procedure cannot do: where it will misfire in practice, whether it double-counts conservatism already loaded into our reserve margins, and whether the whole apparatus earns its overhead compared with simply reporting an honestly wide interval alongside a documented midpoint.
