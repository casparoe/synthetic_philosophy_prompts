Challenger, January 1986, run through the dispositions literature — finks, masks/antidotes, and the simple conditional analysis. Facts as I have them so you don't need to look anything up: the aft field joint of the right-hand solid rocket booster was sealed by two rubber O-rings, a primary and a redundant secondary, with a putty barrier meant to keep hot motor gas off them; when the motor lit, internal pressure made the joint open up slightly, so the rings had to flow into the gap and hold the seal, which only works if the rubber springs back fast. It had been well below freezing overnight, the air at liftoff was barely above it, there was still ice on the pad structure, and an inspection crew had been sent up to say whether it mattered. The rings were stiff and slow to recover, both were breached, gas blew by into the external tank, and the stack came apart 73 seconds in. The flaw had been known since 1977; earlier flights showed the primary ring eroding, with the coldest previous launch, about a year before, showing the worst erosion of any flight to that point, and NASA managers logged those partial burn-throughs as evidence the rings had a "safety factor" of 3. The night before, Thiokol engineers recommended against launching below 53F; NASA managers pushed back, Thiokol senior management went into a half-hour caucus and reversed their own engineers. Feynman later clamped a piece of the ring material, dropped it in a glass of ice water on camera, and showed it didn't spring back.

So: sort the case. Is the cold a fink — the basis of the disposition getting removed — or a mask, where the basis stays and something extrinsic interferes with the manifestation? Note the awkwardness: on Martin's picture the fink is supposed to be set off by the stimulus itself, whereas here the basis-altering condition was standing there for hours before ignition, and on Lewis's repair what's required is that the causal basis persists through the stimulus. Did it? Then tell me where the putty barrier and the joint rotation sit, since those look like genuinely extrinsic interferers, and who the bearer of the disposition even is — the ring, the ring-in-its-groove, the joint, or the whole booster — given that what counts as masked seems to shift with that choice.

Here's a category I'm making up, so don't go hunting for it in the literature: call it attenuation. The disposition is present, its basis is intact, nothing blocks the manifestation, but the manifestation comes out degraded and partial instead of all-or-nothing — the ring recovers, just too slowly and not far enough. Argue whether the framework needs that, and whether the safety-factor-3 inversion is precisely the mistake of reading an attenuated manifestation as evidence of an unimpaired disposition rather than a weakened one.

Then the conditional analysis. "If this ring had been compressed and released at liftoff temperature it would have sprung back" was false, so on the simple analysis the cold ring just wasn't resilient, and NASA wasn't confused about metaphysics at all — it was wrong about a matter of fact nobody had measured. Dispositions are supposed to ground counterfactuals, but here the contested counterfactual concerned a condition outside the flight envelope, and it got settled by a management caucus rather than by anything dispositional. Say what the theory owes us at that point, and be straight with me if the answer is nothing.
