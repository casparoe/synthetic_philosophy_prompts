I am a junior in high school, and I am writing a paper for my school's philosophy symposium on mental causation. I want to work through a disagreement I have with my teacher, and I think a carefully built case would help more than more abstract argument.

The disagreement is this. I had understood the familiar story to be that Kim's exclusion argument gets its bite from treating causation as production — as a kind of causal oomph passing from a physical event to its effect — and that if you instead treat causation as difference-making, asking only which properties an intervention would have to change in order to change the effect, the mental property comes out ahead of its neural realizer, because the realizer is too specific and too multiply realizable to be a proportional cause. My teacher says this is too quick: on a difference-making account, there are perfectly ordinary situations in which the mental property fails to qualify as a cause precisely because it cannot be manipulated except through its realizer, so that adopting the interventionist picture makes exclusion worse rather than better. I want to know whether she is right, and I want a concrete scenario to argue about.

Could you construct such a case to the following specifications?

1. Realistic. An ordinary person doing something ordinary — no brain machines, no zombies, no duplicates, nothing science-fictional. The effect must be a physical event, so that the causal closure of the physical is genuinely engaged rather than sidestepped.

2. The physical realizer must be described as a single, specific physical property, not as a disjunction and not with an "or some other realization" escape clause. I want to close off the reply that the physical description is really a disjunctive property and so not a cause at all.

3. The mental property must not be more proportional to the effect than its realizer is. If the effect is described coarsely — something like "she reaches for her keys" — then the usual move is available, the mental property is at the right level of grain and the neural one gratuitously specific, and the case stops testing what I want tested. Build the effect so that the specific physical property is the better-matched candidate cause.

4. The scenario itself must be one paragraph, and it must generate the verdicts rather than announce them. If the paragraph simply informs me that no intervention on the mental property is available, that is stipulation, not design.

After the scenario, please show your work:

- Name the mental property M, its realizer P, and the physical effect E, and say which premise or premises of the exclusion argument each account leans on.
- List the interventions that are actually available in the case — not the ones someone could imagine if the world were arranged differently — and say which counterfactuals come out true.
- Give the verdict twice, once from a production-style account on which M's causal powers are a subset of P's so that M causes E through P's causing of E, and once from a difference-making account. State plainly whether the two accounts really diverge in this case; if they do not, tell me rather than manufacturing a divergence.
- Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work, and tell me what happens if I remove it — for instance, if I coarsen the description of E, or if I allow that M could have been realized differently in this very person on this very occasion.
- Explain how someone who accepts difference-making causation but wants to defend mental causation would attack the case: which of my four specifications they would deny, and whether that objection shows the case is unfair or only that the dispute has relocated.

I do not need references, a bibliography, or a reading list; I am working offline and the case itself is what I need. I do want to know where you are on solid ground and where you are not: if any part of this depends on a contested view of what an intervention has to be like, flag it, and tell me how confident you are that the case satisfies specifications 2 and 3 as opposed to merely appearing to.

Finally, please push back if you think my framing is confused. If the production and difference-making distinction cannot bear the weight I am putting on it here, or if no single case can meet all four specifications without smuggling in the conclusion my teacher wants, I would much rather be told that directly than be handed a scenario that looks compliant on the surface.
