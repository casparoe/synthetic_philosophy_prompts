I am a public economics doctoral student working on whether offensive preferences — the utility losses bystanders suffer from conduct they merely find repugnant — belong in a social welfare function, and I keep meeting Bentham running a version of the same argument from the other direction, in his treatment of self-regarding offences and of sexual conduct between consenting adults. Please restate that case in the vocabulary I actually use: externalities and their internalization, Pareto against Kaldor-Hicks, whose preferences get weight in the aggregator, punishment as a costly instrument with a deterrence technology, and his own claim that a given sum of money buys more happiness for the poor than for the rich. Take the argument as you find it — that the standing objections reduce to antipathy or to religious authority rather than to assignable mischief; that punishment is groundless where there is no net mischief and inefficacious where it cannot reach the will; and that the legislator must still reckon with the alarm and the disappointed expectations his tolerance provokes. Then give me the residue report, premise by premise: what crosses over intact, what survives only if you silently re-describe pleasure as preference satisfaction, and what has no counterpart in a preference-based framework at all. I suspect his refusal to admit antipathy as a ground, and his treating security of expectation as prior to maximization, are where it breaks; say for each whether the failure shows Bentham is less of a welfarist than advertised, or whether it shows that my aggregator lacks an admissibility condition it ought to have. Do not smuggle contemporary premises in and present them as translation — if a step comes out right only because you have restated it as something a modern planner already accepts, mark that as distortion. Reconstruct it all from your own understanding: no citations, no reading list, no titles or dates. Continuous prose only, please, in the manner of a letter — no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables.
