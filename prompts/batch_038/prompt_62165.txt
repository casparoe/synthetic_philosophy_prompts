I'm MCing a physics-philosophy colloquium Q&A in a couple of weeks and the invited speaker defends a retrocausal reading of Bell-type experiments — the sort of view where the measurement settings work as boundary conditions on the past aswell as the future, so the correlations between spacelike-separated outcomes get explained locally, with nothing travelling between the two wings of the apparatus. I know the area decently but I don't want to phone this in, and I want questions that actually bite rather than ones she's fielded fifty times already.

Her signature framing is that people sloppily run three different claims together when they say Bell ruled out locality: no faster-than-light signalling, Bell's own factorizability / local-causality condition, and separability — the idea that the state of a composite system is fixed by the states assigned to its spacelike-separated parts. Her line is that a time-symmetric account keeps the first and the third while giving up only the second, and that giving up the second is a lot cheaper than everyone assumes. Build the questions around that: I want to make her say precisely what she's surrendered, and whether its really cheaper than what the rivals pay. Assume she's sharp and won't concede anything cheap.

Give me ten questions, each with a short note on the answer she's most likely to give and then a follow-up keyed to that answer. Coverage I need:

- at least one on the statistical-independence / "free choice" assumption, and whether her way of violating it is any less conspiratorial than a superdeterminist's — the worry being that the past gets quietly adjusted so that whatever setting I end up choosing was already correlated with the state of the source;
- at least one on why a retrocausal model doesn't let you signal into the past, and if the answer turns out to be fine-tuning, why that tuning isn't the entire cost of the view rather than a footnote;
- at least one on whether the explanation is genuinely local or just nonlocality smeared out along the time axis — the block-universe objection that a global constraint on a whole history isn't a mechanism;
- one comparing her account against many-worlds, which also claims to save locality, and pressing her to say which of the two is worse off and on what grounds;
- one asking what experiment would distinguish her view from a straightforwardly nonlocal collapse picture or from superdeterminism, or whether it's empirically equivalent to textbook quantum mechanics with the selling point being purely interpretive;
- and a closing one that forces something concrete on the separability point: a case where her account and a naive local hidden-variable account really do disagree about what the state of the whole system is.

Nothing generic about how she got into the subject, please. And keep each question short enough to be said out loud in a room rather than a mini-essay.

One more thing. I'd rather not walk in with a distorted picture of the field, so where a question rests on something genuinely contested — whether factorizability is even the right formalization of locality, say, or whether the loophole-free tests closed everything people claim they closed — flag it, and mark which of the questions presuppose a disputed reading of Bell rather than a settled result. Don't write any question that treats her retrocausal view, or any particular interpretation, as the mainstream position among people who work on this.
