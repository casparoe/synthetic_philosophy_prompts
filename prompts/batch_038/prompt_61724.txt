For three years my dissertation advisor has run a seminar on *The Poverty of Historicism* in which each week a student brings a candidate "law" and she rules on it. Her record so far:

- Gresham's law, restricted to a regime of legal tender at fixed exchange ratios: accepted as a genuine law, on the ground that it holds wherever the institution exists rather than only where it happened to arise.
- The crude quantity theory of money: rejected as stated, though she allowed that a version of it is a legitimate conditional prediction once you specify the monetary system and fix assumptions about velocity.
- Malthus's principle of population: rejected. She noted that it projected the data of its own century successfully and that this earned it nothing — but she did not say its failure was due to any missing institutional restriction.
- Michels's claim that every large organization generates a self-perpetuating leadership: accepted, because it is derived from what organization as such requires rather than from a survey of any era's parties.
- Kondratiev's long waves: rejected, as a trend fitted to a bounded stretch of the record whose periodization keeps sliding.
- The claim that revolutions strike not at the trough of misery but when a sustained improvement is abruptly reversed: accepted, explicitly as a ceteris paribus law, since she said she does not require exceptionlessness — only that a generalization come with a mechanism and survive a wide range of variations in background conditions.

Next week I am bringing a research program that fits coupled equations to long-run series on real wages, population growth, the number of credentialed aspirants to elite positions, and state fiscal capacity; that states its mechanism in terms of popular immiseration, elite overproduction, and fiscal strain; that claims a single specification fits both pre-modern agrarian states and contemporary ones; and that in 2010 published a dated forecast of rising instability in the United States and western Europe over the following decade, which its authors have since scored as borne out. Its parameters are estimated from the same historical record whose episodes it is offered to explain.

Predict her ruling and the reasoning she will give for it. State the candidate rules that her record so far permits, say which of them diverge on this case and what each delivers, and give your best guess with your reasons for preferring that rule over the others. Then identify the precedent above that she is most likely to treat as the closest analog — and tell me what case I should bring the following week to separate the surviving rules.
