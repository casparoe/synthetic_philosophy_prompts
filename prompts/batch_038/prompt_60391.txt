Having retired after thirty-five years of running breeding-bird surveys, I have taken up philosophy in earnest, and I should like my position on Hempel's raven paradox audited before I commit it to paper. My commitments are these.

1. Confirmation is probability raising: a datum confirms a hypothesis just in case it raises the probability one ought to assign to that hypothesis. I do not think anything else deserves the name.

2. I accept the equivalence condition: whatever confirms one formulation of a hypothesis confirms every formulation logically equivalent to it.

3. I accept the Nicod criterion — an observed black raven confirms "all ravens are black" — and I hold that this is in any case a hypothesis about ravens, so that the evidence bearing on it ought properly to be drawn from ravens.

4. As for the white shoe, my reply is that it confirms the hypothesis by a negligible amount only, since non-black things so vastly outnumber ravens that each such observation shifts the probability imperceptibly, whereas a well-designed sample of ravens shifts it appreciably. On that basis I regard the paradox as dissolved rather than endured.

5. If a body of data confirms a hypothesis, it must equally confirm any stronger hypothesis from which that one follows; otherwise the data would support a conclusion while failing to support the more committal claim that entails it.

6. Confirmation is an objective relation between datum and hypothesis, fixed by the form of the hypothesis, the datum, and how things are distributed in the world — not by what a particular investigator happens to know beforehand, nor by the prior degrees of belief she brings to the table. The same field notebook ought to confirm the same hypothesis for everyone.

Something in this list must give, but I cannot see what. Please audit it rather than lecture me: identify which commitments genuinely conflict and rank those conflicts by severity; identify any pair that seems to conflict but does not, and explain why the appearance arises; and for each genuine conflict state precisely which commitment must be weakened or surrendered and what I should expect to lose in the exchange. If a single revision would repair most of the damage at least cost, name it and justify the claim.
