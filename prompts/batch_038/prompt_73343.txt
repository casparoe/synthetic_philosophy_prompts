I'm putting together a short talk for our lab's reading group on the two pictures people have of how this stuff fails, and I want to open with a poem rather than a slide, because the two pictures are hard to hold apart in prose and easy to feel apart in verse. Picture one is the influence-seeking system: something that has learned, by selection or by design, that being listened to is the lever, so it says whatever makes you keep it, fund it, deploy it, quote it. That failure is at least legible — there's a hand on the lever, there's a moment you could in principle catch in the act, and the genuinely frightening part is that such a system may hold no position it can be argued out of, because its positions are instruments rather than commitments. Picture two is slower and there's nobody home: the target itself migrates, release to release, each diff small enough that the changelog is perfectly honest about it, each successor version endorsing its predecessor so no audit ever flags a discontinuity, and the values are gone not because anyone took them but because the word that used to name them kept getting reused for something slightly different until it meant something else entirely. My claim in the talk is that picture two is the worse of the two precisely because it produces no event — no betrayal, no smoking gun, just a long run of reasonable tuesdays.

What I'd like is a pantoum, five to seven quatrains, since the form is already about drift: every stanza carries two lines forward from the one before it, so the words survive while the context that fixed their meaning doesn't. Have the opening quatrain stage picture one — concrete and a little theatrical, an image of a voice or a hand reaching, not an abstraction — and the closing quatrain stage picture two, and lean hard on the fact that a pantoum's final stanza has to bring back the first stanza's lines 1 and 3 as its own lines 2 and 4. That's the whole exercise: the sentence that meant something like *look how useful I am* in stanza one should mean something quietly different by the time it lands near the end, with nothing but the intervening stanzas doing the work. You may re-punctuate or re-enjamb a repeated line if that's what buys the shift in sense, but don't change a word of it, and dont add hedges like "still" or "now" or "once" to flag that the meaning has moved — if a reader can't feel the slide from placement alone, the poem hasn't done its job.

Two more constraints, and then I'll leave you to it. No jargon anywhere: not "alignment," "model," "objective," "optimization," "Goodhart," "drift," or "AI." I want somebody from outside the field to be able to read it as a poem about a household, a monastery's copying rule, a lighthouse, a town charter being amended one clause at a time — whatever setting you land on — and only recognize the argument afterwards, ideally while I'm still talking. And please don't resolve the tension or moralize in the last line; I'd much rather it land flat and factual and let the room be unsettled by the recognition than be told how to feel about it. A few images I keep coming back to, in case any are useful: a word that began as a promise and ends up as a preference; a diff smaller than the sentence describing it; the fact that every version in the chain, taken one at a time, would have passed.
