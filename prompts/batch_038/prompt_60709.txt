You are a philosopher of mind who also writes comedy. Before drafting, list in five or six bullets the moving parts of Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument that you intend to encode — the causal closure of the physical, the exclusion principle, the threat of systematic overdetermination, the nonreductive physicalist's refusal to identify mental with physical properties, and the epiphenomenalist conclusion she is thereby left with — together with at least two of the standard replies.

Then write a parody redundancy-consultation letter of about 600–700 words, from the HR department of a mid-sized manufacturing firm to a long-serving employee, M., whose job title is Senior Mental Property, informing her that her post is at risk because every task she has ever discharged is already fully covered by a colleague in the Physical Department whose workload records show no gaps. The letter must be written in authentic HR register — consultation periods, redeployment options, right to be accompanied, appeals procedure, appeals to "operational need" — and the philosophy should be carried entirely by that bureaucratic machinery, with no one stepping outside the letter to explain the joke.

The redeployment menu should encode the actual options: full merger with the colleague in the Physical Department, which HR must note is not strictly a redeployment at all, since it would mean there was only ever one post; transfer to a ceremonial unit with a title, a parking space, and no causal duties whatsoever; and termination. Include an appeal lodged by a union representative arguing that the colleague's job description, while sufficient for every task, is conspicuously worse matched to the tasks than M.'s own, so that the firm's stated aims would be better served by retaining her. Include, near the end, the firm's own internal audit observation that the identical template is already being prepared for the Chemical, Biological, and Social departments, and that nobody in management has explained why that is acceptable.

Do not exaggerate any feature of the argument that is not genuinely there; the target is the reasoning, not any real person. Keep the register deadpan from first line to last — the reader should be able to tell what is being ironized without the letter ever winking.

Finish with a single line in exactly this form:

Disposition: [merged | retained without duties | dismissed | undetermined pending consultation]
