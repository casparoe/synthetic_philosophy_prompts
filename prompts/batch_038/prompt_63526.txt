I'm a software engineer with about eight years on ranking and recommendation systems. For the last three I've worked on the feed for a mid-sized consumer app — recall and funnel stages, pointwise and listwise objectives, engagement-weighted labels, calibration, guardrail metrics, cohort aggregates, holdouts and interaction effects in A/B tests, moderation queues, repost provenance, rate limits — and more recently on the sampling-temperature and top-p knobs for the LLM features we ship. I have never taken a philosophy course. A coworker lent me the long third section of Kierkegaard's 1846 review of a novel called *Two Ages*, the part that gets published on its own as *The Present Age*, and I have now read it twice, mostly on flights. Part of me thinks it is a postmortem of my own job written nearly two centuries before my job existed. The other part suspects the resemblance is an artifact of my vocabulary being loose enough to fit anything, and that is the part I want your help with.

Here is what I take him to be claiming, in my paraphrase with the passages I kept coming back to quoted from the translation I was given. (1) An age of revolution is passionate: it "accelerates, raises up, and overthrows, elevates and debases," and it is obliged to decide — he calls decision "the little magic word that existence respects." The present age is instead reflective and passionless, "flaring up in superficial, short-lived enthusiasm and prudentially relaxing in indolence," and what it does in place of deciding is level. (2) It aggregates persons quantitatively: "the trend today is in the direction of mathematical equality, so that in all classes about so and so many uniformly make one individual." (3) The public is a phantom raised in order for leveling to happen: "For leveling really to take place, a phantom must first be raised, the spirit of leveling, a monstrous abstraction, an all-encompassing something that is nothing, a mirage–and this phantom is the public"; and "The public is the actual master of leveling, for when there is approximate leveling, something is doing the leveling, but the public is a monstrous nonentity." It is not a people, not a generation, not a congregation, not some particular persons, because those are concretions. (4) The press is the mechanism that produces it: the phantom develops "with the aid of the press, when the press itself becomes a phantom," and "There is no such thing as a public in spirited, passionate, tumultuous times." (5) Leveling is nobody's doing: "Leveling is not the action of one individual but a reflection-game in the hand of an abstract power," and no age can stop it, because the attempt to halt leveling exemplifies the law all over again; it "can be halted only if the individual, in individual separateness, gains the intrepidity of religiousness." (6) Everything that enters the press circulates as talk, and the serious and the trivial circulate identically, so the difference between them stops being visible. (7) A statement can be made, repeated, and re-reported by so many outlets that no individual ever stands behind it, while the sheer fact that many said it acquires the weight of agreement. I am not fully certain (6) and (7) are his points rather than things I absorbed from people writing about him; if either is not actually in the book's argument, tell me instead of translating a claim he never made. (8) He says a passionate age has not nullified the principle of contradiction and so can become either good or evil, whereas the present age has nullified it. I do not know what that means and I suspect it depends on Hegel. (9) The leveling he wants is religious — each individual equal before God — and it requires that each person be passionately related to an idea and, together with others, related to the same idea; the leveling of the public is its counterfeit, producing a union of separately weak individuals.

Now the task. Translate each of those into the vocabulary of my work, one by one, as literally as you honestly can, and tag each one CLEAN, DISTORTED, or NO COUNTERPART, with the translation itself and, wherever the tag is not CLEAN, exactly which term you bent and what the bending cost. Two ground rules. First, no smuggling: if you render his "passion" as something like a temperature parameter or the variance of a distribution, say so and say what is lost, because his passion seems to be about how a person stands toward an idea rather than a property of a distribution. Second, a translation counts as clean only if a colleague who has never read him would recognize the result as a true statement about systems like ours, not merely as a metaphor that sounds apt. Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually want. Where does the residue sit — is it a defect in my vocabulary, meaning we genuinely lack a primitive for a single subject appropriating something, or for responsibility attaching to a named speaker, or for quality as against quantity? Or is it a sign that some of his claims were less clear than they sound: is "an all-encompassing something that is nothing" anything more than the observation that an aggregate statistic is not an agent, which is true of every aggregate statistic ever computed and therefore not much of an indictment? Tell me which of my own candidate mappings are structural parallels and which are homonyms — the public as a cohort aggregate that appears in our dashboards and in executive reviews although no user in it ever agreed to be in it; leveling as literal feature normalization; talk as content whose provenance has been dropped by fan-out, so an assertion is repeated by handles that no longer point at a source; passionless as temperature zero; reflection as an optimizer that keeps evaluating and never commits; the single individual as the N=1 that every aggregate metric is built to erase. And one question in the other direction: is there anything load-bearing in how our systems work that his framework has no place for — personalization is my guess, since he seems to assume a single homogeneous press addressing a single homogeneous crowd — and if so, does that weaken his diagnosis of us, or just mean it applies to the part of our stack he could not have imagined?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence: one sentence saying what survived the translation and what the residue is. Then give the reasoning, claim by claim in my order, then the residue report. No preamble and no restatement of my question. Don't lean on Hegel or on the pseudonym business; I would have no way to check you, and I would rather you say a claim is untranslated than dress it up.
