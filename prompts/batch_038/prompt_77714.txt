Last spring our county council floated a pilot program that would have paid living kidney donors a fixed stipend and paired the payments with a licensing regime for brokers; the proposal died in committee, but our informal reading group kept arguing about it for months afterward, and a friend of mine — I will call him Daniel, who donates plasma twice a week to help cover rent, so none of this is abstract for him — built a defense of the program that I have since come to think proves far more than he intended. I cannot locate the step where it goes wrong, and I would like someone to do it for me. Here is the argument as he wrote it out for us.

(1) A voluntary exchange between two competent, adequately informed adults is impermissible only if it wrongs somebody: only if it is tainted by coercion or deception, or if it leaves some identifiable person worse off than that person would otherwise have been. (2) If both parties judge the exchange worth making at the moment they make it, and neither is coerced nor deceived, then the exchange leaves no one worse off, since each receives what they rank above what they give up. (3) Therefore no such exchange is impermissible. (4) Paid kidney donation, commercial surrogacy, the sale of votes, and post-emergency price increases that buyers nonetheless accept are all exchanges of this kind. (5) So none of them is impermissible, and whatever remains of the objection to them must be paternalism or bare squeamishness. (6) But by identical reasoning a laborer may sell himself into lifelong servitude, a parent may auction a child to the highest bidder, and I may contract away my own standing to object to being harmed later, in each case provided that I judge it worthwhile and that no one deceives me; for in each case the parties get what they rank above what they give up, and the test in (1) is satisfied. (7) Those transactions are impermissible. (8) So (1) is false: there are impermissible exchanges that wrong nobody, and (5) collapses along with it.

Daniel took (8) as a reductio of his own starting principle and has since retreated to the position that such matters should be decided case by case. I think that concedes too cheaply, because I want to keep (1). I still believe that whether a market is objectionable has to have something to do with whether anyone is wronged by it, and if that belief is simply mistaken I would rather be told so plainly than be handed a substitute principle that does all the work by stipulation. I also want to keep (3) for plasma donation, for ordinary wage labor, and probably for the kidney program roughly as it was drafted. What I am least attached to is (4) — though I notice Daniel wrote it as a list precisely so that nobody could quietly drop items from it, and I am not certain I have earned the right to drop any.

So: is this argument valid as stated, or does one of the steps do something illicit, and if so which one and why did it read as innocuous when we went through it aloud? If it is valid, which premise is the cheapest to surrender and what does surrendering it cost, particularly for the cases in (4) that I most want to protect? I would also like to know whether (4) is genuinely a package. I have an inchoate sense that the four exchanges listed there are not all on the same footing with respect to (6), and that a defender of (1) might be able to keep some of them without keeping the rest; if that is so, say which, and say whether the distinction you draw can be stated without simply begging the question against the kidney program. Finally, tell me what someone who holds on to (1) owes us about (6) — not a dodge, but the actual principle that separates the lifelong servitude contract from the plasma appointment, and whether that same principle delivers the verdicts I want on surrogacy and on price spikes in the week after a storm, or whether it delivers verdicts I would have to learn to live with.

Please write the whole thing as continuous prose, the way you would write a letter or a long email: no headings, no bullet points or numbered lists, no bold or italic emphasis, no tables. I want the diagnosis argued out in paragraphs, and I would rather you commit to one than survey the available exits and leave me holding all of them.
