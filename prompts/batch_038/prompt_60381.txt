So I'm helping write the rules for a small housing co-op I'm about to join, and I've realized I've been carrying around a pile of beliefs about binding-yourself-in-advance stuff that I've never actually set down next to each other. I'd like someone to tell me whether they hang together. Here they are, roughly:

(1) Committing yourself ahead of time is real and legitimate — the calm you can tie the hands of the tempted you, and that isn't a loss of freedom, it's a use of it. I do the personal version too: my savings sit in an account I literally cannot touch for another twenty years, and I put them there on purpose.

(2) But such a commitment only does any work if you can't undo it once the pressure arrives. A rule you're free to waive the moment it starts costing you something isn't a commitment, it's a suggestion. So the co-op rule I want is that no unit can ever be rented out, and the only way to lift it is a unanimous vote of the members.

(3) I also think it's wrong for people who won't be around to feel the consequences to lock in how the living have to live. Rules should stay answerable to the people currently stuck with them.

(4) The way I usually square (3) with (1) and (2) is this: it's fine to be bound so long as the people being bound can still be taken to endorse the rule — not because they signed anything, but because they'd still say yes if you asked them in a clear-headed moment.

(5) And separately: I think people's values genuinely change, sometimes completely, and that this is a normal and often good part of a life rather than a symptom of weakness that needs protecting against.

Which of these actually pull against each other and which only look like they do? I can feel something's off but I can't say what, and I don't have a preference yet about which one I'd drop. Rank the problems by how bad they are instead of treating them all as equally worrying, say for each what I'd have to give up or water down and what that would cost me, and then tell me which single change would buy me the most. No headings or bullet points or bold please, and no tables — just write it straight through like an email, continuous prose, the way you'd explain it to someone across a kitchen table.
