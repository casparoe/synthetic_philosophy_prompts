I am setting the final examination for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in ancient ethics. The students have worked through Epictetus's Handbook, selections from Seneca and Marcus Aurelius, and a critical discussion of the contemporary revival of Stoicism as a technique for managing everyday setbacks. Please compose a single essay question on the dichotomy of control, worth 30 marks and answerable in 45 minutes.

The question should be built around two claims that students must weigh against each other, and it should not signal which is correct:

(1) The Stoic agent attaches a tacit qualification to every aim — she pursues the thing only if nothing interferes — so that the value lies in having pursued well rather than in succeeding. But if every intention carries such a qualification, then no failure can ever show that an agent was aiming at something not up to her: whatever she loses can be redescribed as something she was never aiming at in the first place. On that reading the dichotomy ceases to be a substantive thesis about what is good and becomes a report on wherever the agent's effort happened to land.

(2) The Stoic can reply that the dichotomy was never a claim about the success of intentions, but about the proper objects of desire, and that the archer's target stands in for a class of things whose goodness does not depend on hitting.

Students must decide whether (1) demonstrates that the distinction is insulated against counterexample, or whether it conflates two different roles the notion of being up to us is asked to play; and they must say what would actually count as a failure of the distinction if it is not insulated.

Please supply: (a) the question exactly as students would see it, with any short case or argument sketch needed to set it up; (b) a model answer of about 700 words; (c) a marking scheme that distributes the 30 marks across named moves and states what an A answer must contain that a B answer predictably omits; and (d) three specific errors that should cost marks, each with a note on why it is wrong rather than merely underdeveloped. Do not spend words on biography or on surveying the modern Stoicism industry.
