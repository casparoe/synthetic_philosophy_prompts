In a reading group on formal methods and agency, one member proposed that a self-improving system could rationally endorse its own upgrades so long as it checked each one before installing it. A second member closed the discussion with the familiar line that 'a system cannot prove its own consistency', taking it to be decisive: an agent that audits its own reasoning is auditing with the very instrument under inspection. A third said this misreads the result entirely — that the obstacle isn't about consistency at all, and that the second member was invoking a theorem that doesn't say what he takes it to say. I've been asked to referee the exchange and currently can't.

So: what is that slogan actually claiming? Lay out the distinct theses it could stand for — the precise result about a theory proving its own consistency sentence, informal claims about a reasoner certifying its own reliability, claims about an agent's trust in what its successors will conclude, and anything in between — and for each say whether it is true as stated, whether it concerns consistency, soundness, or reliability, and whether it licenses dismissing the proposal. Then tell me which reading the second member would need, and what an agent would have to believe about its own proofs for that reading to have any force.
