So, slightly odd request. I'm a novelist — I've spent three years down a rabbit hole for a book about a society coming apart, and somewhere along the line I stopped caring about the book and started caring about the concept underneath it. The institute where I did a summer residency has a strand for writers who do actual research, and they've invited me to apply for a nine-month fellowship, but the application wants a research proposal, not a book pitch. Two pages. Could you write it for me?

The half-formed idea, such as it is: everyone arguing about "collapse" — the Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, the Classic Maya lowlands, Rapa Nui, wherever — seems to be arguing about four different things at once and not noticing. There's the polity (is there still a palace, a levy, a named king). There's the population (how many people are alive and fed). There's the settlement pattern (cities versus dispersed villages). And there's the record: the writing, the standard weights, the dated monuments, the stuff that lets us know anything at all. Those four come apart constantly. A place can lose its archives and its dynasty and keep its people and its farming for another century and a half, and we still file it under collapse — mostly, I think, because the record is the thing *we* lost. So the word may be doing more work about our sources than about the people.

What I want the proposal to state sharply: is collapse a real kind of event that these cases genuinely share, or is it a plot shape we lay over them — and if it decomposes into those separate tracks, does the concept survive the decomposition or fall apart once you take it apart? I'd also like the proposal to have some view on what an answer to that would even consist in.

What it needs in it:

- the question phrased so it's answerable rather than vibes;
- a section on why the two camps I keep running into don't settle it — the general-theory people (diminishing returns on complexity and so on) and the people who say drop the word entirely, its transformation not collapse, and losing the court was no disaster for the farmers who'd been feeding it;
- the method, meaning what I would literally do, month by month, given that I'm a writer and not an archaeologist — and I want at least part of the method to be something a writer is unusually well placed to do. My hunch is you can test a concept by seeing which narrations it licenses: write the same decades from the polity track, then the subsistence track, then the record track, and see which of those stories the word "collapse" actually permits and which ones it suppresses;
- what a successful result looks like, and what would count as the project failing rather than merely being inconclusive — I want that second part to hurt a little;
- an honest note on scope, because I suspect three case studies in nine months is a fantasy and the proposal should say so and pick.

No bibliography and no citations please — my sponsor is a real archeologist and she'll bolt the references on afterwards. Positions and arguments by description are fine. And don't write it in grant-speak. I want it to read like somebody thinking, not somebody requesting funds.
