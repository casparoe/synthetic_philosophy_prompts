I need to settle a decision about my own compensation, and an argument with my brother has left me unsure whether my reasoning can be defended.

I do contract engineering work. My largest client is letting each contractor pick one of two year-end arrangements. The first is a flat, guaranteed payment of $50,000. The second pays $50,000 with an 89 percent chance, $2.5 million with a 10 percent chance, and nothing at all with the remaining 1 percent. I took the guaranteed payment, and I took it quickly: finishing the year with nothing felt intolerable even at those odds.

Separately, my brother asked me into a speculative property deal and offered two ways to participate. The first gives me a 10 percent chance of $2.5 million and a 90 percent chance of nothing. The second gives me an 11 percent chance of $50,000 and an 89 percent chance of nothing. I took the first — a genuine shot at the $2.5 million rather than a marginally better shot at a sum I can earn in the ordinary course of a year.

My brother says I have contradicted myself. He insists that the second decision is simply the first one with the 89 percent that both options share reset from $50,000 to zero, that no consistent way of weighing outcomes permits me to choose as I did on both occasions, and that a sharp counterparty who spotted the pattern could walk me through a series of paid swaps and finish with my entire bonus while I finished exactly where I began. I cannot picture how that sequence would actually unfold.

I do not feel confused. In the client's case there was a certain fifty thousand dollars on the table; in my brother's there was not. I also checked the obvious worry, and the order does not matter: had I been asked about his deal first and my client's plans second, I would have chosen the same way both times.

Here is what I would like from you. First, whether my brother is right that both choices cannot stand, and what precisely the conflict consists in if it does. Second, whether his swap sequence genuinely works — including whether it still works if I anticipate it, and whether it depends on the swaps being offered before, rather than after, I learn how the year turned out. Third, whether the guaranteed money is entitled to be treated as a different kind of thing from the same amount sitting inside a gamble, since it lets me sign a lease and stop watching my account balance, or whether invoking that is only a dignified way of restating the error. And fourth, if one of my two choices has to change, which one should it be, and why the other is allowed to stay.

Please write your answer in American English.
