I'm a backend engineer at a freight logistics startup. I built a tool that estimates how long a project takes (onboarding a new shipper, standing up a warehouse integration, that kind of thing) and instead of training a model on features it looks up a reference class of past projects that were similar and uses their actual outcomes — basically the outside view, someone at work called it that. My twist on it: the tool walks a hierarchy of classes from narrow to broad and picks the narrowest one that clears a sample-size and a stability bar, and when two levels disagree by more than some threshold it blends them weighted by past calibaration error. My claim for the talk is that this beats a gradient boosting model on features for our data, and that the hierarchy rule basically solves the problem of which class you're even allowed to use.
