I serve on my university's research-integrity panel. A senior professor — call him A — has filed a complaint alleging that a colleague, B, used a former student's unpublished idea in an article without attribution. On the documents the allegation looks credible. But in reviewing conflicts we learned that A himself quietly settled two complaints of exactly this omission years ago, and that one of the students involved now sits on our panel. Our charter directs us to assess complaints on their merits, disregarding who brought them.

The panel is split. Two members hold that A's own record costs him the right to level this accusation, and that hearing him out is an insult to the people he wronged. Two hold that a true complaint is true no matter who files it, and that dismissing it simply lets B off the hook. The fifth argues that both sides have misdescribed the situation: even if A has lost standing to blame B, the university is not A, and nothing in A's history touches the university's standing to act. A further wrinkle is that A appears to have filed now partly to forestall any reopening of his own cases.

Please draft the memo we will circulate before Friday's meeting: a definite recommendation (proceed on the merits, dismiss, or proceed under stated conditions), the reasoning behind it, and a serious treatment of the strongest case for the losing side. It should be precise about what A's hypocrisy does and does not affect, and it should address B in terms B could actually answer.
