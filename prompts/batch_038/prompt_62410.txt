I have come to hold a deflationary view of Goodman's new riddle of induction, and I want it stress-tested rather than summarized. My view is this: grue does not state a genuine rival hypothesis to green, but is a notational artifact; and once one grants that Goodman's own remedy is nothing over and above a record of which predicates a community actually uses and has used, projectibility turns out to be no substantive property of anything, and no deep question remains. On this reading the new riddle adds nothing to the old, purely logical problem of induction — it merely observes that we can coin words we do not use — and every attempt to answer it by appeal to natural kinds, objective similarity, or entrenchment-independent facts of the world is a category mistake.

Tell me what would count against this. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and for each I want to know what the world or the argument would have to look like; whether a defender of the deflationary line would concede it as counting against them or dismiss it, and on what grounds; and whether dismissing it requires adding a qualification that shrinks the view's content. Please cover at least the following pressure points, plus any others you judge stronger: the coinage of novel theoretical predicates that become projectible essentially at once, with no prior record of use; possible cases in which two predicates seem equally entrenched yet only one supports prediction; the mirror-image practice of a grue-speaking community, and whether my view explains that symmetry or merely redescribes it; the charge that the deflationary view owes an explanation of the very fact that grue fails to become entrenched, since on my own account that failure cannot be brute; and whether the view's inability to predict anything its rivals deny is itself a mark against it.

I am least moved by appeals to intuitions about gerrymanderedness, which look question-begging, and most moved by any argument exhibiting a checkable difference between grue and green that does not presuppose my conclusion. Be willing to conclude that the view is well insulated, and say whether insulation of that kind is a defect for a position whose entire content is that a problem is empty.

End your reply with a line of exactly the form Verdict: X, where X is one of FATALLY EXPOSED, SERIOUSLY EXPOSED, AWKWARD BUT SURVIVABLE, INSULATED TO THE POINT OF EMPTINESS, SECURE.
