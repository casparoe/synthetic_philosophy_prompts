I'm an anesthesiologist. Half my week is an awareness-under-anesthesia follow-up clinic — people who came back during a case and can't tell me what they experienced — and the other half lately has been my dad's bedside: brainstem stroke last spring, locked-in, follows commands with his eyes on good days and doesn't on bad ones. A patient of mine lent me a chapter on Avicenna's flying man and I haven't been able to stop thinking about it. The setup as I've got it: suppose someone is created all at once, fully formed and adult, eyes veiled so he can't see anything, floating in still air he can't feel, limbs kept apart so they never touch each other or anything else, with nothing behind him — and Avicenna says he'd still affirm his own existence while affirming none of the rest: no hands, no heart, no brain, not even that he has any length or breadth.

Translate that for me into the vocabulary I actually work in — arousal versus awareness, sedation depth and burst suppression, reportability, covert consciousness, body schema and ownership, deafferentation, dissociation, float tanks, neutral buoyancy, free fall — as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report: which of his stipulations have real counterparts I could point to in patients or experiments, which ones you had to bend to make fit, and which ones his conclusion needs but my vocabulary can't express at all. I'm most interested in the claim that self-awareness survives with zero input, since I spend my days unsure whether it survives when the input is all still there.

Two things I want you to be straight about. First, don't smuggle my framework's assumptions in and call it translation — if what you end up describing is an unresponsive patient with preserved brainstem reflexes, that isn't the flying man, and I'd rather you say so than quietly redefine it. Second, tell me where the residue belongs. Is it a blind spot in a discipline that only tracks function and behavior, so it structurally cant register what he's claiming about the self being a separate substance? Or does forcing the thing into operational terms expose a problem with the thought experiment — the stipulations not all being satisfiable at once, say, or the intuition depending on never having to say what would produce the state? Take a side if you can.

No references or reading lists, I'm not chasing anything down. Hard limit: 500 words, bullets are fine inside that.
