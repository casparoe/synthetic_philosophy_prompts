Seven years ago, after a genuinely bad stretch where I quit a job on a Tuesday and drained most of my savings by autumn, my brother and I wrote up a document about the duplex we own together. If I ever want to sell my half, I have to give him 90 days notice plus a written reason, and he gets to refuse if he judges that it's me-being-me rather than me thinking clearly. I asked him for that clause. His standard at the time was pretty explicit, something like "if the reason sounds exciting, the answer is no."

Since then I got an ADHD diagnosis at 38 and started treatment, and a lot of the assumptions baked into the wording — mainly that any strong desire to cash out is a symptom — look flat wrong now. There's an actual buyer, at a number that isn't coming back, and I want the money for something else.

My brother says the 31-year-old knew me better than I know myself, and that the entire point was to stop me from taking my own side now. My girlfriend says I can't be governed by a guess that's since been falsified, by someone who wasn't even me.

I want the real argument here, not tips about willpower. Which of them is right, and is there some narrower version of the veto I should be fighting to keep rather than kill outright?
