For the better part of two decades I have read and argued in the rationalist corner of the internet — LessWrong, Overcoming Bias, and the descendants of the old astrobiology mailing lists — and I have accumulated a fairly stable set of reflexes about which resolutions of the Fermi paradox feel like explanations and which feel like shrugs wearing an explanation's clothes. I want help turning those reflexes into a principle. Treat my glosses as accurate and reason from them alone: I am not asking which of these is true, and I do not want a survey, a taxonomy, or a reading list. I want to know what my verdicts add up to.

Here they are, each with the gloss I'm working from:

1. **Aestivation** (Sandberg, Armstrong, and Ćirković): computing is cheaper at lower temperatures, so any civilization optimizing total computation should bank its energy and wait for the universe to cool, which means the present epoch is precisely when a galaxy of optimizers would be quiet. Satisfying — genuinely explanatory.

2. **Dark forest**: expansion and broadcasting are lethal because preemptive strikes are cheap and intentions are unverifiable, so survivors are silent. Satisfying, though I cannot quite articulate why it feels better than number 4.

3. **Grabby aliens / we are early**: expanding civilizations restructure the sky visibly and fast, and the sky is not restructured; therefore no such civilization has reached us yet, and our being early is exactly what that observation licenses. Very satisfying.

4. **Zoo hypothesis**: we are observed and deliberately left alone by a community of civilizations that has agreed not to interfere. Unsatisfying — leaky across millions of years and billions of worlds, and it does nothing about astroengineering we should see anyway.

5. **Planetarium hypothesis** (Baxter): something with galaxy-scale control has arranged our observations to present a convincingly lifeless universe, so that the silence is manufactured rather than real. Satisfying — and this is the pairing I cannot reconcile, since 5 is structurally 4 with the restraint turned up to eleven and a deception added.

6. **Transcension / the inward turn**: postbiological civilizations lose interest in expansion and compute inside themselves. Unsatisfying, despite being about as well-grounded as 1 and arguably better grounded than 5.

7. **Gaian bottleneck** (Chopra and Lineweaver): early biospheres are fragile and usually fail to stabilize their planet, so the filter is between simple and complex life. Partly satisfying.

8. **Self-destruction**: they blow themselves up shortly after acquiring the capability. Unsatisfying, and annoyingly so — it explains the silence by predicting our own demise and otherwise does no work.

9. **The priors dissolve it**: propagate honest uncertainty through the Drake factors and there is a substantial chance we are alone in the observable universe. Satisfying as Bayesian hygiene, empty as an explanation: it tells me the silence should not have surprised me, not why there is silence.

10. **They are here**: the silence is a failure of our evidence-gathering. Actively annoying.

My tentative principle is this: *a resolution explains the silence to the extent that the silence follows from physics or biology we already accept, plus the assumption that the relevant agents are competent optimizers, and does not require a fresh assumption about what those agents choose, want, or feel.* I know it is wrong, because it condemns 5 and 2 outright, and I like both, and it barely discriminates 1 from 6.

So: give me the principle, or a small set of competing candidates, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a case I have not seen. For each clause, tell me which of my verdicts motivates it — I suspect I need separate clauses for the leakiness objection to 4, for something about the *scope* of the concealment in 5 versus 4, and for why 1 and 6 differ when both are just a claim about what optimizers would prefer. Then tell me which of my verdicts the best candidate cannot save, and whether a simpler principle that overturns one of them is preferable to a baroque one that accommodates all ten. Apply it to three cases I have not listed: civilizations that broadcast by modulating naturally occurring astrophysical sources so their signals are indistinguishable from noise to anyone without the key; a quiet neighborhood in which expansion is common but clumpy and we sit in a region skipped by percolation, so the silence is a selection effect on location rather than on time; and a single-enforcer interdict, where one dominant civilization polices contact instead of a consensus of many. Finally, tell me whether the principle you land on has any purchase on my confidence in truth, as opposed to my aesthetic satisfaction — if it is purely a theory of which stories feel good, say so plainly and tell me whether that is a defect or the correct verdict. If it generalizes to other places where an unfalsifiable posited agency does explanatory work, note where, in a sentence or two.
