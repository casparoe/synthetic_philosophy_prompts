You are an expert in the philosophy of cosmology and in the technical literature on anthropic reasoning. I want a careful adjudication of an argument I sat through last term and could not referee. Think hard before writing: first work out, for yourself, which premises each move depends on and what would have to be true for it to succeed; only then compose your answer.

My graduate reading group was working through Bostrom's "presumptuous philosopher" case against the self-indication assumption. Two members — I will call them W., a philosopher, and T., a physicist auditing the group — reconstructed the case and then disagreed about what it proves. Neither was posturing.

(1) T.: Stipulate two cosmological theories, on a par a priori, that differ only in how many observers they say ever exist — one trillion under T1, one trillion trillion under T2. SIA then licenses a trillion-to-one posterior for T2 on the sole basis of the fact that you exist. No telescope, no experiment: a metaphysical free lunch. That is a reductio of SIA.

(2) W.: The case assumes that indexical, self-locating information is not evidence, which is precisely what is in dispute. That observers exist is a fact about the world, and a world containing more of them makes your existence likelier. If that inference is illegitimate, so is every update on the information that you are here, awake, and now — and you owe an account of what is special about this one.

(3) T.: Then your objection proves too much against SSA as well, since SSA delivers the doomsday argument; so what we are comparing is two absurdities. But they are not comparable in magnitude: the presumptuous philosopher shifts credence by twelve orders of magnitude on nothing, while doomsday shifts a distribution over the total number of humans by a few orders of magnitude on the genuine datum of my birth rank.

(4) W.: Magnitude is the wrong metric — the doomsday reasoner has a datum, the presumptuous philosopher has none. And your escape from doomsday is to narrow SSA's reference class, which shows your principle is not determinate: the reference class is doing the work, and since retuning it changes the verdict in the trillion-trillion case as well, your argument never isolates SIA.

(5) T.: Then concede where the content lies — drop both labels and ask, theory by theory, which observers one is sampling from. W.: That is a relabeling, not a solution, and in the trillion-trillion case any reference class containing me at all returns the same answer.

Please go through the exchange move by move. For each of (1)–(5), assign a verdict — clearly right, partly right, question-begging, or clearly mistaken — with a brief justification that names the premise doing the work, and flag explicitly any move that simply assumes what the other side denies. Then answer two further questions: whether T.'s "two absurdities" comparison in (3) is well posed, given that the two shifts differ in kind and not merely in size; and what the exchange leaves out, including what becomes of both principles if T2 is a theory on which infinitely many observers exist, where the likelihood ratio SIA requires may not be defined at all. Close with a single paragraph saying which of the two is more nearly right about the case as they set it up, and what you would need to know in order to be confident of that.
