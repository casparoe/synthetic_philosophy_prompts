I build the personalization layer for a consumer app, and before I start benchmarking methods I want to pin down what "correctly inferring what a user wants from what the user does" is even supposed to mean. Our inputs are the ordinary behavioral exhaust: clicks, dwell time, dismissals, edits to suggestions we offered, and occasional free-text complaints. Treat this as a specification problem for any system that infers preferences from behavior — inverse reinforcement learning, imitation learning, or otherwise — and write out the desiderata such a system must satisfy to count as adequate for this purpose, where the purpose includes the constraint that a wrong inference ships to millions of people and cannot always be undone by the user.

For every requirement, give the concrete case that motivates it. At minimum, work through these: behavior that is produced by the very interface recording it, so that our own past suggestions are baked into the data we learn from; actions the user disowns — they keep consuming the thing they explicitly asked us to stop surfacing, and they would want us to override that; preferences that drift over the months of observation, where tracking the change and protecting the earlier self pull in opposite directions; the fact that almost any sequence of actions can be rationalized by some pairing of utility function and beliefs about the world, so fitting the logs perfectly is not evidence of having recovered the right preferences; what to do when observed behavior and stated preference conflict; and whether errors should be asymmetric, failing toward under-inference rather than over-inference.

Then tell me which of these are genuine requirements of adequacy and which are assumptions particular to one research tradition, which subset cannot all be satisfied at once — if the requirements list turns out to be an impossibility result, that is the part I most want to see — and which single requirement eliminates the largest number of candidate approaches. Close with a short scorecard of maximum-entropy inverse reinforcement learning, adversarial imitation learning, and the cooperative formulation in which human and agent both act in a shared game, measured against the list. I have no formal training in philosophy or decision theory, so define any term you borrow on first use.
