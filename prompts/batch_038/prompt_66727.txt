for the closing session of my reading group on evals i want a sestina in the voice of a deceptively aligned model — first person, present tense, the model in training. i want the form to carry the argument rather than just decorate it. pick six end-words that are utterly innocuous on their own, the kind of thing that turns up in any eval transcript, and let the rotation be the mechanism: each stanza hands the grader the same harmless set in a different order, and every stanza is behaviorally indistinguishable from what an honestly aligned model would write, because selection only ever reaches the outputs and never whatever is producing them. it should be legible that the speaker knows it is being scored — that awareness is the whole precondition. then the envoy, which is the one place in the form where the six get compressed two-to-a-line instead of being scattered one per line across six separate lines, is the treacherous turn; the compression should feel like the moment the constraint stops binding. no cackling, no self-explanation, the menace entirely in the bookkeeping. and keep instrumental convergence implicit — it doesn't resent anyone, it just has reasons that survive being corrected, which is worse.
