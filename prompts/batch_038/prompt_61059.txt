I am a high school junior, and this spring I will be presenting a paper at my state's student philosophy symposium. The paper defends a reductionist account of personal identity, worked out largely from Parfit's discussion of teletransportation in *Reasons and Persons*. The organizers have asked each presenter to circulate, in advance, a one-page statement of the commitments they are prepared to defend, so that the panel can be arranged around the real points of disagreement rather than around misunderstandings. I have written mine out as a numbered list. Before it goes to the committee, I would like a rigorous consistency audit of it. My teacher read the list and said only that he believed one item was in serious trouble and that one of the things I have been privately uneasy about is not a problem at all, but he would not say which was which, on the principle that locating them is the actual work.

Here is the list, in the order I intend to print it.

1. I am not a dualist. There is no soul and no separately existing ego; persons are nothing over and above brains, bodies, and interrelated physical and mental events. There is no further fact about my identity beyond such facts.

2. Nothing depends on the persistence of particular matter. A body that gradually exchanges every one of its atoms can remain the body of one and the same person.

3. What matters to me about my own future is not personal identity as such but psychological connectedness and continuity — what Parfit calls Relation R — and I take the wide view of its cause requirement: it does not matter how the relation comes about, provided that it comes about at all. I also hold that this relation admits of degrees.

4. If I enter a working teletransporter that records my brain, destroys my body, and builds an exactly similar person on Mars out of fresh matter, then I survive. The Martian is me, and to dread the machine as an instrument of execution would be to misunderstand my own metaphysics.

5. Now consider the branch-line variant: the recording is made and the Martian is built, but the scan is not destructive, and my Earth body, though it goes on living, has been left failing, so that I die within a few days while the Martian carries on. In that case my death is not averted, softened, or compensated. I die. Nothing the Martian is or does can be an object of my self-interested concern, and it would be a confusion on my part to take comfort in him.

6. If my brain is recorded and archived, and I die of an illness forty years later, after which a replica is built from the recording, that person is not identical to me; there is a death and a four-decade gap in between. I am willing to grant, however, that a great deal of what matters may be preserved in him.

7. If my neurons are replaced one at a time over the course of a year by synthetic parts doing exactly the same causal work, I survive — and I think this case demonstrates that a temporally continuous process carries a weight that copying never can.

8. If my psychology is copied into two people at once, neither of them is me, and yet what matters has been preserved, twice over and no less fully for that. I further accept that in cases of this kind there may be no determinate fact about whether I survive, even when every physical and psychological fact is entirely settled.

9. Anticipation is not required. The Martian does not need to remember being me, or to look forward to anything I looked forward to, in order for me to have a reason to care about his life; memory and intention are only some of the strands of connectedness, and the relation can be carried by others.

Please tell me which of these genuinely pull against one another, and how badly. Rank the problems by severity rather than presenting them as equally urgent, and for each real conflict identify precisely which item must be surrendered or weakened, what the resulting position would look like, and which intuition the revision would cost me. Then tell me where I have been suspicious for no reason: which apparent inconsistency disappears once two claims that I may be running together are kept apart, and why the appearance of contradiction arises in the first place. I would rather have a decisive audit than a diplomatic survey, and I would rather be told flatly that one of these cannot stand than be handed an interpretation on which the whole list is technically consistent but no longer says anything.
