quals in three weeks and my committee wants my actual position on the timing of experience written down, not a lit review, so I wrote it down and now I want someone to try to break it before they do. here's the package:

1. no Cartesian theater. there's no place or moment where all the content converges for a subject; there are multiple partly-overlapping drafts, and which one you catch depends on how and when you get probed. I'm not an eliminativist tho — seeing red is real, it just isn't a finished frame handed to a viewer.

2. following Dennett and Kinsbourne, I take the Orwellian/Stalinesque question to be undecidable in principle from the subject's side: any probe is itself another draft and any report is itself revisable, so there's no measurement that could tell you whether the record got rewritten after the fact or whether the content was edited before it ever got fixed.

3. the postdiction cases still push me Stalinesque. color phi — blue dot up top, red dot down below, people report the thing changing color abruptly about halfway along the path, before the second color could possibly have been available. the cutaneous rabbit is the same shape in touch. on my reading the content had to be assembled out of both and then fixed as a whole.

4. and fixation doesn't happen on a clock, it happens at closure points. this is my own bit: content stays editable until the next closure — the offset of a moving thing, the end of a syllable, a saccade landing — and postdiction reaches back exactly to the last closure and no further. so the half-second-ish window people quote from the Libet work is a task artifact, not a property of experience. we ran a phi variant with a blank gap dropped into the middle of the path and the midway reports went away, which is what closure-boundary fixation predicts and a fixed-duration window doesn't.

5. inside a closure window I don't trust reports about order or timing at all, but I do trust reports about quality — the color, the brightness, how bad a pain was. order is a global property, assembled across drafts; quality is local, sitting in whichever draft you're in. so when someone says it looked green halfway, I throw out the halfway and keep the green.

6. I also think some reports are plain confabulation — choice blindness, the split-brain patients, confident stories about why you picked what you picked. but the phi subject isn't confabulating, they really saw the color change. my line is that in confabulation there's no content anywhere the report corresponds to, whereas in phi there's a draft that genuinely has the color-change content in it.

7. on Libet himself I'm a skeptic: backward referral in time is incoherent on my view, becuase you can't stamp an experience with an earlier time unless there was a determinate earlier time for it to have happened at. so I part company with people who recruit Libet for the same anti-theater conclusion I hold.

8. there's no further fact about phenomenal time over and above the fixation history of the drafts. the extension datum — hearing a melody as a melody rather than as a run of now-points — gets explained by earlier content still being around in the current draft, not by a container of duration.

the one I lose sleep over is whether 1 and 8 can both be true, i.e. whether I've just rebuilt a specious present and smuggled the theater back in through 8. my advisor's charge is different: she says 2 and 4 can't both be true, that if my gap experiment tells me when content stops being editable then I've measured exactly what 2 says is unmeasurable.

what I want is an audit, not a lecture — I know the literature. go through the pairs, say which ones actually pull against each other, rank them by severity, and for each give what would have to give and what that costs me. say explicitly which of the tensions I and my advisor have been worrying about are only apparent and why. then name the single revision that would buy back the most consistency. don't assume I'm attached to any particular item, I'm not, but also don't just tell me to water down everything until nothing conflicts — if the cheapest fix is a fix in name only, say so.
