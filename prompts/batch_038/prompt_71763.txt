I am preparing a seminar on the limits of expected-utility reasoning, and I want to test whether a construction I have been using really forces the issue it is meant to force. Here it is.

Fix an agent A with initial wealth w₀ and a von Neumann–Morgenstern utility function u over wealth that is continuous, strictly increasing, strictly concave, and unbounded above. Consider a ticket T sold at price p, defined as follows: a fair coin is tossed until it first lands heads; if that first heads falls on toss n, the ticket pays A the sum sₙ chosen so that u(w₀ − p + sₙ) − u(w₀ − p) = 2ⁿ. Because u is continuous and unbounded above, each sₙ exists and is unique. The expected utility of holding the ticket, measured as a gain over the wealth A retains after paying p, is therefore Σ 2ⁿ · 2⁻ⁿ = Σ 1, which diverges — and it diverges however sharply concave u is, because the schedule has been defined in terms of u itself rather than in dollars. For a logarithmic u and a post-payment wealth of one hundred dollars, the sum owed on the twentieth toss exceeds anything the physical universe could disburse; that is not an objection to the construction but its point, since each toss contributes exactly one unit of expected utility and the divergence is spread evenly across the entire tail, so no finite truncation of the absurd branches restores finitude.

Call the following proposition P: under these stipulations, there is a determinate answer to what A rationally ought to do at any finite price p, and that answer is to buy the ticket. By "determinate" I mean that the correct normative theory of choice under uncertainty issues a verdict here, not merely that some theory or other does.

I want your credence in P. Not a survey of the resolutions of the classical paradox, which I know well, but a number and the arithmetic that produces it. Decompose P into the sub-propositions you regard as load-bearing, assign each its own probability with a sentence or two of justification, and then show me explicitly how they combine into your figure. I expect at least the following candidates to enter, though you should add, drop, split, or reorder them as your judgment dictates: that expected utility is the correct criterion of rational choice in cases where it diverges; that an agent of the stipulated kind is coherently describable at all, given that the representation results which license talk of unbounded utility may not extend to gambles whose expected utility fails to converge; that the ticket is genuinely offerable, in the sense that a counterparty able to meet the schedule is possible rather than merely notional; that some rival rule — utility re-bounded, expectations relativized or normalized, or the agent's cognitive and practical limits treated as normative constraints rather than as excusing conditions — is both correct and delivers refusal in this case; and that the question is ill-posed, with no determinate verdict for "rational" to track.

Three further requests. First, identify the single term to which your final number is most sensitive, and state what argument, result, or clarification would move that term, and hence the total, the most. Second, flag any place where you think my stipulations have done illicit work — where a candidate resolution has been excluded by definitional fiat rather than by argument, so that the construction begs the question against it. I would much rather be told this than be agreed with. Third, two colleagues who have looked at the case report sharply different figures: one assigns P a credence of about 0.9, on the grounds that the construction closes every escape route and leaves the verdict forced; the other assigns it about 0.05, on the grounds that any decision theory which permits an agent to pay any finite sum for such a ticket is thereby discredited. Tell me where you think these two actually disagree — which term or terms carries the whole of the gap — and whether the gap would survive if the stipulations were laid out to them explicitly rather than left implicit in the description of the case.

You may name positions and principles as shorthand, but I want no bibliography and no suggestions for further reading. The deliverable is the decomposition, not the references.
