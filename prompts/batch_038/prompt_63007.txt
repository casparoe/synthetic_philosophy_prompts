I've been chewing on Scanlon's individualist restriction for ages — the rule that you compare one person's complaint against another single person's complaint and never sum across people. It gets the one-drowning-versus-five-drowning case more or less right, but it leaves the dust-specks-versus-torture case a mess, and worse it looks like it licenses any policy that spreads a one-in-a-million risk of death across a million people, because nobody's individual complaint is big enough to reasonably reject. My half-formed idea: maybe the fix isn't to drop the individualist restriction but to change what a complaint is measured over — not the harm you actually end up suffering, but the harm a principle loads onto one person across a whole lifetime of repeated exposure, so the adding-up happens inside a person instead of across people.
