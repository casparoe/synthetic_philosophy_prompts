Forty years as a field ecologist, retired three now, and of all the things to fall into in retirement it's moral psychology. I've been reading Haidt and I can't work out whether his big political claim is actually doing any work. You know the one: liberals light up mainly on care and fairness, conservatives respond to all five foundations more or less evenly, and Haidt seems to treat that as a point in the conservative favour — a wider palate, more of the data, less of the moral world missed.

Two things I want weighed up, and then a verdict rather than a shrug.

First, my own worry, which I haven't seen anyone put quite this way. Care and fairness look like the only two of the five that you could defend to an outsider. Loyalty, authority, sanctity — those only bite if you're already inside the group, already under that hierarchy, already treating that particular flag or body or food rule as set apart. So maybe the honest description isn't that conservatives taste more of the moral spectrum, it's that they taste more local flavours and liberals only the ones that travel. If universality is a mark of getting it right rather than of being limited, the breadth argument collapses into its opposite. Does that work, or am I just letting my own liberalism install itself as the referee?

Second, a habit from my old trade. We were drilled never to infer what a thing is for from the fact that it's there and pulling its weight — the binding foundations clearly do hold groups together and make them cooperate, that's the whole moral capital pitch, and I want to know wether that functional fact buys you anything normative at all. Please don't give me a lazy naturalistic-fallacy answer, I've had those; say whether the inference is dead on arrival or whether there's a version of it that survives.

Give me a conclusion you're prepared to own, and then tell me at the end what would change your mind: which of the two arguments above is the shakier, and where your reasoning is most likely to break down. British English please, the other spelling gives me a headache these days.
