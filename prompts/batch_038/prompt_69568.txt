I teach the creativity unit of a first-year interdisciplinary seminar — 18 students, a single 90-minute session — and this year I'm running it for the first time myself, after sitting through two versions that amounted to telling people to be creative and collecting nothing. I want to build the session around Hofstadter's account of creativity as knob-twiddling: a fixed theme plus a space of permitted transformations, with the genuinely interesting work happening when someone alters the transformation rules themselves rather than the theme. Design a structured workshop in which students reach that claim by doing rather than by being told. I'd like a small fixed theme shared by everyone, sealed rule sets that differ by group, and a mechanism requiring each student to predict in writing, before any judging occurs, which of their own variations the class will rank highest — so that the gap between their taste and their output is something they can hold in their hands.

Please give me the theme and materials, round-by-round rules with timings, a scoring scheme that costs me nothing, a debrief that maps particular moments onto the distinction between varying within a knob-space and varying the knob-space, and an honest account of what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to recover. Say explicitly which feature of the view the activity cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply myself. Where you make claims about how the session will actually play out, tell me how confident you are in them, and single out the point you are least sure of.
