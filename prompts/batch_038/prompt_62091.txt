Ok so I've been going around in circles on this with my reading group and I want a second pair of eyes.

Here's the argument. It's meant to show that the formal version of Occam's razor is empty.

1. Take the formal razor: among hypotheses compatible with the data, prefer the one with the shortest description. That's the engine behind Solomonoff induction and the universal prior, and the dominance result for the universal mixture is supposed to show this is basically the right way to do induction.
2. But "shortest description" always means shortest program that prints the hypothesis, in some particular language L.
3. For any L and any hypothesis h you like, I can write down another langauge L' in which h has a one-symbol program — I just build h in as a primitive.
4. The invariance theorem is supposed to defuse this, except all it says is that complexities measured in two universal languages differ by an additive constant, and (3) shows that constant can be as large as I please. So it rules out nothing.
5. Therefore for any two hypotheses I can pick a language in which whichever one I wanted to win comes out simpler.
6. And nothing non-arbitrary settles the choice of language: if I try to justify L on grounds of simplicity, I've just selected a language again, and that regress never terminates.
7. So simplicity orderings are arbitrary, the formal razor licenses anything you like, and the uniqueness claim for Solomonoff induction collapses.

I want to keep (1) — I think the formal razor is doing real work somewhere — so I'd like to break the chain at (3), (4), or (5). But I honestly can't see how (3) is false: you obviously can build such an L'. And everyone says "the invariance theorem answers that," and I can't see that it does, since the constant really is unbounded when you range over languages.

So: which step do you reject, why did it look safe when you first read it, and what does rejecting it cost? I also want to know how much of the worry survives your answer — my guess is that some watered-down version of the arbitrariness point is still true and just isn't the version stated here, and I'd rather you say what that surviving version is then declare total victory for the razor.
