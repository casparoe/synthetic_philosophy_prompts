So, no academic background here, I'm just a person who's been doing this for nine years. I volunteer with a small group in our town that takes in one refugee family a year — we raise the money, find the flat, do the airport pickup and the school run and the whole first year. Cases come to us through a resettlement worker and we vote at a meeting, show of hands. Its supposed to be about whether we can actually help this particular person, not about politics.

How we've voted so far, roughly in order:

- A journalist whose own government had locked him up twice and was going to again. Everyone for it, no argument at all.
- A man whose region had had three failed rainy seasons and was going to starve. Everyone against, including me. The feeling in the room was that it was awful but nobody was doing it to him.
- A woman whose brothers were going to kill her for walking out on the marriage they'd arranged, and the police had told her to go home to them. For it, though some werent sure at first because the danger was her family and not the state.
- A young gay man whose own uncle had gone to the police about him. For it. One of our older members asked couldn't he just keep quiet about it, and the answer that carried the room was that keeping quiet isn't being safe, it's being somebody else.
- A family from a city being shelled by both sides. Against, same reason as the drought — you'd be as likely to get killed as anyone else in that city, nothing about them made them the target.
- A family from an ethnic minority whose language got banned in the schools, whose papers say what they are, and whose quarter had been burned twice, though nobody in that family had personally been touched. For it. I argued against, honestly — I said they're not singled out, they're in the same boat as half a million people — and I lost, and I still don't know exactly why I lost.
- A schoolteacher who signed a petition, lost her job over it, and had been followed home a couple of times, but nothing had actually been done to her. Against. "Nothing's happened to her yet" was the phrase someone used.

Next week's referral: a 23 year old who's been drafted. He's never been in anything, no politics, no opinion about his own government either way, he drives a delivery van. There's a lot of well documented evidence that the unit he'd be sent to has been killing civilians from one particular ethnic group, not his. If he doesn't turn up he's a deserter — his cousin says that means years in a military prison and probably a beating, no appeal. He's sitting in a camp two countries over. Nothing has happened to him yet. He just hasn't gone.

Which way do we vote, if we want to be able to look at our own list afterwards and not be embarassed? And which of the old cases is actually the closest one — is it the shelling family, the minority family, or the teacher?

But here's what I really want. I'm pretty sure our seven votes can be explained by at least three different rules, and the new case comes out different depending on which rule you pick. Can you lay those rules out for me, say which one we've actually been running all this time and what in the list shows it, and then tell me what the one referral would be that settles it — the case where the rules we can't tell apart would finally have to give different answers. And if this one is a genuine coin flip, I want to know what we'd be signing up to either way, because whatever we pick becomes the rule for the next nine years.
