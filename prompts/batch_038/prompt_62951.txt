Slightly odd piece of work and I want to do it in the right order. I'm helping draft the criteria a review panel will use to decide, case by case, whether a mass-starvation event gets recorded as a political crime or as a natural disaster. People will use the verdicts to name governments and to move emergency money about, and the reasoning has to survive being read out loud to the government it indicts. Before I read any more accounts of famine causation I want the spec rather than the accounts.

So: write the desiderata for an account of when a famine is attributable to political action or inaction. For each one, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and tag it as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp — I especially want to know which of the things people demand in this area are genuine requirements and which are just one side's taste. The list should cover, at minimum: what the account is even a theory of, since famine could be an event, a process or an outcome; the gap between the trigger and the distribution of vulnerability; causal necessity versus culpability; acts versus omissions, and the difference between a state that starves people and a state that carries on running normally while some people stop being able to eat; keeping causes seperate from blame, or explaining why they can't be kept apart; and what the account is supposed to do when the excess-mortality figures are contested by a factor of three.

It has to fit the three standard anchors — Bengal 1943, Ireland during the blight, the Great Leap Forward — and also these, which I've made up to test particular joints. Tell me if any of them is a badly posed test rather than a hard one.

1. Drought removes 40% of a region's harvest. National stocks ample, roads open, prices up only slightly. 300,000 die anyway, because the relief rule required land title and the worst hit were landless labourers and pastoralists who had none.
2. Same drought, same 40% loss, but cash transfers and a genuinely free regional press mean nobody's entitlements collapse and there's no excess mortality worth mentioning. An account that can't say why this one didn't happen seems useless to me for anything prospective, but tell me if that's asking too much.
3. A siege. Harvest fine, blockade stops imports, the defending authority feeds its own fighters first. Two parties each causally necessary, neither sufficient alone.
4. A democracy with a free national press and real elections, in which a famine runs for two years in a territory whose residents can't vote and whose local paper has been shut down by the territorial administration.

Then: which single requirement knocks out the most candidates? And flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied — I've got a suspicion about classifiability in real time on evidence an outsider can actually get, versus not letting concealment itself do the classifying work, but I can't make the tension precise.

Briefly at the end, five or six lines each, how a food-availability story, Sen's entitlement approach, a straight intentionalist atrocity account and some accountability hybrid do against the list. Keep that bit short, the criteria are the point.
