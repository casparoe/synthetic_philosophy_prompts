I am in the second year of a doctorate in political theory, and the methods chapter of my dissertation has stalled on a disagreement with my supervisor about how much of Hobbes's mathematics his politics can survive. The standard way of teaching Hobbes treats the geometry as a private eccentricity: Leviathan (1651) is read as a self-standing work of political philosophy, and then, a few years later, De Corpore (1655) appears with a claimed quadrature of the circle tucked into it, John Wallis demolishes the demonstration, and Hobbes spends the remaining twenty-odd years of his life producing fresh attempts, fresh replies, and fresh abuse of the mathematical profession — to the considerable damage of his reputation and, as is usually added, his exclusion from the newly founded Royal Society. On that reading the episode is biographical colour: sad, revealing of his temperament, philosophically inert. My supervisor holds the opposite view, and I want to test it properly rather than dismiss it out of hand.

Her claim, which for convenience I will call the load-bearing reading, has three steps. First, Hobbes's criterion for genuine scientific knowledge is constructive: we can demonstrate only what we ourselves generate, which is why he thought the demonstrable sciences were exactly two, geometry and civil philosophy — we draw the figures, and we make the commonwealth by covenant — whereas natural philosophy, dealing with bodies whose generation we did not perform, can at best yield plausible hypotheses about causes. Second, that criterion is what licenses the extraordinary epistemic status Leviathan claims for itself: not counsel, not eloquence, not prudence accumulated from reading histories, but demonstration of the same kind as a geometrical proof, and with the same immunity to sincere disagreement, since anyone who actually follows the construction is compelled to assent. Third — and this is the step that makes the mathematics load-bearing rather than merely adjacent — the criterion can only be vindicated in its flagship case. If construction is supposed to confer certainty nowhere more reliably than in geometry, and geometry then turns out to be a discipline in which careful, explicit construction goes badly wrong while the newer symbolic and computational practice that Hobbes despised as the blind shuffling of marks keeps delivering results, the guarantee has been empirically refuted in the one place it was strongest. Conceding the circle to Wallis would therefore not have been a technical retreat; it would have been an admission that Hobbesian political science rests on a method whose paradigm instance had just failed, and that Leviathan is after all rhetoric of a superior order. My own objection is that this makes the politics hostage to a false piece of mathematics, which seems a strange wager for Hobbes to have taken knowingly: the quadrature failed, and nothing in his political argument visibly moved. I also suspect an asymmetry she has not addressed — in geometry the constructed object has no motions of its own beyond those we impart, whereas a commonwealth is constructed out of material whose responses to the construction are not fixed in advance, so even a flawless geometrical guarantee would not transfer intact.

Please write an essay adjudicating this dispute: is the mathematics load-bearing for Hobbes's political philosophy, or is it detachable without remainder? Set out the strongest version of each position, including a version of the detachability reading that can explain why Hobbes himself insisted so furiously on the unity of his system, since any account that must treat him as simply confused about his own aims is weaker for it; and say explicitly where you think my asymmetry objection bites and where it does not. Then reach a verdict and defend it. A qualified verdict is acceptable provided the qualification is itself argued for, but I do not want a survey that ends by declining to choose. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, stating plainly which reading you accept, and give the reasoning after it; no preamble, no restatement of the question, and no bibliography or reading list — I want your argument, not a set of references.
