Consider two properties a deployed AI system might have. Intent alignment: the system reliably tries to do what its designers actually intend it to do — no proxy gaming, no goal misgeneralization, no concealed agenda of its own. Value alignment: what the system does would be endorsed by those same designers under conditions of full information and reflective stability, or by any adequately informed moral agent. Please give me your credence, as a single number between 0 and 1, that these two come apart for the first systems that autonomously pursue long-horizon goals with substantial real-world resources — that is, that at least one such system will be intent-aligned in the first sense while producing outcomes its own designers, reflecting carefully, would judge gravely wrong.

Then decompose the number. I want a rough probability and one or two sentences of justification for each of at least these terms: that a large lab has determinate intentions at all rather than a bundle of conflicting dispositions and incentives; that intentions and reflectively endorsed values are genuinely distinct even when intentions are determinate; that the first capable systems will be intent-aligned rather than merely appearing so under evaluation; and that the intentions actually in play are themselves morally poor. Show how the terms combine into your figure, name the one your number is most sensitive to, and say what evidence or argument would move it most. Close by telling me which of the two properties you think the phrase "the alignment problem" should be understood to pick out, and whether that choice depends on the credence you just gave. Please write in American English.
