Fix the purpose first, because I do not want a specification floating free of any use. The theory has to be good enough to settle a question I actually face: a festival programming committee must decide how much weight to give two inputs — the considered verdicts of a panel of expert selectors, and an aggregate score compiled mechanically from several thousand post-screening audience ratings — and must decide whether either input can warrant a belief about the merits of a work in a committee member who has not seen it. Everything below is a requirement on a theory capable of settling that, and on nothing more grand.

For terminology, adopt these four and treat none of them as well-behaved until your theory has made them so: **verdict testimony**, where someone tells you that a work is beautiful, or trite, or a masterpiece; **response testimony**, where someone reports only their own reaction — that they wept, that they walked out, that they have not stopped thinking about the third act; **feature testimony**, where someone reports a non-evaluative property that nonetheless bears on the aesthetic — that the second act is lopsided, that the grading is muddy, that the joke lands a beat late; and **aggregate testimony**, where a number is produced from many people's responses by a process in which nobody intended to testify to anything. Also mark, throughout, the difference between a bare verdict and a verdict delivered with reasons, and the difference between taking up either deferentially and taking it up as a prompt to go and look.

The deliverable is the desiderata themselves, each stated with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a taste, and each marked either non-negotiable or a demand that only one camp makes, in which case say what is lost by dropping it. At minimum the list must cover: what the theory owes to the felt asymmetry between aesthetic testimony and ordinary testimony about the height of a building or the date of a battle; what it owes to testimony about the plainly non-aesthetic properties of artworks — attribution, provenance, running time, what happens in the plot — which nobody finds problematic, and why the existence of that unproblematic neighbourhood constrains any account of the problematic one; what it owes to the acquaintance thesis, that no belief about a work's aesthetic character is warranted without first-hand encounter, including whether that thesis is a datum to be explained or a claim to be argued against, and how the theory handles the person who defers now and looks later, whose belief must be classed as warranted, unwarranted, or merely useful; whether the theory is obliged to keep the epistemic question — can testimony confer warranted aesthetic belief? — separate from the evaluative one — is relying on it an aesthetic failing even when it succeeds?; how it handles two selectors of acknowledged equal competence who simply disagree; and whether it may treat the aesthetic domain as sui generis or is required to fall out of a general account of testimony with no aesthetic clause in it.

Two further things, and these are the point. First, identify the subset of your own desiderata that you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and locate the pressure precisely; my suspicion is that something must give between requiring a general, non-sui-generis account, requiring the account to accommodate whatever aggregates do better than expert panels do, and requiring that expert verdicts retain any authority at all. If you make aggregate performance a requirement, state the empirical assumption you are leaning on and keep the theory from turning on it, since nobody has pinned that comparison down and I do not want the list resting on a guess. Second, name the single requirement that eliminates the most candidates, and say which of the three familiar positions — the pessimist who denies that aesthetic testimony can confer warrant, the optimist who assimilates it to testimony generally, and the pluralist who splits the domain by kind — it rules out and which it leaves standing. A list that comes out neutral among all three is a defective list; if that is what you find, revise the list and tell me which requirement you changed and why, rather than reporting a tie.
