I've been using one of those mood check-in apps twice a day for about two years. It says my average happiness at work has slid steadily since last spring and I'm now sitting in what it calls a persistent low band, and I'm trying to work up the nerve to quit because of it. But here's what's making me doubt the whole dataset: the questions aren't neutral. On days when it offers words like drained, hopeless, stuck, I pick them. When it asks what went well, I come up with three things easy and feel fine. I've actually tested this on myself — I can get a "yes I'm unhappy there" or a "no I'm fine" pretty reliably just by changing how I phrase the question in my head first.

My sister, who's a psych major, says this proves I don't have some fixed inner state the app is reading off. Her view is I'm constructing an answer out of whatever cues are in front of me, basically the same way I'd guess at a stranger's mood from the outside — which would make two years of entries worthless as evidence about me.

But then what do I go on instead? The only alternative seems to be trusting how I feel right in the moment, and that's even more obviously shaped by how the moment is framed. So — is my journal telling me about my job or about the app? And how am I supposed to decide whether to quit?
