I'm on the priority-setting committee for a city road-safety fund. Fixed pot each year, roughly 40 applications, and we're required to publish a single ranked list — the solicitor says a published ranking has to be a strict ordering, so no ties. Last cycle an unsuccessful applicant complained after getting hold of our minutes, which showed we'd preferred A to B, B to C and C to A, with the resulting mess settled by my casting vote. So the chair wants the procedure fixed before any of us sees an application.

Two options on the table. Our analyst has a scoring sheet: points for number of people affected times a severity weight (death 1.0, serious injury 0.4, minor injury 0.01, that kind of thing), then rank by total. Its transitive by construction. Trouble is it sometimes says a scheme preventing 30,000 whiplash injuries beats one preventing four deaths, and half the committee won't stomach that — they say you're just adding across categories that shouldn't be added.

One of those members has a counter-proposal she calls the adjacency rule: only compare applications that sit next to each other on the severity scale, because comparing a fatality to a grazed knee is meaningless while comparing a fatality to a broken pelvis isn't. I've pointed out that if every adjacent pair is a wash, stringing enough of them together makes the top of the scale a wash with the bottom. She says that's fine, since you're not allowed to string them.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday. Recommend one procedure — the scoring sheet, adjacency, or something else you can specify precisely enough that we could actually run 40 applications through it — and say how we should settle the choice between "commensurable but repugnant" and "intuitive but circular." Give the strongest honest case for whatever you reject. And please don't end with "the literature is divided," we have to publish in June.
