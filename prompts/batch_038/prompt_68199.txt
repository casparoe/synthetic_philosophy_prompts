Our neighborhood association had a fight last week about whether the city should fund a day-labor pickup point, and it got derailed about ten minutes in. A guy who I'm pretty sure owns a landscaping crew shut down the whole question of what the workers get paid by saying "those are jobs Americans won't do anyway." Someone else chimed in with the 3-D framing — dirty, dangerous, demeaning — like that settled it too. When I tried to push on wages, he said I was committing the lump-of-labor fallacy, that there's no fixed number of jobs so immigrants taking jobs can't hurt anybody.

That pair is what I couldnt get past. On the one hand he's telling me there's a standing set of slots natives refuse to fill, and on the other he's telling me there's no fixed set of slots at all. It felt like the two moves need opposite pictures of the labor market, but I didn't have the words for it in the meeting and he walked off thinking he'd won.

So, two asks.

Main one: break "those are jobs Americans won't do" into the distinct precise claims it could be standing for. I can already feel that "won't" is doing something slippery — wouldn't at any wage, wouldn't at the wage actually on offer, wouldn't given what else is available to them, just aren't observed doing them, or something about the *kind* of work rather than the pay. Lay these out as a menu rather than picking your favorite. For each reading tell me whether it's near-trivially true, substantive but arguable, or just false, and — this is the part I actually care about — whether it supports the use he put it to, which was to make the wage question disappear. I suspect exactly one reading would do that work and its not the one he meant, but show me why.

Second one, shorter: is there a real contradiction between "jobs natives won't do" and "no fixed number of jobs," or can someone hold both coherently? Say what each has to assume about whether the local market clears, and whether the answer changes if you apply them to a small town labor market like ours instead of the national aggregate everybody usually has in mind.
