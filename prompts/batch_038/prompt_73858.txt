I am a graduate student in a research group on markets and ethics, working on commercial surrogacy, and I have kept running into a pattern I cannot find a name for. I would be grateful for your help identifying it, or, if it turns out not to be identified anywhere, for help defining it precisely. Please write in American English.

The pattern goes roughly like this. A market arrangement attracts sustained moral objection: it is said to sell something that ought not to be for sale, or to buy something from people whose circumstances make the transaction suspect. The arrangement is then restructured rather than abandoned. Money still moves, the thing still reaches the person who wants it, and the parties are in broadly the same positions they would have been in under the original form. But the legal and conceptual shape of the transaction is different, and the difference is exactly the difference the objection needed in order to bite.

Some instances, described as informally as I can manage:

- Where paying a donor for blood is objected to as putting a price on part of a person, the transaction reappears as a donation accompanied by a gratuity, a service fee, or reimbursement of expenses and lost wages. The sum is negotiated in advance and varies with how often one gives, yet no one is described as having bought anything.
- Where the objection to commercial surrogacy is that a child, or a woman's reproductive capacity, is being sold, the contract is redrawn so that what is paid for is a service, with the child transferred gratuitously. The dollar figures and the selection criteria survive intact; the object of the sale does not.
- Where the objection to paid queue-standing is that the affluent buy their way past those who cannot afford to, the practice reappears as hiring a person to stand in the queue on one's behalf. The waiting time still goes to whoever can pay for it, but what is purchased is now labor rather than priority, and the objection that priority is for sale no longer has a target.
- Where a prohibition on selling kidneys is defended on the ground that body parts must not carry a price, the practice reappears as paired or chained donation: no money changes hands, yet the arrangement exists solely to route organs from those who can give them to those who most want them, and a participant's willingness to donate is made conditional on receiving one in return.

What strikes me is how the debate responds. Objectors rarely say that the restructuring answers their objection and that they were wrong. More often they say that the new form is the old form wearing a disguise, and that calling it a donation or a service rather than a sale changes nothing that matters. Proponents reply that the objection was always about classification, that the classification has now changed, and that the objection should therefore lapse. Each side is helped by a different reading of what the original objection was about, and the two readings seem never to be separated out explicitly.

I should be clear about what I do not think the pattern is, since several familiar things sit next to it without covering it.

It is not mere euphemism or marketing language. The objection is usually not to the vocabulary; the vocabulary is downstream. Renaming a fee a gift, in a context where everyone knows what is happening, is a poor description of cases in which the enforceable rights, the flow of consideration, and the remedies genuinely differ from what they would have been under the original form.

It is not regulation. Regulation takes an activity that already has a name and constrains, taxes, or licenses it. Here the activity is moved out of the category the prohibition or the objection was aimed at, so that the rule no longer picks it up.

It is not the general contrast between gift relations and market relations. In most of my cases there is consideration, a negotiated amount, and a contract. What is avoided is not the presence of money but the description of the thing as for sale.

It is also not simply hypocrisy or bad faith. Some of the restructurings look as though they may genuinely reduce whatever was wrong with the original arrangement, and I do not want a label that builds in the assumption that the transformation is only cosmetic. But neither do the objectors typically concede the point when it is not cosmetic, and I suspect that is part of the pattern rather than an accident of it.

So I have three questions.

First: is there an established concept, or a settled piece of literature, that names this specific thing, where moral resistance to a market produces a structurally reclassified version of the same transaction, preserving its economics while altering the category it falls under so that the original objection loses its grip? If so, please tell me what the concept is called and how it is ordinarily formulated, without assuming I already recognize the term.

Second: if the nearest available concepts each fit only partially, I would like to know what they are and how far each of them goes. I have in mind commodification and its converse, the idea that certain things are inalienable, the idea that disgust functions as an unreliable moral signal in these debates, the gift-exchange analysis according to which the language of generosity conceals calculation, and whatever account exists of actors reorganizing a transaction to fall outside the scope of a rule that forbids it. For each, I want to know why it does not quite cover the pattern above.

Third: if nothing covers it, please propose a term and define it in a way that fixes its boundaries. I want the definition to do real work: to say what makes a case an instance and what makes a case merely look like one, to distinguish cosmetic reclassification from reclassification that genuinely alters the moral character of the transaction, and to give me two or three hard cases that sit on the edge, explaining why each is borderline. I would also like to know whether the pattern generalizes beyond contested markets, and whether the fact that it might generalize is a reason to think I have not yet isolated the thing I am actually looking for.

A bare name will not be much use to me. What I need is the carving: what this phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
