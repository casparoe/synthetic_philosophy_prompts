I write the science-and-ideas column at a weekly magazine and I've got a five-part series on probability in the many-worlds interpretation coming up — the whole Born rule busines. Trouble is I've got four competing pitches from different writers and I can't tell which is actually worth 1,500 words, so I need someone to sort the field for me.

Here's what's got me going. A physicist I interviewed last month told me flatly that this debate is over: decoherence already hands you the branching structure, everything else is bookkeeping, and anyone still arguing about the Born rule is fighting a pseudo-problem. But when I pushed him on where the squared amplitude actually comes in, he changed the subject, which smells like dodging rather than answering.

So: which open questions here are genuinely live, which are overrated or already dead, and which single one is the crux that the "its settled by decoherence" crowd is walking straight past? I care about the reasoning far more than the list — tell me which questions sit upstream of which, which could plausibly move in the next few years and what would move them, and what evidence would make you re-rank the whole thing. And where the camps actually disagree — the decision-theoretic line associated with Deutsch and Wallace, Zurek's envariance argument, the self-locating-uncertainty move from Sebens and Carroll, Vaidman's talk about measures of existence — say plainly which of those you think is doing real work and which is dressing.

Two extra things I need. First, for each question you rank high, tell me what I'd be allowed to state flat-out in print without hedging, and what I'd have to fuzz over; I get letters when a columnist overstates. Second, if your honest view is that the whole area is a dead end and the series shouldn't be commissioned at all, say so and tell me what I should write about instead.

Format: bottom line first, one sentence, naming the crux. Then your ranked agenda in short entries. Hard cap of 450 words. British English please — house style. And no bibliography or reading list, I'm not putting one in the piece and you'd only be guessing at my readers anyway.
