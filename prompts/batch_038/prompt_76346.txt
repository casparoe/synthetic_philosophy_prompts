I run a small painting-restoration studio, and I would like help thinking through a job I have already accepted.

A client brought in a mid-nineteenth-century family portrait of her great-great-grandmother: unsigned, unattributed, and of no market consequence to anyone but the family. A house fire blistered and lifted a patch of the paint layer across the sitter's left cheek and jaw, so that roughly an eighth of the face is now lost down to the ground. Her request is simple — fill the loss and paint it in so the face reads as whole when it hangs in her hallway. My training pushes the other way: new work should remain distinguishable on close inspection, be reversible, and never pass itself off as the original hand. I could do a muted fill that is legible up close and only seamless from across a room, but she has told me plainly that she would experience that as a refusal. She has also said more than once that this is not a museum object.

Here is what complicates the job. While cleaning a test patch I found that the face had already been substantially repainted, probably in the 1950s: someone rebuilt the eyes and mouth in a smoother, prettier idiom, and the painter's own brushwork survives underneath. The version my client grew up with, the face she wants made whole, is that mid-century repaint. If I strip it back to what the artist actually did, I recover the work's history and destroy the image her family has lived with for three generations. If I keep the repaint, I am preserving someone's unsigned rewriting of the picture, and my infill will be matching a hand that is not the artist's.

A third option is to leave the repaint, fill the loss to match it, and disclose everything in a letter kept with the painting. But disclosure seems to change nothing about what ends up on the wall, and I am no longer sure it changes anything about what I have done.

Which of these should I choose, and on what principle? I would like to understand what I would be preserving in each case, and whether the original here even picks out a single thing.
