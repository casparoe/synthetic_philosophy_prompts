Write a sestina about Turchin's secular cycles — six six-line stanzas plus a three-line envoi, in the standard end-word rotation (ABCDEF, FAEBDC, CFDABE, ECBFAD, DEACFB, BDFECA, with ECA at the envoi's line-ends and BDF mid-line). Let the six end-words be bread, seats, coffers, fathers, swords, and count, and assign each to a quantity in the structural-demographic model: subsistence and real wages; the number of elite positions against the number of aspirants pressing for them; the state's fiscal capacity; the generational transmission and decay of collective solidarity, including the shorter fathers-and-sons rhythm nested inside the long cycle; internal violence; and the tally itself, since on this account a society is a thing that can be counted.

Run the phases through the stanzas in order — an integrative stretch of good harvests, few offices, and a filling treasury; the stagflation as population presses on the land and the queue for those offices lengthens; the crisis of intra-elite competition, fiscal breakdown, and immiseration; the intercycle's emptying out — and let the sixth stanza's split order, evens ascending then odds reversed, be the moment a reader can see that what comes back is not the events but the arrangement among them. The envoi should fold all six words into three lines the way the model folds a history into a handful of parameters, and it should make the missing seventh stanza felt: the one in which the rotation would return to where it began, after the poem has already stopped.

Set it in an agrarian society, named or invented; the claim at issue is about structure rather than country, so no particular dynasty is required. Keep it out of essay-verse — no talk of models, variables, cycles, or predictions inside the lines. A cliodynamicist should be able to name the phase in every stanza and see elite overproduction doing work distinct from Malthusian pressure; a reader who has never heard of either should get a good poem about bread, office, war, and counting.
