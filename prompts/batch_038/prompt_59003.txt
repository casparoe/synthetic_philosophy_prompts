Two labs are haggling over how to split control of a joint frontier project. Each knows that whoever commits first and irreversibly — a public, untakebackable ultimatum — walks away with the better deal, and that if both commit at once everyone loses badly. My friend says the rational thing here is to keep precommiting earlier and earlier, and that there's no stable stopping point: at any moment, having committed a moment sooner would have been better. I think he's wrong and that once both sides know the game there's usually a hold-off equilibrium, because the first-mover advantage partly evaporates when it's expected.

So: your credence that I'm right, decomposed. Give me a number for the probability that irreversible commitment is even achievable by an agent like this (can you really make a threat you can't retract, or does the other side always price in some chance you'll cave?); for the probability that the "earlier is always better" structure is even coherent when time is continuous — this feels like Zeno in a suit and I suspect it's where the whole disagreement lives; for the probability the payoff shape is chicken rather than prisoner's dilemma; and for the probability that real orgs behave anything like the idealized player. Then combine them into the bottom line however you think is right, say which term you're least sure about, and say which of them my friend and I most likely disagree on.
