so i'm a physics/philosophy double major and this term I'm in a seminar on the metaphysics of quantum mechanics. Next week we each have to hand in a written statement of our own position on probability in Everett so everyone else can attack it, and mine has gotten away from me a bit — it's ten commitments long and I've stopped being able to see whether they fit together. I'd rather find out from you than in front of the class. Here's the list, roughly in the order I came to hold them:

(1) Strict unitarity. The wave function is the entire ontology, it always evolves by the Schrödinger equation, no collapse, no hidden variables, no extra "worlds" furniture bolted on. Worlds are just what the wave function looks like once decoherence has done its thing.
(2) That's the whole selling point for me: you don't add anything to the physics. So any solution to the probability problem that posits a new primitive ingredient — a bare propensity, a probability postulate glued on at the end — is cheating, it's collapse theory with better manners.
(3) I'm a determinist about Everett. Given the initial state everything that happens happens necessarily; there's no objective indeterminism anywhere in the picture.
(4) But I'm not a subjectivist about probability. I'm basically Lewisian here: chances are objective features of the world and the Principal Principle says rational credence has to track them. Probability isn't just in the head.
(5) And I mean the Born numbers as chances in exactly that sense. Saying a branch has amplitude-squared weight .99 is saying there's a 99% chance of that outcome, full stop — the same sort of claim a GRW person is making, only without the collapse.
(6) Branch counting is dead as far as I'm concerned. Decoherence is approximate and continuous so there's generally no well defined number of branches, and equal weighting gives the wrong predictions anyway. I don't want probability coming out of counting.
(7) I also don't think the Deutsch/Wallace decision-theoretic program works. Either the axioms about how a rational agent should value her future branches already have the Born rule baked into them, or they're weak enough that an agent who just ignores everything below some tiny weight satisfies them too. Circular or toothless.
(8) What does look promising to me is self-locating uncertainty: between the measurement and the observation there's a window where the physics has already fixed everything but I still don't know which branch I'm on, and probability lives in that window. Sebens and Carroll's version, with the epistemic separability principle doing the work.
(9) And seperately I like Zurek's envariance argument, because it seems to get the weights out of symmetries of the entangled state with no decision theory and no indexicals at all. I've been treating (8) and (9) as two independent routes that corroborate each other.
(10) I'm a physicalist about persons — no many-minds, no consciousness-driven branching. Personal identity is whatever the physics gives you, which after a measurement is several people.

The objection I keep getting from friends is that (3) and (8) can't both be true, since you can't be uncertain about a future that's already fixed. I think I have an answer to that one — self-locating uncertainty doesn't need the world to be open, I can be unsure which day it is in a perfectly deterministic universe, and unsure which of several qualitatively identical successors I am — but I'm not sure the answer generalizes to the rest of the list, and honestly I suspect I've been using it as a shield. I'm deliberately not telling you which of these I'd give up first, because I don't know, and that's the point of the exercise. So: go through and tell me which pairs genuinely can't coexist, ordered worst to least bad, and for each say which commitment has to go and what I lose by dropping it. Where a pair that looks broken to me is actually fine, say so and say why — I don't want everything flagged as equally bad, and I especially don't want a general tour of the Everettian probability literature. Then finish with the single cheapest revision to the whole package, and tell me whether after making it I still have a position I'm entitled to call Everettian, or just a collapse theory I'm too polite to name.
