Third-year PhD student in education policy here, doing fieldwork at a couple of elementary schools for my dissertation on discipline. The one I'm at most runs a token economy under PBIS: kids earn paper tickets all day for lining up quietly, keeping their desk clear, using kind words, and then there's a prize cart on friday plus a monthly raffle where somebody's name gets pulled for a pizza party. Same building also runs a character-education program whose whole pitch is that kids should be honest and helpful because that's who they are, not to get something out of it, and an honor-code setup with shoutouts at assembly and a student-of-the-month board.

On the side I've been reading Locke's Some Thoughts Concerning Education, which is weird for my field but it keeps colliding with what I'm watching. He's suspicious of the rod and just as suspicious of bribing kids with rewards — treats and trinkets just train bigger appetites — and he keeps saying that once a child cares about his own credit and reputation you've got the only lever that actually works.

So what I want is a reconstruction of what the ticket system itself presupposes, not a defense of it. Does it commit the school to something behaviorist, where a behavior is just a function of its consequences and there's no further fact about whether the kid does it for the right reason? Or can it be read as a temporary scaffold toward something internal — and if so, what has to be true about how the tickets get faded for that reading to be real rather than just a nicer story we tell? Which rival accounts does it sit badly with: the virtue view where a disposition only counts if it's stable across situations and comes with understanding, Locke's reputation view, a kantian acting-for-the-reason view?

Then the bit I actually can't figure out. Is there a contradiction between the tickets and the honor-code/character stuff running in the same school, or are they two levers on two different psychological systems, or is one parasitic on the other? And where is the practice underdetermined — what could a school believe and still hand out tickets? Finally, if you swapped the ticket system for the esteem-and-disgrace model Locke wants, what changes day to day, and what happens to the kids at the bottom of the status order once reputation is the only currency?
