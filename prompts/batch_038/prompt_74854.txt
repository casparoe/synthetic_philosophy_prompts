I co-run a reading group that's half historians of computing and half ML people, and for about two years now the same argument has come up whenever we get anywhere near Turing's child-machine material from the 1950 Mind paper, and nobody ever moves. One side says he basically sketched the modern recipe — don't hand-build the adult intelligence, build a small program and then educate it with rewards and punishments, possibly with an evolutionary-style search over variants — so the continuity with today's learning systems is real and the credit should run back to 1950. The other side says that's retrofitting: the passage is a deliberately loose rhetorical move inside an argument about the imitation game, Turing had no mechanism in mind, and mapping it onto backprop or RL imports seven decades of results he couldn't have had. Then there's a third reading a couple of people keep pushing, which is that the actual content of the passage is about the teacher's epistemic situation — you can educate something whose internal states you can't interpret, and giving up on interpretation is the innovation — which is neither of the above.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the debate is stalled because "anticipated" is carrying two different jobs at once, a question about content (is enough shared structure there?) and a question about credit (who deserves the nod?), and the two camps are mostly answering different ones under one heading, with a hidden threshold in the middle about how much overlap counts. I've also been trying to make the content question precise with a counterfactual test — hand the 1950 text to a competent engineer in 1950 with no access to later results and ask whether they could have gotten from there to here — but both camps reject it, for opposite reasons, which makes me suspect the test isn't tracking what anyone is actually fighting about. A colleague has a variant: only count it as an anticipation if the earlier text narrows the search space to a region that contains the later result, without the writer knowing the answer.
