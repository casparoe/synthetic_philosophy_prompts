I am convening an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the foundations of mechanistic interpretability, and I want you to draft the document they would produce at the end of it: not a dialogue, not an adjudication of who is right, and not a diagnosis of why the debate is stuck, but a joint statement both sides could sign. The intended readership is a mixed audience of ML researchers and philosophers who have each already picked a side and who will read every sentence as an admission of what one camp has conceded. Neither camp may be able to say the document reads as a surrender, and no agreed claim may be so thin that a bystander with no stake would sign it for free.

The camps are these.

**Camp R (representation realist).** At least some interpretability reports are literally true of the network's internal states. A distinguished subset of feature attributions is made correct by the model itself rather than by the analyst's choice of method, and the epistemic situation is continuous with that of systems neuroscience, where a claim about what a neural population carries can be straightforwardly true or false.

**Camp I (attribution instrumentalist).** "The model represents X" is at best a compressed way of saying that a certain attribution scheme buys reliable prediction and control of the model's behavior. Which internal directions count as features is fixed only relative to a choice of decoding scheme, dictionary size, fitting corpus, and level of description. The convergence realists point to is exactly what one should expect from well-designed instruments, and is no evidence of scheme-independent facts.

Take the following stipulated findings as the shared evidential base. Treat all three as given and as equally well known to both camps. Do not cite, attribute, or allude to any real published result or laboratory; do not add further findings beyond ordinary reasoning about how these methods behave in general.

1. **Matched core, persistent residue.** Two groups apply different sparse dictionary-learning methods to the same layer of the same mid-sized model, with different dictionary sizes and different fitting corpora. For roughly the two-thirds most frequently activated items in group A's dictionary, there is an item in group B's dictionary whose direction is close and whose activation predictions agree with A's on 95% of held-out text. The remaining third has no counterpart in B. This unmatched fraction does not shrink when both groups double dictionary size and quadruple the fitting corpus; it stays near a third. The unmatched items are disproportionately ones whose stimulus classes look disjunctive or abstract rather than semantically cohesive.

2. **Reciprocal vindication.** For matched items, ablating or patching the direction shifts downstream behavior in the predicted direction and magnitude, robustly across paraphrase. For unmatched items the same procedures do produce effects, but not the ones the item's activation profile predicts. Each group can recover its own prediction only by supplying an auxiliary model of how the network redistributes activity when the item is removed, and the two auxiliary models are mutually inconsistent. Each is confirmed by the ablation data the other group runs. No method-neutral test has yet discriminated between them.

3. **Same role, different vehicle.** An item corresponding to a functional category — text of the kind the model has been trained to refuse — is recoverable in models from three architecture families and four scales. Its ablation yields the same behavioral effect in all of them, and its activation profile over a shared stimulus battery correlates above 0.9 across models. But no fixed linear map carries any one model's direction onto another's; the geometries are not related by a shared rotation or rescaling, and the layers at which the item is best recovered differ.

Write the statement in the first-person plural voice of the two signatories, with these sections.

**A. Agreed claims (numbered).** What R and I can both sign about the base findings, about what the methods do and do not license, and about what remains to be explained. At least two must cut against the interests of the camp signing them.

**B. Disagreements table.** Columns: the disagreement; R's own preferred wording of its position; I's own preferred wording of its position; whether the divergence is verbal or substantive, with the reason; and which crux it depends on, if any. Each side's wording must be one its own camp would accept unedited. Include at least one row you judge wholly verbal, and say what is left over once the words are fixed. In every row, be explicit about which of the following is being asserted: that the model has a representation, that ascribing it is predictively useful, that it is the right level of description, or that a particular internal direction is its vehicle. Much of the public dispute slides among these four; the document must not.

**C. Cruxes.** For each: the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one camp; the test, argument, or case both accept as bearing on it, whether drawn from the base above or from a newly stipulated experiment described in enough detail that both agree in advance what each possible outcome would show; and each camp's prediction, recorded in advance, with a rough confidence and an explicit falsifier — the result that would oblige that camp to revise. At least one crux must be one R fears losing and one that I fears losing.

**D. A third position.** Consider the view that is realist about the roles features play and anti-realist about their vehicles — that finding 3 shows exactly what realism should have expected, and finding 1 shows exactly what vehicle-level realism cannot have. Is this a genuine third position or a relabeling of one of the two? State which camp, if either, should defect to it, and what result would trigger the defection.

**E. Relation to the older debate.** Say whether this dispute is a special case of the standing realism/instrumentalism quarrel over attributing representations to people and to brains, or whether the interpretability setting has structure that quarrel lacked. If it is a special case, say what the older quarrel's inconclusiveness implies for this one; if not, name the structural difference.

Close with a brief note in your own voice rather than the signatories': which agreed claim you suspect one side signed only under rhetorical pressure and would quietly repudiate in practice; where you think your drafting is slanted and in whose favor; which crux you take to be decisive and what would change your mind about that; and where your reasoning is most likely to have gone wrong. Keep the whole document under about 1,500 words.
