**DRAFT — "Two Ways to Be Centered: Why the SSA/SIA Fight Isn't a Fight About Priors"**

**1. Setup.** Bostrom's two principles are usually introduced as competing indexical priors. The self-sampling assumption says you should reason as though you were a random draw from the actually existing observers in your reference class. The self-indication assumption says you should reason as though you were a random draw from all possible observers, which in practice means multiplying a hypothesis's weight by the number of observers it says exist. Each has a known cost. SSA buys you the doomsday argument. SIA buys you the presumptuous philosopher, who walks into the common room certain about the size of the universe before anyone has done any physics. My starting point is that both costs are described as costs of a *prior*, and I think that framing is what keeps the debate stuck.

**2. The absorption thesis.** Formally, both principles are centering maps: rules for turning a prior over uncentered worlds into a prior over centered ones, where a centered world is a world plus an answer to "which observer in it am I." SSA's map spreads a world's weight evenly over the observers in your reference class inside that world. SIA's spreads weight evenly over observers everywhere, so a world containing more of them collects proportionally more.

Claim: for any uncentered hypothesis H, the two maps deliver the same posterior on H once you conditionalize on your total experience. Call evidence of that kind *experience-complete*. The reason is that conditioning on experience-complete evidence restricts attention to the observer-moments that could have been you, and both maps are uniform over exactly that surviving set; the only difference between them is how they distribute mass over centered worlds that the evidence has already ruled out, and mass over ruled-out worlds cancels in conditionalization. So the principles cannot disagree about what you ought to believe once your evidence is complete. Every apparent disagreement is a disagreement about absorbing *incomplete* evidence, which is the ordinary situation — you have learned something that narrows the possibilities without pinning down which observer you are. That is a dispute about the order of bookkeeping steps, not about rationality. Corollary: the doomsday argument and the presumptuous philosopher are both artifacts of conditioning on evidence that isn't experience-complete, namely a birth rank and a bare "I exist."

**3. A toy model.** Twin Earths. World A, prior 1/2: ten observers, all molecule-for-molecule duplicates of me with numerically identical total experience. World B, prior 1/2: a thousand observers, of whom ten are duplicates of me with identical total experience and 990 are nothing like me — different sensory modalities, no color vision, no language. Call those the Mud People. My reference class is observers-with-my-sort-of-experience. Now let E be my total experience, which is as complete as evidence gets.

Under SSA, ten of ten reference-class members survive E in A and ten of ten in B, so P(A|E) = P(B|E) = 1/2. Under SIA, the surviving observer-moments are the ten duplicates in A and the ten in B, and the Mud People drop out, so again 1/2. Agreement, on experience-complete evidence, exactly as §2 predicts — and note that SIA's famous boost has nowhere to bite, because the extra 990 observers are precisely the ones E excludes.

**4. Presumptuousness is symmetric.** The presumptuous philosopher is deployed as a reductio of SIA: SIA alone licenses near-certainty in big-universe cosmology from the armchair. But look at the comparison class. The presumptuous philosopher is being compared not to SSA but to a null principle — no indexical update at all — which isn't a position anybody in this debate defends. Against the live alternative, the charge loses its force, because SSA has armchair cosmology of its own: the doomsday argument licenses confidence about the total future population of your species from your birth rank and nothing else. Both principles let you do demography in an armchair; they simply harvest the confidence from different indexical facts, a total in one case and a rank in the other. So the real question is not which principle is presumptuous but which indexical fact, if any, carries information about the uncentered world. And that question is not going to be settled by intuitions about philosophers announcing results at parties.

**5. A repair, maybe.** I'm tempted to restrict SIA's boost to observers whose experience is possible for me: call it SIA-R. Weight a world by the number of observers in it who could have my total experience, rather than by its total population. SIA-R blunts the presumptuous philosopher, since the extra trillion-trillion observers in the big-universe hypothesis are, on any sane filling-in of the case, not my duplicates, and it reproduces the Twin Earths agreement. I suspect it's ad hoc, and I also suspect it's the reference-class problem wearing a lab coat. I can't decide whether to develop it or cut it.

**6.** What would change my mind: a case in which experience-complete evidence forces SSA and SIA apart. I don't think there is one.
