I teach a 75-minute session on behaviour genetics to about 25 second-years — they know mean and variance, nothing about twin studies. Design an in-class activity where they calculate a heritability number themselves from data I hand out (dice or cards, no laptops), and then walk straight into the trap: the figure they get is high, I hand them a second "population" with a large group gap that is entirely environmental in the underlying model, and most of them will say the gap must be genetic.

So make the setup actually generate that — spell out the variance components and the sampling procedure in enough detail that I can check on paper it works before I run it with real students. Please don't use the two-fields-of-seeds framing, they've all heard it, and don't name heritability until after the trap has sprung.

Then give me the debrief: map each moment of the activity onto what h² does and doesnt license, tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time (including the two or three students who refuse to be fooled and blow the reveal), and be frank about which features the simulation just cant carry — genotype-environment interaction, assortative mating, and the fact that real estimates come from correlations between relatives rather than from known variance components.
