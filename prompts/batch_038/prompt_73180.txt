So I burned four hours last night arguing with my friend Nadia about pragmatic encroachment and, honestly, I'm no longer sure "who's right" is the question worth asking. Help me diagnose the fight instead of refereeing it.

Setup, since you weren't there. She's a purist about knowledge and a contextualist about "knows" — she thinks whether someone knows is fixed entirely by truth-related stuff (evidence, reliability, no defeaters), and that what shifts in the stakes cases is either the standards in play in the *attributor's* conversation or just our willingness to flat-out assert and act on the belief. I'm the encroacher: same evidence, same reliability, but if enough rides on it, they don't know — stakes get into the knowledge relation itself. We both know the bank cases cold, we both know the knowledge-action principle line, I know her reply that the high-stakes subject still knows but would be irresponsible not to check again, and she knows my reply that on her view knowledge stops doing any explanatory work in action. Nobody moved an inch. Standard.

Here's the part that's actually stuck in my head, though. Late in the night I built a case I thought would drive a wedge between us.

Amara is a hospital pharmacist. She looks up a pediatric dose, the system says 2.5 mL, she's used that system for nine years and its never let her down, and she cross-checked it once against the printed reference. Her *personal* stakes are basically nil: salaried, union, the hospital carries the liability, nothing of hers rides on it. But the patient is a neonate in the ICU, and if the number is off the kid dies. Separately, there's Bea, Amara's colleague two time zones away, who is chatting at a bar with someone who has no idea the ICU exists, and Bea says, Amara knows the dose is right.

Three different stakes sources, not the usual two: Amara's own interests, the interests riding on the action, and the standards loose in Bea's conversation. When I ran it past Nadia we agreed instantly that if you ask whether Amara knows, the answer is no. Then we explained it in completely different ways. She said it's because *our* conversation had sky-high standards — we were talking about a baby dying. I said it's because the action's stakes are catastrophic and it simply doesn't matter that Amara personally has nothing to lose: the practical enviroment of the decision includes the patient, not just the agent.

Then I did what I thought was the killer move. I stripped the conversation: Bea at the bar, nobody has mentioned the ICU, standards as relaxed as you please. Now Nadia says the attribution is straightforwardly true and I say it's still false. That's a real divergence... except I walked home feeling like it's a divergence about a sentence rather than about Amara. Everything we'd actually care about — should she check a second time, is she blameworthy if she doesn't, is her belief good enough to act on, what we'd criticize in retrospect — we agreed on in both versions.

So what kind of disagreement is this, really? Verbal, about "knows"? Substantive but theoretical rather than worldly, in the sense that we agree on every verdict about behavior and criticism and only disagree about which facts *explain* those verdicts? Are we answering different questions under one heading — one about the semantics of attributions, one about the epistemic standing of a belief — which is why we keep talking past each other? Or is there a genuine disagreement we both failed to isolate, maybe about *whose* stakes count when the agent and the person harmed come apart, which neither purism nor encroachment was built to handle?

Give me the diagnosis with reasons, and pull apart what each view is actually committed to when it says its thing, because I suspect the ambiguity is hiding in there. Is there a case that comes out differently for the encroacher and the contextualist in a way that isn't just competing intuitions about a sentence? Would a formal result help — say something pinning down how stakes and credences have to interact if knowledge is the norm of practical reasoning, or a closure result that only one side can live with?
