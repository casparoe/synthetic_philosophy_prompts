For the closing session of a graduate seminar on philosophies of history, I have paired selections from Ibn Khaldun's Muqaddimah and from Polybius on the cycle of constitutions with Herbert's *Dune*. I want a reading of the novel that tests a specific hypothesis rather than repeating the familiar claim that the book is a warning about messianic leaders: namely, that *Dune*'s political plot is rigorously Ibn Khaldunian, while its ecological plot is the only genuinely linear element in the work.

On the political side, consider the Bene Gesserit's Missionaria Protectiva, which plants the Lisan al-Gaib prophecy on Arrakis generations in advance, and which becomes causally potent only when Paul attaches it to a people whose group solidarity is already extreme — close to Ibn Khaldun's insistence that a religious call multiplies *'asabiyya* but cannot substitute for it. Then consider the conquest: Paul marries Irulan for dynastic legitimacy, rules from Arrakeen, and leaves the CHOAM and Landsraad apparatus intact, which looks like Ibn Khaldun's pattern of the conqueror adopting the forms of the conquered, and possibly the first stage of his generational slide from desert hardness to sedentary luxury. Against this, ask whether the terraforming — Liet-Kynes's centuries-long, cumulative, deliberately engineered and irreversible transformation of the planet — is Herbert's real answer to Spengler and Toynbee: a civilization escaping the seasons of the organism by becoming an object of long-term design.

The harder question is whether the prescience plot undercuts both readings. Paul foresees a fixed future in which the jihad arrives anyway, which is neither recurrence nor progress but entrapment, and the possibility Herbert gestures at later in the series — that what matters is making history unpredictable rather than making it go anywhere — may be a third option my cyclical-versus-linear framing simply cannot express.

My students have read only the first novel, so keep the reading anchored there. Tell me which scenes and set-pieces to put on the syllabus for each of the three threads, where the analogy strains or fails, and what the novel reveals about *'asabiyya* that argument alone would miss.
