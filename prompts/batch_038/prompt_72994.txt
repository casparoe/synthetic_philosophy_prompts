So my little reading group has been slowly working through Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment — the one where you imagine a container full of tiny hard balls ('simmballs') bouncing around and clumping together, and the clumps are supposed to be symbols, with the shapes of the balls determining which ones link up. Last week two of the regulars, Dev and Mara, got into it over what the thing is actually supposed to show, and it went several rounds and neither budged. I was taking notes mostly to keep myself honest, so here's roughly how it went:

1. Dev: the careenium is a deflationary lesson. At the real level there's only geometry and momentum, and every collision is fully accounted for by that. Saying the CAT ball knocked the DOG ball is a manner of speaking — a compressed redescription, not an extra causal ingredient. Symbols don't push anything.
2. Mara: that's exactly backwards. The micro story can't tell you why *these* clumps form rather than others, and you can't even run it — you'd have to simulate every single collision. The shapes are what constrain the outcomes, and the shapes are where the symbols live. If a level explains what another level can't explain, its not just bookkeeping.
3. Dev: "can't be run" is a fact about us, not about the world. Causation is about what determines what, not about what we can compute. And anyway you can always trade your shape talk for explicit statements about the geometry of spheres, with zero loss of content. So the symbol level is derivative by construction.
4. Mara: fine, then run the counterfactual test. Two careeniums, identical in every respect except a notch on one ball, and they end up with different clumps. The notch is a difference-maker, and difference-making is what causation just is. Your geometry-of-spheres move doesn't make the causal work disappear, it only renames it.
5. Dev: right — and notice what you conceded. The difference-maker is a *physical* property. You've shown that microphysics is causally efficacious, described in a slightly fancier vocabulary. To get genuine symbol-level causation you'd need the meaning to matter, and meaning isn't in the notch.
6. Mara: if the meaning isn't in there anywhere, then nothing in the careenium is about anything, including your own thesis, and you've thrown away the only reason to build the thought experiment in the first place. The question was never which level is more real; it's whether the low level is exhaustive of the high one, and you keep sliding between those two questions.
7. Dev: sliding? Those are the same question. If the low level fixes everything the high level could ever say, the high level adds no facts — and facts are what causation needs.

Can you go through these one at a time and say who's right at each step, rather than just declaring a winner overall? And is there something neither of them is bringing up that would have broken the deadlock?
