I am preparing a session of my graduate seminar on mind and machine creativity in which Borges' "Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote" is read alongside the Letter Spirit project of Hofstadter and Gary McGraw, as presented in Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies. You will know the two objects well enough without my summarizing them: a French symbolist whose invisible masterpiece consists of chapters of Cervantes' novel produced line for line identical to the original, which his narrator nevertheless maintains are infinitely richer; and a research program that renders the Roman alphabet as "gridfont" letters built only from a small fixed inventory of straight horizontal and vertical segments, no curves permitted, and asks what carries across all twenty-six letterforms such that they hang together as the work of a single style rather than a grab bag of admissible shapes.

Here is the conjecture I want tested, and it is mine, not something I have found in the literature. Letter Spirit seems committed to individuating a style by what a generative process does with an impoverished inventory, yet it evaluates gridfont letters by inspecting outputs: we judge an alphabet's spirit by looking at the letters laid out before us. If that is right, the framework has no way of registering a stylistic difference between two token letterforms that coincide stroke for stroke, and so it should classify Menard's chapters as style-identical copies of Cervantes' -- the precise opposite of Borges' narrator's verdict. I further suspect the mismatch turns on whether the project's use of the Pauline letter-and-spirit idiom is doing quiet philosophical work that its computational practice does not in fact support.

Please write me the critical reading I would want to hand the seminar, as exactly five numbered items, each one to three sentences, with no introduction, no conclusion, and no headings. The five items should cover, in this order: the strongest way to state the analogy between Menard's project and the gridfont constraint; whether Letter Spirit has resources to resist my conjecture, for instance by treating spirit as a disposition or a set of choices rather than as a property of output tokens; whether the story's central claim -- that identical strings can be different works -- is compatible with, or simply orthogonal to, the claim that every 'a' is an 'a' across wildly different renderings; one place where my pairing distorts either Borges or the cognitive science, stated candidly rather than diplomatically; and finally what the story contributes to the question of whether a program could ever have a style of its own that the Letter Spirit chapter does not already supply.

Take a position on each item instead of tabulating both sides, and flag within the item wherever you are extrapolating beyond what Hofstadter and McGraw actually claim or leaning on a contested reading of Borges. If you think my conjecture rests on a confused premise -- if, say, it equivocates between two senses of spirit, or treats a modest modeling project as though it were a theory of authorship it never aspired to be, or if the assignment simply cannot be done well at this length -- tell me so plainly in your own terms rather than politely complying with the frame I have given you.
