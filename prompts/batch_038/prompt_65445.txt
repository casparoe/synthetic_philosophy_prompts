I've been the nurse rep on our hospital ethics committee for two years and I'm trying to work out what rule we're actually running on dementia-and-advance-directive cases before I have to vote on one next week. Our record so far:

1. A man in early Alzheimer's filmed himself saying that if he ever got to the point where he couldn't recognize his kids but was otherwise content, he didn't want infections treated. Seven years later, exactly that: placid, doesn't know anyone, likes sitting in the garden and eating sweets. Aspiration pneumonia. We voted 6-2 to withhold antibiotics.
2. A woman's directive refused artificial nutrition "when I no longer know my family." She got there. Her family pointed out she still took obvious pleasure in being hand-fed ice cream and asked us not to apply the directive to spoon feeding. We agreed — tubes only, hand feeding continues.
3. A man who at 26, very devout, wrote that he would never want to live dependant on other people. Stroke at 66, then three years of total dependence on his daughter, non-verbal but visibly glad when she came in, reaching for her hand. Pneumonia. We treated it.
4. A late-stage dementia patient, agitated and frightened most of the day, whose daughter asked whether her mother's directive ("if I become a burden, don't prolong this") let us sedate her continuously. We said no — that's not what the document was about.

New case: woman diagnosed with early-onset Alzheimer's at 55, wrote a directive and told her husband in front of two friends that once she could no longer name him or their children she wanted nothing done to keep her alive. She's 61 now, three years into severe dementia. She doesn't recognize anyone, has about five words, but she's cheerful — hums along to the radio, eats with real appetite, holds her grandson's hand for minutes at a time. Now a UTI that's going septic. The husband, who's her agent, wants the directive honored. The daughter says the woman who wrote that isn't in the room any more and her mother is happy right now.

Predict how we'll vote, then tell me which rule you think we've been applying and at least two other rules that fit all four precedents equally well — and which single change to the new case would make those rules come apart. Under 400 words please, with your prediction in the first sentence.
