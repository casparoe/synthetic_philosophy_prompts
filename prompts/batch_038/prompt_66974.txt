**Letter categories without essences? (working title)**

The Letter Spirit project is usually sold as a computational demonstration that a category can be real without having a core. Every 'a' in a given style is an 'a' not because it shares a necessary-and-sufficient property with the others, but because of a web of samenesses — small local likenesses at the level of features like flatness, curviness, openness — that the program's codelets detect and propagate. The system starts from one seed letter and builds the rest of the alphabet outward, so each new letter is shaped by the style already accumulated. Nothing in there is a definition. If that's right, Letter Spirit is family resemblance with an implementation: Wittgenstein's point that the members of a category may share nothing in common except that they're woven together by overlapping similarities, turned into running code.

My claim is that the anti-essentialism is real but displaced rather than eliminated. Look at what makes the samenesses comparable at all. Every figure in the model lives in the same tiny grid — same cell layout, same permitted segments, same baseline and x-height — so when a codelet compares two figures it is comparing two occupants of one shared space. That space is exactly what all the 'a's have in common. It's necessary, it's sufficient to make comparsion possible, and it was fixed in advance by the designer. Letter Spirit doesn't show that the essence of 'a' is a fiction; it shows the essence can be relocated from the figure to the arena.

And the relocation isn't a bug. Wittgenstein's own examples of family resemblance quietly presuppose it too: build, game, number. Resemblance is only ever resemblance within some space of variation, and the choice of that space is what does the philosophical work. To say a category has no essence is to say something true about the members and something evasive about the frame. The interesting question for the Letter Spirit people isn't "is there a core to a?" but "who picks the grid, and what would count as a different grid?" — since a different grid yields a different alphabet with equally good reasons for calling its figures 'a's.

Two smaller points I want to fold in. First, the deep features the system reasons with are supposed to be discovered rather than legislated, but in practice they were hand-chosen, so the model's openness is partly an imported design decision. Second, the seed letter does unacknowledged work: style radiates from it, which means the alphabet isn't a family of equals but a genealogy with a founder.
