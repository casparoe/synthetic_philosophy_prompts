'Paraconsistent logic is any logic where you don't automatically get everything from a contradiction -- that automatic result is what people call ex contradictione quodlibet, and it's what makes a theory trivial the moment one inconsistency gets into it, trivial meaning every sentence in the language comes out true. Dialetheism is the separate view that some contradictions really are true, and it is not trivialism, because dialetheists only accept the small handful of contradictions the paradoxes force on them. The route Graham Priest takes is to add extra truth values to the usual true and false, in particular the "neither" value, and then to give the Liar -- the sentence that says of itself that it's false -- that value, so the sentence comes out neither simply true nor simply false. That is exactly what he means when he says the Liar is both true and false, and it's why the law of non-contradiction has to be abandoned as a logical law. Once you've done that you also have to give up modus ponens, since modus ponens is the thing that would let you slide from one contradiction to any old conclusion you like. The nice part is that the same move handles the strengthened Liar, the sentence that says of itself that it is not true at all, so there's no revenge problem left over. And the moral is that dialetheism isn't a claim about the world being inconsistent, it's a claim about how we ought to manage our beliefs when they run up against these sentences.'
