The thesis. Cicero's *De Senectute* — written in 44 BCE, addressed to his friend Atticus, staging Cato the Elder at eighty-four lecturing two young men, Scipio Aemilianus and Laelius, on why old age is not the disaster it is said to be — is not in the first instance a consolation. It is a political brief about who is entitled to keep holding authority. My claim: the four charges Cicero enumerates against old age (that it withdraws us from active pursuits, that it weakens the body, that it strips away pleasure, that it brings us near to death) are not four complaints about the phenomenology of aging but four standing objections to the continued public authority of old men, and each of his four answers supplies a capacity only the old possess and that a republic supposedly cannot run without — command of precedent and institutional memory; a body whose weakness is irrelevant to the work that actually matters, deliberation and the weight of one's word; freedom from the appetites that make a man purchasable; and a proximity to death that removes the leverage a tyrant would otherwise hold over him. Add the context: Cicero wrote this at about sixty, while Caesar's dictatorship had shut him out of public life, and he was killed a year and a half later. On my reading the work's real subject is what happens when the ordinary distribution of authority across ages is suspended by force.

The argument sketch the objections will be aimed at:

1. The dramatic form matters. The speaker is not an old man reflecting privately; he is the most famous censor of the republic performing before two young men about to inherit the state. That is an *exemplum* addressed to the next generation of officeholders, not therapy for the old.
2. Every answer is a political capacity rather than a psychological state. The first is literally about who commands armies and speaks in the senate; the second concedes physical decline but insists the work that matters does not require strength; the third reframes the loss of appetite-driven pleasure as a qualification for office; the fourth converts nearness to death into bargaining power.
3. The supporting evidence is entirely a roll call of men who hold or held office, plus the pleasures of estate agriculture — itself a form of household governance conducted by other people's labor.
4. Therefore the genre label is not consolation but apologia: a claim about the distribution of authority across ages, made precisely when that distribution had been abolished.
