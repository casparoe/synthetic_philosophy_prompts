Building the centerpiece of a paper around an invented patient and I want to see how the dialectic actually runs before I commit to it. Here's the case.

M.K., forties, recovered from viral encephalitis. Shown a stimulus for 200ms she's at about 90% on forced choice about shape and colour, can answer open questions about it verbally, and can hold the answer for several minutes and then use it deliberately in a little reasoning task ("so I should press the red one"). But there's no ignition signature at all: no late P3b, no sustained frontoparietal activation, just local occipitotemporal responses that decay fast. And she flatly denies any visual experience — says nothing appears to her, the answer just "arrives" — and she's as confident about that denial as she is about her answers.

So: write me a dialogue, roughly 700–900 words, between (a) a Block-style theorist who takes this to be access-consciousness without phenomenal consciousness and hence a clean wedge between the two notions, and (b) a global workspace theorist who has to say what's gone wrong. Neither one gets to be a strawman. The workspace theorist should have real moves available — isnt report-without-ignition simply impossible on their theory, what does the reliable denial of experience itself presuppose, isn't "poised for report and reasoning" just a gloss on broadcast? — and the A/P theorist has to face the worry that their only evidence for missing phenomenology is yet another report, i.e. more access. They should be responding to each other rather than trading set pieces, I want at least one genuine concession from each side, and one point where they admit the case as I've described it might be incoherent or need re-describing. (b) may contest my empirical stipulations, but has to also say what follows if they hold.

No bibliography, no name-dropping of papers, just the argument. Last line should begin "Verdict:" and say which of the two is better placed to explain M.K. — "Block", "GWT", or "Neither" — followed by a reason in ten words or fewer.
