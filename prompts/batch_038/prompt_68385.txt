Third-year philosophy phd here, background is metaphysics rather than logic, so bear with me — I might be describing something everyone in foundations already has a word for and I've just never heard it. I've been sitting in on our set theory reading group for about a year and there's a move that keeps recurring that I can't pin down.

Two instances of what I mean. First: someone in the room asks whether CH is true, and the answer comes back as basically "what are you asking — it's true in L, false in the Cohen model, so unless you fix which universe you're talking about the question doesn't have an answer." Fine, I follow that. But ten minutes later the same person is arguing about the structure of the reals and talking flat-out about *the* universe of sets, no relativizing whatsoever, and nobody in the room registers any tension. The deflationary switch only gets flipped for the questions where it's convinient. Second: projective determinacy is also independent of ZFC, and yet people talk about it as something we have excellent reason to believe, on the grounds that it settles a pile of other questions in a satisfying way and gives you this clean picture of the projective sets. So the exact same metamathematical status — independent of the base theory — gets read two totally different ways: for one statement it's a reason to say there's no fact of the matter, for the other it's a reason to keep hunting for the right axiom. And as far as I can tell nobody ever states the rule for which treatment a given statement gets.

So, is there a name for this? To be clear about what I'm *not* after: not the general realism/anti-realism debate, and I know the universe-view-versus-multiverse stuff is adjacent, but the thing I keep noticing feels narrower and more specific. It's the double register — the way a truth-deflating reading of independence gets switched on and off within a single conversation, and the fact that what seems to control the switch is whether there's an agreed structural story around the statement, not anything about its logical status. That last part is what I most want a handle on, because it looks like an unstated criterion doing real work in the arguments.

What would actually help me: tell me whether an established concept covers exactly this, and if there are several near-misses, walk me through each one and say precisely where it stops fitting, rather than just handing me the closest label and moving on — I need to be able to write "this is not the same as X, and here's why" in a chapter draft. If it genuinely doesn't have a name, then coin one for me: definition, a couple of clear cases that fall under it, and a couple of boundary cases that look like it but aren't. No need for references or citations, I just want the concept carved up properly.
