I'm a novelist and last week I got stuck between two people at a residency dinner who ended up in a fight I've been trying to steal for a scene ever since. Neither would give an inch and I couldnt pick a side, so referee it for me.

Marcus is a surgeon. He said he deserves his income because he paid for it — a decade of training, the call nights, and he'd have been perfectly happy doing something easier. Nadia, who writes about labor, said effort doesn't rescue him, because the capacity to grind away for years is itself a gift: temperament, health, a household that didn't come apart when he was twelve. Follow the chain back and every link is something he didn't do. Marcus said fine, go all the way with that and nobody deserves anything, which makes the man who works himself sick and the man who doesn't morally identical, and makes his patients not deserve to get better either — the argument is eating things it obviously shouldn't. Nadia said she never claimed they're identical; desert just isn't the only reason to give someone more, and you can distribute by contribution, need and incentives without pretending anybody earned it as a matter of moral desert. Marcus said that's the dodge — contribution just means whatever people will pay for, so she's relabeled the market and called it something other than desert, which is what he said to begin with. She said no: contribution is what you actually do to other people's lives, and the market measures that atrociously, which is why a night nurse and a guy structuring derivatives can earn 20 to 1 in the wrong order — dropping the word desert is the whole point of her view, not a retreat from it.

Go move by move: who's right at each of the six steps, and why. Then tell me what neither of them said that should have been said.
