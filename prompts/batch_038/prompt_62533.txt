Okay so here's the view I've basically settled into, and I want someone to actually try to break it instead of nodding along.

I think the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization are doing real philosophical work, not just modeling housekeeping. Short version: an agent whose credences and utilities aren't probabilistically coherent is exploitable — there's some package of bets it would accept and lose on — and that exploitability is what upgrades coherence from a tidy convention into a requirement of rationality. Then there's the converse, that anything satisfying the axioms is representable as an EU maximizer, which is why people say the axioms characterize rationality up to representation. Put together I take it that incoherence isn't a matter of taste, it's a kind of self-sabotage.

What would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely embarassing, and I want to know which ones a defender of the package would themselves concede as biting rather than which ones critics shout. Some things I want engaged with specifically, not gestured at:

- The exploitability link itself. Does "you could be pumped" survive transaction costs, and does it survive an agent that can see the pump coming and reason backwards through it?
- The gap between "would be cleaned out by an adversary who knows your preferences" and "irrational."
- The converse direction: representation isn't unique, and being representable doesn't say anything about what the agent actually computes with.
- Cases like St. Petersburg where expected utility simply isn't defined — does the argument go silent exactly where it was supposed to be doing the work?

And one concrete scenario. Say someone builds an agent whose credences over a three-way partition sum to 1.3 — visibly, arithmetically incoherent — but because of how its betting module is wired, no sequence of offers it would actually accept produces a sure loss. Is that a counterexample to my view, or does my view already have an escape hatch for it? And if the escape hatch turns out to be that "coherent" just means "not pumpable," tell me straight whether the whole thing has been qualified into a tautology, and whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind or whether tautology is exactly what it was ever entitled to. Finally: what single empirical finding would move you most here, and what would it have to look like to be credible?
