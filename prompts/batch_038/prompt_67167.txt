And three says both of those are literally doing the same math from different angles and the whole thing is a fight about notation rather than about the world.

So: give me your credence, as an actual number, that camp three is right — that the dispute is mostly bookkeeping rather than a substantive disagreement about how evolution works. Then take it apart. I want seperate numbers with a line of justification each for stuff like:

- whether the population-genetics models on each side can always be translated into one another without anything getting lost in the conversion
- whether kin selection plus reciprocity genuinely covers the cases the group people point at (eusocial insects keep coming up, and also that thing a few years back where a pile of biologists signed a letter saying the group-selection crowd had it wrong)
- whether "the unit of selection" picks out one fixed thing in nature or shifts depending on which question you're asking
- whether half the heat comes from people using "selection" to mean different things, like replication vs. getting filtered vs. causing offspring
- and here's one I don't see discussed much: whether what's actually in dispute is the existence of group-level effects or just their magnitude, since almost nobody denies groups can differ in output
