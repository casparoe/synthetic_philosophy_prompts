Do a bit of scratchpad brainstorming first (list the moves each side can make, where they genuinely overlap, and which cases actually discriminate between them) and then throw the scaffolding away and write the essay proper.

The controversy: etiological / selected-effect accounts of function (Wright, Millikan, Neander — a trait's function is whatever its ancestors were selected for) versus causal-role accounts in the Cummins mold (a trait's function is just some causally relevant contribution it currently makes to the containing system's capacities). I want you to actually adjudicate this, not recite the map. And I want you to adjudicate it using cases where the two accounts are forced apart. Biology supplies plenty of these, but I think the machine-learning analogues are sharper, because there we get to *design* both the selection history and the current role and then see which one our function-talk is tracking.

Here's the twist I want to be load-bearing. Two cases:

**Case A — the swapped assay.** Directed evolution in vitro on a library of binders. Rounds 1–40 select hard against target X. Then the assay changes: X is dropped entirely and rounds 41–100 select on a completely different property, Y. Pull a molecule out of the final population. It still has structural features whose only explanation is the X phase — a pocket nobody wants anymore — and those features are still causally load-bearing, just now for Y. Selected-effect says the pocket's function is binding X (so its current contribution is not its function, and in some sense the pocket is malfunctioning). Causal-role says its function is whatever it contributes to now. Which answer does a working biologist or a protein engineer actually want, and does wanting it settle anything about truth?

**Case B — the dead circuit and the exapted circuit.** Two mirror-image sub-cases in a trained network. (i) A component that was causally load-bearing for most of training, that gradient descent later routed around, and that now just fires and nudges logits without changing behavior — etiological says it has a function, causal-role says it does something but is that a *function*? (ii) A component that contributed essentially nothing during training but becomes causally critical in a new deployment scaffold — different prompt distribution, tools, long horizon. Causal-role says it has a function; selected-effect says it has none, and that verdict looks bad when someone is trying to reason about what the model will do off-distribution. Interpretability writing talks constantly as though components have jobs. Which account licenses that talk, and does it matter for safety arguments that lean on it?

Then take seriously the worry that this whole debate is epistemics in disguise: in ML we *have* the training logs, so the history is legible and etiological talk wins by default, whereas in biology the history is mostly unrecoverable so we fall back on causal role. That story makes the disagreement look like it's about what we can know rather than about what functions are. Either dissolve that worry or use it — don't just note it and move on.

Also handle the middle positions and the escape hatch: modern-history views that broaden "selection" to cover drift, bottlenecks, and non-adaptive sorting; and the pluralist move (there are several legitimate function concepts, choose per context). Do not cop out into pluralism unless you can say precisely what explanatory work each concept does and why they shouldn't collapse into one. If you do land on pluralism, name the default one and say why the others are derivative.
