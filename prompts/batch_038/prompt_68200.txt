write me a lab SOP — the laminated kind pinned above a bench — for a facility that manufactures and validates thought experiments for physics. 700-850 words, usual headings (Purpose, Scope, Apparatus, Procedure, Quality Control, Troubleshooting, Limitations, Sign-off), deadpan all the way through: no winking, no authorial nudge, funny to a philosopher of science and merely weird to everyone else.

the jokes have to come from the epistemology being done right. Frame the field's central dispute as a compliance headache: one half of the lab holds that a thought experiment is just an argument in costume, so every run has to survive having the imagery stripped out and the conclusion re-derived from premises alone; the other half holds it's a genuine a priori window onto nature that adds no new empirical input, and files its paperwork accordingly — and the SOP has to accomodate both without ever admitting they can't both be true. Bake in calibration runs against the classics (Galileo's two bodies tied together, Einstein chasing a light beam, the demon at the trapdoor), contamination controls for subjects who've already read the literature, the procedure for when an intuition fails to replicate in a new population, and a troubleshooting table for inconclusive runs and runs that turn out to be naked intuition-mongering. Target the practice of treating intuitions as data, not any named person — invent the PI and the facility.
