I just spent two hours arguing with my friend about whether a country should actually *try* to top the World Happiness Report, and we got nowhere. Her take: the ranking isn't measuring happiness, it's a ladder question put to maybe a thousand people a year averaged over three years, and the six-factor breakdown basically resolves it into income plus social support plus a couple of survey items about generosity and corruption — so climbing it is textbook Goodharting, and any ministry pointed at it would end up coaching citizens to say ten. My take: the alternative is worse, because if you optimise experienced wellbeing instead (the day-reconstruction stuff, how your Tuesday actually felt) you build a nation of comfortable people who've stopped wanting anything.

Write me a short story that dramatizes that fight rather than resolving it. A smallish country decides to genuinely go for number one, appoints someone to run it, and we follow maybe eight or ten years as the number climbs. I want the plumbing visible: the survey wave, the sampling frame, a civil servant slowly realising the ladder question is really asking people to compare themselves against a local reference group — and therefore that you can raise the score by moving the reference group instead of moving the lives. Also a character on the affect-measurement side who thinks the whole project is a category error, but make them annoying enough that the reader isn't sure who to root for.

End it so it's definately ambiguous whether the citizens got better lives or just got better measured — no speech, no reveal, just a final scene where both readings are available. Somewhere around 1500 to 2000 words.
