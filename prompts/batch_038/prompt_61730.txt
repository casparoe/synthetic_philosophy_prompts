I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I have been assigned a long feature on the current state of consciousness research: the rival neuroscientific programs, the arguments over whether unreportable perceptual states should count as evidence at all, and the recurring claim by otherwise empirical scientists that the field is measuring proxies for something no measurement can reach. My editor wants the piece to open with a short box in which I state my own working assumptions explicitly, so that readers can see where I am standing rather than inferring it from my choice of interviewees. Before I hand that box in, I want a rigorous consistency audit of the position I have actually arrived at, because I suspect parts of it do not cohere and I cannot tell which parts.

Here is the package, stated as I would state it to a colleague:

1. I accept the reframing of the hard problem as a meta-problem. The tractable question is not first-order — why is there something it is like to be a subject — but second-order: why do people, naive subjects and expert researchers alike, judge and report that physical and functional accounts leave something out.

2. Even a complete answer to that second-order question would constrain, not replace, an answer to the first-order one. Explaining why we are drawn to the intuition of a gap would not by itself constitute an account of consciousness.

3. I am an illusionist about the phenomenal. The properties people take themselves to be introspecting do not exist as they seem to, and what exists instead is a standing disposition to believe that they do.

4. My preferred mechanism for that disposition is a compression story, which in my notes I have been calling the undisclosed-compression hypothesis: introspection delivers a low-bandwidth summary of processes that are in fact richly structured, and the summary format conceals its own encoding scheme. The gap intuition is what happens when a compressed file is read by something that cannot see that it has been compressed.

5. Constraint on any acceptable solution: it must be theory-neutral. It may not presuppose the truth of any particular theory of consciousness — global workspace, integrated information, higher-order representation, predictive processing — since otherwise the meta-problem simply smuggles the hard problem back in through the choice of framework.

6. Success criterion: an adequate solution dissolves the intuition. Someone who fully grasps the explanation should no longer find the gap compelling, in the way that someone who has been walked through a levitation effect no longer finds it compelling.

7. At the same time, I am drawn to the analogy with stubborn perceptual illusions. I understand precisely how the Müller-Lyer figure works, and the lines still look unequal to me; I expect the gap intuition to behave the same way for anyone who retains the apparatus that generates it.

8. On evidence: first-person reports remain the primary data of this field, and having a story about how such reports are generated is not a license to discount them.

Two things I would like from you. First, the audit. Which of these genuinely pull against one another, and how badly? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally troubling, and for each say what would have to be surrendered or rewritten and what that costs — including whether the cost falls on the coherence of the package or on the interest of the view that survives. Some of these will look like conflicts and will not be; I want those identified as well, with the reason they dissolve, because otherwise I will spend three hundred words apologizing for a problem I do not have. Second, given your diagnosis: which subset of this could I print as a stated position without its being picked apart by a reader who knows the material, and which single revision would render the whole package safe to publish roughly as written. Please do not survey the hard problem or introduce me to consciousness studies; I am not asking what is true, only whether what I have written down hangs together.
