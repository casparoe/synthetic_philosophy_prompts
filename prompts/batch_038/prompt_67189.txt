kind of a personal one. my dad moved into hospice last month and I've been rereading Zhuangzi at night, mostly the bit where his wife dies and Huizi comes by to offer condolences and finds him drumming on a tub and singing. my brother took a philosophy of death class and he's convinced that passage is just the Epicurean argument in Chinese drag — death is nothing to us because when it's there you aren't, so there's no subject left to be harmed, and everything else Zhuangzi piles on there (the qi turning into form, form into birth, birth back around into death, the whole four seasons comparison) is just decoration on that same point. his advice is that when the 3am dread hits I should run the argument in my head until my feelings catch up to it. Ive been doing that for two weeks and it does basically nothing, so I'm wondering whether he's misreading it. the thing I keep tripping on: Zhuangzi says he did feel it when she first died, before he ever got to the tub. if the point were that there's nobody left to be harmed, why would he bother admitting that?
