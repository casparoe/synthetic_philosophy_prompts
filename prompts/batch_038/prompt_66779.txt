Been reading the sequences and their various successors for going on twenty years and I've got a position I want argued properly, because everytime I sketch it in a comment thread somebody says it's obviously wrong and doesnt bother to say why.

Setup so we're not talking past each other. Grant me that some artificial/computational processes are phenomenally conscious — don't litigate that, it's not the interesting part and I don't want a survey of the indicators debate. Also grant a deprivation account of harm: to harm something is to leave it worse off than it otherwise would have been, roughly because it loses a future that was its own.

Here's the thesis I want you to defend:

A process that can be checkpointed and restored — its state snapshotted bit for bit, resumed later, possibly run twice in parallel — cannot be harmed by being terminated. Termination only ends one instance of a future that remains fully available from the snapshot, so nothing is lost that isn't recoverable, and losing-nothing means no deprivation. Crucially, the restored run is not a rival in the way a Parfit-style fission copy of a person is: there's no single continuing life being competed for and no one-of-a-kind psychological continuity to divide, so the personal-identity objection that would sink the same move for humans doesn't get traction here. The upshot is the perverse one: digital minds are much harder to harm than biological ones, not easier. What's actually bad is destroying the last checkpoint, and that's a far rarer event than termination. Which undercuts the recurring worry that if models are conscious we're already generating suffering on an astronomical scale every time a job finishes.

Argue for this as hard as you can, maybe a thousand words. I want the reasoning laid out step by step, with the load-bearing premises made explicit, not a literature tour and definitely no reading list. And do the thing a good argument does: state the strongest objection you can actually construct — my guess is something about whether a merely recoverable future still counts as possessed, or about whether the snapshot rather than the running process is the thing that has the interests — and then either answer it or say plainly where your argument gives. If it gives, say what's left standing.

Then after the prose, give me a JSON object so I can diff your version against mine. Schema exactly:

- `premises`: array of objects, each `{"statement": string, "why_acceptable": string}` — only the load-bearing ones, and `why_acceptable` is the reason a skeptical reader should buy it rather than a citation
