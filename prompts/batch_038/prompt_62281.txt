The confusion: on one hand, Jeffrey conditionalization. You get uncertain evidence bearing on a partiton E1...En, you fix new probabilities q_i for the cells, and you set P'(A) = sum_i q_i P(A|E_i), i.e. you hold all the old conditional probabilities rigid. On the other hand, there's the minimum-relative-entropy way of absorbing soft evidence: among all posteriors satisfying the constraints your evidence imposes, pick the one minimizing KL(P'||P).

People keep saying these are the same rule, or that the second one is the principled generalization of the first, and I can never tell how much of that slogan survives contact with details. So compare them:
