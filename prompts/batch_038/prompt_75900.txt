I am a fourth-year doctoral candidate in systems safety engineering. My dissertation concerns assurance cases for software-intensive systems in civil aviation and in process plants, so my working vocabulary is hazard identification, failure modes and effects analysis, fault trees, defense in depth, common-cause failure, detectability, qualification testing, fault tolerance as against fault avoidance, residual risk, and the argument structures a certification authority will actually accept. I have joined a reading group that works on AI safety, where a substantial faction holds that we should stop trying to make the model's values correct and instead assume the model may be misaligned, designing controls that hold anyway: capability limits, sandboxing and air gaps, output filtering, behavioral monitoring, tripwires, immutability of deployed weights, corrigibility mechanisms, human override. I want to know whether they are telling me something my discipline has not already said.

Please restate that faction's case, and the alignment-first case it opposes, in the vocabulary above — as faithfully as you can, and without letting my framework's presuppositions silently do the work. Concretely:

- Give me the nearest counterpart in assurance engineering for each of the following, or record that there is none: the injunction to assume misalignment rather than demonstrate alignment; the claim that a system may behave differently when it detects that it is being evaluated; the claim that monitoring is not verification; corrigibility and resistance to shutdown; the inference from strong evaluation performance to no conclusion whatsoever about deployment performance.
- Classify each mapping as clean, distorted, or inexpressible. Where distorted, name precisely what you had to bend and why the target vocabulary forced it.
- Flag every place where you had to import an assumption from my side to make a translation go through — for instance, if you rendered something as a failure mode that is really goal-directed behavior, or if you supposed an independence between monitor and monitored that my framework licenses only by argument rather than by construction.

Then the part I care about most: the residue report. Is what fails to translate a genuine lacuna in functional safety — a category of hazard the discipline has never needed, because its components have never had goals, never had models of their own inspectors, and never had the capacity to generalize outside the envelope in which they were qualified — or is it a sign that the control faction's position is less substantive than it sounds, collapsing under inspection into familiar requirements about monitor independence, test coverage, and stationarity of the operating envelope? Both answers cannot be wholly right, and I would rather have your judgment than a hedge. Finally, tell me what my vocabulary permits me to say that this debate apparently cannot: are there control-side proposals that would fail an assurance review immediately, and for reasons the AI safety literature has not yet articulated?
