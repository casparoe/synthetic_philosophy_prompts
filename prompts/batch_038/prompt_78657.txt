I am a senior at a public high school, and our school funds a small number of year-long independent research projects each year. Applicants submit a proposal to a panel of three teachers; accepted projects meet weekly with a faculty adviser and end in a paper of roughly five thousand words. I have eleven weeks of seminar time, and my background is an AP government class plus the student philosophy club I help run, not any formal coursework in ethics. Please write the proposal I will submit to the panel.

My topic is Nozick's entitlement theory, and in particular the Wilt Chamberlain example. As I understand it, the example runs: start from a distribution of holdings that you already accept as just; then let a very large number of basketball fans each voluntarily hand over a small extra charge to watch Chamberlain play (I believe the standard version has a million fans at twenty-five cents apiece, though nothing in my question turns on the exact figures). The resulting distribution came about entirely through voluntary transfers from a just starting point, and Nozick's conclusion is that no patterned or end-state principle of distributive justice is compatible with that kind of freedom, since holding the pattern in place would require either forbidding the transfers or repeatedly stepping in afterward to reverse them.

My half-formed idea is that the example runs together two claims that should be separated. One is a claim about patterns as such. The other is a claim about how a pattern has to be maintained in practice: that someone must watch the transfers, judge them, and undo them, and that this standing surveillance and correction is much of what makes a patterned principle seem to disrespect the people whose lives it manages. Now consider a patterned principle that never requires anyone to notice or reverse a transfer, because the pattern is built into the rules of transfer itself — for instance, a fixed percentage withheld automatically at the moment of each transaction and paid out by a standing rule, so that the patterned outcome is produced by the machinery of the exchange rather than by an official who observes, evaluates, and corrects what Chamberlain and his fans did. I want to know whether the Chamberlain argument still bites against that sort of principle. If it does, then the argument must be resting on something other than continuous interference with people's lives; if it does not, then a good deal of its force is really about the administrative and informational cost of maintaining patterns, which is a different and much more empirical claim than the one usually attributed to it.

Please write the proposal so that it contains:

1. a precise statement of the research question, framed so that it has a determinate answer rather than amounting to a survey of the debate;
2. a brief account of why the familiar responses on both sides — those who accept the example and those who resist it — leave my question open, together with the conditions under which my distinction between automatic and policed patterns would be a real distinction rather than a verbal one;
3. the method: what I would actually do, week by week across the eleven weeks, including at least three concrete variants of the Chamberlain case constructed to isolate the difference I have in mind, with a sentence on what each variant is meant to test and what result from it would be informative;
4. the strongest objection I should expect from the panel — for example, that an automatic rule still presupposes a state that knows what everyone holds, or that the distinction collapses once transfers are numerous, varied, and easy to restructure — and how the project would answer or absorb it;
5. what a successful result would look like, and what would count as the project's failing, including the possibility that I end up showing the argument does not depend on enforcement at all and that my question was misconceived from the start;
6. an honest assessment of whether this is achievable in five thousand words given my preparation, and what I should narrow or cut if it is not.

Please do not include a bibliography, a list of secondary sources, or citations of any kind; my adviser has assigned me the relevant chapter and I will handle references myself. You may refer to positions and arguments by description. Also, do not assume that the panel or I have read beyond the example itself: whenever you use a technical label, gloss it in a sentence rather than leaving it to stand on its own.
