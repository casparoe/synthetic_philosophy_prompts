Something I keep running into and don't have a word for. I work in compliance at a mid-size hospital, and separately I read a fair bit of ethics for fun, and I've become convinced the same shape keeps occuring in both places, but I can't find the shape itself named anywhere.

Start with lying. Take the hard line — Augustine basically, and then the murderer-at-the-door fellow after him — that it is never permissible to say what you believe to be false. Give a community that rule for long enough and it doesn't stop deceiving, it stops saying false things. The deception migrates into sentences that are literally true: an answer to a question nobody asked, a phrase with two readings where you intend the one the listener won't pick, a true statement chosen for what it implies, or the old move of adding a silent qualifier in your own head while saying something that sounds unconditional. By the rule's own test the community turns spotless, nobody ever utters a sentence they disbelieve, and the thing the rule was meant to protect — people not getting taken in — doesn't improve and may get worse, since now the deception arrives wrapped in a claim to scrupulousness. And the rule's defenders point at the spotlessness as evidence it's working.

Same thing on my side of the fence. We're required to log every patient-safety event within 24 hours. For years our numbers looked great. What had actually happened was that the argument moved to the edge of the definition: its a near miss not an event; he lowered himself to the floor, he didn't fall. The form the rule polices went toward zero and the substance never budged.

So: is there an established concept for this — a prohibition framed around a form rather than a purpose, which therefore can't be broken in form but is trivially satisfied in substance, and which people get better at evading the more seriously they take it? I can guess at neighbours and I'd like to know why each of them isn't it: letter versus spirit, gaming a metric, loopholes, self-defeating rules. Which is closest, and what's left over once you subtract it? Does it need the rule to be absolute, or will any narrow strict rule do it? Does the substitute have to be as bad as the original, or does it still count when the substitute is milder?

Don't send me a reading list, I can't look anything up and it wouldn't help. Prose only please — no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables, just write it to me the way you'd write an email. And don't stop at a label: if nothing established fits, coin something, define it, and give me one case that looks like it but isn't and one that is it but doesn't look like it. Smaller question riding along, if you have room: is there a name for the character type — the person who never once says a false thing and misleads everybody constantly?
