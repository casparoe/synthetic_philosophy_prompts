I'm auditing a reading group run by a visiting prof — two weeks on Malcolm's argument that dreams aren't experiences at all, then Dennett's 1976 reply, then free-for-all case discussion. What's bugging me is that when people bring her sleep-lab cases she's incredibly decisive, and I can't work out what rule she's running, becuase the verdicts seem to fit several very different metaphysics of dreaming equally well.

Her calls so far:

- Subject woken out of REM, immediately recounts a long detailed dream → dream, obviously.
- Subject woken out of REM, says nothing was happening → no dream. She flatly refuses to hedge with "well, there was probably one they forgot."
- Wakes absolutely certain they dreamt but can't produce a single image, just the certainty → no dream. Her line was that a dream you can't say anything about isn't a dream.
- Woken at 4am, reports nothing; at breakfast gives a rich account that "came back to them" → no dream.
- Woken out of light stage-2 sleep reporting only a flash of a face and a lurch in the stomach, no story whatsoever → still a dream, a thin one. So narrative structure is not the criterion.
- Hypnagogic material on the way down, eyes shut but not really asleep yet → not a dream, she calls it "dream-adjacent."
- Lucid dreamer who performs the pre-agreed eye-signal pattern during REM and on waking reports the dream including having done the signal → dream, and she flagged this one as the interesting case.
- Guy who insists he has never dreamed in his life but produces a report every single time the lab wakes him → he dreams; his self-theory is irrelevant.
- Morning report that clearly incorporates the real alarm going off in the room → dream; stimulus incorporation doesn't disqualify anything.

Here's the case we're taking her next week. A subject trained to signal from inside REM with the agreed left-right-left eye pattern. The pattern shows up on the recording, time-locked, three seperate times in the night, and the scorer confirms it online while it's happening. On waking: nothing, blank, no impression of having dreamt at all. Then at breakfast the experimenters show her the trace of her own eye signals, and about a minute later she says "oh — that one" and produces an account that lines up with the signal times, which she's confident she would never have come up with unprompted.

So there are two questions in there and I want both answered: was there a dream at the moment of the signals, and is there a dream at breakfast?

What I'm after: your prediction of her verdicts on those two, and then the candidate rules that are consistent with all nine of her earlier calls. Give me at least three genuinely different ones, and for each say why it survives the whole list — I especially want to know how each handles the delayed-breakfast report and the thin stage-2 flash, since those two seem to be doing most of the filtering work and I suspect a lot of the obvious theories die on one or the other. Then tell me which of the nine is the closest analog to the new case, and whether it actually settles anything or just looks like it does. Finally: assuming she answers the way you predict, what further case would we have to build to pry the surviving rules apart? My worry is that they'll all agree on this one too and we'll have learned nothing, so if that's true, say what would break the tie.
