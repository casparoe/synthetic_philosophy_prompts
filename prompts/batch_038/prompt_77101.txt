So I'm prepping a session for our formal epistemology reading group on the Judy Benjamin problem — van Fraassen's thing where you're told a conditional about which box the card is in and you have to work out what should happen to your credences about the box locations. I get the general shape of the debate (straight conditionalizing on the material conditional gives an absurd answer, so people reach for Jeffrey conditionalization, or imaging, or Adams-style rules, or just say nothing truth-conditional was learned at all and it's pure pragmatics). But when I sat down and tried to write out my own verdicts on my own variants, I found I don't have anything close to a consistent view. Here they are.

Setup: three identical drawers, exactly one has my key, the key is either silver or brass. Uniform priors, and given the drawer, silver/brass is 50-50. A PA system announces: "If the key is in the top drawer, it's the silver one." I stipulate the PA is an oracle that says something true and has no selection procedure whatsoever.

1. Naively conditionalizing on the material conditional drops my credence in the key being in the top drawer from 1/3 to 1/5, which feels like way to big a swing — and gets worse the more key-types you add. Wrong.
2. Middle and bottom should stay equally likely to each other. Solid.
3. Top should stay at exactly 1/3. I didn't learn anything about where the key is.
4. But if a colleague says the same sentence to me unprompted in the hallway, I'd take that as a hint the key is probably NOT in the top drawer, and push it below 1/3.
5. If I'd already independently established the material conditional (I checked earlier: either it's not in the top drawer, or it's silver), the announcement changes nothing at all. Zero update.
6. Compare being told "the key is in the top or the middle drawer" — there I'm rock solid that bottom goes to zero and top/middle keep their old ratio. Structurally similar partial information, completely different behavior in my head. I don't know if that's principled or just habit.
7. If instead I'm told the speaker picked uniformly at random among the true statements of the form "if the key is in X, it's Y," then I think the announcement genuinely does tell me something about the location, and I should even be able to work out the direction, Monty-Hall style.
8. Before the announcement I think my credence in the conditional should just equal P(silver | top) = 1/2. After it, P(silver | top) = 1 but P(top) is untouched. So learning the conditional clearly isn't learning some proposition, and I can't say what it is.

Is there an account — or a small set — that delivers all eight? I'd want it stated precisely enough that I could check it, with the mechanism each clause is there for, plus what it says about a case I haven't listed: the PA announcing "If the key is in the top drawer, it's NOT the silver one," and also announcing two conditionals at once, one about the top drawer and one about the middle. And tell me honestly which of my verdicts has to be given up. 3 versus 4 is the one I most expect to lose, and 7 makes me suspect the real question isn't which update rule is right but wether "how should I update on a conditional" is even complete without a specification of the speaker. If that's your diagnosis, say so and say what it implies for the rules-versus-pragmatics split.
