I have set out below the package of views about dispositions that I currently hold, and I would like a careful audit of whether they can be maintained together. I am not looking for background on the debate; I want to know which of these commitments pull against one another, how badly, and what would have to be given up to restore coherence. I have no prior view about which item is the weak link, so please do not take my ordering as a hint about where the trouble lies.

1. A true disposition ascription is made true by a property the object actually has; in the ordinary case the disposition is that property, namely the categorical basis it has at the time. Whether an object has the disposition is settled entirely by how the object itself is then, not by its surroundings.
2. The basis has no identity independent of what it disposes its bearer to do. I reject quiddistic properties, and I take the tie between having the basis, undergoing the stimulus, and manifesting to be considerably tighter than a contingent regularity.
3. The same disposition type can be realized by quite different bases in different objects, and an object can retain its disposition while its basis is gradually replaced.
4. The simple conditional analysis of dispositions fails, but the standard repair — requiring that the object retain the relevant intrinsic character from stimulus until response — is the right repair, and it accomplishes what it was introduced to accomplish.
5. Finks are genuinely possible: a disposition can be lost in the very circumstances that would trigger it, and the object still has it right up to those circumstances.
6. At the level of analysis, what makes a disposition ascription true is roughly how the object would behave across enough of the nearby circumstances in which the stimulus occurs. I want no appeal to a basis, hidden or otherwise, doing work inside the analysis itself.
7. Case verdicts I am unwilling to give up: the wire guarded by a device that would kill its conductivity the instant it is touched is live right up to contact; a styrofoam cup packed in protective material is still fragile while packed; and a glass that is in fact perfectly tough, but that a sorcerer would shatter the moment it is struck, is not fragile.

Two things I want handled explicitly. First, some of these only appear to conflict, and I want those separated out from the genuine problems rather than lumped in with them. Second, for the genuine ones, rank them by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and for each say which commitment has to go and what its loss costs me elsewhere in the package. Close by identifying the single revision that would resolve the most, and sketch what the resulting position looks like.

Please be decisive rather than diplomatic: if you think some item is simply untenable alongside the others, say so and say which one. And if you think the exercise as I have framed it cannot be run — because the items are not determinate enough to stand in logical relations to one another, or because adjudicating them requires settling a substantive dispute that I have illegitimately bracketed — say that plainly instead of producing a careful-sounding non-answer.
