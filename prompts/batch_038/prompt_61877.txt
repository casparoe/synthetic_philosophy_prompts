I am a political theory PhD student working on the reception of Florentine republican thought in contemporary risk governance. Please take the role of a specialist in Machiavelli's political writings and argue this carefully, as a professional philosopher would, before committing to a verdict.

Suppose Machiavelli were shown the modern doctrine of resilience: the now-standard position in flood defence and critical-infrastructure policy that a system should be designed to absorb a shock and recover from it rather than to prevent the shock from occurring. Concretely — the Dutch decision, at stretches of the Rhine branches, to move dikes back and hand land to the river instead of raising the walls, together with the wider practice of pre-authorising adaptation in advance so that no individual officeholder has to improvise once the crisis arrives.

I want two questions adjudicated. First: would he count this as virtù, or as the failing of the cautious man who is ruined because his temperament cannot change with the times, now poured into concrete and renamed a design principle? There is a tension I cannot resolve, and I would like you to face it directly — the same discussion that advises building dikes and embankments before the flood comes also prizes impetuosity over caution, and it is not obvious which of the two resilience doctrine extends. Second: resilience locates adaptability in institutions and standing arrangements rather than in a leader's character, which is roughly where the Discourses' claim that a republic has greater vitality and longer good fortune than a principality, because the diversity of its citizens lets it accommodate itself to the differences of the times, already points. Would Machiavelli accept institutionalised diversity as a genuine solution to the problem of fortune, or object that a system which never requires impetuosity will fail to supply it at the one moment that demands it?

Set out the strongest case on each side before settling the matter. Paraphrase Machiavelli rather than quoting him, and give me no reading list. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble, no restating of the question.
