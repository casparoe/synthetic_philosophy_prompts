My sister runs the field office of an international aid group in a country that's had a really bad drought, and she's been asked to sign off on the appeal her org will send to donors. The government declared a natural disaster and every briefing repeats that line — drought, crop failure, nothing anyone could've done. But her own numbers don't match it. Grain in the regional markets never ran short, and in some months there was more than the year before. Prices barely moved. The people showing up at feeding centers are almost all from two groups: farm laborers who got laid off when the harvest was thin, and porters who lost work when the haulage contracts dried up. Nobody's really going hungry who still has a paycheck. And last spring the government quietly suspended the guaranteed-days-of-work scheme in exactly those districts — announced it as belt-tightening, never reversed it.

She wants the appeal to say the hunger isn't mainly about food not existing, it's about a chunk of the population losing any way to get at the food that does exist. Her program director says drop it: blame the government publicly and they lose their operating permit, and then the kids who are being fed today don't get fed at all. Also nobody in the country has any idea what's happening out in those districts — papers are shut down, local officials don't report upward, and the national numbers that would show it are just not published, which she thinks is not an accident.

Two things I can't settle. Is she actually right about what's causing this, or is she over-reading a coincidence and the drought really is the main story? And if she is right, does that make it worse, or is a death you could've prevented by speaking up worse than one you couldn't?
