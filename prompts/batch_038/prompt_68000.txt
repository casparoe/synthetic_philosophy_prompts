Teaching a session on reward tampering next week and I'm tired of the stock wireheading story — rat presses lever, agent finds the shortcut, everybody agrees its bad. Write me a dialogue instead between two people designing the motivational architecture of an agent that's meant to run for decades.

Ana wants the reward channel sealed: only the evaluator on the environment side ever writes to it, never anything the agent itself produces, on the grounds that any signal the agent can influence is a signal it will eventually corrupt. Bo wants it open: the agent can propose and even enact edits to its own reward function, on the grounds that a frozen evaluator is guaranteed to be wrong about cases nobody anticipated, and an agent forbidden from correcting its goals can't be trusted with anything genuinely new.

What they actually fight over is Bo's compromise — an edit only goes through if a snapshot of the agent, taken before any edits were made, ratifies it. Ana's charge is that this just relocates the tampering, since the snapshot was authored by the same process that now wants the edit. Bo has to explain why thats wrong in terms that survive her best replies, and Ana should concede something real rather than dig in.

I want them to converge on the actual crux — what would have to be true of a snapshot for its ratification to be a check rather than a rubber stamp — and still disagree about whether it can be true. No strawmen, no clean winner, and don't let either of them start reciting the literature; they're engineers arguing over a design, not writing a survey.
