A golf club runs two kinds of competition in the same week. In the handicap event your net score is your gross score minus your handicap, a number worked out from your recent rounds, so the winner is whoever played best relative to their own recent form — and because that number drops when you play well, sustained brilliance just buys you a bigger deduction next time. In the scratch event, and on the pro tour, nothing is subtracted and the best player wins outright.

What theory of desert is the handicap system running on? Tell me what it strictly commits you to, as opposed to what it merely fits nicely with. Two bits I want dealt with specifically: does it presuppose that there's a fixed underlying ability out there which scores are only noisy evidence of — because otherwise sandbagging, playing badly on purpose to keep your number high, doesnt even make sense as a concept — and does it quietly refuse to distinguish natural talent from ten years of paid coaching from a bad season caused by a bad back, since all three move the number in the same direction?

Then: can one institution coherently run both formats, or is one of them conceding something the other denies? And if you decided the handicap view was the true account of merit, what would have to change about prize money, rankings and the tour itself?
