Last month I became coordinator of a community group that sponsors refugees through our government's private sponsorship scheme. We receive a fixed allocation each year — eleven places this year — several hundred files referred to us by partners overseas, and a small discretionary fund that can be spent on legal representation, medical evacuation, or onward travel for people who fall outside the scheme entirely. There are six of us, none with legal training, and about forty minutes per file. The committee is split: half want strict worst-off-first selection, the other half argue we should take the families most likely to settle well, since a sponsorship that collapses harms the people we brought and consumes a place we cannot recover. Our minutes are available to anyone who requests them, including unsuccessful applicants, and one of our founders has already told the committee she expects a particular family to appear on this year's list.

Write the decision procedure my committee could actually run: the questions to put to each file in order, what would count as a sufficient answer to each, the tie-breakers for files that come out level, where the procedure terminates in a refusal, and where it must hand the decision back to us because the question is one of values rather than facts. Draw on what the political philosophy of asylum gives us about the difference between not returning someone to danger and being obliged to go and find people to protect, and about fair shares, but strip out whatever cannot survive contact with six volunteers and a forty-minute slot. Address whether the two funding instruments should run through one procedure or two, and whether people who fail the scheme's eligibility test are inside our obligations at all.

Then run it on three files, one of them the founder's family, and show me the case where the procedure returns an answer the committee will resent. Finish by stating which distinctions you had to flatten to make this usable, and where it will yield a decision that is procedurally clean and substantively wrong.
