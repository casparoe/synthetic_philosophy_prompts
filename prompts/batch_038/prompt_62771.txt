I'm a software engineer with no philosophy training. For the past year I've been reading Avicenna on the self in translation, and separately I write amateur formal verse. I want to attempt a poem on the Flying Man and I keep failing at the ending, so I'd like you to write it.

The setup as I understand it: a man created all at once, fully grown, suspended in air with his vision shrouded from anything outside him, no current of air pressing on him, and his limbs held apart so that no part of him touches another. Asked what he can affirm, he affirms that he exists — but not his heart, brain, or bowels, not his length or breadth, not anything external. And if he could somehow picture a hand, he would not picture it as a part of himself.

Write this as eight to ten couplets, each one self-contained, every second line closing on the same refrain — a phrase the flying man could honestly utter from inside the experiment, of your choosing. Let the couplets proceed as a subtraction: sight, the feel of one limb against another, the pressure of the air, then the imagined hand that gets disowned, until nothing is left but the refrain. Two constraints matter to me. First, the word "soul" must appear exactly once, in the final couplet, and that couplet must also try to sign itself the way a ghazal's last couplet names its author — and fail, because a man created a moment ago has no name to sign with. Second, the poem must not settle the question I can't: whether the flying man has shown that the self is something other than body, or only that being aware of oneself does not require being aware of one's body. In my line of work the second reading is a claim about which inputs a self-model needs, not about what the thing is made of, and the reader should come away unable to tell which of the two Avicenna is offering.

After the poem, give me a JSON object with exactly these fields: "refrain" (the phrase verbatim); "couplets" (an array of objects, each with "n", "subtracts" — what that couplet removes, fails to find, or disowns — "role", one of "setup", "affirmation", "gap", or "maqta", and "note", one sentence saying what someone who knows the text would check for accuracy); "conclusion_left_open" (one sentence on which reading the poem keeps alive and what it withholds from the other); and "riskiest_line" (the line most likely to draw a specialist's objection, with that objection in a sentence).
