I share a flat with someone doing a PhD on the ethics of war, and over the last few months I've quietly started keeping a list, because her answers don't line up the way I expected and I want to work out what she's actually committed to. Here's the record, all of it from her own mouth at dinner: a conscripted rifleman on the invading side is a legitimate target while he's fighting but obviously not once he's thrown his weapon down; a career volunteer officer on the same side is a legitimate target too, and she added unprompted that he, unlike the conscript, has no complaint about being made to bear the costs; a fifteen-year-old press-ganged into one of the invader's proxy militias isn't a target at all even mid-attack, though she allowed you can shoot him at point-blank if it's him or you; a field cook in the invader's army isn't a target, because as she put it he's not a threat; a general planning the offensives from three hundred kilometres back is a target, distance being irrelevent; a woman in a plant making the artillery shells isn't a target, feeding the war economy being a different thing from fighting; and soldiers on the defending side are never targets, since you can't be liable for defending. She says words like liability and status as though I'd know which she means, and I genuinely don't — I can't tell if she's got one theory or two she's alternating between without noticing.

Tomorrow she's on a panel and I'm fairly sure someone in the audience will put this to her, so I want my own answer worked out first. The case: a twenty-two-year-old medic, conscripted by the invading side, whose aid station was overrun four months ago; during the two hours it was being taken she picked up a rifle and killed three defending soldiers, then went back to stretcher-bearing and has spent the months since treating wounded on both sides, including prisoners. She's currently riding in an ambulance convoy fifty kilometres behind the lines. Is she a legitimate target?

So: predict her verdict, but I care way more about the machinery behind it. Tell me which two or three rules jointly fit all seven of the verdicts above — including the real possibility that she's answering some of them at the level of deep morality and others at the level of what the rules of war should say, and switching without saying so — then what each of those rules gives you for the medic, then your best guess at what she'll actually say with the reason you prefer that rule to the others, then one further case I could put to her that would separate the rules for good. Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no preamble and no wrap-up.
