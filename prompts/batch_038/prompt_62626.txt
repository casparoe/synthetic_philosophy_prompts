ok, our decision theory reading group is doing a thing where everyone hands in their actual views in writing and the rest of us spend the session trying to break them, and I'd rather find out where mine breaks before thursday. I've been calling my view superrational-ish but here it is stripped of the label:

1. mirror prisoner's dilemma — me and an exact copy, no communication, simultaneous moves — I cooperate. not because cooperating is evidence the copy cooperates, but because there's really only one decision being made twice, and I should pick whichever option comes out best if both instances of it pick it.
2. same reason for one-boxing in Newcomb: the predictor's model is running my reasoning, so its prediction and my choice arent two independent events.
3. otherwise I'm a completely orthodox causal person. no backwards causation, my choice reaches the world only through its own effects, and I have zero patience for treating a bare correlation as a handle.
4. the symmetry in 1 and 2 means same reasoning procedure, not same evidence. my copy can have totally different evidence and the link still holds, as long as it computes like me.
5. but a copy scanned from me a year ago that has since lived a different life? I two-box against that one. it isnt the same computation anymore.
6. and if my copy is handed the identical choice in a different guise — a payoff table for me, a little story about two farmers for it — I still cooperate. paraphrase doesnt break the link.
7. ten-person public goods game where I know for a fact the other nine are exactly the reasoner described in 1 and I'm the only one who isn't: I defect. the symetry is broken so the argument in 1 doesnt reach me, and I get the good anyway.
8. superrationality isnt morality for me, it's prudence. but it's also why I vote: enough people reasoning my way about the same election is one decision spread thin, so my ballot is a real contribution and not a gesture.

so — which of these actually fight each other, which ones only look like they do, and for the real conflicts what's the cheapest repair and what do I lose by paying for it? please rank them by severity rather than listing everything as equally worrying, and say for each one what I'd have to give up. I'm deliberately not telling you which commitments I'd sacrifice first, partly because I want your ordering and partly because I genuinely dont have a feel for where the soft spots are.
