I'm in a minds-and-machines seminar this term and over break I finally read GEB with two people from the course. We ended up arguing in a café about the MU puzzle — the MIU one where you start from MI and try to build the string MU — and specifically about whether its an actual case of JOOTSing or whether that's a category error.

Marcus: the puzzle is JOOTSing in miniature. Grind the rules from the inside and you never reach MU; step outside, count the I's mod 3, and you see why in ten seconds. That inside/outside gap is exactly what Gödel exploited, so the puzzle is rehearsing the same move.

Priya: no. Nothing here is undecidable. MIU is decidable — you can characterize exactly which strings are reachable — so there's no true-but-unprovable sentence, MU just isn't in the set. And MIU can't even express the claim that MU is underivable, while Gödel's jump needs a system that can talk about itself. You're keeping the vocabulary and dropping the machinery.

Marcus: but the Gödel sentence becomes provable the moment you go up to a stronger theory or add it as an axiom. So moving to a stronger system IS the Gödelian move, and that's literally what I did when I left MIU for arithmetic about I-counts.

Priya: not the same. One jump ends it for MIU — the invariant answers every question about that system for good — whereas every theory you jump into has a fresh sentence, so the climbing never terminates. And your jump is trivially mechanizable, I could script the invariant check, and JOOTSing usually gets wheeled in to suggest minds escaping machines.

Marcus: Hofstadter was no Lucas-Penrose type, so mechanizability isn't a defeater — the point is the stance, that you can always adopt a meta-level view, and a machine can too. Which makes your objection definitional: you've pinned JOOTSing to self-reference so you win by fiat.

Priya: and under your stance-definition a checksum in a compiler counts as JOOTSing, so the term goes trivial.
