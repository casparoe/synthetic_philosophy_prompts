Consider a coherent-extrapolated-volition-style procedure: a large, culturally diverse population is run through idealizing reflection — full relevant information, calm, no cognitive impairment, ample time for self-correction — and the procedure aggregates what these agents would converge on wanting. I want your credence in the following claim: applied to a population like that, the procedure yields an output determinate enough to direct a single course of action, rather than a disjunction of incompatible options or a diffuse set of constraints that almost any policy satisfies.

Decompose the claim before combining. At minimum, give a number and one or two sentences of justification for each of: whether there is any fact of the matter about what a given agent would want under idealized reflection, or whether the idealized agent is underdetermined by the description of the procedure; whether iterating the reflection converges on a fixed point or is path-dependent, so that the result varies with the order in which information and self-correction are supplied; whether aggregating many separately idealized agents preserves determinacy, given the familiar difficulties of combining individual orderings into a collective one; and whether the aggregation step must smuggle in a normative premise rather than merely report a result. Then state how you combine these, which term your final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or piece of evidence would move you most. For reference, my own credence is 0.35.
