I am in the second year of a philosophy PhD and currently taking a seminar on explanation in the social sciences; my term paper is on whether culture can function as a genuine causal variable, with Weber's Protestant ethic thesis as the running test case. Our last two sessions turned on the standard objection: that positing a difference in values to explain a difference in conduct risks redescribing the conduct rather than accounting for it, since the very same cultural factor can apparently be wheeled in to explain opposite outcomes and therefore explains nothing. The reply usually offered on Weber's behalf is the image in which ideas act less as the engine of action than as the switches that determine which tracks material interests push it along — but in seminar that image was simply asserted, and I do not think assertion is enough. I want to see the work actually done, in a case where it is visible.

So: construct a paired thought experiment to the following specifications. Two cases, each no longer than one short paragraph, set in a realistic historical or contemporary economic setting — a region, an industry, an occupational group, or a single firm — with no science fiction, no time travel, and, above all, no stipulation that the two cases are identical in every other respect; that stipulation is precisely what a real investigator could never earn, so tell me concretely which background conditions are held fixed and how someone in the story could have checked that they are. In Case A, a difference in cultural commitments genuinely explains a difference in economic outcomes. In Case B, a superficially similar appeal to cultural commitments does no explanatory work at all and merely relabels the outcome. Further constraints: the cultural factor may not be a disguised incentive, so no differences in pay, taxes, credit access, formal sanctions, or expected returns, and no smuggled reputation or coordination benefits; it must be a belief about what an activity means or what is owed to whom, rather than a preference over outcomes, so that it cannot be trivially re-expressed as a utility function; and you should not simply re-stage Weber's own case, so if religion appears it must be doing something other than reproducing the ascetic-Protestant-entrepreneur story. Most importantly, Case A must contain what I will call an interest-reversal trace — some specific, independently observable behavior on which the cultural hypothesis and the incentive hypothesis make opposite predictions, so that the posited value difference is corroborated by something other than the outcome it was introduced to explain. In Case B, by contrast, the supposed value difference must be recoverable only from the outcome difference itself.

After the two cases, demonstrate that the design works. Please organize the response under these headings, in this order: Design brief (about 100 words, stating what the pair is meant to show and what would count as cheating); Case A (one paragraph); Case B (one paragraph); Two readings of each case (about 300 words, giving the Weberian account and the materialist or rational-choice skeptic's account of both, and showing that the skeptic's account genuinely matches Case B); The load-bearing feature (about 150 words, identifying the single feature that separates causation from redescription, and saying what happens to Case A if you remove it); The skeptic's best attack on Case A (about 200 words, e.g. the charge that the value difference is itself an effect of the outcome difference or of some unmeasured material condition, and whether Case A has a reply); Where this design may be cheating (about 150 words, honestly flagging any place where the stipulations manufacture the verdict rather than letting the theories generate it). I do not need citations or a reading list — I could not check them from where I am sitting, and I would rather have the reasoning than the references.
