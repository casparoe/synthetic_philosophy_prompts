Our 7-person condo board is trying to pick a roofer and it's turning into a nightmare. Four bids, A through D. We had everyone rank them 1–4 and then did head-to-head comparisons, and got A beats B, B beats C, C beats D, D beats A. A perfect circle, no winner. So we gave up and did points instead (4 for first place, 1 for last) and B won. Then somebody noticed our treasurer had ranked A dead last purely to sink it, and if he'd ranked it honestly third, D would have won. And now C has pulled out over an insurance problem, and the points winner has flipped to A — even though not one person's opinion about A, B and D changed.

I want to bring a rule to Thursday's meeting that doesn't have any of these problems, and it doesn't seem like a lot to ask. It just has to: always produce a winner no matter how the ballots come out; respect it when everybody prefers X to Y; not let a candidate dropping out change who wins among the ones still standing; and not boil down to one persons ballot deciding everything for the rest of us.

Is there a rule that does all four at once, or am I about to get embarrassed in front of the whole board? And separately — is just letting the chair pick actually any worse than what we've been doing? If this situation has a name, tell me, but I mainly want the yes or no.

Bottom line up front, then the reasoning, and keep the whole thing under 300 words.
