A couple of the Rapa Nui specialists at our department have been going in circles on Easter Island for two years and my PI wants us to run an adversarial collaboration between them this summer, so I've been stuck drafting the document they're supposed to end up signing. One side argues the island genuinely collapsed before Europeans showed up: palms gone, statue carving stopped for good, the population cratered, and the obsidian spearpoints turning up in late layers are the archaeological signature of war. The other side says nothing of the kind happened — that it's a reorganization, not a failure: the people kept farming, just differently; religious investment shifted into the birdman cult rather than vanishing; and the real demographic disaster is squarely post-contact, with the slave raids and disease of the 1860s doing the work that the collapse story assigns to earlier centuries.

Could you draft what they'd actually produce? I want the claims both can sign about the island's archaeology and about what the record can and can't show; the residual disagreements stated in wording each side would accept as a fair statement of its own position; and the cruxes. My own guess is that one crux is what "collapse" is supposed to mean and another is whether the pre-contact population trajectory really fell before 1722, but correct me if the real fault lines are elsewhere. For each crux, name some specific piece of evidence or line of argument both would agree bears on it — a dating sequence, a class of artifact, an oral tradition read in a particular way — and record what each side predicts it will show. A numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table would be ideal.

And since this is going to get torn apart in review: flag where the wording is quietly doing work for one side, and tell me what would actually change your mind about which of the two positions the evidence favors — which piece of reasoning in your draft is most likely to be the weak one.
