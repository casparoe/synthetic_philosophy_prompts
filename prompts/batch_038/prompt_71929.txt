i'm on the committee of a small student society and our constitution has an amendment clause nobody can agree about how to read. here it is typed out from the original (the society's been going since the 70s):

"Article VII — Amendment
(a) Any article of this Constitution may be amended or repealed by a two-thirds majority of members voting at a General Meeting, provided that written notice of the proposed amendment has been circulated not less than fourteen days before the meeting.
(b) Notwithstanding paragraph (a), neither this Article VII nor any rule conferring power to amend this Constitution may itself be amended, repealed or suspended.
(c) This Constitution shall stand dissolved, and the Society reconstituted under such new constitution as they shall agree, if and only if every member then on the roll signs a written instrument to that effect."

what we want is to drop the two-thirds requirement in (a) to a simple majority, because we keep failing to get two-thirds for trivial stuff. my reading of (b) is that this is permanently impossible — the bar can never be lowered, by anyone, ever. but someone on the committee said: use (c). dissolve the whole thing unanimously, reconstitute under a new constitution with a simple-majority rule, and (c) isn't an "amendment" so (b) doesn't touch it. which seems backwards, since (c) is a far bigger change than nudging (a) — we're allowed to blow up the document but not to make a small edit to it.

can you help me work through whether that actually goes? does (b) reach (c), given that (c) sits inside the very Article (b) says can't be amended — and does the unanimity requirement in (c) make it easier or harder to trigger than (a) in practice? are there other routes: if we amended some other article to redefine "member" or "quorum" so that two-thirds of a much smaller body is easy to get, would that count as amending Article VII even though we never laid a finger on it? i basically want to know whether the society is locked in forever or whether (b) is a paper wall. and relatedly i dont get what (b) is even trying to do — its binding the same members who wrote it, plus their successors, who are in exactly the same position to change their minds. how is a clause like that supposed to have any grip?
