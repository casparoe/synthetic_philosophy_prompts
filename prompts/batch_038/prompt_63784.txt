I'm the recording secretary for a union local — about 400 of us at a food processing plant — and I need a sanity check before Thursday's membership meeting.

Management has been stalling on the contract for four months. A caucus in the local wants us to file a strike notice, but not a normal one: they want to attach a self-executing clause so that once the notice goes in, the walkout starts automatically on day 21 unless a two-thirds vote of the *whole* membership cancels it. They've also been going around collecting signed pledges from members saying they'll walk regardless of what the officers recommend, specifically so we can hand the pledges to the company as proof we couldn't hold people back even if we wanted to. Their argument is that if we still have the ability to back down, we dont have a threat, we have a complaint.

Complication: state law gives a ten-day cooling-off period after filing, during which either side can pull the notice back. The hawks want us to petition the labor board to waive it in our case, on the grounds that the cooling-off period is exactly what makes us non-credible. Meanwhile management has signed a two-year contract with a replacement staffing agency and told us flat out that if the line stops for more than two weeks the plant closes permanently and the work moves out of state. So they've built their own machine and thrown away their own key too, and neither side actually wants what happens if both machines run to the end.

Two things are bugging me and I can't tell whether they're one thing or two.

First: an old guy on the exec board says this is all fine because obviously nobody is going to let it get that far, that's the whole point of doing it. A younger member shot back that if nobody's actually going to do it, then the notice is just a lie told in public — and the lie only deters anyone if enough of us are privately willing to be liars, which is its own problem. Which of them has it right? Is it worse to genuinely intend something awful that you're confident you'll never carry out, or to announce something awful you're sure you wont carry out?

Second: the clause and the waiver are being sold as *improvements* — they make our position stronger — but stronger seems to mean we've made the disaster more likely, not less, because now a scuffle on the loading dock or one bad session at the table can trip something that nobody ever sits down and decides to trip. Is that feeling tracking something real, or am I just squeamish about leverage?

So: tell me what kind of situation this actually is, strategically and morally, without assuming I've ever read anything about it — no reading list, no name-dropping, just the reasoning. Be concrete about the clause and the cooling-off waiver rather than generic about negotiation tactics, and tell me what to do Thursday.

End with a line beginning "Verdict:" followed by exactly one of FILE WITH CLAUSE / FILE WITHOUT CLAUSE / PETITION TO WAIVE / DON'T FILE, and nothing after it.
