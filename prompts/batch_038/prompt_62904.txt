Every design review he makes the same pitch: stop writing a spec and start writing a constitution. His line is that this is just how a country runs itself — you can't legislate for every case that will ever arise, so you lay down broad principles once and hand them to a supreme court, and the court's job on a hard case is to ask what the framers would have decided had they been able to see the case in front of them. So: we're the framers, the model is the court, interpretation beats instruction-following. Anything the principles don't literally cover gets settled by asking what we'd want if we'd thought about it longer, known more, and been less tired and less political about it. He uses this to argue we should drop the whole precise-reward-function effort as a waste of time and just delegate.

I'm not sold but I cant articulate why — I've never studied any of this. The one thing nagging me is that a real court and its framers speak the same language and live in roughly overlapping worlds, and I don't think we and the model do. Tell me whether that's a real objection or a shallow one.
