A friend and I argued until two in the morning about counterpart theory versus transworld identity, and neither of us moved an inch, which I find strange because we share the formal machinery and neither of us thinks any experiment could decide it. His position is that the debate was settled long ago by a translation result: generalise the counterpart framework, drop the requirement that individuals are world-bound, let the counterpart relation be identity restricted to those worlds where the thing exists, and you recover Kripke semantics sentence for sentence — so the two views are notational variants of one formalism and the quarrel is purely verbal. My suspicion is that the translation smuggles in the answer, that it discards precisely the commitments that motivated counterpart theory in the first place, and that what is genuinely at issue is whether two worlds can differ over which individual occupies a given role while agreeing on everything qualitative. I am not confident I have that right either, and I cannot get past the complaint that on the counterpart theorist's account Humphrey never wins the election himself, only someone rather like him. So: what kind of disagreement is this — verbal, substantive but misdescribed by both camps, or two different questions being pursued under one heading? Give me the diagnosis argued from what each side actually says rather than a tour of the literature, and tell me what would count as progress: a theorem, a distinction, a claim about what fixes the counterpart relation, or nothing at all.
