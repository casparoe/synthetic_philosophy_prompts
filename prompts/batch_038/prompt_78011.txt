I got picked by lot for the citizens' assembly my city ran last autumn on zoning, and afterwards I realised the dozen of us who stayed in touch dont actually disagree about whether it went well — we disagree about what, if anything, should replace elections, and none of us can say which disagreement is which. So I want a decision tree that sorts a person into a named position on sortition, to hand out at a local democracy-reform meetup where half the room will be suspicious of the whole idea.

Start from "something is seriously wrong with elections" and branch only on questions someone can answer without knowing the labels. Weight it toward diagnosis rather than cure: is the problem that voters choose badly, that campaigning selects for the wrong traits, that officeholders end up answering their funders, or that letting some people rule others is itself an insult? Then whether selection should be filtered at all (stratified sample, willingness to serve, a competence screen, pure lottery), whether the body deliberates or just votes, whether it is standing or rotates every few months, whether it advises or decides, and whether it sits alongside an elected chamber or replaces it.

Leaves I need in there: a permanent but toothless advisory assembly; a sortition-based second chamber; a legislature made entirely of randomly selected members with elections gone; single-issue citizen juries and no standing body at all; sortition plus a competence screen; plain epistocracy with no lottery anywhere; and "elections are fine, the problem is money." Then mark every combination of answers your questions permit but nobody occupies, and for each say whether the space is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cant actually separate.

JSON please, with these fields: nodes [{id, question, answers [{text, goes_to}]}], leaves [{id, name, path, one_line, weakest_answer}] where weakest_answer is the objection its proponents field most and handle worst, unoccupied [{combination, why, diagnosis}], inseparable [{leaf_a, leaf_b, why}]. No citations or reading lists — I want the logical space, not who said what.
