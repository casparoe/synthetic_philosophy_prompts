For an independent study I've been working through the naturalist account of disease — roughly, the view that a condition is a disease in a patient just in case it is a statistically typical reduction in functional efficiency below the level typical for that patient's reference class, where reference classes are fixed by age, sex, and whatever else the organism's functional design responds to. I built the following argument that this cannot be value-free after all, and my teacher says step (2) is where it breaks, but I can't see how.

(1) On the naturalist account, whether condition C is a disease in person P turns on whether C is a statistically typical departure from normal functioning for P's reference class.
(2) For any C and any P there is some partition of the population — by altitude, by what the local water carries, by whether the condition appears in one's maternal grandmother — relative to which C is typical for P's class; and whether a given variable is one that "the functional design responds to" can itself be settled only relative to some prior partition, so the naturalist's proposed restriction never bottoms out.
(3) If C is typical for P's class, then C is not a disease in P.
(4) So whether C is a disease in P is settled by our choice of partition rather than by anything in P.
(5) So the account does not deliver value-free disease facts; it delivers conventional ones, which is exactly what it was meant to deny.

The case I had in mind for (2): in a certain valley, almost every adult has chronically elevated levels of one hormone, because the drinking water carries a compound from a local plant. Nobody there notices any symptoms, and there is no unexposed comparison population. Relative to the valley, the elevation is normal; relative to the species, it is a large departure.

I would rather keep (3) than surrender it, since without (3) the statistics do no work at all. Which step do you reject? Please commit to one rather than surveying my options, explain why that step looked acceptable in the first place, and say what the naturalist has to pay for giving it up.
