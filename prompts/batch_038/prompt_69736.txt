Im working through the dreams literature for a paper and I'd like a check on whether my package hangs together before I commit to it in print. Here's where I've landed:

1. I'm with Malcolm that "I dreamt that p" isn't a description of an experience that was going on during the sleep period — the report is about the waking impression, and there's no independent criterion for what, if anything, was happening in between.
2. I also buy the Dennett side: the narrative and especially the felt duration get loaded into memory at the moment of waking rather than being lived through in real time. So there is an experience, just not a stretched-out one.
3. But I take the lucid-dream signal verification seriously — the sleeper agrees in advance to make a left-right-left eye movement when they realise they're dreaming, the movements turn up in the REM record at a determinate clock time, and the morning report correctly places the signalling inside the dream story. To me that's evidence that dream experience has real-time temporal structure after all.
4. I want to hold onto something like first-person authority: subjects aren't wrong about what their own experience was like in the way they can be wrong about the external world.
5. Plus a principle of my own that section 3 of the paper leans on. Call it the indexing rule: a report about a past mental state is evidence of what that state was like only if the report could have come out differently had the state been different. If it would have been the same either way, it isn't evidence, full stop.

Go through these and tell me which are genuinely in conflict and which only look like it. Rank the real conflicts by severity, say for each what would have to be dropped or weakened and what that costs me, and then say which single revision buys the most consistency. Please don't just flag every pair — some of these sit fine together and I need to know which.
