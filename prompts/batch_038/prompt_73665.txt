I'm a philosophy PhD student working on distributive justice in health policy, and I've hit a puzzle about the appraisal practice of the UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence that I can't resolve. Since its methods manual was revised, NICE no longer treats a QALY as a QALY: the QALY gains a treatment delivers to patients in severe conditions are multiplied by a weighting factor greater than one that rises with the proportional shortfall in QALYs the condition imposes, so that a smaller health gain to a badly-off patient can be preferred over a larger gain to a mildly affected one. Yet the decision rule around that weighting is still a maximising one — cost per weighted QALY against a threshold — the reference case still leaves out QALY gains to carers and to people who are not patients, and severity is measured as a proportional rather than an absolute shortfall.

What theory of value does the weighting presuppose? Is it prioritarian in Parfit's sense, or something more deflationary: a correction for the thought that the standard instrument understates what matters for people in severe states, a fair-innings claim about entitlement to a normal lifespan, or a political concession to patient groups carrying no evaluative commitment at all? I want the practice's implicit theory reconstructed, with a firm line drawn between what it strictly requires and what it merely sits comfortably with, and an explicit note of where it is underdetermined. Two sub-questions especially: does weighting QALYs commit the agency to anything about the leveling-down objection, or is a weighted maximiser untouched by that objection, given that Parfit aimed it at telic egalitarians rather than prioritarians? And do the exclusion of carers' benefits and the choice of proportional shortfall presuppose anything further — a person-affecting restriction, or a particular account of what makes someone worse off? Please commit to a verdict on each of these rather than presenting the candidate readings as equally live: tell me which the practice licenses, which it rules out, and why.
