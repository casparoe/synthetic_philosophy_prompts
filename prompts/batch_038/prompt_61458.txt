Two years ago I started a spreadsheet, half as a joke, logging what I ate, how many hours I slept, whether I had one of my migraines, and — this is the weird part — which of my long-standing opinions I still found convincing that week. It began because I noticed I was a loud, confident defender of my reading group's big theme on Sundays after a huge roast dinner, and basically a total pessimist about the same theme on Tuesdays after four hours of sleep and black coffee. At first I took that as proof I'm just not a reliable thinker. But the log (n of 1, I know) points at something more annoying than that. It isn't that my views get sloppier or more impulsive when I feel bad — they get organized differently. Well-rested me is a gradualist: change comes through institutions, people mostly mean well, defer to the experts. Under-slept half-starved me thinks in breaks and conversions, distrusts exactly those institutions, and, and this is the part I cant get over, has produced two or three of the ideas I'm actually proudest of, including the one that set my group's whole schedule for a semester.

So lately I've been doing something I'm not sure is sane. I treat my body like a dial I turn to reach conclusions. If I want to know whether I really believe something, I deliberately eat badly and sleep five hours for a few days and see what I think, then I recover for a week and see what I think, and whatever comes out the same on both sides I provisionally accept. My friend Dana thinks this is deranged. Her first objection is that I've built myself a machine for dismissing arguments I can't refute, by pointing at my own blood sugar instead of at the reasons. Her second is that the both-sides rule is arbitrary — why not just privilege the rested state, which is at least the one where I'm not obviously running on fumes?

Here's what I want from you, and please don't just tell me to sleep more and eat vegetables, I've had that. Is Dana right that I'm mixing up where a belief comes from with whether it's true, or is there something she's missing about the case where the origin is *me*, deliberately? Is there any non-arbitrary reason to trust one bodily state's verdicts over another's, or is trusting the healthy me just a preference about what kind of person I want to be, wearing a costume about knowledge? And is there anything actually wrong, practically rather than morally, with making myself worse on purpose in order to find out what I think? I keep having the feeling someone wrote about all of this a long time ago, some philosopher who was obsessed with his own digestion and thought your metaphysics was a symptom of your gut, but I can't place who and I don't want to look him up before I hear your take.
