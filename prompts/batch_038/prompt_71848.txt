I sit on the grant panel for our county arts council — seven of us, only two with any arts background, roughly 180 applications a year for about $1.5M, and we get maybe 12 minutes per file. Last night I got into a long arguement with a friend (ex-curator, very anti-Tolstoy) about whether the council's funding rule — every supported project has to "communicate a feeling to a general audience" — is anything more than a slogan. I said it's just Tolstoy in bureaucratic clothes; he said Tolstoy is unusable and would defund everything worth funding. I think we were both partly right, and I want the thing built properly so I can settle it.

So: take what Tolstoy actually argues in What Is Art? — infection, the conditions he says the degree of infection depends on, his tells for counterfeit work, and the bit about the moral/religious perception of the age — and turn it into a procedure my panel can run on an application without ever having read him. Not a summary of his aesthetics: the steps.

Two constraints from our side that I need you to work inside rather than around. First, we're supposed to score the work itself; applicant statements, CVs and press clippings are out of scope, which I suspect kills his sincerity condition outright unless it can be rebuilt from features of the work alone. If it can't, say so and tell me what the procedure does instead. Second, we're a public body: every reason we write down gets shown to the applicant and has to survive an appeal, and we're not legally allowed to prefer one moral outlook over another — which seems to collide head-on with his religious-perception test. Deal with that collision explicitly instead of quietly dropping the test.

I need the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops at a no, and where it's really the panel voting its taste with a rationale glued on afterward. Then run it on two files: a chamber opera, critically adored and sold out all season, whose dense 40-minute second act the two arts people on our panel love and the other five of us found baffling; and a hospice choir project that produced exactly one live performance for about 90 people and no lasting artifact. Point out where the procedure gives an answer I won't like, and where it will systematically misfire across a whole year of applications.

Bottom line first, in your opening two sentences: is the council's rule a real test or a slogan? Cap the whole thing at 800 words.
