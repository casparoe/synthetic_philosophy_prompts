I am working through the exchange in book 17, chapter 21 of the Analects, the one about the three-year mourning period, and I want help with two interpretive problems rather than background. Since I have no translation to hand, here is the content in my own paraphrase: Zai Wo proposes that one year suffices — the old grain is gone, the new grain has come up, the woods used for the fire-drill have completed their rotation — so mourning should end then. Confucius asks whether, eating fine rice and wearing embroidered cloth that soon, Zai Wo would feel at ease. Zai Wo says he would. Confucius answers that if he feels at ease he should go ahead and do it, and adds that the gentleman in mourning takes no relish in his food, no pleasure in music, and no comfort in his dwelling, which is why he does not shorten it. After Zai Wo withdraws, Confucius tells the others that he is not humane, on the ground that a child does not leave its parents' arms for three years, and that three-year mourning is the practice everywhere under heaven.

First problem: Confucius appears to hand Zai Wo precisely the standard by which he is then condemned — felt ease — and delivers the verdict only once Zai Wo has left the room. Is this a contradiction, a shift to a different question, or a refusal to argue? Explain what the appeal to ease is doing in each of its occurrences, including the one in Confucius's own description of the mourning gentleman, and whether the two uses are the same test.

Second problem: the remark to the other disciples reads as an argument from what the parents did, that is, reciprocity. Does the passage instead support the view that the roles constitute the person, so that mourning is simply what being a son consists in rather than an obligation taken on by a self already complete before its relationships? Point to the features of the exchange that decide between those readings, and say what would have to be in the passage to settle it.
