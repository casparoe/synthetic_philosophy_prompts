So I co-run the ethics club at my school — 14 people, grades 10 to 12, we get a corner of the library for 45 minutes on Thursdays. Our whole equipment situation is index cards, a whiteboard, and about $5 for snacks, and nobody's allowed to use their phone once we start. I want to build an activity that makes people physically feel the difference between taking someone's word on a factual thing (a mechanic saying your brakes are shot, a weather report, whatever) and taking someone's word that something is wrong. Like, my teacher once mentioned there's this puzzle where the second one feels bad even when you know for a fact the person is right and you've got no idea why. I want the club to hit that on their own, no intro speech from me, and I don't want the phrase "moral testimony" or anything like it said out loud until after the game is over.

Here's the rough shape I came up with, tell me if it's dumb. Each table has one person who's the Consultant and gets a sealed set of correct answers. Round 1 is all technical/factual calls where deferring is obviously fine, mostly so everyone builds up evidence that the Consultant is reliable. Round 2 switches to moral calls with the same sealed-answer setup, and the table's payoff depends on matching the sealed answer, so just taking the word is the smart play. But then right after each moral card there's a second thing they have to do — I was thinking someone at the table gets handed a card that says they're affected by the decision and asks out loud "okay but why, convince me," and only the people who reasoned it out themselves can actually answer. Round 3 is the twist I really want: the Consultant's sealed answer is still right, but the reason written under it is embarrassing or just flat-out bad, and the table has to go repeat it to another table. I also want a relay thing going the whole time — in each pair only one person is allowed to talk to the Consultant, and they can pass back the verdict only, no reasons, no gestures, so their partner is stuck acting on a bare "that one's wrong" from someone they trust.

What I need from you: the actual rules and roles, what's on the cards (write me at least 3 sample cards per round, including the bad-reason ones), a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money but still makes people care, and a minute-by-minute timeline that fits in 45. Then a debrief that maps specific things that happened onto the two seperate questions I think are getting run together — did the person who deferred actually know anything, and is deferring a failing even when they did — and that names the different positions people actually take in plain english so I can say them out loud without sounding like I'm reading a paper. You don't need to point me at any sources, I'm honestly not going to read them. Also tell me what's going to go wrong the first time I run this (my best guesses: friends leaking answers between tables, the Consultant turning into a little tyrant, and someone who reasoned it out themselves getting marked wrong and feeling cheated), how to head each one off, and which part of the idea the game just can't reproduce so I know what I'm going to have to explain by hand at the end.
