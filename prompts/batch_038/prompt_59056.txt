I am looking for a carefully developed argument in support of a position about Maxwell's demon that I have come to hold but cannot yet defend properly. I want the physics and the argument, not a survey of the literature, and I have no need of citations or references.

The setup is a modified Szilard engine. A single classical particle is confined in a box in thermal contact with a heat bath at temperature T. Each cycle, the demon inserts a partition, determines which half of the box contains the particle, records the outcome, and then uses that record to drive a piston so as to extract work, up to kT ln 2 per cycle — which, if it were free, would amount to converting heat drawn from a single bath entirely into work.

Now change the demon's memory. Rather than a single cell that is reset and overwritten every cycle, let the demon carry a stack of N cells. Each cell is a two-state physical system with degenerate energy levels, and each arrives prepared in a standard blank state. Within a cycle the demon writes its outcome into the next unused cell and moves on. It never overwrites and never erases. After N cycles the stack is exhausted and is swapped for a fresh stack of N blanks. Call this the stack demon.

The familiar response to the stack demon is that erasure has been deferred rather than avoided, and that a demon with an unbounded supply of blanks is merely a demon that has never come due. I want something stronger and, I think, different. Please argue for the following three theses.

1. The blank cells are not a bookkeeping device but a consumable thermodynamic resource. Because a blank cell is in a pure state while its own equilibrium state at temperature T is maximally mixed, it sits above equilibrium by exactly kT ln 2 of free energy; a stack of N blanks is therefore a fuel tank holding N kT ln 2 of extractable work, and the demon burns one cell's worth per cycle when it converts a blank into a record. On this picture the demon is not a violator of the second law that must be caught out by a cost incurred elsewhere; it is an engine running down a finite store.

2. Landauer's principle is consequently best stated not as a claim about logically irreversible operations — that erasing a bit must dissipate at least kT ln 2 — but as a claim about the free energy content of the memory's physical state, together with whatever mutual information that state carries about the system under control. Erasure, on the second statement, has no special status: it is simply the operation at which a memory's free energy deficit can no longer be postponed.

3. Suppose an entrepreneur offers a factory which, while in contact with the same bath at temperature T and drawing heat from it alone, delivers fresh stacks of N blank cells, so that the stack demon can run forever. Argue that no such factory exists, and state the disjunction it faces precisely: either it dumps at least N kT ln 2 of heat into the bath, in which case the demon has paid the usual price by proxy, or its output cells are not blank in the sense that matters, being correlated with something else the factory has produced, in which case the entropy has been exported rather than destroyed. Then deal with the nastier possibility, that the exported correlations run back to the engine box itself: explain what such a demon would be exploiting in place of measurement, and why that changes the accounting rather than merely relocating it.

Three further requirements. First, do the accounting explicitly, cycle by cycle and stack by stack, stating which subsystem's free energy or entropy changes at each step; assertion will not convince me. Second, meet the strongest objection to thesis 2, namely that it is a redescription dressed as a reformulation, by exhibiting at least one concrete physical situation in which the erasure-centred statement and the free-energy-centred statement yield numerically different answers and in which only one of them is licensed by the second law — a memory whose two levels are not degenerate is one candidate, though you may prefer another. Third, tell me whether, if your argument succeeds, the second law's authority over demons reduces to a claim about initial conditions and finite stores rather than about dynamics, and whether that is a cost of the view or its main clarification.
