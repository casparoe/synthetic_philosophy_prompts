Okay so this has been rattling around in my head for a couple of weeks and I figured I'd write it out instead of staring at the ceiling. I run a team of nine at a mid-size logistics company, nothing glamorous, we basically make sure freight doesn't sit around in yards. Back in the spring a friend of mine who is way too deep into productivity podcasts talked me into keeping a decision log: before any call that's going to stick (hiring, firing, who gets which accounts), I write a paragraph about what I'm leaning toward, why, and how I expect to feel about it six months down the road. Then I actually go back and read the old entries, which is the part he insisted on and the part I now regret.

Two things keep happening and I can't tell if they're the same thing. First, my in-the-moment reasons basically never hold up. There's an entry from April where I wrote that I didn't want to give Dana the Midwest accounts because I thought she'd get steamrolled by the customer's ops guy. Reading it back in July, thats plainly not it — the actual thing was that she'd pushed back on me in front of the whole team the week before, and I remembered that the second I read the sentence, instantly, with the full force of *yeah obviously, that's why.* But in April I'd have sworn on my life the steamrolling worry was the reason. Second, and this is the part that bugs me more, I can never catch it happening. I've never once been aware of swapping a reason out. It only ever shows up in hindsight, and in hindsight it always looks obvious, which makes me suspect I'd say "yeah obviously" whatever the old entry turned out to say. I know how much this sounds like naval-gazing but it's started leaking into things.

Now there's a real decision sitting on my desk. Dana's up for the lead role, and my log from June says I'd already decided yes, in writing, for reasons that still look good to me. My gut this week says no, hard, and I can't articulate why beyond a general unease. One of the other leads told me at lunch, flatly, that I made up my mind weeks ago and I'm now hunting for a way out — she sounded more certain than I feel. And my wife has this running bit where she says "you always say that" any time I tell her I'm not bothered by something, and she's right often enough that it stopped being funny. So which of these do I trust: the thing I wrote while it was fresh, the thing I feel now, or the other people who seem to read me better than I read myself? Please don't hand me a pros-and-cons framework, I've done that, it just relocates the problem. What I want to know is how much stock any of these deserve, and whether there's even a settled fact about what I actually think here that I could be getting right or wrong. Because if the honest answer is that my June paragraph was me making up a story and my current unease is also me making up a story, then what am I supposed to do by Friday? I don't want to flip a coin, but I also don't want to pass someone over for a promotion because of a feeling I'll be able to explain perfectly in six months and not at all right now.
