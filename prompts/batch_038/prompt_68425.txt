I direct a graduate seminar on anthropic reasoning, and after several years of marking papers on the Doomsday argument and its cousins I have become convinced that they are dogged by one structural difficulty for which I have no settled name. Every argument of this family works by treating the reasoner's own position within some large ordered population as a random draw. For the draw to be random, the population must be specified narrowly and non-arbitrarily: only a population of which she is genuinely an unremarkable member licenses the inference, and she may not be admitted to or excluded from it on the strength of what she already knows — that she lives in this century, that she has read the argument, that she is asking the question at all. But narrow the population until she is unremarkable in it and her position within it ceases to carry the information the argument was meant to extract: being the sixty-billionth of the roughly two hundred billion people who have ever wondered how many more will follow tells her nothing whatever about that number. Widen it until the conclusion bites again, and the randomness premise becomes contestable once more, because she is now plainly atypical in respects she is aware of. The two demands pull in opposite directions, and in every version I know, satisfying one undoes the other.

Does this configuration have an established name? I am aware of the familiar general uneasiness about how such populations are singled out in probabilistic reasoning, and of the charge of ad hoc category-manipulation elsewhere in philosophy, but neither seems to fit: this is not the rescue of a cherished thesis by convenient redrawing, and it is not vagueness about boundaries. It is a trade-off in which each condition is met only at the other's expense. What I want is the carving, not the label: the nearest established concepts and how precisely what I have described differs from each; whether anyone has argued that the tension is resolvable in principle, and how; whether the same structure shows up outside anthropic reasoning; and, if nothing established fits, a defensible term with a definition and boundary cases.
