Copy chief at a small science-and-culture weekly. We just adopted a house rule I made up: every occurrence of "free will," "could have done otherwise," "chose to," or "was responsible for" gets tagged by whoever edits the piece, with exactly one of four tags — LEVEY (the sentence only works if alternatives were genuinely open), SOURCE (it just needs the action to issue from the agent's own reasoning, no claim about alternatives), REACTIVE (it's really about our blaming and praising practices, not metaphysics), or NEUTRAL (safe on any view, nothing smuggled in). The point is to stop our writers from settling the compatibilism debate in a headline with noticing they did it.

Write me the procedure a copy editor actually runs to assign the tag: the questions in the order they should be asked, what answer forces which tag, what to do when two tags both fit, and where the editor should escalate to me instead of guessing. Tell me which of the four tags the procedure is worst at detecting, but fold that into whichever step handles it rather than as a separate section.

Give it as exactly five numbered steps, no introduction and no wrap-up at the end. Each step is the question to ask plus one sentence that trips it and one that passes clean, two or three sentences total. Make at least one of your five examples something that reads like completely ordinary news prose but does not come out NEUTRAL once you run it.
