I ran a metrology group for thirty-eight years — resistance standards, thermometry, calibration chains — and retired two years ago. Philosophy has since become my principal occupation, pursued with the habits of the laboratory, which is why I keep looking for the error bars. My difficulty concerns learning from evidence that is itself uncertain, and I have reached the point where I can state both sides of that dispute but neither adjudicate nor set aside. What I want is not a verdict. I want the document that a sincere adversarial collaboration between the two camps ought to have produced.

The camps, as I understand them. On one side: those who hold that all learning is conditioning upon a proposition of which the agent becomes certain, and that apparently uncertain evidence can always be re-described — the certain input being a proposition about the agent's own present experience, with the instrument's error model supplying the likelihood that connects that experience to the world. On the other side: those who hold that probability kinematics is a fundamental update scheme in its own right rather than a derived special case, and that the re-description either smuggles in incorrigible inner states or is simply not defined for the agents who actually exist.

Please draft their joint statement, to the following specification.

1. Agreed claims, numbered, ten to fourteen of them. These should include the shared mathematics — the coherence framework, both static and diachronic; the sufficiency or rigidity condition; the weighted-average form of the rule and its uniqueness; the martingale property; the behaviour of iterated updates — together with what both camps agree needs explaining and what they agree about the conduct of measurement. Every item must be one both would sign without reservation. I would rather have twelve sharp items than forty bland ones.

2. A disagreements table, with columns for the disputed claim in the conditioning camp's own preferred wording; the same point in the kinematics camp's own preferred wording; whether the disagreement is substantive, verbal, or partly both, with a reason; and what each camp would accept as settling it. The rows must include at least: whether any proposition is ever an object of certainty; whether the new probabilities assigned to the observation-cells require an explanation or may stand as inputs; whether evidence is propositional at all; whether the rigidity condition mis-describes observation, given that experience often changes one's conditional credence about the measurand given the very reading just taken — I am thinking of learning that the instrument has drifted; and whether the two schemes ever issue in different recommendations for one and the same real agent.

3. Three to five cruxes: sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp. For each, a concrete case, each camp's prediction recorded in advance, and the outcome that would falsify it. Say plainly whether any crux is a crux only against a background disagreement about the metaphysics of evidence. If you conclude that some part of the dispute is verbal, identify that part and explain why; do not distribute the blame evenly as a courtesy.

4. Case discipline. No urns, no cards glimpsed under a lamp, no tablecloth seen under changing shop light. Build every case from a measurement chain of this sort: a bench voltmeter reading a thermocouple, four and a half digits; an operator who occasionally takes a 3 for an 8 in the last place; a colleague who logs a value called out across the room; a calibration certificate stating an expanded uncertainty; a slow ambient drift; and, in at least one case, two readings taken under different conditions and absorbed in a stated order. I want the order-dependence question worked out on that material, with the partition written down.

5. My own notes, which I suspect are partly garbled and partly partisan. Verify, correct, and tell me which of them already favours a camp:
 (a) The rule leaves the conditional probabilities of the hypothesis given each cell unchanged, and the posterior is the weighted average of those conditionals under the new cell probabilities.
 (b) Applying the rule successively to two different partitions need not commute, whereas always conditioning on the accumulated total evidence makes the order of absorption irrelevant, since the endpoint is a single conditional on a conjunction. I take this asymmetry to be the conditioning camp's strongest technical card, and I should like to see it played or explained away.
 (c) Given the rigidity condition, the rule is the unique update satisfying it, and it generalizes to inputs given as several linear constraints rather than as a partition.
 (d) An agent who expects to update by the rule, and who is not offered bets across the interval, cannot be diachronically Dutch-booked; within its domain the rule is coherence-mandated, not merely permitted.
 (e) The prior credence in a hypothesis equals the agent's own prior expectation of her posterior credence, and something like the reflection principle follows.
 (f) The rule's originator appears to have conceded that the new cell probabilities are not fixed by anything antecedent — they simply arrive — which one camp treats as a decisive admission and the other answers by asking where certainties come from.

6. A closing pair of paragraphs on practice: what each camp tells a scientist to write in the notebook when a reading is uncertain, each account such that the opposing camp would recognize it as fair. This is what I actually want to know about myself.

I am aware of the neighbouring disputes about old evidence and about non-rigid updating generally; bring them in only where they bear on a crux, and say so when you do. No bibliography and no reading list — where a result is load-bearing, state its content precisely enough that I could reconstruct it myself.
