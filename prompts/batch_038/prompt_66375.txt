My colleague Dave has read some logic on the side and keeps saying we're burning money because of Gödel: according to him the second incompleteness theorem says a consistent formal system cant prove its own consistency, so the checker can never vouch for the thing that produced the proof, and we're stuck trusting something no matter how we arrange the pieces. He also says incompleteness guarantees there'll be true facts about our program's behavior that the proof system just can't reach, so there's a permanent blind spot we should budget engineering time for.
