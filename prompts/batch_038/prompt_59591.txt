I've been on our faculty conduct panel for a couple of years now and there's a move I keep running into that I don't have a word for. Someone gets deplatformed, or dropped by their publisher, or loses their column, and the people doing it insist this isn't punishment at all — no rule has been broken, we're simply exercising our own freedom not to associate, nobody owes you an audience. That framing does real work, because it dissolves every question we'd have to answer if it *were* punishment: was the sanction proportionate, was there a hearing, does the person even know what they're being held to have done. And yet the effect on them is, for all practical purposes, exactly what a penalty looks like — income gone, colleagues who used to write now crossing the road, a public marker attached to thier name that they have no way of answering.

Then there's the second half, which may or may not be the same thing. Each individual act in the pile is a private choice that's trivially defensible on its own: a bookshop decides not to stock you, a festival decides not to invite you, a journal declines to referee. Nobody is doing anything wrong, and yet the accumulation is harsher than anything a court could have ordered, there's no one to appeal to, and no single agent whose change of behaviour would make a difference.

So — does this have a name? I can see the neighbours: something about responsibility diffusing across many agents, something about whether private conduct should ever count as state action, "informal sanctions" generally. But none of them seems to cover the two-part thing I'm after, where the denial that it's punishment is precisely what lifts the constraints on it, and where that denial is licensed by the fact that no individual contribution is objectionable. If there's an established concept, tell me which and how much of my description it actually gets; if there isn't, coin one, define it, and tell me where the edges are — what would have to change about a case for it to stop being an instance? I'm after the carving, not just the label.
