my reading group has just spent three weeks on the Meno problem and for the last session i want to hand out a pantoum instead of a summary. five stanzas, twenty lines, and keep the repetition rule honest: lines 2 and 4 of each quatrain come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the last stanza pulls the first and third lines of the opening quatrain back in so the poem ends on it's own first line.

build it out of one concrete case where the same true belief arrives two ways — the shot that lands because it was aimed and the shot that lands because a gust corrected it, or some equally physical pair, but pick one and stay with it. the early stanzas should make the swamping worry felt: once the belief is already true, the reliable process looks like it has nothing left to contribute, since whatever it was worth it was worth only as a way of getting there, and we've gotten there. then i want the pantoum's own machinery to be the reply — a line returning word for word isn't a line that's been used up; the repeat is worth something precisely because the whole poem was aimed at it. the closing loop has to do that work, and it should turn on the difference between the reliability as a cause of the truth and the reliability as what makes the truth no accident, without ever saying that in so many words. no named philosophers, no announcing the moral: if someone who knows the debate can tell that the last stanza is the credit response and not just a tidy ending, it worked, and if someone who doesn't just gets a good poem, that's fine too.
