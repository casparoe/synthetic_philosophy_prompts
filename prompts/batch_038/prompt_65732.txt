For a one-year postdoc application in philosophy of science I need a proposal of roughly 1500 words. My half-formed idea: the received view is that de Finetti's representation theorem settles the dispute between subjectivism and frequentism, on the grounds that any agent who judges an unbounded sequence exchangeable is thereby committed to acting as though a single stable but unknown frequency lay behind it. I want to argue that this relocates the dispute rather than dissolving it, because the entire burden is carried by the extendability hypothesis — and whether a sequence is extendable is very nearly the question the interpretations of probability disagree about. The test cases I have in mind are draws without replacement from an urn holding a fixed finite number of balls (exchangeable, yet not extendable), and a Pólya-urn scheme (extendable, but positively self-reinforcing, so that its limit frequency behaves as a random variable rather than as a property of an experimental setup).

Please write the proposal so that it contains: the question stated precisely enough to have a determinate answer, including what could justify exchangeability without already presupposing one of the rival interpretations; why both the "dissolved" and the "relocated" readings remain live positions rather than settled matters; the method, saying what I would do month by month and why working through these cases is more informative than the obvious alternative of surveying the interpretations against one another; what a successful result would look like, and what would count as failure — in particular, how the project can be told apart from an ordinary defence of probability pluralism; and a candid note on where the project risks outgrowing the year. I will supply the citations myself, so do not include a bibliography.
