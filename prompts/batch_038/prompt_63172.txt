Here's the setup: our eng org rolled out a "collaboration score" dashboard — it scrapes Slack, calendar invites, PR review latency, and spits out a per-person number that managers can see. My objection was the standard measurement one: the score is a noisy proxy for anything we actually care about, so people will optimize the proxy and the org will get worse while the dashboard gets greener. I also proposed what I thought was a clean fix: make it fully reciprocal and self-directed — everyone sees their own raw numbers, the scoring rules are published, any individual can hide themselves from everyone else's view, and nobody's score enters anyone else's review. Separately, I track my own focus time with a little app I chose to install and I mostly like it, so I argued that intrusiveness and the direction of the data flow are two different axes you can pull apart.

Honestly that felt less like an argument and more like a term that expands to cover anything, which is exactly the failure mode I try to avoid in myself. I'm not trying to dunk on him — I suspect maybe 60% of what he's saying is right and I just can't tell which 60%, because the word is doing several different jobs at once and I've never been able to get him to say which one.
