I run a tiny podcast on the philosophy of economics — maybe 900 listeners, mostly grad students and econ people who've had one course in decision theory — and next month's guest is a philosopher-economist who defends a broadly Keynesian account of probability: rational belief is often not pinned down by a single number, evidence has a weight as well as a bearing on the conclusion, and a lot of what economists do with priors and expected values is false precision dressed up as rigor. She's smart and charming and she has one tic I need help with: whenever you press her on the formalism she retreats to "all I'm saying is that real business decisions aren't lottery draws," and then moves on. I want a question sequence where that retreat gets progressively less comfortable to make.

So, play the role of someone who's actually spent time in both literatures — Keynes's early work on probability and the modern stuff on imprecise probability, sets of priors, and all that — and before you write anything, brainstorm out loud for a few bullets: where does the Keynes scholarship actually put pressure on a defender like her, and where is the pressure mostly rhetorical? Then give me nine to eleven questions I can read aloud. I want coverage of at least these: (1) the weight of an argument — is it just the size of the relevant evidence base, what do you say when new evidence raises the weight but leaves the probability alone, and does weight ever tell an agent what to do, or does she need a decision rule on top of it; (2) whether her view is anything more than imprecise Bayesianism in period costume, and what she does about updating and dilation once you allow sets of probabilities; (3) how she keeps some version of the principle of indifference from licensing arbitrary equiprobability splits, without the relevance judgments turning into priors in disguise; (4) probability as an objective logical relation between propositions known by intuition — what survives if you drop that epistemology, given the old Ramsey complaint about queer relations and a special faculty; (5) the 1930s material — the beauty contest, convention, animal spirits, the assumption that the existing state of affairs will continue absent specific reasons to expect change — and here I specifically want a question asking whether the beauty contest is really about uncertainty at all rather than about higher-order beliefs and strategic interdependence, becuase I suspect she slides between the two; (6) whether convention is evidence that rational calculation is impossible or evidence that it's being successfully outsourced; (7) which of the two readings of Keynesian uncertainty she actually holds — the one where there's no probability at all in these cases (the non-ergodic line, Davidson and company) versus the one where there is a probability but it isn't measurable or comparable (the Treatise line, O'Donnell) — and what turns on the difference for her; and (8) the policy payoff: does this generate distinctive macro conclusions, or would it also rule out the cost-benefit reasoning used to defend the intervention she wants. If you can fit in one question on the Moorean ethics side — organic unities, probability as a guide to conduct, whether weight smuggles in a duty to be careful — that'd be great too.

Format: each question short enough to say in one breath, then one line telling me what I'm hoping to get out of it, plus a follow-up for at least half of them keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. No generic warm-up stuff, and nothing that asks her to cite pages, list books, or quote passages — I'll cut those in editing anyway. One more thing I really care about: separate for me what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know this material. Whether the early probability work and the later macro writing are continuous, and which reading of uncertainty she should be pushed toward, are live disputes, so don't build a question on top of your favorite interpretation as though everyone agreed with it — mark the ones that lean on a contested reading, and if you're not sure about some factual detail in the setup just flag it rather than asserting it.
