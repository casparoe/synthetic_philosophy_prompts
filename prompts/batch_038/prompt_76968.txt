**Denial costs: hinges as standing rather than certainty**

**1. The interpretive gap.** *On Certainty* is usually read as identifying a special class of propositions — hinges — that are held fast without being known, and that form the scaffolding, or the river-bed, within which doubting and justifying become possible. The examples Wittgenstein presses on Moore are of a familiar kind: here is a hand, the earth existed long before my birth, I am called by this name. A standing problem is to say what these propositions have in common. Certainty will not do, since we are certain of many things that are not hinges. Immunity to falsification will not do either, since several of the examples look falsifiable in principle, and Wittgenstein himself insists that the river-bed can shift. Being presupposed by inquiry comes closest, but it makes hinges look like a technical category, and it sits badly with the fact that a child acquires them long before she has any inquiry to presuppose.

**2. The proposal.** I want to suggest that the unifying property is neither epistemic nor logical but social: a proposition is a hinge for a community just in case denying it costs the denier standing as a reasoner in that community. The costs have recognisable forms — reclassification as confused, as joking, as unwell, as unserious, as having slipped into a register in which one is no longer offering reasons at all. Note the direction of explanation I am proposing: the community does not withdraw standing because it has detected an epistemic defect in the denial. The withdrawal of standing is what constitutes the defect.

**3. What this would explain.** (i) *Felt certainty.* The certainty attaching to a hinge is, on this account, a registration of anticipated cost rather than an appraisal of evidence; this explains why it can run in inverse proportion to the evidence available, which is exactly the pattern that embarrasses evidentialist readings. (ii) *Revisability.* Hinges change, as the river-bed shifts, but they change through renegotiation of costs rather than by being outvoted by evidence. Hence the slowness, the generational turnover, and the characteristic way a hinge-change is accomplished by reclassifying the denier rather than by refuting him: the crank is later promoted to founder. (iii) *Intractability.* Where two communities have different hinges there is no shared tribunal, because the standing of any candidate tribunal would itself have to be hinge-like for both.

**4. Self-application.** If the account is right, then what looks and feels like a rational dispute over a hinge is in fact a negotiation over standing, and the participants' experience of it as rational is systematically misleading. But the account then applies to itself. Is *denial is costly* itself a hinge of my community? It cannot be: denying it costs me nothing at all. So the account is an ordinary claim, to be argued for on ordinary comparative grounds — which is what section 1 attempts, and what the rest of the essay would have to do. This is awkward but, I think, not fatal: it means only that the account cannot be validated by its own machinery.

**5. The relativism worry.** The obvious objection is that if hinge status is a matter of what a community punishes, the account cannot distinguish a community whose hinges track anything from one whose hinges are arbitrary, and so forfeits the critical grip that Wittgenstein's remarks seem to retain. My response is currently weak and I know it: I want to say that the account is descriptive, and that whatever critical grip survives must come from elsewhere — from the costs a community's own hinges impose on its own members. I am not confident this is enough, and section 5 is where I most expect to lose the reader.
