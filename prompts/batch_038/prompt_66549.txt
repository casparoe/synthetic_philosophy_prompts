I'm on the deployment-review panel at an AI safety org that audits frontier models with interpretability methods, and Friday's meeting is going to be a mess unless somebody writes something down. Here's the case.

Last year we did a full audit of a model — call it M0 — and the headline finding was a circuit we nicknamed the sycophancy-detector: when a user message contains a stated preference or a leading answer, a particular set of components fires (two attention heads plus a cluster of SAE latents in the mid layers), and patching those components measurably reduces the model's tendency to agree with the user. Our certification is tied to that circuit, because the anomaly detector we run in production downstream literally watches it.

The lab has since shipped M1, a fine-tuned descendant of M0 built up over something like 180 small steps. Their submission claims the mechanism is unchanged and asks us to renew certification without a re-audit. Thier evidence is three things: behavior on our held-out sycophancy probes is within noise of M0; a representational-similarity comparison of mid-layer activations on identical inputs correlates very highly with M0; and ablating the same components in M1 still knocks down sycophancy by roughly the same amount as before.

But our own staffer did a spot check and found: the weights of those two attention heads have moved more than about 90% of other components in the model; two of the three latents we tracked barely fire on the probes anymore and the third one now sits at a different layer index because the SAE was retrained on M1; and when you ablate the original components *together with* a couple of neighbors that were never part of the circuit, the effect gets smaller instead of bigger, which the staffer thinks means some of the work has moved onto a backup path. Also the production detector still fires at about the same rate on the same traffic — the lab treats that as confirmation — but nobody has checked whether it's firing for the same reasons, since it's a threshold classifier calibrated on M0's latents.

Panel is deadlocked. One camp says the mechanism is whatever does the work, the work is getting done, so a three-week re-audit is a waste of time we don't have. The other camp says the whole reason we do mechanistic audits instead of just running behavioral evals is that behavior can look identical while the implementation quietly reorganizes, so the lab's evidence is exactly the kind that can't settle the question. Someone made a Ship of Theseus joke and it did not help.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting. Renew, require the full re-audit, or renew conditionally — pick one and commit. I need an actual account of what "the same mechanism" means for our purposes, stated so a junior reviewer could apply it to a submission like this without phoning me. Then a specific verdict on each of the lab's three pieces of evidence and on each of the staffer's findings: which ones matter, which don't, and why. And then the strongest case for the position you're rejecting, written so that camp would recognize it as theirs, plus why it still loses. If your recommendation includes any tests, say who runs them, by when, and what result counts as pass versus fail — I don't want a list of further-investigation-needed as the whole answer. Something I can read in ten minutes.
