Write a short story of at most 800 words. A private pre-commitment broker offers members of the public one standing contract: £50,000, paid at midnight tonight, to anyone who at that moment genuinely intends to swallow a capsule tomorrow afternoon that will leave them fevered and retching for a day but with no lasting harm. Payment turns on the intention alone, verified by a scanner whose reliability nobody in the story has any reason to doubt; the capsule need never be swallowed, and nothing is clawed back if it isn't. The broker states this plainly in writing, and adds that in eleven years only one client has ever been paid. Your protagonist is the broker's own intake assessor, who has read that clause aloud to hundreds of applicants and is now taking the contract herself, for reasons the story should make concrete rather than merely mention. Let the drama lie in whether she can bring herself to intend — in the gaps between deciding, wanting, trying and intending, and in what a person can and cannot achieve by an act of will. Commit to a definite outcome: she either forms the intention or she does not, and she either swallows the capsule or she does not, with that outcome earned by the reasoning the story has staged rather than left open at the fade. No hypnosis, drug or other contrivance that dissolves the difficulty, and no naming of the puzzle or its author. Close with a note of at most 100 words stating which contested account of intention your ending assumes, and where you have taken liberties with it.
