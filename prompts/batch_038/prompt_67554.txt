I direct a hospital clinical ethics service and also run an advanced seminar on practical reason, and before I circulate a statement of my own view to the seminar I would like a consistency audit of it rather than a survey of the debate. I know the internal reasons thesis and the standard objections to it well enough; what I want is for someone to trace what my particular package commits me to, and where it breaks. Here is the package, in seven claims.

(1) Reasons internalism of the Williams sort: there is a reason for an agent A to φ only if A could arrive at the decision to φ by a sound deliberative route from A's actual subjective motivational set — A's existing desires, evaluations, attachments, and patterns of emotional response. I deny that there are external reasons.

(2) Sound deliberation, as I understand it, includes not only inference and the correction of false beliefs but what I call affective rehearsal: imagining some feature of the situation vividly enough that it engages the relevant emotions. An agent who has never been prompted to picture the inner life of a particular kind of person is, to that extent, unable to deliberate soundly about them, and I take this to be a matter of how they were raised rather than of what they willed.

(3) A reason is a consideration that counts in favor of an action, and I take this counting-in-favor relation to be primitive and irreducible: it is not to be analyzed in terms of anyone's desires, dispositions, or deliberative capacities. Reasons are the fundamental normative notion, and obligation and value are to be explained in terms of reasons rather than the other way round.

(4) Blame is appropriate toward A for φ-ing only if A had a reason not to φ and could in principle have come to see that they had it. Where the agent had no such reason, what I am doing in disapproving is not blaming but something closer to registering a difference between us.

(5) Nevertheless, when I sit across from someone who has plainly thought the matter through and remains entirely unmoved — the adult son who has reflected at length on his father's dying, reports that he feels nothing about it, and does not intend to visit — I take my disapproval to be tracking a genuine normative failure on his part and not merely recording my own tastes.

(6) Other people can know an agent's reasons better than the agent does, and the protest that one has reflected and remains unmoved is frequently no defense at all.

(7) Some of an agent's commitments are ground projects: non-optional, and among the things that generate the agent's reasons in the first place. Pressing such a person to justify the project can itself corrupt the standpoint from which they deliberate, and the demand that one step outside one's own character to assess it is not a demand everyone can coherently meet.

Two things I would like from you. First, work through this list and tell me which pairs of claims genuinely pull against each other, and how badly. I am not asking for a catalogue of every possible friction: rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be given up or qualified, what the cheapest available revision is, and what that revision costs me elsewhere — including in my clinical work, since several of these claims are load-bearing for me in cases involving capacity assessment, family conflict, and conscientious refusal. Second, tell me which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, and why they dissolve; I suspect that (1) and (6) look like a flat contradiction to many readers and are in fact perfectly compatible, and if I am wrong about that I would rather find out now than in the seminar. Then close by telling me which single revision to the package would repair the most damage overall, and what the package looks like afterwards — in particular whether it is still recognizably an internalist one, or whether the repair has quietly turned it into something else.

Please write to me as you would write an email: continuous prose, paragraph after paragraph, with no headings, no numbered or bulleted lists, no bold or italicized signposting, and no tables. You are welcome to refer to my claims by the numbers I have given them, but I do not want the reply laid out as a report or a form. Begin with a short paragraph stating your overall verdict plainly — whether the package hangs together as it stands, hangs together only after a specified repair, or does not hang together — and only then move into the detail. Do not re-explain the internal reasons thesis or its objections to me, and do not recommend anything to read.
