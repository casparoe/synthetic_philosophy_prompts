i moderate a large MMO guild and we've been in a weeks-long argument about whether any of this counts as real. one of our members got scammed out of a rare mount she'd sunk two years and a few hundred dollars into — it was resellable on third-party sites and everyone knew the going rate, tho cashing out is against the ToS. i banned the scammer, he appealed, and the head mod reversed it on the strengh of an analogy: game items are like Monopoly money. everyone tacitly agrees at the door what they're worth, and they're worth nothing once you leave the room, so nothing was taken from her, a house rule was just broken. another mod argued back with a different one: a game world is a fiction, so a virtual theft is like a theft in a novel — bad inside the story, no fact about it outside the story.

can you audit both? i'd like the correspondence spelled out for each: for the Monopoly one, what plays the banker, the board, the box everything goes back into, and the agreement to stop playing; for the fiction one, what plays the author, the reader, and the page. then where each mapping breaks, and — this is the part i actually care about — which of those breaks do real damage to the head mod's conclusion and which are just cosmetic. does either analogy quietly import a feature the target case doesn't have? and is there a better analogy for a two-year-old digital mount that can be seperately converted into cash by someone who needs it to pay rent? once all the disanalogies are on the table, does "no real harm, just a rule violation" still stand, or was the analogy carrying that conclusion the whole time?
