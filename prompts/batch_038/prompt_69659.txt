I am revising a session of my graduate seminar on moral responsibility, and I want to test Strawson's framework against an institutional case rather than the three it is always tested against — the psychopath, the addict, the deterministic cosmos. The case is the amnesty process of South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission.

So that we work from a common factual base rather than from your recall, here is the setup as I take it. The Commission was created by statute during the negotiated transition, chaired by Desmond Tutu, and operated through three committees; the Amnesty Committee is the one that matters here. Amnesty was neither blanket nor automatic. It was individualized and conditional: an applicant had to make full disclosure of the acts in question, show that they were associated with a political objective, and show proportionality between means and end. Applications came from the apartheid security apparatus and from the liberation movements alike. Of roughly seven thousand applications, fewer than nine hundred succeeded. Where disclosure was incomplete amnesty was refused — as it was, on grounds including inadequate disclosure, for the former security policemen implicated in Steve Biko's death in detention, whose widow testified against their applications. Where a victim's family embraced the outcome, as the parents of Amy Biehl did with respect to the four men convicted of her killing, amnesty was granted and the family's relationship with the applicants sometimes continued afterward, to the point of employing them. The amnesty provision itself withstood constitutional challenge, and Tutu framed the whole enterprise in the idiom of ubuntu and of restorative rather than retributive justice.

Four things from you, in this order.

First, the mapping. Identify the parties whose attitudes are in play — the victim and the victim's family, the applicant, the bystander community, and the state acting through the Committee — and say, for each, what Strawson's account says they faced and what it entitles them to feel and to do. Be explicit about which of Strawson's categories each party occupies: the personal reactive attitudes, their impersonal analogues, the self-reactive attitudes, exemption, justification, and the objective attitude. Do not let the categories do work they were never designed to do; where one simply does not fit a party, record the misfit.

Second, I want you to use a threefold distinction I am imposing on the material. Call a reactive attitude suspended when it persists with its original object and content but its characteristic expression is stayed, indefinitely or pending a condition; nothing has changed in what the victim feels, only in what she may do with it. Call it displaced when it persists but is redirected, either to a new object — from the individual perpetrator to the chain of command, the regime, the political order — or into a new register, from the personal resentment of a participant to the impersonal indignation of an official. Call it converted when its content is transformed, so that what remains is not the same attitude under restraint but a different one: grief, or the second-personal demand that the wrong be named. For each of the three sites where the Commission operated — the victim's resentment, the applicant's own remorse and shame, and the wider community's indignation — say which of these the amnesty process actually effected, and what in the record supports the assignment. Where the evidence does not decide between them, say so.

Third, the two cruxes. (a) The political-objective requirement: does the claim that one's acts served a political objective function, on Strawson's terms, as an exemption, as a justification, or as neither — and what is the significance of the Committee's treating it as a condition of admissibility rather than as a defence on the merits? (b) The disclosure requirement looks backwards from a Strawsonian starting point. If resentment is a response to the quality of will another displays toward me, then a full and credible account of that will should confirm the ground for resentment rather than dissolve it. Explain how the Commission could have supposed otherwise, and whether the political-objective finding supplies the missing step by redescribing the quality of will in question as something other than personal contempt.

Fourth, the objective attitude and its costs. Strawson holds that adopting the objective attitude toward a person is adopting a stance of social policy toward an object of treatment, training, or cure, and that it is available to us only at the price of the interpersonal relations it displaces; he also thinks wholesale abandonment of the participant stance practically inconceivable, because our commitment to it is too deep. Assess two challenges the case raises. Does the Commission institutionalize precisely that wholesale stance, and if so does the outcome — the Biehls employing two of the men convicted of killing their daughter — refute Strawson's claim about what the objective attitude costs, or show only that they were not taking it at all? And is what happened better described as a distributed stance, in which the state takes the objective attitude toward the perpetrator while the victim retains the participant attitude, and is that a stable configuration on Strawson's account or one that presupposes the objective attitude is essentially interpersonal?

Close by running the analysis back the other way: is this a confirmation of Strawson, an anomaly for him, or a case his framework never contemplated? Be specific about the places where he is silent — collective victims, structural wrongdoing, the state's appropriation of a standing that on his account belongs to the person wronged, and whether a third party can forgive at all.

Two constraints. Aim for two thousand to twenty-five hundred words. No bibliography, no reference lists, and no citations beyond naming the essay and the Commission: I want your analysis, not your scholarly apparatus, and I would rather you reasoned carefully than accurately recalled. Paraphrase Strawson throughout; I have the text in front of me. Flag any point at which you are unsure of the facts as I have stated them, and where the theory runs out, say plainly that it runs out.
