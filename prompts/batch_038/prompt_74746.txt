I run a tiny podcast out of our cog sci building — one long interview a month with whoever's paper a few of us have been quietly fighting about. Next month's guest is a philosopher who defends real-pattern realism: when Dennett says beliefs and desires are real patterns rather than things, and sorts explanation into the physical, design, and intentional stances, she doesn't read that as a polite concession to instrumentalism. On her view the patterns are genuinely out there in the world. But she's added a twist I can't get a handle on — she says the three stances aren't a ladder from less real to more real, they're three different explanatory *targets*, and that once you see it that way the question "are beliefs real?" stops looking like a deep question at all. She's really good at absorbing objections into that framing; my co-host and I have both tried and both lost. (Background on why I care: my partner builds recommender systems and says out loud, daily, that the model "wants" retention. After eight months of that I can no longer tell whether she's being sloppy or being literally correct, and I went and read up on real patterns to settle it and came away closer to her side than mine, which is not the result I was shopping for.)

Write me eight to ten interview questions, and for each one a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give — I want the second question to be the one that survives her first reply. I want pressure on at least these places: the compression criterion, i.e. that a pattern is real when its description is shorter than a bit map of the phenomenon, and specifically where the choice of vocabulary and units sneaks in, since whoever picks the description language seems to be picking the patterns; the proliferation worry, that lots of mutually incompatible and pretty gerrymandered regularities are compressible, so what singles out the belief-and-desire pattern as *the* pattern rather than one of indefinitely many; the reflexivity problem, where a system models itself being modeled and taking the intentional stance toward it changes what's there to be a pattern in — that's the recommender-system question, and it's the one my partner would actually have an opinion about; whether learned systems with no clean functional specification squeeze the design stance out of existence and leave her trichotomy looking like three levels of ignorance after all; and finally what the difference is, cashed out, between her realism and someone who just denies there are beliefs. Skip anything warm-up shaped. No "what drew you to this."

Also, be straight with me about where you're probably reading her uncharitably: flag any question that only bites if she's committed to something she may not be committed to, say which of the ten she'll swat most easily and why, and tell me what she could say in her first two minutes that should make me drop that question and ask something else instead.
