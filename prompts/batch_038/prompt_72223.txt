I direct institutional research at a public university, and my office fields roughly a dozen program evaluations a term — first-year seminars, tutoring, bridge programs, advising models — each comparing outcomes for students who participated against students who did not. Nearly all of them arrive with a headline number that reverses direction once a committee breaks it down by something: entering GPA band, prior credits, Pell status, transfer versus first-time enrollment. I have about an hour per evaluation, the committee is mostly non-statisticians, and the recommendation that comes out of this can close a program.

Write a decision procedure my analysts can run on any such comparison: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the point at which a reversal can be declared an artifact of unequal composition across the groups rather than evidence about the program, and the points where the procedure must stop and hand the decision to a person because the remaining question is about which quantity the decision ought to turn on. It has to cover the case where the variable the committee wants to condition on is itself produced by participation, and it should mark explicitly which steps are judgment calls dressed as procedure.

Then run it on three cases: (a) a first-year seminar whose participants graduate at higher rates overall but lower rates than non-participants within every GPA band — the one where I have already told the provost the seminar works; (b) a tutoring program whose entire aggregate advantage comes from a thirty-person transfer-student cell that disappears under every other partition; (c) an advising redesign the committee wants evaluated by splitting on whether students declared a major within a year, when accelerating declaration is one of the program's stated aims. Close by stating what no data my office can collect would settle. Under 900 words.
