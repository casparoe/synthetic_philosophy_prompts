I maintain the data layer for a multi-hospital mortality and organ-procurement registry, and I have no training in philosophy, so I am asking for a specification rather than an essay.

Every record we ingest has to collapse into one boolean and one timestamp, even though the contributing hospitals run different protocols and sit under different jurisdictions, and even though the underlying event is a process rather than an instant. Building that normalization layer has dropped me into a dispute I lack the vocabulary for. The uniform statute that most American states have adopted defines death in two prongs: irreversible cessation of circulatory and respiratory functions, or irreversible cessation of all functions of the entire brain including the brainstem. That formulation has stood since 1981, and a drafting committee convened in 2021 to revise it suspended its work indefinitely in 2023, reporting that the politics and the downstream consequences made revision infeasible. A definition that has not moved in four decades, plus a failed attempt to update it, suggests to me that the hard part is not drafting but knowing what a definition of death is supposed to accomplish. I want that stated before I read anything else.

Write the desiderata: the requirements any adequate definition, criterion, and test of death must satisfy, with the case or consideration behind each. My tentative list is below; consolidate the items that turn out to be one requirement in disguise, correct the ones that are misconceived, and add what I have missed.

(a) Univocity — a single concept must cover both prongs, so that circulatory death and death by neurological criteria are the same kind of event and not two events the law merely agrees to treat alike.
(b) Determinate time — a single instant must be identifiable, because inheritance, homicide liability, and transplant logistics all need one.
(c) Decidability at the bedside — the criterion must be applicable by a non-specialist with ordinary equipment in bounded time, using only information available at that moment. Irreversibility looks to me like a claim about every intervention that could ever be attempted, which is not a bedside measurement. If the standard tests are only a proxy for it, I want to know which concept the proxy is actually tracking.
(d) Insensitivity to resources and intentions — whether someone is dead should not turn on whether an extracorporeal circuit is primed in the next room. In controlled donation after circulatory death, circulation has been absent for only a few minutes when death is declared, and the team present could restore it but has resolved not to. On a strict irreversibility reading that patient looks alive; on a permanence reading, death depends on other people's plans. Neither is acceptable to me and I suspect one is unavoidable.
(e) Independence from downstream uses — the definition must not be shaped by what it licenses, and in particular must not be drawn so that organ recovery turns out not to kill the donor.
(f) Accounting for continued somatic integration — patients meeting whole-brain criteria have been physiologically maintained for years with wound healing, growth, and fluid and temperature regulation continuing, and there are reports of pregnant patients meeting those criteria supported for weeks until delivery. The definition must explain why none of this is the organism functioning as a whole.
(g) Fixing the boundary against neighbors — persistent vegetative state, locked-in syndrome, advanced dementia, and the fact that British practice rests on brainstem function while American practice rests on the whole brain. Should an adequate definition entail that one of these jurisdictions is simply wrong?

For each requirement, say whether it is a genuine demand on the concept or a preference of a particular camp. I also keep encountering the claim that death is a normative or legal construction rather than a biological fact, and I cannot tell whether that is a rival definition or a denial that the project has an answer; place it.

The part I most want: identify any subset of these requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, and state it the way you would state an impossibility result — hold this and that, and you must give up the other. Then tell me which single requirement eliminates the most candidate accounts, and whether an unmeetable list means the concept is defective or that my list is. Skip the survey of theories; if you indicate how two or three leading accounts fare, one line each is plenty. Please define technical terms on first use.
