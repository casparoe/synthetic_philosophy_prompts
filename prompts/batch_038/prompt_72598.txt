ok so our team does a little internal zine, mostly bad poetry and screenshots, and I want to contribute something that's actually a bit smart. I've gotten mildly obsessed with the phrase "history is written by the victors" — everybody says it like its a fact and nobody ever says what would make it false. I read a little about how historians separate "the past" (what actually happened) from "history" (the account of it), and about how an archive isn't a random sample of the past, it's a sample of whatever somebody bothered to keep. That's the material I want the piece to be made of.

Form: a parody git repo. A fake README, 10-14 commit messages with dates spanning about 150 years, a CODEOWNERS file, one issue thread, and a short git blame excerpt at the end. 650ish words. The repo is the official national history of an invented border war — make the countries up, I don't want anyone fact-checking real casualty numbers against our blog.

What it has to do, without explaining any of it in prose:
- the opening commits are the winning side squashing everything into one tidy "Initial narrative"
- about sixty years later somebody force-pushes and the losing side's wistful version becomes the default branch, so the "victors" part quietly stops being true and nobody comments on it
- a revert of a revert, plus a merge conflict where both branches agree on every date and disagree on every verb
- CODEOWNERS is three families and a monastery
- git blame attributes the single most quoted line to a copyist who was just retyping it
- the issue thread: a maintainer insists the events are the commits and the narrative is only the commit messages; someone replies that you can't check out the events, only the messages; then one person argues every branch is equally valid while another shows the casualty count is off by 10x and traces back to one press release. Nobody wins.

Deadpan throughout, no winking, and every git joke has to land as a point about how the past gets retold — some of the zine readers aren't engineers and I don't want this to just be "lol git is confusing."

Don't quote or cite anything real: no book titles, no named historians, no invented quotations from archives, I can't check any of it. After the piece, add a short note saying how confident you are that the points your satire is making are the ones people who study this actually argue about, and single out the one thing in that note you're least sure of. Also flag anywhere you stretched the git metaphor past where it holds.
