I've been going around in circles on suffering-focused ethics for like three years now — started with the usual LessWrong rabbit holes, ended up reading a bunch of the negative-leaning stuff — and last week I finally wrote out my actual verdicts on cases instead of my stated views, and they don't line up. Here they are, roughly in the order I thought of them.

1. One person tortured for a day versus a billion people each getting a dust speck in the eye: take the specks. No hesitation at all.
2. Same structure, ramped way up: one person tortured for a day versus a googolplex of people each getting a very faint itch for one second. At some point before that I flip and choose the torture... I think. I dont actually know where the flip is, which bothers me, because I want to say it's a real fact about the cases and not just me being bad at arithmetic.
3. Not creating a person whose life would be miserable-but-net-positive seems clearly better than creating them, maybe even required; but creating a person whose life would be great doesn't seem required at all. Each half feels rock solid on its own and slightly insane next to the other.
4. Relieving a dying person's last few days of agony matters more to me, per unit of pain, than preventing an equal amount of agony spread over a year in someone who fully recovers. I know that's probably a scope-neglect artifact but I'm not willing to give it up.
5. I'd trade a lot of minutes of moderate pain to spare someone one minute of genuinely extreme pain, but not literally any number of minutes — there's some finite amount I'd accept, and it feels categorically different from case 2, not just quantitatively different.
6. Wild animal suffering: I think it's genuinely terrible and genuinely underweighted, but I don't feel the pull of "painless-kill everything" or "sterilize the biosphere" even a little, which is confusing because the arithmetic looks like it should at least be tempting.

My tentative principle, which I made up at 2am and don't trust: something like "count each person's worst moment, not their total pain-minutes" — a peak rule. That gets (4) and half of (5) for free, and it makes the itch-world in (2) score near zero, which explains why I flip. But it also says an hour of torture is exactly as bad as a day of the same torture, which I definitely don't believe, so it's too crude.

What I want is the principle — or the small set of candidate principles — that actually generates this list, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a new case without waffling. Which of the six does it save, which one does it force me to throw out, and is there a formulation that keeps the (2)/(5) distinction I care about without collapsing into peak-only scoring? Then tell me what it says about cases I haven't listed. Two off the top of my head: a machine that reduces every instance of intense suffering everywhere by 10% but gives a new mild permanent ache to everyone who would otherwise have been pain-free; and a choice between a world of ten billion people with modest, ordinary suffering and a world of ten trillion people whose lives are wonderful except that each contains one hour of real agony. And be honest with me about whether the whole set is just intransitive and I should burn the list down and start over from one axiom.
