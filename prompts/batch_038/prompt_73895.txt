I'm on the exec board of a student housing coalition in a mid-size college town and we're locking in the planks for a resolution we'll read at the city council's housing element hearing next month. Everything we put out — the flyer, the instagram, the yard signs somebody's roommate screen-printed — carries the slogan "Housing is a human right, not a commodity." The problem is that the three planks underneath it are pulling in what look like different directions, and one of our members (philosophy TA, will not let this go) claims the slogan can't support all three at once. Plank A is a $40M general-obligation bond to build permanently affordable, publicly owned units on the two vacant parcels by the bus depot. Plank B is legalizing fourplexes and accessory units in every residential zone citywide, by right, no discretionary review. Plank C is a moratorium on new market-rate construction in the three cencus tracts bordering campus until displacement protections pass. His argument, roughly: A treats housing as something the public owes people directly, B treats it as something the market will supply fine if we stop blocking it, and C treats the market as actively harmful — so if the same banner justifies all three, either the slogan is ambiguous or one of the planks is just riding along on vibes.

For context on where people in the group think it comes from: one organizer said the "right" half is backed by the UN covenants' adequate-housing language, and another said the point of the slogan isn't about who builds anything at all, it's about commodification, and that Lefebvre's right-to-the-city stuff is the real ancestor of the second half. I'm not sure those two are even talking about the same sentence.

So: can you lay out the different precise claims that one line could be making, ordered from the near-platitude to the genuinely radical — I'd want at least six, and please include at least one reading that's flat-out false as stated and at least one that nobody in our group has articulated but that a hostile councilmember could accuse us of meaning. For each I want the deontic structure pinned down: who owes what, to whom, enforced against whom, at whose expense, and what exactly is being denied when you call housing a commodity (is it tradability? profit? price signals? absentee ownership? land value?). And I especially want to know whether "human right" and "not a commodity" are one claim or two that can come apart, since I suspect plank B is sitting right in the gap.

Give me the menu as a markdown table with exactly these columns, and keep each cell to a sentence or two so it stays readable: **reading** (a 3–6 word label), **claim_spelled_out** (the precise assertion, including the obligation structure above), **strength** (one of: platitude / substantive-and-defensible / contentious / false-as-stated), **halves_relation** (under this reading, are the two halves the same claim, two compatible claims, or in tension), **supports_A**, **supports_B**, **supports_C** (each: yes / no / only-with-caveats, plus half a sentence of why), **who_means_it** (which camp of slogan-repeater most likely has this one in mind). After the table, give me a short paragraph answering the only question that actually matters for the hearing: is there a single reading that licenses all three planks coherently, and if not, which plank should we drop and which defintely should stay.
