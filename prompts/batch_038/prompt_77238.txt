I am preparing an application for a twelve-month postdoctoral fellowship at the intersection of philosophy of science and New Testament studies, and I need a research proposal of roughly 1,500 words. The subject is the criteria of authenticity in the quest for the historical Jesus: dissimilarity in its single and double forms, embarrassment, multiple attestation across sources and forms, coherence, Semitic linguistic influence, environmental fit with first-century Palestine, and the tendency of the developing tradition.

The proposal should develop, rather than merely assert, the following working hypothesis. The case against the criteria runs together two very different objections, and prising them apart changes the picture. The first is a calibration objection: each criterion is a detection procedure whose error rates are unknown and plausibly asymmetric, so that dissimilarity filters out precisely the material that made Jesus intelligible to his contemporaries and to his first followers, while embarrassment can register only what embarrassed the later community, never what embarrassed Jesus himself. This objection looks repairable in principle, by estimating error rates and by aggregating the criteria probabilistically instead of applying them one at a time and informally totting up the results. The second is a target objection, associated with the turn to social and cultural memory: there may be no well-defined item — a saying as Jesus actually uttered it — for these criteria to be criteria of, in which case better calibration buys nothing.

Please state the research question precisely enough that it could fail. I want it to be: for each criterion, is its breakdown a failure of calibration or a failure of the target; and what becomes of the cumulative case once the criteria are treated as tests whose errors are correlated, because all of them are applied to the output of a small number of communities subject to similar selection pressures? Since the data required to estimate those error rates largely does not exist, the proposal should be explicit that the deliverable is conditional — a taxonomy of failure modes, together with a statement of the parameter ranges in which multiple-attestation-style aggregation gains anything and the ranges in which it cannot.

The proposal should also include: why the existing positions, rehabilitation by substitute criteria on one side and wholesale abandonment on the other, leave the question open; the method, month by month, marking where conceptual work ends and any formal modelling begins; a defence of this approach against the obvious alternatives, namely proposing yet another criterion, a genealogical critique of the enterprise, or an accumulation of case studies; what a successful result would look like to a historian who is not a philosopher; and what would count as failure, including the risk that the formal apparatus amounts to pseudo-precision smuggling in assumptions nobody can check. End with a candid assessment of scope: what I should cut if the year proves shorter than the project. Do not supply a bibliography or a literature review by citation — I will handle references myself — though you may name positions, arguments, and the standard criteria as I have done above.
