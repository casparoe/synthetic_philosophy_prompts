I am three-quarters through a novel whose central set piece is a negotiation, and the wall I have struck is not dramatic but conceptual. The scene: a reservoir is to be built; a public authority whose only metric is aggregate welfare across living and future persons must reach terms with a community that holds it an unconditional duty, owed to named ancestors, never to move their remains, and treats that duty as not tradeable for anything. Neither party is a zealot in my book and neither is stupid, and I have no intention of resolving the scene into the usual fiction of discovered common humanity. Before I write the ending, I want to know what I am entitled to demand of an account of compromise between genuinely different value systems. So: write the specification.

Set out the desiderata — what any theory of rational compromise between parties holding distinct and non-commensurable value systems must deliver in order to count as adequate — and behind each one, the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a matter of taste. At minimum I want the list to address:

- the cases it must get right, including: a good that cannot be divided or partially realized; a party whose value system contains an absolute prohibition on compromising as such, so that any terms it accepts it is forbidden to accept; a compromise outcome that both parties rank below either of the two positions they started from; and the case where the parties' orderings are simply opposed, with no differential weighting to trade on, so that nothing resembling a gain from trade exists at all.
- the distinctions it may not collapse: compromise versus capitulation, versus a mere truce or accommodation no one experiences as loss, versus the discovery that the parties were disputing different questions, versus a change of heart; and terms each party can endorse by its own lights versus terms that are optimal by some external aggregative standard. I also want to know what the theory must say about residue — whether the party that traded a duty away retains any standing obligation toward it, and whether a compromise can corrupt the agent who makes it.
- the formal results it must respect or explain away: that the standard axiomatic bargaining solutions presuppose cardinal utilities comparable across persons, and the Arrow-style impossibility results about combining distinct orderings into one. Tell me plainly whether gains from trade can be had without a common scale of value, and if so, what is doing the work the scale was supposed to do.
- reflexivity. Because the choice of a procedure for compromising is itself a contested normative matter between parties with different value systems, an adequate account must not presuppose the shared standard whose absence generated the problem. If that cannot be managed, I want it stated as a result rather than concealed by a change of subject.
- strategic corruption of the theory. If the terms a party receives improve with the intensity or intransigence of its avowed values, the account must say whether it survives the parties' knowing this about it, and whether it thereby rewards cultivated extremism.
- persistence: whether a compromise can bind a party whose values are predictably going to change, and who will later judge the bargain by standards she does not yet hold.

Then mark which of these requirements are non-negotiable for any account and which are the commitments of one camp in particular; tell me which single requirement eliminates the largest number of otherwise attractive accounts; and if some subset of the requirements turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, say so outright and say which must be surrendered. That is what I most need, because my ending depends on whether an honest compromise is available in this case or whether the scene is in truth about two parties discovering that nothing of the kind exists.

Briefly at the end, and only briefly, score the obvious contenders against the list: a Pareto-style account that asks only for improvement by each party's own ranking, an account that first installs a common currency of value and then divides, and an account on which compromise is not about value at all but about living alongside others on terms no one endorses. Two or three sentences each. I am not asking for sources, citations, or a reading list — only for the requirements, and the argument for each.
