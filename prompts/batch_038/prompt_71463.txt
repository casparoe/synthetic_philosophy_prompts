I work on a data platform, mostly entity resolution and identity plumbing, and I've been reading around modal metaphysics on the side to see if any of it applies to what I do all day. No philosophy background, so be blunt. I wrote down what I actually believe and I want to know whether the package hangs together.

1. I'm a realist about possible worlds in roughly Lewis's sense: they're concrete and completely cut off from each other. Nothing over here causes anything over there, and there's no global state tying them together. I like this because it feels clean — no spooky action at a distance.

2. Accidental intrinsics is what sold me on counterpart theory in the first place. A bent thing could have been straight, and if the very same object has to be in each of those worlds I don't see how that works, since being bent and being straight are both intrinsic.

3. But the Humphrey complaint gets me too, honestly. When I say this record could have had a different email address, I want that to be about *that record*. Some lookalike row in another world having a different email doesn't help me any more than some other guy winning helps Humphrey.

4. Identity is never vague and never comes in degrees. Either it's the same thing or it isn't. I think there's a good argument for this and I'd hate to drop it.

5. At the same time, whether "the same service" survived a two-year strangler-fig rewrite where we swapped out every module one at a time — I genuinely think that one has no determinate answer, and that it depends what you're auditing for. I've said exactly that in design docs, and nobody pushed back.

6. Here's my own idea, and I haven't seen anyone propose it so I may be reinventing something: make the counterpart relation a *lineage* relation instead of a similarity relation. Causal ancestry, like git ancestry, or the lineage_id column we keep on rows. Objects in two worlds are counterparts when one descends from the other through a chain of the right kind of events, and how much they resemble each other is beside the point. On forks: after a fork both children are counterparts of the parent, I'm fine with that, both are legitimately descended. I think this answers (3) without giving up (1), and it also delivers the origin intuitions I have — the pipeline that first wrote a row is essential to it, it couldn't of come from a different importer.

7. I don't think "could have" is context-sensitive the way "tall" is. There's a fact of the matter about what this row could have become and it doesnt shift depending on who's asking.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I'm fairly sure they aren't all in trouble and I don't want an answer that flags everything. Rank the real conflicts by severity, say what would have to give in each case and what the cheapest fix costs me, and then tell me which single change would clean up the most. And after the cleanup, what should I say about the fork case and about the rewrite? Bonus question: is my lineage_id column tracking a counterpart relation at all, or something else entirely?
