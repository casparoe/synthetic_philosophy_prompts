I'm a phil-sci PhD student stuck on the opening of a chapter about pseudohistory. Instead of Atlantis or Fomenko, where everyone already has a take, I invented a case so we can argue about it cleanly:

**The Cassiteride Project.** Four researchers argue that the Bronze Age Atlantic tin trade was run by a single seafaring cartel that kept its accounts on bark and hide (none survives), and that its fingerprints are matching weight standards at sites from Iberia to Cornwall, a suspiciously uniform pottery style, and some odd lead-isotope clusters. Details that matter: their statistics are checkable and so far they check out. In 2019 they predicted a specific isotope signature in ingots from a wreck off Galicia, and a lab that had never heard of them measured it and it matched. When the pottery uniformity turned out to be a sampling artifact, they swapped in a revised dispersal model plus an auxiliary about looted hoards, neither independently evidenced. Two of the four have archaeology PhDs, they answer every criticism point by point in their own journal with a real editorial board, and there's a society and an annual conference. Most of the money comes from a regional heritage foundation that likes the story, and their popular books outsell everything else in the field. Meanwhile mainstream archaeology has no explanation for the weight standards.

Now the ask: rate each of these five demarcation accounts from 0 to 10, whole numbers, on how well it lets me call the Project pseudohistorical for defensible reasons rather than sociological ones — Popper's falsifiability, Lakatos on progressive vs. degenerating research programs, Thagard's cluster of criteria for pseudoscience, Longino's norms for critical discourse, and Laudan's take that demarcation is a pseudo-problem. One sentence per score saying what verdict the account actually delivers here and whether the reason its giving is any good. Then a strict ranking of the five, and tell me which single feature of the case does most of the work — the one that would move the most scores if I deleted it. American English, please.
