I retired three years ago from a sensory physiology lab where I'd spent most of my career on weakly electric fish — the electrosensory side, both the wave-type South Americans and the pulse-type Africans. Day to day that meant corollary discharge: a copy of the electromotor command coming into the electrosensory lobe and cancelling the reafference from the animal's own organ, so it can see the world instead of drowning in its own noise, plus all the plasticity that makes the cancellation and its negative images work. Since retiring I've fallen into philosophy of mind, which my wife regards as a symptom of under-stimulation, and I've just read Chalmers's 2018 paper on the meta-problem of consciousness. Twice. I think I follow the shape of it: we make these reports and judgements about experience — that there's something it's like, that it's a mystery how neurons could produce it — and those reports are themselves things that need explaining; and there's an argument about whether a full causal story of why we say such things would shrink or dissolve the hard problem, or leave it sitting exactly where it was. But the vocabulary I actually think in is efference copies, reafference versus exafference, forward models, cancellation, pacemakers and command nuclei, and the jamming avoidance response. Assume I'm expert in that and a beginner in yours — gloss any philosophy term in a clause and move on.

So here's what I want: restate the meta-problem in my vocabulary, not as a history of who said what but claim by claim, as faithfully as you can manage. Say six claims: (1) that there are reports and judgements to be explained at all, and that they're the real data; (2) that those reports are produced by underlying machinery whether or not that machinery has anything inner going on; (3) the idea that the system's model of its own processing is impoverished in some specific way, and that this is what generates talk of raw feels that physics can't reach; (4) the hope that explaining the reports gets you most of the way to explaining the experience; (5) the illusionist's stronger move, that the reports are flat-out wrong about what they seem to be about; and (6) if you want to take a swing at it, why the meta-problem is supposed to be hard in its own right. For each one, give me the translation and then a blunt label — exact, strained, or impossible — and if I've mangled any of the six in stating them the way I just did, correct me before you translate it rather than quietly repairing it.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. What doesn't survive? Which claims only translate if you let my framework's assumptions do the work, and which have no expression in it at all — and for each of those, tell me whether the fault is with my vocabulary (it's missing something, the way a rig with no reference channel can't answer a question about phase) or with the original claim (it looked like it said something and didn't). Related to that, I have a hunch I want you to attack rather than flatter. In the jamming avoidance response the fish computes its frequency shift from the two signals it recieves at the skin, with no internal reference to its own pacemaker — the curare experiments make that pretty clear — so there you have a system producing a precise behavioural correction with, as far as anyone can tell, no access to the command it's correcting around. Is that a legitimate analogue for the meta-problem's claim that we lack access to the processes generating our reports, or am I sliding between two very different things: a fish that isn't sent an efference copy, and a person who can't introspect a computation? Can your framework even hold that distinction — is there room in it for the difference between a model being wrong and a model being merely coarse, or between a report being caused and a report being about something? One warning: don't translate "what it's like" into "the reafferent consequences of one's own activity," declare the job done, and hide the body — if that substitution is where the strain is, put it in the residue column and say so. No reading list at the end, please, I've got the papers. What I want to know is whether my instinct that the meta-problem is basically a corollary-discharge problem with extra steps is roughly right, or whether it's just what happens when a physiologist is handed a hammer.
