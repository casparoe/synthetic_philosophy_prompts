I'm the council officer who set up our town's first citizens' assembly — 40 people drawn at random from the electoral register, plus a handful of 16- and 17-year-olds and two residents who aren't on the register at all, because we argued for including them. On Thursday I give the eight-minute opening remarks before they start work on the actual question, which is what to do with a one-off £9m windfall from a land sale.

The room will have: the 40 members, most of whom have never sat on anything in their lives; three councillors who voted against the assembly and think 40 unelected people have no business speaking for 90,000; two politics lecturers who are observing and will be disappointed by anything vague; and a reporter from the local paper.

Two members have already said things to me at the pre-meetings that I can't duck on the night. One said "nobody voted for us, so don't let anyone tell us we speak for the town." The other said "we *are* the people, so whatever we come up with is the will of the people and the council has to do it." I think they're both half right and I need a speech that manages to say that without sounding like I'm just splitting the difference down the middle.

The bit I actually need help with: I want to be honest that there might not be any single thing called the will of the people — put the same options to a group in a different order, or ask them to rank instead of choose, and you get a different winner, and I know thats not a quirk of this particular group. But I have to say that to non-specialists without it landing as "so whatever you conclude won't mean much," and it has to leave them with a reason to give up four weekends. The assembly's output is only advisory, and I'm not allowed to promise otherwise.

Can you write the speech? Roughly eight minutes, speakable — real sentences someone says out loud, not bullet points — no named philosophers and no term that needs a definition I haven't got time to give. Then as the very last line, outside the speech, give me one sentence saying what this speech is claiming "the will of the people" actually turns out to be, because a councillor will corner me afterwards and I need an answer ready.
