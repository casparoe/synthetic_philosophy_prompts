Compose a satirical document in the genre of a corporate regulatory filing: a Welfare Impact Statement submitted by a fictional agrochemical company in support of a new insecticide, under a fictional assurance scheme that now requires such statements. Approximately 900 words, formatted with numbered sections, one boxed sidebar, a short summary table, and a signature and independent-assurance block.

Adopt and develop the following setting. The applicant is Alder & Voss Agroscience. The product is LULLABY 4G, a granular chitin-synthesis inhibitor blended with a fast-acting knockdown agent, so that treated insects are rendered immobile before they reach the moult at which the growth regulator kills them; the marketing claim is that the insect is asleep for the difficult part. The scheme is the Invertebrate Welfare Assurance Standard (IWB-2), administered by a fictional council, which requires applicants to quantify in Suffering-Avoided Insect-Days the welfare effect of a pest-control product against a declared counterfactual baseline, and to state whether that effect is net positive.

The satire must run from inside the methodology. The filers are sincere, numerate, and scrupulous: they declare their assumptions, they run sensitivity analyses, they do not hide unflattering line items, and every figure in the summary table sums correctly to the headline result. Nothing in the prose may be funny on its own. The humour is to come entirely from the arithmetic and from what the definitions commit the company to.

The filing must work through, without ever commenting on, at least the following:

1. Baseline selection. The counterfactual is one in which the same insects die anyway — of predation, parasitism, desiccation, or starvation — but later and less predictably, so that killing them is booked as an avoided-suffering gain.
2. System boundary. Because growth regulators are selective, the natural enemies survive; the spared parasitoids and predators therefore continue to consume the target population slowly and over a long period. The statement handles this with a boundary drawn at the treated field, and the resulting disclosure should be large enough to be embarrassing.
3. Composition of the benefit. Most of the claimed gain falls on eggs and early instars, whose capacity for experience is the least well evidenced, and who are nonetheless assigned the same welfare weight as adults.
4. The measurement proxy. Video-tracked quiescence is used as the indicator of insensibility, with a sensitivity analysis showing that the sign of the headline result turns on a parameter the company candidly states it cannot estimate.
5. Precaution. The standard's precautionary clause is invoked against the do-nothing option, on the grounds that it is the alternative with the highest expected suffering.
6. Additionality. Some of these deaths would also have occurred in the drought year, and the company must avoid double-counting them against credits already claimed by an irrigation supplier.
7. The closing move. A sector-wide science-based target expressed as a percentage reduction in insect biomass; an offer to license the methodology to competitors so that the gain can be booked collectively; and an application to have the product classified as an animal-welfare intervention rather than a pesticide.

At least one of these must be handled correctly by the company's own lights — an objection the reader wants to raise, anticipated, answered within the framework, and still standing afterward. Include in the boxed sidebar a dissenting note from the company's own ecologist, two or three sentences and technically specific, together with the filer's procedural response to it.

Constraints: deadpan throughout, with no winking, no stage directions, no editorial asides, and no exclamation marks. A reader should be able to tell by the third section that something has gone badly wrong and should never be informed that it has. Use no real companies, products, standards, regulators, or people, and include no citations, references, footnotes, or named theories or theorists — the ethical distinctions are to be embodied in the definitions and the sums rather than labelled. Do not distort the entomology to make a joke: the modes of action sketched above are real and should be described accurately, since the piece depends on the account being technically correct. An entomologist and a moral philosopher reading it should each conclude that the author understood what was being satirised and thought the satire fair.
