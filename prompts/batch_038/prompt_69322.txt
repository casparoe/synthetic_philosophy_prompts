ok so I'm doing an independent study thing at school on consciousness and my teacher wants me to hand in a list of what I actually believe before we meet, so he can attack it. I'd rather get attacked by you first. I'm a panpsychist, or I think I am — here's the list, please tell me which parts are fighting each other:

1. Consciousness is fundamental. Physics tells you what stuff *does*, all structure and relations and dispositions, but something has to be the intrinsic nature that's doing it, and the one case where we know what an intrinsic nature is like from the inside is experience. So experience is basic, and it goes all the way down — an electron has some tiny amount of it, not zero.
2. The way you get from electron-sized experience to me is a fundamental bonding relation, the Goff move: some micro-experiences stand in it and get bound into one bigger subject, most of them don't.
3. So no, I don't think a rock is conscious, or a pile of sand, or my desk. A heap isn't a subject. I'm totally fine saying every part of the rock has experience while the rock itself has none.
4. Physics is causally closed and complete at the micro level. Nothing mental adds a push. I don't believe in spooky extra forces.
5. But my choices are actually mine and actually make stuff happen — I decided to write this list, and that's why it exists, and that's the whole basis for holding anyone responsible for anything. I'm not an epiphenomenalist about myself.
6. Behavior tells you nothing about whether there's experience in there. A chatbot could act exactly like it feels things and feel nothing, and there's no outside test that settles it either way.
7. If micro-panpsychism turns out to be unworkable I'll just go cosmopsychism instead — the whole universe is the one subject and people are like dissociated fragments of it. Either way I'm still a panpsychist, so I feel like I can't lose.
8. I admit none of this is testable, it makes no new predictions, and I still think it's the best explanation going.

So: which of these actually can't all be true? Rank them by how bad they are rather than listing everything as equally broken, and for each one tell me what I'd have to give up and what the cheapest repair is and what that repair costs me. Then tell me the single change that fixes the most at once. And be honest about which ones I'm stressing over for no reason — I suspect 3 sounds self-contradictory to people but isn't, and same with 8, but maybe I'm wrong about that. Please don't just say 'drop panpsychism,' that's not a repair, that's a different study.
