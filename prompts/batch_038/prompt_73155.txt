I am a novelist, and the book I am working on turns on a decision its protagonist cannot evaluate in advance: whether to have a child. Before I build three hundred pages on the idea, I want to be certain I have not borrowed it from a journalist who misstated it. A long essay in a weekend magazine introduced me to L. A. Paul's notion of transformative experience, and rendered her argument as follows:

"Some decisions, Paul argues, are transformative: they change you so completely that the person who makes the choice is not the person who lives with its consequences. Her example is deciding whether to have a child. Because you cannot know what being a parent will be like until you are one, and because the experience will rewrite your deepest preferences, there is no fact of the matter about how good either option would be for you — the two outcomes cannot be weighed against each other at all. It follows that this is not a rational decision. It is a leap, and the most you can do is admit that you are leaping. This is also why the testimony of parents is no help here: your friends who have children cannot tell you what it is like, any more than a description can tell you what it is like to be a bat. Paul's own answer is that we should treat such decisions as opportunities for discovery: we choose in order to find out who we will become."

I want an audit, not a lecture. Sentence by sentence: what would Paul accept, what is the sort of simplification a specialist would let pass in a magazine, and what is simply wrong? In particular, tell me whether the last sentence is consistent with the third — whether a choice made for the sake of discovery still counts as rational on the corrected account, or whether the essay has quietly swapped one thesis for another. And once the errors are stripped out, is there a version of the argument that licenses what my protagonist tells her sister — that she is choosing in order to meet the woman she will become — or does the theory, properly stated, deny her even that self-understanding?
