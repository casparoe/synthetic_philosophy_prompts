Built an argument for my reading group about whether introspection is reliable, and it backfired — it seems to prove far more then I wanted.

(1) If a faculty gives systematically divergent verdicts across subjects who are in the same evidential position with respect to what it tracks, and there's a determinate fact about whether the tracked condition obtains, then the faculty is unreliable.
(2) Introspective reports diverge systematically like that: whether your anger has really gone, how much detail there is in the unattended periphery of your visual field, whether the afterimage is still there.
(3) In each of those cases there is a determinate fact about whether the state obtains.
(4) So introspection is unreliable.
(5) But run the same test on color vision — unique-hue boundaries, that dress photo, borderline shades — and on testimony across communities, and they come out divergent too.
(6) So either (1) condemns color vision and testimony along with introspection, or it condemns none of them.
(7) Color vision and testimony aren't unreliable.
(8) Therefore (1) is false, and (4) goes down with it.

I actually want to keep (4): I think the divergence data is real and I don't think it vindicates introspection. So which step goes, and what do I pay for it? And relatedly, is there a principled difference between subjects diverging on where unique green starts and subjects diverging on whether they're currently annoyed — or is (2) just a worse analogue of (5) then I thought?
