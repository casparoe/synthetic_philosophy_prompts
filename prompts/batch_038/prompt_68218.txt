I'm drafting an internal standard for our evaluations group, and I want the underlying theory pinned down before I write a word of policy. The standard has to say when a report is permitted to state that a deployed model believes something. These reports go to leadership and occasionally to a regulator, and both failure modes are live: overclaiming inner states nobody has demonstrated, and hedging everything into "the model produces text consistent with p" until the report carries no information. I've read far too many arguments that slide from "the intentional stance predicts this system's behavior" straight to "therefore the system has beliefs," and I want to know exactly what work is being done in that slide.

So: write the desiderata any account of belief attribution to AI systems must meet to be adequate for this purpose — the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse (ascription-for-prediction versus ascription-for-accountability; the content of a state versus the format in which it is retrievable; degree versus kind), and the empirical facts about such systems it must stay consistent with. Give the case or consideration behind each requirement, and mark explicitly which are non-negotiable and which are one camp's commitments smuggled in as neutral criteria.

The list must handle at least these: a model whose disposition to assert p flips under paraphrase and across output language while passing every consistency probe administered in the single format we happened to test; a model that acts to preserve access to a resource over a long-horizon task while disclaiming any goal in self-report; and the background fact that self-report here is a channel we know can be reconstructed to order.

Say which single desideratum eliminates the most contenders, and flag any pair you believe cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, whether a practice robust enough to license prediction can also license holding a system to a standard. Close with a brief scorecard of instrumentalist, realist, and interpretivist treatments against the list. No citations; I know where to look.
