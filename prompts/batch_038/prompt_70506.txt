Here's my opening. I built you a robot. It has a register R running from 0 to 99, and it pumps water at a rate that tracks R continuously. Two disjoint ranges of R -- call them low and high -- get it pumping by two mechanically unrelated routes: different wiring, no shared intermediate structure, nothing that makes one the privileged route, and I've scrubbed any fact about what the designers intended. Your favorite macro variable, WANT, is the union of those two ranges. Now run your own test on it. Any intervention that sets WANT also pins down a particular value of R, so your interventions are fat-handed all the way down. The two routes aren't interchangeable in their other effects -- power draw, timing, how the thing fails. And the rate of pumping tracks R continuously, not anything about WANT. So WANT is never required for any effect, and no coarse-grained variable here is invariant across its realizations. Tell me which variable is actually doing the causal work in this robot, and why your account doesn't quietly collapse into the exclusionist's as soon as realization gets heterogeneous.
