I work on resource-allocation tooling — multi-team budgeting and internal governance systems — and on my own time I have been reading around bargaining theory and the literature on compromise between conflicting value systems, trying to work out what principles such a system should encode. I have no formal training in philosophy, so before I commit to a design I would like an audit of the position I have assembled. Here it is:

1. When agents with conflicting values must decide jointly, compromise is rational because there are gains from trade: usually some outcome both prefer to walking away.
2. Interpersonal utility comparisons are meaningless. There is no fact of the matter about whether my loss of five units outweighs your gain of three.
3. The right model of a fair compromise is the Nash bargaining solution: maximize the product of each party's gain over the disagreement point, with symmetric treatment of the parties and no side deals.
4. For my own decisions under moral uncertainty, I maximize expected choice-worthiness across the moral theories I find plausible, weighted by my credence in each — which is why part of my giving is on broadly consequentialist grounds and part is set aside against constraints I am not sure about.
5. Some considerations are lexical for me: there are constraints I would not trade away at any finite price, and I do not think that is irrational, since I do not think those considerations come in degrees.
6. Case verdict: when two of my teams split a contested budget and one plainly cares far more about the outcome than the other, I think the split should tilt toward the one that cares more, and I am comfortable eliciting how much each cares with numbers.

Tell me which of these pull against each other and which only look as though they do. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad; for each, say what would have to be given up or restated and what that costs; and point out explicitly anywhere I have helped myself to something my own stated principles forbid. Do not assume I already know which commitment I would drop.
