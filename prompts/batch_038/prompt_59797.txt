- The Imperial Household Law has no abdication provision. The emperor reigns until death; the last actual abdication in Japan was in 1817.
- July 2016, NHK reports he wants to step down in a few years; the Imperial Household Agency pushes back hard and calls the report unrealistic.
- 8 August 2016 he goes on television with a short address about his age, his health, and the difficulty of carrying out his duties. He never says the word abdicate, adn he never asks for anything, because the constitution bars him from politics — article 4 says he "shall not have powers related to government," and article 3 requires cabinet advice and approval for all his acts in matters of state.
- Abe's government convenes panels, and in June 2017 the Diet passes a one-off law for him personally — a special exception to the Household Law, the title Emperor Emeritus, the date to be set later by cabinet order. One small party abstains on the grounds that this should have been a general amendment. The Diet attaches a non-binding resolution about the shrinking imperial family and the succession rules.
- He abdicates 30 April 2019; Naruhito accedes the next day.
