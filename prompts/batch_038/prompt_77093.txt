I run a small electrical contracting outfit and I've been dragged into the same argument maybe a dozen times now: what actually counts as corruption. My brother-in-law says a gift is a gift and the anti-corruption crowd invented a rule that doesn't fit how anywhere in the world really works. My wife says if money moves anywhere near an official, it's dirty. I want to build a little decision tree I can hand people — "which view of corruption are you actually holding?" — built out of cases, not vocabulary.

Give me 8 to 10 questions, each a concrete situation with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions. Leaves I want at minimum: the misuse-of-public-office view, the view where the wrong is that somebody got cheated or lost a fair shot, the "it's only corruption where the local rules say so" view, and the view that gift exchange is legitimate social glue and the whole bribery category is a Western export. Add more if the space wants them.

Make some of the cases genuinely hard and pulling in different directions — like my brother-in-law happening to be the low bid on a city job the mayor knows about, or a company hiring a regulator's kid where the regulator himself pockets nothing, or a mayor steering a school contract to the firm that finishes a year early and under budget. And include something legal-but-reeks, like campaign donations from the exact people a council votes on.

Nobody should be able to answer a question by recognizing a label — no "do you think bribery is wrong" type stuff. Also tell me which pairs of positions the tree can't really separate, and if some combination of answers lands on a view nobody defends, say whether thats because it's incoherent or just because it's unattractive.
