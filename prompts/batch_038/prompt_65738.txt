So my flatmate and I have got properly stuck into that whole rabbit hole — sorry, vole hole — about how many wild animals get killed by crop cultivation as against by meat production, and we've been going back and foward on it for a fortnight and honestly my brain is fried. We started with Steven Davis's least harm argument, then read Michael Archer's 2011 piece (something like "Slaughter of the singing sentients: Measuring the morality of eating red meat"), which claims pasture-raised ruminant meat might cost fewer field animal lives than a vegetarian diet, then the rebuttals that take his arithmetic apart, then Matheny and Chan's "Human diets and animal welfare: the illogic of the larder", and finally Fischer and Lamey on field deaths in plant agriculture. We run a scrappy little ethics reading group on Thursday nights and I'd like to kick off next week's session with a joke so nobody glazes over before we've even started.

The bits I reckon are comic gold, in case it's useful for you: Davis's principle tells you to pick whichever diet causes the least total harm, counted in deaths and suffering. Archer does the sums and lands on the conclusion that there are more dead mice on a plate of beans than on a steak, and then people point out that he's counted animals that were going to die anyway, or counted the same hectares twice, or whatever. Matheny and Chan then observe that for a fixed amount of protein you kill enormously more chickens than cows, because a cow is one gigantic animal and a chicken is basically an edible budgerigar — so if you actually take least harm seriously you ought to be giving up chicken in favour of beef, which is the last thing anyone expects to be told. And Fischer and Lamey note that these are deaths of wild animals whose suffering is happening regardless of whether we harvest the grain.

What I'm after: a joke, a few lines at most, either stand-up style or a short pub exchange with the punchline at the end. It has to turn on the actual content of this debate rather than being a generic vegans-are-smug gag or a pun on the word beef. Ideally somebody who's read the literature groans and then laughs, and it gets funnier the more of the detail you know — so something hanging on least harm making the biggest animal the most efficient one, or on both sides counting the same mice and reaching opposite conclusions, would be spot on. Please don't explain the joke afterwards, and don't make the field animals the butt of it; the humour should come off the philosophers doing the counting.
