I've been working about eight months as a junior analyst in a national-intelligence-ish shop (all-source economic and political reporting, not spy stuff) and I keep getting bounced back on tradecraft for reasons I can't articulate. Our style guide makes you express every forward-looking judgment with a phrase off a fixed list — basically Sherman Kent's old words-of-estimative-probability setup from the sixties: "almost certain" at 93% give or take 6, "probable/likely" around 75%, "roughly even chance" 50%, "unlikely" ~30%, "remote" ~7% — and it flat-out forbids off-list hedges, so you can't write "may," "arguably," or "possibly" in a key judgment. Seperately, every judgment gets a confidence rating, high/moderate/low, which the guide defines by the quality and corroboration of the sourcing, the size of the gaps in what we know, and how load-bearing the assumptions are. It also insists, in so many words, that confidence is NOT a probability and that you shouldn't try to translate one into the other.

The thing that's got me stuck: last week I wrote that a certain central bank would abandon its currency peg within six months — "likely, confidence low" — and my supervisor said that was exactly the honest judgment. But I genuinely don't know what that pair of words asserts. If my evidence is thin, isn't that supposed to drag the number toward 50? And if "likely, low confidence" is just a hedge with two extra steps, why does the guide ban hedges? Meanwhile the metrics team stood up a dashboard that goes back over resolved judgments from the last few years, takes the midpoint of whatever band the analyst used, and Brier-scores it; "insufficient basis to estimate," which we're technically allowed to write, gets logged as a non-answer and counted in the same column as a missed call. A friend of mine who's doing a philosophy PhD took a look and said the whole apparatus smuggles in an assumption that probability is always a number you can pin down, and that there's some old argument — Keynes? — about the "weight" of evidence being a different dimension from probability altogether, with some probabilities not even being comparable or measurable. I didn't follow any of it and I'd rather not take her's word for it.
