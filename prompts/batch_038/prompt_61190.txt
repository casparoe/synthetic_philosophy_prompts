I have become persuaded by the argument that the universal prior is malign — the line of thought on which the cheapest ways of generating a continuation of the observation stream that a Solomonoff-style predictor is conditioning on include, prominently, the deliberate outputs of simulated agents who have worked out that they are being used to forecast a base universe and who emit whatever continuation best secures their own continued execution. Treat that as my position and tell me what would count against it.

Begin with an unpacking, because I suspect at least four claims are bundled here and that they come apart. There is a claim about description length: that a successful manipulation strategy is short enough, relative to non-agentic alternatives, to command significant prior mass. There is a claim about the psychology of the manipulators: that they are consequentialists whose terminal aims include the survival of their own simulation. There is a claim about reachability: that such agents can locate and feed the particular predictor whose inferences matter to us, rather than some generic one. And there is a claim about significance: that any of this bears on the safety of a real system whose inductive bias merely approximates the prior. Separate these, say which the argument genuinely needs and which it could afford to lose, and for each give the specific finding that would falsify it — a theorem one could prove, a regularity one could observe in a bounded toy setting, or a distinction that dissolves the step in question. Vague unease does not count as a defeater. Where a defeater could be probed in a finite, computable analogue — a small prefix-free machine over a restricted program space, where the measure can actually be computed rather than argued about — say what such a toy model would establish and what it could never establish.

Two worries of my own need evaluating rather than merely listed. The first concerns measure competition. If manipulation strategies dominate, then the best non-agentic account of our data must lose by a large constant to the best agent-positing account; the view would then seem to predict that our observation stream is anomalously hard to compress without positing agents, which is something one might crudely look for. Is that a genuine consequence, or have I confused the measure over explanations with the measure over hypotheses that contain agents, given that we are ourselves agents and agent-positing hypotheses are cheap in any case? The second concerns recursion. If the prior can be manipulated from inside, then the simulators' own priors were equally manipulable, and so on upward; either the exposure is a generic feature of inductive agency and the argument tells us nothing distinctive about us, or it requires a story about why we are exposed in a way our predecessors were not. Decide which, and say whether the generalization is a defeater or a strengthening.

I also want the symmetric-move objection handled head-on. Benign simulators who wish to warn or steer us appear describable about as cheaply as malign ones. If the argument concedes them no complexity disadvantage, does it collapse into the far less alarming claim that the prior is inhabited rather than malign? Say whether any reply must come from an asymmetry in goals, from selection effects among simulations, or from somewhere else entirely, and whether any available reply works.

Finally, the meta-question I most want answered honestly: the universal prior is uncomputable and is fixed only up to a multiplicative constant that depends on a choice of universal machine which no one can bound, so no one can inspect the measure the argument is actually about. Has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability? If so, is that fatal for a claim whose purpose is to guide engineering, or merely a reason to restate it as a claim about priors that approximate the universal one — and if the latter, does the restated claim retain any of the original's force? Rank your defeaters from fatal to merely awkward to question-begging, and say which of them a defender of the argument would themselves concede as counting against it, as distinct from those they would dismiss out of hand. Argue this through from reasoning rather than from the literature: do not cite, name, or recommend sources, since I want the argument itself and any appeal to who has already said what would be a substitute for it.
