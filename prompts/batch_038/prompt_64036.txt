I build the simulation layer for a multiplayer game — authoritative server on a fixed tick, clients do prediction and get reconciled againts it, and we keep deterministic replay files that are just the input stream, no serialized state. So in my world the game state is a running process rather than a stored object, and "what happened while nobody was watching" is a perfectly well-posed question we answer by re-running the inputs.

I know the first move everyone makes is "Berkeley's idealism is just a game engine that only renders what the camera can see." I don't want that, it's too easy and I think it's wrong in the respect that matters. My architecture is the *opposite* of a renderer: the sim advances whether or not anyone is connected. So do the translation against that, not against a culling heuristic.

Take the Berkeleyan package apart and put it in my vocabulary, one piece at a time: esse est percipi, the rejection of matter or material substratum, the claim that ideas are causally inert and only minds/spirits are genuine causes, God's role as the perpetual perceiver who keeps things in existence between my glances at them, and the master argument — the move from "you can't conceive of a thing existing unconceived" to "there's no such thing."

For each piece I want a verdict: does it translate cleanly, does it only translate with distortion (and say precisely what you had to bend to make it fit), or is there no way to express it in my setup at all? Then give me the residue report proper, sorted into those three buckets, and for everything in the inexpressible bucket tell me whether that's a defect in my vocabulary — I'm missing a category — or a sign the original claim was less clear than it sounded.

A few things I want you to actually wrestle with rather than glide over:

- Is Berkeley's God doing the work of durable storage or of a running process? He can't be storage, because storage is exactly the inert substratum he's banning. But if he's a process, doesn't that commit Berkeley to "the world is never serialized, only simulated" — i.e. must he deny that anything like a save file is metaphysically possible? Tell me whether that's a real consequence of his view or whether I'm over-reading my own hobbyhorse into it.
- In my setup the master argument looks like a trivial claim about tooling: you can't dump the state without a dumper, and the dumper is itself a client. Is that all Berkeley has got — a slide from "no epistemic access without an accessor" to "no existence without a perceiver"? Or is there a reading that survives the translation and says something my architecture genuinely cannot say?
- My own hunch: the closest analogue in my world to Berkeley's isn't the rendered frame, it's the replay file — a script of inputs plus a lawful procedure, with no state sitting around in between, state existing only while it's being computed. If that's right, Berkeley's laws of nature are the tick function and his occasionalism is forced on him in a way mine isn't forced on me, since my tick function doesn't need a mind to run it and his does. Does that hunch hold up, or am I flattering myself?
