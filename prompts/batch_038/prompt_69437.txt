A friend and I spent most of yesterday evening arguing about the bootstrapping problem, and then most of the drive home doing it again, and I have come away unable to say which parts of my own position survived. Rather than reopen the argument with him, I would like it audited by someone who was not in the room. Here is the package I defended, in roughly the order I defended it.

(1) Perceptual beliefs can be justified without any antecedent justification for thinking the faculty or instrument in question reliable. When the fuel gauge reads half a tank, I am justified in believing the tank is half full; I need not have established the gauge's trustworthiness first.

(2) Justification is closed under known entailment. I am not willing to surrender this in general, and I do not think I need to.

(3) Nevertheless, the reasoning that collects a long series of such pairs — the gauge read F, and the tank was indeed F — and concludes inductively that the gauge is reliable does not give me justification for that conclusion. The diagnosis I pressed hardest: evidence bearing on the reliability of a source must be independent of that source, and this reasoning is not. He asked me to state that requirement without begging the question against the reasoning, and I could not. I also flirted with saying that justification here simply fails to transmit from premises to conclusion, but I could not say what that amounted to beyond the bare claim that the conclusion is unjustified, which was the thing in dispute.

(4) I do not think this commits me to skepticism about induction. An argument for a rule that itself employs the rule — justifying induction by listing how often induction has worked — strikes me as legitimate, because the alternative is that we have no reason whatever to think induction works. What I reject is only the vicious species, in which the conclusion appears among the premises.

(5) The same goes for memory. I take myself to have justification for believing my memory is broadly reliable, even though any test I could run on it would have to rely on it.

(6) Testimony I do not treat this way. I think I need antecedent reasons for trusting a particular speaker, and that those reasons cannot themselves be purely testimonial. Speakers are checkable against my own perceptions in a way that my faculties are not checkable against anything.

(7) Finally, and this is where the evening turned, because it concerns my work. I help run evaluation for a team that builds a document classifier. Our accuracy figures come from a review set assembled as follows: an earlier version of the classifier scored a large pool of documents, human reviewers examined only those the model flagged as uncertain, and the remainder were taken to be correctly classified by default. I maintained that this involves no bootstrapping, since human beings supplied the ground truth wherever the classification was genuinely contested. He maintained that it is bootstrapping in its purest form, and worse than the gauge case, because the model chose where the scrutiny went.

What I want is an audit of this package, not an introduction to the literature it sits in. Go through it and tell me which of these commitments are genuinely incompatible with which, and which only appear to be. Where two are genuinely incompatible, say which one has to go and what I lose by giving it up; where two only seem to conflict, identify the distinction I was implicitly relying on and say whether I am entitled to it. Rank the real problems by severity rather than presenting them as an undifferentiated list, and be as clear about the tensions that dissolve on inspection as about the ones that do not — I would rather not have everything flagged. I have no view about which claim I should surrender, and I would prefer that you not assume the ones I stated most confidently are the most secure. Close by telling me what the single cheapest revision to the package would be and what it would cost me elsewhere, including whether it settles the disagreement in (7) or merely relocates it.

Please answer in continuous prose, the way one would write a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold or italic emphasis, and no tables. Referring to my claims by the numbers above is fine where it saves words.
