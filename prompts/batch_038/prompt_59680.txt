Four of us code events (coups, banking panics, coalition collapses, that sort of thing) and assign each one a "redundancy count" — my name for it — meaning the number of distinct minimally sufficient sets of antecedent conditions the sources actually attest to. Nobody's numbers agree, and I think that's because no one has said what the concept is supposed to do.

Before I read another chapter on causation in history, write me the spec. What does any adequate account of causal overdetermination and multi-causal explanation in history have to deliver? Exactly six numbered items, a sentence or two each, no intro and no conclusion: items one through five are requirements with their motivating case and the non-negotiable/prejudice tag, and item six is the pair of requirements you think can't both be met, plus a word on which one people quietly give up.
