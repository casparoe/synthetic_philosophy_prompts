I'm preparing a seminar handout on the color-phi case and I've assembled an argument whose conclusion I cannot live with.

Setup as I take it: two stationary lights of different colors flash in quick succession at nearby positions, and subjects report a single object traveling continuously from one to the other and changing color at the midpoint — content that can only be fixed by the second flash, which arrives after the reported midpoint has supposedly already been traversed. Two accounts compete: Stalinesque revision, on which the content is assembled before it is experienced, so the midpoint change is genuinely seen; and Orwellian revision, on which the subject sees the first color at the first position and the trace is edited afterward. Dennett and Kinsbourne hold that no experiment can tell which is happening.

(1) All the evidence we have about when an experience occurred, or in what order experiences occurred, is a report — verbal, button press, or confidence rating — produced after the experience.
(2) Any post-experiential report is a trace whose content can be altered between experience and reporting.
(3) So for any report R about the temporal structure of experience there is a hypothesis on which R transcribes the experience and a rival on which R reflects a later edit.
(4) Where two hypotheses license the same report, no report can decide between them. Call this Report Parity.
(5) Hence no report can decide between the Orwellian and the Stalinesque reading of color phi.
(6) But (1)–(4) are not special to color phi. Consider the discriminating design I sketched for the handout: after the second flash, interpose a 900 ms backward mask plus a tone-counting task before the report, on the theory that an edited trace should be more fragile than a delivered experience. That predicts midpoint changes lost more often under the Orwellian than under the Stalinesque account. Yet by (1)–(3) the report of what survived the mask is itself post-experiential and itself editable, and a rival pair — Stalinesque experience degraded by the interference versus Orwellian experience whose edit was consolidated before the mask — licenses the same numbers. By Report Parity the design decides nothing.
(7) So nothing distinguishes any claim about when, or in what order, experiences occur.
(8) That is intolerable: it would make the reliability of temporal-order reports a standing miracle, and it would leave me unable to know the order of the two flashes I am watching in the very experiment that generated the problem.
(9) Therefore something in (1)–(4) is false.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I would sooner sacrifice (5), the local color-phi claim, than (8), but I suspect that is the wrong offering. Say also what would change your mind about the diagnosis — which consideration would move you to a different culprit — and where your own reasoning here is most likely to break.
