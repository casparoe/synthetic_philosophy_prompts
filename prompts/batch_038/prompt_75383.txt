Watched a video essay on the math of the singularity and the host's bit on von Neumann went roughly like this: "Von Neumann was the first person to see that technology would accelerate until it broke history — he talked about 'the accelerating progress of technology and changes in human life, which gives the appearance of approaching some essential singularity in the history of the race beyond which human affairs, as we know them, could not continue.' But unlike the doomsayers he had an exit. He proved a machine can build a copy of itself, and argued the only way to survive technology at our own hands is to seed the galaxy with self-replicating probes that mine moons and asteroid belts and multiply exponentially. That's why we still call them von Neumann probes."

Some of that rings true and some of it feels like its been sanded smooth for the thumbnail. Can you audit it claim by claim? I mostly want to know: who actually produced that singularity line, in what setting, and whether the host's gloss on it is fair or drops something load-bearing; what the self-replication work actually established and whether von Neumann ever hitched it to spaceflight or to survival at all; and what his thinking about surviving our own technology was really about, given he was sitting on the Atomic Energy Commission when he was writing it. Then say which of the host's conclusions survive the corrections and which ones fall over.
