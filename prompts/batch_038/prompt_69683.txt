His whole case leans on the ship-of-state passage in the Republic — the one where the sailors brawl over the helm, drug the shipowner, and the only guy who's actually studied the seasons and winds and stars gets written off as a useless stargazer. But the clever bit, and the bit I can't get any traction on, is that he doesn't claim it as an ideal. He says we already sail the ship this way in one domain: we handed the helm on interest rates to unelected people (think the Bank of England), we wrote the destination down as an inflation target that the elected side sets and could revise, and democracy didn't collapse. So on his telling the analogy isn't a fantasy about philosopher-kings, it's a description of an arrangement we already accept, and the only live question is which other policy areas have enough star-knowledge to earn the same treatment — he nominates climate rules and pandemic powers.
