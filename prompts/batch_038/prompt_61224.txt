**Draft: The Reference Class Is Doing All the Work**

**1. The argument I am targeting.**

The line of reasoning is old. Boltzmann, in reply to Zermelo's recurrence objection, offered (through his assistant Schütz, in 1895, and in print in 1896) two answers to the question of why the universe is not in equilibrium: either it began in an improbably low-entropy state, or it is overwhelmingly in equilibrium and we happen to sit inside a rare fluctuation. Eddington pressed the second answer in 1931 with the observation that the penalty for a fluctuation grows exponentially with the entropy deficit, so smaller fluctuations are vastly cheaper than large ones; by the early 2000s this had been sharpened into the modern form. Dyson, Kleban and Susskind argued that a cosmological constant, described patch by patch under holographic assumptions, leads to deeply paradoxical consequences for observers; the version I care about runs as follows. Our far future, on the standard model, is asymptotically de Sitter: a horizon with temperature H/2π and entropy of order 10^122 in Planck units. If that phase lasts forever and is described by a finite-dimensional Hilbert space — an assumption, not a theorem, though a well-motivated one — then it thermalises and recurs, with a recurrence time doubly exponential in the entropy, and over infinite proper time every fluctuation occurs. Fluctuations are weighted by roughly exp(−ΔS), so a brain-sized departure from equilibrium is exponentially cheaper than a galaxy, and a galaxy is exponentially cheaper than an ordered observable universe. Hence in such a cosmology the observers are overwhelmingly momentary freak systems with fabricated memories, not evolved creatures with records. Call this the freak-observer conclusion.

The two standard responses are, first, that a theory entailing the freak-observer conclusion cannot be stably believed, because the very evidence and reasoning that led you to the theory would, on the theory, be fluke-generated — Carroll's cognitive-instability line, which I take to be the strongest form of the objection; and second, that we should simply accept atypicality and keep the cosmology.

**2. My thesis.**

Both responses accept the step that actually does the work, and it is not a physical step. Physics, in this picture, delivers a measure over microstates or over histories. Nothing in the physics delivers the further principle that your credence about which observer-moment you are should be given by that measure, unrestricted. That is a semantic premise — a typicality or self-sampling assumption — and the entire force of the reductio depends on it. So the disagreement between "freak observers refute the cosmology" and "freak observers are harmless, we are just lucky" is not a disagreement about Λ. Both sides accept the thermodynamics, the fluctuation weighting, and the recurrence structure. They disagree about the mapping from measure to self-locating credence, and they conduct that disagreement under cover of a dispute about the value of the cosmological constant.

**3. The asymmetry nobody mentions.**

Here is what I think is genuinely missed. The freak-observer inference and the inference that licenses trust in my own records are applications of the same machinery at opposite temporal ends. My memories and fossils and starlight are evidence about the past only because the past was low-entropy in a very particular way; the freak threat arises because the future, in these cosmologies, is thermal. Both inferences run: equilibrium statistics, plus a boundary condition, plus a typicality assumption about where I sit in the resulting distribution. That has a consequence for the instability response. Cognitive instability cannot be a special plea reserved for the future-directed case, because any statistical-mechanical route to distrusting my cognitive reliability also undermines the low-entropy-past reasoning that made my records meaningful in the first place. If you want to keep one and drop the other, you owe a principle that separates them — and the principle cannot be "the one that yields an absurd conclusion," on pain of making cosmological theorising hostage to how we feel about results.

**4. My proposal.**

Restrict the reference class. Include in the class over which typicality is computed only those observer-moments that stand in the right causal and counterfactual relation to what they represent: an observer-moment counts as a competitor for my self-location only if its apparent evidence tracks something. A freak observer's apparent memories track nothing at all; it is a momentary physical state whose intrinsic character is indistinguishable from mine but whose history contains no process that produced that character in response to anything. Under this restriction — call it the tracking restriction — an eternally recurring de Sitter phase generates no competitors, the reductio loses its engine, and Λ is left alone.

The restriction is not, I think, empty. It still predicts that I should not see macroscopic violations of the second law, since tracking observers are embedded in ordinary thermodynamic history. It still makes our orderliness informative, because it requires that tracking observers be sufficiently numerous, which constrains cosmologies that produce few of them. And it still leaves the ordinary measure problem intact for cases where all candidate observers track.

**5. Where I expect to be attacked, and where I am weakest.**

(a) *Circularity.* Whether an observer-moment tracks is precisely what I would use my evidence to determine, so the restriction looks question-begging. My defence is that this is rule-circularity rather than premise-circularity: I am not inferring that I track from the assumption that I track, I am adopting a rule for weighting self-locating evidence and then noting that the rule has consequences. I am not confident.

(b) *Ad hocness.* Is the tracking restriction independently motivated, or is it tuned to dissolve this one paradox? I claim independent motivation from the fact that we already treat non-tracking states — dreamless duplicates, hypothetical perfect replicas with fabricated histories — as irrelevant to questions about what evidence I have. A critic will say that is exactly the point at issue.

(c) *Extrinsic duplicates.* This is my deepest worry. On the picture I am using, a freak observer-moment and an ordinary one can be intrinsic physical duplicates differing only in global causal history. If self-location is settled by intrinsic character, no restriction on the reference class can separate them, and my proposal fails at the root. Note that this is not merely a quibble: if it succeeds against me, it also succeeds against any response to the freak-observer problem that relies on the unreliability of the freak's evidence, since the freak's evidence is internally just as good as mine.

(d) *Regulators.* In an eternally inflating multiverse both tracking and non-tracking observers are infinite in number. Without a regulator the restriction may change nothing.

(e) *States versus processes.* A momentary brain-sized configuration may not be an observer at all; and a more careful treatment of what such a fluctuation would look like suggests it would not appear all at once but as a sequence of smaller fluctuations resembling a decay path run backwards. If observer-hood requires a process with the right temporal profile, the exponential bookkeeping changes, and part of my section 1 is sloppier than I would like.
