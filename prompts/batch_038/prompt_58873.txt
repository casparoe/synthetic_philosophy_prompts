So my town in the foothills burned in September — about 300 houses on the north side gone — and I'm one of seven people on the rebuild commission. We vote on the 14th, nine days out, on the question that's split us: do we re-plat the north side, or put it back on the old lot lines exactly as thier deeds describe them?

Background you'd need. The old layout is a mess with no logic to it. Two streets curve because a trolley line ran there until 1929. Lots are 25 feet wide and 200 feet deep because somebody in 1903 sold by frontage. The bend in the main road is why the grocery and the post office have sat on the same corner for a hundred years. And Maple is 18 feet wide end to end, which is its part of why the engines had trouble getting in.

Half the commission says this is the only window we get in a century — titles are being reworked anyway, nobody has moved back yet, and the state money is on the table now and will not be in ten years. The other half says the town comes out the same whatever we draw: our planner, before she left, said the highway interchange is what decides where a center is, not the map, and that we'd be spending our credibility on cosmetics while families are still in trailers.

Write the memo I'll circulate before the vote. Nine hundred words or so, plain enough that a resident who's furious at the meeting can follow it. I want an actual recommendation with a date attached to it, not a both-sides summary. And here's the part I keep failing to make people hear: when folks say we're "locked in," I think they're rolling at least three different claims into one — that the current layout happened by accident, that changing it is expensive, and that it'll keep reproducing itself even if we do change it. Pull those apart and say which of them actually bear on the vote and which are just noise. Then give the strongest honest version of the case against whatever you recommend, and tell us what we'd be able to see ten years from now that would mean we got it wrong. One disclosure to work in near the top: the re-plat would clip about a third of my family's parcel for the road widening, so I'd rather say that myself than have somebody raise it. No citations or references, please, and don't tell us to commission more analysis — we don't have the days for it.
