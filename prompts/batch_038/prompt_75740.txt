I pulled cores out of lakes and off the continental shelf for thirty-five years, and only got properly into philosophy after I retired, so forgive me for framing this like a field problem. What I want is the joint statement two camps could both put their names to after a real adversarial collaboration about the Anthropocene.

Camp A is the stratigraphers' camp: the Anthropocene is a genuine geological time unit, definable the way any other unit is, by a boundary you can point to in the rock — and the fact that the formal proposal got voted down a few years back (the Ontario lake, 1950, the plutonium spike) is a fact about procedure and the thinness of the record, not a finding that there's no such unit.

Camp B holds that the Anthropocene is a historical period concept that the stratigraphic machinery was never built to carry: when it starts depends on what you're trying to explain, several start dates are defensible (first farming, the dip in CO2 in the early 1600s after the Americas were depopulated, the steam engine, the bomb tests), and "who decides" is not a question a subcommission vote answers.

Give me a numbered section of claims both camps can actually sign, then a disagreements table where each row is worded so that side's own people would accept it, then the cruxes: the sub-questions where settling them would move someone. I have a suspicion about two, and I'd like them tested rather than assumed. First, whether a unit may be defined by its cause — human agency — instead of by its material signature. Second, whether a boundary has to be synchronous in order to be one boundary at all. For the second, run both camps through a concrete case: two cores, one high-latitude lake and one tropical peat swamp, that record the same human signal three centuries apart. Say what each camp is commited to about whether that is the same boundary, name the evidence or argument both agree would bear on it, and write each side's prediction down before anyone is declared right.

Two more things. A short joint glossary pinning three words down (epoch, onset, boundary, or whichever you choose) so we can see plainly which part of the fight was only about words. And a clause on what the rejected vote does and doesn't settle, including each camp saying whose verdict they take to be authoritative and whether they think the other side's authority is the wrong kind of authority or just wrong. Finally, for each crux, one line on what would count against that side's own position. No bibliography, please — I couldn't check the references anyway.
