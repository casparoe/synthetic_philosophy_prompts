I sit on the governance committee of a four-household cohousing community and need a memo to circulate before Tuesday's meeting.

Two clauses of our draft bylaws are individually popular. Clause 3 gives each household sole authority over what happens on its own plot; no collective body may direct a household's conduct within its own boundaries. Clause 7 is our founders' anti-tyranny rule: whenever every household strictly prefers one arrangement to another, the community must adopt the preferred arrangement.

A live dispute seems to have broken them. The Marshes and the Delaneys each decide independently whether to hold amplified music nights in their own yards on Saturdays. Polling the two households, I got these orderings over the four possible configurations. Marshes: both host > neither hosts > Marshes host alone > Delaneys host alone (they would rather have a quiet street than be the sole nuisance, and they can't stand being the only silent house while the Delaneys party). Delaneys: Marshes host alone > both host > neither hosts > Delaneys host alone (they would rather drop in on someone else's party than throw one, and hate making noise on an otherwise silent street). The other two households prefer both-host to neither-host. When I try to turn Clauses 3 and 7 into a single ranking of the four configurations, I get a circle rather than a ranking, and I can't tell which clause ought to give.

Two fixes are on the table. One member proposes a waiver clause letting a household voluntarily hand its Clause 3 authority over to the community. Another argues that the real trouble is that the Delaneys' preferences concern the Marshes' conduct rather than their own, so Clause 7 should count only preferences over one's own affairs.

Please draft the memo. Open with a concrete recommendation for Tuesday — adopt as drafted, adopt with a specified amendment, or table — and then give the reasoning behind it, including the strongest case for keeping both clauses exactly as written and an explicit verdict on whether each proposed fix dissolves the problem or merely relocates it. I dimly recall an impossibility result from a social-choice course that bears on this, but I couldn't reconstruct it, and I don't want the memo to lean on my half-memory of it. American English, please, and continuous prose only: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — write it the way one would write a letter.
