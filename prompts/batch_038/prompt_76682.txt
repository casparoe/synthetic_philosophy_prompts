My friend and I spent most of a train journey arguing about testimony and I have come away unsure which of our claims were substantive and which were just the two of us assuming different things about what an account of testimonial justification owes us. His position, roughly: when a stranger tells you the way to the station and you believe her, you have no positive evidence about her at all — no track record, no corroboration, no independent access to the facts — and you are nonetheless perfectly justified; the demand for reasons is a philosopher's fantasy, and the fact that you take your own doctor, your own historian, and your own parents on their bare word shows that the demand would bankrupt almost everything you claim to know. My reply was that this makes justification a function of which community one happens to have been raised in: on his picture, the person who trusts an anonymous forum about vaccines is doing precisely what I do when I trust a textbook, and the only difference is that I approve of my sources. He said yes, essentially, and that my discomfort was parochialism dressed up as epistemology. Neither of us could say what would settle it.

So before I read further, I want the specification rather than the contenders. Fix the target first: I want the requirements for an account good enough to adjudicate disputes about contested public claims — the cases where the hearer cannot check, where informed people disagree, and where who is speaking is itself doing work — not merely one that handles the single-speaker, single-hearer classroom example tidily.

Write out the desiderata: what any adequate account of when a hearer is justified in accepting what she is told must deliver. Number them R1, R2, and so on. For each, give the case or consideration that motivates it, described concretely enough that I can see why it bites, and say whether it is a requirement on the account or only a desideratum it would be nice to meet. I would like the list to cover at least the following regions, plus anything important I have overlooked:

- hearers who cannot in principle have a track record: small children acquiring a first language, and novices in technical domains where the content is beyond their competence to evaluate;
- what the account must say about the difference between being lied to and being given a sincere mistake, and whether that difference is epistemic or only moral;
- whether it must explain knowledge surviving transmission along a chain, and whether any link may add warrant that no earlier link possessed;
- the boundary of testimony itself — clocks, maps, instruments, and machine-generated text that reports without arguably asserting anything. I want to know whether an account is obliged to draw that line and, if so, which side of it its own machinery pushes it toward;
- whether it must be able to condemn a hearer's credibility judgment as distorted by prejudice about the speaker's identity, and whether that is a demand about justification or only about the hearer's character;
- and whether it must avoid making justification turn on the epistemic community one is embedded in — my objection to my friend — or whether it may swallow that consequence and explain why it is not fatal.

Two things then matter more to me than the list. First, sort it. Mark which items are, so far as you can tell, genuinely shared ground among people who work on this, which are live disputes with real arguments on both sides, and which are not desiderata at all but the local preferences of one camp smuggled in as neutral requirements. Be explicit about that sorting, and where you are unsure whether an item is consensus, say that it is unsure rather than presenting a partisan requirement as though everyone granted it. Second, identify any subset that cannot be jointly satisfied. My suspicion is that the demands concerning hearers who cannot check, hearers who must not be gullible, and hearers whose credibility judgments may be prejudice-shaped are in tension, since the reasons available to someone who cannot evaluate the content are exactly the thin, surface reasons that bias is best at colonizing. If you agree, say so plainly and show where the inconsistency lands; if you disagree, tell me what I have misdescribed.

No bibliography, no reading list, no name-tagged survey of who holds what — I am not asking for attributions, and I would rather you omit a label than guess at one. Keep the weight on the requirements and their motivating cases; the scoring of particular views I will do myself.

Close with one final line in exactly this form:

Verdict: [the R-label of the single requirement that excludes the most candidate accounts], because [one clause].
