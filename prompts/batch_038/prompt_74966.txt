So I've basically bought the acausal trade story — agents in different causal patches (or just our own future AIs, honestly) can strike deals with each other purely by modelling each other's decision procedures, and that gives a real reason to be the kind of agent who honors a deal nobody could ever verify. I want the case against, though, not strawmen. What has to be true for the whole thing to collapse?

Two things I especially want pushed on. First, doesn't the trade depend on having a model of your counterpart accurate enough to predict its choice, with no way to check the model? Does that just degrade into noise, or into something worse — the acausal version of blackmail, where being visibly the sort of agent who keeps their word also makes you the sort who pays off threats, and paying once selects for more threats? Second, does anything survive the usual retreat to "well, conditional on the counterpart reasoning like FDT does" — at what point has the view been qualifed into asserting nothing?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a serious proponent would actually concede rather than wave off, name one thing the view predicts that a purely-causal account doesn't, and be honest about whether the position risks anything at all.
