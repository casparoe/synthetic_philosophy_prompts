For years I've followed the argument between the republican account of freedom as non-domination and the Berlinian negative-liberty camp, and it strikes me as the cleanest case I know of a dispute in which both sides agree on every descriptive fact and still never converge. The setup: A and B have exactly the same actions actually open to them today, but A's options are secured by rules that hold irrespective of anyone's goodwill, while B's depend on an employer's or a spouse's continuing benevolence — the stock case being the kind master who happens never to interfere. The republicans and their neo-Roman allies say A is free and B is not; critics like Kramer and Carter say the two are equally free and that what the republican has really identified is a distinct good — security, or robustness — which should not be smuggled into the concept of liberty itself. I tried the usual move of tabooing the word freedom and restating both positions without it, and the disagreement survives the tabooing; something is left over, and I cannot tell what.

What I want is a diagnosis, not a survey of the positions and not a ruling on who is right. Is this a substantive disagreement, a merely verbal one about an honorific label, or two projects answering different questions under a single heading — one asking what freedom is, one asking what the state should protect? My working hypothesis, offered only as a hypothesis: the hidden crux is formal rather than evaluative, concerning the logical type of the freedom predicate — whether it is assessed at the actual option set or across the modal profile of that set under other people's possible choices — with all the talk of value on both sides sitting downstream of that. Tell me whether that is the real structure, and then say what would count as progress on your diagnosis: a formal model, a decisive case, a stipulation both camps could accept, or an argument that none should be expected and why.

Please answer in exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no preamble and no closing remarks: open with your diagnosis in a single line, spend the middle items defending it against the alternatives I listed, and close with what would count as progress.
