For a chapter I am writing, I need four analyses of singular causation ranked by how well they cope with *prevention* and *double prevention* — that is, cases in which the putative cause is an absence, or in which one process prevents a second process that would itself have prevented the effect. The four are:

1. Lewis-style counterfactual dependence, together with the standard appeal to chains of dependence to handle preemption.
2. Woodward's account, on which a causal claim is true to the extent that the relevant generalisation remains invariant under some range of interventions on the putative cause.
3. The NESS-style view, on which a condition caused the effect just in case it was a necessary element of some set of conditions actually present that was jointly sufficient for the effect.
4. A view I shall call the *audit account*, which I define as follows: c caused e if and only if an auditor who is forbidden from intervening, but who may condition on any variable that is neither downstream of c nor a common effect of c and e, would assign a different probability to e on learning that c occurred than she would assign otherwise.

Award each a score out of 10 for its treatment of prevention and double prevention, list them in descending order of score, and attach one sentence explaining each score. Finally, identify the single score you are least confident in and say what would change your mind about it.
