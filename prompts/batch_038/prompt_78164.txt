ok so my roommate and I got about four hours deep into the special composition question last night — it started because he was assembling a bookshelf, I said "just set the box down on the floor," and he said, completely straight-faced, "there is no box" — and somehow we ended up in the worst argument of the semester. He's a nihilist, nothing ever composes anything, just simples arranged bookshelfwise, and I'm... I honestly don't know what I am, I want to say fastening or contact matters, but he kept hitting me with the vagueness thing: give me the exact torque at which two boards become one object, and if you can't do it non-arbitrarily you might as well admit that everything composes everything. Then our friend Priya, who is a universalist and deeply insufferable about it, texted into the middle of the argument to note that the scattered object made of my roommate's left shoe and a rock on Mars has existed this whole time and he's been under-counting his belongings his entire life.

Anyway, all three of us are moving this summer and renting storage units, and it struck me at 2am that this fight is really just a dispute between a self-storage company and its customers. So here's the ask: write me a piece of satire in the form of an escalating email thread, around 700–900 words, four or five messages, at a mid-market chain — call it BunkerBox Self Storage. The tenant is a mereological nihilist disputing a per-item surcharge and a "no loose materials" clause; he's perfectly willing to pay, he just keeps demanding the invoice be reworded so it says what it's actually billing him for, and his paraphrases should genuinely do the work they're supposed to do rather than being a cheap verbal trick. The on-site manager is a cheerful universalist who responds by billing for every sum in the unit, including some nobody would have guessed were in there. Regional compliance gets looped in and insists on a restricted criterion, issuing a "fastening schedule" with a bright line — and then has to deal with one specific item that lands right on it (a half-mortared brick stack, a tent, whatever you pick). Finally in-house counsel writes the arbitration email, and the punchline is that everyone settles by rewording the contract without changing who pays a cent: the ontology never touched the money.

Keep it deadpan corporate throughout, nobody winks, and don't name any real philosophers or cite anything — a layperson should find it funny and a metaphysician should recognize every single move, including the arbitrariness pressure on the compliance officer's threshold and the nihilist's problem of explaining why the simples are arranged that way without invoking the object he's denying. Bonus points for a clause about what happens when a customer's stored stuff gets stacked on a BunkerBox-owned pallet — whose sum is it, whose insurance covers it — and for one last invoice line item that is genuinely absurd but follows from the rules as stated. I'd also love it if the three positions each got one moment where they're obviously right and one where they're obviously screwed, since that's kind of where my roommate and I actually got stuck.
