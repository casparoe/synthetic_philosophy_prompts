I am working through the Formula of Universal Law for a dissertation chapter, and my considered verdicts on particular maxims refuse to line up with the standard two-tier story, so I want to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

Borrowing money with a promise of repayment I know I cannot honor: fails, and fails at the first tier — the practice of promising could not survive universalization. Promising while privately reserving the right to break the promise should it become burdensome: fails as well, though I suspect it only fails because I have described the maxim widely enough; the same maxim specified to my exact circumstances seems to universalize without trouble. Refusing ever to assist anyone, however great their need: passes the first tier, fails the second, since I cannot will a world in which no one would aid me. Devoting one's life to idleness and cultivating no talents at all: I think this passes both tiers — I cannot identify any end of mine that it necessarily frustrates — even though the argument against it looks structurally identical to the non-beneficence case. Ending my life to escape misery: fails. Telling a would-be murderer at my door that my friend is not home when she is: fails. Telling that same murderer, truthfully, that I saw my friend in the garden ten minutes ago, knowing full well what he will infer: passes, and I cannot say why, since the intent to mislead and the reliance on my word are the same in both. Submitting a term paper generated by a model while representing it as my own work: fails at the first tier, on the ground that the assignment would cease to evidence anything if everyone did it. Depending on data that colleagues share while sharing none of my own: fails at the second tier.

My tentative hunch is that the second tier only bites where a necessary end is frustrated, but "necessary" is doing all the work and I cannot cash it out in a way that keeps the idleness case on the passing side.

Is there a principle, or a small set of candidates, that delivers all of these? For each, state the clauses precisely and the case that motivates each clause; say specifically how it handles the description of the maxim, and whether the lie/evasive-truth pair can be preserved without a clause that looks ad hoc. Then give its verdict on a case I have not listed: the graduate student who never reads the secondary literature, forming interpretations of the primary texts directly, while depending on a scholarly culture sustained by people who do. Finally, say plainly which of my verdicts cannot be accommodated.

Please begin with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
