I'm auditing the coding sheet my RA has put together for a project on insulated online groups. The scheme is the bubble/echo chamber distinction I make the second-years read — bubble = you just never hear the other voices, echo chamber = you've been trained not to trust them — and her codes have drifted off the handout in ways I can't quite pin down. So far:

- flat-earth board where your first post has to explain what NASA gets out of the lie: echo chamber
- village Facebook group where everything comes from the same three pages and nobody says a word against any outside source: bubble
- day-trading Discord that bans Bloomberg and Reuters links and calls financial journalists shills: echo chamber
- indie film sub that hasn't posted a negative critics' review in five years, again with zero disparagement of critics anywhere: bubble
- city sub where every local outlet gets posted, including the ones readers hate: neither
- philosophy jobs-market list that sneers at science journalism constantly, but where everyone reads the primary literature and the outside critics anyway and unsubscribing costs nothing: bubble
- fitness group whose claims are basically what the literature says, but which bans anyone who questions the mod and talks about the people who want you soft: echo chamber
- prayer chain that describes non-members as sincere but blinded, and gives you a reason in advance for why the smart outsiders sound so reasonable: echo chamber
- rare-book sellers' group that never links to auction results or trade press, and doesn't mention them either way: bubble

The new one, tenth on the sheet: a parents' group for a rare pediatric condition. It links freely to hospital pages and PubMed, tells people to take the threads to their specialist, and I've never seen it say anything against doctors or journalists. Nobody gets punished for leaving — the kids improve and people drift off. But the consensus on an off-label protocol hasn't moved an inch in three years, and one member I know spent a year reading everything outside the group and came back holding exactly the view she started with. Their standing line is that lived experience outranks what the papers say, and they deploy it gently, never as an attack on anyone.

So what's she going to code it? I care less about the label than the machinery. Tell me which rule her nine verdicts are actually consistent with — I can think of at least three, and the handout definition isn't one of them — lay out the rivals side by side, say which one you'd bet on for the new group and why, and then give me one more group I could add to the sheet that would split your top two candidates. And be straight with me about how much of this her data settles versus how much is just me importing my own intuitions about what a community is doing.
