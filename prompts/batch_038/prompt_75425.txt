I am preparing a one-page handout for a graduate seminar on self-locating belief, and I want a decision tree rather than a survey of the Sleeping Beauty literature. Build the space first and name positions only at the end: five or six branch nodes, each a question a student could answer without knowing which camp it leads to — whether learning which day it is can rationally move one's credence in an uncentered proposition such as the outcome of a coin toss; whether an event that was antecedently certain to occur can serve as evidence once it occurs; whether Beauty's epistemic state should be pinned down by what she would accept as a fair bet or by something independent of betting; whether her evidence is sharp or is better modelled as indeterminate; whether indifference considerations apply to centered propositions at all. At each node, say which way the halfer and the thirder go and give the reason that choice is not merely stipulated. Then place the leaves: Lewis's halfer, Elga's thirder, the double halfer, the two positions that part company over whether to reason from a self-sampling assumption or a self-indication assumption, and any view that assigns Beauty an imprecise or indeterminate credence. For every region of the space your questions reach that no position occupies, say whether it is empty because the view there would be incoherent, because it is unattractive, or because nobody has thought to go there; and finish by listing the pairs of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate.
