I am putting together a small collection of comic writing about the physics of information, and my contribution is a parody of a genre I have spent too many hours inside: the referee report. Please write one for me, between 800 and 1,000 words, in the standard format — a summary of the manuscript in the reviewer's own words, an overall assessment, a numbered list of detailed comments, and a short confidential section addressed to the handling editor.

The manuscript is invented, and I would like you to treat everything below as the given facts of the case rather than as material to embellish. It has been sent to a specialist journal in statistical mechanics and nonequilibrium physics. It is titled something along the lines of "Experimental Realization of Net Positive Work from a Single-Ion Feedback Cycle Using Back-Action-Free Measurement and Deferred Memory Erasure." Its headline result is an excess of (2.9 ± 0.4) × 10⁻²¹ joules of work per cycle at 300 kelvin, accumulated over 10,000 cycles of a Szilard-engine-style protocol on a trapped ion. The authors' case rests on four moves:

- The measurement is a quantum nondemolition measurement, and therefore, they argue, costs nothing thermodynamically, since it leaves the measured observable and the ion's energy undisturbed.
- The resulting record is written into a shift register of 4,096 ion qubits that is "recycled" rather than "erased," so the Landauer bound is never triggered; where the register's entropy is reset, they claim the cost is exported into a spin reservoir rather than paid as heat, and hence does not appear in their calorimetry.
- They acknowledge the objection that a finite register eventually fills, and reply that this takes 4,096 cycles, so the cost is amortized away and is in any case "a boundary term, not a cycle term."
- They cite a private communication with a philosopher of physics who, they report, agreed that the erasure objection "presupposes a contested reading of the second law."

Their supplementary material contains an entropy-accounting table whose imbalance column sums to a nonzero value, which they present as the paper's central triumph.

The referee is courteous, tired, and technically exact, and has reviewed a version of this manuscript before, possibly under a different title. Here is the part that matters most: the referee privately holds the heterodox view that the standard exorcism of the demon leans on Landauer's principle being a theorem of statistical mechanics when it is better read as a corollary of the second law it is invoked to defend, and that the demon has been settled in an engineering sense rather than a conceptual one — and yet recommends rejection. Every objection in the report must therefore go through even on the referee's own skeptical premises. The private heterodoxy should be legible to a specialist from the confidential section alone; the body of the report must never announce that its author is a heretic, and the recommendation must not wobble.

The comedy has to come from the physics being correct, not from the referee being rude. Include, at minimum: the point that a measurement which avoids back-action on the measured observable is not thereby a thermodynamically free measurement, since the cost lives in the amplification and in the eventual disposal of the record; the point that deferring erasure is amortizing it, that amortization over a finite register returns exactly kT ln 2 per bit, and that a device requiring an unbounded register is not a finite device; the point that exporting entropy into another conserved quantity still exports entropy, and that the spin reservoir must itself eventually be reset; the point that the reported excess work is measured on the expansion stroke alone and omits the work of inserting and withdrawing the partition and of preparing the trap; and the point that once the acquired information enters the second law as a mutual-information term, their data confirm the feedback-corrected bound instead of violating it. On the last point the referee may suggest, without enthusiasm, that the paper be resubmitted as a precision test of that bound.

The pivot of the report should be the observation that the claimed anomaly, (2.9 ± 0.4) × 10⁻²¹ joules per cycle at 300 kelvin, is one bit — kT ln 2 — to within the stated error bars, so that the authors have measured the price of information using an apparatus designed to demonstrate that it has no price. If you state a number anywhere in the piece, the number must be right; if you would rather not commit to the arithmetic, leave it out.

Formal constraints: deadpan throughout, no winking, no asides to the reader, no exclamation marks, no "one almost suspects." No real people, no real institutions, no real papers, no quotations from the literature, no citations, and no reference list — the report may name concepts freely, but must put no words in anyone's mouth. Refer to the manuscript only as "the manuscript" and to its writers as "the authors." It should be readable by someone with one term of statistical mechanics, in the sense that they can follow the shape of every argument even where the details escape them, and it should make a specialist laugh rather than wince.

After the report, and clearly separated from it, add a note of about 250 words in your own voice. Tell me which of the physical claims the report relies on are settled among people who work in this area and which are genuinely contested — in particular the status of Landauer's principle, and whether the demon has been exorcised in a foundational sense or only an operational one. If the referee's private skepticism is a defensible position rather than a crank's, say so, and say plainly what the majority view is regardless; do not let the note, or the report, present a contested position as though it were the consensus. Flag any place where the satire compresses a live dispute or leans on one side of one, and if you think any detail I have given you above is physically wrong or would make the report look incompetent to its intended audience, change it and tell me what you changed and why.
