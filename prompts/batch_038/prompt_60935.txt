I am about three-quarters of the way through a novel set in an unnamed mid-sized post-industrial republic that, eleven years before the action begins, decided to stop treating religious belief as an error to be argued with and started treating it as a natural phenomenon to be measured, mapped, and managed. Chapter 22 opens with a single document: the eleventh and final annual evaluation of the Directorate for the Naturalization of Belief, signed by its founding Director-General — a sincere, scrupulous, entirely humorless methodological naturalist who has never for one moment considered herself anything but an administrator. The Directorate was founded on the confident premise that once the natural causes of belief are correctly identified, belief can be switched off at the source rather than refuted point by point; eleven years of fieldwork have not borne this out, and the report is where the reader is meant to see, all at once, exactly why, without the Director-General seeing any of it.

Please write that document, roughly 1,100 to 1,300 words, in the flat register of a genuine program evaluation: an office that is competent, well-funded, and honest about its own numbers, and that has drawn the wrong conclusion from all of them. Then add one titled section of commentary. I want the piece to be funny to a cognitive anthropologist and legible to my editor, which means the humor has to come from the science being applied correctly and pushed to its end — never from the Directorate being corrupt, stupid, or secretly pious, and never from a wink. Nothing in the report may acknowledge that anything is strange; the irony must live entirely in the substance, so that a reader who knows the literature recognizes each absurdity as an accurate caricature of a real mechanism rather than an invented one.

Everything must be invented and self-contained: no real states, no real religions, no real scholars, journals, studies, or statistics, and no citations that a reader could go looking for. Where the report refers to evidence, it refers to its own internal instruments — the Registry, the district returns, the coders, Memorandum One. The residual communities should have neutral invented designations of the kind a bureaucracy would assign them.

At least five of the following eight mechanisms must appear, rendered as operational findings, budget lines, or incident entries rather than as exposition — and two of them are mandatory, because the closing recommendation depends on them:

- hyperactive agency detection: removing the gods does not switch off the detection apparatus, and the complaint forms keep arriving about intentionality in weather, machinery, and empty rooms;
- minimally counterintuitive content and recall: the Registry notices that the prohibited entities most reliably re-reported are those violating exactly one ontological category, and that its own insignia and mnemonic verse are doing something structurally identical;
- costly signaling: the austerities the Directorate imposes on itself — no ornaments, a recitation at the opening of every meeting, compulsory attendance at audit — measurably raise staff cohesion and are formally indistinguishable from the rites they displaced;
- substituted monitoring: compliance in a district rises sharply once the Directorate's own surveillance saturates it, which is written up as proof that the gods were merely low-resolution surveillance;
- security and the demographic transition: the residual-adherence curve tracks infant mortality and life expectancy far better than it tracks enforcement intensity, and the Director-General rejects this reading;
- the fertility reversal: cadre and compliant households run below replacement while the residual congregations run above it, so the long-run projection turns against the program, prompting a corrective incentive scheme that the reader will recognize as a fertility cult;
- canonicalization of the founding instrument (mandatory): Memorandum One has acquired authoritative commentary, a strict and a lenient construction, a doctrinal dispute among the auditors that functions precisely as a heresy does, and a small office of scribes whose budget now grows faster than any other line;
- the coding baseline (mandatory if you use only five): the Registry's definition of a prohibited entity cannot be applied consistently by its own coders, and inter-coder reliability falls as the Registry expands.

The final recommendation must be a reductio, not a punchline: the Director-General notes, correctly, that the only belief system in the republic showing perfect attendance, negligible running cost, high in-group cohesion, zero supernatural content, and a stable transmission mechanism across generations is the Directorate's own program, and proposes on strictly efficiency grounds that it be formalized accordingly. She must present this as housekeeping.

Please structure the response in exactly these sections, in this order:
