My EA reading group built this argument and now nobody can kill it. It's meant to show that the importance/tractability/neglectedness framework advises you to ignore itself.

(1) An extra person's work on a cause is worthwhile only if her expected marginal contribution to it is high.
(2) That contribution is high only where the cause is important, tractable, and neglected — i.e. where comparatively few people are already working on it.
(3) How many people are already working on it is a fact about the whole pool of would-be contributors, not about any one of them.
(4) So the framework's recommendation to me turns entirely on what everyone else does.
(5) If every competent agent used the framework, they'd all be pointed at whichever important and tractable cause is currently most neglected.
(6) Their arriving there destroys the neglectedness that pointed them there, and elevates some other cause in its place.
(7) So the framework's advice, if followed, becomes advice to do something no longer worth doing.
(8) Any rule that makes itself incorrect when followed is not a reliable guide.
(9) Therefore I should defect: work on causes the framework ranks low, since those keep their neglectedness.
(10) But (1)-(8) dont mention EA anywhere. They work for any rule that ranks options by how much unclaimed value is left in them.
(11) So no such rule can be followed at all — which has to be wrong, because something like this is how people pick research topics and careers, and they mostly do fine.

Where does it break? I'd rather keep (2) than (9), since (2) is the whole reason neglectedness is in the framework to begin with. But just refusing (9) feels like a dodge and not a diagnosis.
