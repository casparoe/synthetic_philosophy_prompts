2. thumbtack, no data, never seen one before, credence it lands point up. imprecise, and not just allowed but compulsory — he says any single number you write down is pulled out of thin air and you'd be lying about your evidence.

4. the dilation example he's weirdly attached to, where you know in advance that a piece of data you're about to get is going to widen your range rather than narrow it. he calls it a black eye for the view but insists it isn't irrational.
