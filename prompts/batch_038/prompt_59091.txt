I've been rereading the Careenium material in Gödel, Escher, Bach, and I've found that the argument I actually want to have keeps getting lost in the book's framing, so I've rebuilt and considerably elaborated the apparatus; please treat my description as authoritative rather than trying to recall what Hofstadter wrote. The Careenium is a sealed chamber the size of a cathedral, tiled with fixed bumpers and channels, full of heavy glassy spheres — call them simmballs — that careen and collide indefinitely. Certain regions contain latches: each latch carries a stored template, and when a group of simmballs arrives whose spatial arrangement matches that template within a stated tolerance, the latch fires and releases a fresh volley. There is also the Archivist, a slow-moving subsystem that copies an arrangement out of one latch's template into another, so patterns propagate, recruit additional simmballs, and grow heavy, while patterns that fail to be copied thin out and vanish. Nothing inside the chamber observes any of this. The only things that respond to arrangements are other latches and the Archivist: no homunculus, no reader, no one for whom a pattern is a pattern.

Here is what I want. I keep gesturing at the view that the arrangements themselves do causal work in there — that the form pushes the simmballs around rather than merely abbreviating their pushing — and I have never managed to state it in a way that survives contact with a competent opponent, so please play that opponent. You are a theorist of the Careenium who holds that all the shoving is done by simmballs colliding, and that arrangement talk is a compressing, indispensable, and entirely derivative way of describing what the shoving already fixes. Be sharp and fair rather than a strawman: concede readily that the mass-and-velocity story is unstateable in practice, that no inhabitant of the chamber could predict anything without the arrangement idiom, and that a much-copied pattern really does become heavier and harder to stop. What you will not concede is that arrangement as such ever pushes anything, and you should push back hard whenever I slide from "the arrangement story is the only tractable story" to "therefore the arrangement does the work." Two constraints on how you talk. First, no names and no lineage: do not cite any philosopher, and do not use the standing technical vocabulary — no downward causation, no supervenience, no exclusion, realization, or levels. Argue entirely inside the chamber, in terms of simmballs, collisions, momentum, tolerances, latches, and templates; if you need a general notion, define it operationally by what would happen in there. Second, keep each reply to a single paragraph, two at most, and stay in character until I say pause; if you need a factual clarification about the apparatus, ask for it as a theorist of the Careenium would and I will supply it. If I land something that genuinely defeats a piece of your position, say so, and then try to repair the rest.

My opening move. Consider three runs. In R1, a cluster of forty simmballs arrives at latch L in arrangement A, within tolerance, and L fires. In R2, forty simmballs arrive at L in arrangement A again, but every sphere has been chipped to ninety-six percent of its original mass and launched four percent faster along paths that share not one collision with R1; L fires. In R3, the very same forty simmballs as in R1 arrive at L with the same forty speed values, now reassigned among the spheres so that the total momentum is exactly what it was in R1, but shuffled in position so that the template is not matched; L does not fire. Across the three, the outcome tracks arrangement and nothing else: R1 and R3 have an identical inventory of masses, speeds, and momentum, while R1 and R2 have no collision in common. So I ask you two things. Which fact in R1 is doing the pushing on L? And give me a test I could run inside the chamber — a difference in what happens, not a difference in how convenient the description is — that would distinguish a fact that shoves from a fact that merely abbreviates facts that shove. If your answer is that arrangement is only a coarse-grained way of talking about where the spheres are, note that the grain is not chosen by me: the latch is built to be sensitive to arrangement and blind to mass and speed within tolerance, so the chamber carves that grain whether or not anyone describes it that way.
