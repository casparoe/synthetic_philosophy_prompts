Okay so this is a family thing but I keep going back and forth on the actual argument, and I figure someone with a philosophy brain would help, because I'm the one who has to say something at the meeting on Sunday and I cant tell who's right.

My uncle Daniel died three weeks ago in Perth. He left when I was four — sold his share of the family business, stopped answering calls, and for basically thirty years the only contact was a cheque at Christmas that my grandmother refused to cash. He died in a rented flat and a neighbour found him. Now the family has to decide about the burial: plot in the family ground, the full service, his name read out with the rest of them. My cousin Sipho is dead against it, and it's his reasoning that I can't get a handle on. He says Daniel wasn't a member anymore, and that membership isn't a blood fact, it's something you keep doing — you keep showing up, you stay woven into people, and Daniel stopped doing that around 1994. So there's nothing left to bury in the sense that matters, and putting him in the plot would be a lie told in stone. He insists it isn't punishment, it's just accurate. My other cousin says that's monstrous, that every human being has the same worth regardless of what they've done or not done, and that Sipho is dressing up resentment as bookkeeping.

Here's the part that messes with me: I don't think Sipho is just being cruel, and I don't think my grandmother would have either. She was very deliberate about which names she said out loud, and she used to say something to the effect that you're only properly gone once the last person who knew you stops saying your name. If that's roughly the picture — if being one of us is a thing that lives in other people and can thin out and run dry — then I don't see how it stays all-or-nothing, and Sipho looks like he's just drawing the obvious conclusion. But then I also can't see how to stop the argument where he wants it to stop. Daniel has a daughter, mid-thirties, never met any of us, who turned up last week wanting the service; on Sipho's own logic she's even less of a member than Daniel was, and she's the one who'd be doing the remembering going forward. And Sipho spent four years abroad barely calling his own mother, so I don't know why the standard doesn't apply to him too, or to the neighbour who found the body and knew him better in the last five years than we did.

So: is Sipho's claim actually coherent, or does it quietly depend on things he'd refuse elsewhere? What would have to be true about people for membership to work the way he says, and does the same view give him anything he actually wants — like the idea that we should keep saying Daniel's name at all? And practically, what do I say on Sunday? The thing everyone keeps dancing around is that there are two different questions tangled together, what Daniel was owed then and what we owe each other now, and I suspect Sipho is answering one with the other. Is there a version where we do the service and it isn't a lie, or would that be exactly the dishonesty he's accusing us of?
