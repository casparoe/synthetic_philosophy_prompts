Third-year aesthetics PhD, and I've gone over to Tolstoy. Not the Christianity so much as the mechanism: art is the deliberate transmission of a feeling the artist has actually had, infection is what makes a thing art at all, its degree (individuality, clearness, sincerity) sets the quality, and subject-matter gets judged seperately against the religious perception of the age. I also buy the sociology — that borrowing, imitation, effectfulness and diversion are the tell-tale marks of counterfeit art turned out by a professional caste cut off from everybody else.

My supervisor thinks I've mistaken a pamphlet for an argument. So what would actually count against the view? Ranked — fatal, serious, merely awkward — and say which ones a Tolstoyan should concede rather than explain away. Specifically: (a) the self-sealing worry, since infection is supposed to be evident to anyone whose taste isn't already perverted, and whether that's genuinely different from how we treat a trained ear or a native speaker's judgements; (b) what reception evidence across classes and cultures would threaten the universality claim instead of just getting re-described as further perversion; (c) whether those four marks fit commercial mass culture far better than the elite art Tolstoy was aiming at, and if so whether that rescues the theory or wrecks it; (d) anything the view can't describe at all — non-programmatic music, work whose force isn't emotional — as opposed to describes badly. And does it risk anything, or is the insulation harmless for a theory of this sort?
