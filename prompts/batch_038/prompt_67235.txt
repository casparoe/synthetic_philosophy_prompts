I am a second-year M.A. student in philosophy, and my department runs a competitive summer research grant: ten weeks, a modest stipend, and an expectation that the funded student present the result at the department's graduate workshop in the fall. The application is a form with fixed sections and word limits. I would like your help drafting it. Please write in American English.

My seminar this term has been on the ethics of historical memory — Margalit on why remembering can be a duty, and Rieff on the political case for forgetting — and I want to build the project around a gap I think both leave. Assume I know both well enough to summarize them; what I need is help turning a half-formed suspicion into a project that a committee would fund.

Roughly, the suspicion is this. Margalit's account of duties of memory seems to presuppose a community of remembrance that persists: people standing to one another in the kind of thick relation that makes memory something they hold jointly and something they can be asked to keep alive. Rieff's skepticism, as I read it, is aimed at institutionalized remembrance inside a durable political relationship, where two living communities' memories of the same events can be mobilized against one another indefinitely. But there are cases in which neither presupposition holds. A group destroyed or dispersed as a community within a generation, so that no one remains who stands in thick relations to the dead and no adversary remains to be inflamed. Victims whose only surviving trace is administrative documentation produced by those who harmed them. I keep coming back to Margalit's contrast between memory a community holds together and memory that survives only as documents, and I suspect the second kind is where the interesting question lives: in those cases, what is the duty of memory, whose is it, and what could discharge it? My instinct is that the answer is neither that the duty lapses nor that it transfers intact to humanity at large, but I cannot yet say anything more precise than that, and I do not want the application to pretend otherwise.

Please draft the form to fit these sections, for a total of roughly 1,300 words:

1. Statement of the research question (200 words maximum). State it so that it has a determinate answer rather than being a topic heading.

2. Why the question is open (400 words maximum). What in the two positions I have described leaves it unsettled — and, just as important, the strongest objection to my own framing: the best available case that one of these authors already has the resources to answer the question, so the committee can see that I have tried to break my own idea rather than protect it.

3. Method and plan. What I would actually do, week by week, over ten weeks. Tell me which of these you would choose and why: a conceptual analysis of the structure of the duty; a close reconstruction of one author's argument and its presuppositions; a case study of a particular destroyed or dispersed community; or some hybrid. Say explicitly what I should cut if the analysis turns out to be more demanding than the summer allows, and where in the schedule the cutting happens.

4. Deliverable and criteria of success. I am imagining a paper of roughly 8,000 words for the graduate workshop, reworked afterward into a journal article. Say what the finished thing has to show in order to count as a result rather than as a survey of the difficulty.

5. Risks. An honest account of how this could fail. One failure mode already worries me: that the project turns out to show only that these are ordinary moral duties owed to the victims of serious wrongdoing, with nothing specific to memory left over — in which case I will have spent a summer establishing that the topic label was decorative. Tell me whether that worry is fatal, and what would have to be true for the project to escape it.

No bibliography or citation list is needed; the form does not ask for one and I will handle references myself. Please do not spend any of the word count telling me what to read. If you think the question is mis-framed, or that the gap I have described is not real, say so plainly before you begin and tell me how to repair it — then write the proposal on the repaired version rather than on mine.
