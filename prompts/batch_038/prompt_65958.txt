Our reading group is dissolving after fifteen years -- seven of us, mostly living rooms, one bad year over email. Two are moving, so we voted to end it, and I've been asked to give the closing remarks at the last meeting, six minutes or so. The partners and two teenagers will be there, so it can't turn into a seminar.

The thing that has to carry it: for a decade we argued about whether a group can believe something none of its members believe, and in 2019 we accidently settled it. We voted premise by premise on whether to sign a short joint statement about a book we'd all read; it went up on the group's little website under all seven names, committed to a conclusion a majority of us rejected and still reject. Nobody has managed to get it retracted, so it's officially our view to this day. Also worth knowing: there was never a single meeting all seven of us individually wanted to attend, and we went anyway, because we had decided to. Two of us work on this stuff and will catch any fudge; the rest just don't want to be bored.

Write the speech. The hard constraint is that I can't attribute to "us" anything that isn't actually ours -- no invented unanimity, no "we all felt" -- but I also can't dissolve the group into seven separate friendships, or there's nothing left to mourn. And it should get somewhere on what happens to that orphaned commitment at midnight. Pick a definite answer about what a group's beliefs and intentions are and let that answer decide what the speech is allowed to say -- don't survey the options and leave me holding them. Then straight after, in the same plain register, a short paragraph telling me which line you took and where you bent the ideas to make it land. Continuous prose throughout: no headings, bullets, bold, or tables, just the way you'd write a letter.
