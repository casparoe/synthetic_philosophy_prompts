Setup. The old evidence problem: my agent already has credence 1 in E (call it the perihelion data, doesn't matter), then along comes theory T which deductively implies E, and strict conditionalization says C(T) can't budge at all, since conditioning on something you're already certain of is a no-op. The fix I keep running into is to say the agent shouldn't re-condition on E, she should condition on the entailment claim itself — let A be "T deductively implies E" — because that's the proposition she actually learned. Call it the entailment relay.

Arithmetically it goes through if you grant the premises. Say C(T)=0.2, C(E)=1, and we allow C(A)=0.6. Then T∧A is just T∧E, which given C(E)=1 has probability 0.2, so C(T|A) = 0.2/0.6 ≈ 0.33. Nice boost. But here's my worry, and its what I want your number on: if her credences obey the ordinary probability constraints and she's certain of E, then E logically entails A, so she should already have C(A)=1 and the relay delivers nothing. The trick only works if you let an agent hold C(E)=1 and C(A)=0.6 at the same time, i.e. if you drop logical omniscience — and then I can't see which constraints are still supposed to bind, or why 0.6 is anything other than a number you picked because it makes the answer come out right.
