ok so I need a consistency check on my own head. Got into an argument at the weekend and my friend's parting shot was that my position is incoherent, so I've written it all out to see whether she's actually got a point. Here's what I hold about religion as a natural phenomenon:

1. The cognitive-science picture is basically right. We've got a hair-trigger agency detector that misfires and finds minds behind weather and rustling bushes; god-stories catch on because they're the right kind of weird — mostly ordinary, with a couple of violations that make them memorable; and synchronised, expensive ritual is what actually glues a group together. I dont think this is a hand-wavy sketch, I think it genuinely explains stuff.

2. But how a belief got into your head tells you nothing about whether it's true. That's the genetic fallacy and I won't commit it. If someone argues that my agency detector explains religion and therefore there's no God, I think they've made a mistake, and I say so.

3. Except my own main reason for not being religious is that once you've got (1) there's nothing divine left over to explain. The natural account is sufficient, and I treat that sufficiency as a reason to think the gods aren't there. I'd insist that's an inference to the best explanation rather than the genetic fallacy, but I'll admit it sounds like I'm having it both ways.

4. I also think religious belief could in principle be warranted with no evidence at all — properly basic, produced by some natural capacity for it — I just think in this case the capacity is misfiring.

5. Religion began as a by-product rather than an adaptation, but it stuck around because groups that had it out-competed groups that didn't.

6. And I still practise. Parish church most Sundays, communion, the whole liturgical year, and I dont think its dishonest or self-deceiving, because what I get out of it — the people, the moral formation, the year having a shape — doesn't depend on any of the doctrines being true.

7. Though I do accept the costly-signalling part of (1): the group coheres because the commitments are visibly expensive and sincerely meant, and a congregation that knows it's only playing at it won't get those benefits.

So — which of these pull against each other, and how badly? Rank them by severity rather than giving me a flat list, because I want to know which one I have to abandon first. Say explicitly which pairs only look like they clash and why they don't. Then give me the single revision that would clean up the most, and what it'd cost me, including whether I get to keep going to church on (6). No reading lists, no citations and no name-dropping papers please, I can't look anything up — I just want the audit. British English, if you don't mind.
