So I run a food bank type operation, not a church one, a community one. About 35 people, mix of paid staff and volunteers, two vans, a walk-in freezer, a big whiteboard in the hallway, we push maybe four thousand meals a week. Last month a friend of mine who reads way more economics than I do started telling people we're a working refutation of what he calls the socialist calculation debate. He says it at our Monday huddles, like we've proven Mises and Hayek wrong on their own turf, because we allocate vans and freezer space and volunteer shifts with zero prices, just a spreadsheet and people talking to each other, and everybody gets fed. His bigger claim is that a market is only a special case of a big enough spreadsheet, so central planning could work the same way if it was run by people who actually cared, and he wants me to say so publicly because apparently a real food operation saying it would carry weight.

Here's the wrinkle I think he's skipping over and I cant tell if it kills his argument or secretly helps it. We were doing badly. Produce was going off, the freezer was always fought over, one van got used by everybody and the other sat unused for weeks. So about six months ago we started charging our own teams fake internal money, twenty bucks for a van day, five bucks a week per freezer shelf, that kind of thing. Teams earn the credits by hitting delivery targets and spend them to reserve stuff, nobody ever cashes them out, the numbers just live in the spreadsheet. Waste is down like a third and the van fights stopped. My friend says that's beside the point, its just bookkeeping, not a market, because no real money moves and nobody can go buy groceries with a shelf credit. But from where I'm sitting the thing that fixed us was... inventing prices. Which sounds like it cuts against him, and when I said that he got kind of annoyed and said I was proving his point instead, which I dont follow.

Can you audit his analogy properly rather than just picking a side? I want the correspondence table laid out first, what in our setup plays the central planning board, the market prices, the profit signal, the capital goods, the consumers who vote with their feet, and then where the mapping breaks. Be honest about which breaks actually matter for his claim and which are just interesting but leave it standing, because right now everything gets lumped together. Then tell me what the internal credit system does to the case: is it evidence the calculation problem is real, evidence it's solvable, or neither, and what would we have to change about it for it to be one or the other. And if you think his analogy is just the wrong shape entirely, say what better one describes what we actually do, and whether that better one still supports what he wants it to support. Answer as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no introduction and no conclusion.
