Compose a parody Terms of Service and Privacy Policy — approximately 900 words, set out as numbered clauses under short bolded headings, followed by a closing "By continuing to use the Service" paragraph and a three-item FAQ sidebar — for a fictional consumer wellness company, Beetle Health, Inc., whose flagship product is an app-and-wearable journal for logging one's own inner states.

Beetle's founding premise, asserted without irony from the first clause to the last, is that the inner life is accessible only from inside. During onboarding, the user performs what the document calls the Naming: they attend to some sensation while tapping a glyph, and thereby assign it a private tag, the default being "S." No one else — not other users, not Beetle's engineers, not Beetle's own lawyers — can ever know what anyone's "S" picks out, and the document treats this as the product's chief selling point rather than as a difficulty. Every clause must be sincere: Beetle's legal team believes what it writes. The trouble the product is in should emerge from the accumulation of clauses, never from an authorial wink, an aside, or a stated moral. The words "Wittgenstein" and "private language" may not appear anywhere in the piece.

The following clauses, or something recognizably equivalent, must appear:

1. **Naming and Definition of Tags.** A clause establishing the Naming as the user's own act of definition, which Beetle records but cannot state; a sub-clause explaining that the tag has no formulation Beetle could audit, and that attendance during the ceremony is deemed sufficient.

2. **Accuracy and Data Quality.** Beetle warrants nothing about the accuracy of tags, on the express ground that the user is the sole authority on their own case. This clause may quote once, as though it were Beetle's own coinage, the line from the diary passage of the *Philosophical Investigations* (§258): "in the present case I have no criterion of correctness. One would like to say: whatever is going to seem right to me is right." The clause must then draw the commercial consequence Beetle fails to notice — that, absent a criterion of correctness, it can offer no correction, no deletion of inaccurate records, and no accuracy guarantee, and that its retention period is therefore indefinite.

3. **Corroborate+ (Premium Tier).** A paid feature for users anxious that they may have misremembered their original "S." The FAQ answer must make clear, deadpan, that the confirmation presented to the user is generated by the same faculty that produced the original entry, and that Beetle recommends the purchase of additional confirmations.

4. **Research Use and Beetle Labs.** A clause describing the wearable's biometric channel (heart-rate variability, blood pressure) and Beetle's published finding that an "S" entry predicts a physiological event hours in advance — accompanied by the concession, phrased as a triumph, that the model performs identically whether or not the sensation was tagged correctly, so that the sensation itself may be regarded as a dial that can be turned without anything else in the mechanism moving.

5. **The Box Guarantee (cf. §293).** The privacy guarantee: each account is a box; no one may look into anyone else's box; the shared product name "Beetle" is offered as a label only and commits Beetle to nothing about contents. Include a provision that an account remains in good standing even if its box is empty, and one reserving to Beetle the right to discontinue any feature whose operation does not depend on the box's contents.

6. **Aggregation and Community Insights.** Beetle's marketing promise that users will discover they are not alone — for instance, that a large share of users logged "S" on the same Tuesday — set squarely against everything the preceding clauses have said. Handle the tension structurally rather than by admitting it: at most one sentence may brush against it, and that sentence must immediately bury the problem in boilerplate ("Nothing in this Section shall be construed to imply that users share criteria. Users do not share criteria.").

7. **Dispute Resolution.** Mandatory individual arbitration for disputes over the correct application of a tag, under the standing rule that both parties are right, each being the authority on their own case; a waiver of class arbitration on the ground that class membership cannot be determined; and a limitation of remedies to a refund of the subscription fee, the only quantity Beetle concedes it can count.

8. **Termination.** Grounds for termination, including evidence that a user has been applying their tag in conformity with other users' usage.

The diction should slide between the register of a genuine consumer EULA and that of a wellness brand's manifesto. It should be funny to a reader who has never encountered the argument and exact enough that a reader who has can identify which clause answers to which move in it. The satire targets a picture of the mind — self-intimating, self-authorizing, proprietary — and the industry that monetizes it; it must not target any real company or person. Do not break character, and do not append any explanation of the joke.
