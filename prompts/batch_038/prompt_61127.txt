I defend a relational account of personhood, of the sort expressed by the Southern African maxim usually rendered in English as "a person is a person through people." As I hold it, the view has three parts: personhood comes in degrees rather than all-or-nothing; it is constituted relationally, by taking part in reciprocal communal life, both conferring and receiving; and it can be diminished or forfeited through one's own wrongdoing. Where is this view exposed? I want the considerations that should actually move me, not a survey of who has said what.

In particular, address: the cases the theory struggles to describe — the infant with profound and permanent cognitive impairment, the irreversibly unconscious patient, the self-sufficient hermit, the outsider and the enemy who stand beyond the community, non-human animals, and generations not yet born; the charge that a scalar account makes standing hostage to a community's recognition, so that whoever a community excludes is thereby demoted, and that forfeiture licenses treatment of offenders a rights framework forbids; the collapse worry, that grounding personhood in a *capacity* for relationship is an individualist capacity theory in relational dress, while grounding it in *actual* recognition is something else again — tell me which horn is worse; and the historical objection that this ethic is substantially a modern political reconstruction rather than a lived tradition, together with your judgment of whether that touches the theory's truth or only its pedigree. Say also what the view predicts that binary, individualist rivals do not, and whether any of those predictions are observably false.

Sort the defeaters by severity, from view-ending to merely inconvenient, and for each say whether a committed relational theorist would concede it as counting against them or dismiss it as question-begging, and why. Then judge whether the theory has been padded with qualifications until nothing could tell against it — and whether that is a failing here or a legitimate feature of a framework-level view.

Please use these headings in this order: "What the view risks" (what each of the three commitments separately entails that rivals deny; ~200 words), "The defeaters, ranked" (~800 words, each with its severity and what would make it decisive), "Concede or dismiss" (~300 words), "Insulated?" (~250 words), and "Bottom line" (the single consideration I should weigh most, and what would change my mind; ~150 words).
