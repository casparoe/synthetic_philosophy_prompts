My brother-in-law and I have been going in circles since Thanksgiving about whether eating animals is defensible, and I want a third party. He's the vegan, I'm not, and at some point he built this argument that I'm pretty sure is bad but I can't locate the bad step. His version, as near as I can write it out:

1. What gives a creature moral weight is the mental stuff it has — pain, self-awareness, memory, relationships. Everyone in this debate already accepts that.
2. You can't measure the mental stuff directly, least of all in an animal that's already dinner, so you measure the physical thing that tracks it. Brain mass is the one anybody can actually put on a scale.
3. If brain mass is your best available measure of the thing that matters, then differences in brain mass just are differences in moral weight. Anything else is saying the ruler is fine but the length doesn't count.
4. A sperm whale's brain runs five or six times heavier than a person's. An elephant's, three or four.
5. So whales and elephants have more moral weight than people.
6. You're going to say that's absurd, but notice you only say that once the ruler stops putting you on top. Which means the whole rank-animals-by-capacity project was never about capacities. Its a way of dressing up "we win" as a measurement.

I've pushed back with the brain-to-body-size stuff, and with a claim I read that an elephant actually has more neurons in total than we do but nearly all of them sit in the cerebellum, so it depends which part you count. He says both moves prove his point: every time the metric stops flattering us we change the metric, and the changing is the tell.

That last bit is what I cant answer, because I have a feeling it should turn around and bite him too and I can't make it work. So: which step do you reject? I'd rather lose 3 than 1, but tell me what giving up 3 actually costs me — and whether his "you keep swapping rulers" reply is a real objection or one that eats his own position as well.
