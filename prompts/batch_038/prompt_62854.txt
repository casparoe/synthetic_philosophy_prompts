I am a junior in high school, and I got into an argument during class last week that I could not resolve on my own. I would like a careful explanation of the conceptual issue underneath it, because I think the argument went badly for reasons nobody in the room could name.

The topic was whether race is a biological category. One student said that race is socially constructed. Another replied that if it is socially constructed, then it is not real, in the same way that dragons are constructed and are not real. I objected that this must be wrong, because money is also socially constructed and nobody says money is not real: it plainly does things, it changes how people behave, and a particular piece of paper functions as a twenty-dollar bill. A third student then turned my own example against me, pointing out that the money case seems to make things worse rather than better, since a bill is only a bill because we treat it as one, which arguably makes it less real than a mountain, which would still be a mountain even if every person forgot about it. By the end I could not say what "constructed" was supposed to mean, or what "real" was supposed to mean, and the class moved on.

I would like an explanation that gets to the bottom of this. Please assume I am a careful reader but that I have not read any philosophy, so define any technical term the first time you use it. I am not looking for a survey of what different thinkers have said, and please do not include a reading list or references; I want the reasoning itself. If there is a standard name in the literature for the feedback phenomenon I describe in the fourth section below, I would like to know it, but naming it is not the main point.

Please organize your response in exactly these sections, in this order:
