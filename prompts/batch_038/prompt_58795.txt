I'm running a two-hour session with undergrads on Hempel's ravens and I'd rather hand them a decision tree than give a lecture. Entry point: someone who's just been told a white shoe confirms that all ravens are black and who finds that unaceptable. Every node has to be a question they can settle by working a small case — no node may name a philosopher, a doctrine, or a principle. The tree needs to land them on all of these: biting the bullet, dropping the equivalence condition, dropping Nicod, making confirmation relative to background knowledge, restricting what counts as a lawlike universal, and any leaf you think belongs there that I haven't listed. Three cases must show up somewhere as nodes: (a) "all my socks are blue," where I own nine pairs and could just check the drawer; (b) a subject term that's empty, so Nicod is vacuous but the contrapositive still bites; (c) a Good-style background where knowing there's a lot of non-black stuff around flips the direction of the evidence. Then tell me which answer-combinations are logically available but have no defenders, and whether that's because they're incoherent or just unfashionable — and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate. Tree first, no preamble or restating the task; notes and the empty-cell diagnosis after. Give leaves their standard names where they have one, but don't guess at attributions.
