The essay question was:

"Historians increasingly write about non-human causes — pathogens, climate variability, volcanic eruptions — as though they were actors in history. Is this way of talking merely figurative? In what sense, if any, can a non-human cause be a historical actor, and does granting it that status commit a historian to environmental determinism? Distinguish at least two senses in which the determinism charge has been pressed, and say what turns on the distinction. 1,500 words max."

Here's the answer, unedited:

---

The claim that germs, climates or volcanoes were actors in history can be taken in two senses, and most of the debate consists of sliding between them. In a minimal sense an actor is anything that makes a difference to how events unfold and whose contribution cannot be recovered from a description of what people intended. In a maximal sense an actor is an agent: it has intentions, it responds to reasons, its behaviour can be understood as aimed at something. Yersinia pestis is an actor in the minimal sense and not in the maximal one, and neither is the Tambora eruption of 1815 or the run of bad harvests that followed it. So the maximal claim is false and the minimal one is uncontroversial, and the interesting work lies elsewhere.

The interesting work is in the determinism charge, which also comes in two forms. The first is modal. An account is deterministic in this sense if it treats the outcome as necessary given the environmental inputs: given the drought, the harvest fails; given the harvest failure, the regime goes. That is a fair description of much early twentieth-century climate history, which ranked civilisations by temperature and rainfall and presented the ranking as a finding rather than a hypothesis. It is not a fair description of work being done now, and the reason is methodological. Current environmental history trades in proxies, ensembles, error bars and competing scenarios; what it claims is that a volcanic forcing made a run of harvest failures substantially more likely, or that a monsoon anomaly raised the probability of dearth in one region and not another. Determinism requires necessity and statistics does not supply necessity, so the charge has been dissolved by the methods.

The second form of the charge is political, and here the statistical point does no work at all. Famines are the clearest case. Sen's entitlement argument showed that a food availability decline and a famine are different things, and that the second depends on who could command food, which is a question about wages, prices, relief policy and the legal entitlements people actually held. Once you have that distinction, an account that explains a colonial famine by rainfall alone is not merely incomplete, it is doing something: it relocates a death toll from a policy to the weather. This is the real peril of environmental determinism, and it is not that the claims are false but that they are the wrong shape to hang responsibility on.

Diamond is usually brought in here and usually brought in wrongly. Guns, Germs, and Steel explains the conquest of the Americas by the fact that Europeans carried pathogens against which they had acquired resistance and Americans did not, so the result was settled before anybody decided anything. On that account the environment, in the form of the germs it generated, does the causal work and the conquistadors are its instruments. That is determinism in the strict sense and it is why the book is criticised for having no people in it. It is also why the political form of the charge bites: if Cortés is an instrument of epidemiology, the dispossession that followed stops being something anyone did.

Which suggests how to answer the question. Non-human causes are actors in the minimal sense, which is the only sense that carries explanatory weight, and granting them that status commits no one to determinism. Determinism is a thesis about necessity, and the environmental explanations worth having are probabilistic ones about which outcomes were made more likely. What has to be watched is not the modality of the claim but its placement: an account becomes determinist when it puts the environmental variable where the political, economic or intentional one belongs, and it stays honest when it keeps both in the same explanation and says which is doing what at which level.
