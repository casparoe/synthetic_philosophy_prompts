Here's my opening move:

The standard answer to cluelessness is what I'll call the washout reply. Sure, your act ripples out into the far future in ways you can't trace, but those ripples are indepedent of which option you pick, so they add the same expected value to every option and cancel when you compare. Only the local, visible difference does any real decision-guiding work. I think that reply needs something it doesn't have. Independence fails for exactly the acts worth agonizing over, because those acts change *who exists* -- a vaccination drive, a resettlement policy, a war, they shift conception timing, and once the people downstream are different, comparing the two futures isn't a matter of summing the same individuals' welfare under two scenarios. It requires ranking whole histories with different populations, which requires a population axiology you haven't got, and not because you're ignorant of the facts -- because there's no settled answer to whether a bigger happy future beats a smaller one. So the far-future term doesn't wash out. It's not even signless-but-bounded, it's undefined. And then your comparison collapses into "both options undefined," which means the washout reply isnt a modest fallback you retreat to, it's the load-bearing wall, and it's not available.
