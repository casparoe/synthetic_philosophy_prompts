Second-year philosophy PhD, writing a dissertation chapter on pain, so I know the asymbolia literature reasonably well — the case reports where people localize and grade a stimulus, sometimes call it painful outright, and just aren't bothered by it, will leave a hand on a hot plate while chatting to you. What I've been failing to find is whether there's a name for what looks to me like the mirror image of that, except that it isn't a clinical population at all, it's ordinary people, and it doesn't seem to get discussed on its own anywhere.

Here's the pattern I keep running into. Someone does something painful on purpose, repeatedly, and says two things that both sound completely sincere: that it was awful while it was happening, they hated every second; and that they're going back for more, not because of anything that comes afterwards. Not the runner's high, not the medal, not the story, not desensitization — I ask, and they specifically deny those. A friend of mine swims in the sea all winter and describes the first ninety seconds as agony and describes wanting it, and when I pushed her she said the wanting wasn't about what came after, it was about the ninety seconds themselves. Same shape with the chili people, the tattoo people, the cold-plunge people, though with those it often does seems to be about the afterglow, which is why the winter swimmer is my clean case.

The part that puzzles me is that this doesn't look like a trade across time — the endure-now-benefit-later structure everyone assumes. It looks like one and the same episode, at one and the same moment, being disvalued by the person undergoing it and wanted by that same person. The asymbolic case has a name and a whole debate hanging off it about whether unpleasantness is separable from the sensory quality. This one just seems to get absorbed into talk of endurance, or into benign masochism, which as far as I can tell is a different thing — there you like the sensation because you've judged the body isn't actually in danger, so the concurrent aversion is what gets overcome, not what gets kept.

So: is there an established concept for the mirror-image dissociation, genuine concurrent disliking plus a global wanting that isn't instrumental? I know the obvious reply is that I'm equivocating between the pain and the activity, and that's the objection I most need help with, because if that defuses it then I want to know that too. I'd want the nearest neighbors, where each one stops fitting, and if nothing fits, a name and a definition with the edges marked — what counts, what doesn't, what would make a case ambiguous rather than clean. I care a lot less about the label than about whether this is a real category or a confusion I've talked myself into.
