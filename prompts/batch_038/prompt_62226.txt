I cover science-and-ideas stuff for a magazine and I'm trying to work out when a bug, a volcano or a drought earns the right to be called a historical actor. I keep handing out these verdicts on instinct and they dont hang together.

Tambora, 1815: yes, an actor. The following year's ruined harvests, the typhus, Mary Shelley's ghost story — the volcano belongs in that chapter and nobody flinches.

The Black Death in England: yes. Surviving laborers get leverage, wages move, Parliament tries to legislate the old rates back into place. I'd write the bacillus straight into a political history.

The same die-off east of the Elbe, where the lords clamped down harder and serfdom tightened instead: no. There I want to say the plague explains nothing — it was grain markets and lordly power. Same microbe, opposite verdict, and I can't tell you why.

Yellow fever wrecking Leclerc's expedition in Saint-Domingue: yes, but reluctantly. I have a nagging feeling that crediting the mosquitoes takes something away from the people who actually beat the French.

The 2006–10 Syrian drought that people keep citing as a cause of the civil war: no. I want to reject that one out of hand, even though it's a climate event with big political consequences, just like Tambora.

The 1918 flu: no. It killed more than the war did and left almost no trace in the political history of the interwar years, so it doesn't go in the book — even though it obviously changed what everyone could do.

Potato blight in Ireland: I want to say the blight is an actor and simultaneously that the famine wasn't caused by the blight. I know how that sounds.

My tentative rule is: a non-human thing counts when it changed what humans could do, and never when it's invoked to explain why they did what they did. I think that breaks on 1918 and on Syria, so take a swing at it. Then give me the principle that actually captures the pattern — two or three candidates, each with the case that motivates its clauses — and say what your favorite one predicts about cases I haven't listed, like malaria at the Panama Canal or the Little Ice Age and the seventeenth-century revolts. Be blunt about which of my gut calls is indefensible and has to go, and about whether the Syria/Tambora pair can be saved at all.

Last line, please: Verdict: followed by the single principle you'd settle on, in one sentence.
