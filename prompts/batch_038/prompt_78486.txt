We read Taurek early on and my friend in the seminar is convinced she's just a straight-up Taurekian, but I don't buy it, she's too careful for that. Here's what she's said: (1) Boat case — one person drowning on the near shore, five on the far shore, your boat reaches only one group: you may go either way, a coin flip is at least allowed, and if you let the one die you haven't wronged them; when someone pushed back with "but it's five lives" she said something like the five don't get to add themselves together. (6) The one she won't fully explain: one stranger versus a whole town of about 12,000 about to be wiped out by a flood — she said of course you save the town, and when asked whether that's the same principle with bigger numbers she said no, at some point they stop being numbers and start being a world. She wouldn't say where that point sits, and when someone asked whether 100 versus 1 was past it she said she'd have to think about it, which is extremely not like her.
