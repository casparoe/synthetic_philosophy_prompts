Arendt wrote about political lying mainly twice — once in a sixties essay on truth and politics, once in a piece on the Pentagon Papers — and the two are doing different jobs: the first asks why factual truth is so fragile in politics generally, the second asks what a particular government actually got up to.
2. Her starting point is that lying is nothing new: truthfulness, she says, has never counted among the political virtues, and lies have always been regarded as justifiable tools in political dealings.
3. What changed in the twentieth century is that governments stopped merely hiding facts and started unmaking them — the modern lie doesn't cover over something secret, it destroys the very thing it contradicts.
4. It can do that because a factual truth, unlike a logical or mathematical one, carries no necessity with it; whatever happened could always have happened otherwise, and that gap is precisely where the liar sets up shop.
5. This is also why the liar starts out ahead of the truth-teller: he knows the facts and can build a story that is tidier and more appealing than the mess they really make, which is why lies travel faster and stick harder than the truth does.

She holds that lying about the past is the worst kind, since the past is fixed and, in a bureaucratic state, documented, whereas lying about the future is almost respectable — there are no facts about it yet, and the liar's "things will be otherwise" is close to what the statesman is claiming when he acts.
7. The Pentagon Papers confirmed all of it: they revealed an administration that had spent years successfully rewriting the record of the war for the American public, and that only came apart when the documents got out.
8. Arendt's conclusion is the bleak one — that once every institution has an image to manage and every fact is up for arguement, nothing is so well established that it can't be argued away.
9. So the real damage isn't done to truth as such but to the common world people share, and that's a loss no amount of fact-checking repairs.
