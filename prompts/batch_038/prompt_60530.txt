Our department's internal research fund has one round left this year, and the award amounts to a single semester of teaching relief — realistically enough for one article of about eight thousand words and a departmental seminar, not for a monograph or an edited volume. I work in philosophy of biology and want to pitch a project on Popper's shifting verdict about natural selection, though not in the form the topic usually takes. My half-formed idea is this. Popper's short 1974 piece in Nature classed natural selection as a metaphysical research programme — fruitful, guiding, but not seriously testable and close to tautological — while the lecture he delivered at Darwin College, Cambridge in November 1977, published in Dialectica the following year, opens by announcing that he is recanting his earlier view of the theory's testability and logical status. The two standard readings of that reversal are either dismissive (he simply did not understand the biology and said so under pressure) or rehabilitative (work on the logic of fitness and adaptation gave him a genuine reason to revise). I suspect both miss what is actually doing the work, because Popper's demarcation criterion is not a free-standing instrument in his system: it is load-bearing for his evolutionary epistemology, the claim that scientific knowledge grows by the same generate-variants-and-eliminate-errors schema as organic evolution, differing chiefly in that a scientist can let his conjectures die in his stead, as he liked to put it when comparing Einstein to an amoeba. My hunch is that the recantation is driven by the needs of that epistemology rather than by the biology: if natural selection is only a metaphysical research programme, then the schema invoked to explain the growth of knowledge borrows its authority from something that fails Popper's own test for being science, and the analogy becomes circular. The sharp version of the hunch, which is what I want the proposal built around, treats the selection schema as an abstract template with separately stateable conditions — that variants are generated, that they are differentially retained, that retention is transmitted to later variants, and, decisively, that the bearer of an eliminated variant is itself eliminated, so that elimination is of a lineage and not merely of a representation. Call this last condition substrate non-identity. Popper's demarcation argument seems to require it, since Darwinian selection is selection by death and differential reproduction; his epistemology explicitly prides itself on dispensing with it, since the whole point of critical discussion is that the organism survives the refutation of the theory. If that is right, the two projects cannot share one schema, and the 1978 recantation is best read as an attempt to raise natural selection to the status of a testable, law-like principle so that the epistemology no longer borrows warrant from the unscientific — an attempt that cannot succeed, because it leaves the two uses of the schema pulling in opposite directions on exactly that condition.

Please turn this into the proposal document itself. I need a statement of the research question precise enough that a reader could tell whether it had been answered; an account of why the two standard readings of the recantation leave that question open rather than merely disagreeing about it; the method, spelled out phase by phase across the semester, including how the abstract schema gets formalised or at least regimented, which of Popper's own illustrations would be run through it as test cases (the amoeba, the animal learning by trial and error, the Einsteinian conjecture, and his treatment of fitness and adaptation), and what a decisive finding — textual or purely conceptual — would look like; a defence of this method against the obvious alternatives, namely intellectual biography, a reconstruction of the fitness-tautology debate on its own terms, and the attempt to settle once and for all whether Darwinism is falsifiable, which is not my question; a description of what a successful result would be; and a candid assessment of scope that names the specific temptations to expand (the three-worlds ontology and the rest of the World 3 apparatus, the broader adaptationism controversies of the 1970s, the later evolutionary-epistemology literature) and says where I would cut. Be explicit about failure rather than burying it: I have at least two ways the project could collapse in mind, and I want the proposal to state them — first, that the schema turns out so permissive that every one of Popper's examples satisfies it trivially, in which case the conditions do no work and I have merely relabelled a familiar tension; second, that the recantation can be fully explained by arguments internal to the fitness literature with nothing left over for my epistemological story, in which case the article should not be written at all. Say which of the two is the live risk and what early diagnostic in the first six weeks would reveal it.

Two practical constraints. First, no bibliography and no literature review by citation — I will supply references myself; you may name texts, positions, and arguments where you are confident, and you should flag anywhere you think my reconstruction of Popper's reasoning or of the chronology is shaky, or where a title, date, or attribution would need checking before it goes in front of a committee. Second, keep the proposal to roughly two pages of prose with clear headings, written for a mixed panel that includes at least one working biologist who has never read Popper and one Popperian who has not read four decades of philosophy of biology; the pitch has to convince both that this is a real question with a determinate answer and that one semester of relief is enough to answer it.
