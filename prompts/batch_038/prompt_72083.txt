So here's my situation. I'm a novelist — I just finished a book set in the Rudolfine court at Prague in the first decade of the 1600s, and Kepler is one of the secondary characters, a guy who's up all night with Tycho's observation logs and down at the tavern casting nativities for whoever will pay. The book comes out in the spring and my publicist has lined me up for a run of author events, and one of them is not a bookshop. It's a planetarium's adult sky-talk series, thirty minutes, a lectern and a whiteboard, no slides, audience of maybe sixty amateur astronomers — star-party people, retired engineers, the crowd that can tell you what an ellipse is without being asked. They'll arrive already knowing the three laws and already having a settled opinion about the astrology part, which is basically that it was the embarrassing day job that funded the real science, a hustle he did for emperors and generals because it was a hustle or nothing. The one idea I want them to leave with, and it's the thing my novel is actually about, is that Kepler had a theory. He didn't just practice the inherited stuff and hope nobody looked closely. He took it apart, threw away most of it, and tried to rebuild what was left on the geometry of the circle, and the thing he kept is genuinely strange: not planets shoving your personality around, but something like souls — the Earth's soul, our souls — reading configurations that are objectively there in the geometry, the way you hear a consonance rather than get pushed by it.

I don't want you to explain any of that to me, I've spent two years in it. I want a plan for how I explain it to them in thirty minutes. Specifically: the order the pieces have to go in and what depends on what — my instinct is that the aspect/geometry material has to land before the soul-perception material makes any sense at all, but I may have that backwards, and I'd rather be told. Which single case should carry the whole thing, and why that one and not the others I keep circling: the horoscope he cast for Wallenstein, the new star of 1604 and his obsession with great conjunctions, the fact that one of the five books of the Harmony of the World is entirely devoted to astrology, his annual prognostications back in Graz, or his own birth data and the note where he works backward from being born to the moment he was conceived. Tell me what the case has to show and what it must not invite. Then the misconception map, in the order they'll surface: the day-job hustle line; the opposite error, which is turning him into a modern skeptic debunking from the inside; the one I'm most guilty of, the cliche that there was no distinction between astronomy and astrology back then so none of this means anything — I've said that in interviews and now I think it's a cop-out and I want to know how to un-say it; the idea that aspects are forces; and the question that will definitely come from the third row, which is whether he was ever right, and what I can say about that in thirty seconds that neither sneers at him nor lets the room off the hook. I'd also like to know what I should openly refuse to cover at this level — the musical theory, his whole ranking of aspects by which polygons are constructible (he adds the quintile and the 135-degree one and drops the rest, if I'm remembering that right), his mother's witchcraft trial — and how to say so without it sounding like I'm padding.

One last thing, and it's the part I can't judge from the inside. I'm a novelist, so my reflex is always to dramatize, to find the scene, and I can already hear myself doing the bit where the guy who defended the Copernican system is also being paid to tell a duke what month to sign a treaty in. Tell me where a scene-able moment would actually do explanatory work and where it would just be me being charming at the expense of the idea. And give me one question I can put to the room at the end — not any-questions, an actual question with a right answer — such that if two thirds of them get it wrong I'll know exactly which link in the chain snapped. No reading list, no bibliography, I've got names and dates covered; I just need the architecture.
