My opening move: mitochondrial replacement. Clinic takes the nuclear material from the intended parents, transfers it into a donor egg that's had its own nucleus removed, kid is born carrying the donor's mtDNA, and the press calls it a three-parent baby. If the egg is part of my origin, which part is essential -- the nucleus, the cytoplasm, the mitochondria, the whole egg as a lump? Say the whole egg and that kid has two origins with two different ancestries, and your principle can't tell me which of them does the individuating. And I'll grant you the frozen embryo case rather than make you fight it: same two gametes, combined in the dish, implanted five years later, still the same person on any sane view. But that concession is what worries me, because it means origin isnt a spacetime event for you. So it must be the matter. And the matter turns out to come in parts with different pedigrees -- this cytoplasm and these mitochondria trace back to a completely different woman, that nuclear material traces back to two other people. So pick a level and tell me why that level is the one that makes me me. (I'm also keeping the identical twins case in reserve: one zygote, two people, so origin doesn't even look like it individuates within a single origin.)
