A colleague of mine forecasts with ranges rather than single numbers, and I have run into two features of his practice that I cannot find words for.

The first: he was modelling a pair of events about each of which he was willing to commit to a sharp figure — chance of exactly a half, no interval at all — while remaining entirely uncommitted about whether the two tend to occur together or to occur apart. He then learned how the first event had turned out, news he had been expecting for days, and his figure for the second event dissolved from 0.5 into a range running from 0.25 to 0.75. He insists the arithmetic is right and that the news was not something he would previously have recognised as bearing on the question. So information arrives and precision is lost, and the starting point was not a wide interval but a point.

The second, and stranger: asked in advance whether he would like to be told, at no cost, he says he would rather not be, because after being told he can no longer settle on a course of action, whereas before he could.

Does either half of this have an established name? I know the general neighbourhood — agents who refuse to state a single probability, and people who will not bet on draws from an unknown composition — but what I am describing seems to be neither. What are the nearest concepts, how does each differ from this, and does the second phenomenon follow from the first or is it independent, perhaps turning on the rule he uses for converting a set of figures into a choice? If nothing covers the package, say so and propose a term. I am after the boundaries of the thing — where it stops being ordinary ignorance — rather than a label, so please draw them explicitly.
