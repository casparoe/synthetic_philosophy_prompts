Our school's philosophy lecture series brings in a visiting professor each spring, and this year's speaker defends a deflationary account of ontological disputes: roughly, that questions such as whether composite objects or numbers exist are verbal rather than substantive, because the quantifier admits more than one legitimate meaning, so each disputant can speak truly in her own idiolect. I'm a junior, and I've been asked to moderate the thirty-minute Q&A after her talk. Could you write eight to ten questions I could actually put to her, and for each, a one-sentence note on the reply she is most likely to give plus the follow-up that reply invites? I would like the list to cover at least the following: whether her thesis can be coherently stated at all, since stating it seems to require quantifying over meanings of the quantifier; whether her position collapses into the claim that any dispute whatsoever can be diagnosed as verbal, and what her criterion of substantivity is supposed to be; how she would answer the charge that stipulating a new meaning merely changes the subject and therefore settles nothing; how her view differs from Carnap's distinction between internal and external questions, and whether that difference is substantive; and whether she allows that some ontological questions really are non-verbal, and if so, which ones and why. Please leave out anything generic, such as how she came to the view or why metaphysics matters to non-philosophers. And if you think the way I've framed this is mistaken — for instance, if these five headings are really one objection in five costumes, or if a speaker simply cannot be pressed this way in a student Q&A — please say so plainly rather than padding the list to meet my number.
