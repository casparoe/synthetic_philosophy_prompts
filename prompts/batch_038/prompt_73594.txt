I'm not a college person — I service furnaces and heat pumps for a living — but on our town's select board there's this one member, Marlene, who basically decides anything with an "is this safe" question attached to it, and I've been to every meeting since 2019 and I keep notes. There's a big one coming up in the fall and I want to know whether it's worth spending my summer on it, so I'm trying to see if she's predictable. Her record as I've got it:

1) 2019, a cell tower about 300 feet from the elementary school. Parents brought a petition, she voted yes. Said nobody had shown her a mechanism. There was an alternate site 400 yards farther off for about 80 grand more and she said eighty grand isn't a reason to say no.

2) 2021, after the ice storm took the power out for six days and everybody was boiling water: a diesel generator with a fuel tank at the water plant. She voted yes, made them use a double-walled tank and sign a spill-response contract, and said "no generator is the bigger risk."

3) 2022, parks wanted to put a systemic insecticide in the elms for the beetle. She voted no. There's actual published work on that stuff and bees, the alternative — treating the trees one at a time and cutting the worst ones — costs about the same spread over a few years, and she said we don't need the elms that bad.

4) 2023, a startup wanted to test those little delivery robots on the sidewalks. She voted yes with conditions: insurance, a speed limit, and a permit revocable on a week's notice. "We can yank this whenever we want."

5) 2024, a gravel company wanted to blast closer to the wellfield. Their own hydrogeologist said the aquifer was fine and they offered a bond. She voted no — "a bond doesn't give you back an aquifer," and "if they're wrong they're not the ones drinking it." There was another parcel they could of used.

6) Last spring, fluoridating the water. Every dentist in town was for it, the cavity numbers are real, and the harm stories are thin. She voted no anyway: "you can decide whether to take a pill, you can't decide whether to drink the water." The schools already run a free varnish program.

Now the fall one. We had two West Nile cases last summer and one of them was a guy I'd done a job for, and he died. A company spun out of a university wants to release modified mosquitoes over the Great Marsh for three summers so the local population crashes, and the state has to permit it too. They're offering a bond and full indemnifaction, and they say the modification is built to fizzle out on its own after a few generations. Two snags. One, the marsh straddles the town line, so the town downwind of it gets no vote and never sees the agenda. Two, when a woman at the informational night asked what happens if it doesn't fizzle, their rep said there's a "reversal" version but admitted it exists only on paper — once they're out, they're out. One state ecologist says nothing eats enough mosquitoes for it to matter, another says the bats and the swallows are a genuine question mark. And the county already runs larvicide trucks all summer, which the health department says gets maybe two thirds of the breeding sites.

So: which way does she go, and how sure are you? Which of my six is the closest match, and what part of that match is doing the work? Mostly I want the rules. I'm pretty confident more than one rule fits all six, and I want them spelled out in plain language — what each one actually says and what it predicts for the mosquitoes. I also want the ones that look like they should fit but are already dead; like "she only cares whether there's evidence of harm" can't be it, because I can name the precedent that kills it, and I'd like to know what else dies the same way. And the town-line thing: nothing in my six resembles it, so if that's the feature that decides her vote, what is she committing the town to that she hasn't committed to before? Last thing — is there a case I could get onto the agenda, or a hypothetical I could float at a meeting, that would split the surviving rules cheaply, something where they'd be forced to disagree? I'm not after a lecture on the precautionary principle and I'm not going to read anything you point me to, so skip the sources, I just want your read on her.
