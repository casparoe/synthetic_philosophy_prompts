So my friend and I got into a two hour argument last night about collapse and I still haven't won it, which is annoying. His position: basically nothing ever "collapses." What collapses is the elite layer — palaces, kings, inscriptions, monument building — and the farmers underneath keep farming, so all the collapse talk is archaeologists mistaking the end of a royal inscription corpus for the end of a people. He thinks Rapa Nui in particular is a modern myth, and that the Late Bronze Age and the Classic Maya are just palace economies getting replaced by something else that was fine for everyone who mattered.

I don't buy it wholesale, but I also can't say he's wrong case by case, which is the real problem. So help me settle it with a ranking.

Here's what I want: rank these cases by how strongly the evidence supports genuinely bad stuff happening to ordinary, non-elite people. Not whether the state ended, not whether monuments stopped going up, but whether most of the population died off, got shorter and sicker, lost access to trade goods and specialized technology, got pushed onto worse land, or lived through sustained violence:

- Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean
- Classic Maya southern lowlands
- Rapa Nui / Easter Island, pre-European contact
- Cahokia and the American Bottom
- Chaco Canyon and the Ancestral Puebloan great houses
- Angkor and the Khmer lowlands
- Norse Greenland
- Great Zimbabwe
- Indus Valley deurbanization
- Roman Britain after the legions pulled out

Give it to me as a tier list, top tier meaning his objection basically fails there and bottom tier meaning he's right and the "collapse" was elite turnover plus a change in what happens to survive archaeologically. Next to each placement, give me two numbers out of 10: one for how bad things actually got for non-elites, and one for how confident you are in that first number given what the evidence base actually is. Those two numbers should disagree a lot in places, and I want you to say where and why.

Then a few more things:

- Two or three sentences per case on which kind of evidence is doing the work — bioarchaeology like stature, enamel hypoplasia, trauma markers, infant burials; settlement survey counts; paleoclimate proxies; texts — and be blunt about which cases we simply have no non-elite data for at all.
- A separate short ranking of the evidence types themselves: which one should count most when someone says "but the people were fine," and which is most likely to mislead you.
- Name the single case on the list where you'd most likely change your mind if one good new dataset turned up, and say what that dataset would be.
- And one number at the end: what fraction of our argument was actually about the world versus about the word collapse.

Don't give me a both-sides answer or a definitional shrug. Take a side on each case, tell me which of us is more right overall, and flag the spots where you're guessing because the record is thin. I can't definately tell whether he's just better at rhetoric than at evidence, so make the reasons load-bearing. American English, please.
