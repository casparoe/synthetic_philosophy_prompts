(1) Ravi: these users just haven't been shown the other side, full stop. Fix is exposure, and the numbers back him — dwell time on the outside sources went up for roughly a third of the cohort, and clicks into fact-check pages went up too. He calls it a plumbing problem, not a psychology problem.

(2) Nadia: some of them aren't in that situation at all. She brought up the distinction she says comes from Thi Nguyen — a bubble is where outside voices got left out, an echo chamber is where users have been actively taught to distrust outside voices. Chambers are supposed to resist exposure, or even be reinforced by it, because the injected content confirms the teaching. Her evidence: in the same experiment, for about a fifth of the cohort, sentiment in their posts got more negative, they rated the injected outlets less trustworthy at the end than at the start, and churn was higher. She says Ravi is averaging two structurally different populations and the mean is meaningless.

(3) Ravi: that backfire is an artifact of how we shipped it. We labeled the injected items, picked the outlets with a crude left/right score, and users could plainly tell the feed had been messed with. Distrust of an obvious clumsy intervention tells you nothing about echo chambers. Fix delivery, re-measure.
