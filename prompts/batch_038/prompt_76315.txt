I write reliability tooling, so half my job is probability × impact: rank the failure modes, fix the top of the list. It works great until the impact gets huge and the math starts telling me to spend the whole quarter on something with a one-in-a-million chance. Concrete version: we once argued for two weeks about building a failover path our model put at ~1-in-10,000 per year, where if it ever mattered it'd cost the company something like $40M. Half the room said multiply it out, obviously worth it. The other half said that's exactly how "expected value fanaticism" eats an engineering org, and the first half said fine, then give me a rule that isn't just vibes. Nobody could.

Before I read anything about it, I want the spec. What does a decision rule for low-probability / very-high-stakes choices have to deliver to count as adequate? I want the requirements list with the case behind each one — what it has to get right (mugging-style cases where a stranger offers astronomical payoffs for a small cost; cases where people who discounted tiny probabilities ended up dead or bankrupt; whether you're allowed to round small probabilities to zero without being exploitable across a sequence of decisions), the distinctions it can't collapse, and the results from probability and decision theory it can't contradict. Then say which requirements are hard constraints and which are just the taste of one camp, and whether some subset of them is jointly impossible — that's the bit I actually want. Also: which single requirement knocks out the most candidate rules.

Purpose, so adequacy isn't floating: decisions made by one agent or a small team, roughly once, with real money and real timelines — not civilizational bets on the far future. Add a line at the end on whether the spec would change if it were.

Sections in this order, with rough lengths: "Purpose and scope" (~100 words), "Requirements" (the bulk — numbered, each with its motivating case, ~700 words), "Hard constraints vs. camp preferences" (~150 words), "Where the list fights itself" (~200 words), "Most exclusionary requirement" (~100 words). No scorecard of existing views, I'll do that myself later.
