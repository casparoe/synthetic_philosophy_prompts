The question (10 marks, ~250 words): "Nietzsche contrasts the noble pairing good/bad with the slave pairing good/evil. Explain the difference in structure between the two, and say how the priestly revaluation manages to succeed."

The answer:

"For Nietzsche, noble valuation is self-referring. The noble type first names himself and his own kind good, and 'bad' comes afterwards as an almost incidental afterthought, picking out whatever is not like him — the common, the plebeian. Slave morality reverses the order: it starts with a no. Because the powerless are cut off from real reaction and cannot discharge their affects outward, they take revenge in imagination, and it is this ressentiment that turns creative and produces values. Its first act is to call its oppressors evil, and 'good' is then derived as the opposite of that — the meek, the patient, the poor. The priests are the crucial agents. Being impotent and cerebral rather than physical, they cannot meet the knightly type on its own ground, so they revalue from the inside: they get the strong to see their own strength as a chosen thing and therefore as blameworthy, and this is what makes the slaves' values win. Because the strong are then unable to act, they develop bad conscience, and it is out of this cruelty turned inwards that the ascetic ideal grows. The revaluation succeeds, on Nietzsche's account, because it gives suffering a meaning — the sufferer can now see himself as good rather than merely unlucky — and because the nobles' own word 'bad' could simply be turned back against them. The upshot is that any morality speaking for the powerless should be read as a symptom of envy, and any egalitarian reform movement as ressentiment in secular dress."
