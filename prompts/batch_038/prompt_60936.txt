So I'm the grad student rep on my university's Student Employment Advisory Committee — it's the body that makes recommendations to the provost about on-campus student jobs — and I've been volunteered to write the memo for next Thursday's meeting. Here's the case, roughly.

Dining services took the automatic 18% off the campus coffee cart last year and replaced it with a tablet that prompts you to tap 15/18/20% or "no tip." The cart is staffed by students on an hourly wage that's basically minimum wage. The international student office ran an informal poll and their numbers say students coming from countries without a tipping culture (a big chunk of our exchange cohort) hit "no tip" at roughly triple the rate of domestic students. Two of the student workers have filed a complaint. The committee has four options in front of it:

(a) leave the tablet as is;
(b) replace the tip prompt with a mandatory service charge folded into prices;
(c) kill the prompt entirely and recommend a higher posted wage instead;
(d) keep the prompt and publish guidance for incoming international students explaining that tipping is expected here.

Half the committee wants (d) — the workers are losing money through no fault of theirs and a short orientation is cheap. The other half thinks (d) is exactly backwards, because it makes the university the enforcer of a convention it didn't create and quietly benefits from not having to pay for.

Draft the memo for me. Around 800 words, addressed to the committee, with an actual recommendation and a date it would take effect.

Here's the thing I want you to work with rather than around. In my own writing I've been trying to sort conventions into two kinds and I think it bites here, so I'd like the memo to use the distinction — or tell me plainly that it's useless:

- A **load-bearing** convention: deviating costs identifiable other people something, because their plans are built on you doing it. Queueing for a single-door bus. Deciding to cross a busy street somewhere other than the marked crossing.
- What I've been calling an **outsourced** convention: the practice exists because some institution has shifted part of a cost it would otherwise carry onto a voluntary norm, so compliance mainly props up that institution's arrangement rather than anybody's plans. Tipping at a cart like this one looks outsourced to me.

If tipping at the cart is outsourced, then (d) is asking newcomers to subsidise the university's payroll decision, and calling it "the local norm" hides who's actually being paid. But I can see the pushback and I don't want the memo to steamroll it: the people who lose out are the student workers, not the university in any visible way, and they're identifiable, they're our peers, and they didn't choose the wage structure either — which makes the thing feel load-bearing again from where they're standing. Maybe both descriptions are true at once and the memo should say so and then decide anyway.

Stuff I need in there:

- the reasoning laid out so the losing side can actually reply to it — name their best argument in its strongest form and answer it, don't just nod at it;
- whether it makes any difference that the complaint came from the workers rather than from the international students (i.e. who is complaining about whom, and who has standing to enforce the norm);
- whether the answer changes for someone here one semester who never opted into anything and won't be around for the reputational consequences;
- one condition attached to your recommended option, because none of (a)–(d) is getting through this committee without an amendment.

No citations and no lit review — I'll handle that myself — and please don't invent survey numbers or policies beyond what I've given you above. If you think the distinction I've sketched doesn't actually separate (a)–(d) the way I hope it does, say that in the memo rather than pretending it works.

Last line of the memo, on its own, in exactly this form:

Verdict: [one letter, a–d] — [six words or fewer]
