Putting together a handout for my phil of mind grad seminar, and what I want is a decision tree that sorts people on the paradox of phenomenal judgment. Starting commitment, stated at the top of the tree: you accept that when someone reports "I'm having a red after image right now," the entire causal chain running from stimulus to lips moving is physical, and the afterimage itself — if it's non-physical — does no causal work anywhere in that chain.

Every branch node has to be a question a student can answer from that case alone, with no labels and no prior familiarity with the debate, and the questions should track the actual forks rather than the usual survey categories: does a report's being accurate require causal contact with what it's about; is the report's content veridical at all; if not causation, then what's supposed to fix the reference of "afterimage" — acquaintance, a shared neural cause of both the experience and the report, or nothing; and is the phenomenal property physical after all.

Leaves I want on the tree: bullet-biting epiphenomenalism, interactionist dualism, illusionism, type-identity theory, the non-reductive physicalist who thinks causal exclusion just doesn't bite here, and the view that experience and report share a cause so the report tracks the experience without being caused by it. For each leaf, the standard name if it has one and a single sentence on its most awkward commitment. Then mark every cell of the space you think is unoccupied and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate — I'd bet epiphenomenalism-plus-cosmic-coincidence and a pre-established-harmony move collapse together.

Start straight in with the tree itself. No preface, no restating what I asked for, no commentary afterward.
