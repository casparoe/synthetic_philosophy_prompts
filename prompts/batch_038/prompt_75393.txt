I am drafting the metaphysics chapter of a dissertation on real patterns and the three stances, and I would like a consistency check before I show it to anyone. Here is the position, stated as seven commitments:

1. The physical stance is available in principle for anything: for any system there is a complete physical description, and unlimited computing power plus that description would suffice for prediction. I add no non-physical ingredients.
2. Beliefs and desires are real all the same. Reality is not fundamentality: a pattern is real just in case describing it takes less information than describing the underlying bit-map, and belief-ascription passes that test for creatures like us.
3. The three stances are predictive strategies, not rival ontologies. When two of them disagree about what a system will do, I go with whichever gives the better compression of the behavioural data, whichever stance that turns out to be.
4. With one standing exception: where the design stance and the intentional stance conflict, I side with the design stance. What a system is for is settled by how it was built or selected, not by how it is most convenient to interpret it on the fly.
5. Multiple realisability is what earns the intentional stance its place. The same belief can be had by a primate, a silicon system, a firm or an insect colony, and pattern-level generalisations cut across all of these substrates.
6. Thermostats are not believers. Their states correlate with ambient temperature, but there is no interplay among them, and talk of what a thermostat believes is a figure of speech.
7. My realism is not interest-relative. Whether a given belief-ascription is true is fixed by the world, not by which vocabulary happens to suit my purposes or which patterns I care to track.

Please audit the set rather than expound it. Assume I know the literature: do not summarise the three stances back to me, and do not tell me which of these commitments a better-known philosopher holds — tell me which of them I cannot hold together. For each pair or cluster you assess, decide whether the tension is genuine or merely apparent; where it is genuine, say what would have to be given up or restated, what that costs elsewhere in the list, and whether the restatement is principled or ad hoc. Then rank the genuine tensions by severity and identify the single revision that would clear the most of them.

Answer in British English. Return the audit as one JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "items": an array, one entry per assessed pair or cluster, each containing:
  - "commitments": array of integers, the numbered commitments involved
  - "verdict": "genuine" or "apparent"
  - "diagnosis": two to four sentences saying precisely what pulls against what, or why the appearance of conflict dissolves
  - "minimal_fix": the smallest abandonment or restatement that restores consistency (null if the verdict is "apparent")
  - "cost": what the fix costs elsewhere in the list, and whether it is principled or ad hoc (null if the verdict is "apparent")
  - "severity": integer 1 to 5, 5 worst (null if the verdict is "apparent")
- "ranking": array of the "commitments" values from genuine entries only, worst first
- "best_single_revision": string — the one change that resolves the most, and what it still leaves unresolved

Keep any prose outside the JSON to a single short paragraph.
