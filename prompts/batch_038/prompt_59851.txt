I sit on the pre-deployment review panel of a frontier AI laboratory. Our interpretability group has asked for a fourteen-month delay to the next release so that it can build and validate a suite of internal controls, and its own modelling puts us roughly a generation behind our closest rival at the point of release if we grant the request. The chair has asked each of us for a short written opinion before Friday's meeting. The difficulty is not whether such controls would be worth having in the abstract, but whether the competitiveness constraint should settle the question. It is said that a laboratory which has slipped out of the lead has also lost whatever power it had to shape what gets built, so a restraint that hands the frontier to another party defeats itself however prudent it looks; and it is said in reply that this consideration, once admitted, vetoes every restraint at the margin, and is therefore quietly doing all of the argumentative work while presenting itself as one input among several. Draft my opinion. I want a definite recommendation — grant the delay in full, grant a reduced version of it, or refuse it — together with reasons the losing side could actually engage with, rather than a weighing of the two. Please be explicit about the empirical claims the recommendation rests on: how large an alignment tax controls of this kind really carry, whether a laboratory running second retains any influence over the norms set at the frontier, and whether a visible restraint would be reciprocated or simply exploited. Then say which of those claims, if false, would reverse the recommendation, and whether the panel is being invited to decide something that is not in its gift to decide at all. Continuous prose only, please: no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables, written as one would write a careful letter to colleagues.
