I run a philosophy-of-action reading group, and for our session on the Frankfurt-style attack on the principle of alternative possibilities I would like a piece of parody to circulate in place of yet another summary of the debate.

The conceit: the counterfactual intervention device that these thought experiments posit is an ordinary commercial product. It is sold to philosophers who need to demonstrate moral responsibility in the absence of alternative possibilities, and it ships with the usual corporate technical documentation. Please write that documentation — an operator's manual, roughly 800 words, addressed throughout to a purchaser who is about to run a demonstration on a compliant subject.

Name the manufacturer after the character conventionally cast as the intervener in these examples, and give the unit a model number. The prose should be deadpan product documentation: numbered clauses, headed subsections, a specification table where useful, and the flat optimistic register of a document drafted by a legal department that has never read philosophy. No winking, no asides, no signal that any of this is odd. The humour must come entirely from the manual getting the philosophy exactly right, so that a specialist recognises each clause as a real objection rather than an invented one. Do not name any actual philosopher, living or dead, anywhere in the document, and include no citations or references. A non-specialist should still be able to read the piece as a coherent, plausible manual.

The following must appear as genuine engineering content, not as signposted punchlines:

1. The device never fires in any demonstration unit, and this is by design; its entire output is the counterfactual. Treat zero delivered intervention as the selling point rather than as something to apologise for.
2. A trigger-configuration section offering the operator a choice of settings, both of which are out of specification. If the trigger condition is a causally sufficient antecedent of the subject's choice, the demonstration collapses into an ordinary deterministic case and proves nothing against the principle; if the trigger is instead an indeterministic prior sign, the subject retains a residual alternative in the interval before the choice. Present this as a known engineering trade-off with a recommended workaround, and do not use the word dilemma.
3. A latency specification requiring the device to close the window between the subject's decision and the onset of the act, on the ground that an alternative still open after the decision would allow the subject to prevent the deed even though he could not have decided otherwise — so a prevention-based principle survives demonstrations that defeat the choice-based one. Give a recommended timing margin.
4. An overview listing the standards the unit is certified to defeat, distinguishing the simple formulation (an agent is responsible for what he did only if he could have done otherwise) from the stronger one on which responsibility is excluded only when the inability is what explains the act, and noting that the second requires a firmware flag.
5. The device leaves the subject one residual alternative — a twitch, a hesitation, the bare beginning of a deliberation — and the manual insists this does not count, because only alternatives that are robust in the relevant sense, those in which the subject's own moral position genuinely differs, could ground an exemption. Include a customer complaint that this quietly adds a clause the original principle never contained, answered in a corporate, untroubled voice that concedes nothing.
6. A warranty section stating precisely which form of control is voided and which remains valid: the two-ways control requiring an open alternative is voided, while the actual-sequence control requiring only that the mechanism actually operating be appropriately sensitive to the subject's reasons remains fully valid and is declared sufficient for all warranty claims, including claims about desert and blame.
7. A liability section resolving whether the manufacturer is answerable for anything the device would have caused, concluding that it is not, since it caused nothing, and confirming that the operator remains fully liable.
8. A troubleshooting table classifying any unit observed to fire as defective and subject to return, on the ground that a firing device turns the demonstration into straightforward neurological coercion and thereby excuses the subject.
9. A FAQ entry in which a customer asks why anyone should buy a device that never does anything, answered with complete sincerity in terms of what the actual sequence supplies.
10. A note that demonstration subjects are supplied and should not be sourced locally.

Please deliver the response in exactly these three sections, in this order:

**Product Summary** — two to four sentences stating what the unit is, what it is certified to demonstrate, and what it does not do.

**The Manual** — the piece itself, 750–850 words, with numbered clauses and at minimum these headed subsections: Overview and Intended Use; Package Contents; Installation and Trigger Configuration; Latency and Timing; Residual Alternatives; Warranty; Liability; Troubleshooting; FAQ.

**Design Notes** — around 200 words and no jokes. For each of the ten items above, say in a sentence or two which philosophical move or objection it encodes and why the manual handles it the way it does. This is the only place in the response where you may leave the corporate voice.
