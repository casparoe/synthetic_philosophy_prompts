hey — I cover science and ideas for a general-interest mag and I'm deep in a piece about why institutions keep producing genuinely awful outcomes without anybody inside them being a villain. Think compliance desks, content-moderation pipelines, loan-origination teams, the mid-level engineers who wire up systems they never chose.

My main source is an organizational psychologist who sells ethics training to banks, and her whole pitch runs on one analogy: Arendt's Eichmann as a cog in the machine. Her version goes roughly like this — Eichmann wasn't a sadist and wasn't really an ideologue, he was a functionary; Arendt showed that the operator's inner life is beside the point; therefore character screening and any bet on individual courage are theater, and the only lever that actually works is redesigning the machine so the cog can't turn the wrong way. She pushes it further and says intent-based accountability is a category error once you accept the cog picture, and that claim is load-bearing in her consulting deck, so I need to know whether it holds.

A historian I checked with says she's got it backwards: the cog line was Eichmann's own defense at trial, Arendt spends part of the postscript swatting it down on the grounds that a court addresses a person and not a component, and banality for her meant thoughtlessness — the cliché-saturated inability to think from anybody else's standpoint — not ordinariness and not interchangeability. He also says the Argentina material, the Sassen interviews and the later archival work on them, has Eichmann sounding like a committed and articulate true believer rather than a blank functionary, which would take out the empirical premise of the analogy before the philosophy even gets going.

- which disanalogies actually matter for the use she's making of it versus which ones are cosmetic. My instinct is that a machine has a single purpose and a single designer, parts can't refuse, and responsibility in a bureaucracy is distributed rather than located, but I want to know which of those bites and which is just window dressing.
