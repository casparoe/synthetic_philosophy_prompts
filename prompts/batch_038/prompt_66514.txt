I write a weekly column on climate policy and the economics of growth for a general-interest science magazine, and I need to handle one phrase precisely before this week's piece goes up.

In a follow-up interview, a degrowth-aligned ecological economist I had quoted on green growth read me a sentence from my draft — one noting that a number of wealthy economies have brought their territorial carbon dioxide emissions down while their GDP kept rising — and dismissed it with four words: decoupling is a myth. He then used that dismissal to argue that putting a price on carbon is a distraction and that what is required instead is a binding physical cap on extraction and throughput.

I do not think the sentence is empty, but I do not think it says one thing either, and I suspect that some of what it could say is perfectly compatible with my draft while other readings are not. So I want the disambiguation, not a summary of the debate. Lay out the distinct, precise claims the slogan can be making, separated along the axes that actually do the separating: which variable is being decoupled from which (CO2, primary energy, material extraction, waste and land use); absolute versus relative; territorial versus consumption-based accounting; one country versus the world; what has happened so far versus what is achievable in principle; and the observed rate of decoupling versus the rate a given remaining carbon budget would demand. Add any axis you think I have missed.

For each reading, tell me which of these it is: a platitude no serious person disputes, a substantive empirical claim with real support, a claim that is true but far weaker than it sounds when compressed into four words, or simply false. Wherever a reading's truth depends on a target that moves — a budget, a baseline year, a definition of what counts as growth, a boundary drawn around the accounting — say so explicitly, because that is where I expect the sleight of hand to live.

Then answer the questions that matter for my reporting. Which readings, if true, would actually license his move from rejecting decoupling to rejecting a carbon price in favour of a physical cap, and which would leave my sentence standing untouched? Is there a version of the claim on which he and I could agree word for word and still disagree about policy, and if so, what does the disagreement then really turn on? And give me the two questions I should put back to him that would force the slogan to commit to a single reading rather than slide between them.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the reading he most plausibly needs, and whether it survives scrutiny — and only then give me the menu and the reasoning. No preamble, and do not restate my question back to me. I want no references, no named studies and no reading list: just the logical anatomy, in prose or as a list, in a form I could defend in an editorial meeting tomorrow morning.
