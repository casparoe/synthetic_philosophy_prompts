Write me a short story, 1,200–1,800 words, built out of the two-envelope problem — two envelopes, one holding twice what the other holds, you pick one, you're shown your amount, and then you're offered the swap.

The situation: for something like fourty years a woman runs this game every Christmas for her two kids out of her own savings, and the narrator is the one who always swaps, because she worked out the arithmetic once as a teenager and never re-did it — if mine holds X, the other holds either 2X or X/2, so swapping is worth 1.25X every single time. Her brother never opens his envelope at all; he just holds it, and I want him to have been right, but not in a way that feels like a win. The story turns on the mother dying and the narrator finding the ledger she kept: every year's pair, plus a ceiling on what she was ever willing to put in them, set by what she could actually afford. So the narrator has to absorb two things, and I want both felt rather than explained — that with any ceiling at all, the big years were precisely the years she should have kept what she was holding, and that her swap-always rule only ever works in a world where her mother's generosity has no upper bound. Somewhere in there somebody should say the wrong thing cleanly and confidently, the bit about X being the same number in both branches, because that's the sentence that let her believe it for four decades, and it should cost her something to hear it taken apart.

Constraints: nobody says the word "paradox," no equations on the page beyond maybe a half-remembered scrap of arithmetic from when she was thirteen, and dont explain the resolution to the reader — let the ledger do it. Make definite choices: one ending, one thing the brother finally says, no alternatives offered up at the end for me to pick between. American English throughout.

After the story, add a paragraph — no more — telling me where you bent or simplified the math to make it work as fiction, and which of the competing ways of dissolving the puzzle you leaned on and why you chose that one over the others.
