So, small confession: I'm a math person who wandered into a pragmatics seminar this term and I've become obnoxiously fixated on Grice. Not the maxims so much — everyone does the maxims — but the split between conversational implicature and conventional implicature. Like, the difference between meaning that gets generated by the whole cooperative-principle setup in context, versus the meaning that's basically welded onto a particular word ("but", "therefore", "even", "yet") and travels with it no matter what the context does.

I want you to write me a joke where the punchline turns on exactly that asymmetry. Concretely: a character who has so thoroughly internalized the idea that implicature is cancellable/defeasible that they've made a lifelong habit of the little tacked-on disclaimer — you know, "not that I'm implying anything by it, I'm just telling you what happened" — and it works for them everywhere, all the time, right up until the moment they try to deploy it on a conventional implicature and it just... doesn't take. The meaning is stapled to the word and the disclaimer slides off. I'd really like the payoff to come from the other person in the conversation reacting, rather than from the character privately realizing their own mistake — that version feels like a groaner to me.

A few ground rules. The punchline has to actually require knowing the distinction; a generic nerd joke that happens to have the word "implicature" in it is a fail. But the setting should be aggressively ordinary — no philosophy department, no conference hallway, and please no "a Gricean walks into a bar." A couple fighting over a text message, someone at a hardware counter, a parent at a school pickup line, whatever. Four to eight lines, real setup, real punchline, and if you can sneak in the fact that his habit is genuinely rational everywhere else (because conversational implicatures ARE context-sensitive and retractable) then the joke lands harder — he's not stupid, he's over-generalized a true rule one word too far.

Oh, and I'll be reading these aloud at a thing with my roommate's book club, so nothing that needs a footnote to be gotten. If it needs a footnote to work, tell me that instead of writing it.
