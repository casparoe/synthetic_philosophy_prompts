I serve on the capital-review panel of a regional flood-risk authority. Our standing rule requires that any engineering investment recommendation rest on a single point estimate of the annual probability of the design event. A climate-model ensemble for our watershed does not support one: members yield annual probabilities for the event in question ranging from roughly 2% to roughly 9%, and there is no principled basis for weighting them, since the relevant record is too short and too nonstationary to score the members against. The levee raise now before us is cost-justified at 6% and clearly not at 2.5%; staff recommend returning the analysis for a point estimate, which would delay the project about eighteen months.

Draft the memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Write as an expert in formal epistemology and decision theory who has actually sat on technical advisory panels, and think the competing considerations through before settling on a recommendation. The memo should:

(a) decide this project — approve, reject, defer, or approve subject to conditions — with any conditions stated precisely enough that staff could implement them, and with the bottom line given in the first two sentences;

(b) say what the panel's general rule ought to be when the available evidence supports an interval rather than a point, setting out the candidate rules for acting under such evidence (acting as if the worst case in the range held, acting on some midpoint or weighted combination, comparing options pairwise across the whole set of admissible probabilities, deferring until precision improves) and which one you would adopt and why;

(c) state whether the standing point-estimate rule should be amended, and how.

Then give the strongest case against your recommendation — in particular, the worry that once intervals are admissible, advocates will simply widen theirs to whatever serves their project, and that a panel unable to insist on a number has lost the ability to say no. Engage that worry concretely, with a workable constraint if you can find one, rather than acknowledging it in passing. No citations or bibliography; I want the reasoning, not the references.
