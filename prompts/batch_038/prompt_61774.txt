You are a decision theorist with a specialist's command of the literature on Newcomblike problems, and I want you to work through a hybrid case that I have constructed and that, so far as I know, does not appear in that literature. Think carefully, and before committing to any numbers, privately sketch the case's decision-theoretic structure and mark every point at which two defensible positions part company; do not include that scratch work in your answer. Everything you need is stipulated below, so do not reach for outside sources, and do not cite or recommend any.

The case. Omega is a perfect predictor and simulator. It flips a fair coin whose outcome it observes and you do not.

- If the coin lands tails, you are placed in the familiar XOR-blackmail situation. Your house either is or is not infested with termites — a settled natural fact, with prior probability one in a hundred, entirely independent of Omega and of anything you decide — and an infestation would cost you $1,000,000 in damage. Omega sends you a letter if and only if exactly one of two conditions holds: the house is infested; you pay Omega $1,000 upon receiving the letter. The letter explains all of this plainly, and Omega never misstates anything.
- If the coin lands heads, Omega leaves you entirely alone. Instead it runs a high-fidelity simulation of the whole tails scenario — the same termite fact as actually obtains, the same letter or absence of a letter, the same evidence — and transfers $10,000 to you if and only if the simulated you pays the $1,000.

Take it as given that the simulation instantiates your own decision procedure on your own information, so that the assumption that one algorithm on one input yields one output is available here to whatever extent it ever is. Note also that in the one world in a hundred where the house really is infested, an agent disposed to pay receives no letter at all, so there is no simulated payment for Omega to reward. You are in the tails branch, and the letter is in your hand.

Two calculations are already on the table; take both as given rather than redoing them. Compared from behind the coin flip, the policy of paying comes out ahead of the policy of refusing by roughly $4,500. Compared within the branch you are actually in, refusing comes out ahead by roughly $1,000, since a payment here can never avert the infestation.

Give me your credence that it is rational, all things considered, for you to pay the $1,000. I want the derivation, not just the number: assign a probability to each of the four factors below, with one or two sentences of justification each, and then combine them.

Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items and nothing else — no preamble, no restatement of the case, no closing summary.

1. The probability that a payoff accruing in the heads branch is a genuine payoff of the choice you are making now in the tails branch, rather than a merely counterfactual reward that the actual you has no way of earning.
2. The probability that the XOR conditioning preserves the identity of the decision — that is, that the simulated you Omega consults is really making your decision, given that a payer is lettered only in uninfested worlds and a refuser only in infested ones, so the two of you may never receive the same input at all.
3. The probability that the customary refusal in the unhybridized blackmail case expresses a general principle about never letting a predictor profit from your compliance, one that keeps its force even when a compensating reward is attached, rather than a verdict that rests wholly on the payment there being a pure loss.
4. The probability that the letter in your hand is evidence you ought to be using about your own dispositions — what its arrival tells you about the termites depends on which policy you run — and that this self-referential evidence, not the behind-the-coin-flip comparison, is what governs the choice.
5. Your resulting credence, produced from the four factors by whatever combination rule you judge appropriate, plus a single clause naming the factor the result is most sensitive to and the argument or piece of information that would move it furthest.
