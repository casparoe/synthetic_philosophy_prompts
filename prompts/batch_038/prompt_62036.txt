I'm trying to work out what I actually think about predictions of my own choices — Death in Damascus territory — just going by my gut on cases.

- My physio, who is uncannily good at reading her patients, tells me flat out that I'll have stopped doing the exercises within a month. I think that's a reason to prepay for sessions and set alarms. The prediction is information about my own dispositions and the sensible response is to use it.
- A rival who reads people just as well announces, in front of the whole department, that I'll pull out of the debate. Here I think the announcement gives me a reason to stay in that I didn't have a minute earlier.
- The Damascus case: Death looks surprised to see the man, says he has an appointment with him in Aleppo tomorrow; the man rides for Aleppo and dies there. Fleeing seems obviously futile, but I can't say staying is any better, and I don't think either choice is irrational.
- My grandmother, who has never been wrong about these things, says I'll take the safe job offer. Part of me wants to take the risky one just so she's wrong. I also think that impulse is embarrassing and should be ignored.
- A colleague wrote down yesterday, and sealed in an envelope, a prediction of which dish I'll order at dinner tonight; if she got it right she buys the meal. I think I should just order what I most want and trust her accuracy.
- Exact same setup, except she reads the prediction out loud first: you'll order the fish. Now I feel I should order the fish too — not to prove anything, but because the free meal depends on her being right and my ordering the fish is how I make that happen. Except the envelope was already sealed yesterday in the other case as well, so why would hearing it change what to order?
- The one I keep flipping on: a friend bets me fifty dollars that I'll give to the charity appeal arriving tommorow, and I was already going to give. Does the bet give me a reason to give, a reason not to, or neither?

Is there a single principle that gets all of these, or nearly all? Answer as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no introduction and no conclusion: the principle stated precisely; what each of its clauses is doing and which case forces it; what it says about the fifty dollar bet; one new case of your own where it comes apart from what my gut would say; and which of my verdicts it can't save and has to overrule.
