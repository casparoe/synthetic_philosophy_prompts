Something keeps recurring in the etiquette around money and punctuality where I live, and I have no word for it.

Tipping at sit-down restaurants here was fifteen percent when I was young. It is now twenty, and I regularly hear twenty-five offered as the mark of a genuinely generous tipper. As far as I can tell, nobody wants this. The people who leave twenty percent complain about the going rate as bitterly as I do. Yet it keeps climbing, and I think I can see why: each of us, deciding what to leave, aims a little above what we take the going rate to be, because landing precisely on it risks reading as cheap. The next person, seeing what we left or hearing about it afterward, revises their estimate of the rate upward. Each increment is a small, well-meant gesture, and the increments compound.

I see the same drift in how much notice one is expected to give when cancelling a dinner plan — a same-day call used to be entirely acceptable, now several days is the polite minimum — and in how quickly a message from a friend must be answered.

Is there an established term for this: a norm that escalates against the settled wishes of everyone subject to it, because the only reputationally safe position is marginally above the perceived norm, and every marginal excess becomes the new norm? I can name nearby ideas — the standard picture of a convention as self-enforcing because everyone prefers to conform when others do; systematic misperception of what others privately endorse; costly signals that work precisely because others decline to match them — but none seems to fit exactly. The first assumes a stable equilibrium, and this process appears to have none, or at least none at any particular level. Do I need to invoke misperception at all, or would the same escalation run when everyone knows perfectly well what everyone else is doing? And where does it stop being the same phenomenon: does a norm that drifts downward count, or one that drifts and then settles?

If there is no established name, I would much rather you coin one and define it precisely than be told that the label doesn't matter. What I'm after is the carving: the mechanism, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
