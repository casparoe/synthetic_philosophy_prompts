Write a short story, of roughly two thousand words and carrying a title of your own choosing, on the following conceit.

**The world.** In a near-future maritime jurisdiction, a licensed trade has grown up around a piece of equipment known as a variation bench. Underwriters, admiralty courts, and cargo registries pay for it, and loss adjusters are required to sit in the chair. The bench does not detect lies, does not measure confidence, and does not read the mind of the person in it. It takes one precisely worded belief that the subject already holds and reports whether that belief satisfies the third of Robert Nozick's four conditions on knowledge — the condition usually glossed as variation or sensitivity: had the matter stood otherwise than it does, the subject would not have come to hold that belief. What no bench on the market can test, and what a rival firm has been advertising for eleven years as nearly ready, is the fourth condition — adherence: had the proposition been true, and had the subject applied the same method to it, the subject would have arrived at the belief. Everyone in the trade knows they are buying a half-instrument. Much of the friction in your story should come from that.

**The second machine, equally important.** A bench never certifies a belief simpliciter; it certifies a belief against a described method of forming it — reading the logger's readout, reading the logger's readout on the night watch, accepting the depot's upload, accepting the word of the chief engineer, accepting the word of a chief engineer who is one's brother-in-law. Which description is entered on the form is not determined by the equipment, and the verdict flips between descriptions. Two of your characters must quarrel over a description, and the quarrel must read as an ordinary, procedural, faintly venial dispute about paperwork rather than as a seminar.

**What the story must do.**

1. Set it on a single claim: a refrigerated container, a temperature record, an intact seal, a cargo alleged to have been switched or the record alleged to have been prepared in advance. The protagonist is the adjuster.

2. Give her one belief that the bench certifies twice, under two rival descriptions of the method, with opposite results, and make the reader feel the money and the exposure attached to each certificate.

3. Give her a second belief — held because she happened to look, and one she would never have formed had she not looked — on which the bench's certification is simply silent, and let someone in the story notice that the silence is not a defect of the machine.

4. Have her seek a certificate for the negation of the rival account: she knows, and can get certified, that the seal was intact when she read it, and that fact entails that the manifest was not forged at the depot; but the bench will not certify the negation, because in the world where the manifest was forged she would be sitting in the same chair, reading the same seal, holding the same belief. She must not be rescued by a better instrument, a cleverer method-description, or a more careful form. The refusal has to feel structural rather than technical, and both verdicts — that she knows about the seal, and that she does not know about the forgery — have to strike the reader as correct.

5. Include at least one piece of in-story documentation set off typographically: a certificate, a form clause, a refusal notice, an invoice line. Include at least one moment in which a person, and not a policy, is what is at stake.

**What the story must not do.** The words *sensitivity*, *adherence*, *variation*, *closure*, *justification*, and *Nozick* may not appear in the narrative prose; they are permitted only in the coda below. No character may be a philosopher, an academic, or anyone whose occupation is to think about knowledge as such. Nothing may be explained in exposition: the machinery is to be overheard, in procedure, in quarrels, and in consequences.

**Coda.** After the story, in no more than five sentences, state which of the two counterfactual conditions the bench certifies and which it does not; identify the description of the method on which the central dispute turns and say why the rival description is not merely a clumsier phrasing of the same thing; and state in one sentence what the sealed-container case shows about passing from a certified mundane belief to the negation of the account that entails it.
