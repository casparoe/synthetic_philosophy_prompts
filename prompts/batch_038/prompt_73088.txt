You're a political philosopher who's actually sat on institutional ethics boards — think this through carefully, and start with a short brainstorm of the ways a distinction like this gets mangled in practice before you write anything normative.

I chair a standing committee at a two-hundred-year-old university that receives proposals for acknowledging past wrongs: roughly one a month, from survivor groups, students, claimants' lawyers, our own departments. The format varies — formal apology, plaque, renaming, a fund, a research programme. Right now we argue in circles and split the difference, which is it's own kind of dishonesty, and whatever we publish gets cited back at us years later, sometimes in litigation. I want to try running proposals through Jaspers's split of guilt into criminal, political, moral and metaphysical, from his postwar lectures on German guilt.

Write the procedure my committee could actually run inside a 90-minute meeting, by members with no philosophy: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, which kinds of act or commitment each species of guilt licenses and which it forbids, where the procedure terminates in a "we can't do this one", and where it has to hand the call to a person because it's a question of values rather than fact. Our general counsel must sign off on anything readable as admitting legal liability, so mark the points where the procedure and counsel will collide.

Also tell me which of Jaspers's four distinctions you'd quietly drop for practitioners and which absolutely have to survive the simplification, and what the framework loses by becoming a checklist.

Then run it on three cases: (a) living survivors of a 1950s study run by our medical faculty, perpetrators all dead, limitation period long expired, they want an apology plus money; (b) a demand to rename a hall whose benefactor's fortune came from colonial plantations, no identifiable claimants; (c) an alumni petition asking us to apologise "on behalf of every graduate". One of these should come out a way I won't like — say which and why. And flag where the procedure produces something that reads as a dodge even when it isn't one.
