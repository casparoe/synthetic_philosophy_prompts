I have just been made technical lead on a small team designing a cryogenic co-processor for inference workloads, and at last week's budget review our CTO defended an aggressive power target by asserting three things: that Landauer's principle sets a hard floor of kT ln 2 per bit erased, that reversible computing is how we get anywhere near that floor, and that Maxwell's demon has been settled for decades, so the floor is the only thermodynamic constraint worth modelling. I owe him a written response this week, and the substance of it turns on which parts of the demon literature actually bear on the engineering.

Please rank the following candidate resolutions of the paradox by how much of the paradox each genuinely dissolves, assigning each a score from 0 to 1 and stating the resulting ordering explicitly:

1. Szilard's one-molecule engine, with the claim that a single bit acquired buys kT ln 2 of work and that the acquisition is what costs.
2. Landauer's erasure principle: the demon's memory must eventually be reset, and the reset pays.
3. Bennett's completion, treating the measurement itself as physically reversible and locating the entire cost in erasure.
4. The modern feedback-control formulation, in which the second law is generalized so that acquired mutual information licenses additional work extraction, with the demon's bookkeeping restored by a later erasure.
5. The deflationary response that the paradox rests on averaging over measurement outcomes that the demon never averages over, and that no single trajectory ever violates anything.

Then, for each of the five, say in one or two sentences whether it licenses the CTO's floor claim, undercuts it, or is simply silent on it. Flag which of your scores would shift for a device operating far from equilibrium, or cold enough that coherence persists across many gate operations, and say which item you would delete from the list as not really a distinct resolution at all.
