In a reading group on anthropic reasoning, a member dismissed the doomsday argument with the line that it involves no new information: you already knew that you existed and that you would occupy some birth rank, so learning that you are roughly the sixty-billionth human to be born cannot rationally move your credence about how many humans there will ever be. I suspect that slogan is doing several different jobs, and that at least one of them is not obviously a mistake.

Please separate the distinct claims the objection could be making — for instance, a claim about what counts as new evidence at all, a claim about whether indexical information can bear on a non-indexical hypothesis such as the total number of humans, a claim that the apparent update is really the repair of an incoherent prior rather than a response to anything learned, and whatever further readings you think belong on the list — and for each say whether it is true and whether it actually blocks the argument. Note which readings are compatible with one another, and end by identifying the one the objector needs in order to succeed, together with what a defender who accepts the self-sampling assumption while rejecting the self-indication assumption should say in reply.
