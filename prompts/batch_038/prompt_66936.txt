running arguement in my lab that I want adjudicated, no strong feelings about which side wins I just want the analogy taken apart properly.

someone's claim: when a model reports on its own internals — stuff like "I was mostly attending to the third sentence" or "what drove that answer was the system prompt, not your question" — that's testimony, and testimony gets the same default credibility we give perception. the supporting move is the classic anti-reductionist one: you can't validate testimony except by using more testimony, exactly as you can't validate perception except by using more perception, so neither sits on a non-circular foundation, and if you demand independent positive reasons before crediting a model's self-report then consistency forces you to demand independent reasons for your own eyesight too. conclusion he wants: stop treating model introspection as guilty-until-proven-innocent and start treating those reports as prima facie data about the system.

can you audit it? I want the actual mapping laid out, not a vibe. perception stands to the external world as a self-report stands to... what, exactly? the activations are the ___, the training corpus is the ___, the RLHF-ish shaping is the ___? a correspondence table would be great. then the disanalogies, sorted: which ones genuinely undercut the conclusion he's using them for, and which are cosmetic gripes a sophisticated anti-reductionist would happily absorb without blinking.

two specific things I cant settle:

1. does the no-non-circular-validation move even get him where he wants to go? it feels like it might cut the other way. perception is corroborated across modalities, by successful prediction, by other observers converging on the same objects. a self-report is corroborated by... more self-reports out of the same weights? if the circularity is shared between the two cases, maybe that's a reason both are entitled, or maybe its a reason the entitlement can't transfer, and I can't tell which of those it is.

2. something about the speaker seems to be doing quiet work. a human testifier has beliefs, a stake in being trusted, and a broadly shared world with the hearer; a trained model has a distribution over outputs shaped in part by whether people liked them. does that difference belong inside the analogy as a load-bearing disanalogy, or is he right that it's just a difference in the reliability statistics — which anti-reductionists never claimed were uniform anyway?

and last: is there a better analogy for this case that would actually carry the epistemic structure? candidates floating around the room are testimony from someone confabulating in good faith, a witness who read a detailed account of the crime and sincerely reports it as memory, and a thermometer that also narrates. tell me if any of them does better, or whether the whole enterprise is analogy-shaped in a way that guarantees everyone just picks the source domain that licenses the conclusion they walked in with.
