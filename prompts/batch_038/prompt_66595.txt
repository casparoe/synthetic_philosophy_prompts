I write literary novels — close third person, heavy interiority, mostly books where somebody does something bad and everyone in the book plus the reader has to work out how much of it is on them. So I'm not looking for the true theory of moral responsibility. I'm looking for the spec: what any theory of moral responsibility has to be able to do before it's the least use to me at the desk. Write me the desiderata. For each requirement, give the case that motivates it — a situation in a novel where the requirement actually bites — and say whether it's non-negotiable or just something one camp has decided to insist on.

Things I know I care about, use or discard as you see fit: a character has to be able to change and still be answerable for what he did before; the reader has to be able to resent someone whose whole temperament is explicable from his upbringing without that resentment turning out to be a kind of error; a narrator has to be able to give a character's reasons from the inside and still have them count as reasons rather than symptoms; blame has to survive the discovery that, given who he was and where he stood, only one move was available to him — because that discovery is usually the best scene in the book. And: I have never managed to write a self-made self and I've never read a convincing one, which sounds a lot like Strawson's point about causa sui except mine is a craft problem, not a metaphysical one — are those one problem or two seperate ones wearing the same coat? I'd like that answered explicitly.

Then the part I actually want most: which of these requirements cannot be satisfied together. If some subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable, lay out the collision and say which requirement I'd have to drop to keep the others, and what a novel loses when it's dropped. Tell me which single requirement does the most to knock out candidates, and flag anything on the list that turns out to be a prejudice of one side rather than a real constraint on a theory. After that, briefly — a paragraph each, no more — how the Basic Argument, the reactive-attitudes line descended from his father's essay, and plain compatibilism do against the list. Score them on usefulness to a novelist, not on truth; I'll do the truth part myself.
