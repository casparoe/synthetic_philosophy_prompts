Retired from plant physiology three years ago, and now, apparently, I keep a spreadsheet for my vegetable garden and read ethics books instead of napping. Hornworms are back on the tomatoes and I have four options and none of them are good in the same currency.

One: spinosad. Fast knockdown, but while it's wet it takes out pretty much anything that walks across the leaf, and there is a lot walking across my leaves. Two: Bt. Narrow, caterpillars only, but they quit feeding and then take a couple of days to go, which looks to me like the slowest death on the menu. Three: I pick them off by hand into a bucket of soapy water — targetted, but it means I'm personally drowning one worm at a time, and I honestly don't know whether the soap does it in a minute or in ten. Four: do nothing, which is what the woman two doors down says is the only clean option, except I know what nothing looks like. Braconid wasps put eggs inside them, the young eat their way out, and they spin little white cocoons right on the worm's back. I've found them. It goes on for days.

So my spreadsheet tries to collapse all four into one number, basically minutes of misery per animal times how many animals, and I can tell its a little insane, I just can't tell which part is the insane part. Should I be driving that number down? And here's what breaks it: last spring I put in a big pollinator strip to be a good neighbor to the bees. It worked, there's maybe ten times the insects back there now. If insect deaths count as losses, did I just do a bad thing on purpose, and should I rip it out?

Tell me what to spray Saturday, and whether the arithmetic is worth keeping.
