Our strategy office is preparing a fifty-year horizon paper for the board, and its centrepiece is what the authors call the outside view. Rather than reasoning from our own country's institutional strengths, fiscal position, or innovation system, we are to take the historical record of great powers, extract the distribution of their hegemonic lifespans, and read our own prospects off that distribution. The argument runs as follows: essentially every polity that has ever held the top position eventually lost it, so the base rate for eventual decline is close to one; the only genuinely open question is therefore timing, and timing is settled by asking where a polity of our current age sits within the observed lifespan distribution. On that calculation the paper concludes that we should plan for losing the top position within two or three decades.

I have been asked to check the analysis before it goes forward. My instinct is that something has gone wrong, chiefly because the conclusion sits badly with what the same paper says elsewhere — that nuclear weapons and deep economic interdependence have made the forcible displacement of one great power by another historically unusual, and that any decline of ours would be relative and gradual rather than a discrete loss of position. So I would like to locate the error precisely. Is it in the reference class, in that we have pooled land empires, maritime hegemons, and ordinary great powers when only some subset is genuinely comparable to us? Or is it in the step that converts a distribution of completed lifespans into a per-decade probability for a polity that is still standing — a step that depends on the appendix's deciding, case by case, both when each polity began to hold the position and when it ceased to? And once whichever of these is the mistake has been repaired, does the outside view yield a number the board could rely on, with the nuclear-and-interdependence material entering as a downward correction — or is there something further wrong with the enterprise that no repair to these two steps would fix?
