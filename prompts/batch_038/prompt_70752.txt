You are a philosopher of science with a serious working knowledge of historiography and comparative economic history. I am a second-year doctoral student in economic history. Over the past term I have hit the same wall in three separate conversations about my project, which concerns the mechanization of cotton spinning in the 1770s, and I have no name for the wall.

First, at a workshop I posed the question in its usual comparative form — why did spinning mechanize in Britain and not in the Yangzi delta? — and was told it cannot be answered as posed, because Britain and the Yangzi delta are not the right units, and a century is not the right interval. Second, my supervisor reads the very same sentence as asking why the 1770s rather than the 1870s, and answers with fuel and factor prices; a colleague insists the real question is why spinning rather than weaving, which gets a different answer again. None of these three answers contradict one another, yet each was offered as a refutation of the others, and on the page the questions are indistinguishable. Third — this is the part that worries me — when I try to pin the question down by listing everything that had to hold jointly, a wage-to-fuel ratio, a market for the output, a supply of people able to build and repair the machines, an arrangement for hiring them, and so forth, I find that no single item would have made the difference alone; and if I say the difference-maker is the whole list, the reply stops addressing my question, since most of that list was in place in several other regions too. The precisely stated version thus seems to have no answer, while the versions that do have answers are not the question I thought I was asking.

I am aware that the general comparative form of this has a famous name attached to it. I am not asking about that debate, nor about which region industrialized first. I want to know whether what I have described has a name of its own. If there are established concepts in the vicinity, tell me what each one amounts to, how far my description matches it, and how far it does not; I am especially interested in the mismatches, since that is presumably where the thing I have noticed lives. If nothing fits, say so plainly and coin a term, with a definition and — more importantly — boundary conditions: two or three nearby situations that look like mine but are not instances of it. A bare label will not help me. I want the carving.
