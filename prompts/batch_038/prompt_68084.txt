I write firmware for things that aren't allowed to hang, and I fell down a philosophy hole through deadlock analysis, of all things. Our scheduler constantly hits states where two ready tasks are exactly tied — same priority, same deadline, nothing measurable separating them. We break the tie with a hash of the task id: deterministic so a replay reproduces, and completely arbitrary with respect to anything that matters. Nobody on the team thinks that's a flaw in our reasoning. Not breaking the tie is the only actual bug. Then I find out this is basically Buridan's ass and that philosophers treat the tie as a deep problem, and I want the spec before I read any of the theories.

So: pretend you're a decision-theory philosopher who's had to defend a view in a room full of engineers, and think it through carefully before you write — scratch work is fine. What does any theory of choice under exact indifference have to deliver to count as adequate? Give me the requirement list, each with the case or consideration behind it: the starving ass, sure, but also the boring ones like picking between two identical parking spots, which is where the paradox seems to lose all its bite. Fold in the distinctions a theory can't collapse (no reason to prefer vs. equal and oposing reasons; exact equality vs. the options not being comparable at all; the choice being arbitrary vs. the agent being arbitrary; whether randomizing or drawing lots is a solution or a dodge) and the results it has to stay consistent with (whatever Spinoza's determinism and Leibniz's sufficient-reason line actually force, and whether a theory is even allowed to just deny that perfect ties ever occur). Mark which of these are hard constraints and which are one camp's taste dressed up as a requirement. At the very end, one line each on how the usual answers do against your list — one line, the list is the deliverable.

The part I most want: if some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, say so flatly and say which one you'd cut — and specifically whether "hash the id and move on" is a legitimate rational procedure or a cheat that smuggles in a reason. Don't lay out the options and leave me holding the bag. Pick a position, argue for it, and if one of my engineering intuitions above is just wrong, tell me that too.
