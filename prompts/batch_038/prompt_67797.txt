A friend of mine on LW keeps waving von Neumann around in uploading arguments. His move is basically: the guy who built the stored-program computer wrote a whole book comparing the brain to a computer, noted the neuron is an all-or-nothing binary device, and concluded the nervous system computes — so the brain is a computer in the non-metaphorical sense and whole-brain emulation is an engineering problem, not a metaphysics problem. When I pushed back he said I was just equivocating on computer.

My memory of *The Computer and the Brain* (the 1958 Yale Silliman lectures, published posthumously) is that he's way more hedged than that, and that some of the hedges cut against the uploading conclussion rather than for it: he treats the nervous system as a hybrid of digital and analog elements, not a clean digital machine; he stresses that neurons are low-precision components next to the ten-to-twenty digit precision of a good digital machine, so reliability has to be bought differently; he suggests biological memory is reused in only a handful of contexts, unlike machine memory, and throws out a capacity estimate that comes out almost embarrassingly small; and — the big one — he seems to think the code the nervous system runs is not mathematics at all, but a structurally different and much shallower calculus, more a logics than an arithmetic. There's also the awkward fact that a von Neumann architecture is defined by seperating memory from processing, and the brain is the paradigm case of not doing that, which is the whole reason neuromorphic hardware exists.

So: audit the analogy. Give me a table with columns `analogy move | computer side | nervous-system side | mapping (strong / partial / fails) | what my friend uses it to license | where it breaks`, then sort the disanalogies into the ones that actually matter for the use he's making of it and the ones that are decorative, and tell me whether the emulation conclusion survives once they're on the table — or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time. If it was carrying it, say what a better source domain would be.

Then a short JSON object: `{verdict, load_bearing_moves, equivocations, what_von_neumann_actually_licensed, better_source_domain, confidence_0_to_1}`, where `equivocations` lists every place the word computer or the word computation slides between something trivially true of any physical system and something strong enough to imply emulability.

Two things: if you think the framing is confused — e.g. that whether the brain is a computer isn't a substantive question at all, just a fight over a word — say that plainly instead of humoring me. And mark which of your claims about the book are reconstruction from memory versus your own extrapolation, since I can't check the text right now either.
