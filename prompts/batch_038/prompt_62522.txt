This term we're on diachronic stuff so I want to do one about van Fraassen's Reflection principle, and I want it to actually be about the principle rather than just using the word "Bayesian" as a nerd signifier.

So: write me a joke whose punchline turns on what Reflection actually says — that my present credence in P conditional on my future self assigning credence x to P is supposed to be x, i.e. a rational agent has to endorse right now the opinions she expects to have later. What I think is funny about this territory is that Reflection tells you to follow the principle, so the humour should come from somebody *obediently* deferring to their own future self rather than from a logician being pedantic, if that makes sense. There will be at least one actual formal epistemologist in the room and she will absolutely tell me afterwards if the setup misstates the principle.

Deliverable: the joke (setup and punchline, short dialogue is fine), then two or three sentences saying which specific part of Reflection is carrying the weight in the punchline, so I can check you're joking about the principle and not just the vibe of it.
