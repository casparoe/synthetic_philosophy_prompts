I write about markets and math for a science/ideas desk, and I'm working on a piece about the subprime credit-default-swap trade of 2005-2007: the handful of hedge funds that bet the mortgage bonds would blow up, and the big banks that wrote them the insurance without much hesitation. I want to run it through Aumann's agreement theorem instead of the usual greed-and-denial story, and I need help getting the mapping right. Who exactly are the players here, what would the common prior even be, and is the gap between the two sides private information in the technical sense or something else entirely — a difference of model, of attention, of what counts as evidence? Both camps had access to much the same delinquency numbers and the same bond documents, which is what makes it interesting to me.

Then fold in the related no-trade result, the one saying a speculative trade that's common knowledge to be mutually agreeable can't happen at all. Does that make the sheer existence of that market in 2006 a proof that somebody on one side wasn't rational or wasn't informed? And be honest about where the theorem goes quiet. My suspicion is it has nothing to say about motivated reasoning, and nothing to say about a bank selling protection it doesn't actually believe in because the plan is to hedge the position or flip it onward — in which case the price was never an expression of a belief at all. Tell me whether the episode confirms Aumann, embarasses him, or is a case his setup never anticipated.
