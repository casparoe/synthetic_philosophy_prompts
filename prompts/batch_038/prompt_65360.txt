Last night I spent three hours arguing with a friend about Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes, and he closed by claiming that the position I was defending is not merely wrong but internally incoherent. I would like a disinterested audit rather than a defense of either of us. These are the commitments I actually hold, stated as carefully as I can manage:

1. What makes a research programme progressive is empirical progressiveness: its problemshifts must generate predictions of novel facts, and some of those predictions must be corroborated. Theoretical progressiveness on its own does not earn the label.
2. Ad hocness comes in degrees, and the degree is fixed by how much independently testable content an auxiliary hypothesis carries, not by the intentions or the confidence of the person proposing it.
3. For many modifications of the protective belt, whether the modification was ad hoc cannot be settled when it is made. The verdict emerges only once later work shows what the modification was worth. This is why I reject any demand for instant rationality in appraisal.
4. The hard core is irrefutable by decision. No body of contrary evidence can compel its abandonment, since its defenders are entitled to redirect the modus tollens onto the belt.
5. I am nonetheless a thoroughgoing fallibilist: every substantive claim, including the hard core of the best-supported programme currently on offer, could rationally be given up.
6. The Copernican and the Newtonian programmes were progressive from very early on, well before their celebrated novel successes.
7. Because a degenerating programme can in principle be revived, continuing to work on one is never irrational; and yet the methodology still yields a real demarcation between science and pseudoscience.

Work through these pairwise. Some of the apparent conflicts will, I suspect, dissolve on inspection, and I want those marked as such rather than padded into problems. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity, give the cheapest revision that removes it, and say what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. I do not know which of these I would sacrifice, so please do not assume I am protecting any of them. Finally: was my friend right, or does his charge of incoherence rest on a conflation you can name? I will handle the references myself; I want the structure of the commitments, not a bibliography.
