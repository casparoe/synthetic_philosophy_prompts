I run a fortnightly reading group — six of us, two philosophy grad students, a neuropsych postdoc, a couple of people who just like this stuff, and me, I write software and read on the side — and we've been going through the material on the Cartesian theater and the multiple drafts model for about a month now, and honestly we've ground to a halt. I don't want a survey. I've read enough of those to know that both positions can be stated in a paragraph and that every argument on both sides is already well known, and re-hearing them won't move anybody. What I want is a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, because my suspicion is that we've been aruging past each other for four meetings without noticing that we're doing it.

Here's the stalemate as best I can lay it out. One camp takes the multiple drafts model to be a real positive account: there's no place and time where it all comes together, there are instead parallel streams of editing and content-fixation at various depths, and what experience amounts to for a subject is a kind of abstraction over the resulting narrative rather than a property of any single moment. The other camp says that isn't a theory, it's a dissolution — that all the work is being done by the color phi arguement, where the Stalinesque and the Orwellian tellings of the very same display are supposed to come out empirically indistinguishable, and that showing two accounts are indistinguishable doesn't establish there's no fact of the matter, it only establishes that we picked a bad test case. They also keep pressing what I've started calling the timestamp problem: if there's no privileged moment at which content gets fixed, how are we supposed to explain the fact that temporal order judgments come out determinate rather than smeared? The cutaneous rabbit, the phi display, the Libet-style timing results — all of them seem to deliver a definite order to the subject, and a definite order is exactly what the model says isn't there to be found. Then there's the twist I find most interesting and that nobody in the group has managed to resolve: several of us noticed that global workspace accounts — ignition, broadcasting a message out to a lot of specialist processors, information becoming globally available — look structurally very close to what multiple drafts describes, and it's not obvious to me whether the difference between the two is a difference about the brain or just a difference about whether you're willing to call the broadcasting moment the theater. If they're rivals, in what sense are they rivals; if they're compatible, what have we been fighting about.

My hypothesis, which I'm sure is wrong in the details: this isn't a substantive disagreement about mechanisms, and it isn't a purely verbal one about the word conscious either. It's a disagreement about whether a theory of experience is obliged to hand out timestamps at all — about whether there's a determinate answer to the question when did this subject's experience have this content, and whether a mechanism has to reproduce that answer — and both camps think they're fighting about neural architecture. Test that against what each side actually says rather than taking my word for it, and tell me if you land somewhere else; I'd rather be corrected than agreed with. I'd also like you to be concrete about what would count as progress given whatever diagnosis you end up giving: a distinction, a change of question, a formal result, an experiment, or an argument that no progress should be expected and why. Here's the sort of thing I have in mind and I have no idea whether it's any good — suppose you built a phi display where the manufactured midpoint color is supposed to matter for a separate later judgment, so subjects watch the apparently moving light and then half a second afterward have to name the color of a small dot that appears near where the light seemed to be. On the Stalinesque story the midpoint color was genuinely constructed before the report and ought to prime the naming; on the Orwellian story nothing was ever there and the priming shouldn't show up. Would a result like that break the tie, or would both sides simply redescribe it — and is there any conceivable experiment that couldn't be redescribed?
