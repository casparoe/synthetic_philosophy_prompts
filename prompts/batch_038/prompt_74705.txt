For a reading group I have agreed to set out my own position on self-replication and on the DNA–Gödel analogy in full, so that the others can attack it rather than a caricature of it. I have condensed the write-up below without softening any of it, and I would like an audit before I circulate it. I am not asking for background, for a survey of the field, or for sources and further reading; what I want is the logical geography of my own package — what commits me to what.

My commitments, in roughly the order in which I acquired them:

1. I am a strict mechanist about life. Nothing a cell does requires a property absent from machines, and the difference between a cell and a computer is one of organization, not of kind. I reject strong emergence and any residual vitalism, however dressed up.

2. When two systems stand in a structure-preserving correspondence, results established in one are genuinely transferable to the other, not merely suggestive of it. I take this to be a real methodological license and the main engine of understanding in this whole area. I came to it through Hofstadter's invented genetics of strings and string-decoding enzymes, where the toy formalism is meant to line up with molecular biology; I know the toy omits the RNA intermediary and other machinery, but I do not think that damages the correspondence.

3. Strings and rules have no content by themselves. Content enters only through a correspondence with some subject matter. On that basis I hold that the genome is genuinely about the organism it helps produce, and that this aboutness consists in nothing over and above the correspondence.

4. Genuine self-replication, following von Neumann, requires a description of the machine that is used twice over: interpreted, by a constructor, in order to build a new machine, and copied uninterpreted, in order to be handed to that machine. Systems that merely produce similar-looking things, or that produce a copy without constructing it, do not qualify.

5. The cell is a literal instantiation of that scheme, not a loose analogy to it. The genome is the description; DNA replication is the uninterpreted copying; translation is the interpreting; the ribosome together with its supporting apparatus is the constructor.

6. The codon-to-amino-acid assignment is chemically arbitrary — nothing in the structure of a triplet dictates which residue it selects — in the way that a numbering of expressions is arbitrary. Any fixed, effectively specifiable assignment would do the same job. I grant that it is degenerate and mildly non-random: several codons per residue, chemically similar residues tending to share similar codons, and consequently a code that is unusually forgiving of single-position changes. I treat that as tuning of a convention, not as a constraint that gives the convention its content.

7. Because some proteins bind and regulate the very genes that encode them, the cell contains real self-reference of the kind Gödel exploited. I therefore think living systems stand in relations to themselves — self-description, self-modification — that systems not arranged this way cannot.

8. Notwithstanding 2 and 7, incompleteness results have no application to biology whatever. There is no biological counterpart to an undecidable sentence, and I regard proposals to find one as confused.

9. A program that outputs its own source code is not a self-reproducer. Nor is a virus exploiting a host cell, nor a self-extracting installer, nor a general-purpose machine running self-modifying code. Software-level replication is always parasitic on hardware that is doing the actual constructing, so it never counts on its own; only a description that governs machinery at the level where construction physically happens qualifies.

10. I reject the picture of the genome as a program or blueprint and of development as its execution. Causal responsibility for a phenotype is distributed across the genome, the cytoplasm, maternal provisioning, and environment; DNA is a resource the cell draws on, not a set of orders the cell obeys. I regard the program metaphor as a category error.

What I would like from you is the audit, not a lecture. Take these ten items and tell me which pairs genuinely conflict, ranking the conflicts by severity rather than listing them as equally serious. For each real conflict, be specific about the structural feature that fails to match or the verdict that cannot be derived, state the cheapest revision that removes the conflict, and say what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package — which other items it forces me to weaken or abandon. Separately, tell me which conflicts only look like conflicts and dissolve on inspection, and give the reading that dissolves them, since I would rather not be accused of the ones I am innocent of. Finish by identifying the single revision that would clean up the largest amount of the package at once. I have deliberately given you no indication of which items I hold most loosely, and I do not already suspect where the trouble is; please do not infer my priorities from the order of the list.
