In my philosophy of mathematics seminar we keep returning to Brouwer's rejection of excluded middle, and no argument offered on either side looks capable of moving anyone. What strikes me is how little the two camps actually disagree about at the level of results. Both accept the metatheory of intuitionistic logic, including its completeness for Kripke models — although those completeness proofs run on classical steps a strict intuitionist ought to refuse — and both accept Gödel's double-negation translation, which embeds classical arithmetic into Heyting arithmetic and leaves the two equiconsistent. Classical mathematicians treat Bishop's constructive analysis as evidence that the intuitionists merely imposed optional restrictions; intuitionists treat the same work as evidence that most of classical analysis was constructive all along, once read properly. And constructive mathematics is not one thing: Brouwer's continuity principles and fan theorem, against the Russian school's Markov's principle and Church's thesis, generate different and in places mutually incompatible mathematics, so there may be no single constructive reading of the existential quantifier for the dispute to turn on. My working hypothesis is that this is a verbal dispute — the sides mean different things by the connectives and are therefore not contradicting each other at all — though the translation result seems to cut both ways on that, and the fragmentation of constructivism cuts against it too. Diagnose the impasse for me: verbal, a clash of standards over what counts as a proof or as an account of how we grasp mathematical meaning, a substantive question in disguise, or two projects answering different questions under one heading. Then say what would count as progress — a theorem, a semantic or conceptual distinction, a reformulation of the question — or argue that none should be expected.
