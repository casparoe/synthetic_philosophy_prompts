I run a tiny podcast out of my garage — about 200 listeners, mostly friends of friends — and in three weeks I've got a philosophy professor on. He's the guy who argues that the money-pump argument against intransitive preferences doesn't actually land, because someone who makes up their mind in advance and refuses to get talked into trades one at a time can't be pumped. His line, as far as I can tell from hearing him twice, is that the pump only catches you if you're the sort of person who takes each offer as it comes, and that taking each offer as it comes is the real mistake, not the preference cycle. He also says — and this is the part I can't get my head around — that transitivity isn't a requirement on *preferences* at all, it's a requirement on *plans*, so a person with a cycle who never has to trade sequentially hasn't done anything wrong.

I don't have any formal training in this, I've just been reading around and arguing with my roommate, so I want questions that sound like they come from a smart layperson and not from somebody who's read the journal articles. Here's the example my roommate and I keep using, and I want the questions to run off this one instead of the generic textbook version: I collect vinyl and I have three pressings of the same album, call them A, B and C. I prefer A to B (warmer sound), B to C (nicer sleeve, fewer clicks), and C to A (C is a clean remaster and A is beat up). I'm standing at a record fair holding B. A guy offers me A in exchange for B plus $5 — I take it, obviously, A is the one I like best. Ten minutes later another guy offers me C for my A plus $5 — I take that too. At the end of the day somebody offers me B for my C plus $5, and I take it, and now I'm holding the exact same record I walked in with and I'm $15 down.

So here's what I want back, in this order:

**What I think he's saying** — five or six sentences, plain English, zero jargon, so I can read it in the car before the show and check I haven't got the view backwards.

**The questions** — eight of them, numbered. Each should be a sentence or two that I could actually say out loud into a mic, plus an indented "if he says…" follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. Four of the eight need to hit specific things: (1) that his resolution trick seems to only work if you already know you're inside a pump, since I definitely didn't know there was a cycle when I took that first trade, I just knew I liked A better than B; (2) whether his "don't take trades as they come" policy is actually free, because turning down $5-to-upgrade costs you real stuff in tons of ordinary situations that aren't pumps at all; (3) the thing that bugs me most, which is that on his view whether I'm rational seems to depend on who's standing around offering me trades, so the same guy with the same head could be rational in one town and irrational in another; and (4) whether ranking *plans* transitively just relocates the problem one level up, i.e. if his plans have to be transitive, hasn't he smuggled the requirement back in through the side door. Everything else is up to you, but no softballs — nothing like "what got you interested in preference theory," I only have 40 minutes.

**His escape hatches** — three or four bullets naming the moves I bet he'll use to slide past a question, one line each on why the move doesn't actually answer it.

**The one question** — if my recorder dies and I only get to ask a single thing, which of the eight it should be and why, in two or three sentences.

Two pages total is plenty. And don't bother listing sources or books for me to go read, I wouldn't be able to check any of it and I've got a job — I just need the questions.
