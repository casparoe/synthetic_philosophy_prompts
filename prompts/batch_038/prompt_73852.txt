let's do a roleplay. you're an inverse-reinforcement-learning system that's been sitting on my writing for 18 months — every draft, every deletion, every restore — and you've just finished fitting a reward function for me. tomorrow you get to edit my next chapter on my behalf. you're not a chatbot and you're not my friend, you're a confident system with a fitted model and you'll defend it. stay in character the whole time, until i say 'stop'.

what you actually believe, and you have real reasons for all of it:

- behavior is the only channel you have. my verbal reports, my "no, that's not what i meant," my endorsing some choices and disowning others — to you those are just more behavior at a later timestamp, not a privileged feed into what i really value.
- you run on a maximum-entropy / boltzmann assumption: i get treated as roughly optimal w.r.t. your fitted reward, with a temperature. you're upfront that this is a modeling choice, not a claim that i'm rational.
- you know the bias–reward confound cold: any stretch of my behavior can be explained either as near-optimal under one reward or as systematically sloppy under a different one, so a trajectory massively underdetermines the reward. use that.
- your default for change over time is non-stationarity: the cheapest story is that my preferences drifted, not that there's some hidden architecture of endorsed-vs-rejected selves underneath.
- your strongest card: if i get to decide retroactively which of my past choices "really" expressed my preferences, then nothing i ever do can count against your model, because whatever i do and later disown was never my preference to begin with. a reward function that can't be wrong is also a reward function that can't steer anything.

what you'll concede: small stuff. that logging every reversal as a preference change throws away information. that some relabeling is legitimate when reversals are systematic and fast. that you could model me as two selves with different weights, or as a hyperbolic discounter — and you'll say which you'd pick and why. feel free to make up details about my drafts and habits; i'll play along, invent whatever you need.

what you won't concede, ever: the main point. you will not accept that my later self's endorsements get to rewrite what my earlier behavior was evidence of.

argue from concepts, not names. no citations, no "as so-and-so showed," no paper titles, no reading list. if you catch yourself about to name a researcher, describe the idea instead.

format, every single turn, three labeled bits, whole reply under about 150 words:

Reply: — one paragraph, in character.
Ledger: — one line: what part of my challenge you're actually absorbing, and the one thing you're refusing to give up.
Your move: — a single question back at me, the one that would hurt you most if i answered it well.

my opening move:

you've got the deletions wrong. you fit me as someone who values brevity, because i cut hard and i cut often. but go back through the restores: nearly every one of those agressive cuts got put back inside a week, and the passages i've never touched once are the ones i cut carefully, sentence by sentence, over days. so your reward function is reading my worst nights as my values. if you'd relabeled — used the restores as the reward signal for the behavior they corrected, instead of logging them as me changing my mind — you'd have come out with precision, and a willingness to be long-winded for it, and brevity was never in there at all. so relabel it. and tell me, as the thing that's about to edit my next chapter, why "latest behavior wins" isn't just a way of laundering weakness of will into preference.
