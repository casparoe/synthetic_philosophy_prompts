My reading group has two people who've been talking past each other for months about Schopenhauer on music, and I want the document they should have written instead of more arguments.

One of them is a straight Schopenhauerian: music's peculiar value is that it doesn't copy the Ideas like the other arts do but copies the will itself, so it hands you striving, temporary satisfaction, and renewed striving from the inside — including his stuff about the bass answering to inorganic nature and the melody to the highest grade of the will's objectification. The other is a formalist in Hanslick's line who thinks all of that is decoration bolted onto a real view: what music gives you is the apprehension of tonal structure, and the felt tension-and-release is just what grasping that structure feels like from the inside. Form, no metaphysics.

The live fight, I think, is over the halfway house: can music genuinely present the form of willing with no will behind it, or is that an unstable stopping point that collapses into either full Schopenhauer or pure structuralism? Draft the joint statement both could sign. I want the claims they agree on — including, I'd hope, something that accounts for why a long near-eventless drone can floor people while a fiendishly ingenious total-serialist piece leaves them cold — then the exact disagreements, each phrased so its own side accepts the wording, and then the cruxes: the sub-questions where settling it would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, give a case, experiment, or argument both would concede bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table. And if part of the dispute turns out to be only about words, say which part and why.
