Our philosophy of science reading group is half working physicists who have never opened the realism literature and half grad students who talk in nothing but labels, and the last two sessions went nowhere. Before the next one I want to hand around a sorting instrument: a decision tree that lands people on a named position by what they answer, not by what they recognise.

The axis I want it built on, before any positions get hung off it: when a sucessor theory replaces a predecessor, what exactly gets carried across, and does carrying it across commit you to anything existing? Leaves should include entity realism, epistemic structural realism, the eliminativist ontic version, the Ramsey-sentence route, constructive empiricism, and whatever else you think genuinely earns its own leaf — aim for 6 to 10, and keep the whole thing under about 15 questions so it can be done in twenty minutes with a coffee.

Every node has to be answerable cold by someone who's never heard of any of this. So no node may ask whether structure is all there is, and no term of art appears anywhere except at the leaves. Drive as many nodes as you can off these three cases, so that what a person says about the case is what picks their branch:

- caloric theory into the mathematical theory of heat flow: the equations survive, the fluid doesn't.
- phlogiston into oxidation, where I claim essentially nothing survives except bookkeeping. If you think that's a bad case, say so and swap in a better one.
- Pauli's neutrino: a posit introduced to save the conservation principles, later actually detected, which entity realists never let anyone forget.

Plus one branch I don't think the standard maps include: the person who says the successor theory keeps all the structure but there's nothing the structure is structure *of* — no relata left over. Some people get there by Ramsey-sentencing the theory and saying the Ramsey sentence is all there is; others get there by a category-theoretic route that skips Ramsey altogether. If your questions can't tell those two apart, treat that as a finding and report it rather than inventing a fake distinction.

Deliverable: the tree as an indented outline, questions with two or three answers each, then a key that maps each complete path to a leaf and gives, for each leaf, the position's name plus one sentence on the commitment its occupant is going to find most awkward to swallow. Two more things I want in the key. First, at least one path should dead-end — a combination of answers that no coherent position answers to. Name it and diagnose why the cell is empty: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Second, there are bound to be at least two pairs of leaves your questions won't actually seperate; tell me which, and whether some extra question could split them or whether they're really one view wearing two hats.

No citations, no reading list, no who-said-what. Position names at the leaves are fine, that's the whole point.
