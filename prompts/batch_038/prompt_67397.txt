Help me prep for a talk. Argument sketch is below so you know exactly what's being attacked — I don't want a defense of my view or a refresher on the hole argument, assume I know it cold and have read the SEP entry twice.

The room: 45 min plus questions at a workshop co-hosted by a gravitational physics institute and a philosophy of science group. Maybe twenty people — about a dozen working relativists (several numerical, two postdocs in GW data analysis, one formalist on the initial value formulation and constraints), five philosophers who do representation and realism debates but mostly in the quantum context, plus two historians of GR who've actually sat with Einstein's 1915–16 correspondence. Nobody works on the hole argument full time, which is the problem: I'll get the physicist version of the objections, and I've only ever practiced against the phil-of-spacetime version.

My sketch:
1. If a quantity isn't invariant under the theory's gauge group it isn't physically significant; diffeos are gauge in GR; so "the metric has value g at manifold point p" isn't physically significant.
2. The indeterminism Earman and Norton extract is precisely the underdetermination of those quantities: d and d* agree on everything invariant outside the hole and differ only in point-indexed assignment inside it. So the argument only bites if you've already decided point-indexed values mean something.
3. Stated relationally — dust-style clock and rod fields, the invariant-coordinate trick of labelling points by curvature scalars and their derivatives, dressed obervables — the initial value formulation is deterministic in the sense that matters.
4. My actual claim: "do d and d* represent the same physical situation?" is not a question with an answer prior to fixing an observable algebra. Leibniz equivalence, anti-haecceitism, keeping the manifold while accepting equivalence — they all assume the order of explanation runs the other way. That assumption is the shared mistake.
5. So substantivalism is neither refuted nor rescued, it's bypassed. Both camps lose the argument they thought they were having.
