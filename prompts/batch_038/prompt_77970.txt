I TA a social choice seminar where everyone proved Harsanyi's aggregation theorem and then argued for an hour about what it's supposed to show. Write me a decision tree that takes a student from "fine, the math works — Pareto plus the expected utility axioms give you a positively weighted sum of individual utilities" to a named position on wether that vindicates utilitarianism.

Ground rules: every node has to be a question a student can answer from the theorem and their own ethical instincts, never something like "do you think Harsanyi was a utilitarian." I want at least four nodes on the stuff the theorem quietly leaves open: whether unanimity has to hold over prospects or only over outcomes, and what the conclusion costs you if you can only get the weaker version; whether anything pins down the zeros and scales of the individual utility functions, given each one is only unique up to positive affine transformation; whether the weights come out equal or whether that needs a seperate premise; and whether the "utility" in the theorem is meant to measure wellbeing or just to represent preference coherently. Add whatever other nodes you think belong — I suspect one about whether people have to agree on the probabilities is missing from my list. Roughly eight to ten questions, six to eight leaves.

For leaves, make sure plain sum utilitarianism is there, plus a weighted/prioritarian reading where the weights come from somewhere other than the theorem, plus the view that the theorem is true but normatively empty, plus the view that unanimity is so strong that the theorem only bites where there was never a disagreement to aggregate in the first place. Anything else you want to add is fine. Then tell me which combinations of answers nobody actually holds and why — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored — and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate.
