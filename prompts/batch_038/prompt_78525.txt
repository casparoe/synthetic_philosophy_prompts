I am twenty-nine, and I have been offered the chance to sign a pledge committing me to give a fixed tenth of my income to charity for the next twenty years. Everyone urging me on says I should do it now, while I am still idealistic and unencumbered — before a mortgage and children make me careful. The trouble is that I accept the diagnosis and not the conclusion. I am confident that at forty-nine I shall look back on this pledge as a piece of youthful self-righteousness, and part of me thinks that future self will be the better judge, since she will have actually lived through the trade-offs I am only imagining. Another part thinks she will merely be tired and compromised rather than wiser, and that a change of heart produced by circumstance is not the same thing as a change of mind produced by reasons.

There is a further wrinkle I cannot work out. The verdict I expect from my future self does not look independent of the choice itself: if I sign, I expect I shall grow to value the giving; if I decline, I expect I shall come to think the whole idea was naïve. She therefore seems to be a product of whichever way I go now, rather than a judge waiting ahead of me whose finding I could sensibly defer to.

Whose judgement ought to govern — present me, or the one I am confident I shall become? And is it legitimate to bind her at all? Please set out what is really at stake here rather than offering general financial or charitable advice, and write your answer in American English.
