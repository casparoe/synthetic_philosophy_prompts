I am preparing a session for a graduate seminar whose students have already worked through the standard dementia cases, and I need a scenario built to order rather than another variant of them. The point of the exercise is to test whether an advance directive can retain authority over someone who is fully competent when the time comes to obey it. So: design a case in which a directive written by a woman in her thirties remains, on at least one defensible account, binding against her own settled and articulate objection in her sixties.

Constraints. The later self must be fully competent — unimpaired reasoning, full memory of having written the directive, and no break in psychological continuity with its author — so that appeals to diminished capacity, to adaptive preference formation, and to personal identity are all unavailable to you. Her objection must be one she can state in her own terms and would still press had the directive never existed. The directive must not concern life-sustaining treatment, pain relief, or anything to which a third party has an independent claim. The setting must be realistic: ordinary law and ordinary practice, no speculative technology, no family pressure.

Keep the scenario to a single paragraph. Then show the design works. Put the directive and her objection in her own words; give the verdict of four accounts — one on which the integrity of a life and its critical interests outrank present experiential welfare, one on which only presently felt interests generate moral claims, one that grounds authority in psychological continuity and identity, and one that asks what she would have decided at the time of writing had she accurately foreseen the person she would become — saying in each case why it lands where it does. Name the single feature of the case that splits these verdicts apart, say what happens to the case if that feature is removed, and give the strongest attack available to whichever account fares worst. Begin with one sentence naming that feature. No more than 650 words in total.
