This term I am taking a formal-verification course alongside a seminar on the philosophy of time, and my fluency is heavily weighted toward the first. In the vocabulary I actually work in, a system denotes a set of executions through a state space; a temporal formula is satisfied either relative to one path or at a state, in the latter case by quantifying over all paths from that state, possibly narrowed by a fairness assumption; and satisfaction is always indexed to the structure, never to whatever single execution happens to occur at run time.

Restate the sea-battle argument — that if it is already true now that there will be a battle tomorrow, no deliberation of ours can affect the outcome — together with the standard moves for resisting it (denying bivalence for future contingents, restricting excluded middle rather than bivalence, relativizing truth to a history), into that vocabulary as literally as you can manage. I want the mapping given term by term, and then a residue report: for each philosophical claim, whether it translates cleanly, whether it can be translated only by distorting it, and whether it has no counterpart at all in a language of paths, path quantifiers, and satisfaction. Do not quietly build my framework's assumptions into the translation and then announce that they were already there.

My hypothesis, which I would like you to test rather than indulge, is that the dispute reduces to whether "will" is a universal or an existential path quantifier, and that once the semantics are pinned down the disagreement is notational. Tell me what survives that deflation and what it destroys — in particular, whether anything corresponding to the one execution that actually occurs is expressible in my framework, and whether the absence of such a thing is a limitation of the framework or evidence that the philosophers' notion of an actual future was never doing the work they assign to it.
