Design a thought experiment to specification in the philosophy of art, then demonstrate that the case you have built satisfies every part of the specification.

**Purpose of the case.** The point is to test the internal stability of Tolstoy's account in *What Is Art?*: whether the mechanism he treats as the measure of artistic merit — infection — is systematically biased toward the very feelings that his standard of subject-matter condemns. A case that obtains its verdicts by stipulating them (declaring the audience corrupt, the artist insincere, or the work a counterfeit) fails. The verdicts must fall out of the theory applied to the described features of the case.

**Premises to work from.** Take the following as given, and argue only from them: no outside scholarship, no citations or chapter references, no named real works, artists, or critics.

1. Art is an activity in which one person, having experienced a feeling, deliberately transmits it to others by means of external signs, and those others are infected by it and experience it too.
2. Infection is the mark of art; without it an object is a counterfeit, however poetic, effectful, or interesting. The degree of infection is also the sole measure of merit in art considered apart from subject-matter — that is, apart from the quality of the feeling transmitted.
3. Infectiousness depends on three conditions: the individuality of the feeling transmitted, the clearness with which it is transmitted, and the sincerity of the artist. Sincerity is the most important and tends to carry the other two with it.
4. A recognized sign of genuine infection is that the receiver feels the work as though it were his own, expressing what he had long wanted to express, and feels united both with the artist and with everyone else infected by the same work.
5. Subject-matter is evaluated by reference to the religious perception of the age, which for Tolstoy identifies the good with the union of human beings: feelings that unite are good subject-matter, while feelings that separate people, or that are available only to a cultivated few, are bad.

**Specifications for the scenario.**

- One invented work, described concretely enough that a reader can see which of its features are load-bearing: its medium, its rough content, and how it circulates to its audience.
- Contemporary and ordinary in its mode of transmission — no state propaganda apparatus, no patronage intrigue, no censorship, no exotic technology.
- The feeling transmitted must be one that premise 5 condemns, but not one of the obvious kinds: not patriotism, not sectarian religious feeling, not sensuality, not despair. Choose a feeling that a present-day educated audience would find attractive or even admirable rather than objectionable. The pleasure of exclusive discernment — counting oneself among the few who understand — is one candidate; you may substitute another that meets the remaining constraints.
- The work must be maximally infectious on Tolstoy's own conditions: individuality, clearness, and sincerity all present to a high degree, with an artist who genuinely feels what he transmits. You may not close the case by making the artist calculating, insincere, or professionally manufactured.
- The decisive structural requirement: the feeling's exclusivity must be the engine of its spread, not a limit on it. The case must make it intelligible how a feeling that sets its possessors apart from everyone else can infect a very large and demographically mixed audience, each of whom takes the feeling to be addressed to him personally and finds himself joined, by catching it, to all the others who have caught it.
- Describe the audience's response only through behavior and self-report. You may not characterize it as perverted, atrophied, or corrupted, since that is among the things at issue.
- The scenario itself: one paragraph.

**Deliverables, after the scenario.**

(a) Apply premises 2–4 to the work as described. State whether it is art and what rank it takes as art apart from subject-matter, showing that the answer follows from the theory rather than from the description.

(b) Apply premise 5. Note that Tolstoy's standard speaks of feelings that unite people without settling whether the relevant union is union within any group or union among all. Your case is built to sit exactly on that ambiguity: give both readings, give the verdict each yields, and say which reading the rest of the theory better supports.

(c) State the general prediction that follows if cases of this kind are typical rather than freakish — a prediction about which feelings the infection mechanism favors — and derive it from premises 3 and 4 alone, not from sociological claims about audiences.

(d) Identify the single feature of the case doing the work, and report what the verdicts in (a)–(c) become if it is removed — in particular, if the exclusivity is publicly advertised rather than privately conferred on each recipient.

(e) Set out the strongest Tolstoyan replies: that the audience's reaction is the excitement of divertisement rather than the specific feeling of spiritual union; that infection of this sort is manufactured by professionalism and criticism rather than produced by the work; that the religious perception fixes what counts as union and rules this feeling out from the start. For each, say whether your stipulations leave it open, and whether it succeeds.

(f) End with a verdict on the underlying question: does the case expose a genuine structural instability in the theory, or only a misapplication of it? Commit to one.

Keep the analysis proportionate — roughly 800 to 1,100 words in total after the scenario.
