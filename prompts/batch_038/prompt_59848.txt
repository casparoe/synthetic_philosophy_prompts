Last month I joined the product team for a workplace wellness platform that will be permitted to override an employee's own configured settings whenever its model judges those settings inconsistent with the goals the same employee declared at onboarding. The framing my lead offered in the kickoff was that we should serve the version of the person who turns up to the planning session rather than the version who turns up to the vending machine at three in the afternoon. I could not argue with it in the room and I have not been comfortable since. The nearest respectable ancestor of that framing is coherent extrapolated volition, and the largest-scale historical experiment in it that I can think of is national Prohibition in the United States: a polity that wrote its own extrapolated preferences into its constitution and then tried to live inside them for thirteen years. I would like a disciplined analysis of that episode through the extrapolated-volition lens, running in both directions.

So that you are not guessing at what I mean: extrapolated volition asks what people would want if they knew more, thought faster, were more the people they wished they were, and had grown up farther together, and the coherence clause says an agent should act only on those parts of the extrapolation that agree, holding the options open rather than choosing wherever several attractors remain. The episode, in the facts I take to be settled: the Eighteenth Amendment was ratified by state legislatures in 1919 and took effect in January 1920; the enabling statute permitted medicinal and sacramental use, which became a large and badly policed loophole; enforcement was thin, corrupt, and unable to suppress supply; the repeal campaign gained its most damaging recruits in the late 1920s from formerly dry women's organizations; the Depression made alcohol tax revenue newly attractive; and repeal came in December 1933 through the Twenty-first Amendment, ratified by specially elected state conventions — the only amendment ever ratified that way, and the only amendment ever repealed. After repeal the decision was devolved, so that a wet nation and dry localities could coexist.

What I want from you:

1. The mapping. Whose volition was being extrapolated — the drinker's, his household's, the electorate's, or the polity treated as a single will? Which of the four extrapolation clauses did the drys claim to be applying, and which were they actually applying? And was the fact that many drinkers sincerely endorsed temperance as an ideal while going on drinking evidence of an extrapolated preference, evidence of internalized social pressure, or evidence of an adaptively formed preference of the kind Elster made famous? Commit to one reading and defend it.

2. The coherence clause against the episode. Where did Prohibition act on parts of the extrapolation that did not agree, and where did it close avenues rather than hold them open? Treat the post-1933 devolution as the alternative design and say whether it satisfies the clause.

3. What the theory cannot say. Extrapolated volition tells you what to want, not who may bind whom, when, or irreversibly; self-binding and other-binding are the same operation in the formalism and nothing alike in politics. Mark the places where the framework goes silent instead of stretching it, and say what a theory of idealized preferences would have to add — an endorsement condition, a reversibility condition, a stability condition on the thing being extrapolated — to handle a case like this. Note in particular that thirteen years of illegality altered the very preferences it was meant to serve.

4. The verdict, in both directions. Does Prohibition refute extrapolated volition, embarrass it, or describe a case it never anticipated? And separately, which parts of my product's framing survive the case and which do not.

Rather than reciting consumption or mortality figures you may not be sure of, tell me what the post-1933 evidence would have to look like for the drys' extrapolation to count as vindicated, and what it would have to look like for it to count as refuted.

Bottom line first, in two or three sentences, then the four sections in order. Hard cap of 900 words. No sources and no reading list — I want the analysis, not the bibliography.
