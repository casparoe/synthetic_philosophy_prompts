For several years I ran a small engineering group, and a pattern kept recurring that I have never found a name for. Our safety manual contained a step requiring a valve to be cycled twice before startup; the manual had carried it for decades; nobody living could say why; and yet whenever anyone proposed dropping it as redundant, the response was not a defense but a shrug plus a gesture at the binder — it's in there, so it gets done. A second case: our screening rubric weighted "prior work on legacy systems" heavily, because a founder had argued in an old memo that such work correlated with patience. Years later nobody in the group believed that claim, several people thought it was probably false, and still the weighting survived, because in meetings the memo could be invoked as a reason and nobody had to personally endorse it for it to carry the decision.

What strikes me is that in both cases the justification is not a belief anyone holds, yet it stays fully operative: it is a piece of text that the group keeps re-adopting, and it steers behavior without being in anyone's head. This is not, as I understand it, the situation where a fence stands across a road and you must not remove it until you understand why it was put there — there the point is that a real function may exist and you are ignorant of it, whereas here there may be no function left at all and the written rationale does the work anyway. Nor is it people dressing up a judgment they reached independently: the binder entry genuinely comes first and causes the outcome.

Is there an established concept, in philosophy or in the study of organizations, for a reason that persists as an artifact after the reasons people actually had for it have gone — a justification that remains causally effective while no longer being believed by anyone it governs? If there is one, I want to know how the cases above differ from it and where its limits lie. If there is not, define a term for it and give me the boundary conditions: what separates it from a rationale that is simply unknown, from a rationale that is disbelieved but retained out of prudence, and from a rule imposed by people long dead on people who never agreed to it. A bare label will not help me; I need the edges drawn.
