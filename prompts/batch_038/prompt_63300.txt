I'm running an upper-level phil of mind seminar, 16 students, all of them have read Malcolm and Dennett at this point. I want to hand them a self-sorting quiz — 'which theory of dreaming do you actually hold?' — eight questions, three options each, plus a scoring key. The rule is that every question has to be a case someone could actually report on, either as the sleeper or as the person holding the clipboard: waking with a strong sense you just dreamed but nothing to say about it; the subject who's certain he never dreams and still gives reports off REM awakenings; a report that changes when you wake the same person ten minutes later; a scanner that picks up the gist of the imagery before they're awake. No question may ask anything like 'do you think dreams are experiences?'

The key needs to land people on Malcolm's dreaming-isn't-an-experience view, the cassette theory, the harsher cousin were nothing gets composed at all and the report is straight confabulation, plain experientialism, the imagination/simulation model, and the view that something is experienced during sleep but isn't conscious while it happens. Then tell me which of those the quiz genuinely can't separate, and where an answer pattern has no known view sitting on it, whether that's because the view is incoherent or just unclaimed.
