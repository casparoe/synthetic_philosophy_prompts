I've done process-safety incident investigation for about twelve years — refining and chemicals. My native vocabulary is fault trees and bow-ties: top events, basic events, house events, barrier registers, latent conditions, root cause versus contributing factor. I don't do philosophy, but causation by omission keeps turning up in my reports and I'd like it translated properly rather than waved at.

Here's what I want translated. The debate over whether absences can be causes: the counterfactual view on which my plant died because nobody watered it; the objection that this makes causation absurdly profligate, since millions of other people also didn't water it; the contrastive repair (it died rather than lived, because I didn't water it rather than did); the repair via default or normal conditions; and the view that omissions aren't genuine causes at all, only something weaker that does explanatory work.

Restate each of these in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. What is an omission in a fault tree? What's the counterpart of the profligacy objection — I'd guess it's about how many basic events you're allowed to list, but check me. What does a contrastivist think a basic event actually is? Is a house event really the counterpart of a default condition, or is that too neat? Where does the "omissions aren't real causes" view land — as a claim about modelling conventions, or as something my framework can't even state?

Then the residue report, in three piles: (1) claims that translate cleanly and do the same job in both frameworks; (2) claims that only translate with distortion — say what you bent, and whether the bending changes anyone's answer to the underlying question; (3) claims with no expression at all in a barrier/fault-tree ontology. For pile three, tell me which side is deficient: is my vocabulary missing something that matters for the reports I sign of, or is the philosophers' quarrel mostly bookkeeping that makes no difference to whether the alarm that never sounded was a cause?

One concrete stake. Our house standard won't let me list an absent barrier as a root cause — only as a contributing factor — and I've never worked out whether that's a metaphysical commitment or a management one. If your translation settles which, say so.

And please don't smuggle my assumptions in and call it translation. If a philosophical claim has no fault-tree counterpart, say that rather than inventing one.
