For a history-of-science reading group (15-ish people, mostly grad students, 90 minutes) I'm making a one-page positions map on simultaneous invention — Newton and Leibniz, Darwin and Wallace, the rival telephone filings by Bell and Gray, the disputed 1900 rediscoveries of Mendel. I want a decision tree, not a list of names: every branch node should be a question my group can answer without already knowing where it leads, and every leaf a descriptive label for the position. Six to eight leaves.

The distinction I most want the tree to turn on is upstream of the usual inevitability debate: how finely do you individuate a discovery? If fluxions and the differential calculus are only the same discovery under a coarse description, then much of the multiple-invention evidence could be an artifact of how we describe things rather than a fact about the world. Build the first nodes out of that, then out of the counterfactual — would the result have come anyway? — keeping seperate the at-least-three senses of anyway that people slide between: anyway given the state of the field, anyway given enough capable people, anyway given enough time.

Then cross that with a second axis: does it matter who? I want leaves for priority as pure social convention, priority as a coordination device with instrumental force, and whatever belongs in the cells nobody occupies — for each empty cell, say why it's empty (incoherent, unappealing, or just unexplored). Finish by flagging any pair of leaves your tree can't actually tell apart, and give me one invented case per flagged pair that would seperate them. No references or reading list needed; only attach a person's name to a position if you're confident of it.
