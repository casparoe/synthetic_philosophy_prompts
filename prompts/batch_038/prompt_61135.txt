I am working through Dedekind's *Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen?* (1888) and have hit a tension between two of his claims that I cannot resolve. Since I do not have the Beman translation to hand, let me render both loosely in my own words rather than quote them.

Near the end of the preface to the second edition, Dedekind answers the question posed in his title: numbers are free creations of the human mind, and they serve as a means of apprehending the difference of things more easily and more sharply; only by building up the science of number in a purely logical way, and so acquiring the continuous number-domain, are we enabled to make the idea of a variable magnitude precise and to apply it to space, time and motion.

But in §10 he argues that an infinite system actually exists. His own world of thought — the totality of all things that can be objects of his thought — is infinite, because if s is such a thing, the thought that s can be an object of his thought is another such thing, distinct from s, so the world of thought is mapped injectively into a proper part of itself.

My half-formed reading: this kind of creation is not invention out of nothing but something like stipulative abstraction — what is created is the simply infinite system of positions, not each number separately — and the clause about apprehending the difference of things shows that numbers are there to articulate distinctions rather than to name residents of a separate realm. That would make Dedekind a structuralist rather than either a platonist or a nominalist, with §10 supplying a base domain he does not strictly need.

Can you help me test this? Specifically: what creation can mean for Dedekind when §10 finds the infinite system rather than making it, and whether the appeal to the world of thought lands him with the platonists or the idealists; whether my structuralist reading is consistent with that clause about the difference of things, or whether it points the other way, toward numbers as instruments for discriminating objects, which would be closer to a nominalist use of them; and what each of a contemporary platonist and a contemporary nominalist would identify as the fatal step in my reading. Do not supply citations; I will chase the secondary literature myself.
