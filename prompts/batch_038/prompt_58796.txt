I run localization at a mid-size software company — roughly 400 strings a sprint going out into eleven languages, two of which nobody on my team actually reads, so we lean on vendors and scorecards. Those scorecards grade accuracy, fluency, terminology, style, locale conventions: basically, did the payload arrive intact. A contractor I've worked with for two years has been arguing that for some fraction of our content the scorecard is measuring the wrong thing entirely, and he keeps waving around Hofstadter's book on translation, the one built out of endless versions of a little sixteenth-century French poem, where the claim is roughly that translating isn't content transfer at all but re-performing what the original does — so a system that's just matching surface patterns can score clean and still have done nothing. He also says the same thing about modern neural translation, which I find more annoying because on our content it mostly just works. I took one semester of philosophy of language a long time ago and retained nothing usable, and I have no way to turn his argument into anything my project managers can act on.

So what I want is a triage procedure, not an essay. An ordered set of questions a PM asks about a source string or a batch that routes it one of three ways: payload-dominant (meaning is separable from the wording, machine translation plus light post-editing is fine), performance-dominant (the wording is doing the work and a human has to go re-do the act in the target language), or can't-tell. For each question I need what counts as a sufficient answer, roughly how long it takes, what the PM does when they genuinely can't answer it, and — this matters — which of the questions are judgment calls wearing a procedure costume. It has to survive our volume: call it two minutes per string, fifteen for a batch, run by people with no linguistics training and no time to think about what meaning is. It has to end in a routing decision and a budget line, not a score out of ten.

Then run it on four real things from last sprint: (a) a UI string with a count variable that has to pluralize correctly in Polish and Arabic; (b) a marketing tagline built on an English homophone that I already know doesn't exist in nine of the eleven; (c) a patient-facing leaflet for a connected insulin pen, where the failure mode isn't a mistranslated word but a reader who doesn't do the thing; (d) a changelog, sixty entries, mostly tedious, three of which are jokes the engineers think are very funny. Give me the routing for each and be blunt about the one where your procedure is going to annoy me — I'm braced for it to say the changelog is fine and the leaflet isn't, and I want the reasoning, not a hedge. Finally, tell me what the procedure can't settle and hand back to a human, and give me your honest read on whether the payload/performance split earns its keep or collapses into "scary texts get humans, boring ones don't," which would just be risk ranking with a philosophy hat on.
