As of this month I am chairing a nine-person standing committee that has been asked to produce a single jointly signed statement on the deployment of a contested class of predictive tools in our institution's clinical workflows. The members do not share a moral framework. Two would ground the analysis in patient welfare alone, one in procedural fairness, one in the claims of the people whose records the tools are trained on, and the remainder have declined to say where they stand. What I have been reading in preparation is not committee-management literature but Paul Christiano's HCH thought experiment and the factored-cognition work descended from it: the proposal that a hard judgment becomes tractable when it can be decomposed into small sub-judgments each of which the deliberator can actually check, that the recursive structure of a human consulting copies of a human is our best available idealization of enlightened judgment, and that the question which decides everything is whether checking the parts licenses belief in the whole. Before I let that idealization structure my own process, I want it tested against a real deliberation, and the case I keep returning to is the drafting of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, from the committee's formation through its adoption on 10 December 1948.

The features I take to be uncontroversial, and which I want the analysis to work with rather than re-establish: a drafting committee chaired by Eleanor Roosevelt; a first outline prepared by John Peters Humphrey and then restructured by René Cassin, who described the document's architecture by analogy to the portico of a Greek temple — foundation, steps, four columns, pediment; a sustained philosophical disagreement between Charles Malik, who wanted the declaration grounded in natural law, and P. C. Chang, who pressed for formulations that did not depend on any single metaphysics and who argued for drawing on more than one civilizational tradition; a separate UNESCO committee under Jacques Maritain that surveyed philosophers and public figures around the world on the theoretical basis of human rights and whose results the drafters largely did not use; an autumn 1948 session of the Third Committee in which the text went article by article through something on the order of a hundred and seventy proposed amendments; and a final plenary vote of 48 in favour, none against, 8 abstentions.

Set that against the idealization and give me a disciplined mapping.

First: who is the root deliberator, and what is the root question? Flag the mismatch immediately — HCH amplifies one judgment outward, whereas the Declaration aggregated many judgments that were known in advance to differ. Is the factored-cognition apparatus applicable at all, or only once the committee is treated as a single human by an act of idealization? If the latter, what exactly does that idealization cost, and is the cost visible anywhere in the record?

Second: identify the actual factorings — Cassin's architecture, the delegation to a small working group, the article-by-article amendment procedure, the UNESCO questionnaire. For each, state the child task, the reassembly logic, who was in a position to verify the child's output, and whether verification was possible even in principle. Where the theory has nothing to say, say so rather than making it speak.

Third: the UNESCO channel is the case within the case. An output was produced and then set aside by the parent. Is that a factoring failure or a correct pruning, and what does the discarding reveal about where the drafters located the hard part of the problem?

Fourth, and what I most want: the Declaration repeatedly terminates a sub-question by encoding it in the text instead of answering it — Maritain's own diagnosis, as I understand it, was that people had converged on a list while disagreeing about its foundation. Introduce a distinction between a sub-question that is resolved and one that is deferred into the language of the output, and tell me whether deferral is unfaithfulness or a legitimate result for which the framework has no vocabulary. If the latter, what has to be added to the framework to accommodate it?

Fifth: does 48–0–8 indicate a faithful factoring, or indicate that the root question was answered at a level of abstraction high enough that nobody present could contradict it? What would the framework have predicted about the later splitting of the covenants along civil-political and economic-social lines — confirmation, anomaly, or a case it never anticipated?

Close with whatever design rules for my own committee the analysis genuinely licenses, marked as licensed rather than dressed up as consequences of the theory. I am not asking for sources or a reading list, and I would rather have two honest places where the mapping fails than a smooth one.
