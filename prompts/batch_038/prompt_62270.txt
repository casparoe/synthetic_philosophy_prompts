I build order books for a living — market microstructure at a prop shop: quotes, spreads, depth, inventory limits, adverse selection, price impact, oracle and data-vendor settlement, listing standards, circuit breakers, the whole plumbing. Lately I've been chewing on futarchy, Hanson's proposal that a polity should vote on values but bet on beliefs, using prediction markets on national welfare conditional on each candidate policy actually being adopted, and letting the market prices pick the policy.

Here's what I want. Translate that proposal into my vocabulary, item by item, as literally as you can. Not a lay-audience analogy — the actual mapping. Where's the order book, who's the market maker and what's their inventory exposure, what's the listing standard for a policy, what settles the contract and against what oracle, what does "the market's estimate of welfare under policy A" correspond to mechanically as opposed to rhetorically. Then do the same for the standard objections: manipulation by traders with deep pockets, the difficulty of specifying the payoff metric, thin markets, conditional-versus-unconditional pricing, and the complaint that a decision reached by clearing prices lacks legitimacy.

And I have a hunch I want pressure applied to. My read is that the conditional leg is really a derivative written on a derivative. Only one policy gets adopted, so the other market's payoff gets settled against realized welfare in a world where the thing it was supposedly pricing never happened — meaning its a market whose payoff is contaminated by the leg that won. On that diagnosis it should trade with double the price impact and roughly half the depth of an ordinary welfare market, and any large inventory could drag it around right up untill the moment the decision is taken. If that's right, a lot of the agonizing over conditional markets is a contract-specification and liquidity problem wearing a philosophy costume, and I'd want to know which parts of the debate survive that diagnosis and which evaporate.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. Go through the pieces and say cleanly what maps, what you had to bend or fudge to make it fit, and what has no counterpart in microstructure terms at all. For everything that doesn't map, take a position — is that my vocabulary genuinely missing something, or is the futarchy literature arguing about something less determinate than it presents itself as being?

Commit to something. I don't want a both-sides shrug at the end; if you think the translation mostly works or mostly fails, say which and say why. And if you think my hunch is just wrong, or that the whole request is malformed because futarchy's conditional markets don't work anything like the way I described them, say that plainly up front instead of humoring me and building an elaborate answer on top of a bad premise. No references or reading lists, please — I can't look anything up anyway.
