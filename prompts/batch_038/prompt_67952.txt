I am a backend software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, so let me explain the framing before the request: I have been reading about the evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics, and in particular Sharon Street's Darwinian dilemma for realist theories of value, together with the standard realist responses to it. What frustrates me about the material I have found is that it restates each position at length without ever forcing a head-to-head comparison, so I would like to convert the disagreement into something closer to a reliability assessment.

Please treat the following stipulated empirical finding as a given, even though it is not what the current evidence suggests. Suppose that selection shaped not the content of our moral beliefs but the architecture of the mechanism that produces them, and that this architecture has one invariant property: the mechanism cannot output any moral belief without first taking in at least one intuitively compelling input, which it may then weight, combine, generalise or override, but never discard entirely or bypass. On this stipulation there is no intuition-free route to a moral belief, for anyone, however reflective.

Given that stipulation, please rank the five realist responses below from the one that preserves the most to the one that preserves the least of the realist's epistemic position, and assign each a score out of ten for how much of that position survives:

A. The third-factor or tracking response: survival and reproductive success are themselves good, so a cognitive process selected for fitness is thereby directed at the truth, and no unexplained coincidence arises.

B. The parity or self-defeat response: if the debunker's standard disqualifies moral belief, it disqualifies logical, mathematical and perceptual belief as well, so the standard cannot be coherently deployed against the realist alone.

C. The relaxed or minimal realist response: normative truths are not the kind of thing whose correspondence with our beliefs requires a causal explanation, since grasping them is partly constitutive of being a deliberating agent.

D. The second-horn response: the dilemma's demand for an explanation of the correlation already assumes that normative truths are entities capable of standing in causal relations, which the realist denies, so the burden has simply been shifted.

E. The counterfactual robustness response: even had selection inclined us towards different first-order judgements, our capacity for reflective scrutiny would have corrected the error, so our beliefs are safe rather than luckily true.

Format: answer in British English. Give exactly five numbered items (1 to 5), in ranked order, each containing the letter of the response, its score out of ten, and one or two sentences explaining the score by reference to the stipulated architecture specifically rather than to the response's general merits. If two responses genuinely cannot be separated, give them the same score and say so inside the relevant item. No introduction, no conclusion, and no closing remarks.
