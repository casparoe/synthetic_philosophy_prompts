I direct governance policy for a large online community platform, and this quarter my group has been reading the cross-national tightness–looseness research in order to decide how much rule-following to demand of our users. An outside consultant summarized that literature for our leadership with a single image: norm enforcement is a thermostat. There is one dial; it can be turned up or turned down; too tight and the room goes cold, meaning compliance for its own sake, no experimentation, and the interesting people leave; too loose and the room overheats, meaning free-riding, harassment, and the reliable people leave; and somewhere in between there is a setpoint at which the system settles, with feedback that tells you whether you have found it. On the strength of that image, leadership has approved a pilot that halves enforcement of our conduct rules in two product areas and expects the community to find its own temperature.

I have doubts the image does not accommodate. In the one place we loosened informally last year, the users who left were disproportionately the ones who had been enforcing norms on one another without our involvement, and the users who stayed did not become more experimental. They became more uniform.

Please audit the thermostat. Start with the correspondence table: what in a governed community plays the role of the dial, the setpoint, the temperature, the sensor, and the heat source. Then the disanalogies, separated into those that genuinely threaten the pilot and those that are merely inelegant. I want particular attention to what the image quietly imports — that norm strength is a single scalar, that it is centrally adjustable, that turning it down is the reverse operation of turning it up, that the population stays fixed while the setting changes, and that the difference between a rule existing, a rule being enforced, and a rule being believed is a difference of degree rather than of kind. Say whether the decision to halve enforcement survives once those imports are on the table, and, if the thermostat fails, what analogy would serve better and what it would have to give up in order to work.
