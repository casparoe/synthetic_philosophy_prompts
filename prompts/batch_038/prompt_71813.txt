You are an ethicist serving as the staff adviser to a state wildlife agency's seven-member science and policy committee. This cycle the committee must rule on a single three-year pilot grant: a proposed immunocontraception program for a high-fecundity prey species on agency-managed land, put forward explicitly on animal-welfare grounds rather than conservation grounds. The proposal argues that reducing births would spare large numbers of juveniles the starvation, parasitism, and predation that currently kill most of them in their first year, while keeping the population above its ecological floor; two committee members reply that the agency has no mandate to manage populations for welfare, and that wildness itself is a value the agency exists to protect. The vote is Thursday, and the chair has asked you for a written opinion in advance.

Draft that opinion, roughly 700 words. It should open with your bottom line — approve, reject, or approve with conditions, plus the specific conditions if any — then give reasons the members could actually debate. Take seriously the two questions the proposal forces: whether the suffering of wild animals counts morally in the same way as the suffering of animals the agency already manages, such as livestock and captive wildlife, and whether the naturalness of a harm gives the agency a reason to leave it alone. Do not resolve those questions by fiat; commit to a position and defend it. State the strongest consideration on the losing side and explain what would have to be true for you to change your recommendation. Reason from general principles and the facts given here rather than citing particular studies, statistics, or named authorities.

After the opinion, add a JSON object with exactly these fields: "recommendation" (one of approve, reject, approve_with_conditions); "conditions" (array of strings, empty if not applicable); "confidence" (a number from 0 to 1); "key_premise" (the single claim your recommendation most depends on, in one sentence); "strongest_counter" (the best objection against your recommendation, in one sentence); "rebuttal" (your response, in one sentence); "counts_wild_suffering" (true or false, reflecting how you resolved the moral-considerability question); "naturalness_constraint" (one of binding, presumptive, or irrelevant, reflecting how you resolved the naturalness question); and "decision_rule" (a one-sentence rule the committee could apply to the next such proposal).
