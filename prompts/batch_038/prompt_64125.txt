so i've been going around in circles on this for a few months and i've decided i want a poem instead of another argument, because the argument keeps beating itself up.

background, so you know what you're dealing with: no philosophy training whatsoever, i just read stuff on my phone at night. i stumbled into eliminative materialism, the view that beliefs and desires aren't real items waiting for better names, they're more like phlogiston or the four humors — posits from a rotten old theory that we'll just stop using once we get a decent one. what hooked me wasn't the theory though, it's what i keep running into when i go looking. i'll sit with some decision and try to catch the belief underneath it and i never catch anything, i catch the decision-shaped behavior and a picture in my head. like a drawer with a label stuck on the front and nothing inside.

here's what i want. six stanzas, one ordinary moment described six times — something small and physical, say a guy sitting in his car in his own driveway after work, engine off, not going in yet. every stanza is the same moment, recognizable, but each one loses a category of words:

1. everything: what he believes, wants, intends, feels, and the first person
2. no desire words left (want, hope, need, dread, wish)
3. no belief or thought words either (think, reckon, suppose, mean, know that)
4. no intention words (decide, choose, try, plan, resolve)
5. no feeling words and no I at all — only what someone could see from the street
6. same bare vocabulary as 5, but this one has to actually say what the theory says

stanza 5 is where i want the turn to land, that's my drawer going empty. and stanza 6 is where it should hurt: someone told me the whole view refutes itself, since if there are no beliefs then nobody believes it, and i want that stanza to walk into that wall rather than around it — strained, unfinished, whatever it takes, as long as it doesn't cheat. don't let it grab "neurons" or "brain states" as a rescue word, because the point isn't that science has nicer labels for the same things, its that there were never any things. and no explaining yourself in the poem, no thesis line at the end, no wink.

i want somebody who's never heard the term to read it as a good poem about a man in a driveway, and somebody who has to read it as a correct one. mostly i just want to find out whether the idea survives being said without any of its own words.
