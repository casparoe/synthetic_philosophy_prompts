Our community garden is run by an elected board of seven, and everything is supposed to happen through the bylaws that the members vote on. Two neighbors who both sit on the board got into a genuine argument about this last Thursday, with maybe twenty plot-holders standing around listening, and I still dont know who came out ahead.

The situation: there's an article in the bylaws, passed properly years ago by a big vote, saying that anyone who lets their plot go unplanted for a season forfeits it and the board reassigns it. Two wrinkles. It says the forfeiture applies to any season before the article was adopted, so it reaches backwards. And no board has used it in something like twelve years — it isn't even in the membership handbook they hand out now, and the chair told a newcomer last spring it was dead letter. Now that same chair wants to use it against a guy named Dov who's been fighting her about the water bill, and Dov's plot did go fallow once in a season before the article existed, back when the garden was on the other lot.

Here's how it went.

1) Marisol said the chair simply can't do that — not that it's unwise, but that there's nothing there to apply. A rule that isn't published to members, that punishes conduct from before it existed, that's been ignored for a decade, and that gets dug up only against the one person the chair is feuding with, isn't a rule, it's the board's mood with a stamp on it. She said pile up enough of those failures and there's no rule left, so the chair has no authority to touch Dov's plot.

2) Ken said that's backwards, and also dangerous. The article was adopted by the members using the procedure the members themselves set up, and that's what makes it binding. Retroactive, ignored, unfair to Dov — those are reasons to repeal it or to refuse to apply it, but they don't turn it into a non-rule. He said if "that one's unjust so it was never really binding" is allowed to work, every future board gets a free way to dissolve any rule it dislikes: just declare it unfair and poof. He said he'd rather stand up and say "this is a real rule and it's rotten, and I'm declining to enforce it," because then everybody can see the choice being made and blame the right people.

3) Marisol said Ken's version sounds more honest but does more damage. Under her account the chair has to justify reaching into a drawer for a twelve-year-dead rule to use against one specific person, and she can't. Under his, the rule sits there fully valid and the only open question is whether the chair feels merciful — which makes Dov keeping his plot a gift from the board instead of something he was entitled to. She said that's not wordplay, it's a question of who owes reasons to whom.

4) Ken said fine, then asked where her line is. One season of non-enforcement? Five? If the board ignores the article for a decade and then applies it evenly to eleven people at once, is it a rule again? Marisol said she'd read that in some places a law that's been left alone long enough just dies on its own and she wanted to know why that wouldn't cover this. Ken said that only works if the club's own rules say disuse kills a rule — and theirs don't — which is his whole point: what makes something binding here is a social fact about what the members did, not whether it's decent.

5) Marisol said the test isn't a number of years, it's whether people can actually plan their lives around the rule, and Dov couldn't have. Ken said that's a nice-sounding test any board satisfies by printing a newsletter.

6) Then a third neighbor cut in and said you two are arguing about the word "rule" and nothing else, since you both agree Dov keeps his plot. Marisol said it matters because next time the vote goes the other way and one of them is the one deciding what to do. Ken said that was the first thing he'd agreed with her about all night.

So — go through it round by round and tell me who's right at each one, not just who wins the whole thing. I've never studied any of this and I don't know what the two positions are called, so if there are real terms for them please define each in one sentence instead of assuming I know. Also tell me what both of them are missing, and give me a straight answer on the third neighbor's point: was it actually just a fight about words? Under 500 words please.
