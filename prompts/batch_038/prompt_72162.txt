I am drafting a section of a monograph on the rival accounts of distributive justice, and I want to pressure-test prioritarianism against a pair of real decisions rather than against the abstract examples the literature usually runs on. What follows is a case-analysis brief. Please treat the history as settled — I have supplied the facts I consider load-bearing — and do not spend words recounting it back to me.

**The material.** In 1962 the city of Jackson, Mississippi operated five public parks with swimming pools on a segregated basis: four pools were for white residents only, one for Black residents only. A federal district court declared that arrangement unconstitutional but declined to issue an injunction. Jackson then desegregated its parks, auditoriums, golf courses and zoo, but its city council declined to attempt integrated operation of the pools; it surrendered the lease on one pool and closed the four it owned. Black citizens sued to compel the city to reopen and integrate them. The district court found the closure justified to preserve peace and order and because the pools could not be operated economically on an integrated basis; the court of appeals, sitting en banc, affirmed with six of thirteen judges dissenting; and the Supreme Court affirmed in *Palmer v. Thompson* (1971). Justice Black's majority opinion stressed, among other things, that "neither the Fourteenth Amendment nor any Act of Congress purports to impose an affirmative duty on a State to begin to operate or to continue to operate swimming pools," and distinguished *Griffin v. County School Board of Prince Edward County* (1964), where a Virginia county had shut its public schools rather than desegregate them while state and county tuition grants and property-tax credits supported a whites-only private academy, and where schools elsewhere in the state stayed open.

**What I want.**

1. **The mapping.** State prioritarianism precisely enough to apply it: the currency of advantage, the strictly increasing and concave weighting of absolute levels, and the baseline against which "worse off" is measured here. Then say what the view actually implies about the Jackson closure — for the Black residents who lost their only pool, for the white residents who lost four, and for the city as a whole. Be explicit about where the weighting function does decisive work and where it does not.

2. **Is this leveling down at all?** Parfit's objection is aimed at a telic egalitarian who must concede that a world in which everyone is equally worse off is in one respect better. Determine whether Jackson's decision is an instance of that structure, a merely apparent one, or something the objection was never formulated to cover — noting in particular that the closure was not symmetric in opportunity cost, and that a *pure* leveling down would have to be constructed counterfactually. If you construct it, say what assumptions the construction smuggles in.

3. **Test the claimed immunity.** Prioritarians advertise that their view escapes the leveling-down objection. Ask whether the escape is substantive or purchased at the cost of silence: in the pure case, does prioritarianism have nothing at all to say about the *choice* to close the pools, so that the whole of its condemnation must be imported from non-distributive considerations (waste, expressive harm, the loss of a public good)? If so, is that a defect or a correct diagnosis of what was really at stake in Jackson?

4. **The Griffin contrast.** Does the prioritarian distinction between the two episodes track the distinction the Court drew, and should it? What does prioritarianism say about a symmetric version of Prince Edward County — every child, white and Black, out of school, no tuition grants, no academy — and is the fact that a state-of-affairs theory cannot register *why* the closure was chosen, or *by whom*, an objection to it or a feature of any view of that shape?

5. **Verdict in both directions.** Conclude with an honest assessment: does this pair confirm prioritarianism, expose an anomaly, or fall into a category the theory never anticipated? Name any place where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak, and where my facts underdetermine the analysis.

Aim for roughly 1,400 words under five headed sections corresponding to the above. No bibliography, reading list, case citations or footnote apparatus — I want argument, not apparatus. If you are unsure of a historical detail beyond what I have given, flag the uncertainty rather than filling it in.
