Here's my opening. Two first-order states, call them V and V*, both carrying the content <red patch, two o'clock>, but produced by two seperate pathways off the retina. Normally they run in lockstep. I can dial an arbitrary delay into V*'s pathway, so for a ten-second window only V is present, and after that only V* is present because V has decayed. Now stipulate — this is important, don't try to wiggle out here — that your monitor is perfectly well-behaved throughout: whenever either V or V* is present it fires and produces a higher-order representation with that same content. So the subject's introspective profile is identical at every moment. Same reports, same confidence, same reasoning, no gap, no confusion, no error. Nobody, including the subject and including you from the outside, could ever tell which window they're in.

So: is the conscious experience in the early window the same experience as the one later? Your theory pins consciousness down by higher-order content plus the fact that the higher-order state is *about* some first-order state. But aboutness can't be fixed by content here, since V and V* are content-identical, and it can't be fixed by the subject's evidence, since that's identical too. Which leaves a brute causal fact about which vehicle the monitor happened to be latched onto.
