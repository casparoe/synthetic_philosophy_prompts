I am preparing a talk on the problem of logical omniscience, and before I commit to a position publicly I would like an audit of whether my own package hangs together. Here is what I intend to say.

(1) Propositions are sets of possible worlds, and believing is a two-place relation between a subject and such a set. I am not prepared to give up possible-worlds content.

(2) I reject hyperintensional refinements — impossible worlds, structured propositions, guises or modes of presentation — as either ontologically suspect or as restating the problem in new vocabulary.

(3) Logical omniscience is not a benign idealization. A theory on which anyone who believes the axioms of arithmetic thereby believes every arithmetical truth is straightforwardly false of me, and I do not want that falsity excused by saying the theory is only about ideal agents.

(4) Probabilistic coherence is a genuine requirement of rationality: an agent with positive credence in a contradiction is irrational in virtue of that, however cognitively limited they may be.

(5) Rational norms must be norms finite agents can comply with, and I follow Harman in treating clutter avoidance as itself a rational virtue — it can be a defect to accumulate the trivial consequences of what one already believes.

(6) The right model of update for such agents is Jeffrey conditionalization rather than strict conditionalization on something certain; evidence need not come with certainty.

(7) Deductive work is epistemically significant: reasoning from what I already believed to a theorem I did not believe teaches me something, and an account on which I believed it all along gets that wrong.

Go through this and tell me which of these genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what the cheapest revision would be, and what that revision costs me — including where the cheapest fix is not the one that first suggests itself. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and end by telling me which single commitment, if modified, would clean up the most.
