**Draft: Mutual revisability, or why meshing isnt enough**

1. Bratman's account of shared intention says roughly that we intend to J when each of us intends that we J, those intentions interlock and mutually support one another, and our sub-plans mesh. The meshing condition is normally glossed as a consistency requirement: our respective plans for how to bring J about have to be compatible.

2. That gloss is too weak, because lots of pairs of agents have perfectly compatible plans without doing anything together. Two strangers on a train who each plan to keep out of the other's way mesh impeccably. A pair on an assembly line working to a fixed script, each executing instructions out of the same manual, mesh by construction. In neither case is there something they are doing together; there are just two causal processes that happen not to collide.

3. What's missing isn't interlocking intentions or common knowledge, it's a loop. Call the condition mutual revisability: each participant can propose a change to the plan-in-progress, and the others are disposed to take that proposal up as a reason to alter their own contribution, inside the same episode of deliberation. Joint action on this picture is a process that any of its participants can steer, not a state that their plans happen to be consistent with.

4. This gets the AI case right, at least superficially. Me and a model co-writing a section: I propose a change, it revises its draft, I revise mine in light of that, it revises again. The loop is live and symmetric in the sense that matters — either party can move the plan. So there's joint action, even though the model plausibly has no intentions at all. That's actually a selling point, if the condition is stated dispositionally (how the parties respond) rather than in terms of attitudes.

5. Cost: it also denies joint deliberation to a sealed-ballot committee. Members who vote anonymously and never see each other's proposals until the result is announced arguably satisfy interlocking intentions and meshing plans while failing revisability. On my view they aggregated; they didn't decide together.

6. Objection (my mate's, and I think it's the good one): this makes committee decisions not collective, which is close to absurd. And on the AI side — revisability without standing is just an editing channel. I can propose changes to the model's draft, but it can't hold me to anything, can't resent me, has no claim on me. That's Gilbert's territory: joint commitment is what generates the obligations and the standing to rebuke, so my condition quietly presupposes something like it while pretending to be cheaper.

7. My reply, which I suspect is the weak joint in the whole structure: standing isn't needed, only a disposition to treat the other's proposals as reasons. But then my account is a deflated Gilbert, and I cant say what makes the disposition a joint one rather than two parallel dispositions that happen to line up.
