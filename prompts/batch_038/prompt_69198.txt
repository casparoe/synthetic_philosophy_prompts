I teach a second-year seminar on law and political morality — 18 students, one 100 minute session, no prep reading. They'll have had one lecture giving the bare outline of the postwar tribunals and nothing more. Design an in-class structured deliberation that makes them feel the difference between two complaints that always get fused together under the label victors' justice: that the charges were retroactive, and that the court was staffed, funded and framed by one side only. I want them to notice you can grant the first and still press the second, and the reverse too — and I want them to hit the queasy part, which is that the no-crime-without-law principle is something they'll want to invoke on behalf of people they think deserve punishment most, and they'll feel the pull to carve an exception for exactly those people.

Setup requirements. Use an invented war between two invented coalitions, not the real 1939–45 one, so nobody's recall of history is being tested and the case does the work. I'd like a drafting phase where the losing side's delegates get to write exactly one article of the tribunal's charter and the winners are bound to accept it, then a sealed-envelope reveal partway through containing the winning coalition's own conduct — you invent the contents, something along the lines of a sustained bombing campaign against cities, a merchant-shipping campaign, and a decree passed after the acts it's meant to cover. Finish with a neutral panel of students who held no other role deciding whether the convictions stand up; that's the only stakes I want, no points or money.

Give me: role sheets and how many of each, the one-page charter template with the blank articles, a minute-by-minute timeline, the debrief questions in the order I should ask them (name the technical terms only there, not before), what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it, and an honest paragraph on which feature of the real problem the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself. No reading list or bibliography. And if you do gesture at the actual tribunals anywhere in the debrief, keep it to what your confident about and flag the rest as uncertain.
