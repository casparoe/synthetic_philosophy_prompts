I co-run a little prediction market for about a dozen coworkers (points, convertible to a bar tab at the end of the quarter) and there's a pattern I keep seeing that I don't have a word for.

Two people land on different numbers for the same question — say 30% vs 70% on whether some product ships this year. Neither budges from talking it through, because each knows the other is smart and has done the reading, so each figures the other must know something. So they bet. And then they keep betting, and what ends the disagreement isn't that one of them gets convinced. It's that one of them runs out of points to keep taking the other side. Whoever's got the deeper bankroll basically sets where the price lands, and then everybody else looks at that price and goes "market's at 65, so probably around 65." Meanwhile the person who got worn down will happily tell you afterwards they still think it's 30%.

So the group gets to unanimity without anyone changing their mind, and then treats the unanimity as if it were information.

Is there an established concept for this? I know the no-trade theorem says rational people with common priors shouldn't be trading speculatively at all, and I know Aumann's thing about people who know each other to be rational not being able to agree to disagree — but neither seems to describe what's happening here, since here there's tons of trade and the trade is what manufactures the agreement. Closes concepts I can think of are "limits of arbitrage" and noise-trader risk, but those look like they're about why a wrong price hangs around, not about agreement being produced by who can afford to keep holding on.

Is there a name for agreement-by-attrition as opposed to agreement-by-updating? If not, coin one and define it properly — I care about the edges more than the label. What wouldn't count: a bookie posting odds nobody bets against? one whale dominating? two people who just get bored of arguing and settle? And how is it different from plain herding, where people copy each other instead of grinding each other down? No need for a reading list, just the concepts.
