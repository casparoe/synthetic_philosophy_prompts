Write an essay that adjudicates a dispute about what the Death in Damascus problem really demonstrates. Treat the following stipulated version of the case as authoritative, and do not import details from any retelling you may have encountered.

A traveler in Damascus encounters Death, who looks startled and informs him that they have an appointment the following day. The traveler knows that the city entered in Death's appointment book was written before the encounter and cannot now be altered. He may spend the night in Damascus or ride to Aleppo. If he wakes in the city named in the book, he dies; if he wakes in the other, he survives. Survival is worth 1000 units, death 0, and the ride costs 10. Death is an unerring reader of the traveler: the city in the book matches the city the traveler will occupy.

Under evidential decision theory, each option is catastrophic news about itself. Conditional on riding to Aleppo, the book says Aleppo; conditional on staying, the book says Damascus. Deliberation accordingly fails to converge: the traveler's assessment of an option and his inclination toward it chase each other around a circle. Neither option is ratifiable, where an act counts as ratifiable only if the agent, having become fully confident that she will perform it, would still prefer it to the alternatives as evaluated under that very confidence. The stock escape is to let the traveler settle instead on a randomized policy, at which point the oscillation is said to terminate in a self-consistent state.

The controversy you are to address is whether that escape works, and what its success or failure implies about evidential decision theory. Roughly, four readings compete: (i) the case is a genuine pathology of evaluating acts by the news they bring, since it makes recommendation hostage to the agent's credences about her own acts; (ii) the instability is an artifact of idealizing prediction as infallible, and would damp out under any finite reliability; (iii) the instability is benign, showing only that pure-strategy deliberation can fail to converge, with randomization the correct and principled termination; (iv) the case is not a well-posed decision problem at all, and theories should be judged only on well-posed ones.

Your essay should argue its way to one of these readings, or to a better one of your own, and in doing so should address at least the following.

First, the mechanism. Distinguish two ways of stipulating Death's infallibility: the book matches the traveler's realized choice, versus the book matches the traveler's settled policy or credence about that choice, so that a traveler settled on an even randomization faces a Death who flips a coin. Determine whether the mixed-strategy escape is available under each stipulation. If it is available only under the second, is that a substantive discovery about rational choice, or a quiet downgrade of the predictor dressed up as a resolution?

Second, the asymmetry. Suppose the ride costs not 10 but 400 units — a sizable fraction of the value of survival. Does this select a unique self-consistent policy? Should the recommendation change continuously as the cost rises from 10 toward 900, and if it does not change, why not?

Third, side bets. Suppose the traveler is simultaneously offered a ticket costing 1 unit that pays 12 if he survives. What should a settled policy say about the ticket, and would a mismatch between that verdict and the verdict about the cities discredit the settled policy as a whole?

Finally, the requirement itself. If ratifiability can fail of every option in a case, is the right conclusion that the requirement must be weakened or restricted, or that the case is degenerate and therefore no verdict is owed?

Do not end on a non-committal note or with a mere survey of the options. State plainly which reading you accept, justify it, and defend it against the strongest objection available to the reading you reject. Argue from the stipulated case and from general principles of decision theory; no bibliography, citations, or references are required or wanted.
