Here's the situation. I teach a grad seminar on reduction and explanation, and a pattern keeps surfacing — in student papers, in stuff I referee, in the endless AI-and-consciousness discourse — and I don't have a clean name for it, which annoys me, becuase I need one to be able to say "that's the thing you're doing, stop it."

The pattern: someone offers what looks like a textbook bottom-up explanation of some high-level capacity. Every step is a mechanism. No ghosts, no design-first postulates, nothing you could reasonably call a skyhook — and if you press them on it they're entirely right that they haven't appealed to anything non-natural or top-down. And yet when you trace where the explanatory lift is actually coming from, the capacity being explained was already sitting in the setup, distributed across a bunch of individually innocent choices made before the mechanism ever started running: which environment the simulation gets dropped into, what the reward is defined over, what counts as the stimulus, which idealizations were made, what "solved" means in the write-up. So the ascent is real and the height was pre-loaded.

Three instances, so this isn't just an abstraction:

(1) An artificial-life demo where agents evolve the ability to find food. Presented as design without a designer — and at the level of the algorithm, it is. But the fitness function is a restatement of the target behavior, and the world was built by someone who already knew what solving the problem looks like, so the search space is pre-seeded with the answer. Remove the one correlation the builder put in and nothing evolves at all.

(2) A naturalizing account of content on which a neural state's meaning is fixed by its causal covariance with X. Fine — except that the covariance is constituted by the experimenter's decision about what counts as X and how it's presented. The content gets fixed at the level of experimental design and then "discovered" in the tissue.

(3) A model trained on people's moral judgments that is then offered as an explanation of why people make those judgments. The fit is genuinely impressive and genuinely irrelevant to the question asked.

I know what the neighbors are and none of them is it:

- Greedy reductionism — but that's about flattening too fast, skipping levels, explaining too much too soon. What I'm describing isn't too fast; its not about speed at all. The levels are all there and honestly traversed.
- The homunculus move — there's no little person in here, and that's precisely the point. The smuggled competence isn't located in any component; it's smeared across the setup, and each smear is defensible on its own.
- Dormitive virtue / circular explanation — the explanation isn't literally the effect renamed. The mechanism does real work, it makes novel predictions, it would be a fine piece of engineering.
- Begging the question — sort of, but question-begging assumes some premise of the arguer's is doing the work, and here no single premise is load-bearing.
- Regress stuff and promissory notes about future explanation — not it either. These people aren't deferring, they're claiming they've arrived.
- And I know there's a literature about whose intentionality a machine's intentionality is borrowed from, but that's aimed squarely at (2) and doesn't obviously reach (1) or (3).
