Quick one, and I know this is a slightly weird ask. I write novels — literary-ish — and one of my recurring characters is a philosopher of science. In the new book there's a wake for her brother, and halfway through she's a couple of glasses in and breaks the tension with a joke. She's a Humean about laws of nature; the man shes trading with, her brother's old supervisor, emphatically is not.

The joke has to be about that fight specifically. My own attempt was a Humean walking into a bar demanding to be shown the law of nature, which I now see is both unfunny and backwards, since she's the one who thinks there's nothing extra there to be shown — on her side the laws are downstream of the whole pattern of particular events, the best compression of what happens, not something sitting above it pushing things around; on his side there really is some governing oomph over and above the pattern, and her view leaves the laws explanatorily hollow. That's the bone I want the joke chewing on.

So: write me the joke, plus one alternate that's a bit safer for readers who've never met any of this. Constraints — the punchline has to turn on the content of the two positions, not on jargon sounding funny; no lightbulb jokes, nothing walks into a bar, and it can't depend on knowing any real philosopher's name or any book title. It has to be sayable aloud by someone slightly drunk and slightly wrecked, so keep it tight. And it's fine — maybe good — if it mines the setting: a room full of people who would dearly like the universe to amount to more than a record of what happened.

Then, in a couple of sentences each, tell me why it lands for someone who knows the debate, and what the supervisor's comeback should be. I want him to win the second beat, so give me a retort that is an actual objection to her position — one a real anti-Humean would make — and not just a wisecrack.
