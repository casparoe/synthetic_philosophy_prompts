"Maxwell's original demon sat at a trapdoor in a partition between two chambers of gas and let fast molecules through in one direction and slow ones in the other, producing a temperature difference out of an equilibrium system with no apparent expenditure. Szilard stripped the case down to a single molecule in a box. The demon first measures which half of the box the molecule occupies; it then inserts a piston at the midline and allows the molecule to expand isothermally against it, extracting work kT ln 2 from a single heat bath. That is a violation of Kelvin's formulation, since heat has been turned entirely into work with no other change. The engine works because the measurement supplied one bit of information about the molecule, and one bit is worth exactly kT ln 2 of work.

Landauer's principle is what closes the loophole. It says that any logically irreversible operation — paradigmatically the resetting of a memory bit to a standard state — must dissipate at least kT ln 2 of heat into the surroundings. Erasure maps two distinguishable physical states onto one, and by Shannon's result the lost bit has to reappear as entropy somewhere; the principle follows from the definition of information itself rather than from any contingent property of matter. So once the demon completes its cycle and wipes its memory it repays exactly the kT ln 2 it drew out, and the second law is restored with equality.

Where the cost falls is therefore a subtle question. Bennett's point that the demon must erase in order to return to its initial state is really Brillouin's older argument in new dress: Brillouin held that the demon cannot see the molecule without a light source whose entropy cost exceeds the gain, which is just to say that acquisition cannot be had for less than kT ln 2. Szilard was right that the cost is fundamentally located in the measurement. A demon with an unbounded memory that never reset itself could accordingly keep running at no net thermodynamic cost, though no actual computer could do this, since finite memory forces eventual erasure. Information is physical; thermodynamics survives because you cannot know without paying."
