I have seventy-five minutes with a second-year physics seminar to teach the bucket argument and the dispute over whether space is a thing in its own right or only a system of relations among bodies. These students have just finished a mechanics course in which they derived the centrifugal and Coriolis terms, they have watched a Foucault pendulum, and they can tell you that a curved water surface is a signature of acceleration relative to an inertial frame. That background is my main problem: several of them will treat the metaphysical question as already settled by their coursework, and one philosophy major will try to steer everything toward whether general relativity decides it. I have a whiteboard, a motorized turntable, a shallow dish of water, and a phone camera.

Plan the session rather than explaining the argument to me. Tell me what has to come before what and why each step depends on the previous one; which single demonstration should carry the whole thing, what it must show, and what it conspicuously fails to show; where in the sequence to break the reaction that mechanics has already answered this, without implying their physics was wrong; the misconceptions they will form — I expect something along the lines of the distant stars literally causing the curvature, and the idea that relationism is merely an aesthetic preference — and the point at which to head each off; how to handle the general-relativity detour, including whether to admit it at all; one closing question that would reveal whether the distinction between absolute velocity and absolute acceleration actually landed; and what I should openly refuse to cover at this level.

Where the issue is genuinely contested among people who work on it rather than settled, mark it as such in the plan, so that I do not teach a live dispute to undergraduates as established fact. Begin with your bottom line — the one-sentence spine of the session and the demonstration that carries it — and then give the plan, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
