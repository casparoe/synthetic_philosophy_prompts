From next month I sit on my school's Student Council Rules Panel, and I would like a decision procedure the panel can actually run. Two or three proposals reach us each month — for instance, banning anonymous posts about teachers on the school forum, requiring club presidents to pledge not to publicly deny the council's legitimacy, restructuring the school day — and we get twenty minutes each before writing a recommendation that the head teacher signs off. Nobody on the panel has studied law or philosophy, and at present we decide by whoever talks loudest.

Please build the procedure out of Spinoza's case for democracy in the Theological-Political Treatise: his view that the aim of a commonwealth is freedom rather than obedience; that its right extends to what people do and not to what they judge or believe; that a rule which can only be followed hypocritically breeds resentment and instability rather than peace; and the line he draws between merely voicing an opinion and acting in a way that usurps the authority of the commonwealth, since most of our proposals sit close to that line. Give me an ordered set of questions we can work through in twenty minutes: what each question tests, what counts as a sufficient answer to it, at which question a proposal dies outright, at which point the procedure must hand the decision back to a person because the question is one of value rather than fact, and what to do when we cannot tell which side of that line a case falls on.

Then apply it to three proposals: banning anonymous posts that mock teachers on the school forum; requiring elected club presidents to sign a pledge not to publicly call the council illegitimate; permitting an organised walkout during a whole-school assembly. I expect at least one of these to fail, and if the procedure clears something I would personally rather see restricted, please say so plainly instead of softening the result. Close with the limits: what Spinoza's argument does not support, where this procedure will misfire in a school of six hundred pupils, and which of our real disputes it simply cannot settle.

Begin with your bottom line in the first sentence — the single most important thing the panel should take away — and then give the procedure and the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. Please write in British English.
