Every year our county rock club opens its field weekend with a Friday-evening talk and this year it somehow fell to me. About thirty amateur rockhounds — retired civil engineers, a couple of secondary school teachers, one guy with very strong views about the K-Pg boundary. They know what an unconformity is, they've handled cores, and not one of them is a professional stratigraher. I've got forty minutes, a whiteboard, and one physical object I'm allowed to bring. Topic: the Anthropocene — specifically the start-date question and who actually gets to decide it. I want a plan for the talk, not the talk.

What has to be in place before the who-decides question is even intelligible to them, and what depends on what after that? My read is that they'll hear "the working group's proposal got voted down by the Quaternary stratigraphy subcommission in 2024, and the higher bodies confirmed it" as either a scandal or a relief, and that both reactions come from assuming the thing being voted on was the question *have humans changed the planet?* So maybe my first job is to get clear what the committee's question actually was and what would have counted as a yes. Is that the right opening move, or does something need to come before it?

Which example carries the whole thing? I was going to build it around the Crawford Lake varves and the plutonium spike — tell me whether that's the right carrier or whether it makes the debate look like a lab measurement with a wrong answer somewhere, and what the example has to have and must not have.

Then the misconception map. I've got three guesses: the vote settled it one way or the other, the Anthropocene is just climate change with a grander name, and the start date is a question about when humans became the biggest force on Earth. Add the ones they'll bring that I haven't thought of, and say exactly where in the sequence each one gets headed off — before or after the Crawford Lake bit, and why that order and not the reverse. Finish with one question I can ask them at the end that would tell me whether the distinction landed rather than whether they enjoyed the evening.

And tell me what to cut at this level and what I should say out loud that I'm not going to explain — I'd rather announce the gaps than fudge them. No reading lists, no papers to send them home with, I'm not handing anything out.
