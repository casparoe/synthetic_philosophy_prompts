Been assembling my views on knowledge for a quals practice session and I'd like someone to try to break the package before I have to defend it. Full list, no cherry-picking on my end:

(a) Knowledge isn't analyzable. You don't build it out of belief plus truth plus justification plus some fourth thing; it's the basic notion and the other ones get explained in terms of it.

(b) That said, I'm fine stating necessary conditions. You know p only if you can rule out the relevant ways p could be false — relevant, not all of them, because I'm a fallibilist.

(c) 'Knows' is context-sensitive. The standard in play gets set by the context of whoever is atributing the knowledge, not by the subject's own situation. This is how I handle the Saturday/Sunday bank cases and the skeptic without giving anything weird away: in the skeptic's context the bar is so high that "you don't know you have hands" comes out true, and in ordinary contexts my claiming to know I have hands is true too, and neither of us misused the word.

(d) Pragmatic encroachment. Same evidence, same mental state, different practical stakes for the subject, different verdict about whether they know. My reason for this is the link to action: knowledge is the kind of thing you're entitled to treat as a reason for acting, and whether you're entitled to that can depend on what's riding on it.

(e) Knowledge is the norm of assertion, flat out — assert p only if you know p. I don't want a weaker norm (justified belief, warrant, whatever) because the weaker ones predict that lottery-style assertions are perfectly fine and they clearly aren't.

(f) And I want that norm to be invariant. Whether you're well enough positioned to assert p doesn't depend on who's listening or on what they need from you.

(g) No purposes, no degrees. I'm not going to retreat to "she knows-for-these-purposes" or "she sort of knows." Either she knows or she doesn't.

(h) I've seen people say that if you're a contextualist you don't need encroachment at all, and I don't buy that. They look like they're doing different jobs — one about the word, one about the thing itself.

Now the case, which I think my package handles but I'm honestly not sure of. Elena does the cooking for our friend group and keeps track of who's allergic to what. She confirmed from the group chat about a month ago that Dana is allergic to shellfish. Nothing has changed since, and nothing much rides on it for her. Two weeks ago Dana ended up in the ER after a reaction — turns out it wasn't the shellfish at that dinner, though nobody knew that at the time — and a doctor asks Elena point-blank whether she's sure about the shellfish, because the answer goes into the chart and shapes what they test for. Elena's own position hasn't shifted at all: she isn't cooking anymore, nobody is blaming her, there is nothing she stands to lose either way. She says yes, flat out, no hedging.

My verdicts: she knew it before the hospital. She still knows it now. It was nonetheless out of line for her to flat-out assert it without rechecking. And the doctor was entitled to push back on her for doing it.

For each real one, say which item has to go and what I lose elsewhere in the list if I give it up; I have no attachment to any of them and I'd rather find out which are load-bearing. Two specific things I want answered straight: are (c) and (d) actually one view wearing two hats, and can anything on this list deliver all four Elena verdicts at once, or am I asking for something the package can't produce?
