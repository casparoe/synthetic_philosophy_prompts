I am a first-year doctoral student in sociology, drafting my theory chapter, and I keep running into an argumentative move I cannot label. An author explains something by appeal to a group-level property — a norm, an institution, a market structure. A critic objects that the property is nothing over and above what the relevant individuals do, believe and expect, then shows that the explanation can be restated, without loss, in those individual terms, and concludes from this that the group-level property was doing no explanatory work whatsoever. What puzzles me is that the restatement usually succeeds, and yet the conclusion seems not to follow: the two accounts do not appear to compete, because the group-level one seems to be telling us which configurations of individual behaviour are stable and which are not, rather than citing a further cause.

Two instances from my reading. First, a claim that a norm of reciprocity sustains a trading network, met by the reply that only the traders' own expectations did any work — though the reply never says why those expectations rather than others should have arisen and persisted. Second, a claim that the structure of a local labour market fixes wage levels, met by the reply that wages are set by individual employers, so that structure is mere redescription.

Is there an established concept for this inference from 'can be restated in lower-level terms' to 'explains nothing'? I know the neighbourhood — methodological individualism against holism — and I have a vague sense that philosophers of mind have wrestled with a structurally parallel problem about mental causation. But I want the specific idea, if it exists: what exactly it asserts, what it is called, how it differs from an ordinary charge of redundancy or of adding no new causal powers, and where the standard replies locate the flaw. If nothing settled fits my description, say so plainly and propose a term of your own, with a definition sharp enough that I could tell which of the two instances above falls under it and which does not. Please write your answer in British English.
