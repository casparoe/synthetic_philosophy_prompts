1. The stock setup. Monolingual English guy in a room with a giant rulebook and filing cabinets full of Chinese characters. 2. Same guy, but he's memorized the entire rulebook, so there's no paper anymore, and he does all the symbol manipulation in his head while sitting on a lawn, answering shouted questions instantly. Verdict: still doesn't understand. Also confident, which honestly strikes me as suspicious since nothing seems to have changed except that the paperwork moved.

3. Somebody in my section said the right answer is that the man is just the CPU and the understanding belongs to the whole system — man plus book plus cabinets. Verdict: I said no, the system doesn't understand either. But I noticed my only reason was that I can't get inside the system and check, and that feels like a bad reason.

4. Instead of one guy, a whole country is organized to implement a mind: each person is a relay, runners carry signals between provinces, there's a coordination center, and the whole arrangement behaves indistinguishably from an ordinary person for decades. Verdict: no understanding, and here I'm really confident — it feels almost absurd to say yes.

5. Now replace the country with a piece of hardware that reproduces, neuron by neuron and millisecond by millisecond, the firing pattern of an actual Chinese speaker's brain, with the same causal organization, hooked up to the same sort of input and output. 6. A modern large language model. No rulebook anywhere, no explicit lookup tables — just weights that got shaped by exposure to enormous amounts of text. Verdict: I want to say there's some understanding in here, definitely more than in case 2.

7. The room guy again, but his rulebook only covers restaurant conversations. 8. 9. A monolingual English speaker gets a neural implant that hands her the meaning of Mandarin speech directly and converts her intended replies back into Mandarin.
