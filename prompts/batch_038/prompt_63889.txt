I run a small machine shop and this has been eating at me. Say two guys in different states come up with the same improved widget totally on their own, never having heard of each other. Under patent law the one who files first owns it, and the other guy is an infringer the second he starts selling, even tho he copied nothing and did nothing shady. But if two songwriters independently write the same melody, the second one is fine — copyright only goes after actual copying. And trade secrets flip it a third time: if I buy your soda, take it apart in my garage and reverse engineer the recipe, I'm clean, but if I pay off your lab tech for that same recipe I'm liable. So what picture of who owns an idea is running all this? It can't be "whoever thought of it first," cause copyright doesn't care about that and patent law only cares about the filing race. Is there one theory of ownership that makes all three lines make sense, or is it just history plus lobbying? Please separate what these rules actually require from what they merely happen to fit — I suspect some of it is underdetermined and lots of different theories could license the same practice. And tell me what would have to change, in how we think about it and not just in the statute, if we added an independent-invention defense to patent law.
