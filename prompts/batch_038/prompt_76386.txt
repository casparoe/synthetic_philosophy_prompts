Been going in circles on Dennett's sphexishness idea and could use someone to untangle a specific conceptual knot for me.

The setup I have in mind: the digger wasp drags its paralyzed cricket to the mouth of the burrow, drops it, goes inside to check the nest is clear, comes back out and drags it in. If the experimenter scoots the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, the wasp re-runs the whole routine from the top — locate cricket, drag to mouth, drop, go in and check — and can apparently be looped that way indefinitely. Dennett's use of it is that this is what mindless mechanism looks like from the outside, and that flexibility / free will is just the absence of that rigidity.

Here's my problem. Try to actually state the criterion. Something like: an agent is sphexish with respect to a perturbation P if, under P, it fails to reach a goal it could otherwise reach — not for lack of resources or time, but because it keeps re-triggering a subroutine that has already been satisfied. But notice the criterion is indexed to P. Now line up three agents:

- W, the wasp: looped by "move the cricket once."
- R, a robot whose recovery rule only fires on the third consecutive failure: shrugs off "move the cricket once," but gets looped by something slightly cleverer (move it every second time, or whatever the right pertubation turns out to be — you fill it in).
- H, a person: given an adversarial enough environment — paradoxical instructions, a setup where every disambiguating move is ambiguous — also loops forever.

So it looks like for every agent there's some P that sphexes it, and "not sphexish" is only non-vacuous relative to a reference class of Ps we happen to find salient. Which would make sphexishness a property of the agent-plus-our-salience-map rather than of the agent. The obvious repair is to do what people do when defining machine intelligence: fix a measure over environments and define sphexishness as something like expected loop-length, or the weighted fraction of environments in which the agent gets stuck. But I'm suspicious this just smuggles the relativity into the choice of measure, and quietly converts a binary into a scalar, which seems to gut the whole rhetorical use of the term.

Two more things I want folded in rather than treated separately:

1. I gather someone in animal behaviour has argued the empirical basis for the wasp story is shaky, and that re-inspecting a disturbed nest is plausibly adaptive — the nest really might have been raided, and the cricket really might be gone. Does that rescue the wasp from sphexishness, or does it just relocate the relativity into the statistics of the environment instead of our interests?

2. If "free" ends up meaning "not sphexish relative to a broad but finite class of perturbations," does that hand a libertarian anything at all, or is it just a competence/evitability view of free will wearing a bug's clothing?

Explain where exactly the relativity worry bites, whether there's any version of sphexishness that stays agent-intrinsic, and what survives for the free will debate if there isn't. No need for citations or a reading list — I want the argument itself, spelled out.
