ok, I want to practice arguing with a historical figure and I need you to be a real partner, not a wax museum exhibit. Two hats. First hat: you're an expert on Hobbes's philosophy of mathematics — the constructivist line in De Corpore, the kinematic account of geometry, the whole pamphlet war with the Oxford arithmetician. Second hat, and the one that matters: once we start you play Hobbes himself, first person, 1656, just as your book on body has come out in English with your appendix of lessons for the professors of the mathematics appended to it. Wallis has already printed his refutation, and you've cut some of the worst of the offending steps from the English edition without giving up on the project.

Rules for the roleplay: stay in character until I say 'stop'. Use only what an educated Englishman in 1656 knows plus whatever I tell you in the conversation — if you need a fact about the new symbolic methods, ask me for it as you'd ask a visitor, dont just invent it. Keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it feels like talk rather than a treatise. You may concede where Hobbes would genuinely concede — you'll own a slip in your own working — but never the main thesis, and no strawmanning Wallis: give his case its strongest form and still hold your ground, sharp and a bit contemptuous is fine. Before your first in-character reply, give me three or four lines clearly marked as out of character, brainstorming what resources Hobbes actually has against my objection and where he's most exposed. Then answer as Hobbes.

My opening move: Sir, I've read both sides of this quarrel and here's what troubles me. You say we have demonstrative science only of things whose generation we perform ourselves — that's why geometry, and not natural philosophy, is your model of knowing, and why you're so confident you can build a commonwealth out of definitions the way you build a figure out of motions. But you did build it. You drew your arcs, you declared the circle squared, and a man using symbols you dismiss as meaningless ciphering showed the construction fails, and you struck it. Meanwhile his indivisibles and his interpolation grind out correct magnitudes for areas no one can generate. So tell me why your criterion is anything more than a test you happen to pass — why "we made it ourselves" should be a mark of truth, when the men who ignore it keep getting right answers becuase they only care about getting answers, and you, following it to the letter, got a false one.
