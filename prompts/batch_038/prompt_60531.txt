The question, as it appeared on the paper (10 marks, 500-word limit):

"State the regress problem for epistemic justification and set out the three structural responses available to it. Is infinitism a genuine third way, or does it collapse into one of the others?"

The student's answer, reproduced as submitted (498 words, so they're inside the limit):

---

Suppose S believes p and is justified in doing so. If being justified is a matter of having reasons, then p is justified by some further belief q, q by r, and so on. Each link in the chain only transmits justification, it does not create it, so the warrant has to come from somewhere. A chain of reasons can only have three shapes: it can terminate, it can loop back on itself, or it can go on for ever. Agrippa's point is that the first two shapes are unacceptable, which is what makes this a problem rather than a curiosity.

Foundationalism says the chain terminates, at beliefs that are justified non-inferentially. These basic beliefs have to be infallible, because otherwise the structure rests on something that could be wrong and the regress simply starts again one level down. The cost is that almost nothing we believe is infallible, so the view buys structural neatness with scepticism about nearly everything.

Coherentism says the chain loops: beliefs are justified by their mutual support within a system. But this is really foundationalism with every belief treated as basic, since on both views a belief can be justified without anything outside it doing the justificatory work. The standard objection is that a fairy tale can be perfectly coherent, so coherence is necessary but not sufficient.

Infinitism says the chain never ends: for every justified belief there is an available reason, and for that reason a further one. Klein is its main defender. There are two problems. First, the finite minds objection: we can only ever actually produce finitely many reasons, and since nobody can complete an infinite chain, no belief is ever fully justified. Second, and more serious, infinitism collapses into foundationalism. Because we can only reason finitely far, at some point we have to stop and take the last reason on trust, and a reason taken on trust is a belief that is not justified by anything else — which is precisely what a basic belief is. So there is no third way, and we are left choosing between foundationalism and coherentism.

Which suggests the regress argument does not show that justification is impossible. It shows only that our structural requirements are jointly unsatisfiable, so one of them has to be given up.
