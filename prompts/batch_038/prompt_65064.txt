I'm chairing our departmental colloquium next Thursday and the speaker defends a hybrid expressivism: on her view, judging that lying is wrong is a matter of disapproving of lying while also believing that lying has the descriptive property of being such that any fully informed, imaginatively vivid observer would disapprove of it. So the belief component handles embedding and the attitude component is there to explain why moral judgments motivate. I've got about twenty minutes of Q&A and I dont want to spend it on softballs. Write me six to eight questions aimed squarely at the Frege-Geach problem, and for each one, the answer she's most likely to give plus a follow-up that cuts off that escape route. I want coverage of: the negation problem -- if the belief content is purely descriptive, what exactly does someone who says lying isn't wrong disagree with, and does the hybrid just relocate the old expressivist difficulty instead of solving it; conditional embeddings, where the moral content shows up only in an unasserted antecedent and the attitude seems to have nothing to attach to; and whether two people who agree on every non-moral fact but differ morally end up sharing her belief component, in which case what is their disagreement a disagreement about. Save the last slot for the charge that the attitude does no semantic work at all and could be deleted with no change to truth conditions or validity -- that's the one I want her to squirm on. Keep the questions short enough to ask out loud, and nothing in the register of "what drew you to metaethics."
