I have been asked to circulate a short statement of my own commitments on disagreement ahead of a joint workshop our forecasting group is running with a philosophy department, and I should like a consistency check before it goes out. What I want is an audit rather than a tutorial: I know what Aumann's result says and I do not need the proof rehearsed to me.

Here is what I hold.

(a) I accept the agreement theorem and I read it normatively. Two agents who share a common prior and are fully transparent to one another about their credences cannot rationally rest in a difference of opinion; a divergence that survives complete mutual disclosure marks a failure of reasoning on at least one side rather than an innocent difference of temperament.

(b) I do not think a difference in priors can ever be a proper object of criticism. A prior encodes everything an agent is disposed to make of any evidence she might receive, and it is fixed by a formative history she never chose and could not have evaluated from the outside. When two people diverge, the observation that they simply began from different priors is therefore a complete explanation, and it carries no reproach. I would add that one can always relocate a stubborn disagreement into the priors, and I do not regard that relocation as a merely formal manoeuvre.

(c) I am a conciliationist about acknowledged peers. If I learn that someone I take to be my epistemic equal on the question has settled on a credence well away from mine, I ought to move substantially towards hers, and I ought to do so before any evidence has been exchanged.

(d) I am sceptical that common knowledge is ever achieved in practice. Real interlocutors manage a level or two of mutual knowledge at best, and I take that to be why the theorem so rarely gets a grip on an actual dispute.

(e) I accept the standard dynamic result: two agents with a common prior who alternately announce their posterior credences in some proposition arrive at common knowledge of that posterior after finitely many rounds, and so agree.

(f) None of this, I think, bears on outright belief or assertion. The theorem constrains degrees of confidence, and I am perfectly happy for two people to assert flat contradictions while their credences, properly understood, remain in good order.

Please tell me which of these pull against one another, and how badly. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them indiscriminately; for each, say what would have to be surrendered and what the least costly repair would be. I suspect that at least one pair which looks like a conflict is not genuinely one, and I should like to be told which and why; I also suspect that one of the commitments I find most comfortable is quietly undermining another, though I cannot see where. Finish by identifying the single revision that would resolve the most, and what I should expect to lose by making it.
