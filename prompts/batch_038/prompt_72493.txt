I serve on the quality committee of a regional health system. Next month we vote on whether to make a new sepsis protocol the default for all adult admissions, replacing standard care. The supporting report draws on two years of observational data from our own units. In aggregate, 120 of 310 patients managed under the new protocol survived to discharge (38.7%), against 172 of 300 under standard care (57.3%); the report's author concludes that the protocol is harmful and should be dropped. The stratified figures run the other way. Among patients judged severe at presentation, the protocol saved 75 of 250 (30%) versus 12 of 60 (20%) for standard care; among those judged mild, 45 of 60 (75%) versus 160 of 240 (66.7%). The author's response is that the stratified table is a diversion, since the committee is voting on what will happen to our actual case mix, not to a counterfactual one in which severity is balanced by design.

Two further facts complicate the picture. Severity is recorded by a standing triage rule that has routed most severe presentations to the new protocol, and a colleague who split the severe band into moderate and very severe found the protocol still ahead in all three bands, with the aggregate unchanged. And the protocol requires nurse staffing we can sustain for roughly a third of admissions, so a vote in its favor is in practice a vote about which patients receive it.

Write the memo I will circulate ahead of the vote. It should state a recommendation — implement system-wide, hold, or implement in a restricted form — with a time frame and any conditions the data can support. It should say explicitly which question each table answers and which one bears on our decision; engage my colleague's objection on its own terms rather than writing it off as a statistical artifact; and assess how far the stratified comparison can be trusted given how patients came to be assigned, including what we would have to run to settle the matter. End with the strongest case for the recommendation you reject, and why it nonetheless loses (or why it does not).
