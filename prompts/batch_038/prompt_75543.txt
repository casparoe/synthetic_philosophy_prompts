so my mom died in April, she was a physicist, and for the last six weeks I've basically been sleeping in her house and going through her books. One of them is a beat-up copy of Schrödinger's philosophical essays, the ones where he gets into Vedanta and the Upanishads, and there are notes in the margins in her handwriting I can't half read. I've been up at 3am with it and what I want is to find out whether the thing is actually true, not whether it's comforting.

The claim as I can restate it: when you say "I" and I say "I," there aren't two subjects of experience, there's one and the same subject apprehended from inside many times over — the plurality is appearance, not fact. Part of his support, as best I can tell, is that experience never gets given in the plural, only ever as this one here-and-now; and that when a body dies or a brain is surgically divided you don't subtract a piece out of the world, the whole stage goes instead. He also seems to think the question how many minds there are is malformed, more like asking how many sides a thing has than asking for a number.

Here's what I want. Give me your actual credence, as a number, on two things separately and then on their conjunction:

(a) that there is literally one subject behind all the "I"s, and
(b) that this is a genuine rival to pluralism about minds rather than a relabeling of it that makes no difference anywhere. I think (b) is the shaky one — if nothing could ever distinguish them, does "one mind" even say something? My own worry is that counting seems to presuppose bounded, mutually inaccessible instances, which is exactly what the view denies, so maybe it's not one-versus-many at all but uncountable-versus-countable, and then I don't know what's left.

Decompose it. Put in whatever terms you think belong — whether individuating minds has determinate truth conditions to begin with, whether reports of unity or of dissolution under anaesthetic are evidence about metaphysics or only about report, whether minds are individuated by causal/informational isolation and if so whether that's a fact or a convention — with a number and one line of justification each, then show me how they combine. Say which single term the final number is most sensitive to and what would move it.

And then be straight with me about one more thing: how much of a discount should I apply to my own reading of him, given where I am right now. What number would you give if someone had picked this book up for fun?
