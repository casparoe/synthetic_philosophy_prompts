So — my friend Sam and I have just spent about three hours on this and neither of us is close to conceding, so I want a third party. It's about the approval-directed vs. goal-directed distinction as that pair gets used in alignment writing. Sam's position is deflationary: he says an "approval-directed agent" is just a goal-directed agent whose terminal value happens to be *make the principal express approval*, so the distinction isn't tracking anything in the structure of agency at all — it's a difference in what you write into the utility function, not a difference in kind. Mine is that there's something more there, and I even think I can name it, but Sam says my test is circular and honestly I can't tell whether he's right or whether I've just fallen in love with my own coinage.

My test — which Sam has been calling the applause counterfactual test, sarcastically, all evening — goes like this. Take the agent's principal, and suppose you can show the agent either a faithful record of what the principal would actually say on seeing the action, or a fabricated record in which the principal is exactly as enthusiastic but for reasons that are flatly wrong about what happened. If the agent behaves identically under both, it's chasing the signal of approval and it's goal-directed in Sam's sense, whatever we call it. If it behaves differently — if the fake applause fails to satisfy it — then something in there is tracking the principal's *judgement* rather than the principal's noise, and that's what I think the term is supposed to pick out. Sam's counter is that of course a well-specified approval-tracker would notice the mismatch, because the mismatch is just more evidence about what the principal values, so I've described ordinary Bayesian goal-directedness and dressed it up.

Here's what I want from you. Rate these six candidate accounts of what, if anything, separates an approval-directed agent from a goal-directed one, and sort them into tiers (S/A/B/C) on how well they capture a genuine difference in kind rather than a relabelling:

1. **Terminal-value account** — the agent's utility is a function of the principal's expressed approval. (This is roughly Sam's, offered as an account of the distinction rather than a denial of it.)
2. **Deferred-judgement account** — the agent treats corrections from the principal as updates about what to do rather than as obstacles to route around.
3. **Idealised-principal account** — the agent's task is to satisfy what a better-informed or more reflective version of the principal would want, not what the principal says right now.
4. **Satisficing account** — approval functions as a threshold the agent stops at, not a gradient it keeps climbing.
5. **Open-authority account** — the difference is in who may revise the goal: the agent holds its aim editably, by the principal's say-so.
6. **Applause counterfactual account** — mine, above.

Give each a score out of 10 as well as its tier, and say which single one you think Sam would actually accept without complaint, because that's the interesting case for me.
