ok so three weeks ago I got talked onto the board of my housing co-op — 14 units, one building, everyone knows everyone's business — and because I'm apparently "the one who reads a lot" they handed me the job of writing the principles preamble for the revised bylaws. My partner thinks I'm massively overthinking this. I'd rather find the cracks now than at the AGM in three weeks, so: does the following set hang together, and if not, which piece has to go?

1. Every resident has a private sphere — basically the inside of their own unit plus their own balcony — where their own preference is the only thing that counts. The board can't overide it by vote no matter how lopsided the vote, and can't fine anyone for what they do in there. Not "they get extra voting weight." They get the decision outright.

2. If literally every member, including the one whose sphere it is, prefers one option to another, we go with it. Feels too obvious to write down but people keep forgetting it.

3. Whatever the co-op ends up on record as saying has to hang together. If our minutes record that A is better than B and B is better than C, we cannot then turn around and record C as better than A. Otherwise every decision gets re-litigated forever and we look insane.

4. The private-sphere right is inalienable. You can't consent your way out of it ("fine, let the majority decide my balcony") and the board can't buy it off ("we'll knock $200 off your fees if the thing comes down"). The second rights become tradeable they erode, and they erode fastest for whoever is poorest or most conflict-avoidant.

5. No intensity. We are not having people rate how strongly they feel out of ten. Feelings aren't measurable like that and the loudest person always wins.

6. A real case from last month, which I'm pretty sure I handled well. Ines has a huge tomato jungle on her balcony and a political banner zip-tied to the railing. Dev lives opposite. He came to the board and said — this is the important part — that he does *not* want her stopped and would not vote for a ban, he just wants it minuted that the co-op considers the balcony as it currently stands worse than the balcony without it, because we face a public street and a building should stand for something. He also said he'd rather the whole street had nothing on display at all, his own stuff included, than have displays going up; but that if there has to be one, he'd rather it be his ceramic shrine in his window than her banner, and he'd take his own down first. Ines, for her part, says she actually likes his shrine and thinks it's charming, and that if only one of them gets something up she'd genuinely rather it was his; but that if he's not putting his up, hers is going up. Everyone else on the board agrees with Dev's ranking, mostly because the shrine is pretty and the banner is loud. I told Dev no vote happens, the tomatoes and the banner stay, but sure, we can minute a general view. Everybody seemed happy and we all went to the pub.

7. Another real case, which I'm also sure I handled well. Marcus keeps his unit at maybe 8 degrees all winter to save money, the pipe in the wall he shares with Priya froze and burst, water everywhere. The board ordered him to hold a minimum temperature and split the repair cost. Clearly right, I'd say the same again tomorrow. His defence was "it's inside my unit," which under clause 1 is... a thing he can say.

8. I also think "neighbours should work it out between themselves first" is a genuinely good norm — it's in our newsletter and I'd defend it at the AGM — and that includes making deals. If Dev offers to buy Ines a nice planter in exchange for the banner coming down and she says yes, that's the system working, not the system failing.

Two things people have thrown at me that I don't think land, but tell me if I'm wrong:

- Our retired-econ board member says there's a well-known result showing that a minimal liberalism condition plus the Pareto principle can be flat-out impossible, and he wants to use it to argue we shouldn't have clause 1 at all. I told him we never produce a ranking of every possible state of the building; we vote on one concrete proposal at a time, so whatever that theorem is about, it isn't about us.
- Someone else said clause 2 is a utilitarian principle and therefore contradicts clause 1 being a trump. I don't buy it. Pareto is such a weak requirement that it can't possibly collide with anything.

So: go through my list and tell me which commitments actually pull against each other and which only look like they do. Rank the real ones by severity rather than dumping them all out as equally bad. For each, say specifically which numbered item has to go or get weakened, and what giving it up costs me in practice — not in principle, in terms of what happens at this building. I honestly don't know which of these I'd drop and I'm not going to pretend otherwise, so don't assume I'm attached to any of them. And if there's one revision that cleans up the most damage at once, tell me what it is and what its price tag is, including whether it reopens something I currently think is fine.
