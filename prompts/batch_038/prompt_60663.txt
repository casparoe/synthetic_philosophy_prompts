I work as a software engineer on capacity planning for a cloud platform, and I have no formal training in philosophy; what follows comes out of a fortnight of reading on the simulation argument after a long on-call rotation, plus an extended argument with a colleague who is considerably better read than I am. We got stuck in the anthropic reasoning literature, specifically on the distinction between the self-sampling assumption (roughly: reason as though you were a random sample drawn from the observers in your reference class) and the self-indication assumption (roughly: your very existence is itself evidence in favour of worlds containing more observers), and on the charge that the second of these makes us absurdly presumptuous — the thought experiment usually called the presumptuous philosopher, in which a philosopher claims to settle a live physics question without the experiment ever being run, on the sole ground that one candidate theory predicts vastly more observers than its rival. My colleague finds that charge decisive. I find myself half-sympathetic to the philosopher, partly because the failure mode I see most often in my day job is the reverse one — extrapolating wildly from a sample of size one — and partly because I cannot identify what is doing the work in the charge other than an intuition I do not entirely trust.

So that you need not reconstruct anything from the literature, here is the case I want your view on, stated as self-containedly as I can manage. Two cosmological theories, T-small and T-big, agree on every proposition that could in principle be settled by observation: identical constants, identical histories, identical predictions for every instrument that we or any civilisation like ours could ever build. They differ in exactly one respect — T-small entails that the universe contains about a million observers in total, T-big that it contains about a trillion trillion, the additional observers all lying in regions permanently outside our light cone. Suppose an agent's background evidence gives each theory a prior of exactly one half. Claim P is this: given the case precisely as described, the correct norms of rationality require that agent to move to a credence of at least 0.99 in T-big once it has taken the difference in observer numbers into account, and no legitimate manoeuvre — reference-class restriction, a typicality assumption, an appeal to self-sampling, or a refusal to treat indexical information as evidence at all — can block that conclusion. I take P to be the kind of thing that can be true or false, and by resolution I mean an argument showing either that the shift follows from premises about confirmation that do not already presuppose it, or that some particular blocking manoeuvre is principled rather than ad hoc.

My own credence in P is about 0.25 and my colleague's is about 0.8. Please give me your number, and then decompose it: assign rough probabilities to the sub-questions you think P actually turns on — for instance whether there is a determinate fact about which norms are correct in a case like this at all, whether indexical or self-locating information can license a large Bayesian shift in the first place, whether the many-observers weighting is the right weighting if any such shift is licensed, whether the reference-class problem is soluble or fatal, and whether two theories can really be indistinguishable in the way my stipulation requires without some further empirical difference creeping in — with a sentence of justification for each term, and then show how the terms combine to yield your headline number. Tell me which sub-question your answer is most sensitive to, which you are least confident about, and state plainly what argument or piece of evidence would move your number furthest. Then answer two follow-up questions: would your credence in P change, and by roughly how much, if the extra observers in T-big were not a trillion trillion distinct beings but a trillion trillion exact duplicates of the same million people; and, given the spread between 0.25 and 0.8, on which single sub-question do you think my colleague and I are most likely diverging? Please write the entire answer in British English.
