Last month someone at the county emailed asking how I'd validated it, and I told her honestly: every time it rains I check what the gauge recorded, and that's how much rain fell, so it got that one right. I've logged roughly 300 of those confirmations and not one miss. She said that's circular and that I proved nothing at all. I pushed back that I'm not just assuming it works — I'm checking it against the actual weather, every single time, and a test that passes 300 times running has to count for something.

She also didnt like the second thing I told her, which is that I can look at the display and see that it recorded 12mm, and if it recorded 12mm then it recorded that accurately, so I know it was accurate on that occasion, and I can just pile those up.

Two details that maybe help my case. First, when I set it up I poured a measured volume of water in and it came to exactly the depth it should have, so I know the bucket mechanism and the tipping math are fine. Second, the gauge runs a nightly self-diagnostic and has never once failed — though if I'm honest I think that test only checks whether the electronics answer when poked.

So is she right that two years of me "checking" it was worthless, or is she being a stickler about something that works fine in practice? And what should I actually do now — keep uploading as-is, upload with some kind of flag, or pull the record down? I'd really rather not throw out two years of data if I don't have to.

Give me your actual verdict on who's right in the first sentence, then the reasoning behind it. No preamble, and don't restate my setup back at me.
