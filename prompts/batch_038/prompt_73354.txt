I write about language and cognition for a general-interest science-and-ideas magazine, and I've been commissioned a long piece on why people so reliably mishear one another. I want to build it around Jane Austen's Emma as a Gricean case study: Grice's account of speaker meaning, on which what a speaker means is what she intends her hearer to recover by means of recognizing that very intention, plus the conversational implicature that rides on top of what is literally said.

Two set pieces interest me. First, Mr. Elton's charade, whose answer is courtship, which Emma confidently reads as a declaration to Harriet when it was aimed at herself: she gets the act-type right and the addressee wrong, and given Grice's uptake condition it isn't clear that any implicature was communicated at all. Second, Frank Churchill, who spends the novel saying nothing false and implicating nothing true — from the anonymous pianoforte to the running speculation about Jane Fairfax and Mr. Dixon — so that his deceptions consist entirely in exploiting Emma's assumption that he is being cooperative.

Read the novel through the theory and tell me where it illuminates and where it misfires. What does Emma's inference actually consist in, given that she reasons from relevance and context and Mr. Knightley reasons better from the same evidence? Is Austen's free indirect narration itself an implicature-generating device aimed at the reader, for which Grice's speaker-hearer dyad has no room? And is the novel's moral verdict — that Emma's fault is not stupidity but inattention to other minds — a Gricean point or an anti-Gricean one? Finish by saying what the book shows about implicature that argument alone does not, and where the reading has to be abandoned to stay honest.

Please describe scenes rather than quoting them, and don't give me a bibliography or citation list. Structure the piece as: The theory, in brief (~200 words); The charade misfire (~350 words); Frank Churchill and the ethics of implicature (~350 words); Narration as a second channel (~250 words); Where Austen resists Grice (~250 words); What the novel adds (~200 words).
