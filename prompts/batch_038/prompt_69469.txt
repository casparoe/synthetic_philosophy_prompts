I am writing the term paper for my decision theory seminar, and before I go any further into the literature I want to settle the specification. The question is narrower than the usual one. I am not asking which theory of choice under risk is true in general; I am asking what a theory must deliver if it is to license a public body's choice of a safer option over one with the higher expected payoff, and to let that body defend the choice afterwards to the people who actually bore the risk.

The case I am working from: a city council approved an expensive flood-defence scheme over a much cheaper alternative. On the staff analysis, the cheaper scheme had the better expected value for the city as a whole, because the savings would have funded other resilience work, but it left one riverside neighbourhood carrying a small probability of a failure mode that would have fallen almost entirely on that neighbourhood. A consultant to the council told the members their decision was irrational. I want to know what has to be true of a theory of rational risk-taking for that consultant to be wrong, and what a theory has to surrender to make him right.

So: write the desiderata. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and tag it as non-negotiable, negotiable, or a preference of a particular camp. Where a demand that is treated as mandatory in this area is really just one side's ideology, say so and argue it — my suspicion is that consistency of choice across time belongs in that category, and I would like either to be argued out of the suspicion or confirmed in it. Other regions I expect the list to cover, without pretending this is exhaustive: whether the theory must reproduce expected utility's verdicts on large-stakes or indefinitely repeated gambles; whether its recommendations are allowed to shift when the same decision is redescribed over a finer outcome space; how it keeps attitudes to risk separate from attitudes to ambiguity; and what it owes us on the common-consequence and common-ratio patterns.

Three requirements are my own. I want them assessed on their merits rather than located in anybody's system. Call the first exposure-relative admissibility: the same probability-and-outcome profile can be permissible for one agent and impermissible for another, in a way that is not reducible either to curvature in the utility function or to a difference in the probabilities assigned. Call the second non-repudiation: the decision rule has to survive contact with both branches, so that the body can still defend it to those who lost after the bad outcome, and to those who paid after the good one, without relying on hindsight. Call the third articulability: the rule must be statable as premises a non-specialist could check, not merely recoverable from the axioms of a representation theorem.

Please organize the answer in these sections, in this order:

1. Purpose and target — roughly 120 words restating what adequacy is being measured against here, and what would count as a failure.
2. The desiderata — ten to fourteen numbered items, about a thousand words in total. Each item gets a name, the motivating case in a sentence or two, and its tag.
3. Distinctions the theory may not collapse — five or six entries, about 200 words.
4. The squeeze — about 300 words. Which subsets of the list cannot be jointly satisfied, and why; and which single requirement does the most to eliminate candidates. If the calibration results for small-stakes risk aversion, including their extension to rank-dependent and other non-expected-utility models, rule out something I care about, say so plainly rather than diplomatically.
5. Contenders, briefly — about 200 words: expected utility theory, a risk-weighted view of Buchak's kind, and one reference-dependent or probability-weighting account, each in a short paragraph on how it fares against the list. No fuller scorecard.
6. Verdict on my three — about 120 words saying which of my coinages is load-bearing, which is redundant with something already on the list, and which is untenable.

I do not need citations or a reading list; I have those. Reason from the cases.
