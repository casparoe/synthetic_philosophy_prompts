A woman I have known for years farms a few miles from me, and she is the person our county turns to whenever something new is proposed. She says she goes by precaution, though she has never studied the idea anywhere, and neither have I — I have been turning it over on my own. Her record, as I remember it:

- When the water district wanted a persistent herbicide to clear an invasive vine out of the reservoir, she fought it, even though the vine was costing the town money every year. Her reason: a ruined aquifer cannot be repaired, and a small chance of that beats a certainty of vine.
- During the measles outbreak she backed the vaccination clinics and took her own grandchildren, while conceding that a very few children would be hurt by them, because the disease was returning either way and every family could still decline for itself.
- She opposed the cell mast beside the primary school while admitting that nobody has shown how it could harm anyone, because the children have no way of refusing the exposure.
- She sets fire to her own woodland on purpose every few years, accepting that a burn could get away, because the alternative is a fire that will certainly come and burn far worse.
- She turned down a blight-resistant tree a nursery was selling, one engineered to be planted and left to cross with the wild stand, on the grounds that a change written into a wild population's heredity is not something anyone can later take back.

Now the county has a proposal. An imported beetle is killing the last stand of ash in our valley — not the species everywhere, only ours, but ours seeds everything downstream — and the entomologists say the stand is gone within a decade if nothing is done. What they want to release is the same beetle carrying an engineered hereditary element that leaves females infertile; the element is built so it cannot sustain itself, being passed on for a handful of generations and then running out of fuel, with the local population back to its original genetics inside perhaps ten years. Every household in the valley was canvassed and roughly four in five want it done, but the one in five who object will still get beetles in their gardens, and no fence would keep them out.

Which way will she go? I want your prediction and the reasoning behind it, an account of the rule her record actually supports, the other rules that fit the record just as well, and — most useful to me — the next proposal you would bring her, the one that would finally show which rule she is running.
