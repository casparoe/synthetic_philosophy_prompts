I am a high school student writing an independent research paper on the ethics of worlds that contain infinitely many people, and before I build an argument on top of it, I would like a consistency check on the framework I have provisionally assembled. My commitments are these:

1. A moral theory may not dismiss worlds containing infinitely many people as a mere thought experiment. If the universe is spatially infinite or everlasting, such worlds are not exotic possibilities but the ones we are actually in, and a theory that goes silent or arbitrary there fails precisely where it is most needed.
2. How good a world is comes down to the total wellbeing it contains: sum the wellbeing of everyone who ever lives in it, and the world with the greater sum is the better world.
3. If world A contains every person world B contains, each of them exactly as well off as in B, plus at least one additional person whose life is worth living, then A is better than B.
4. For any two worlds whatsoever, exactly one of the following holds: the first is better than the second, the second is better than the first, or they are equally good.
5. Distance in time does not diminish value. The wellbeing of a person who exists a billion years from now counts for exactly as much as mine does.
6. Even so, I think we ought to devote much more of our effort to the near future than to the far future, because we are far better informed about the consequences of what we do soon.
7. I am not committed to maximizing. I think there are constraints on what an agent may do even when doing it would bring about a better world.

I have been told that not all of these can stand together, but I cannot locate the trouble myself, and I would rather not abandon a commitment I could have kept. Please tell me which of these genuinely conflict with which, what exactly the conflict consists in, and for each one the cheapest available revision together with what the resulting theory loses by it. Please also identify any pair that only seems to be in tension, so that I do not waste space defending it, and rank the real conflicts by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad.
