A friend lent me a pop-math book about games, and one chapter argues like this:

"Strategy stealing is one of the most beautiful results in game theory. Suppose the second player had a winning strategy in a symmetric game like Hex. The first player makes any old opening move, then swaps roles in his head and follows the second player's strategy; if the strategy ever calls for a cell he has already taken, he simply plays somewhere else instead, since an extra stone of your own color can never hurt you. So the first player wins after all — contradiction. The upshot is that in any game where both players have the same moves available, the first player must have a winning strategy: tic-tac-toe, Hex, even misère tic-tac-toe, where whoever completes a line first is the loser. The only catch is that the proof never tells you what the winning moves actually are."

The misère example makes me uneasy, and I'm pretty sure tic-tac-toe is a draw with best play, so I don't see how both things can be true. Go through the passage and tell me what's solid, what's overstated, and what condition the argument really needs in order to run. Exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or wrap-up.
