Doing a master's in philosophy and I've been working through the arguments that say your degrees of belief have to obey the probability rules on pain of being cleaned out by a clever bookie. There's a feature of them I keep running into and I haven't got a word for it, and I'd like to know whether one exists.

Here's the setup from a seminar exercise. Three runners, exactly one of them wins. I'd pay up to 30p for a £1 ticket on Alice, up to 30p for a £1 ticket on Bob, and — asked separately — up to 80p for a £1 ticket on 'Alice or Bob'. The bookie sells me the third ticket for 78p and buys the first two off me for 32p each. I'm 14p out of pocket and the payouts cancel exactly whichever horse wins. Every one of those three deals cleared my own bar at the moment it was put to me. But if he'd been made to offer the whole thing as one contract, it would have read 'hand over 14p and recieve nothing', and nobody signs that.

That's the bit I want a name for. My twin in the next room has exactly the same numbers and is facing exactly the same bookie, but he insists on being shown all the offers at once, and he can't be touched. So whatever the vulnerability is, its not sitting in the credences on their own — it's a property of the pair (credences, offer protocol), and you can add it or take it away by changing how the bets get presented, without anyone's mind changing at all.

Is there an established concept for this? I don't want the nearest label just slapped on it, I want the carving: what the thing is, what it isn't, and where the edges fall. In particular, tell me how it differs from the old objection that these theorems idealise away finite minds, from the way behavioural economists describe people evaluating gambles one at a time rather than as a portfolio, from the version of the argument that runs over time and turns on how you plan to revise, and from the general point that what you pick depends on the menu you're handed. And if nothing fits properly, coin something and define it tightly enough that I can settle the borderline cases — does it still bite when the bookie gets only one approach but I don't know what he'll offer, and does it survive if the one-at-a-time presentation is something I chose for myself rather than something done to me?

Skip the references, I'll chase those down myself. Answer in exactly five numbered points, two or three sentences each, with nothing before or after them; between them they should cover whether a term exists, the nearest candidates and what each of them misses, the definition you'd give, and where the boundaries lie.
