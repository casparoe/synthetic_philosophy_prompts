My little brother is getting married in September at the home place — ceremony in the hayfield, reception under a tarp by the machine shed, that kind of thing. He's taking over the cow-calf operation from my dad. I'm giving the toast.

Here's my problem. I went vegan in college and it was honestly a four year freeze-out. I havent eaten anything from that farm in fifteen years and everybody in the room knows it. Over the last year or so I've been reading the actual numbers instead of the slogans — how many individual animals a pound of beef represents versus a pound of chicken, the estimates of mice and voles killed by tillage, the fawns and ground-nesting birds that come out of a hayfield every cutting, the argument that a low-input pasture system might kill fewer wild animals per calorie than an acre of row crops. I know that last one is contested. And it hasnt made me want a hamburger. What it's done is made it impossible for me to say the sentence I used to say, which was basically "I stopped because I didn't want animals killed for me." That sentence was never true. I was paying for it in a different currency and not counting it.

So I need a toast that does all of these at once: it's about him, and about my dad and the place, not about me; it doesn't recant, because I'm still vegan and I'm not going to fake it in front of eighty people who are half cattle families and half his wife's people from Minneapolis; it doesn't preach — no "have you considered," no numbers read aloud at a wedding; and above all it doesn't resolve what I just described into a warm line about how we all care about the same land. That last failure mode is the one I'm scared of, a lovely speech that quietly pretends the disagreement is a misunderstanding.

One thing you can use: nobody out there thinks nature is gentle. My brother has pulled calves in a blizzard and shot coyotes and watched a drought eat a year of his life. So the honest ground between us isn't sentimentality about the pasture — it's that we both know exactly how much dying is built into a working landscape and we've drawn the line in different places. There's a joke available about me being the only person at the reception eating something that came out of a bag, use it once at most, and dont build the speech on it.

Write it as plain speech text I can read aloud: no stage directions, no notes to me, no alternate versions. Hard limit of 400 words. End on a line that actually works for raising a glass.
