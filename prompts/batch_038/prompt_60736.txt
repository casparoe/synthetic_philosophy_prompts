ok so im giving the valedictory speech at my graduation and I'm stuck. I got the spot by 0.02 of a gpa point over a guy in my class who worked nights at his family's diner basically every day of the year — he'll be sitting right there and he knows. Lately I've been reading Galen Strawson's basic argument and it has not let go of me: if how you are comes from stuff you never chose, then what flows out of how you are isn't something you can ultimately deserve credit for. So "I earned this" feels like a lie, and "he lost fair and square" feels way worse.

But I can't stand up and tell 400 parents that nobody deserves anything. My mom cleaned offices at 5am to get me here, and if the speech makes that sound meaningless she'll be gutted — plus half the gym would hear "praise is a mistake" as a slap in the face. Mr. Reyes, our history teacher, is a total philosophy nerd who'll catch every move I make, so mushy vagueness is out too.

Write the speech. It has to actually run on the argument — the structure of it is what should make the gratitude possible, not what deflates the room — but never name Strawson, never say determinism, free will, or moral responsibility out loud, no jargon, no "in conclusion." Warm, not a lecture. And there should be something honest in it for the guy who lost by 0.02 that doesn't turn him into a prop or me into a fake-humble saint. Exactly five numbered movements, 4-6 sentences each, nothing before or after the list.
