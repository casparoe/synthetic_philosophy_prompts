I've been asked to comment on a colleague's draft concerning the hole argument in general relativity. His proposal is to fill the hole region with a dense lattice of ideal clocks and gyroscopes, so that metric components can be recorded relative to physically identifiable matter rather than to bare manifold points. On his account, this makes the two diffeomorphism-related solutions empirically distinguishable: one predicts one pattern of clock readings across the lattice, the other predicts a different pattern, so the indeterminism that the hole argument appears to generate could in principle be settled by measurement — and general relativity would be exposed as genuinely indeterministic. Two questions. Does his lattice really let an observer tell the two solutions apart, given that the clocks and gyroscopes are themselves carried along by the same diffeomorphism that relates the solutions? And if it does not, does the hole argument still pose a live threat to determinism in the theory, or has a material reference frame like his quietly dissolved it rather than tested it?
