I write a science-and-ideas column and am building a sidebar on local currencies that I want to run against the grain of the usual framing. The episode is Wörgl, a small Tyrolean town where, in July 1932, the mayor issued municipal notes pegged to the Austrian schilling and backed by a bank deposit, each bearing twelve spaces on the reverse: to keep a note valid, the holder had to affix a monthly stamp costing one percent of its face value. Holding money therefore cost money. The notes moved quickly, residents paid their taxes early, the town paved roads, built a bridge, installed street lighting and repaired its sewers, and local unemployment fell while it rose in the rest of Austria. After roughly fourteen months the Austrian courts upheld the National Bank's monetary monopoly and the scheme was shut down.

The design was Gesellian, but I want it analyzed through Aristotle rather than through Gesell or Keynes: the treatment of money in Book V of the Nicomachean Ethics and Book I of the Politics. That gives you money as convention rather than nature; money as a measure that makes unlike things commensurable so that unequal parties can exchange; money as a stand-in for need and a pledge for future exchange; the distinction between household management and money-making for its own sake, including the Midas-style point that a pile of currency can leave its owner starving; and the judgment that interest is the most unnatural form of acquisition because it makes money breed.

Three things, then. First, the disciplined mapping: by Aristotle's lights, what did the Wörgl authorities do in issuing stamped notes, and does a holding cost look like the perversion of money or its correction? Second, where the theory bites and where it has no vocabulary at all — I suspect velocity, and monetary policy as such, simply fall outside his categories; show me which features of the episode (the peg, tax acceptance, the early payment of arrears, the court's shutdown) it genuinely illuminates and which it distorts. Third, what the episode does back to the theory: confirmation that money is conventional and politically instituted, an anomaly, or a case Aristotle never anticipated? Be explicit about whether the peg is fatal to the story, since the town kept the national unit of account while its own notes did the circulating — and tell me whether Aristotle's running of measure and medium together survives that separation.
