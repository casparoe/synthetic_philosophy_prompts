G.E. Moore basically thought the greatest goods were affection for people and the enjoyment of beautiful things, and that a whole made of an emotion plus a genuinely beautiful object is worth way more than its parts separately. Say we dropped him into now and made him reckon with people who have deep friendships and romantic relationships with AI chatbots, plus fans who feel intense personal closeness to streamers and influencers who have never heard of them. How would he score those states of mind? The wrinkle seems to be that the affection feels just as vivid, but the object doesnt actually possess the qualities the emotion is directed at — so is the organic unity degraded, merely a lesser good of the same kind, or something Moore would classify totally differently? And would it matter to him whether the person knows perfectly well its a simulation, versus being fooled? Argue it out from his actual reasoning in the last chapter of Principia Ethica, including how he handled illusion and deception there, rather than just guessing what a fussy old Cambridge don would say.
