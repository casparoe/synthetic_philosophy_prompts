For six years my firm and the one next door have shared a loading dock, a part-time forklift driver, and a fuel account, on the understanding that we split everything half and half and neither of us invoices the other. Last quarter they billed me a full month of the driver's wages and did not send me my half of the fuel I had fronted. Their explanation is that a new office manager had never been told how things worked and simply did what the ledger suggested, and I honestly cannot tell whether that is true or whether it was a probe to see what I would do. I am weighing three responses: absorb it and carry on exactly as before; quietly under-credit them on the next round of expenses; or convert the whole thing into itemised invoices and a written cost-sharing agreement.

One friend says an arrangement that survived six years is worth more than a quarter's money. Another says it only held because each of us knew the other would respond in kind, and that anything less teaches them the arrangement is cheap. We are two of about a dozen small tenants on this estate, and people talk to one another. What unsettles me most is that both our leases expire in roughly fourteen months, one of us will probably not be renewed, and I suspect we are each already deciding how much to put into the shared equipment.

Which course is actually the better one, and does the case for it survive the fact that there is now a visible end in sight?
