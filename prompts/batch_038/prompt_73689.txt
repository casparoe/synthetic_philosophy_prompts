I'm the part-time archivist at a tiny historical society upstate (town of about 9,000, one paid staff member, me being the other half of the operation). At the last monthly meeting the room split badly over a 1908 plaque on the courthouse wall honoring the mill owner who founded the place — half the room wants it down, half wants it defended, and one guy has read a blog summary of Nietzsche's essay on the use and abuse of history for life and keeps throwing monumental history at people like a brick. The board asked me, because I'm the one who supposedly reads, to do an hour before the next meeting on the three modes — monumental, antiquarian, critical — so the argument has some shared vocabulary. I get 55 minutes, a whiteboard with a dying marker, and roughly 18 people, mostly over 60, none of whom have opened a philosophy book since high school and several of whom have a personal stake in the plaque.

So: don't explain the essay to me, plan how I should explain it to them. What goes on the board first and what depends on it — my instinct is that the whole thing hangs on the fact that each mode comes as a use-and-a-disease pair, and that the frame about history being for life rather than for its own sake has to be in place before I can even name the three modes, but tell me if I've got that backwards. Which single case should carry the hour: I assume it can't be our own plaque, so say why not and then tell me what a stand-in case has to contain and what it absolutely can't contain for the thing to work. Then the wrong turns — my list so far is that one of the three is the correct one and its probably critical, that critical history just means tearing things down, that antiquarian is harmless nostalgia, and that monumental history is nothing but hagiography so we can skip it — and for each one, the point in the sequence where I have to head it off rather than patching it afterward. Give me one question I can put to the room at the end that would actually show they've got the distinction rather than just enjoyed the hour, and tell me what I should flatly leave out at this level and announce that I'm leaving out. And finally: how do I keep this from turning into a referendum on the plaque while still making it unmistakably relevant to the plaque, since if it stops being about the plaque nobody will show up.
