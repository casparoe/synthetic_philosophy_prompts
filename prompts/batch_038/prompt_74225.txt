I sit on the panel that runs a small futarchy pilot at a research institute: for the past two years we have settled some internal questions by opening prediction markets rather than by voting. Our record so far is as follows. We approved a market on whether a software migration would be completed by a fixed date, resolved against the vendor's public release log. We refused to open a market on whether the institute's director would step down within the year, on the ground that the best-informed traders were also the ones with most to gain from the answer and some ability to produce it. We approved, subject to a rule excluding senior staff from trading, a market on whether a major funder would renew a programme. We rejected a proposed market on whether a new internal policy would improve the culture here, because nothing could later be checked against it. We approved a thinly traded market on which of two contractors would be selected, on the explicit understanding that the price would inform our deliberation but not settle it. And we adopted a standing gloss distinguishing traders who can bring an outcome about from traders who merely know about it, barring only the former — a gloss that sits somewhat uneasily with the director case, where our stated worry also concerned who stood to gain.

The director now proposes something closer to futarchy proper: two markets, one on the institute's refereed publication output over eight years if we adopt restructuring plan A, the other on the same measure under plan B, with whichever market clears higher to be adopted automatically. The measure is defined in advance and auditable; the horizon outlasts every current member of the panel; only one of the two markets can ever be resolved; and plan A's principal advocate has offered to fund the payout pool and intends to trade in both markets.

Which ruling best coheres with our own precedents — approve, approve with conditions, or reject? Please identify the closest analog among the five earlier cases, lay out the candidate rules our record supports (they should not all point the same way here, and say what each implies for this proposal), state which of our precedents we would effectively have to overrule or reinterpret on each option, give your recommendation, and tell us honestly what principle we would be committing ourselves to if we decided the other way.
