Before I assign readings on whether "why did industrialization take off in Britain rather than in China?" is even a well-posed question, I want the specification rather than the literature. Write the desiderata a contrastive question about a large historical divergence must satisfy to count as a legitimate object of explanation: the status of the explanandum (an event, a process, or a standing difference in level?); how determinate the foil has to be, and whether "China" and "Britain" are the right units or stand-ins for regions within them; what the space of alternative outcomes must look like for the question to have a non-vacuous answer; whether an adequate response must cite a cause or may consist of a coincidence-plus-selection story; and how much of the gap must be accounted for before the question counts as answered. For each, give the concrete worry drawn from the divergence case itself rather than from toy examples, and mark which requirements are genuine and which are the smuggled preferences of one camp — comparative institutionalist, cliometrician, or narrative historian. Finally, a colleague proposes what she calls the Substitution Test: the question is well posed only if you can name a third case that could equally have filled the "why here" slot and a fourth the "why not there" slot without changing the answer. Fold it into your list and report whether it survives contact with your other desiderata; if some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, say which requirement does the most damage, and what a historian who accepts the list would be licensed to ask instead.
