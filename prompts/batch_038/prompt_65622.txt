I run a little epistemology reading group on Tuesdays and every single year somebody drags the surprise exam paradox back onto the table, and every year we go around in the same circle — someone says the induction is fine, someone says the base case is bad, someone says the whole thing is a trick about what surprise means. This spring I finally sat down and wrote out where I've actually landed, partly to stop the conversation from restarting from zero again, and I want to know whether the package hangs togeher before I hand it out. Here's the setup I have in mind: on Monday morning the teacher announces there'll be an exam somewhere that week, on a day the student won't know the exam is coming until the morning it happens; the student believes her, and she is in fact both truthful and right. My commitments:

(1) Knowledge is closed under known implication, and under conjunction — if you know p and you know q, you're in a position to know the pair of them.
(2) KK is false. Knowing doesn't automatically bring along knowing that you know; I find the margin-for-error style arguments against luminosity convincing, and I'm sympathetic to the view that a sound announcement can defeat its own uptake.
(3) The student does know the teacher is trustworthy and does know she made the announcement — this is ordinary empirical knowledge, no different in kind from knowing the kettle is on.
(4) The announcement, in the situation it's actually uttered in, is a blindspot for the student: it's true, it's consistent, and he's in no position to know it. I like this way of putting it because it makes the paradox look like a discovery about knowledge rather than a mistake somebody made.
(5) The teacher is blameless — nothing has gone wrong on her side, she hasn't said anything she shouldn't have.
(6) The base case fails: come Wednesday night, with Monday through Wednesday gone, the student still isn't automatically in a position to know the exam is Thursday. But I also don't think Thursday-knowledge is somehow ruled out — it might come back once the week has whittled down, and if it does, there's no surprise left to be had.

So: audit these for me. I already suspect (3) and (6) are rubbing against each other but I can't quite see whether the trouble is inside (1) alone, or between (1) and (4), or whether I'm misreading my own (6). I'd also like to know whether (2) is doing any real work here at all, because there's a part of me that wants to say dropping KK is the whole solution and another part that suspects it never mattered — tell me plainly which. Order the tensions by severity, give me the cheapest repair for each, and then tell me which single revision buys the most peace, and specifically what I'd have to give up about (4) — do I still get to call the announcement unknowable by this student, and if so in what sense?
