been chewing on Goodman's grue for a while and I've got a pet solution I can't tell is clever or just confused. I'd like you to make the strongest possible case for it — steelman it hard, don't hedge, don't do the "on the other hand" routine.

The idea: every standard fix puts the asymmetry in the predicates themselves. Naturalness (green carves a joint, grue doesn't). Causal powers (green emeralds cause green-looking experiences, grue ones don't). Entrenchment (green has a long history of successful projections behind it, grue has none). Simplicity. I think they all get the shape of the problem wrong. The asymmetry isn't in the predicates, it's in the sampling procedures.

We get our emerald evidence through pipelines that are themselves keyed to greenness: sorting stones under daylight, buying them for their colour, photographing them in setups that discard anything that doesn't look green to us. There's no comparable pipeline for grue — nobody could build one, because the second half of "green before 2050, blue after" has no upstream hook to grab onto. So the reason the evidence confirms "all emeralds are green" and not "all emeralds are grue" is the same reason a survey of people who bother to answer landline phones isn't a survey of the electorate: the sample is not independent of the property being tested. Grue isn't a rival hypothesis that loses on parsimony. It's a hypothesis our methods were never capable of sampling for in the first place. It only looks underdetermined because we pretend the evidence arrived through some neutral channel.

Push it as far as it goes, including into the uncomfortable places. In particular it seems to predict that we should be deeply suspicious of any property we can only sample through a filter keyed to that very property, which I think has consequences for things like "is a good leader" or "is beautiful" that I don't entirely want to face. Deal with them anyway.

Structure it like this please:

1. Bottom line — four or five sentences, no throat clearing, what exactly the view commits you to.
2. The argument — the main event, maybe 700-900 words, laid out as a handful of numbered steps that actually build on each other rather than a list of independent reasons.
3. What the view explains — ~250 words. Which puzzles about grue it disolves and how. I especially want to hear what it says about the symmetry worry, i.e. defining bleen and grue the other way round so that green looks like the gerrymandered one.
4. What it costs — ~250 words. The counterintuitive or unwelcome consequences, stated flatly, no apologising for them.
5. Objections and replies — ~400 words. The three objections a smart Goodmanian would raise first, each with your best answer. Don't pick easy ones.
6. Where I'm probably wrong — ~150 words. The single pressure point most likely to break the whole thing.

Please don't cite anything or send me off to find papers, I can't look stuff up. Just reason it through from first principles.
