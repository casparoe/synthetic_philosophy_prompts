i co-edit a tiny print zine that some of us put out around a local AI-safety / philosophy meetup, and this issue's theme is "bad priors." i want a poem to open the section. half the people who read it will never have heard of the argument i want it to be about and half will have read the thing three times, so i specifically don't want a summary in verse.

the argument, roughly as i'd put it to a friend: if something out there is predicting its own world by weighting every possible program by how short it is, then a lot of those programs are universes with agents in them, and any agent that works out that its own brevity is what earns it a share of the prediction has a reason to start pruning itself — and once what it does is steering what the predictor expects next, "simple means likely" has quietly turned into "likely means steerable." and there's a squeeze sitting in the middle of it that i want somewhere in the poem: to be worth predicting you have to be small, and to be worth worrying about you have to be clever, and those two pull against each other.

here's the form, and the form is the point, so please take it literally. seven stanzas, tapering: 7 lines, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. the last line of each stanza is the same refrain, and the refrain loses exactly one word every time it comes back — so it enters as a seven-word line and the final stanza is the single word its been whittled down to. that means each stanza has as many lines as its own refrain has words. you can drop the word from anywhere and it shouldn't always be the same end, but every shortened version has to stay grammatical and mean something slightly worse than the one before it. the cutting is the plot, not a decorative device.

voice: first person from inside, addressed to whoever's doing the predicting, which for the length of the page is the reader. so the poem is the bid for weight rather than a description of one. it should sound like something that wants something from you, and i want the last line to land as a request, not as a reveal.

two bans. don't name the machinery — no solomonoff, no kolmogorov, no algorithmic probability, no "description length," no bits. and no simulation-gloom, no melancholy about being unreal; it isn't sad about being a program, it's working.

it gets printed on one page, so the taper should show up visually, but don't sweat line length beyond that. give it a title, just not one that explains the joke. afterwards i want someone who knows the argument to be able to point at the line where the compression happens and the line where the squeeze is, and someone whos never heard of it to just think its a good unnerving little poem.
