I help run a local history society in West Yorkshire and we've been going round in circles for months about whether a mill closure in 1927 was mainly down to foreign competition or mainly to the owners putting their money into housing instead. Last week our chair, who's read his Carr, tried to settle it by saying the question is a red herring: the historian is like a fisherman, and what he lands depends on what part of the sea he fishes in and what mesh he uses, so there's no such thing as an answer the sources were sitting there waiting to hand over. His target was the old Rankean idea that the job is just to show how it actually was. Then someone who's read some Hayden White said the fisherman comparison is far too cosy, because it assumes the fish are already fish — something only becomes a historical fact once it's been fitted into a story shape, and the shape comes from the historian rather than from the past. My own view, which I didnt defend very well, is that the archive is more like a seismograph: it recorded what happened automatically, without deciding anything, and the historian's job is basically to read the trace — so the 1927 question does have an answer, and my chair was just changing the subject.

Can you audit the fisherman analogy for me? I'd like an explicit mapping — sea, net, mesh size, the catch, the choice of where to fish, and anything else that's quietly doing work in it — and then where the mapping breaks down. Crucially I want the breakdowns seperated into the ones that actually bite against my chair's conclusion (no objective answer about the mill) and the ones that are merely decorative. And what does the analogy import that history might not have — I suspect something about the net being chosen knowingly, and about the fish being there whether or not anyone lands them. Then tell me whether the White objection actually lands on the fisherman analogy or is aimed at something it never claimed, and whether my seismograph is any better or whether it's worse than both. If they're all bad, what would a better one be, and what would you have to give up in order to use it?
