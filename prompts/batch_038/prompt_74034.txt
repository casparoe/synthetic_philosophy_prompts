I'm giving a talk at our political philosophy workshop next month on a version of futarchy I've been working on for a couple of years. Rough shape: a citizen jury, redrawn every four years, sets the weights in a public welfare index, and the proposal that gets adopted is whichever one a prediction market prices as maximizing that index. My twist is that traders are also betting on how *future* juries will reweight the index — so the market has to price the chance that today's winners look like losers from the evaluative standpoint we're actually going to end up with, and I argue that's what makes the thing more than technocracy. Audience is mostly Rawlsian and deliberative-democracy types for whom legitimacy comes from exchanging reasons, plus one market-design economist and a public choice person whos seen it all before. Give me a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from these people, not the ones I'd get online, ranked by how much damage they'd do, phrased the way each of them would phrase it, with a two-minute reply for each and a note on where my reply is softest. And tell me which question I should be praying doesn't come up.
