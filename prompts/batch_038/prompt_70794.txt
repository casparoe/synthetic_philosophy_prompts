I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on Pascal's political thought — the fragments on justice and force, on custom as the whole basis of equity, and on the thought that, being unable to make what is just strong, human beings made what is strong just. I want a decision tree that sorts a reader into a named interpretation of what Pascal is doing in those fragments, not a survey of the interpretations that exist.

Please build the tree from the logical space first: the questions that genuinely separate readings, and only then the positions located in it. Every branch node must be a question a student can answer on the basis of a case or a claim, without already knowing which reading the answer leads to. The cases should include a usurper's regime now in its third generation, stable and obeyed; a law the student is confident is unjust within a settled order, and whether to obey publicly while dissenting privately; two communities on either side of a river holding contradictory property customs; and a conqueror's redistributed land whose new titles everyone has since accepted.

The nodes should be drawn from distinctions the readings tend to collapse: whether Pascal thinks there is a true justice and whether it is accessible to unaided reason; whether existing laws are partly just or merely obeyed; what grounds the duty to obey — prudence, on readings where avoiding civil war is the worst of evils, the authority custom has simply by being received, or something theological; and whether the remark about making the strong just is offered as genealogy, as justification, or as a diagnosis of the part imagination plays in authority.

The leaves must include at least: quietist conventionalism; the reading on which Pascal sides with those who revere established law against the clever who despise it; a fideist reading on which true justice belongs to an order inaccessible after the Fall; a political-realist reading; and a deflationary or genealogical reading on which Pascal leaves no room for justice at all. Where your questions permit a combination nobody defends, mark it unoccupied and say briefly why — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot actually separate (I suspect realist and genealogical readings may agree on every first-order verdict and differ only about whether Pascal thinks this is a tragedy), and say what further question would separate them, if any exists.

Finally, tell me which single node does most of the sorting work, which leaf's placement you are least confident about, and what consideration — a fragment that reads differently under a rival interpretation, some structural feature of the text — would move you. Note anywhere you have leaned on a contested reading rather than on something Pascal plainly says.
