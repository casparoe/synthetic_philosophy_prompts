A friend and I spent most of yesterday evening on an argument neither of us could close, and I should like a clear-headed adjudication rather than more heat.

The immediate subject was whether the principle that nothing can be both wholly red and wholly green is knowable independently of experience. I suggested it was a poor candidate for a mere empirical generalisation about pigments and retinas; my friend replied that this is precisely the sort of confidence mid-century empiricism dismantled, and closed the matter by invoking Quine's line to the effect that nothing is immune from revision, adding that Quine himself floats the idea of adopting a non-classical logic in much the same spirit in which one adopts a new physical theory. I am reconstructing both from memory. Please do not trouble yourself with locating the passages, quoting them, or supplying references of any kind: I want the argument assessed, not sourced.

What I want instead is a menu of the distinct and precise claims that slogan can be made to carry, since it did an enormous amount of work in the conversation while remaining wholly unexamined. For each reading, say what it commits one to, and whether it is a platitude, a substantive but defensible thesis, something merely contentious, or straightforwardly false. The readings I half-articulated at the time included: that it is not conceptually impossible to abandon any belief; that there could be evidence which would make abandoning it rational; that no belief is more entrenched or better warranted than any other; that no belief is justified otherwise than through its contribution to the system as a whole; and that the meaning of any statement is fixed only by its place in that system. Please add whatever I missed, and be careful to keep apart three questions that we ran together — whether a statement may be revised, whether it may be revised without compensating adjustments elsewhere, and what would count as revising it rather than changing the subject. He also maintained, in the same breath, that any statement can be retained come what may provided enough of the rest of the system is adjusted; tell me whether those two claims taken jointly say anything at all about the epistemic standing of a particular belief, or whether satisfying them both is so cheap as to be compatible with everything I was trying to claim.

Then, and this is the part I actually care about: state which of the readings, if true, would license his dismissal of my claim about red and green, and what further premises he would need to supply before it could. Please also address whether the slogan survives being turned on itself, and what becomes of his appeal to a non-classical logic if the very rules by which he would assess that change are themselves among the things up for revision.

Finally, offer me a short and non-combative reply I can make the next time the line is deployed against me — one that concedes everything the slogan legitimately establishes and declines only the rest.

Please write throughout in British English.
