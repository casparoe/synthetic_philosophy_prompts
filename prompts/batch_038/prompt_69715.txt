I am preparing a handout for the first and last meetings of a graduate seminar on moral responsibility, and I want a self-diagnostic decision tree that tells participants which position they actually occupy rather than which label they would claim. The tree should be built out of Strawson's apparatus: reactive attitudes, participant attitudes, the objective attitude, and his two kinds of plea — those that take an action out of the range of ordinary interpretation (it was an accident; he did not know; he was forced) and those that present the agent as someone to be handled rather than engaged with (immaturity, psychosis, compulsion, pathological formative circumstances).

Two constraints matter most. No node may mention determinism, free will, or compatibilism, and none may be answerable by recalling a philosopher's slogan; every node is a concrete case — a particular person who has done a particular wrong — followed by two or three options about what the participant would feel, say, or do. And at least one node must set side by side two cases that look alike but should sort people differently: a wrongdoer whose act is traced to a brain tumor, and a wrongdoer whose act is traced through an equally complete causal history containing nothing pathological.

The tree should have about seven nodes and terminate in eight leaves: Strawsonian compatibilism; semi-compatibilism of the Fischer kind; revisionism of the Vargas kind; free will pessimism; optimistic skepticism with a quarantine rationale, of the Pereboom kind; illusionism of the Smilansky kind; abolitionism of the Greene and Cohen kind; and a "thoroughgoing objective attitude" leaf, on which you should tell me whether anyone can coherently stand.

Please organize the response under these headings, in this order:

1. **Starting point** (~100 words) — the commitment every participant is assumed to bring into the tree.
2. **The tree** (~900 words) — numbered nodes, each with its case, its options, and where each option leads.
3. **Leaves and scoring key** (~500 words) — each leaf with the path that reaches it and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
4. **Empty rooms** (~250 words) — three or four answer-combinations the tree permits that nobody holds, each diagnosed as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
5. **Where the tree fails** (~200 words) — the pairs of leaves this instrument cannot separate, and why.

No footnotes and no bibliography; the point is diagnostic, not scholarly.
