Right — I cover science and ideas for a weekly magazine and I've got a piece coming up on whether science actually gets us nearer the truth, with a sidebar that's going to be one of those tap-your-way-through flowchart things. The problem is that the ones already out there are either insulting to the reader or written for a graduate seminar, and I need something in between. Can you build me the flowchart?

The entry point is fixed, so don't make it a question: everyone starting has already accepted that science is absurdly good at prediction, and that some theories which were brilliant at predicting things got binned anyway. From there, take them to a named position.

A few things I need:

Roughly ten to twelve nodes, and each one has to be a concrete case or a claim the reader can react to on its own terms. No node may contain an ism or a philosopher's surname — I want people sorted by what they think about the aether, not by which label they'd pick off a menu. Two sentences per node at most, and phrase them so nobody can tell which way a branch is trying to push them.

Cases I definitely want working somewhere in the branches: Fresnel's aether theory, where the equations largely carried over into the successor theory even after the aether itself was scrapped; phlogiston; caloric; the four humours; and at least one going the other way, i.e. something predicted on theoretical grounds before anyone had seen it. If you need a fifth or sixth case to make a distinction bite, add one, but tell me why it's load-bearing.

The leaves should carry the standard name where one exists — the realist and anti-realist families that actually get defended, including the selective versions that only commit on part of a theory, the structuralist move, the intervention-and-manipulation view, constructive empiricism, plain instrumentalism, and the quietist position that says the whole quarrel is a pseudo-problem. Where two names pick out what your tree treats as one destination, say so rather than inventing a fork to keep them apart. And if there's a combination of answers your questions make available but nobody occupies, leave it as a leaf and diagnose it: is it empty because it's incoherent, because it's just unattractive, or because nobody's got round to it?

Flag every pair of leaves your tree can't genuinely separate. I'd bet there's at least one somewhere near the structural realist corner, and I'd much rather you admitted it than fudged a branch to make the chart look tidier than it is.

Under each leaf, one line naming the case that most embarrasses it — specific and a bit spiky, because that's what the letters page will argue about. Not "it struggles to account for theory change", which tells me nothing.

The angle, so this isn't just a textbook tree with the serial numbers filed off: I want the typical reader to land somewhere they wouldn't have guessed. The fun version of this piece is "you're convinced you're a hard-nosed realist, but it turns out you're a realist about electrons and an instrumentalist about everything else". So the questions should mostly pull apart what someone is a realist about, rather than whether they are one at all.

Audience is intelligent British general readers with no philosophy training. No reading list, no citations, no bibliography, and don't hedge it all into mush — if you think one of these positions is right, let the tree still work for someone who disagrees. Also, one thing I keep getting wrong when I write these up: its the difference between a theory being approximately true and a theory's central posits being real, and my last draft collapsed them. Build that distinction into the structure explicitly if you can, and tell me which node does the work.
