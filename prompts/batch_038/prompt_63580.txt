I just started a PhD in a group that builds little information engines — a colloidal bead in water held in an optical trap, we image where it is every so often and then shift the trap so the favourable thermal kicks get rectified into something useful. My proposal has a section on the conceptual foundations of all this and my supervisor handed it back saying it doesnt hang together, then wouldnt tell me which part he meant. So before I rewrite it I want an audit of what I actually believe, not a demon tutorial.

1. Landauer's principle is a theorem of statistical mechanics, not a rule of thumb: resetting a one-bit memory in contact with a bath at temperature T can't be done for less than kT ln 2 of work.
2. The measurement itself can be made reversible and effectively free; the whole bill comes due at the reset. That's the answer to the demon, full stop.
3. Our engine is a real one. Over a complete cycle it produces net work, taking heat from a single bath and turning it into a raised weight, up to about kT ln 2 per bit of information acquired.
4. Some individual runs beat kT ln 2 per bit and that's fine by me — the bound is on the average, which is what the fluctuation relations are about.
5. But the Kelvin statement of the second law is an exceptionless law of nature, not an overwhelmingly likely tendency. I really dislike the probabilistic way of putting it.
6. The information term in the generalized second law is mutual information between system and memory: an objective physical correlation, nothing to do with anybody's state of knowledge.
7. And yet in the outreach talk I give about this work I say the engine runs on knowledge — that two people who know different things about the same bath can pull different amounts of work out of it, and both be right.

Which of these genuinely collide, and which only look like they collide? Rank the collisions by how much damage they do to the proposal, and for each say what I'd have to give up and what that costs me. Please don't just flag all seven as sitting uneasily together — I'm fairly confident some of them are perfectly fine as a set, I just can't tell which, and the ones that are fine matter as much to me as the ones that aren't.
