I want a number, not a survey. I am familiar with the standard moves on Gettier-style cases; what I am after is your credence about one particular case, together with the decomposition that produces it.

The case — call it the Depot. Everything below is stipulated, so please take it as given rather than trying to argue me out of any of it:

1. Mira has supervised Tank 3 at a fuel depot for eleven years. At the start of every shift she reads gauge G, mounted on the tank, and forms a belief about the tank's contents on that basis. So far as anyone can tell, G has never been wrong in those eleven years, and it passed its scheduled recertification last month.
2. Three weeks before the night in question, G's sensor lead was severed. Since then G has displayed the reading FULL continuously, whatever the actual state of the tank.
3. Tank 3 is refilled only when a downstream order comes in. During those three weeks, orders came in on two nights; on every other night the tank was below half at the hour Mira read G, and on each of those nights she formed the belief that the tank was full and failed to detect that it was false.
4. Tonight an order came in, the tank was topped off two hours before Mira's shift began, and Tank 3 is full. G displays FULL, and Mira forms the belief that Tank 3 is full.
5. That belief is caused by, and based on, her reading of G and nothing else. Tonight she also glanced at the fill-pump's runtime log and at the intact departure seal on the tanker, each of which would by itself have been adequate grounds for the belief; but she took in neither as evidence, could cite neither if asked, and would have formed the belief had both been absent.
6. Mira believes, on her supervisor's say-so, that G was recalibrated last week. That belief is false, and it is inferentially inert: the eleven-year record alone would have carried her to the same belief by the same route.

Target proposition: *Mira knows that Tank 3 is full tonight.*

Give me your credence in that proposition, then show the derivation. I expect the terms to include at least the following, though you should add any I have missed:

- how finely the belief-forming method ought to be individuated, and which individuation is the one that matters for the modal and process-reliabilist conditions;
- whether safety, sensitivity, or a proper-function/reliability condition is the right constraint, and what verdict each returns on these stipulations;
- whether the three-week run of undetected false beliefs does any epistemic damage to tonight's true belief, or whether what matters is only how tonight's belief was produced;
- whether the adequate grounds in (5) that Mira never uses can contribute anything — what propositional justification is worth for a belief not based on it;
- whether the false background belief in (6) operates as a defeater or a false lemma despite playing no role in the inference;
- whether 'knows' is context-sensitive to a degree that leaves the target proposition without a stable truth value, and if so, how you are fixing the context in order to be able to report a number at all.

Assign each term a rough probability with a sentence or two of justification, and then actually combine them — a weighted aggregation, a product, a veto structure, whatever you think is right — rather than leaving the terms standing side by side.

After the combination, answer three further questions. Which single term is your final figure most sensitive to? What one additional stipulation, or what one argument, would move you furthest? And given that my own credence is 0.2 while a colleague's is 0.75, which term do you think accounts for most of that gap?

Finally, be explicit about your own confidence, at two levels: how sure you are of the number, and separately how sure you are of the decomposition itself — that these are the right terms and that they are near enough to independent for the way you have combined them. Single out the point in the whole answer you are least sure of, and flag any place where your reasoning depends on a contested reading of my stipulations rather than on the stipulations as written.
