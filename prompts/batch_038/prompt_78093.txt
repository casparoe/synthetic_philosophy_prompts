My friend Priya is deep in a metaphysics PhD and she has extremely strong intuitions about origin essentialism — the whole "could you have come from a different sperm and egg" thing. She talks about it constanly and I've started keeping a list of her verdicts, because I'm fairly sure she's less consistent than she thinks she is.

Her record so far. Each one is framed as: is the person in the scenario you, or is it somebody else?

1. Same sperm, same egg, everything else identical — you, obviously, she says this isn't even a question.
2. Same sperm, but a different one of your mother's eggs, released a different month — not you, that's a sibling, full stop.
3. Same egg, different sperm from your father — not you, also a sibling.
4. Same sperm and egg, but your mother had a bad flu that week and you're born in a different hospital on a different continent — you.
5. Same sperm and egg, except they meet in a dish and the embryo sits frozen for four years before it's implanted — you. She says freezing just isn't the kind of thing that could matter.
6. Same sperm and egg, but at the one-cell stage a technician edits out the allele for Huntington's — you, same person, better off.
7. Same sperm and egg, the zygote forms normally, but a few hours later a technician removes the two pronuclei and replaces them with the pronuclei from some unrelated couple's zygote; that thing develops and your mother gives birth to it — not you. She's very confident here, and her reason is that the egg's cytoplasm can't be what makes you you if the nucleus belongs to strangers.
8. Identical twinning at day three — she says one of the twins is you, but there's no fact about which one. I think that's a dodge but she wont budge.

She also describes herself as a plain Kripkean: the sperm and the egg are essential, done. Which seems odd given 6 and 7.

Here's the case she can't settle, and it's the one I want your prediction on. A woman carries a nasty mitochondrial disorder. The clinic does a spindle transfer: they pull the nucleus out of her egg and put it into a donor egg that has had its own nucleus removed, then fertilize that with her partner's sperm. A child is born. Stipulate that the nuclear DNA is exactly what it would have been had her own egg simply been fertilized normally, base for base — the only difference is the mitochondria, which come from the donor, so a few dozen genes out of the whole hereditary package. The child is healthy either way.

Question: could that child have come from the mother's own intact egg instead? Is the ordinary, unassisted conception a way things could have gone for that very child, or would it have been a different child?

So — what does Priya say? Which rule is she actually running? I'm certain more than one candidate fits 1 through 8, so name the candidates, say what each implies about the spindle-transfer case, and then commit to a prediction with your reasons. Also tell me whether her record is even consistent, and if it isn't, which verdict she should give up to make it so. And what's the single further case you'd put in front of her that would settle which rule she's on? I was thinking something with a gradient, but say so if that's the wrong instinct.

One more thing: she's going to insist the mitochondria don't count because they aren't really part of "the egg" in the sense that matters. Does that move save her, or does it blow up 7?
