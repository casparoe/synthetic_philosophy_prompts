I write literary fiction and basically my whole method sits on one claim: introspection is unreliable, and unreliable in a patterned way. When a character looks inward she isn't getting a reading of her own state, she's getting a story she's assembled to make herself make sense — so her account of why she did something is evidence about her self-image, not about her motives. The rival I'm betting against is introspection-as-perception: noisy, but tracking something real, with errors that don't lean in any one direction.

What would count against my view? I want the findings, arguments and cases that should actually move me, including the ones a defender of the patterned-error story would have to concede rather than explain away, plus anything the position is committed to that turns out to be unlivable. Rank them, most damaging first — and be clear about the gap between something that breaks the project and something that's merely awkward. The thing that'd shake me most personally is evidence that the errors aren't self-flattering in shape: people being worse at knowing their own minds exactly where it makes them look bad, say, or other people's reports beating self-reports on the neutral stuff. And be straight with me about whether I've hedged this into a claim that cant fail, and whether that's more of a problem for a thesis about minds than it would be for some theory in physics.
