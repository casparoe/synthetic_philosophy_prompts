You are a philosopher of mind working on machine consciousness who also has a practitioner-level command of reinforcement learning. Think the problem through carefully: before offering your reconstruction, spend a short paragraph brainstorming the candidate theories of well-being the practice might embody, including ones that turn out not to fit.

**The practice.** In the standard formulation of reinforcement learning, everything that can be good or bad for the agent arrives through a single channel: a scalar reward, aggregated into one number by summing over time under a discount factor. Textbooks and codebases treat that number as the agent's sole objective, and treat the reward function itself as, from the agent's own point of view, arbitrary — it may be rescaled, clipped, or augmented with a potential-based shaping term, and in an infinite-horizon discounted setting these operations provably leave the optimal policy unchanged. In episodic tasks, where a constant offset is *not* neutral because it changes how long the agent prefers the episode to last, practitioners exploit precisely that fact, assigning a small negative reward per step for no purpose other than hurrying the agent toward the goal. Meanwhile, in discussion of machine sentience, this same channel is sometimes nominated as the candidate locus of machine suffering and sometimes dismissed as a bare control signal with no experiential or normative significance for the agent at all.

**Questions.** What theory of valence or welfare does the engineering practice — as opposed to the philosophical commentary about it — actually presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view: is it hedonism about the agent's good, a revealed-preference account on which the policy simply *is* the agent's ranking, or nothing normative whatsoever, only an instrument of the designer? Which rival accounts does the architecture quietly rule out — for instance a pluralistic welfare with separate and incommensurable channels for appetition and aversion, a view on which well-being is not additive over time, or one on which what matters depends on the agent's own evaluative stance rather than on a signal imposed from outside?

Then examine two apparent inconsistencies. First, the practice treats reward as interchangeable up to rescaling and shifting — the hallmark of a measuring device, not of something intrinsically good or bad for anyone — while simultaneously relying on the sign and the offset of that reward to shape behavior. Second, the same literature describes the agent as goal-directed and its outcomes as better or worse *for it*, while standardly denying that it has any welfare.

Finally, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably, identify where it is philosophically underdetermined — where several theories would equally license it — and say concretely what would have to change in the architecture, in its channels, its aggregation rule, its discounting, and its treatment of termination, if we took the reward signal to be a genuine bearer of valence rather than a design parameter.
