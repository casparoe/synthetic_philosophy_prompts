My thesis is that masking is not a distinct phenomenon from finking: masks are covert finks, and the taxonomy of failure modes should be replaced by a single account of defeasible sufficiency. The argument I will sketch in the talk runs roughly as follows. First, a disposition's causal basis is not merely a contingent accompaniment of the disposition but a constituent of it; at minimum, no basis at a time, no disposition at that time. Second, in any purported mask case the interfering condition either removes or neutralizes that basis when the stimulus occurs, in which case the object simply stops having the disposition then — which is exactly the fink's structure — or else it leaves the basis intact and blocks the manifestation downstream. Third, the second branch is not actually available: any downstream block is a change in the total state of the object or its immediate environment that removes an item from the set of conditions which, together with the basis, are jointly sufficient for the manifestation, and removing an item from the sufficiency set is what an antidote does to the basis and what a fink does to the disposition. Fourth, the line between "the basis" and "a background condition" is not principled: it is drawn by which removed item we happened to name inside the dispositional ascription, so the fink/mask distinction tracks our choice of description rather than a distinction in the world. The upshot I want to defend is that there is one phenomenon — the defeasibility of the sufficiency set — and the interesting question is what fixes that set: a normality ordering, the object's kind, or the context of ascription. I already expect to be pressed on the wrapped-glass case, on whether my sufficiency-set talk is simply Lewis's reformed analysis with the second-order property renamed, and on whether my account is trivially circular, since if a fink is defined by the loss of a disposition and I have defined loss of disposition as removal from the sufficiency set, then everything becomes a fink by stipulation. I want the attacks I have not already thought of at least as much as I want sharper versions of those three.
