Four months into not sleeping because of the baby, and last week I found an email I'd sent my advisor at 3am saying the proof in my third chapter goes through — confident as anything, no memory of writing it, and it doesn't go through. What rattled me wasn't the error, it's that I now can't tell which of my confident judgments were made by the wrecked version of me. So I wrote down my gut verdicts and I want to know what principle they add up to, if any.

1. What the 3am email asserts: don't believe it, even though I can't find a flaw in the reasoning it lays out, and even though past-me clearly found it compelling at the time.
2. I re-read that same argument at 2am last night and it still seemed airtight, and I felt mildly reassured by that, which I know is dumb — but I also don't feel *undermined* by having checked it while tired. Tired re-checking seems not to count in either direction.
3. My advisor says the proof fails: I lose a lot of confidence. My three labmates all say it goes through: I feel nothing whatsoever. Structurally that's the same kind of news and I treat the two completely differently.
4. When my advisor actually walks me through where the gap is and I can see it myself, I don't settle at half — I go all the way to disbelieving. Once I can see the error the disagreement stops mattering.
5. Finding out I'd had two glasses of wine the night I originally convinced myself the argument was sound: that feels worse than finding out I'm fried right now. Past impairment seems to kill the belief outright; present impairment only makes me hold off.
6. I still believe my sister's version of what our dad said in his last week, and I freely admit I cant check it and everyone else remembers it differently — but I don't feel like I'm doing anything wrong. Whereas still believing my chapter is publishable while granting that the reviews don't support that feels like something is broken in me.
7. Believing my kid comes through this fine when the statistics are grim: fine, maybe even required. Believing a defendant is guilty on statistics shaped like that: not fine.

My tentative principle is that impairment evidence only discredits beliefs you formed while impaired, and does nothing to beliefs you're merely reviewing now — but 3 and 5 both strain it, and 6 strains it twice over.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or the most of them for the fewest moving parts? And what does it say about the one I keep circling: the realization that the proof fails also came to me at 4am, equally wrecked — so does the same discredit apply to the debunking, and if it does, where does the regress stop?

Answer as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no introduction and no wrap-up: items 1–4 each state one clause of the principle plus the case from my list that earns it, and item 5 says which of my verdicts the principle forces me to give up and how it rules on the 4am question.
