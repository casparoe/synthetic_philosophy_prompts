I'm building a side-effect penalty for a fleet of household robots — narrow tasks (tidy the kitchen, water the plants, haul boxes to the curb) but one real house they act in over and over, and the owner cant be asked to enumerate what matters to them. Before I read any more proposals I want the spec. Write the desiderata: what an adequate measure of "how much impact did this action have," usable as a penalty term, has to deliver. Give the concrete case behind each one — the urn of a parent's ashes that looks exactly like a decorative vase, the stack of old tax records vs the stack of newspapers, the thing the robot breaks but could repair an hour later, the deletion that's unrecoverable next to the sandwich that's eaten, the tidy-up that also rearranges a workspace someone was mid-thought in.

Two wrinkles I want the list to survive. One: the measure cant presuppose the reward function or the human's values beyond what's in the task description — that's the whole point. Two: the robot itself changes — it learns to fix things, gets a better gripper, gets a second unit — so whether a state is recoverable isnt a fixed property of the world but partly of the agent. Does the list stay coherent when the thing doing the measuring is also the thing changing?

Then tell me which single desideratum knocks out the most existing candidates, and whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — if so name it and say which member you'd give up, and why that one rather than another. A short scorecard of the main families at the end is fine (reachability-style penalties, attainable-utility ones, whatever else you'd add), but keep the weight on the criteria; I can do the scoring myself once I have the list. Mark clearly which items are non-negotiable and which are really just one camp's commitments dressed up as requirements.

And be straight with me on the framing: if you think a measure of side effects genuinely does need the value specification I'm trying to avoid, so that this list can't be written honestly, say that plainly up front rather than producing a polite list that quietly assumes it can.
