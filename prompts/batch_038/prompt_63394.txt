My understanding of heterophenomenology is that it's basically close reading with a scanner bolted on: you transcribe what the subject says they're experiencing, compare it against the neural data and the behaviour, and throw out whatever the brain doesn't corroborate. What survives is the cleaned-up phenomenal data. That's handy for me because my character does the same thing to herself — she keeps a notebook of her own inner life and marks which entries are backed up by her own recordings.
