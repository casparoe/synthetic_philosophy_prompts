I sit on a working group of a regional health authority that is drafting the "scope of responsibility" clause for a new integrated care organisation, and the group has split in a way I cannot adjudicate. The clause must say what the organisation is answerable for. One faction wants it to commit the organisation to housing instability, food insecurity, loneliness, and debt-related stress as matters within its remit, on the ground that these are health needs; when someone objects that this stretches the concept, they invoke the World Health Organization's founding definition, adopted in 1946: "Health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." The other faction, mostly clinicians, replies that this is a category error: that disease can be characterised in purely biological terms without reference to what anyone values, that health is a matter of the organism's functioning, and that a definition making health coextensive with well-being renders health something no one has and the boundaries of medicine infinite. Both sides treat that sentence as settling something. I do not think it settles anything until we know what it is claiming.

So I want the menu of precise claims that sentence could be making, not a survey of the literature and not a summary of the dispute. Please consider at least the following candidates, and add any I have overlooked: that it is a stipulative definition fixing the meaning of a technical term; that it states an aspirational ideal rather than a binary condition anyone actually occupies; that it is a thesis about the proper scope of health institutions' obligations rather than about the meaning of a word; that health is multidimensional — physical, mental, social — without being identical to well-being; that it does no more than deny that the absence of disease suffices for health; that it identifies health with well-being outright; and that "complete" functions as an intensifier rather than as a threshold condition. For each reading, tell me whether it is a platitude nobody could deny, a substantive and defensible thesis, or false or self-defeating — and in the self-defeating cases, exactly what goes wrong and at which step. Say also which of these readings you think my colleagues are actually reaching for when they quote the sentence, since I suspect it is not the one they would defend under questioning.

Then two further questions. First, which reading, if true, would actually license the conclusion that housing instability and loneliness fall inside the organisation's remit? My suspicion is that the readings on which the definition is most useful to that faction are not the readings on which it is most plausible; I want that either confirmed or corrected, with reasons rather than reassurance. Second, does the rest of the same document help disambiguate it — the constitution states WHO's objective as "the attainment by all peoples of the highest possible level of health," and I want to know whether pairing the definition with an objective phrased in terms of a highest attainable level pushes toward reading "complete" as an ideal rather than a threshold, and if so whether that defuses the clinicians' objection about unattainability and boundlessness or leaves it standing on some other footing.

Finally, tell me where you think our working group's disagreement actually lives: is it a disagreement about the extension of a word, about the values built into the concept of disease, or about the proper reach of an institution's obligations — and which of those three, if it were resolved, would make the other two stop mattering?

Please write all of this as continuous prose, the way you would write a letter: no headings, no bullet points or numbered lists, no bold or italic emphasis, no tables. Take the readings in order, give each its own paragraph, and identify each one in the sentence that opens its paragraph rather than by any label, abbreviation, or number. I want something I could circulate to the group as a memo without embarrassment.
