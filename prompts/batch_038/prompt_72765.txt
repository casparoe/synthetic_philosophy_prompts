At the close of a reading group session last term on the transitivity of comparative value, two colleagues of mine got into an argument I was unable to referee, and I should like a careful adjudication of it. The group had been working with a constructed spectrum: a sequence of one thousand possible lives for a single individual, L1 through L1000, in which L1 is a life of sustained achievement, deep attachment, and vivid appreciation of the world, each successive Li+1 involves a small but real diminishment, and L1000 is a life barely worth living. Everyone present accepted the endpoint judgment that L1 is better than L1000. The dispute concerned what to say about the individual steps.

The exchange went roughly as follows. Marguerite argued first; Owen replied.

(1) Marguerite: for each i, the difference between Li and Li+1 is too small for Li to be better than Li+1. Nothing in the case singles out one step as significant, so if any step is a betterness step, all of them are — and a thousand such steps would place L1000 above L1, which nobody accepts. Either betterness fails to be transitive, or the series contains no betterness steps at all.

(2) Owen: that is a false dilemma built on an equivocation. "Too small to tell apart" is doing perceptual work in the setup, and it has been silently promoted into the normative claim that Li is not better than Li+1. Indiscernibility is not equality. Separate the two, and the natural verdict is that every step is a genuine betterness step and transitivity is intact; your argument only seemed compelling because it borrowed an epistemic premise it was not entitled to.

(3) Marguerite: then tell us where the significant steps are. You cannot, and any line you draw will be arbitrary in precisely the way a line between bald and not-bald is arbitrary. Better to hold that betterness is only quasi-transitive: the large-scale comparison survives, the stepwise comparisons fail, and that is a coherent structure rather than a collapse.

(4) Owen: quasi-transitivity is not stable. If L1 is not better than L2, and not better than L3, and so on, the negative relation spreads in exactly the fashion you claim to reject; you cannot halt it short of contradicting the single judgment the whole room shared. You have relocated the sorites, not answered it.

(5) Marguerite: then not lines but clumps. There are ranges within which outcomes are on a par, and it is parthood within a clump that makes the stepwise comparisons fail without disturbing the endpoint comparison. The clumps have boundaries, but a vague boundary is not an arbitrary one.

(6) Owen: that is redescription rather than reply. Either the clumps are fixed by some weighting across dimensions of value — in which case you have reinstated the transitive comparison you denied — or they are unexplained primitives, in which case the view amounts to nothing beyond the assertion that certain steps do not count. And the self-torturer structure sits on your position: if no step downward is a worsening, each step is permitted, and a thousand permitted steps deliver us to L1000. You must either swallow that or concede that something transmits across the steps after all.

(7) Marguerite: I will swallow it. Permissibility need not be closed under concatenation, and it is your account, not mine, that owes an explanation of why a series of individually imperceptible losses amounts to a loss.

Please go through the exchange move by move rather than in the aggregate. For each of the seven moves, assign a rating — clearly right, defensible, weak, clearly mistaken — and justify the rating briefly, making explicit what the speaker is committed to at that point and whether the objection lands against that commitment or against a stronger thesis than the speaker actually holds. Two questions deserve separate treatment: whether the spreading objection in (4) is sound or whether it tacitly assumes the transitivity at issue; and whether (6)'s self-torturer point genuinely engages (7)'s reply about closure under concatenation, or whether the two are talking past one another. Conclude by saying which of them is more right overall, and whether there is a position the exchange never reached that would handle this spectrum better than either of the two on offer. I am interested in the reasoning, not in a catalogue of named views, though you may gesture at familiar positions where that aids clarity.
