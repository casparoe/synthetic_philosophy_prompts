I am preparing an assessment for my graduate seminar on the ethics of punishment, in which students must evaluate the Nordic custodial model on normative rather than empirical grounds, and I want a worked model of that exercise. Please assign each of the five features of a Halden-style regime listed below a score from 0 to 1 for the seriousness of the moral cost it imposes, calibrated so that 0 means no genuine cost and 1 means a cost that could outweigh the model's demonstrable benefits. By moral cost I mean specifically a loss in what the institution owes to prisoners, to victims, or to the political community — not a shortfall of hard treatment relative to some retributivist ledger, which is the point I want the students to get past.

The features: (i) the substitution of an administrative and therapeutic relationship for one of judgment, and what that does to the prisoner's standing as a responsible agent; (ii) the state's claim to have reformed the prisoner, which both takes credit for a change it did not cause and withholds from the prisoner the authorship of his own reform; (iii) the regime's dependence on an extensive welfare state outside the walls, so that transplanting its architecture without its social infrastructure yields a façade; (iv) the message the regime conveys to victims about the standing of the wrong done to them; (v) the possibility that rendering incarceration visibly humane lowers the political threshold for resorting to it at all.

Please structure your response in these sections, in this order. Scoring table (roughly 200 words): each feature with its score and a single sentence of justification. The two extremes (roughly 350 words): a paragraph defending your highest score and a paragraph defending your lowest, each anticipating the strongest counter-consideration rather than merely restating the case. Transferability (roughly 200 words): whether feature (iii) is a moral cost of the regime itself or only a caution about borrowing it, and how that distinction should move its score. Bottom line (roughly 100 words): the five features ordered from most to least serious cost, together with a judgment on whether any of them deserves to be struck from the list altogether as a political objection masquerading as a moral one.
