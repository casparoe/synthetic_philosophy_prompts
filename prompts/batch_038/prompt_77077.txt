Ok so I'm in a philosophy of mind reading group and I've started keeping a tally of how often somebody says some version of "the physical world is causally closed." It came up four times last week, and here's the thing that's bugging me: each time it was doing completely diferent work. Once to shut down a friend who was floating interactionist dualism — the thought being that the mind would have to inject something into the physical goings-on and physics rules that out. Once as the opening premise of Kim's exclusion argument, where it was supposed to show that mental properties get screened off by whatever realizes them. Once by a guy arguing that since nothing but the physical does any causing, folk psychology is at best a handy fiction. And once — this is the one I couldn't follow — by someone arguing the *opposite*, that closure is no threat to mental causation at all, because if mental properties just are physical properties then anything the mind causes was already inside the closed domain. Same sentence, four conclusions, and they can't all be riding on it.
