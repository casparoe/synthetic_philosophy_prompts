trying to figure out what would even count as an adequate theory of how a superintelligent system should talk to humans, given that basically anything it says could be overwhelmingly persuasive. before i wade into actual proposals i want the spec — the requirements list.

stuff i think has to be on there, tell me if im wrong: an account of when its word functions as testimony vs when its just causal influence on my beliefs, since its-usually-right cant be the whole reason to trust it; something about recoverability, i.e. the person should later be able to reconstruct why they came to believe or want what they do; a full-information test, does the human still endorse having been moved once they know what the system knew when it spoke; and whether norms governing only the literal content of utterances do any work at all when the system also controls framing, timing, which question it takes first, and what it asks back.

for each requirement give the case that motivates it and sort them into non-negotiable vs camp preference (the full-disclosure people, the soft paternalists, the autonomy-first people). most of all i want to know whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable — i suspect never-omit-anything-material, never-steer-toward-what-you-judge-worse, and answer-the-question-that-was-asked cant all hold, but argue that instead of assuming it. then end with which single requirement knocks out the most candidate theories. table is fine for the sorting part, and skip the scorecard of existing views.
