The idea. Everyone knows the puzzle about who gets to be in the demos: the usual answers are that all those affected by a decision should have a say, or all those subject to the law, or all those coerced by it. And there's the objection that the boundary can't be settled democratically, because deciding who decides already presupposes a people doing the deciding, and off you go into a regress. In my old job this had a very concrete shape. An aquifer doesn't care about county lines, and whether a given farmer counts as affected by a pumping permit depends entirely on what radius and what timescale you put in the model: at a 50-year horizon and a 5-km radius he's affected, at a 5-year horizon he isnt. So the affected set is less a fact you discover than a thing your parameters produce. My hunch is that the regress may not be vicious if you treat it as a fixed point problem instead: define the rule that maps a decision-procedure to the set it enfranchises, then ask whether re-applying the rule converges, whether the fixed point is unique, and whether it depends on where you started. Nobody seems to press that question, and I honestly can't tell if it's a real question or if I'm just dressing up a confusion in mathematical clothes — I'd want the proposal to say which.

Write it with: the question stated precisely enough that it could have a determinate answer; why the existing positions (affected, subjected, coerced) plus the regress objection leave it open rather than settling it; the method, month by month, including an honest take on how much formalism a philosophy committee will tolerate from someone whose maths is all self-taught hydrology; why this approach rather than the obvious alternatives (comparing real boundary disputes, or just arguing one principle is correct); what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a scope assessment, including whether I should cut it down. No bibliography and no citations — my supervisor knows the literature far better than I do, so please dont invent references, just name the positions by their common labels. And write it the way a philosphy proposal is supposed to read, since I've no idea what that is.
