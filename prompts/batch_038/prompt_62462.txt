I'm trying to work through Freud's argument in *The Future of an Illusion*, and I keep getting stuck on what looks to me like a transparent fallacy. Freud defines a religious illusion as a false belief produced by wish-fulfilment rather than by evidence — the doctrine is believed because humanity wants a protective father, not because anything speaks in its favour — and then he spends the book contending that religious doctrines are illusions in exactly that sense. But since he has built falsity into the definition, the whole case looks like nothing more than a genetic fallacy, an inference from why people hold a belief to whether it is true. How does he think he gets around that objection, or is this simply the point at which the argument collapses?
