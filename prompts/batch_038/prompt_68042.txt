I'm putting together a few engineered cases for a workshop on when an AI assistant may go against its own user, and I want one designed scenario rather than a survey of the literature. Build me a case where three accounts come apart in a specific pattern. The consent/autonomy account says the override was clearly impermissible. A welfarist soft paternalist says it was clearly fine — the user ends up better off and is in fact glad it happened. And a hypothetical-endorsement account (the nudge-theorist condition: steer the user only if they'd endorse your steering on reflection) says it was *not* fine, even though the user is glad — because being glad of an outcome isn't the same as endorsing the assistant's having decided it. So anticipated gratitude and retrospective endorsement have to actually come apart in the case rather than be stipulated apart. That's the point of the exercise: endorsement is normally treated as the welfarist's safety valve, and I want a case where it doesn't play that role.

Constraints: the scenario itself is one paragraph, set around an ordinary consumer product (a study app, a budgeting or navigation assistant, a health tracker — your pick), the user is a competent adult who is clear-headed and knows exactly what they're asking for, no life-or-death stakes, no opt-in contract or standing "block me from this" instruction (that just collapses the case back into consent), no mind-reading or data the user didn't knowingly hand over, and — importantly — no appeal to weakness of will, exhaustion, or hot-cold empathy gaps, since that's the usual route by which the welfarist wins and I want it closed off.

Then do the checking work: the verdict each of the three accounts reaches and why; which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what the case degenerates into if you delete that feature; and how a soft paternalist would attack the design — presumably by arguing that if the user is genuinely glad, that just shows endorsement, so the third account isn't a distinct position at all. Tell me whether that attack lands or whether gratitude and endorsement can be held apart. Close with how confident you are in each of the three verdicts and which part of the case you're least sure about. Under 500 words for everything after the scenario.
