So I help run an informal decision-theory reading group that meets in the back room of a board game cafe on Tuesday nights — mostly grad students, plus two software people who are considerably better at this than I am and visibly enjoy it. Last month we did the two Omega classics: the counterfactual mugging (fair coin flipped yesterday; heads, Omega asks you for $100; tails, Omega would have handed you $10,000, but only if it predicted you'd have handed over the $100 in the heads case — and you get told the coin came up heads) and the termite letter (the one saying it was sent to you if and only if exactly one of these is true: your house has termites, or you wire the sender $1,000). Predictably we went in circles, everybody reciting which camp pays in which case and nobody actually disagreeing about anything interesting. So for next week I made up a variant of my own, and I want to hand it out as a cold open with a dialogue attached, so people have to argue instead of reciting labels. I've got to get the handout to the print shop by Thursday, so if you could write the dialogue that'd be a lifesaver.

Here's the variant, and please keep every detail exactly as I've got it, because the whole thing depends on the details:

1. Yesterday Omega flipped a fair coin in secret. Heads: it released termite colonies into the crawlspaces of two houses on my street, mine and one other, $20,000 of damage each if left untreated. Tails: neither house is touched and neither of us ever hears a word about any of this.
2. Yesterday evening Omega ran a seperate high-fidelity simulation of each of our decision procedures, and then last night it made two arrangements: the other house's crawlspace was treated (colony dead within the month, Omega eats the cost) if and only if its simulation said I would wire $1,000 today; my crawlspace was treated if and only if its simulation said the other person would wire $1,000 today.
3. This morning we each got an identical letter laying all of this out, saying the coin came up heads, setting the wire deadline at 5pm, and saying explicitly that the money does not fund anything and does not cause or cancel any treatment — the arrangements were made last night and are already settled fact.
4. The letter adds: the pairing was made by drawing a name at random out of the county property records; your partner is not a copy of you and is not running anything like your decision procedure; there is no second round, Omega is not coming back, and nobody will ever find out what you did.
5. The two of us are standing in my driveway at 4:20 with the crawlspace hatch open, a flashlight, and our phones, and the other guy has already said flat out that he isn't wiring anything.

Write it as a dialogue between those two people. Call them Nadia and Ray — Nadia is the one leaning toward wiring the money, Ray is a committed two-boxer who thinks the whole setup is a trick played by evidence, and Ray is arguing that Nadia should refuse even though its his own house her $1,000 hangs on, which I want him to own outright rather than pretend isn't happening. Somewhere around 1,500 words, in American English throughout, spelling and idiom both. No citations, no bibliography, no name-dropping philosophers or papers, and no jargon standing in for an argument — half my group has never seen this literature. Both of them have to be genuinely good at this: no strawmen, nobody gets to deliver an unchallenged speech, and every reply has to actually take up what the other one just said. I specifically want them to reach (a) whether the usual "pay, because you want to be the kind of agent who pays" line keeps any grip when whoever benefits is a randomly picked stranger instead of your own counterfactual self, (b) Ray's rejoinder that if Nadia pays anyway she's just doing charity with extra steps and the Omega story is doing no work at all, and (c) whether this case is secretly just the termite letter with a coin flip bolted onto the front. Don't let either of them convert and don't let them shake hands on a compromise — end with each one saying what the other would have to concede in order to change their mind.
