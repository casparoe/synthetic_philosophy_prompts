For years I've been circling Escher's Drawing Hands and the idea Hofstadter took from it: that every tangled hierarchy has some level the tangle never reaches, a vantage from which the loop stops being paradoxical. I have an argument of my own that I don't fully trust. When I test whether my standards for judging arguments are any good, the testing is done by those same standards, so the test settles nothing; and stepping up a level to audit the auditor reproduces the difficulty exactly. Whatever finally halts the chain would have to be a standpoint I cannot occupy — which, from where I sit, is hard to tell apart from there being no such standpoint at all.

Give me a number: your credence that this inviolate-level claim is true, taken to mean that no system capable of representing and evaluating its own representational states is inescapably tangled, so that for any such system some part of its operation lies outside the reach of the loop. Then decompose it. For each of the following I want a rough probability, a sentence or two of justification, and then the way they combine into your total: whether incompleteness results about arithmetic genuinely license a claim this general; whether level means something precise enough here that the claim is true or false rather than merely evocative; whether an unending regress and a closed loop are two different situations or one situation described from inside and from outside; and whether a person auditing her own standards belongs in the same category as a formal system to begin with. End by naming the term your total is most sensitive to and what would shift it.
