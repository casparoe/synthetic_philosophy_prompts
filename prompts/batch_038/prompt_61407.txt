I'm building the reading-day session of a grad seminar on the paradox of deontology and I want my students to sort the standard escape routes into tiers, but first I need my own tier list to argue with. So: rank these responses by how well they actually solve the paradox rather than just restating the intuition behind agent-centred restrictions more forcefully.

My test is deliberately narrow. Top tier only if a response does both of these things: it blocks the move from "restrictions sometimes forbid you to bring about the best outcome you could" to "restrictions are irrational," and it does that without letting minimising structure back in through the back door — i.e. without making the content of the restriction depend on how many violations are lying around out there to be prevented. If a response mainly relocates the problem onto a neighbouring concept (rights, doing versus allowing, intention), that's bottom tier no matter how popular it is.

Second thing I want: for each item, say which version of the paradox it's even addressed to. I've been splitting the paradox into a structural version (restrictions are incompatible with the idea that practical rationality consists in ranking outcomes at all) and a comparative version (restrictions can be so costly that refusing to violate one looks worse than violating it). I'm fairly sure half of these responses answer one and not the other, and my students never notice which.

The list: threshhold deontology; the goal-rights-system / agent-relative-value move; double effect; killing versus letting die; rights forfeiture; the line that restrictions express a form of practical rationality that isn't outcome-directed at all; contractualist grounding of restrictions; Scheffler-style agent-centred prerogatives substituted in for restrictions.

Then three follow-ups. Which single one would you stake the second half of the session on, and why that one over your runner-up. What's the sharpest objection to your top pick — the one I should put on the board before they find it themselves. And is anything on my list misfiled, i.e. something there that isn't really a response to this paradox at all?
