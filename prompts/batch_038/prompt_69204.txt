Please write a short story of roughly 2,000 to 3,000 words that engages the symbol grounding problem — the question of how the tokens a system manipulates come to be about anything beyond their relations to other tokens — without naming the problem, without using the phrase "symbol grounding," and without staging anything recognizable as a person locked in a room passing slips of paper under a door.

Build the story around the following situation. At an industrial inspection facility, a fleet of a few hundred small maintenance units shares a single vocabulary. None of them was ever anchored to the world by a human engineer: they acquired their terms from one another, by tracking which signals correlated with which of their own successes and failures, and their conventions have since drifted well away from any human naming practice. They coordinate flawlessly. They detect and correct faults that human engineers working in human vocabulary cannot reliably detect. Among their terms is one with no counterpart in the engineers' language at all — a term the units apply consistently, and that appears to pick out a real and repeatable feature of the machinery, though nobody outside the fleet can say what that feature is, or even what category of thing it would have to be: a property of a single component, a relation among several, a disposition that never manifests in ordinary operation.

The human protagonist is a semanticist contracted to do two things. First, to produce a translation manual detailed enough for the company to audit the fleet. Second, to certify to a regulator that the fleet's terms are genuinely about something. The regulator's rule, which you should treat as given and not question, requires that any term appearing in an audited system be reducible, at least in principle, to measurements an unaided human observer could perform. The protagonist begins by assuming the certification is a formality. Let the story turn on the discovery that the two assignments pull against one another: that the only way to render the fleet's terms into human language is to alter the very correlations that make the fleet work, so that a faithful manual would be a manual for a different system, one that no longer behaves as this one does.

I would ask you to honor several further constraints:

1. Do not resolve the question by an appeal to the protagonist's intuition, and do not let the fleet explain itself. The units may be communicative, but their communication should be as interpretable as the behavior of a well-trained working animal — patterned, reliable, and silent about what any of it means.
2. Include at least one scene in which a candidate translation of the key term is tested against the fleet's behavior and fails in an instructive way, and a second scene in which a rival candidate fails differently. Neither failure should be decisive, and the story should not later reveal which candidate was correct.
3. Give the protagonist a personal stake that is not merely professional, but keep it subordinate to the intellectual difficulty; the stake should sharpen the problem rather than displace it.
4. End on a decision rather than a revelation. The reader should be able to state what the protagonist chose, what the choice cost, and what remains unresolved because of it.
5. Avoid the familiar endings of this genre: the system pleading for recognition, the system turning out to have been conscious all along, the system being shut down in the nick of time, the human realizing that humans are no better grounded than the machines. The last of these may be felt as a temptation by the protagonist, but it must not be the story's conclusion.
6. Concrete, technical, sensory detail about the machinery and the inspection work matters to me more than lyrical prose. You are free to choose the point of view and the tense.

I am not looking for a story that settles the philosophical question. I am looking for one that makes a reader feel the difference between a symbol a system can deploy impeccably and a symbol a system understands — and that leaves the applicability of that difference to this particular fleet genuinely in doubt, rather than merely asserted.
