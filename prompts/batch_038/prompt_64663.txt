I have spent something like fifteen years in rationalist comment sections, long enough to know that the consciousness thread reboots every eighteen months with the same four posts, and long enough to stop trying to win it. This spring I tried a demonstration instead. I invented a rulebook, written in plain English, for a made-up positional script of twelve marks; I put one member of the group in a closet with a stack of cards and a slot cut in the door; the rest of us fed sequences in and read his replies back. For the first hour he was painfully slow and consulted the book for everything. By the third hour he had stopped consulting it for the common patterns. By the fourth he told us, and he was visibly shaken, that he could tell a query from a statement before he had decoded it: not what it asked, only that this cluster was asking and that one was not. Half the room said that was the beginning of understanding. The other half said it was precisely what a lookup table does as it gets fast, and that nothing at all had changed. We never settled it.

I am writing up the evening and I want a sestina to open the post, because I think the form is the argument: six words returning in a fixed permutation, never accumulating into anything they were not before.

Requirements. Thirty-nine lines: six sestets and a three-line envoi. The six end-words, in first-stanza order, are SHAPE, RULE, SLOT, MEAN, FLUENT, OUTSIDE, and they rotate in the standard pattern, so the sestets end: SHAPE RULE SLOT MEAN FLUENT OUTSIDE; OUTSIDE SHAPE FLUENT RULE MEAN SLOT; SLOT OUTSIDE MEAN SHAPE RULE FLUENT; FLUENT SLOT RULE OUTSIDE SHAPE MEAN; MEAN FLUENT SHAPE SLOT OUTSIDE RULE; RULE MEAN OUTSIDE FLUENT SLOT SHAPE. The envoi must carry all six, two to a line, one of the pair mid-line and the other at the line's end, in the traditional order, so that the poem's last word is SHAPE: it should close on a mark, not on a message. Do not bend the rotation. If a word will not sit at the end of a line without contorting the syntax, the line is wrong, not the word.

Content. The poem is about the man in the room, not about our card game, though the fourth hour belongs in the fifth or sixth sestet: the moment he begins to anticipate the marks from their form alone. The envoi should be the systems reply granted in full, with the rules memorized, the answers internalized, the room dissolved and the man standing outdoors, and it should let both readings land at once without choosing between them. Two of the end-words must shift sense across their six appearances. MEAN should move between signification and the tally, the frequency, the average case he has quietly computed. OUTSIDE should move from beyond the door, to beside him rather than in him, which is where the systems reply locates the understanding, to simply outdoors.

Accuracy. The conclusion is not that machines cannot think, and the poem must not imply it; the claim is the narrower one, that manipulating uninterpreted marks is not sufficient for understanding them. The rulebook is in his own language and the marks mean nothing to him. He is not stupid, and the failure is not a failure of effort or speed, so the poem should make speed and internalization irrelevant rather than triumphant. Do not install a smaller reader inside his head to do the work.

Craft. Unrhymed, lines of roughly comparable length, concrete nouns and a hand in them. It has to be a good poem for someone who has never heard the argument, and it has to be right for someone who has argued about it for twenty years. No slogans, no bumper stickers; the thread has enough of those already.
