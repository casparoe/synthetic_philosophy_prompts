Schopenhauer holds that music stands apart from the other arts: painting and poetry copy the Platonic Ideas, whereas music copies the will itself, and that is why it speaks a universal language and strikes us far more immediately than anything representational does. Yet he also gives music an internal architecture that reproduces precisely the ladder of grades of objectification he says music bypasses — the bass corresponding to crude inorganic mass, the middle voices to the intermediate grades of plant and animal life, melody to the highest grade, the striving and intelligence of the human being. I cannot see what is left of the claim that music skips the Ideas if music turns out to encode the whole hierarchy of them in sound.

So: is the bass-to-melody mapping a covert reintroduction of the Ideas, or is music related to those grades by isomorphism rather than depiction — a structural analogy that does not make music about anything? What work is the contrast between a copy of the will and a copy of an Idea actually doing for him, and what would his view lose if the two claims are irreconcilable? And a connected difficulty I would like handled in the same answer: aesthetic experience is supposed to be will-less, the subject ceasing to strive and merely contemplating, but music grips us through the affects more directly than any other art. Does that survive contact with his account of disinterested contemplation, or does music end up being the one art that re-engages the very thing contemplation suspends?

Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion: (1) whether the pitch-hierarchy mapping reintroduces the Ideas or is compatible with bypassing them, (2) what turns on the will-versus-Ideas contrast, (3) what Schopenhauer would have to give up if that contrast collapses, (4) whether the affective power of music is compatible with will-less contemplation, and (5) which of the two difficulties is the more damaging to his account of music.
