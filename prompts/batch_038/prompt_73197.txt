For nine years I have run a small company importing hand-thrown ceramics from a studio outside Seoul. I have no Korean and the studio's owner has almost no English, so everything between us — purchase orders, glaze specifications, packing instructions, the occasional disagreement about a cracked crate — passes through a free translation app. I never gave the arrangement much thought, because the shipments arrived, the invoices matched, and in all that time only one dispute arose, which we settled amicably. Then a friend who runs a bookkeeping practice remarked that she had once been burned by software that reported confidently on its own accuracy, and I decided to satisfy myself that my system was sound.

Over the past year I have kept a log. Whenever a message matters, I take the app's English rendering of the studio's Korean, run it back through the same app to be re-translated into Korean, and check that what comes out matches what the studio originally sent. I have also taken to pasting entire exchanges into the app and asking it to flag any place where the meaning may have drifted; in almost every case it reports none. The log now runs past two hundred entries with a success rate near ninety-five percent, and the handful of failures I have written off as stylistic variation, since what came back was in each case recognizably the same content. It seems to me that this is a substantial body of evidence, gathered systematically, and that I am entitled on the strength of it to conclude that the app is reliable.

My business partner is not persuaded. He notes that the studio's owner, whom I have met twice and who is by all accounts scrupulous, would gladly review a sample of messages for us, and that a freelance translator would do the same for a modest fee. I have declined both offers: after two hundred checks, one more small sample could not plausibly move the needle, and if the app were systematically distorting our correspondence, my checks would surely have exposed it by now. My partner calls this going around in circles, though I cannot locate the circle — each individual check, as I perform it, seems to tell me something true about a particular message, and I have simply been adding those truths up. I would rather have a real assessment than a hedge. Is my reasoning defective, and if so exactly where does it give way, or is there something in it that my partner is overlooking? And what should I do next in practice, given that I do not want to pay for a translation of every routine message, but do not want to learn about a mistranslation from a customs officer or a lawyer either?
