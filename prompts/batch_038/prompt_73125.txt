Three weeks from now I'm giving a work-in-progress talk at our department's philosophy-of-language session, and I want to walk in already knowing what's coming at me. The room is a specific mix: mostly Lewis-flavored Humean metaphysicians who think reference is settled by eligibility plus fit, two or three people who think the realism/anti-realism debate got buried somewhere in the nineties and are only there for the cookies, and one actual working set theorist who comes to philosophy talks specifically to explain that philosophers of set theory are confused. He's already told me after a previous talk that anything interesting in this area needs transitive models, and that consistency of ZFC doesn't even get you a countable transitive model, so I'd better be careful what I claim to be deriving from Skolem's paradox.

Here's the thesis I'm defending, which is not the usual one. I'm not arguing that Skolem's paradox is a genuine puzzle — I grant the standard line that "countable" isn't absolute and that nothing contradictory happens. I'm arguing that the paradox, plus the Löwenheim-Skolem machinery behind it, still does real work against the metaphysical realist, and specifically that the "just more theory" reply to Putnam's model-theoretic argument (the Models and Reality one) fails. My own contribution is a principle I've been calling reabsorption: any constraint the realist adds in order to pin down reference — causal constraints, theoretical-role constraints, idealization-under-improvement constraints — has to be stated in the object language, and whatever is stated in the object language is itself available for the kind of reinterpretation it was supposed to block. The Lewisian answer assumes the constraint can be laid down from somewhere outside; my claim is that the metaphysical realist, who insists reference is a non-semantic relation between words and mind-independent items, has no such outside, and so the constraint just gets folded back into the theory that is itself up for reinterpretation. My officemate's version of the objection, which I could not answer well at the time: he says reabsorption is either a triviality about nonstandard and ill-founded models wearing a metaphysical costume, or else it secretly assumes semantic anti-realism and therefore begs the question against the realist from the first premise.
