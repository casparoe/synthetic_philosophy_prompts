The write-up I'm bringing to next week's epistemology reading group defends the following package about certainty, and I want an audit of it before I hand it round — not a survey of how the field divides.

1. Hingeness is a role rather than a content: p is a hinge for S at a time just in case S's inquiries presuppose p, and any attempt to doubt p would dismantle the setting in which S's evidence counts as evidence instead of opening a live alternative.
2. Hinges are neither justified nor unjustified. They are arational, and treating them as objects of knowledge is a confusion: justification has its sense only against a background that is not itself justified.
3. Even so, S is entitled to accept S's hinges — a default, non-evidential warrant, but a genuine epistemic standing, and one that can in principle be forfeited.
4. What counts as evidence for or against p is fixed by the hinges. Evidence bears on propositions inside the framework, never on the framework itself.
5. Some hinges are given up anyway, and when a villager stops holding that nobody has ever landed on the moon, that is a rational achievement rather than a conversion.
6. The most general hinges — that there is an external world, that the earth existed long before I was born — cannot be given up at all; there is no standpoint from which to weigh them.
7. Moore's announcement, hands raised, that here is one hand and here is another, made in his study to no one in particular, is not false, merely idle.
8. The same words do real work elsewhere: a parent saying "I know the world was here long before you were born" to a child who has just asked is neither idle nor confused.

A colleague insists the package is either incoherent or just the standard picture with one label relabelled, and I cannot tell which. Go through it and commit to a verdict: identify which pairs are in genuine tension and rank those tensions by severity, say which only look conflicted and why the appearance misleads, give for each genuine tension the cheapest repair and what that repair costs me, and end with the single revision that would resolve the most. Then tell me which of my colleague's two descriptions is correct, and why.
