I am drafting a position paper whose subject is not the empirical findings about cultural variation in philosophical intuitions, but the standard such findings must meet before they can be used at all. Before I write it, I want the specification itself: the list of requirements that any account of cross-cultural variation in intuitive philosophical judgments has to satisfy in order to count as adequate, with the case or consideration motivating each requirement, and an explicit marking of which are non-negotiable and which are in truth the local commitments of one camp in the debate.

So that "adequate" is not left floating, here is the job the account has to be good enough for. It must let a philosopher decide, for a given piece of evidence and a given argument, whether a demonstrated difference in intuitive responses between two populations undercuts the philosophical claim the intuition was offered to support. I therefore want requirements on an account that licenses or blocks that inference, not requirements on a description of the variation for its own sake, and not requirements on a general sociology of concepts.

Three things I do not want. I do not want a literature review, citations, or a reading list; where you invoke a known result, describe it in prose so that I can check it against my own memory of it. I do not want you to take a side on whether intuitions are evidence, except insofar as some requirement you propose already commits you to a side, in which case I want that flagged. And I do not want requirements stated as slogans: each must come with the case that motivates it and with a statement of what would show that a given account violates it.

Here is my current list of candidate pressures, deliberately over-inclusive and unordered. Part of the work I want done on it is telling me which entries are genuine desiderata and which smuggle in a position.

1. Level. A reported difference in verdicts may be a difference in what the translated vignette asserted, a difference in the concept deployed, or only a difference in the willingness to assent to a sentence in a survey format. An account must say which of these its evidence bears on, and how the levels are told apart in practice. Motivating case: if the word used to render "knows" in the target language is not factive, then a lower rate of knowledge attribution in Gettier-style cases measures the translation rather than the epistemology.

2. Instrument robustness. A difference that survives paraphrase, reordering, and a change of elicitation format is a different object from a difference produced by one instrument in one administration, and the account must say which of the two it is claiming to explain.

3. Sample matching. The contrast is only as good as the match on everything that is not culture. When the sample intended to be non-Western is itself composed of university students in a large city, and the comparison sample is composed of students who have taken a philosophy course, the measured gap may reflect schooling, literacy, or familiarity with abstract hypotheticals rather than a shared way of thinking.

4. Expertise. If an account allows that philosophical training can explain away variation, it must say what the expertise consists in and why it should be expected to track the truth of the matter rather than to produce convergence within a profession. An appeal to expertise that cannot be distinguished from an appeal to in-group agreement does no work.

5. Self-application. An account concluding that intuitive judgments are largely local artifacts owes an explanation of why the intuitions supporting that account — about evidence, about what counts as the same case being presented twice, about inference — are not equally local. It must either be stable under application to itself or state which of its own premises it is prepared to surrender.

6. Charity. Registering a disagreement at all requires assuming a great deal of shared conceptual scheme. An account that posits wholesale conceptual difference threatens to dissolve the disagreement it was invoked to explain, leaving two populations talking past one another rather than differing, and must say how much shared structure it is assuming.

7. Mechanism. Culture causes intuitions is not an explanation. The account must identify the class of mechanism it is proposing — linguistic, pedagogical, religious, ecological, demographic — and say what would discriminate it from the alternatives.

8. Sensitivity to convergence. If the difference is deep conceptual difference, rapid convergence under schooling, migration, or sustained contact is surprising; if it is a performance or framing effect, convergence is expected. An account should issue a prediction about what happens to the gap within a generation and should be wrong about something if that prediction fails.

9. Indexing to argumentative role. Variation bears differently on an argument depending on the work the intuition was doing: as a premise, as a conceivability or possibility claim, as a coherence constraint across a family of cases, or as an intuition pump doing no real work. The account must say whether the same measured variation is equally damaging in each role, and if not, why.

10. Symmetry. The account must state the conditions under which it would take the other population's intuitions to be the better ones. An account that can only ever vindicate the intuitions of the population doing the theorizing is not an account of variation but a defense of home advantage.

What I want back:

- The list, sorted. For each entry, whether it is a genuine requirement on an account, a prejudice of one camp, or a requirement properly addressed to experimental design rather than to the account itself — and, for the genuine ones, the strongest case behind it plus what would show an account failing it.
- Any requirement I have omitted, especially ones that would come from the metaphysics or epistemology of the philosophical claims at issue rather than from methodology.
- Which subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied. My suspicion is that expertise, symmetry, and self-application pull against one another, since an account that lets training resolve variation must explain why the trained population's convergence is the target rather than one more local artifact. Tell me whether that is the real tension or whether it lies elsewhere; if the list is itself the result, say so plainly.
- Which single requirement eliminates the most accounts currently on offer.
- Finally, and briefly, how the account you would yourself endorse scores against your list. If none survives it, say which requirement you would drop to save one, and what that concession costs.

A table is fine for the sorting; I want prose for the tension analysis and the scoring. Be as long as the material requires, but not longer.
