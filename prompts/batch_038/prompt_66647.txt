Our department's graduate workshop wraps up with dinner tomorrow and I've been volunteered to open with a joke. My area is the metaphysics of composition, so I'd like one about the special composition question. What I'm after is a joke that lands with people who actually work on this, which I take to mean the punchline has to turn on the content of the rival answers rather than on a philosopher-adjacent pun. Two directions seem promising to me, though you should pick whichever you can execute best: the fact that a nihilist and a universalist agree about every arrangement of simples in the room — and hence about everything anyone could observe or measure — while disagreeing about whether anything is there at all; or the fact that the most-discussed restricted answer makes composition coincide with life, so that the only composite objects are organisms and my beer glass is a fiction. A "philosophers deny that tables exist" gag is precisely what I don't want, since it would work just as well no matter what anyone in the room believed. Commit to a single joke, setup and punchline, rather than giving me a menu to choose from, and resist the urge to gloss it afterward. Then add one sentence telling me where the joke takes liberties with the views it uses, if it does.
