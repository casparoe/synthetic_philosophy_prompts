I'm a performance/infra engineer with zero philosophy training, but next Saturday I'm giving a 20-minute talk at an animal welfare research workshop. The room is mostly comparative cognition grad students and a couple of neuroscientists, two philosophers of mind who work on consciousness and are allergic to neural "proxy" measures, and a handful of people from farmed-animal advocacy groups who think this whole debate is a distraction from pigs and chickens.

My pitch, which I basically arrived at from a systems angle: people argue about moral weight scaling with brain size, usually via neuron counts, but size is the wrong axis — throughput is. Smaller animals tend to have much higher temporal resolution (the flicker-fusion stuff, "small animals live in slow motion"), so if what matters morally is how many moments of experience get processed per unit of wall-clock time rather than how much neural hardware is running, a bee or a small fish could rack up moral weight at a rate comparable to a cow, and the usual brain-size ranking partly flips. I then integrate over lifespan, which pushes the other way for short-lived animals, and I want to land on something a funder could actually put in a spreadsheet.
