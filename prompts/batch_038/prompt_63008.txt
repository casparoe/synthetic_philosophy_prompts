i'm in a phil of science seminar that did three weeks on Hume and then, as a curveball, two sessions on the no-free-lunch theorems in search and learning — about half the room does ML, half has never seen the theorem. for the last meeting each of us reads something aloud and i want to read a sestina. can you write it?

the six end-words have to rotate the way a sestina actually rotates them (and the envoi gathers all six), because the rotation *is* the argument i want to make: the same words coming back in a new order, no stanza getting any further than the last one, which is what the theorem says when you average over every rearrangement of the payoff, and also what Hume says about piling up instances. the envoi should be where the trick comes apart, since that's the one place you're allowed to stop averaging and keep the arrangement you actually observed.

it has to be right, not just evocative: whoever knows the result should be able to tell it's a claim about a uniform average over all the possible objective functions taken before you've seen any data, and not a claim that benchmarking is pointless or that all methods tie in practice. whoever doesn't know it should just get a poem about never quite learning anything from experience. keep the words concrete, no jargon dumps, and don't name-drop anybody — roughly 39 lines plus the envoi.
