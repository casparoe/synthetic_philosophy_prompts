So I'm sitting in on a consciousness seminar this term and this week is colour phi, and I've read the material twice and I can recite the setup back, but I don't think I actually understand the argument. Setup as I've got it: two dots flashed in quick succession, one at the top of the screen in (say) blue and one at the bottom in red, at the right spacing and timing. Subjects don't report two flashes — they report a single dot travelling down and changing colour abruptly somewhere around the middle of the path. Which is weird, because at the moment when that mid-path colour change would have to be experienced, the second dot hasn't been flashed yet, so nothing in the world or in the eye could have told the visual system the colour was going to change. I gather Kolers and von Grünau ran the original experiments partly because Goodman asked what a colour change would do to the phi phenomenon, which is a fun piece of triva but I don't know whether it matters.

Anyway. The two standard responses are the Orwellian and the Stalinesque ones. On the Orwellian picture the subject really does experience the blue dot and then, belatedly, the red one, and a memory of smooth-colour-change-in-motion is written in afterwards, overwriting the veridical trace. On the Stalinesque picture there's a delay, and by the time anything gets presented to the subject the two flashes have already been reconciled into one moving-and-changing event, so the memory is honest and it's the experience that's late. And the punchline everyone repeats is that the two are empirically indistinguishable — same neural story, same reports, the theorists only disagree about whether a given stage counts as pre- or post-experiential — so there's no fact of the matter, and hence no cartesian theatre to look for. I'd like you to compare these two properly rather than just restate them. What has to be held fixed between them for the comparison to bite, and what is the single thing they genuinely disagree about? Is the indistinguishability claim a claim about brains (no experiment could ever tell them apart), about the concept of experience (the two labels don't pick out anything distinct), or about reports (the subject's evidence is exhausted by what they can say)? Those feel like very different claims with very different consequences, and I suspect people slide between them. Also: is "no fact of the matter" doing any explanatory work here, or is it a refusal dressed up as a result? Someone in my group argued the whole puzzle is fake anyway — that whatever the mechanism, the internal states produced by the two-flash sequence just end up matching the states a genuinely moving-and-changing stimulus would produce, so there was never a premature colour change to explain — and I couldn't tell whether that's a real third rival or a way of changing the subject.

Here's the thing I actually want your help with, though. I've been toying with a third option of my own and I can't tell whether its any good. Call it the split-content view: the colour-phi experience isn't one item happening at one time, it's a bundle whose components get fixed at different times by different processes — roughly, the motion content is committed early, on the basis of the first flash plus an extrapolation, while the colour-change content isn't fixed until the second flash lands, and there is no moment at which the whole bundle exists as a single unified experience. Reports then sample whatever fragments happen to be available, which is why subjects say they saw the change halfway along. My worry is that this is just Stalinesque revision applied component by component, and that "there's no moment at which the whole thing exists" is a way of denying the cartesian theatre while keeping one, only smeared out over a few hundred milliseconds. So: is the split-content view a genuine third option, a repackaging of one of the two I started with, or a confused fourth thing? And does it predict anything the others don't — e.g. could you dissociate the components, getting subjects who report smooth motion but a jump in colour, or a colour change with no motion, and would that actually tell you anything about timing rather than just about which questions you asked? I want a verdict on which account does the most work and where mine would have to be fixed up or thrown out. No reading list please, I'm on a plane and just want the reasoning.
