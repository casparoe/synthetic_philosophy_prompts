I write a weekly column on minds and brains for a general audience, and next week's is about the colour-phi trick: two spots flashed a fraction of a second apart in different places, and people report a single spot sliding across and changing colour halfway — which it cant possibly have known to do. My editor wants the piece to open with a poem instead of another lab-scene anecdote, so can you do a pantoum, five stanzas of four lines?

The constraint I actually care about: the recurring line where the colour turns should land *after* the arrival line on its first pass and *before* it on the second, so the poem itself can't tell you whether the change was already written into the story or spliced in afterwards. One refrain pair is the red spot's report, the other the green's.

Don't name Stalin or Orwell anywhere in it, and don't say "Cartesian theatre" — a reader who's followed the debate should be able to point at which stanza is the delayed verdict and which is the rewritten memory without being told. And the close has to land the real point: there's no fact about which of the two happened, not because we measured badly but because the question assumes a moment of verdict that was never there. Plain words, don't force the rhymes, under 24 lines total.
