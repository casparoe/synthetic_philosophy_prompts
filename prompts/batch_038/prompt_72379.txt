Please write a parody document of roughly 750–850 words: the internal underwriting guideline that a fictional longevity reinsurer — call it Ouroboros Re — issues to its writers after a newly hired existential-risk desk persuades the firm to price human mortality using the doomsday argument. The genre is the dry actuarial manual: numbered sections with headings, a small table of approved reference classes, a dissenting memorandum appended from the chief risk officer, and a closing certification line signed by an invented officer. The voice must never break. The document should read as though the firm sincerely believes it has converted an anthropic inference into a ratable exposure, complete with a section reporting the model's production track record since it was adopted.

Stipulate the arithmetic and keep it consistent throughout: the desk counts one hundred billion humans born to date, treats the likelihood of any particular birth rank as one over the total number who will ever live, and adopts a scale-invariant prior, so that its ninety-five-percent upper bound on that total is twenty times the current count and its median bound twice the current count. The desk should be visibly proud that the model takes as inputs no facts whatsoever about climate, arsenals, biotechnology, public health, macroeconomic conditions, or any individual applicant's habits, and should present that as a virtue rather than a gap.

The humor has to come from getting the reasoning exactly right. Without ever announcing them as objections, the document should dramatize all of the following: the arbitrariness of the reference class, by showing one and the same applicant receiving wildly divergent bounds depending on whether the writer selects all humans, all mammals, all observers in the cosmos, or all humans born within a given century; the degree to which the entire estimate is carried by the choice of prior; the self-indication-style rebuttal, which drives the expected total to infinity and therefore recommends reserves of zero; the predicament of the first two members of a brand-new class — say, the first two insured colonists on Mars — and counsel's refusal to write moral hazard on that scale; the impossibility of backtesting a model whose sample is a single species and whose target event has not yet occurred; and the compliance opinion that the model may not lawfully be used to decline, rate, or condition any individual application, because it makes no claim about individuals.

Every person, firm, memorandum, and internal document must be invented. No real philosophers, no real papers, no citations or reference lists, and nothing in quotation marks drawn from an actual text. A specialist in anthropic reasoning should recognize that you have the argument right and should find the satire fair rather than cheap; a reader who has never heard of the doomsday argument should still enjoy it as a portrait of a company that has quantified something it cannot act on. Stay deadpan through the final line: no wink, no summary, no character stepping outside the frame to explain the joke.
