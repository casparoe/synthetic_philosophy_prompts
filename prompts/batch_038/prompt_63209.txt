Take its main claims one at a time and restate each in my terms: initiating events, independent protective layers, detection latency, common-cause failure, safety margin, tolerable risk, normalization of deviance, gross disproportion. My rough first pass is tight = lots of layers plus a strict permit regime, loose = fewer layers and more operator discretion, but I want you to actually do the work rather than just label the two ends.

Then give me the residue report: which claims land cleanly, which ones you had to bend to fit, which have no expression at all. I suspect the creativity claim (tight societies innovate less) and the whole normative half — how much conformity a society is entitled to demand — are going to be trouble, since "reasonably practicable" is doing a lot of quiet work in my field and its not obvious it means anything like legitimacy.

Two ground rules. Don't smuggle: if a process-safety assumption is doing the translating (risk is quantifiable, layers really are independent, everybody already agrees what the plant is for), flag it instead of passing it off as neutral. And say whether the residue is a limit of my framework or a sign the original claims were vaguer than they looked.

Tell me if that's expressible in the source framework or if I'm just rebranding.
