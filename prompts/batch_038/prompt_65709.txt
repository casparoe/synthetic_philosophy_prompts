I sit on the science advisory panel for a sample-return mission from an icy moon — the plume kind, not Mars. Six of us, and on Thursday we have to sign off on a classification call that everyone knows its going to leak within a week.

Situation. Aliquot A: over eleven days a microcalorimeter logged three heat bursts, each starting roughly 40 minutes after the feed line released a dilute nutrient mix, each burst bigger than the one before. During the bursts, high-res mass spec shows the feed molecules going down and a family of heavier ions coming up, spaced about 14 Da apart. On day 12 everything went quiet and hasn't restarted, even though we fed it twice more. Imaging caught the material fragmenting just before the last burst, and the fragments kept producing signal for a while afterwards.

Aliquot B: no heat signature above noise at all. But there are particles that, when we add monomers from the same feed, build new shells matching the parent particles' geometry, and those shells go on to do the same thing — six rounds so far. No gradient, no measurable free-energy turnover, nothing that looks like metabolism.

Our charter says a positive finding means satisfying at least four of six operational markers: a maintained boundary against a gradient; catalytic free-energy dissipation; selective growth from the environment; heritable variation across generations; feedback response to perturbation; and no known abiotic process that accounts for the observed rates. It also says the markers are indicative rather than strictly necessary, which all six of us read differently. The bite: a positive finding forces Tier-3 containment, meaning the remaining sample can only ever be handled robotically, plus a same-day public announcement. Negative means Tier 1 and normal open science. There is no third category, and creating one takes a two-thirds vote of the parent committee, which doesnt meet for another five months.

Both aliquots are contested. A's bursts could be slow exothermic polymerization, and the fragmentation may not be reproduction at all if nothing is inherited. B looks like reproduction, but the templating could be surface chemistry on mineral grains, and there's no energy story whatsoever.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting. It needs to land on an actual recommendation for each aliquot — positive, negative, or a motion to create the third category with proposed language for it — and say what containment tier and what announcement wording follows from each. Give the strongest honest case against your own call, not a strawman. And please deal head-on with the worry that whichever way we rule we're effectively fixing the definition of life for every mission after this one, given that the charter's counting rule was written by people who never imagined a case where the markers split across two samples like this.
