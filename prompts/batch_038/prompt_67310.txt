Hey, so — I'm about two thirds of the way through a novel set maybe fifteen years out, in which fertility clinics are reviewed publicly the way restaurants are. Star ratings, photos, owner responses, the lot. My protagonist is a man in his early forties who has just learned two things: that his mother put off having him for a decade because of her career, and that he was chosen as an embryo out of a batch of four by a scoring system the clinic insists was doing him a favour. He goes online at about one in the morning and writes a one-star review. That's the piece I want from you — the review itself, in his voice, 600 to 700 words.

Its not supposed to be jokey. It's supposed to be the kind of funny that makes you feel slightly ill. The engine of it is that he cannot get the review to work. Every harm he alleges he has to retract a sentence later, because had they done it differently he wouldn't be the one typing. He tries the complaint about being conceived late. He tries the complaint about the sibling who could of been. He tries the complaint that they ought to have picked the sturdier embryo, the one with the better score. Each collapses on him in the same shape and for slightly different reasons, and he keeps thinking he's got it this time. He also can't get any comfort out of saying it would have been better if he'd never been born at all, and he should notice that, and be rattled by it.

Then, somewhere past the middle, let him get so frustrated that he slides sideways into a way of thinking that isn't about him anymore — that a different, happier child would simply have been the better outcome, nobody wronged, nothing owed — and let him find that even less bearable, because now he hasn't been harmed, he's been replaced, and he's apparently supposed to be pleased about it. By the end I want someone who's never heard of any of this philosophy to have felt the trap close, and someone who has to be laughing at exactly how faithfully it's been reproduced. Nobody states the problem. That's the whole game.

Put the clinic's reply in near the end: warm, corporate, genuinely unanswerable, signed off by someone with a title like Patient Experience Lead. And give him one edit to the review a couple of days later, where he's changed the star rating for a reason that is very definitely not a resolution.

Two rules. Don't name any philosopher or book, and don't use any terms of art — no person-affecting, no nonidentity problem, none of that vocabulary. He's not an academic, he's an annoyed customer with a text box. And please keep it all in continuous prose: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, nothing that looks laid out. Just paragraphs running into each other the way someone types at 1am, with the star rating spelled out in words at the top.
