So two guys I bowl with got into it last Thursday and I still don't know who's right. Neither one has a philosophy degree — Dev has been reading a lot of stuff online about evolution and morality, and Marcus thinks Dev is being sloppy about it. I was just the guy sitting next to them eating nachos, but I ended up thinking about it all week.

Here's roughly how it went, in order:

1. Dev: evolution built our moral instincts to help us survive and reproduce, not to get at the truth. If it had been useful for our ancestors to think raiding the next valley over was fine, we'd think that today. So the fact that our moral beliefs line up with survival explains them all by itself, without ever needing them to be true. Which means we've got no reason to think they are true, even if some happen to be.

2. Marcus: that's the genetic fallacy, plain and simple. Where a belief comes from has nothing to do with whether it's true. I believe there's a lane divider over there because I can see it, and my seeing it is also a product of evolution, but that doesn't make it unreliable. And if you say evolved beliefs are suspect across the board you've sawed off your own branch, because the argument you just gave me came out of the same evolved head.

3. Dev: it's not across the board, and that's the whole point. For stuff like spotting a predator or counting, getting it right kept you alive, so evolution had a reason to make those accurate. But there's no built-in link between surviving and moral truth — being loyal to your kin could boost your fitness whether or not it was actually right. So the sawing-off thing helps me, not you: evolution is a reason to trust my faculties exactly where truth paid off, and a reason to doubt them where it didn't.

4. Marcus: then you owe me an argument that moral truth doesn't pay off. And here's the one you're skipping: surviving is good. Not just useful, good. So when evolution pushed us toward cooperating and caring about our kids it wasn't pushing us away from moral truth, it was pushing us toward it — the same reason a thermometer reads correctly. There's a shared explanation for why the useful beliefs and the true ones overlap, so it isn't a miracle that needs debunking.

5. Dev: you just used a moral claim — surviving is good — to rescue moral claims that came from the same place you're trying to rescue them from. That's circular. And even if I handed it to you, it only covers a slice. Maybe it works for don't-kill-your-kids. But evolution had zero interest in whether I owe anything to a stranger on the other side of the planet, or whether its ok to torture one guy to save a million. There the survival story runs out and your shared-explanation thing doesn't reach.

6. Marcus: notice what shape your argument actually has, though. If moral truths are just projections of our feelings, there's nothing to debunk — evolution explains them and they're not false. So you're really saying either there's no moral truth, or there is and we couldn't know it. That isn't evidence that any particular thing I believe is wrong. It's a reason for me to be less cocky about realism, that's all.

At that point they went back to move 3 and I tuned out.
