Consider the color phi illusion. A subject fixates a screen on which a green light is flashed at one location and, a fraction of a second later, a red light is flashed a few degrees away. Nothing moves, and nothing on the screen is ever a green thing in motion; yet the subject reports seeing a single dot travel from the first position to the second and change color, green to red, partway along the path. The reported change of color therefore involves information that was not physically available until the second flash: the subject seems to have witnessed an event before its final ingredient existed. This is the feature Dennett pressed into service as an argument against the idea that somewhere in the brain there is a finish line at which unconscious processing becomes conscious experience.

Philosophers of mind standardly sort the possible reconciliations of the report into two families, named after the political techniques they resemble. An Orwellian reconciliation holds that the subject really did see two stationary flashes in their true order, but that the record was altered almost immediately afterward, so what she now reports is a memory rewritten by later events. A Stalinesque reconciliation holds that the verdict was reached before the trial was held: the two flashes are composed backstage into a single moving color-changing dot, and that finished product is what she then experiences and reports accurately. What makes the pairing philosophically interesting is that the two accounts can agree about everything physical in the head — same events, same causal order, same predictions — and differ only about whether the point at which the misbegotten content enters is to be called pre-experiential or post-experiential, a difference the subject is, by the construction of the case, unable to detect from the inside.

Write a joke about this. The following conditions apply:

- The punchline must turn on what actually distinguishes the two reconciliations: the direction of the revision, and the fact that nothing the subject could ever say or do would settle which of them happened. A joke that would work equally well with the two labels transposed is not acceptable.
- Neither the setup nor the punchline may be replaceable by a generic gag about unconscious processing, subliminal perception, or confabulation in general. Someone who knows this particular phenomenon should be able to tell that the joke is about it.
- You are welcome to exploit the political resonances of the two labels, but the totalitarianism cannot be doing all of the work; the joke must still be about a green dot and a red dot.
- Deliver a single joke: setup and punchline, no more than six lines in total. Do not append a gloss explaining why it is funny.
