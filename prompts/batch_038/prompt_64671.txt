Thirty-odd years I spent doing insect neuroethology — mostly timing how long a wasp keeps at a blocked route before giving up — and I've only picked up philosophy properly since retiring. Dennett's four sorts of creature has me going round in circles: Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and then Gregorian on top, which get their extra purchase from tools that live outside the skull — writing, number, maps. It reads like a ladder you climb, and I can't tell whether it's meant to be one.

So run it on a real episode. Terrace's project with the chimp Nim, the one named as a dig at Chomsky. The bits of the record that matter to me: Terrace was himself a Skinner-trained experimentalist; Nim was raised in people's houses with a rotating cast of carers, most of them not fluent in the sign language he was supposedly being taught; signing was rewarded, largely with food; and the negative verdict came only afterwards, when Terrace went through the videotapes and saw how much of the signing was imitation of what the teacher had just produced, and how nearly all of it was requests rather than statements. He concluded Nim had never made a sentence. Not long afterwards other workers raised apes on lexigram keyboards, stressing comprehension before production and without the food-reward drilling, and got quite different results — one bonobo apparently picked the symbols up by watching his mother being taught rather than being trained himself.

What I'd like:
- Say in advance what would have counted as evidence that Nim was operating at each rung, and then be honest about where the record speaks to it and where it simply doesn't.
- Then the knot I can't get loose. Was the apparatus itself Skinnerian? Rewards, prompting, turn-taking by adults who needed the session to go well. If the rig can only ever yield requests for food, is the taxonomy describing the animal or the animal-and-apparatus pair — and if it's the latter, doesn't the ladder stop being a property of a mind and become a property of a system, which looks to me like it guts the whole point of it?
- Verdict on what the episode does to the theory: confirmation, anomaly, or something it was never built to handle. And say plainly where it's silent rather than making it speak — I'd sooner hear that the taxonomy has nothing to say than a forced answer. One place in particular: the ladder is about kinds of creatures, but all the evidence here is one traumatised individual chimp. Can a null result ever license a claim about a species?
