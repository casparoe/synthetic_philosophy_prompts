I am putting together a diagnostic exercise for a postgraduate seminar on assertion, and I want a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually believe rather than by the name of the norm they say they hold. Two axes matter for my purpose, and the tree must keep them apart: what assertion is governed by (truth, knowledge, justified belief, reasonable belief, certainty, a standard that shifts with context, or nothing at all), and whether that standard governs the act of asserting or only the speaker's blameworthiness for it. Since most people's answers mix the two, please include at least three branch questions whose sole job is to separate an act-level verdict from an agent-level one.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of views: each branch should be a question a respondent can answer without knowing which position it leads to, with the positions then located at the leaves. I would like these to appear as leaves: the knowledge norm, the truth norm, some version of a justified- or reasonable-belief norm, the certainty norm, contextualism about assertion, the view that assertion has no constitutive norm, and the excuse view, on which one should assert what one reasonably believes and is not blameworthy when one does so. Aim for eight to twelve branch questions.

Phrase the branch questions as cases, not labels: a lottery ticket; a case in which what is asserted is true and well evidenced but not known; a case in which the speaker has excellent evidence for something that turns out to be false; a case in which someone asserts, and has good grounds for, something they do not believe; and a high-stakes twin of a low-stakes case in which the very same proposition is asserted.

Where a combination of answers is available in principle but no position occupies it, mark it as unoccupied and say whether that is because the resulting view would be incoherent, because it is unattractive, or merely because nobody has developed it. Finally, flag explicitly any pair of leaves the tree cannot really separate, and say which question would separate them if I had room for it; likewise flag anywhere the leaf reached depends on the order in which the questions are asked.
