I am midway through a novel whose central institution is a breeding programme for human transplant donors, administered by people who consider themselves kind, and before I commit to it I want to know how much of the work Ishiguro has already done. Read *Never Let Me Go* against Nozick's treatment of animals in *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*: the two-tier arrangement in which a broadly utilitarian calculus governs one class of beings while side constraints shelter persons, together with his suggestion that what grounds those constraints is a being's capacity to shape its life in accordance with some overall conception of it. Where does the novel put that criterion under test — the Gallery, and Miss Emily's account of what the artwork was supposed to prove — and what does it say happens when the criterion is satisfied and nothing changes? Weigh Miss Lucy's sense that the students were informed in a manner designed to prevent understanding, and decide whether Kathy's composure is best read as adaptive preference or as something Ishiguro wants us to respect. Say plainly where the analogy strains, since the donors are persons and Nozick's unease concerns non-human creatures, and finish with what the novel contributes to the debate over two-tier moralities that argument alone cannot. Refer to scenes rather than lines; I am working from memory too.
