I'm reading a pop book on machine minds and one chapter takes a quick run at the Searle/Dennett fight over derived intentionality. The passage goes:

'Searle's claim is that the man in the room manipulates symbols without understanding them: syntax in, syntax out, and no meaning anywhere inside his head. Meaning, for Searle, has to be original — intrinsic to the organism, produced by the biology of the brain, and there whether anyone interprets it or not. Dennett's answer is the two-bitser. A coin sensor built for American soft-drink machines gets shipped to Panama and installed in a machine there, where it spends the rest of its working life accepting quarter-balboas. Not one wire is altered, but what its states are about has changed: it used to detect quarters, now it detects balboas. So the content of a state isn't fixed by what's physically inside it; it's fixed by the environment and by whoever puts the thing to use. Which means there is no such thing as original intentionality — all meaning is assigned meaning, and any physical state can be read as meaning whatever the reader decides. On that picture the man in the room does understand Chinese after all, because understanding just is a matter of the whole system being interpretable as understanding. Searle's only reply was to insist that real meaning needs the right sort of wet chemistry, which begs the question, and that's why the argument is generally considered settled in Dennett's favor.'
