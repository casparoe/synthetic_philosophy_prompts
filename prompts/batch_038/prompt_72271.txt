So here's my situation: six weeks ago I started doing allocation analysis for a mid-size health funder, which basically means I rank candidate programs by cost per year of healthy life gained and hand the shortlist to a committee. One of the committee members has a philosophy background and she's been telling me the entire exercise is illegitimate, not because the numbers are wrong but because of what the numbers do — they treat the pool of lives we're distributing over as one big container of welfare to be topped up, and in doing so they stop tracking the fact that these are distinct people, each of whom has exactly one life to live and none of whom gets compensated by somebody else's gain. Honestly the more I've sat with it the more I think she's right, and I've come over to the seperateness of persons objection. But I converted in about three weeks off one persuasive conversation, which isn't a great epistemic track record, and I'd like to know what would count against the view before I start arguing for it in meetings.

So: give me the defeaters, ranked from fatal through serious to merely awkward. A few threads I already suspect matter and want you to run down rather than skip. One: if the objection really holds, what does it commit me to in the rescue cases — is the coin-flip answer about five versus one a consequence I have to swallow, or is there a non-aggregating way to get to saving the many, and how ad hoc are the available versions of that? Two: how load-bearing is the metaphysics? I've read that Parfit thought his own reductionism about personal identity blunts the objection, on the grounds that the boundaries between persons aren't as deep as the objection assumes — if that's right, does my conversion depend on getting personal identity wrong, and is the intrapersonal side of the comparison (the tradeoffs I make across my own years, which the objection seems to bless) actually as secure as it needs to be? Three: is the priority view a counterexample? It's additive and maximizing but weights each person's absolute level rather than comparing people, and its fans say that's precisely how you respect the fact that persons are distinct. Either that works and the objection is a narrow point about functional form rather than a deep one, or it's utilitarianism in disguise — I can't tell which, and I'd like you to take a side. Four: the objection has to mark off the aggregation we all happily do — seatbelt mandates, drug-approval thresholds, engineering safety factors, the triage protocol I used at my last job — from the aggregation it condemns. If it can't draw that line in a principled way, does it overgenerate into a view nobody actually lives by? Five: is it possible the objection has no extensional consequences at all, just a complaint about the shape of a theory rather than its verdicts, and if so is that a fatal weakness or a perfectly fine thing for a structural criticism to be? Relatedly, what does it actually predict that a sum-ranking rival doesn't — which concrete verdicts change, and are those verdicts ones I'd want my name attached to in a funding memo?

For each of these I want to know what a committed defender of the objection would themselves concede as counting against them versus what they'd wave off as a misreading, and why — I care more about the internal standard than about what the critics say. Then give me the honest meta-answer: has the view been insulated by qualifications, especially the threshold versions that permit aggregation when the stakes get big enough, and is that a real problem for a claim of this kind or is a thesis about moral structure just not the sort of thing that has to risk being empirically wrong? Finally, and this is the part I most want: tell me your confidence on each ranking, say what would change your mind — which argument or consideration would flip your verdict on the two or three you rate most serious — and flag where you think your own reasoning here is most likely to break down. If I've mis-stated the position she was actually making, say so before you answer.
