I teach a graduate seminar on the foundations of welfare economics, and I want a handout that sorts students' competing readings of Harsanyi's aggregation theorem. Build a decision tree that starts from the theorem's bare content — individual and social preferences over a common set of prospects, each satisfying the expected-utility axioms, plus a Pareto condition, yielding social utility as a positively weighted sum of individual utilities — and ends at an interpretive position. Each node must be a question a student can answer from the mathematics alone, without knowing which leaf it leads to. The nodes should turn on the cruxes that actually generate disagreement: whether the weights are equal by derivation or by an anonymity assumption; whether the aggregated quantities are von Neumann-Morgenstern indices or measures of wellbeing; whether the social observer's own preferences are among those being aggregated, or she stands outside them; whether the Pareto condition is imposed only ex ante or also statewise on the outcomes; whether social preferences over prospects are required to satisfy the independence axiom; and whether the population is held fixed across the prospects compared.

The leaves should include at least the following, each labeled by name or, if no one defends it, explicitly marked as unnamed: classical utilitarianism; weighted welfarism with unequal and unnormalized weights; the reading on which the theorem is neutral between utilitarian and distribution-sensitive aggregation precisely because the summed quantities need not be wellbeing; the reading on which the theorem delivers only that a rational social observer's preferences are an affine combination of individual preferences, with no moral content; and the reading on which the premises are jointly unsustainable once ex ante and statewise Pareto are both demanded.

After the tree, add two short sections. First, list the combinations of answers the tree permits that no position occupies, and say for each whether the vacancy reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Second, flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot genuinely separate, and state what further question would be needed to split them. Please write in American English.
