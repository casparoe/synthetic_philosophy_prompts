I spent most of yesterday evening arguing with a friend about Rousseau's *Letter to d'Alembert on Spectacles* and we ended at an impasse I would like to convert into a design problem. Our reading group had been going through the section where Rousseau answers d'Alembert's suggestion, in the *Encyclopédie* article on Geneva, that a well-regulated playhouse could be established in that city without damage to its morals. My friend's position is that Rousseau's objections are at bottom objections to commercial professional representation: the actor who sells his person and plays at being someone else, the enclosed building, the ticket, the author who assigns fixed characters, the idleness and expenditure a theatrical season requires. On that reading the civic festival Rousseau recommends toward the end of the letter — the open-air assembly in which nobody stands on a stage and the people, as he roughly puts it in the passage about planting a stake crowned with flowers in a public square and gathering the citizens around it, become the spectacle to one another — is immune by construction, because every offending feature has been deleted. I argued back that his mechanisms are not tied to those features: that theater on his account mirrors and inflames what a public already feels instead of correcting it; that it turns pity into a sentiment spent in one's seat, leaving the object of that pity unreached; that any assembly large enough to be worth assembling breeds comparison, display, and amour-propre among those who show themselves as much as among those who look on; and that hours given to a festival are hours taken from work whatever the festival charges. He said this was question-begging — that I was asserting the harms would follow rather than producing them — and that unless I could exhibit a festival meeting every condition Rousseau attaches to the good assembly and still generating the harms, the objection was empty. That seems fair, and it is the challenge I want you to take up.

Construct a thought experiment in which a fully Rousseau-compliant civic festival reproduces at least three of the four harms Rousseau attributes to the stage. The specifications are deliberately strict, because the whole point is to deny my friend the reply that the case was rigged:

1. **Setting and scale.** A small self-governing city of the sort Rousseau had in mind — Geneva-like, republican institutions, a citizen militia, a few thousand households — in the later eighteenth century. Nothing anachronistic: no mass print driving attendance, no amplification, no recorded images, no transport capable of importing a crowd of strangers.
2. **Compliance.** The festival must satisfy every condition Rousseau attaches to the good assembly: held outdoors; no building, no stage, no proscenium or any architectural device that sorts those present into performers and onlookers; nobody paid to appear and nobody whose livelihood consists in appearing; no admission charge; no single author's script assigning anyone a fixed character to sustain; and the assembled citizens must themselves be what there is to see, so the gathering is an activity rather than a seated audience.
3. **The harms.** It must nonetheless produce, concretely rather than by stipulation, at least three of: (a) a sentiment of pity discharged within the assembly that leaves its object unrelieved; (b) an intensification of the attachments, passions, and resentments the citizenry already had, with nothing corrected; (c) a competition for display and precedence inflaming amour-propre, among those who show themselves no less than among those who watch; (d) labor hours lost and private expenditure incurred on dress, decoration, and preparation.
4. **No smuggling.** The harms may not come from reintroducing any feature banned in (2), nor from a corrupt magistrate, a decadent elite, a visiting troupe, a riot, or an unusually vicious crowd. They must fall out of the festival's being large, voluntary, and emotionally focused, involving nothing more sinister than citizens in good standing behaving as citizens do.
5. **Form.** The scenario itself in no more than two paragraphs, with specifics: what is being commemorated, what people actually do, who stands where, what it costs, how long it runs, and what the city looks like a week later.

After the scenario, do the audit. Take each of the four mechanisms in turn and say whether it fires, how, and which of Rousseau's own stated reasons for preferring the festival to the theater it collides with. Then name the single structural feature of your case doing most of the work, and state what happens if it is removed — shrinking the assembly to a size at which everyone present is also doing something, substituting competitive for expressive activity, eliminating any shared focal object of attention, making the gathering routine rather than exceptional. If removing that feature dissolves the case, say so plainly; that would vindicate my friend and I want to know it. Then give Rousseau's best defense of the festival against your own construction, including whether he can distinguish the two kinds of assembly without appealing to any feature excluded by (2), and whether that defense costs him the critique of theater — that is, whether it forces the conclusion that his objection was never to the theater as such but to any large voluntary assembly, or to representation wherever it appears, which would connect it to his broader suspicion of political representation rather than to the stage. Finish by telling me which of us is right, or whether the case shows something neither of us said.

Be as committed as the material permits: I want your verdict on the immunity claim, not a balanced survey of it. But flag every point where you are leaning on a contested reading of Rousseau rather than on something he clearly says — I am least sure of myself about mechanism (b), which may be my imposition rather than his, and if you think my summary of the mechanisms distorts the text enough to rig the exercise, say that before building anything. And if the specification simply cannot be met — if no festival satisfying (1) through (5) generates the harms without cheating — I would far rather you demonstrate the impossibility and locate its source than quietly relax one of the conditions and hand me a case that merely looks like it works. I am not looking for a courteous answer here; I want to know whether he has found the exit.
