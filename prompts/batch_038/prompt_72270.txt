Please write the minutes of an ordinary meeting of the Standing Committee on Self-Reference, a departmental body at an unnamed university that has by resolution adopted a three-valued paraconsistent logic as its official procedure: some sentences may be recorded as both true and false, every classical tautology remains in force, the law of excluded middle is retained, and *ex contradictione quodlibet* has been struck from the rules of order. The target of the satire is the view's own dialectical predicaments together with the bureaucratic register in which they are absorbed; 750–900 words, formatted as genuine minutes — header (date, chair, members present, apologies for absence), numbered items each giving the mover, the substance and the recorded outcome, a closing entry for the time of adjournment and for confirmation at the next meeting, and a signature block. Register: third person, past tense, formulaic, abbreviation-happy, and entirely humourless on its own account; the clerk writes as though nothing untoward were occurring. You may name the logic if you wish, but the piece should not depend on the reader knowing its name.

Setting details, all invented and to be used as given — do not substitute real institutions, and do not name or caricature any living philosopher:

- Chair: Professor A. Salmela. Clerk: Mr Tew. Other members: Dr Okonjo-Blythe, Mr Raffe, Professor Ilse Vantongeren.
- Standing Order 7(b): a motion may be recorded as both carried and not carried only where it is self-referential or concerns a limit-concept; every other motion is recorded as carried or as not carried, and the clerk is obliged to state which.
- Precedent: the Ashgrove Ruling of the previous term, in which a resignation letter referring to its own resignation was held both effective and ineffective, and the member concerned has been attending meetings ever since.

The following items must all appear, each carried by procedure rather than by commentary:

1. A routine motion — a room booking, a travel grant, the renewal of the catering contract — on which the clerk cannot complete a step the committee needs: the record states that the motion was either carried or not carried, and that it was not carried, yet the standing orders no longer license the move from those two entries to the conclusion that the alternative obtains. A member proposes restoring the step by adding a premise to the effect that this particular motion is not one of the special ones; the difficulty to be dramatised is that the committee has no criterion for telling which motions those are, and never agreed one.
2. A self-referential conditional motion of the form "if this motion is carried, then [some arbitrary further thing]", which under the old rules would have licensed any conclusion the mover pleased. Record the remedy the committee has adopted — a conditional that does not support detachment, or that restricts contraction — and let a member complain of its practical cost: that the minutes may now faithfully enter both a conditional and its antecedent without entering the consequent, and that nothing can be done about a motion until somebody says what follows from it.
3. An amendment moved by Mr Raffe to introduce into the standing orders a predicate "carried simpliciter", so that the committee can say plainly which of its own motions were merely carried and which were not. The clerk's objection is that a body able to express that predicate can be handed a motion reading "this motion is not carried simpliciter", and that legislating the predicate would oblige the committee to do the very thing it was constituted to refuse. The amendment is tabled indefinitely, with that reasoning left implicit in the entry.
4. One item in which the new procedure simply works — the committee disposes of a genuinely self-referential motion without the meeting dissolving — and one item recording that a motion of the form "the committee either has a quorum or does not" is carried nem. con. as a tautology from which nobody proposes to infer anything.
5. The preface paradox applied to the minutes themselves: the clerk is prepared to warrant every item severally while declining to warrant the whole, and a member asks whether the minutes may be approved as a true record on those terms.
6. The ending. The motion to adjourn is not self-referential and so cannot be a glut under 7(b); the clerk has entered that it was not carried and that either it was carried or the meeting continues, and cannot take the second step. The committee's status is left indeterminate, the chair's declaration is entered under protest, and the minutes go unsigned.

Two constraints on the humour. First, accuracy: every logical claim the piece makes or presupposes must be one a working paraconsistent logician would recognise as correct — explosion is rejected rather than embraced, the classical tautologies and excluded middle survive, and the casualties are disjunctive syllogism and detachment for the material conditional. Under no circumstances may the joke be that these people believe contradictions and therefore anything goes. Second, restraint: the standing objections to dialetheism — that the class of eligible contradictions looks arbitrarily drawn, that the paradoxes return the moment the view tries to state its own doctrine, and that surrendering detachment and disjunctive syllogism costs more in ordinary reasoning than the paradoxes ever cost — must all be legible in what the committee does and fails to do, and must never be voiced as objections by you, by the clerk, or by any member. No winking at the reader, no authorial asides, no exclamation marks.
