In cross-cultural psychology, the tightness of a society is commonly analyzed as having two components: how strongly and clearly its norms are specified, and how little deviation from them it tolerates. In the comparative data the two tend to move together, which is why they are usually combined into a single tight–loose dimension. Conceptually, though, they are independent, and collapsing them conceals two logically possible societies that a one-dimensional scale cannot represent: one whose norms are exacting, ubiquitous, and known to everyone but whose members are astonishingly unbothered when they are violated, and one with almost no settled norms at all but a ferocious appetite for sanctioning whatever counts as a violation of them.

Write a joke set in the second of these societies — the one with weak norms and severe punishment. It should have a recognizable comic architecture (setup, escalation, turn) and should work as a joke for a reader who has never encountered tightness research; but the particular turn it takes must be one that only a reader who grasps the distinction between norm strength and intolerance of deviation can fully see, so that a specialist recognizes which cell of the two-by-two the joke occupies and why that cell is a genuine possibility rather than a contradiction. Keep it to something that could be told aloud in fifteen or twenty seconds; a brief dialogue is acceptable if that serves the turn better than narration. Do not label the cells, name any researchers, or explain the joke afterward. Please write in American English.
