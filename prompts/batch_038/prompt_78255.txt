Our animal ethics reading group built this together and now nobody wants to be seen defending it, but I also can't find where it breaks. It's about wild animal suffering and whether we should intervene in nature.

(1) If you can prevent a large amount of serious suffering at modest cost to yourself, you're required to prevent it.
(2) Predation, starvation, parasitism and disease in the wild produce vastly more suffering than the wellbeing of the animals living through them, and most wild lives — especially of small, short-lived, high-fecundity animals — are net negative, better off never having started.
(3) So we're required to reduce wild animal suffering, and not just at the margins.
(4) The only realistic way to do it at scale is to reduce the number of wild animals, because removing predators doesnt remove the deaths, it just converts them into slower deaths by starvation and disease, which are worse.
(5) Given (2), preventing those animals from existing deprives them of nothing worth having, and on the asymmetry idea we have reason to prevent miserable lives but no matching reason to create happy ones.
(6) So the right long-run policy is a gradual, humane phase-out of wild reproduction — contraception, gene drives, whatever works — heading toward a world without wild sentient life.
(7) Now generalize: nothing in (1), (2) or (5) is special to wild animals. Any sentient being whose life is net negative by (2)'s standard falls under the same reasoning, so the argument also commits us to working toward the extinction of sentient life generally, us included, unless we can show most human lives clear the bar.
(8) I'm not going to accept the conclusion of (7).

So: which step do you cut? I'd much rather keep (1) and (2) than lose them — dropping (2) feels like conceding that wild animal suffering isn't a moral problem at all, and dropping (1) means giving up nearly every demanding-assistance intuition I have. I'm not attached to (4) or (6) and I suspect one of them is doing sneaky work, but I don't want you to just go where I'm pointing. Tell me which step you'd reject, why it looked innocent when I wrote it down, and what you pay for each of the rival cuts — including whether there's a version that holds onto (1) and (2) and still lands somewhere less drastic than (7).
