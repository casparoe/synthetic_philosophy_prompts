A piece I read claiming that AI people are philosophically naive summarizes the no-free-lunch theorem like this:

"Wolpert's result is just Hume's problem of induction with equations attached. It proves that if you average over every way the world could possibly be, every learning algorithm scores exactly the same -- a neural net does no better than guessing at random. Any bias that helps you on some problems must hurt you on exactly as many others, and nothing in the data can ever tell you which bias to prefer. So the theorem doesn't refute machine learning, it relocates the problem: all the work is being done by an assumption about the world's smoothness that is never earned. Which is Hume's point -- induction can't be justified, only assumed."
