I'm running the annotation scheme for a corpus project — we tag explanatory passages in a few hundred history books, and the coders keep landing on different labels for the same paragraph. So what I need is a sorting instrument, not a lit review: a decision tree of about seven questions a coder can answer from the passage in front of them without knowing any of the philosophy, which then assigns that passage to a theory of historical explanation.

Constraints. The leaves have to include a covering-law view, Dray's rational explanation of action, Scriven's explanation-by-sketch, Collingwood's re-enactment, and at least one position on which explanation is a property of the whole narrative configuration rather than of any single sentence. Two of the questions must turn on detachability — whether you can lift the explanans out of the story and it still explains on its own — because that's where our coders are worst, and one should be about whether the passage could be told differently without the explanation failing.

Then there's the case we actually fight over: a passage that leans on a tendency, say that revenue crises cost governments their legitimacy. Is that a law in disguise or just a rhetorical bridge to the particular cause, and which node on your tree is supposed to settle it? Phrase every node as a test on the text rather than a question about what someone believes, flag any pair of leaves your tree cant genuinely separate, and say of each empty combination of answers whether the missing view is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored. British English, please.
