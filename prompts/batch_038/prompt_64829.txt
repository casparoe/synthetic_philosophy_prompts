Right, I need a number off you rather than a survey of the literature. I write the data column for a magazine and every spring I end up filing something about Finland topping the happiness league table again — it's been number one since 2018 — and my editor wants the piece to actually take a view this year. My view keeps wobbling, so I want to pin it down.

The claim I want a credence in is the one the headline makes: that Finland is the happiest country in the world, read the way an ordinary reader reads it — i.e. that people living in Finland are, taken all in all, happier than people living anywhere else. Not the technical claim about mean ladder scores, which I'm granting you by stipulation. I want your number for the headline claim.

Then decompose it. At minimum I'd like terms for: whether the quantity the ladder actually elicits (a global rating of your life against your own idea of the best possible life for you) is the same thing as happiness in the ordinary sense, or something closer to a considered endorsement of how your life is going; whether self-reports are comparable across cultures at all, given that there seem to be systematic differences in scale use and in how comfortable people are rating themselves highly; whether Finland would still come out on top under a different operationalisation — say, the share of waking hours in which people report positive affect, sampled at random moments through the day rather than asked about in one reflective judgement; and whether "the happiest country" even picks out anything determinate when the gaps near the top are small relative to the standard errors on a sample of about a thousand respondents a year. Rough probability for each, then how you're combining them — and say explicitly whether you think the terms are independent, because my suspicion is that several of them rise and fall together, which would make a naive multiplication wrong in some direction or other and I'd like to know which.

Two more things after that. First: which term is the final figure most sensitive to, and what single piece of evidence would move the headline number furthest? I dont want "more research is needed" — I want the kind of finding that would make you revise by 0.15 or more, and what it would have to show. Second, and much shorter: a separate credence that a government which deliberately set out to raise its ladder score, and succeeded in climbing a few places, would thereby have made its citizens' lives better rather than merely made them report better. A line or two of decomposition is plenty for that one but I do want a number.

Please don't retreat into "it depends what you mean by happiness" — that ambiguity is precisely what I'm asking you to put a price on.
