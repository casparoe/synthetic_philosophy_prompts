I am finalizing a statement of my position on causation by omission, and I would like an audit of the package before it goes into print. My commitments are these:

(1) Omissions are genuine causes. When the on-duty nurse fails to administer the medication and the patient dies of the untreated condition, her failure is one of the causes of the death; nothing metaphysically bars an absence from doing causal work.

(2) Causation is difference-making, and I treat that as a necessary condition rather than a heuristic: c causes e only if, with the relevant background held fixed, e would not have occurred without c.

(3) Still, most omissions are not causes. My failure to water a stranger's houseplants, or a head of state's failure on another continent, is not among the causes of their withering — and this is not merely a point about which causes I choose to mention.

(4) I reconcile (1) with (3) by taking the relata to be contrastive: causation relates events-with-contrasts, so the true claim is that the absence of treatment caused the death rather than the recovery, while no comparable claim holds of the head of state. Which contrasts are admissible is fixed, I think, by which alternative counts as the default or normal course of events in the situation; I take that normality to be a mind-independent feature of the situation, though I have little to say about what makes it obtain.

(5) Causal facts are objective, interest-invariant, and they ground normative facts rather than the reverse: part of what distinguishes killing from letting die, and part of what makes the nurse culpable, is that in one case harm is caused and in the other it is not.

(6) Otherwise my metaphysics is austere: I am a Humean, holding that fundamentally there is only the distribution of local positive matters of fact — no negative facts, no absences among my basic entities, no irreducible powers.

Which of these pull against one another, and how badly? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally damaging, and for each say what would have to be given up or revised and what that costs me. Separate the genuine conflicts from those that only look like conflicts, and close by naming the single revision that would repair the most.
