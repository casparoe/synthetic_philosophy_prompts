Our department of corrections has, in this scenario, quietly adopted the reductionist account of personal identity — the one on which identity over time is nothing over and above psychological connectedness and continuity, holding to a degree, and on which identity therefore is not what matters for holding someone responsible. It now needs to fill the remaining four years of a sentence.

Write the vacancy notice it posts: a public-sector job advertisement recruiting someone to serve out the balance of a term on behalf of an offender whose continuity with the offence has fallen below the department's own threshold. Around 600 words, in the deadpan register of a recruitment page — About the role, Essential criteria, Desirable, Remuneration and benefits, Safeguarding, How to apply, and the equal-opportunities statement at the foot.

The target is the reductionist view and the administrative style that grows out of it, not any individual philosopher, and the humour must come from getting the metaphysics right rather than from stock absurdism. The continuity rubric needs an explicit numeric threshold and a stated weighting of connectedness against continuity; the posting must cope with branching, including two applicants who both clear the threshold, the tiebreak the department applies, and the appeal route for the rejected one; a genuinely repentant applicant who has changed so much that he no longer scores high enough is simultaneously ineligible for the post and, on the department's own logic, not the person who deserved it; and a line to the effect that the department cannot promise the successful candidate will be the same person who walks out at the end of the term should be doing real work rather than serving as a throwaway gag. A reader with no metaphysics should still find it funny, while someone who teaches personal identity should be able to tell that you know the difference between the worm and the stage readings of perdurance and which of them this department has committed itself to.

Commit to one reading and write from inside it; where a clause turns out to be inconsistent with the view it advertises, make the inconsistency load-bearing and legible rather than accidental. Then, outside the advertisement, add three or four sentences stating which reading you chose, why it makes for the sharper satire, and where you have taken liberties with the view or leaned on a contested interpretation of it.
