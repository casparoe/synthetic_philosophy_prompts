I work in formal verification: for the better part of a decade I have written and reasoned about model checkers for distributed protocols, and my native vocabulary is Kripke structures — sets of states, an accessibility relation between them, labels, invariants, and temporal operators. I know S4 and S5 as what you get when that relation is reflexive and transitive, or reflexive, symmetric, and transitive; I have used epistemic modal operators to say what an agent can rule out at a given state; and the only place I have met higher-order logic is as the thing that makes verification undecidable, not as a tool for building arguments. I have no training in philosophy. A colleague recently sent me a machine-checked formalization of Gödel's ontological argument, and in trying to read it I also went back to the ancestor version associated with Anselm, on which God is characterized as the being than which nothing greater can be thought and which reasons that such a being cannot merely be conceived without existing, since existing in reality would be greater than existing only in the understanding. I have been staring at both for a week and I cannot tell whether I am missing a concept or whether the concepts are missing content.

Take on the role of a philosopher of logic who is equally at home in formal methods, and think carefully rather than answering from the surface of the question. Before writing any formulas, spend a short paragraph listing the translation decisions you face and the points at which the source arguments leave those decisions open; if several translations are defensible, pick the best one and flag where a different choice would change the conclusion. Then do the work. For Gödel's version: tell me what the model is, what the states are and what relation holds between them, and render the axioms governing positive properties, the definition of a Godlike object, the definition of an essence, and the definition of necessary existence as formulas in a notation I can read; say which modal system the derivation actually needs and what symmetry and transitivity of the relation would mean operationally for a model of this kind. Be explicit about exactly the choices I would have to pin down in a specification: whether the domain of individuals is fixed across states or varies from state to state, whether existence can be a property at all when my quantifiers already range only over things that exist, and whether the positivity predicate is being read intensionally or extensionally. For Anselm's version: tell me whether the contrast between existing in the understanding and existing in reality has any counterpart I can express — a declared name that never denotes, a well-formed but uninhabited type, a specification with no implementation — and whether his inference is really a maximality argument over some ordering, and if so, an ordering on what.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Sort the claims into three piles: those that translate cleanly into states, relations, and invariants; those that translate only with distortion, where you should say precisely what you bent and how much load the bending bears; and those that have no expression at all in my vocabulary. Two things I could not shake, and I want you to tell me whether each is an insight or naivety. First, the axioms on positive properties look to me like they amount to saying that the positive properties form an ultrafilter on the Boolean algebra of properties, which in my world is the structure you get by singling out one maximally consistent state — so it looks as though positivity merely designates a state, and a Godlike object is whatever satisfies everything true at that designated state, which does not obviously produce an object rather than a description. Second, I have read that this family of axioms can be shown to make every proposition necessary, which in my terms would mean the whole model collapses to a single state up to bisimulation, at which point the modal operators in the argument are doing no work. Address both. Finally, render a verdict: does what fails to translate indicate that my vocabulary is genuinely missing something — a substantive notion of value or greatness rather than a predicate letter, quantification over properties, a real distinction between being an object and being a value of a bound variable — or does it suggest that the arguments were never as determinate as their formal dress implies? And once both have been translated, tell me whether Anselm and Gödel are making the same move or two different ones, and whether one of your translations had to be distorted to make them look alike. Define any philosophical term you need in my language rather than assuming I know it, and do not send me to a reading list: I want your reconstruction, not references.
