Two camps in the metaphysics of omission causation have not moved in decades. Process and mechanism theorists, along with a number of moral philosophers, hold that causation requires something productive to be transmitted: the nurse's failure to administer the medicine did not cause the death, the disease did, and responsibility attaches without the nurse being a cause at all. Counterfactualists treat dependence as sufficient, accept negative causation, and then must explain why every absence in the universe does not qualify as a cause of the plant's death — reaching for contrastivist relata, default values in structural equations, or normality conditions. Neither side budges, and each regards the other's move as obviously question-begging.

My hypothesis, offered only as a hypothesis: the two camps are answering different questions under one word. One asks what makes an attribution of responsibility apt and answers with production; the other asks what makes a dependence claim true and answers with difference-making. On that reading neither claim contradicts the other, which is why the arguments feel decisive to their authors and empty to their opponents.

Here is a case I cannot settle, and I think any diagnosis should be tested against it. Two sluice gates, physically identical. In one, the keeper is required to keep the gate shut; he opens it, the reservoir drains, and the mill wheel stops. In the other, he is required to open the gate at noon; he does not, and the mill wheel stops. The description coincides down to the gate's position and the pattern of flow; what differs is which position was called for. Yet the double-prevention maneuver used to reclassify certain omissions as genuine causation seems available in both directions — the opening can be redescribed as a failure to keep the gate shut, and the failure as the removal of a standing prevention by whatever last closed the gate. If that boundary is norm-sensitive and freely redrawable, I am unsure what work it is doing in the metaphysics rather than in the explanation.

So: is this a verbal dispute, a disagreement about the relata and arity of the causal relation, a normative question wearing metaphysical dress, or two questions under one heading? Give a diagnosis supported by what each side actually claims, and state what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or a finding about how causal judgment selects among absences — or argue that no progress should be expected, and say why the impasse is stable.
