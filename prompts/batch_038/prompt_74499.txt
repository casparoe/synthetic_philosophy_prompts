So I've been down a rabbit hole for a few months now on the whole "social construction" business — race, gender, money — with no formal training at all, just reading whatever I can get my hands on and arguing with my brother at the kitchen table. I've talked myself into an argument that seems to prove way too much and I can't find where it breaks.

Here it is.

1. Saying some kind of thing is socially constructed can only mean this: things of that kind exist at all only because people take a certain attitude toward them — count them, treat them, respond to them as there.
2. Money is like that. A twenty dollar bill is money only because we all treat it as money. Take the attitudes away and you're holding a rectangle of paper with ink on it.
3. But "rectangle of paper with ink on it" is itself one of our groupings. Nothing out there comes stamped *paper* or *bill*. Those are cuts we make because they suit us.
4. And this generalizes without limit. A weed isn't in the plant, it's in the gardener. Same goes for chair, for fish, for planet (ask Pluto), for invasive species — and same goes for race. Race is a grouping, groupings are ours, so race exists only in virtue of our attitudes toward it.
5. Now look at what (1) plus (4) buys you. The whole selling point was supposed to be that race and money are constructed while quarks and mountains are not. But if every kind we can name is a grouping we make, then *everything* is socially constructed, and "socially constructed" stops picking anything out.
6. So the claim is either trivially true of everything including the mountains, or — if you insist it's supposed to mean something special — false of the very cases people wheel it out for, because there was never any contrast class to begin with.

The bit that got me going is that this doesn't feel like a word game, I can test it on something I actually know. My town runs a time bank. You fix somebody's sink, you get 2 hours credited on a spreadsheet, you spend them on someone teaching you Spanish. Those two hours are real in that system — people genuinely show up, and if the coordinator quit keeping the ledger the hours would evaporate exactly like money in a bank run. But the ledger is rows in a shared doc, and the "hours" don't correspond to any clock time anybody actually measures. Is my 2 hours constructed in the loaded sense or the trivial sense? By my own argument I can't tell, and thats the entire problem, because the loaded sense is the only one doing any work.

I already tried one patch. I said construction only counts when the attitudes are what *keeps* the thing existing right now, not what originally caused it. Mountains fail that test, money passes, spreadsheet hours pass, so I thought I'd won. Except then race slides out of the constructed column, or at least half out — the categories get their grip through what people did, not through what they're currently counting — and that can't be right either, that's about the last conclusion I want. So the patch leaks.

My brother says step 1 is just false and construction is a matter of degree. I don't want to go that way honestly, "it's all a spectrum" feels like it drains the concept out too, just from the opposite end.
