Can you write me a decision tree / sorting instrument that takes someone from a concrete situation to a named position on why roughly-rational people trade at all, why prices move, and whether markets know anything?

Start node, fixed: two people who each count as roughly rational, who have both looked into the same question, who both know that they disagree, who both have access to the same trade at the same price, and where neither one updates and neither one trades.

Then build the branches out of the logical space, not out of a list of famous economists. Every node has to be a question I could answer honestly without knowing which way it leads — no node should be phrased like "do you accept the agreement theorem?" Things I want on there somewhere (reorder, merge, split, or add as you see fit):

- whether the two of us could plausibly be described as having started from the same prior, and whether that assumption is doing real work or is just a modelling convenience everybody pretends is neutral
- whether the thing that fails in a real argument is common knowledge of each other's beliefs, common knowledge that we're both rational, or the common prior itself — I always mush those three together and I want the tree to pry them apart
- whether a trade that got offered would even be believed, or whether offering it is self-defeating because the offer leaks what you think before anyone accepts
- whether the disagreement is stable, i.e. whether just announcing our numbers at each other in sequence would force us to converge, and whether that trick still works with three or more people in the room instead of two
- whether what's happening between us is speculative (a bet on who's right about the world), hedging/liquidity (we just want different things at different times and no belief is actually in dispute), or noise (one of us is trading for reasons that have nothing to do with information)
- whether a price moving at all tells you someone out there knows something, given the worry that a price which perfectly reflected everything known couldn't be an equilibrium, because then nobody could afford to find anything out
- whether the market is best read as a machine for pooling knowledge that nobody holds whole, or as a casino with a very good accounting department

Leaves I want to end up at, at minimum: the agreement-theorem position (if you really knew what I believed, you'd believe it too); the no-speculative-trade position; the common-knowledge-fails position (we know, we know that we know, but it doesn't go all the way down); the dispersed-knowledge / price-as-signal position; the position that says prices can't be fully informative so there's a built-in floor on how much the market can know; the noise-trader position; and the deflationary one that says our argument was never about beliefs at all, it was about endowments, time horizons and tax situations.
