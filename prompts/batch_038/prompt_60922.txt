I've got a small podcast — recorded in my garage, maybe 300 regular listeners, mostly friends of friends — and next month's guest is a researcher who takes the Hofstadter line seriously and defends a specific version of it: that a self isn't sealed inside one skull, it's a pattern that can get instantiated, partially and at lower resolution, in the heads of other people, so that you go on in diluted form in the people who knew you well. Strange loops, selves coming in sizes, all that.

I've read I Am a Strange Loop and I basically get the pitch, so I do not want an episode where he explains it to me slowly in nicer words. I want him to have to actually think. Draft me 8 to 10 questions with a follow-up under each one, where the follow-up is keyed to the answer he's most likely to give — and if there are two plausible answers, give me the follow-up for each and tell me which one you'd bet on. Order them so the conversation builds. He's the kind of guest who can absorb almost any objection into "well, it's a matter of degree," so I want the later questions to make that move harder and harder to pull off, and the last one to be about what would count against the view.

Here's the stuff I've been chewing on that I want in there somewhere:

- My grandmothers been dead four months and my aunt has started saying her exact turns of phrase, her little noises of disapproval, all of it. Half my family finds that comforting and half find it creepy and I can't work out which they should be. If grandma's pattern is running partly on my aunt's hardware, is what's running there the phrases, or the wanting to say them?

- I keep landing on this distinction and I dont know if its any good, so put it to him: is a person surviving in someone else more like a saved game state, which could in principle be resumed, or more like a replay file, which is a recording of moves already made and can't do anything new? Because my aunt doesn't surprise me the way grandma did. And if he says neither, make him say what the third thing is.

- If selves can be spread across heads, can they be edited? My aunt's version of grandma is warmer than my dad's, and they argue about it. On his view is that one person inconsistently instantiated, or two people? And does the version that gets revised more often count as less of her?

- Degrees means counting. Two people who each carry a big chunk of somebody — is that 1.5 people now, or still one, and where is the fact of the matter supposed to come from? Related: does partial survival in other minds carry any moral weight at all, in how bad a death is or what we owe the dead, or is that just a comforting story with extra steps?

- And the one I'm almost afraid to ask because it sounds dumb: what work is his view doing that "she lives on in our memories and habits" isn't already doing? Make him show me the difference in what the two claims explain or predict, not just in how they feel to say.

Also work in something about copies and uploading, because he'll get asked about it eventually anyway — why gradual spread through other people's brains is different in kind from gradual replacement of a brain with something synthetic, or whether he thinks it isn't different at all.

No softball questions. Nothing about what drew him to this, nothing about where he thinks the field is heading. Everything should turn on the content of what he actually believes.
