I clerk for a state legislative committee drafting its first comprehensive statute on adult sex work. The committee has taken no formal position on any of the three reference regimes it keeps invoking — the Nordic approach of penalizing buyers rather than sellers, the German and Dutch licensing models, and New Zealand's decriminalization — but it has voted provision by provision:

1. Rejected criminalizing the sale of sex by a consenting adult.
2. Rejected criminalizing the purchase of sex from a consenting adult.
3. Adopted a licensing requirement for any premises where sexual services are offered, with record-keeping, condoms on site, and inspection, enforced by fine and closure.
4. Adopted a prohibition on any third party taking a share of a worker's earnings, applying even where the worker consented and there is no evidence of coercion.
5. Adopted strict liability as to age for any purchase from a person under eighteen.
6. Rejected giving municipalities a zoning power to exclude adult businesses from the vicinity of schools and houses of worship.
7. Adopted a provision allowing a worker to sue a client in civil court for nonpayment or assault, instead of treating such disputes as falling outside the law.

Next week the committee votes on whether a licensed agency may lawfully employ consenting adult workers, pay them a wage or a set percentage, and retain the remainder of the fees — in effect, whether provision 4 survives inside the licensed sector. Predict the vote. Then set out each candidate principle that is equally consistent with provisions 1 through 7, say what each implies about the new proposal, and identify which prior provision is the closest analog and why. Give your best guess, with the reasons for preferring that principle over the alternatives, and note where provisions 4 and 6 pull in different directions on the surviving candidates. Finally, if the record genuinely underdetermines the outcome, state what substantive commitment the committee would be adopting with each choice, and name the single further provision that would discriminate decisively among the rival principles.
