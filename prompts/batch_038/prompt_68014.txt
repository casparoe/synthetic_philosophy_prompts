so for the last seminar of my philosophy of physics course each of us has to write down our own view and then get torn apart by everyone else. here's roughly where I've landed and honestly I can't tell anymore whether it hangs together. my tutor read it and just said one of these is going to cost you, and wouldn't say which. so: go through the list, tell me which of these genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do, and for each real conflict say what has to give and what giving it costs. then rank them worst-first and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. please don't lecture me about structural realism in general, I've done the reading — I want an audit of this specific package.

(1) I'm an ontic structural realist. At the fundamental level there are no objects with their own intrinsic natures, only structure. Objects aren't unreal, but they're derivative — whatever falls out of the structure.

(2) I take the Newman objection seriously. If you spell out the structure of the world by Ramsey-sentencing a theory and letting the second-order variables range over absolutely anything, then any true theory comes out true of any collection of things with the right cardinality. So structure can't be characterised that way, and I don't want to pretend otherwise.

(3) My fix for (2) is to restrict the second-order quantifiers to natural relations — the ones that carve nature at its joints. Then the Ramsey sentence isn't trivial, because it stops being satisfiable by any old assignment.

(4) But I'm a naturalist and I don't want joint-carving to be a primitive bit of metaphysical furniture sitting over and above the physics. Which relations are natural has to be a structural matter itself.

(5) The reason I'm here in the first place is the pessimistic meta-induction. Caloric, phlogiston, the luminiferous aether — the ontologies all went, but in the good cases the structure survived: Fresnel's wave equations reappearing in Maxwell, the limit relations between Newtonian and relativistic mechanics. So structure is the only part of a theory with any track record and hence the only part worth believing.

(6) I refuse to retreat to epistemic structural realism, tho. The view that the world is made of things with natures we can never know, only relations, seems becuase of that to posit exactly the unknowable intrinsic stuff I want gone. Ontic, not epistemic.

(7) Quantum mechanics is my best concrete argument for (1): identical particles, permutation-invariant states, no fact about which particle is which. I'm also sympathetic to the weak discernibility response — particles get discerned by irreflexive relations, so they count as objects without being classical individuals — and I think that's fine for me since the relations are doing all the work either way.
