i've been running a Franklin-style virtue chart for about a year — thirteen virtues, one per week in rotation, a dot every time I catch myself in a lapse — and I want to write up what I've concluded for a reading group. Before I do, tell me whether the package hangs together.

(1) Moral perfection is actually attainable by a determined ordinary person given enough time and the right method, and Franklin quit for a bad reason — the whole ax business, where he decides a speckled blade is good enough, is a rationalization of a man who got tired.
(2) What makes the method work is that the dot lands at the moment of the fault: attention is the engine, not willpower.
(3) A lapse I never notice doesn't get marked, because I can only be held to what I actually catch.
(4) The point of the project is to become someone who no longer needs the chart — it's scaffolding.
(5) I won't revise the list of virtues mid-project, no matter how impossible one of them turns out to be, since editing the standard to fit your performance is obviously self-serving.
(6) In month nine I turned on auto-detection — the app infers lapses from my calendar, screen time and spending — and the dots spiked, then fell steadily over the last three quarters, which I take as evidence the method is working on me.

Rank the tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and for each one say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me. I don't have a view yet on which one I'd drop.
