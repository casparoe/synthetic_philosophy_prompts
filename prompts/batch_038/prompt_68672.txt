Write a customer-support FAQ page, in the flat, apologetic voice of a corporate help-centre article, for a start-up selling automated second-strike retaliation as a subscription service: the product is called Assured Response, and it comes in two tiers, Retaliate Lite (a human stays in the loop) and Full Irrevocability (currently in beta). Around 750–850 words, nine to twelve numbered Q&A entries, ending with the customary "Was this article helpful?" footer. The humour should come from getting the deterrence theory right rather than from doomsday-movie tropes: the machine is worthless unless rivals know it exists and believe it will fire; the warranty is void if it is ever used, so a satisfied customer is one for whom nothing has ever happened and who can never confirm the product works; the fourteen-day window in which a subscriber may revoke launch authority is a strategic liability the company must quietly apologise for; customers are required to form an intention to do something they will never, once the moment arrives, have any reason to carry out; and the cheaper tier, whose ambiguity deliberately leaves something to chance, may be more stabilising than total automation. Include one invented five-star testimonial from a pleased head of state, and one entry about what happens if a third, non-subscribed country acquires the same product. Never state the philosophical objections outright — the reader should feel them. Do not name real people, and do not cite sources.

Then, under the heading **Design notes** (150–200 words), say which argument or paradox each of your four strongest entries turns on, and identify the one joke you think is least fair to the position you are satirising, and why.
