I spent three hours last night arguing with a friend who builds vision models, and we got nowhere because we never agreed on what we were asking for. His position: a Bongard problem is a solved benchmark — twelve figures in two classes of six, one concept separates them, and a system that classifies held-out figures correctly thereby has a theory of the concept. My position: accuracy is downstream of a question he has not answered, namely what the concept is before it gets applied. Before I read another paper from either side, I want the specification.

Write the desiderata for a theory of one-shot concept induction of the Bongard kind — what any account must deliver to count as adequate, where adequate means good enough to tell a builder which machinery is mandatory and which is merely one camp's taste. For each requirement, give the case that motivates it and tag it as non-negotiable, contested, or a prejudice of a particular camp (psychological realism, minimal description length, no hand-coded feature vocabulary, and the like). The list should cover the phenomena a theory must explain rather than just the classifications it must match; the distinctions it may not collapse — between forming a concept and applying one, between a description of a single figure and a hypothesis about a class, between the vocabulary available and the search conducted over it; and the results it must remain consistent with.

Test the requirements against three probes I made up: (a) a problem whose discriminating concept cannot be read off any single figure, only from a relation holding across the two sets, which breaks the assumption that figures are classified independently; (b) a garden-path problem where the most salient low-level description covers five of six figures on each side and fails on the sixth, and the sixth is not noise; (c) a problem where the correct description of a figure is unavailable until the solver has committed to a partial description and can return to revise it.

Then tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates, and — this is the part I actually want — whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. Can a theory demand no privileged innate feature vocabulary, one exposure per class, and a concept that is afterward articulable and checkable, or must one of the three go? Close with a paragraph on how a description-length account, a Bayesian-program-learning account over a supplied domain-specific language, and an open-ended bottom-up account assembled from many weak partial concepts fare against the list.
