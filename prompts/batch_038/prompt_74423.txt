So my sister is a TA for a first-year seminar called something like "Do Cultures Evolve?" and she's asked me to build a handout where the students score a bunch of candidate cultural items on how genuinely Darwinian thier transmission is. I don't want to hand them the standard memetics-versus-cultural-attractors foodfight, I want them to actually crunch the rubric, so I made one up and I want you to produce the reference answer I'll grade theirs against. Four axes, each scored 0–10:

- **F — fidelity.** Is each new token made by copying a template that itself persists between transmissions (a recording, a printed score, a text), or is it rebuilt from memory and inference, so what carries over is more like a schema? 0 = pure reconstruction, 10 = effectively error-corrected copying.
- **V — vehicle.** Is there a durable physical object that survives the gap and does the causal work of transmission (a book, a master tape, a church, a binder), or does the pattern exist only as standing dispositions in heads? 0 = nothing but heads, 10 = an artifact is the real transmitter.
- **B — blindness.** Do the variants that end up mattering arise independently of whether they'd be good, or are they targeted, i.e. people deliberately improve the thing and it's the improvement that spreads? 0 = fully guided, 10 = blind.
- **S — what gets selected.** When one variant beats another, is that mostly because of properties of the item itself, or properties of the hosts carrying it (thier status, institutions, demography, who they're friends with)? 0 = all host, 10 = all item.

The item's total is the **minimum** of the four, not the mean, because I think one catastrophic axis should sink the whole thing: something scoring 1 on S isn't undergoing selection on its own properties no matter how crisp the copying is.

Score these, one short sentence of reasoning each — the kind of thing a TA can read out loud without the class glazing over — then give the min-total and sort everything into tiers S/A/B/C: (1) the slang word "ghosting" as used by twenty-somethings; (2) an oral folk ballad handed down by singing, never recorded; (3) the Najdorf Sicilian as played by club players who learned it from opening manuals; (4) a binding precedent as cited and then distinguished in later judgments; (5) a family recipe that was never written down and gets adjusted every time it's made; (6) a viral short-video sound reused as the audio bed under thousands of different videos; (7) an image-macro template where the layout stays fixed and only the caption changes; (8) a rumor about the mayor; (9) a creed recited word-for-word in weekly liturgy for centuries; (10) a written lab SOP that's also taught hands-on to each new cohort of grad students; and (11) "Happy Birthday" — but here score the *tune* and the *practice of singing it at parties* as two separate rows, because I'm betting they come apart violently and that contrast is the whole point of the handout.

Then three follow-ups, and these are the part I actually care about. First: which axis is doing most of the discriminating work in your ranking? Second: redo the tiers using the mean instead of the min and tell me exactly which items move and how far — I want to know whether my aggregation rule is earning its keep or is just decoration, and if it's decoration, what the smallest repair is. My own worry is that B is basically 0 for almost everything on this list, in which case the min rule flattens the whole handout into one tier and I've built a rubric that can't see anything. Third: pick the single item where a Dawkins-style memeticist and a Sperber-style attractor person would score most differently, say which axis the fight is over, and tell me who's right and why — no fence-sitting on this one.

Please don't give me the general "is memetics even a coherent enterprise" debate as a preamble, I've read it, it's in every intro text, and the students will get that in week three anyway. Just do the scoring and defend the numbers. And no bibliography, no suggested readings, no paper titles — the handout has to stand on its own and I'm not going to be able to check anything you cite, so argue it out in plain prose instead.
