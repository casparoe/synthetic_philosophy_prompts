I spent thirty-five years measuring trace gases in ice cores and lake sediments, and only since retiring have I begun reading philosophy of science seriously. I am applying for a one-year interdisciplinary fellowship and need a two-page proposal built around a half-formed idea of mine: the long argument over when the Anthropocene began — the 1610 dip in the ice-core CO₂ record, the mid-twentieth-century acceleration, the early spread of agriculture — is routinely presented as a dispute about evidence, but I do not believe it is one. Each camp has quietly adopted a different reference condition for an "unperturbed" Earth system, and once that reference condition is fixed the onset date follows almost mechanically. If that is right, the live question is whether any principle can choose among reference conditions, or whether the choice is underdetermined and must nonetheless be closed by an authoritative decision — which is roughly what happened when the stratigraphic authorities declined in 2024 to ratify the Anthropocene as a formal unit of the time scale.

Please write the proposal as a philosopher of science who also knows earth-system science and the philosophy of measurement and classification. Think the framing through carefully first and show me two or three sentences of that reasoning at the top before the document proper. Then give me: a precise statement of the question in terms that admit of a determinate answer; an account of why treating it as a factual dispute about markers leaves it open; the method, month by month, including what I would actually be doing rather than reading; what a result would look like; and a genuine failure condition — a state of affairs at the end of the year that would mean the project had not worked, not a shrug. Also say explicitly why this framing beats the two obvious alternatives, namely asking which date the evidence best supports and asking who has the standing to decide; noting that they exist is not enough. I will supply the citations myself, so invent none and give no bibliography.

Above all, be committed: take a definite position on what the question is and defend it, rather than laying out the candidate interpretations and leaving the choice to me. If you think my baseline reading of the dispute is mistaken, say so plainly and propose the framing you would fund instead — but still commit to one.
