I am writing a seminar paper in normative ethics on whether genuine moral dilemmas are possible, and before taking up any positions I would like the specification they must answer to. Set out the desiderata: what any account of moral conflict must deliver, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, marking which are non-negotiable and which are commitments of particular camps.

I want the list built to handle one case. A nurse promised a dying patient she would keep his diagnosis from everyone, and separately promised the patient's estranged son, who does not know his father is in her ward, that she would tell him if anything happened. Both men die within the hour, and the son leaves the country before she can reach him. The two obligations are identical in kind and in weight, and no further act of hers can discharge either.

So the desiderata should say how an account is to treat conflicts that are symmetrical rather than weighable, conflicts that persist rather than resolve at a moment of choice, and residue owed where there is no one left to receive it. They should also say whether the consistency principles of standard deontic logic constrain the phenomenon or license a revision of its logic, and whether a conflict can be genuine relative to the agent's evidence but not relative to the facts. Close by telling me which single requirement excludes the most views, and whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable. A brief scorecard of the main contenders is welcome, but it should not be where the work goes.
