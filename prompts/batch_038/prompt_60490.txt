I am a fourth-year PhD student in philosophy working mostly in formal epistemology, and I have been asked to circulate a short statement of my own position on the lottery and preface paradoxes for our work-in-progress group, where the format is that people attack the statement rather than discuss the state of the question. Before I send it round, I would like a careful audit of whether the package hangs together. Here is what I propose to assert.

(a) A Lockean threshold view of outright belief in its strictest form: there is a single, context-invariant threshold t below 1 such that it is rational for me to believe that p just in case my credence in p is at least t. Nothing but my credence bears on the rationality of the flat belief — not what is at stake, not the character of the evidence on which the credence rests, not the rest of my belief set.

(b) I accordingly deny that rational flat belief is deductively cogent. It can be rational to believe each of p₁, …, pₙ and irrational to believe their conjunction, and I treat this as a piece of arithmetic about thresholds rather than as a paradox awaiting dissolution.

(c) I do accept closure under known single-premise entailment: if I rationally believe p and know that p entails q, I may rationally believe q.

(d) I reject pragmatic encroachment. Neither what I know nor what I may rationally believe shifts with how much turns on the matter.

(e) In the lottery case it is rational for me to believe of my ticket that it will lose, and not rational for me to believe that every ticket will lose.

(f) I nonetheless accept the usual intuition that I cannot know my ticket will lose, however long the odds, when the odds are all the evidence I have.

(g) I am a fallibilist about knowledge generally: I take myself to know a great many propositions on testimony, memory, and ordinary perception whose credence for me falls short of 1.

(h) I do not think the lottery and the preface are one case presented twice. The author of the preface believes each claim on the strength of her own examination of the evidence for that claim, whereas the lottery player believes nothing beyond a probability. That difference in the kind of evidence, rather than any difference in degree of probability, is what makes her individual beliefs candidates for knowledge and mine not — and it is not a difference I think my credences could register.

(i) So I hold that the author is rational to believe each claim in her book, and I am inclined to grant that she can know each of them, while she is equally rational to believe, on the strength of her track record and the sheer number of claims she has made, that at least one of them is false.

(j) I hold that knowledge is closed under known entailment, including entailment from finitely many premises each of which the knower knows.

(k) And I hold that a flat belief is epistemically permissible only if it could amount to knowledge. I do not accept that there are two independent standards operating here, one governing belief and another governing knowledge.

Two things I would like from you. First, tell me which of these genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to. Two objections I expect from the group are that (f) and (g) cannot both be true, since the lottery proposition is at least as probable as most of what I claim to know, and that (b) and (c) together mark an arbitrary stopping point. I am not persuaded by either, but I cannot tell whether my reasons for resisting them are any good, and I suspect that in resisting them I may be relying on something else in the list that the objection actually threatens. Second, for each real tension you identify, rank it by severity and say what would have to be surrendered or weakened to remove it, and what that surrender costs — in particular whether the cost is local or whether it takes with it the account of (b) that I regard as the main virtue of the whole package. If no single revision repairs more than one tension, say so; if one does, say which and describe what is left standing afterwards. Please work through the set as I have stated it rather than mapping out the available positions or telling me who defends what; I am not after a literature review, and I would rather you not spend space on views I have not claimed.
