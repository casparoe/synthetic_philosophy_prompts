ok so I'm putting together a 3-month project proposal for a small alignment residency — they want about two pages, and I have the shape of an idea but not the spine of it. Hoping you can build the spine.

The idea: basically everyone doing mechanistic anomaly detection (probing internals, sparse autoencoder features, activation patching to find something like a backdoor or a latent planning capability) validates their detector by showing it fires on a known-bad model and stays quiet on the clean one. But nobody seems to say what it would take for that detector to be detecting *the same mechanism* in a different model — different seed, different scale, different data mix. So there's a dilemma I keep circling, and I want the proposal built around it rather than around the detection task:

either the identity conditions are fine-grained — the mechanism is these components, in this arrangement, doing this — in which case there's no reason to expect transfer across runs at all, and a detector that works on the one adversarially-trained model we happened to be able to build tells us nothing about the model that actually gets deployed;

or they're coarse — the mechanism is whatever realizes the function, i.e. whatever set of internal states you could intervene on to produce this behavioral signature — in which case the detector is keyed to something a careful behavioral monitor might already pick up, and I'm not sure the mechanistic route is buying us anything.

I want the proposal to take that dilemma seriously rather then talk around it. Concretely: state the question so it has a determinate answer (something like: is there an intermediate grain at which mechanism identity is both stable across training runs and genuinely finer than behavior, and if so what's the test for it?), say why the obvious moves don't settle it — my sense is the causal abstraction framing gets you partway but presupposes the correspondence between the variables instead of deriving it, and multiple realizability arguments out of philosophy of mind give you the dilemma without any of the engineering — and then give the method. Month by month: which models, what interventions, what would count as a successful transfer, and what negative result would still be worth the three months. Be explicit about what would falsify the intermediate-grain hypothesis rather than just fail to support it.

Also be honest about where the scope blows up. I suspect I can't do the cross-scale version on my compute budget and should say so instead of promising it and quietly dropping it in month three.
