My opening: your metric is endorsement measured after the default has been in force, but the default is one of the main things producing that endorsement. Once a diabetic is on pathway A, her doctor's vocabulary, her appointment schedule, her sense of what's medically possible and her memory of how the decision went all form downstream of the default, so a year later she endorses A partly because A has become her life. Worse, your model is trained on endorsements collected from people who were defaulted by the previous version of the model, so the loop never touches anything outside itself — and its optimized, which means it will happily converge on defaults that manufacture contentment rather than defaults that track what people would want. Sharp version: you can't run the counterfactual, because "what she'd have endorsed without the default" isn't a state of the world you ever get to observe, and every instrument you'd use to approximate it — surveys, regret measures, later switching — is itself downstream of the architecture. So tell me what makes your criterion non-circular, and don't answer that you'd also track health outcomes, because that's a different criterion and you just told me officials' ideas of the good aren't the standard.
