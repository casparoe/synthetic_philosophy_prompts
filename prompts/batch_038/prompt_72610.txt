Our new CTO wants to run our 150-person SaaS company in Austin on what he calls the Danish model: salaries published in a band by level, nobody gets fired for performance, six weeks of vacation you're actually expected to take, hiring by consensus, no options — upside capped and spread around. He picked it up from a book on the flight home from a conference.

There's two kinds of pushback and I can't tell which one is real. Our infra lead says it only works in Denmark because everyone there already trusts each other — small, homogeneous, strong unions, and the state catches you if you lose your job — so without that background the rules just reward the one guy who coasts and drive out the good people. A coworker from Aarhus says the opposite: that the trust is something the rules create, not something they require first, and she keeps mentioning that the government is doing the part he's trying to do inside the company, which I dont follow.

I'm the one writing the proposal either way. Which of them is making an actual argument and which is just a vibe? And is there some test I could run — at our scale, not a country's — to tell whether the trust has to be there before the rules or shows up after? I'm an engineer with no philosophy training, so please don't assume I know the jargon.
