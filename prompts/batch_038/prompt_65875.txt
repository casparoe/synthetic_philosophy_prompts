Last month I somehow ended up as one of the two "citizen members" on a charter commission in the city I live in (pop. ~90k), which is a fancy way of saying I have a full-time job that isn't law or philosophy and now I'm supposed to help write the rules for who gets to vote on things.

The fight that got me appointed: council wants to put a measure on the spring ballot to fund a waste-to-energy plant sited right at the edge of town. The plant would serve a five-county waste district, so a lot of the benefit and a lot of the cost lands outside city limits — the township next door gets the truck traffic and probably the stack emissions, and they get no vote whatsoever. Meanwhile inside the city we're arguing about whether long-term residents without legal status should vote, whether 16- and 17-year-olds should, whether the people who commute in every day and work downtown but sleep out in the county should, and — the part that actually enrages me — second-home owners who live 400 miles away, own one rental property here, and have voted in every city election for twenty years.

Every one of those arguments gets made by somebody pointing at a single case and saying "obviously." I'd rather know what I should be demanding of a principle before I start comparing them. So: write the desiderata — the full list of what any account of who belongs to the demos has to deliver in order to be usable by someone in my seat.

For each requirement I want the case that motivates it (use mine where they fit, invent others where they don't) and a line on why it's a requirement rather than a preference. I especially want the ones people skip: that the rule has to produce a determinate electorate a clerk can actually build a roll from; that it has to survive the fact that the body setting the boundary is made of people who benefit from wherever it falls; that "affected" comes in degrees and over time horizons and the requirement should say what to do about that rather than pretend the problem isn't there; that it has to give an answer for bodies like ours that aren't states and sit inside other jurisdictions; and that it has to stay stable when people move, die, turn 18, or sell the house. And be explicit about which requirements are load-bearing — no adequate theory gets to fail them — and which are really the pet commitments of particular camps dressed up as requirements. I suspect several things I've been told are obviously mandatory are the second kind.

Then the part I most want: tell me which subsets of these can't be jointly satisfied. I have a nagging sense that you can't have a rule that's both administrable and genuinely binding on the people it excludes, because somebody has to draw the line and whoever draws it is drawing it for themselves — and that this is the actual problem, not a detail to clean up later. If that's right, say which requirement has to be dropped or reinterpreted and why. If it isn't right, show me exactly where my reasoning breaks.

Finally, briefly — 250 words, no more — sort the main contenders against the list: the ones that say anyone whose interests are affected, the ones that say anyone subject to the laws, the ones that say membership is about reciprocal obligation among people who share a life together, and the ones that say there's no single demos at all, just a different one per decision. Then tell me which single requirement knocks out the most of them.

Format, please:

- "The job this principle has to do" — one paragraph, ~120 words, saying what adequacy means for my situation specifically, not for a seminar's.
- "The requirements" — numbered, the bulk of it, ~1200 words. Each entry: the requirement, the case, why it's a requirement.
- "Load-bearing vs. camp furniture" — ~250 words.
- "Where the list breaks" — ~350 words, the unsatisfiability stuff.
- "Quick sort" — ~250 words on the contenders.
- "What I'd tell the commission" — ~150 words: the two or three requirements you'd actually write into our charge.

No bibliography, no list of who said what, no reading suggestions — I can't check any of it and its not what I need. Describe the positions by what they say. And dont pad: if a requirement is really just a restatement of another one, merge them and say so.
