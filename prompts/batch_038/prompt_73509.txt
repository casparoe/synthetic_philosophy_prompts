Longtime lurker on the rationalist blogs, and I've spent about a decade doing data science, so the vocabulary I actually think in is the economics of information: adverse selection, signaling and screening, lemons markets, externalities, transaction costs, the unraveling result, data as a nonrival good, mechanism design. I've read the law-and-econ side of privacy — Posner, Stigler, the newer empirical stuff — and it mostly seems right to me: people trade information for things they value, the market absorbs it, and a lot of the hand-wringing is a preference dressed up as a moral discovery.

What I can't get a handle on is the other side. So: take the strongest version of the anti-econ position on privacy — the one that says privacy isn't just concealment of discrediting facts, isn't just a commodity, and that something goes wrong even in a fully informed, fully voluntary market in personal data where nobody can point to a measurable harm — and restate it in my vocabulary, claim by claim. Attempt the hard ones, not the easy ones: privacy as constitutive of intimacy and of having a self at all; privacy as a precondition for being able to change, for not having your worst past permanently priced into you; the objection to the nothing-to-hide arguement that doesn't rely on hiding anything; the aggregation point, that individually trivial disclosures become dangerous in combination; the idea that some flows of information are inappropriate regardless of who consents.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Sort the claims into three piles:

- ones that translate cleanly — and in that case tell me what they add over what my framework already says, because if the answer is "nothing," say so;
- ones that only translate if you distort them — and say out loud what the distortion is, don't just convert "dignity" into "a strong preference for privacy" and score that as a successful translation;
- ones that have no expression in the econ-of-information vocabulary whatsoever.

For the third pile, pick a side. Is my vocabulary structurally blind here — e.g. does treating voluntary exchange as Pareto-improving by construction make a whole class of objection inexpressible rather than merely wrong — or were the privacy theorists never saying anything precise, and the untranslatability is the tell? I'd rather have a verdict than a both-sides shrug. Also flag any place you catch yourself smuggling my framework's assumptions in and calling it translation.
