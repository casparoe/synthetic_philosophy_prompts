I run a small philosophy of biology reading group and I want a diagnostic — a decision tree that sorts people into theories of biological function based on what they'd say about cases, not which label they fancy. Please build it from the logical space first: what are the actual axes (does the trait's history matter? is there real normativity, so that a thing can malfunction? are functions out there in the world or do they need someone to attribute them?), and only then put the views at the leaves — the selected-effect/etiological family, the Cummins-style causal-role view, the organizational account, proper-function views, and the deflationary option that says there's nothing here but dispositions plus our interests. Maybe seven-ish branch nodes, each one a concrete case someone could answer without knowing where it leads: an appendix that does nothing much now but keeps getting infected, a mutant enzyme that has never once catalysed anything, the wing of a flightless bird used only for balance, a heart still beating in a brain-dead body, and — my favourite — a hand-written artificial-life agent with a genuinely useful trait but no ancestors at all, which I think is the case that hurts the historical views most. Each leaf should name the view and give one sentence on its most awkward commitment, note any combination of answers no view occupies (and whether that's because it's incoherent, unappealing, or just unexplored), and flag any two leaves your tree can't really seperate. British English please.
