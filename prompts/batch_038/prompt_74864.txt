The fourth movement of my novel consists of a found document: a joint statement co-signed, after a year of private correspondence, by two philosophers of truth — one holding Peirce's position that a true belief is one that inquiry would come to rest on in the ideal limit, the other holding James's position that an idea becomes true in the course of the experience that confirms it. Please draft that statement as it might actually have been signed. It should contain a numbered section of claims both can endorse, including what each rejects in the view of truth as copying reality and what each is prepared to say practical consequences amount to; a table of the residual disagreements, each row worded so that the corresponding party would accept the description of its own side; the cruxes — those sub-questions whose settlement would actually move one of them — with, for each, the case both agree bears on it and each side's verdict recorded in advance; and a closing passage identifying which stretches of the quarrel were merely verbal, if any, and why.

Organize the cruxes around a proposition on which my plot turns: a letter written in 1848 and burned unopened by its addressee, so that no trace of its contents can ever serve as anyone's evidence. I need both philosophers to speak plainly about whether it is true that the letter contained a confession, about what could make that so, and about what a reader who now believes it thereby acquires or fails to acquire. Give me also the concession each signatory would have preferred not to make, and a note on what would count against each view if it were found.
