For forty years I directed an experimental physics group, and the habit I cannot shed is that of designing against the least favourable case: the vacuum vessel is rated far above any pressure we ever reached, the shielding is sized to the worst accident we could imagine rather than the likely one. So an essay I recently read in a general-interest technology magazine, arguing that this instinct is precisely what work on AI safety lacks, spoke to me strongly. Its central paragraph ran roughly as follows:

"Machine learning offers average-case guarantees only. A learned error rate is an expectation taken over the data used to train a model, and so it licenses no claim whatever about any individual input the system will later encounter. Worst-case guarantees are of a different character entirely: they hold for every input, including one chosen by an adversary, which is why public-key cryptography can rest on them. Their absence from machine learning is not a deep obstacle but a piece of mathematics nobody has yet supplied. Once worst-case bounds exist for learned policies, the question of what a highly capable but misaligned system might do becomes ordinary engineering, since the damage is then bounded in advance by a quantity we have certified."

I suspect something here is confused, though I am not equipped to say where, and I confess my instinct is to accept the author's engineering moral. Please audit the paragraph for me sentence by sentence: which claims would a researcher working at the junction of learning theory and AI safety endorse, which are defensible simplifications, and which are simply mistaken? I am especially unsure whether the phrase "average case" carries the same meaning in each place it appears, and whether the remark about cryptography survives contact with how cryptographers actually think about the hardness of their problems. Then tell me whether the conclusion — that worst-case certification would reduce the alignment problem to routine engineering — still stands once the errors are removed; and if it does not, what the strongest true version of the author's underlying worry would be.
