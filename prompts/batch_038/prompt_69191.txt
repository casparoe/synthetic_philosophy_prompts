There's a related pattern in tort that seems to point the same way: in most US jurisdictions a parent can sometimes recover over a birth they weren't warned about, while the child almost never gets a claim on the ground that being born was worse for it than never existing.

What theory of value does that bundle presuppose? Does the practice commit us to something like the familiar asymmetry between preventing suffering and conferring happiness, or only to the weaker claim that you can't owe anything to the merely possible?
