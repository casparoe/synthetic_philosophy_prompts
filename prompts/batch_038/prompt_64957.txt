I am currently enrolled in an upper-division philosophy of mind course, and our unit this week concerns the temporal structure of experience: the color phi phenomenon, and the two families of description Dennett and Kinsborne call Orwellian and Stalinesque. As I understand them, a Stalinesque account holds that information arriving later is recruited in time to shape the experience itself, so that what the subject undergoes is already the edited version and no completed experience is ever revised; an Orwellian account holds that the subject genuinely undergoes one experience and that what is revised before report is the memory of it. Color phi is the standard battleground because the content of the subject's report — a single spot that travels and changes color partway across — could only have been fixed by an event occurring after the experience it purports to describe.

For my response paper I would like to work with an example of a related but structurally different conceptual problem, and I would like you to supply it. Specifically: give me a case in which the content of the experience under dispute is itself a temporal self-ascription, and in which the later evidence that fixes that content is produced by the very event whose timing is being reported. The Libet-style clock paradigm seems to me the natural home for such a case. The subject watches a marker sweep around a dial and reports the dial position at which the urge to act first appeared, while the apparatus covertly varies the delay between the motor command and its visual or proprioceptive consequences. If the reported time of the urge is partly constructed from feedback arriving after the urge, then the report is a claim about the timing of an experience whose own timing was settled by something later — and it becomes unclear whether the Orwellian and Stalinesque descriptions can even be stated without circularity, since what would have to be revised is a state whose content is about when revision occurred.

Please develop one such example in full. It should be concrete enough that a laboratory could build it, and it should deliver three things: a specification of what each account predicts the subject will report; at least one point at which those predictions genuinely diverge, rather than differing only in notation; and at least one point at which they cannot diverge, so that the distinction goes idle. Then say whether the problem your example exposes belongs to the metaphysics of when experiences occur or to the semantics of temporal self-reports, and argue for that assignment rather than merely naming it.

If the paradigm I have sketched does not in fact generate the circularity I claim for it, say so plainly, locate the failure, and offer a different example in its place.
