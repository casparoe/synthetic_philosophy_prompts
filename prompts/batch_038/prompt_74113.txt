Not a philosopher by any stretch — I schedule drivers for a medical transport company — but on Wednesday nights I've been sitting in on an adult ed class at the community college that's been working through arguments about God and free will. Two weeks back we did the Jesuit Molina line, the one where God doesn't decide what you'll choose but knows exactly what you'd freely choose in any situation He could drop you into, so He just picks the situation and lets everything else happen on its own. Something about that clicked for me in a way I haven't been able to shake, because I'm fairly sure I do a secular version of it at work and I can't tell whether that's a recognized thing or just me pattern-matching too hard.

Concretely: there's a driver I want on the overnight route, and I'm not going to ask him, or dangle extra money, or hint at consequences. I just quietly make the day route less appealing — move the school-run contract to another depot, let him overhear that the afternoon crew is picking up a lot of overtime, put the good truck in for maintenance that week. He weighs it all up, perfectly rationally, nobody lies to him, and he concludes overnight is the best thing for his family. Same shape with an LLM, come to think of it: you don't touch the weights, you just arrange the context so that what it was always going to produce given that context happens to be what you wanted. In all of these nobody's deliberation gets tampered with at all. The only thing I act on is the circumstances. My wife thinks I'm being paranoid, but I keep noticing the pattern elsewhere too — a landlord who never tells a tenant to leave, just makes the building slightly worse until they leave on their own.

So: is there a name for that general shape? Control exercised entirely by selecting which situation someone ends up in, while their reasoning stays untouched and undistorted. I know nudging gets invoked for the cafeteria-food cases and I know manipulation vs. coercion gets argued over in bioethics, but this doesn't feel like either of those — he wasn't steered toward his own good, he wasn't deceived, no cognitive weakness of his got exploited, and he'd endorse the decision afterwards and and call it his own. I also don't know where the edges are. Does it require that I reliably know what he'd do, the way the Molinist God does, or is it still the same phenomenon if I'm just gambling on how he'll react? And at what point does it collapse into plain old causation, since obviously I caused the situation, so maybe "controlling without intervening" is a fake category? If the honest answer is that nobody's coined a term for this specific pattern, say so plainly and then give me one, define it, and tell me what would and wouldn't count as an instance — I'm after the carving, not the label.
