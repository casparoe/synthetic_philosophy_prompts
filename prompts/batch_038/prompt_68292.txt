Last night a friend and I spent three hours arguing about heritability, and I came away unsure whether the position I was defending is even internally coherent. I am not looking for a tutorial — I can define narrow-sense heritability and I know the standard caveats. What I want is an audit of a specific package of claims I committed to out loud, in the order the argument happened to run.

(1) Heritability is a ratio of variances — additive genetic variance over total phenotypic variance — indexed to a particular population within a particular range of environments. It is a claim about the sources of differences among people, not about the composition of a trait within a person, and I refused to accept any phrasing of the form "your intelligence is sixty percent genetic."

(2) Consistently with that, I held that a trait with a heritability of 0.9 can be far more responsive to environmental intervention than a trait with a heritability of 0.2, and that anyone who slides from a high heritability estimate to a claim about the futility of intervention has made a plain error.

(3) I held that high within-population heritability is fully compatible with a between-population difference that is entirely environmental — the familiar seed-tray argument. But I insisted the blockage runs in both directions: a low within-population heritability likewise licenses no conclusion that a between-population difference is genetic.

(4) I said twin-based estimates should be read as upper bounds, because gene–environment correlation, particularly the evocative and active varieties, means genetically influenced traits recruit environments that then amplify them, and the twin design cannot separate the amplification from the initial genetic contribution. I added that the equal environments assumption fails in precisely this way: identical twins are treated more alike, and are treated more alike partly because they already behave more alike.

(5) In the same breath I cited the well-documented rise in the heritability of general cognitive ability from early childhood to adulthood as evidence that genetic influence genuinely strengthens across development, and I explained it by exactly the mechanism invoked in (4): people increasingly sort themselves into environments matched to their propensities.

(6) I said the gap between twin-based and SNP-based heritability estimates for behavioural traits is mostly a confounding artifact, which I offered as further support for (4). But I also said that a substantial gap is expected on independent methodological grounds — arrays tag common variants imperfectly, and rare variants and non-additive effects are picked up by the twin design but not by the SNP design — and that the gap has been narrowing as genotyping improves.

(7) I rejected "innate" as a synonym for "heritable," offering the usual counterexample that the number of fingers a person has is a paradigmatically innate trait with near-zero heritability, since almost all of its variance is accidents and prenatal insult. But when pressed about eye colour I said that eye colour is both highly heritable and genuinely innate, and that nothing in my previous remark rules that out.

(8) Finally, I said that although heritability is irreducibly a statement about variance across a population, an individual's polygenic score can be a legitimate and increasingly informative individual-level prediction, and that there is no inconsistency in holding both this and (1).

We ended with two accusations on the table: that (2) is an evasion nobody is entitled to while also appealing to (5), and that (4) and (5) simply cannot both be true. I am not convinced either lands, but I cannot say why, and I suspect at least one of them is gesturing at something real even if the diagnosis is wrong. Please do not take either accusation for granted, and please do not confine yourself to the pairs I have flagged — I may have missed the worst one.

So: go through the eight and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Rank the genuine ones by severity rather than listing them flat. For each, say what specifically has to be surrendered or reformulated, and what the reformulation costs me — in particular whether any of them forces me to give up something I need for the anti-determinist half of the argument, which was the point of the whole conversation. Where I am collapsing two distinct notions, name both and say which of my claims survives under each. And where a move of mine was actually clean, say so outright instead of hedging, because part of what I want to know is which pieces I can keep saying without qualification.
