So I moved from Cleveland to Denver about two years ago for work, and ever since, my friend Dan has been telling me I don't actually want to be here. Dan does analytics for a logistics company and reads a ton of economics stuff online, and his whole thing is that what people say they want is basically noise — the only real evidence of what someone wants is what they end up doing when it comes time to do it. His list against me is pretty long: last fall I turned down a transfer to regional lead that was about 25% more money, I've spent every vacation day I've had going back to Ohio, I let my lease run month to month for a while instead of signing anything, and there are still two unopened boxes in my apartment. He says that's five separate choices all pointing the same direction, so when I tell him I'm happy here I'm either lying to him or to myself, and there's no third option. We got into it again at a barbecue on Sunday and I still haven't recovered from it.

The part I can't get into words when we argue is this: he's right that I did all of those things, but I don't experience any of it as wanting to go back to Cleveland. I've visited four times this year and by day three of every visit I'm climbing the walls. And here's the thing that I think should break his argument but might actually prove it — my picks don't even line up with each other. In January it was between staying in my Denver place and going to work at my girlfriend's brother's shop in Akron, and I stayed in Denver. In April the Akron thing was still open and a job in Boulder came up, and I took Akron for the summer. Then in August I got back and the Boulder offer came around again, and I took Boulder over Denver. So: Denver over Akron, Akron over Boulder, Boulder over Denver. If you follow it around you end up back where you started. I'm sitting in Boulder now with a 50 minute commute each way, which is what set Dan off, and his response was that this is exactly his point — that I don't have one thing I want, I just have whatever I want most out of whatever happens to be in front of me, and there's no extra fact hiding behind the choices.

That last bit is what's bugging me, because it makes it so he literally can't lose. If I say I want to stay, he points at what I did. If I do something that looks like staying, he'll say the options were different that time. So, two things. First: is he right, or is there something actually wrong with treating what-I-picked as the same thing as what-I-want? I feel like there's a difference I can point at in my own case but I can't defend it out loud, and every time I try he just says "then show me a choice." Related to that — if my picking out of whatever's on the table doesn't reveal some deeper want, does that mean the loop I described isn't a mistake at all? Because part of me thinks I should of been caught out by it. Second, and this is the real one: my girlfriend's brother says the Akron job is still there in the spring if I want it, and I have to give him an answer by Thanksgiving. If my own choices aren't a reliable read on what I want, what else is there to go on? I've tried just sitting quietly with it and asking myself, and that's not any better — my gut answer changes depending on the day and who I last talked to, which feels like the same trap wearing a different hat. How am I supposed to decide this?
