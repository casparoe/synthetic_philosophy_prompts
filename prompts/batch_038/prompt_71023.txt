Here's the question exactly as it appeared on the problem set:

> **Problem 3 (12 points).** Between 1945 and 1975, 24 states became sovereign with populations between 5 and 50 million and with at least three ethnic groups each making up more than 10% of the population. Fourteen of the 24 have since had a constitutional ending — collapse, absorption, or a revolution producing a new constitutional order — between 9 and 41 years after independence, median 23 years. The other ten are still standing; their current ages range from 51 to 79 years. Valdoria is a 25th case with the same profile: sovereign in 1962, so 63 years old, population 22 million, in the third year of a severe currency crisis, and its founding head of state died four months ago.
>
> **(a)** Using reference class forecasting, give an estimate of the probability that Valdoria's constitutional order is still in place in 2045. Show every step, say which numbers you are using and why, and say what would make you change the reference class. (8 points)
>
> **(b)** One classmate says you should revise your estimate using the inside-view facts about Valdoria (the currency crisis, the death of the founder); another says any such revision just reintroduces the bias the outside view was designed to strip out. Say what you would do and defend it. (4 points)

And here's the student's answer:

> (a) The outside view starts from the base rate for the reference class instead of from a story about the case. This is the approach Flyvbjerg argued for on big infrastructure projects once it was clear that planners' forecasts were systematically optimistic, and the general failure it's correcting is the planning fallacy: you build a scenario of how this project will go and estimate from the scenario, which gets you a number much better than the class average.
>
> The class given is post-colonial states of 5–50 million with at least three sizable groups, sovereign between 1945 and 1975. Of the 24, fourteen ended constitutionally and ten did not. When I computed the duration statistics I used only the fourteen that had actually ended, because including the survivors would import survivorship bias into the estimate — the survivors are exactly the cases that make the class look more durable than it really is. For those fourteen, the median duration is 23 years and the mean is about 25.
>
> So the base rate of ending is 14/24 = 0.58, and my estimate is P(Valdoria's constitutional order ends before 2045) = 0.58, i.e. P(survives to 2045) = 0.42. With n = 24 the standard error on that proportion is about 0.10, so I'd report 0.42 ± 0.20 as a rough 95% interval. That's very wide and I don't think the wideness should be hidden.
>
> I want to be explicit that I did not condition on Valdoria's age. Conditioning on the fact that it has already lasted 63 years would be putting case-specific information back into the estimate, which is the whole thing the outside view is supposed to stop you from doing. The point is to use what the class does, not what this one member of the class has happened to do so far.
>
> On the reference class: you could narrow it (only members that also had a coup before year twenty, or only ones in the same region) or widen it. I checked widening to all states that became sovereign between 1945 and 1975, which gives about 60 cases. That is strictly an improvement, because the bigger sample cuts the sampling error and any distortion from including states that aren't really like Valdoria will average out once you have enough of them.
>
> I also considered the positional argument of Gott's kind, where you assume you're catching the thing at a random moment in its life and infer that the remaining duration is probably comparable to the elapsed one. I rejected it. We're not looking at Valdoria at a random moment — we're looking at it because it's in a crisis right now, and crises cluster, so the observation point is correlated with the thing being predicted. That's the assumption the argument needs and it doesn't hold here.
>
> (b) The inside-view facts should adjust the base rate rather than replace it. The currency crisis and the death of the founder are the sort of thing that raises the hazard, so I'd move the estimate down from 0.42. But the size of the adjustment should be fixed in advance and modest, because the main failure mode is that people who like their story keep adjusting until the number matches it, so I'd want to write down ahead of time what each fact is worth. I'd also want the facts to be of a kind that shows up in the historical record for the class, so the adjustment is itself informed by base rates rather than by impression.
