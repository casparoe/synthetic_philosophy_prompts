I've been sitting in a jurisprudence reading group where the Hart–Fuller thing gets re-litigated every autumn and nobody has moved since 1958. My own angle is empirical: I've hand-coded around sixty legal systems on Fuller's desiderata — generality, promulgation, non-retroactivity, clarity, and above all congruence between what officials announce and what they actually do — and correlated those scores against survey measures of whether people obey rules when nobody is watching. The correlation is strong. Trouble is both my advisors claim it. The positivist says it just shows the desiderata are a decent description of state capacity, which is exactly what a separable, morally neutral concept of law should track. The Fullerian says it shows legality and morality cant come apart, since the systems that fail the desiderata are precisely the ones where law stops generating any felt obligation at all. Same table, two victories, and neither of them seems bothered.
