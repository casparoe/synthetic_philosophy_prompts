I'm giving a paper at a quant history / applied micro seminar next month. The room is basically econometricians who live and breathe identification strategy, plus two comparative politics types who favour process tracing, and no philosophers at all. My argument is that the great-man-versus-structures fight is a category error: a structural explanation isn't a rival to an individual one, it's a claim about how elastic an outcome is to swapping a different person into a given role, and that elasticity is estimable. Sketch: (1) any claim of the form 'X happened because of Y' commits you to some substitution set over which the outcome would have come out differently; (2) structural conditions set the size and shape of that set rather than competing with it; (3) near-miss events — sudden deaths in office, botched assasinations, unexpected appointments — give you something close to random assignment into 'the individual changed, the structures didn't', so you can bound the elasticity across matched cases.
