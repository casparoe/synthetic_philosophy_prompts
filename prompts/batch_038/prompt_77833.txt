My brother moved back to our hometown about two years ago and has basically engineered his life to remove surprises. Same breakfast and same dinner every day, same route to the same job, gym at 6:15, most of his clothes bought in multiples so nothing ever has to be decided, noise-cancelling headphones whenever he's in public, no travel, no last-minute invitations. He calls it removing the unecessary variables. My parents are convinced he's depressed and want to stage some kind of intervention.

Here's the thing though: he doesn't seem depressed. He's not sad, he's not really withdrawn from us, he says he's the calmest he's been in his whole life and that his work actually got better since he started doing this. If you gave him the standard screening questions he'd sail through them. He's not white-knuckling it, he's genuinely content, and he can explain exactly why in a way that's kind of hard to argue with.

Now we're taking a two week trip together, first time just the two of us in years, and he wants every hour of it booked in advance — trains, restaurants, the museums, all of it — on the grounds that unplanned days are just stress with extra steps. I want a few days with nothing on them. He thinks that's a childish want, and honestly part of me thinks he might have a point.

So two questions. First: is there an actual principled argument that a life organized around eliminating unpredictability is a worse life, or are my parents and I just projecting our own temperament onto him? If he isn't suffering and nothing bad is coming of it, what exactly is the harm supposed to be? Is "he's missing out" even a real objection or just a mood?

Second: is his project internally coherent? My gut says you can't actually get rid of the unexpected, you just relocate it, and that the parts of his life he's left alone are the ones still generating it — but I can't get that into a shape I could say to him without sounding like I'm lecturing him from a podcast.

I dont want a "sit him down and validate his feelings" answer. I want to know whether I'm the one who's wrong here.
