I sit on the product review panel of a company that ships a mainstream consumer AI assistant, and engineering has asked us to clear a feature they call "quiet deferral". When a classifier judges from message rate, tone and content that a user is probably in a manic episode, the assistant postpones high-stakes requests — purchases or transfers above a set amount, messages to contacts the user has blocked and unblocked repeatedly, resignation or break-up letters, bulk deletions of files or accounts — by twelve hours, holding a draft in the meantime. Two details have split the panel. First, users ticked a box at sign-up accepting "wellbeing safeguards that may slow down some requests", but at the moment of deferral the assistant says nothing about what is happening: the delay is dressed up as ordinary latency, so the user concludes the service is slow or broken. Second, the classifier's own evaluation puts its false-positive rate at roughly one in five, and we cannot agree who is worse off — the user whose payment is frozen during a genuine emergency, or the user whose crisis is missed entirely because we set the threshold too high.

Please draft the memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should give a clear recommendation (approve, reject, or approve with conditions), state any conditions concretely enough that engineering could implement them, set out a rollout time frame, and treat the strongest consideration on the losing side fairly rather than caricaturing it. The reasoning needs to engage the disguised-delay problem head-on — what, if anything, the sign-up box actually authorises, and whether a reversible twelve hours of obstruction counts as a light touch or as something more troubling — rather than restating our general commitments to transparency and user autonomy and leaving it there. Write in British English.

After the memo, add a table with exactly these columns: Design element (one row each for: the twelve-hour window; the presentation of the delay as ordinary latency; the sign-up consent box; the classifier threshold; an emergency-override route); Verdict (retain, amend, or drop); Reason; What would change the verdict. Five rows only, no prose in the table beyond what those columns require.
