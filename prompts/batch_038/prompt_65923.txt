Ok so I spent like three hours last night arguing with my roommate about consciousness and I need somebody to make my side better than I made it at 1am.

He's a cog sci major and he thinks Block's phenomenal/access distinction shows the whole field is measuring the wrong thing. His line: every experiment that claims to be about "conscious experience" — including that big adversarial collaboration where the integrated information people and the global workspace people pre-registered their rival predictions against each other and then scanned people looking at briefly presented images for varying durations — bottoms out in report, discrimination, confidence ratings, whatever. And report is access by definition. So at best those studies test A-consciousness, and if P-consciousness is genuinely something else (that's the whole point of the distinction, the zombie possibility and all that), then none of that data touches phenomenality and the "results challenge both theories" headline is just noise. He thinks the neural correlates enterprise is secretly about access while advertising itself as being about experience.

My side, which I articulated badly: I think that's backwards. Global broadcasting was never supposed to be a theory of information becoming available to the reasoning systems. It's a claim about what an experience IS — content that is broadcast in such a way that it can be attended to, compared, and talked about. Availability isn't a rival to phenomenality or a downstream byproduct of it; it's what phenomenality consists in. Everybody treats the workspace story as the paradigm case of an access theory, and I think that mislabeling is what manufactured the problem my roommate is now using to declare the field bankrupt.

Please argue for my position. Specifically:

1. Make the case that broadcasting is constitutive of phenomenal character rather than merely correlated with it — i.e. that nothing can be globally broadcast without there being something it's like to have it.
2. Handle the obvious counterexample class: rich phenomenology nobody can report. The gist of a crowded scene, partial-report paradigms where people clearly saw more than they could name, dreaming, driving home on autopilot. If broadcasting constitutes experience, all of that comes out non-phenominal, which sounds absurd. Bite the bullet or find a line that isn't ad hoc, and say which.
3. Show exactly where his inference breaks: why "the dependent variable was a report" doesn't license "only access was measured."
4. Tell me what would actually falsify my view, and whether it collapses into a flat identity of P and A or into something with the direction of explanation reversed — access explained in terms of phenomenality rather than the other way around. Pick one and defend it.

Write the whole thing in American English. No bibliography, no reading list, no name-dropping papers — I just want the argument.
