I facilitate a monthly evening workshop for about 18 adults at a public library — mixed ages, no philosophy background — and I have 90 minutes. The topic is echo chambers and epistemic bubbles, but almost every exercise I have seen on this depends on somebody circulating falsehoods, and that is not the case I care about. I want an activity in which a community ends up confidently and collectively wrong while every statement anyone makes is true: no participant is assigned a deceiver, troll, or bad-faith role, and there is no payoff for misleading anyone. The distortion must emerge entirely from who speaks, what gets passed along, and what gets rewarded with attention.

Concretely, I would like the group split into several "channels," each of which receives a set of individually accurate reports about a fictional municipal pilot scheme and must decide which few to forward to a shared board, with a lightweight scoring currency awarded for the reports other channels find striking. Each channel ends by committing to a numerical estimate and a recommendation, which I compare against ground-truth figures I hold.

Please provide: the fictional setting and its ground-truth numbers; the card content, or a template I can use to generate it; exact rules and a timed run sheet for 90 minutes; the scoring scheme and what the currency buys; and a materials list assuming only paper, pens, and a whiteboard. The debrief should map particular moments in the play onto the two distinct things going wrong — the non-random sample each channel happens to see, and the further skew their forwarding choices introduce — and should let me show numerically how much of each channel's final error came from each source. Tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time this is run with a group like mine, including what to do if one channel spots the sampling problem within ten minutes and the effect collapses. Finally, be frank about which feature of real information environments this design cannot reproduce, and that I will have to supply myself in the discussion.
