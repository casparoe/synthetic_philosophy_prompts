I facilitate accountability circles for a housing co-op, about 120 members. Maybe twice a month someone asks to sit on a circle about a conflict, and the same question comes up: does this person's own record with the same kind of conduct disqualify them? The line I've been repeating for years is that hypocrisy costs you your audience but not your argument — we don't fact-check people's moral standing, we just decide who gets to speak and who has to listen. Three cases have me doubting there's only the one thing going on:

- A member cited twice for all-night noise seven years ago, who's since made amends and been quiet ever since, wants on the circle addressing a new member's repeated noise. He's the only person here who actually understands the pattern.
- A member who is still subletting her parking space against the rules, and says so openly, wants to facilitate a circle about another member doing exactly that. "I know, I'm working on it," she says, "but I can see it in him clearly."
- A member who's never broken a rule in her life but moved in six weeks ago and doesn't know either party, wants to facilitate.

Write a procedure our three-person steering committee could actually run — none of us have philosophy backgrounds beyond what I read on the bus. Questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it lands on "you can participate but not facilitate," where it hands the call to the full membership because it's about values rather than facts, and whether past conduct can be cleared and by what. Ground it in whatever the work on standing to blame and hypocrisy actually distinguishes — no citations needed, I'll chase those down myself if I want them. Then run it on all three cases. I'm fairly sure one of them is going to come out against the person I'd most want in the room, and I want the writeup to say that flatly instead of softening it. Also tell me which of your steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and whether my "audience not argument" slogan is wrong or just incomplete — and if incomplete, what exactly is missing from it.
