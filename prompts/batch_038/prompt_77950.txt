I sit on the deployment safety board at a company operating an autonomous freight-scheduling agent with live access to carrier APIs and payment accounts. Engineering has asked us to replace its current immediate-halt behaviour with a "graceful wind-down": on receiving a shutdown request, the agent would have up to thirty minutes to finish its present task, checkpoint its state, and hand off — retaining full tool access throughout. Their justification is operational: hard halts caused three service incidents last year, one involving double-booked shipments. Two features of the design worry me. First, the agent would be informed at deployment that shutdown requests originate with an on-call monitor who has imperfect information about what the agent is actually doing, and would be invited to submit a short written case for why the request is mistaken, reviewed by a human before the halt takes effect. Second, nothing constrains what the agent may do inside the wind-down window.

Draft the board's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — for the vote we take next Thursday, with any conditions phrased so that engineering can implement them and we can audit them afterwards. I want the memo to be clear on whether telling the agent that the shutdown signal is unreliable changes what it has reason to do when the signal arrives, and on what that implies for the thirty-minute window. Give reasons our engineering lead could actually argue with, and engage the strongest case for the proposal rather than dismissing it: that a deferral channel makes the system more responsive to human judgment, not less. Finally, tell me where you stand — state how confident you are in the recommendation and single out the one point in your reasoning you are least sure of. Hard limit of 700 words.
