**The exam question (Question 3, 25 points).**

*(a) (5 points)* State Harrigan and Spekkens' distinction between ψ-ontic and ψ-epistemic ontological models as precisely as you can. What exactly does the distinction quantify over?

*(b) (8 points)* Pusey, Barrett and Rudolph argue that ψ-epistemic models are ruled out. Sketch the argument, and identify every assumption it requires beyond the requirement that the model reproduce the quantum probabilities. Does the argument succeed? Describe the principal routes of escape that have been proposed and say what each one costs its proponent.

*(c) (7 points)* Consider the following argument, stipulated for the purposes of this question. Fix a single qubit and an ontological model for it.

1. The model satisfies λ-mediation: for every measurement, the probability of each outcome is a function of the ontic state λ and of the measurement performed, and of nothing else.
2. |0⟩ and |1⟩ are orthogonal and the model reproduces the quantum probabilities exactly; therefore the distributions over Λ induced by preparing |0⟩ and by preparing |1⟩ have disjoint supports.
3. Inspecting the reasoning in (2), nothing in it appealed to the particular pair of states considered, but only to the fact that the two preparations are associated with distinct pure states; therefore the supports of the distributions induced by any two distinct pure states of a qubit are disjoint.
4. Therefore every ontological model of a qubit is ψ-ontic, and no assumption concerning independently prepared systems is needed anywhere.

Identify the first step that fails and explain why it fails. What does the failure of this argument tell us about why Pusey, Barrett and Rudolph need the further machinery they use?

*(d) (5 points)* Where do Spekkens' toy model and QBism fall with respect to the ψ-ontic/ψ-epistemic distinction, and why?

**The student's answer.**

*(a)* The framework assumes a space Λ of ontic states, which are supposed to be the physical states the system really is in, and each preparation procedure associated with a pure state |ψ⟩ induces a probability distribution μ_ψ over Λ. A model is ψ-ontic if every ontic state is compatible with only one pure state, equivalently if any two distinct pure states induce distributions with disjoint supports. It is ψ-epistemic if at least one ontic state is compatible with more than one pure state, equivalently if some pair of distinct pure states induces distributions that overlap on a set of nonzero measure. In the ψ-epistemic case the quantum state can be read as a state of knowledge about λ rather than as a constituent of λ, which is why Harrigan and Spekkens connect the distinction to Einstein's complaint that quantum mechanics is incomplete.

*(b)* Beyond reproducing the statistics, PBR needs λ-mediation and preparation independence. Preparation independence says that when two systems are prepared by separate, independently chosen devices, the joint distribution over the pair's ontic states factorizes into the product of the distributions for each system. The argument then takes two systems, each prepared in one of |0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, |−⟩, and considers the four joint preparations |0⟩|0⟩, |0⟩|+⟩, |+⟩|0⟩, |+⟩|+⟩ together with a single entangled measurement on the pair having four outcomes, arranged so that each outcome is orthogonal to exactly one of those four product states. Since each outcome has quantum probability zero for one of the preparations, λ-mediation forces the corresponding response function to vanish on the support of that preparation's distribution; if the four distributions shared any ontic state, that state would have to give probability zero to all four outcomes of a complete measurement, which is impossible. Hence there is no common overlap, and a generalization to n systems yields disjointness of supports for distinct pure states. So yes, given the assumptions, ψ-epistemic models are ruled out.

The main escape is to deny preparation independence, and the standard example is Bohmian mechanics, where the physical state of a pair includes the wavefunction of the whole, so two systems prepared independently in the laboratory are not independent at the level of λ. The cost is a theory that is explicitly nonlocal, though not one that permits superluminal signalling, which is why I do not think this is a devastating cost. A second escape is to reject the ontological models framework altogether. QBism does this: it denies that λ is an observer-independent physical state, so it is simply not in the market for an ontological model, and PBR is not addressed to it. Whether that counts as an escape or as a refusal to answer the question is, I think, genuinely disputed. A third is to look for ψ-epistemic models of restricted fragments of quantum theory rather than of the whole thing. Finally, even if one rejects preparation independence, later results due to Leifer and Maroney and to Barrett, Cavalcanti, Lal and Maroney show that maximally ψ-epistemic models must be measurement-contextual in dimensions of at least three, so the most attractive versions of the epistemic view are constrained independently of PBR's locality-flavoured assumption.

*(c)* The step that fails is (2). Step (2) is presented as following from λ-mediation plus exact reproduction of the statistics, but λ-mediation is precisely what preparation independence delivers in the two-system case: it is the assumption that λ does not retain a trace of which procedure produced it. Drop preparation independence and λ may depend on the preparation procedure, in which case the distributions induced by |0⟩ and by |1⟩ can overlap even though the two states are orthogonal, and (2) is false. That is what the failure shows about PBR: a single system is not enough, because a single system's ontic state can carry information about how it was prepared, and PBR needs the second system and the entangled measurement in order to force the ontic states of independently prepared systems apart. Step (4)'s claim that no assumption about independent preparations is needed is therefore the conclusion the whole argument was trying to avoid smuggling in.

*(d)* Spekkens' toy model is ψ-epistemic: its epistemic states are probability distributions over a set of ontic states, and distinct epistemic states overlap, which reproduces the nonorthogonality-like behaviour of quantum states and gives a natural account of no-cloning and of the information-theoretic restrictions the model imposes. It escapes PBR for the reason given in (c), namely that its ontic states do not factorize across independently prepared systems, so preparation independence fails in it. QBism, as I said, does not sit on either side of the distinction, because the distinction is defined over ontological models and QBism denies that quantum states are candidates for being either ontic or epistemic in that technical sense; its use of "epistemic" is the ordinary one, about an agent's beliefs.
