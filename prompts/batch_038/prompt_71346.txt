I write a fortnightly column on science and ideas, and I am currently drafting a long feature about authenticity — specifically, about what happens to a forgery's standing once everyone knows it is a forgery and it has nevertheless gone on doing real work in the history of art. My beat includes the psychology of how people judge objects' histories as well as the philosophy, so I would like your help fixing a number to the central claim, and I would like the reasoning behind the number laid out rather than the number alone. Please write in British English throughout.

The case I am using is stipulated rather than reported, because I have not found a real one that isolates the variable I care about. In 1928 an unknown painter produces a canvas in the manner of an established modernist and ages it convincingly. It is acquired, authenticated by the leading scholar of the period, exhibited, reproduced. Over the following thirty-three years it becomes one of the most discussed images of its moment: two movements identify it as a founding gesture, a generation of painters studies its handling of the ground, and it enters every standard survey. In 1961 the fraud is established beyond argument — the supposed author never painted it, the provenance was invented, the materials are of their true decade. The picture is not withdrawn. It stays on the wall under a new label, continues to be reproduced, and continues to be taught as the pivot it always was.

Now a counterfactual. In an otherwise identical world, the fraud is detected in 1929; the canvas is discredited, sold off, and effectively lost, having influenced almost nobody. Same hand, same materials, same deception, same thirty-three years of elapsed time. The only difference between the two worlds is what happened to the picture after it was made.

Here is the proposition I want a credence for, which I have tried to state so that it is true or false rather than merely interesting:

P: the forgery in the first world is aesthetically more valuable than the very same forgery in the second world.

Give me your number for P — not a range, and not a note about how the question is underdetermined, but the credence you would actually defend — and then decompose it. I want the sub-questions on which P turns, a rough probability for each with a sentence or two of justification, and then an account of how the terms combine into the final figure. Since they plainly are not independent, I would rather you said how you are handling the dependence than pretended to multiply. These are the terms I expect to see, though you should add, discard or replace any of them as you think fit:

- whether aesthetic value is a single property or a bundle — and if a bundle, whether P holds of the bundle as a whole or only of some of its components;
- whether facts about how a work came into being can bear on its value even when no difference in the work is perceptible to a suitably attentive viewer today;
- whether a work's subsequent history — its documented influence, the eyes it trained, the arguments it provoked — is the sort of thing that can bear on aesthetic value at all, or only on historical and market value;
- whether the intuitions people reliably report in duplication and forgery cases track something evaluative, or merely register a bias about origins that better-informed judgement would correct;
- whether public exposure subtracts anything that the ensuing influence fails to restore.

Then tell me which single term your final number leans on hardest, and what you would need to be shown — an argument, or some kind of finding about how people actually judge — to move that number by more than a few points.

Finally: my own credence is about 0.65. An art historian I interviewed is at about 0.1, on the grounds that influence is a property of art history and not of the work. Tell me where you think that gap actually sits — which of the terms above the two of us are disagreeing about, and how much of the remaining distance is a difference in what we each mean by the comparative in P rather than a difference in view.

I am not after a survey of the literature, and I would rather you supplied no references, titles or names: everything I need here is reasoning you can do without looking anything up, and I will handle the sourcing myself.
