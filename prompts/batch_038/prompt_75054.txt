Last week one guy in the group — who also used to run clusters for a living — made a move that basically took over the discussion, and I still can't tell whether it's brilliant or garbage.

He says a callosotomized patient is literally just a two-node cluster that lost its heartbeat link. Each hemisphere goes on serving its clients (a visual field, an ipsilateral hand, half the body's motor program) out of its own local state; the interconnect is gone, so the two can't reach consensus, and what you get is precisely the thing we called split-brain in ops — two divergent replicas, each behaving like it's the primary. Then he ran it backwards as well: ordinary unified consciousness is the same cluster with the link up, i.e. quorum consensus over a fast interconnect, so "one mind" isn't metaphysically special at all, its just an engineering property of bandwidth and arbitration. His payoff: the split-brain patient really is two subjects of experience, and people who resist that are just squeamish about counting.

Half the group says this is a terrible analogy that smuggles in everything it needs in order to prove its conclusion, but nobody could say exactly where it breaks, and I want to be able to.
