So I run a tiny podcast out of our department — basically me, a borrowed mic, and a seminar room — and next week's guest is a visiting metaphysician who works on dispositions. She's got an unusual package and I want to press that package rather than let her give me a field survey. Two claims do the work. First: masking is the general phenomenon and finking is just a special case of it, namely a masking case where the interfering thing also takes out whatever property was grounding the disposition, so we should stop treating them as two seperate problems. Second: the simple conditional analysis survives if, instead of Lewis's clause requiring the object to have some intrinsic character in common with the disposition, you relativize the stimulus to the circumstances that are relevant to the manifestation — the electro-finked glass and the packed glass are both just cases where the stimulus as originally described wasn't yet the manifestation-relevant stimulus.

Her reflex is to answer any counterexample I throw at her by saying it's obviously a masking case, which her account handles by construction, and I'd like the questions to close that escape hatch step by step instead of all at once. So: write me eight to ten questions in the order I should ask them, each with a follow-up tucked in right after it, and each follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give rather than to some answer I'm hoping for.

Things I want covered, at least one question apiece. Whether collapsing finks into masks costs her the very distinction that made the two worth seperating — in a fink the disposition is gone by the time it would have manifested, in a mask it's still there — and if everything is masking, what it comes to for her to say the packed glass is still fragile rather than only fragile-relative-to-being-unpacked. The intrinsicness clause: what her relevance-relativizing move adds over a plain ceteris paribus or normal-conditions clause, how she handles people who say some dispositions are extrinsic anyway so Lewis's clause was never the right fix, and whether she doesn't run straight back into the circularity worry, since you seem to need to know the disposition before you can sort relevant from irrelevant circumstances. The mirror-image failures, where the conditional comes out true but the object plausibly lacks the disposition because something external would produce the manifestation regardless — whether her fix needs yet another clause there, and if it does, whether she's quietly converging on the no-principle style view she tells people she rejects. Multi-track dispositions: how a single template copes when the stimulus conditions and the manifestations are plural and open-ended (struck, twisted, shaken; shatters, splinters, collapses) without her listing them forever. And graded dispositions — what "more soluble than" amounts to on a circumstances-relative account, given it looks like it has to be some measure over circumstances and I can't see what fixes the measure non-arbitrarily.

Then I want one question aimed at the powers theorist or dispositional monist who thinks analyzing dispositions into conditionals is explaining the wrong thing entirely, especially when the microstructural bases she appeals to are themselves usually described in dispositional terms. And close with the blunt one: not "what are the open problems," but literally what case or argument would make her give the analysis up.

Please no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — I want it as continuous prose, the way you'd write me an email, with the questions spelled out in full and the follow-up right there in the next sentence. American English. And no "what first drew you to this topic" filler; she's fielded that a hundred times.
