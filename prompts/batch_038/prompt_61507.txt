I cover the neuroscience of volition and the free-will debate for a general-interest science magazine, and I have inhabited one philosophical commitment long enough that I want it stress-tested before I build a long essay on top of it.

The commitment: Frankfurt-style cases succeed. Where an intervener stands ready to force a choice but never acts, because the agent makes that very choice anyway, the agent is fully responsible — so responsibility does not require the ability to do otherwise. More than that, I take the deeper lesson to be that alternative possibilities were never doing the work our attributions seemed to rest on; what matters is the actual sequence, and whether it issues from a mechanism that is reasonably responsive to reasons. And I take this to defuse the intuitive engine of the consequence argument, not merely to dodge it.

What would count against that position? I want the map of its exposure, not a survey of the debate.

Specifically:

- The residual-alternatives line — that in every well-built Frankfurt case something still remains open to the agent (choosing on one's own, refraining from beginning to decide, forming or withholding a prior intention) — together with the reply that whatever survives is not robust enough to ground responsibility. Is there a version of this whose surviving alternative is robust without smuggling the conclusion back in, and what would it take to settle robustness non-question-beggingly? Would a successful robustness case be fatal, or merely embarrassing?

- The dilemma about the trigger sign: if the sign causally determines the choice, the case is just an ordinary deterministic scenario and proves nothing; if it does not, alternatives remain. Buffered designs were built to escape it. After several rounds of redesign, has either side produced a case the other accepts as a genuine counterexample? If not, does the stalemate itself count against my position, and how should I weigh an arms race in which every proposed case is met by a further refinement?

- The position's collateral commitments: what does it assume about causation by omissions and prevention, and about whether an intervener's non-intervention figures in the explanation at all? Would the case for Frankfurt-style reasoning weaken under a process theory of causation, or a contrastive account, or a view on which absences are not causes?

- The empirical leg, which is where my own reporting lives. The classic design assumes a neural precursor that reliably precedes and predicts the choice, readable in time for an intervener to act. If that precursor is better understood as stochastic accumulation of noise toward a threshold rather than the onset of a decision, then the thing the thought experiment asks us to read may not be the kind of thing that exists. Does that touch the argument at all, given that thought experiments do not owe neural implementations — and is the position better or worse off if the neuroscience stays permanently agnostic?

- What does the view predict that a principle-of-alternative-possibilities-friendly rival does not, and where can those predictions be checked outside the seminar? I have in mind practice rather than intuition: the duress standard in criminal law, framed around what a person of reasonable firmness in the actor's situation would have done; and cases of structural pressure where the agent would have acted identically anyway — the worker who would have taken the shortcut even without the bonus. An actual-sequence account calls those agents fully responsible. Tell me whether that is a cost, and how large.

Please sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each say whether a committed defender of the position would themselves concede it as counting against them or dismiss it as misdescribing the target — I want their concessions, not only their critics'. Then give me a straight answer on whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, whether its opponents enjoy the same immunity, and whether insulation of that kind is a defect for a claim of this sort or simply how this corner of philosophy works. Say what you think would move me most, even if it is not what would move the literature.

No bibliography, reading list, or citations — I have the papers. I want the analysis and the ranking.
