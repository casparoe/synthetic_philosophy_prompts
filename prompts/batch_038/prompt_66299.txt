I want to run one real episode through Braudel's three time scales and see whether either of them survives the contact.

The episode: the Panama Canal drought of 2023-24. As I understand it, the locks are gravity-fed out of Gatun Lake, which is also the drinking water supply for something like half of Panama; a dry season with an El Nino flavor dropped the lake; the Canal Authority cut the normal thirty-six-ish transits a day down in steps through the autumn to twenty-five and lower, added draft limits, and started rationing slots by auction, with one ship reportedly paying close to four million dollars for a slot in November 2023 against a usual transit cost well under a million. Correct me where I've got that wrong, but please don't let the analysis hinge on the exact figures.

First, do the sorting. Put the ingredients into his layers: the isthmus, the watershed, the rainfall regime; then the conjunctural middle (El Nino cycles, the 2016 expansion and whether its bigger locks made the water budget better or worse, the Suez alternative, the post-pandemic shipping glut); then the event layer (the individual advisories, the auctions, the anchorage queues). Say which layer is actually doing the causal work and which ones are just scenery.

Then the two things I think break it. One: the canal is an artifact, built 1904-14 and re-engineered in the 2000s, that has hardened into geography -- a slow structure that only exists becuase people keep maintaining it. Braudel's Mediterranean is a given basin; nothing in his scheme was built. So either man-made geography needs its own layer, or geographic time turns out to be very slow social time and the tripartition collapses into a continuum of speeds. Which, and what does that cost the theory? Two: the event-level account (bad rain, cautious managers, clumsy auctions) is complete without ever mentioning structures, and the structural account is only assembled afterward. Is it-was-the-longue-duree-all-along a claim that could be wrong, or does the lake just look structural because it drains slowly?

Take a position on both. Not a balanced survey of what Braudel said. And if you think structure-versus-event is the wrong lens for this episode, or that I've garbled him, say so up front rather than humoring me.
