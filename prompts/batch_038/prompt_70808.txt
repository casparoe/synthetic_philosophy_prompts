Write an essay in the metaphysics of causation, taking a settled position on a question that the case below is designed to make genuinely difficult. The case is invented; nothing in it corresponds to a real dispute, and you will not need any outside sources to handle it.

**The case**

A hill village keeps a communal water supply for its terraced gardens. A sluice gate at the head of a single open channel controls the flow from a reservoir; below the sluice the channel runs about a mile and a half before reaching the terraces. The sluice is worked by a hand winch, with a detachable crank kept in a store whose key hangs in the keeper's hut. A trust maintains the sluice and keeps a roster of three keepers, each obliged to open the sluice at first light on their appointed mornings.

On the morning in question the sluice stood shut, the channel ran dry, and the terraced beds withered beyond recovery. Fix the following as given.

1. The sluice and channel are the only source of water for those beds. There had been no rain for a fortnight and none fell that day.
2. A storm on the far side of the ridge overnight had, by first light, carried a landslip into the open channel some two hundred metres below the sluice, choking it completely. Nobody — not the keeper on duty, not any other resident — could have learned of the blockage or cleared it in time to save the beds that day. Had the sluice been opened at first light, the water would have ponded behind the slip, and the terraces would have gone without water just the same.
3. Ada was the keeper on duty. The roster obliged her to open the sluice that morning; she knew this, had been reminded the evening before that the winch was stiff and that the crank would be needed, was able-bodied, and had the crank within reach. She never went up to the sluice at all. No one else was under any obligation to open it that morning.
4. Bruno lives on the terrace below, was at home all day, and owns a rainwater butt holding several hundred litres, forty watering cans, and a barrow. Hand-watering for a single evening would have kept most of the seedlings alive. He owed nothing to anybody, was on no roster, had no reason to think the beds were at risk, and did nothing.
5. The beds died of drought alone; a surveyor's report filed a week later attributes the loss to the terraces having been denied water that morning.

**What the essay must do**

First, set out precisely how each of the following three families of view is meant to handle absences, and what verdict each delivers on the case. Describe the views rather than name-dropping them, and introduce any technical vocabulary you need:

- the view that absence claims are never literally true causal claims, on the ground that causation is a relation among goings-on with location and temporal extent, and that there was no going-on at the sluice that morning;
- the view that omissions are genuine causes, together with the familiar complaint that counterfactual dependence alone is far too cheap — dependence of the withering on countless non-events across the world — and that the theorist owes some principled selectivity;
- the view that causal statements are irreducibly contrastive, so that "Ada's failure caused the loss" is elliptical for a claim indexed to a contrast class of alternative situations.

Second, give your own verdict on each of three separate questions: did Ada's failure to open the sluice cause the loss; did Bruno's failure to hand-water cause it; and did the landslip cause it? Say explicitly whether your answers may diverge, and why divergence or uniformity is the mark of an adequate account.

Third, deal with the sharpest feature of the case: the blockage means that opening the sluice would have made no difference, so the difference-making intuition seems to fail where the intuition of authorship seems strongest. Explain what is really going on in the intuition that Ada caused the loss. Either it is a causal intuition and the difference-making account must be bent to accommodate it, or it is something else — a conflation of causing with blameworthiness, a sensitivity to norms and expectations, a pragmatic feature of how we talk about absences. Argue for one of these.

Fourth, state the strongest objection to your position and answer it. In particular, if you appeal to contrast classes, show what your account says about the same facts under the contrasts "rather than opening it" and "rather than letting the neighbours go unwatered as they ordinarily do," and explain why that variation is either a discovery about causation or a re-description of the problem.

**Form**

Write a continuous argumentative essay of roughly 1,500 to 2,500 words. Do not spend space restating the case. Do not conclude by surveying the options: commit to answers and defend them, though a conditional verdict — this about Ada, that about Bruno — is acceptable if you say what it is conditional on and argue that the condition obtains here.
