ok so I've been going round in circles on my Fitch's paradox paper and I want a sanity check before I commit to a thesis. My claim is that the Church-Fitch thing is secretly a KK paradox in disguise.

Here's the reasoning. The whole argument turns on taking some truth that nobody currently knows, conjoining it with its own unknownness, and then applying the knowability principle to that conjonction. But for anybody to ever come to know "p and no one knows p", they'd have to know the second conjunct too — they'd have to be in a position to know that they don't know p. And that's an introspection requirement, isn't it? Same species of assumption that gets rejected when people deny that knowledge is luminous or deny that knowledge iterates. So if you just refuse to grant it, doesn't the derivation lose its grip, and can't the Dummettian anti-realist hang on to unrestricted knowability after all? I quite like this framing because it makes the paradox not really about knowability at all but about second-order access, which feels like a much more interesting story than the usual ones.

Walk me through the steps that would have to fail for my objection to bite.
