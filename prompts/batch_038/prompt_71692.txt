Since my father died this spring I have taken on the job of digitizing his home movies: some forty reels of Super 8 and a shoebox of Hi8 cassettes, nearly all of it undated and unlabeled. The work has produced an obsession I did not anticipate.

On most of the reels I can identify the correct running direction within a second or two, and I can do it without any appeal to subject matter: smoke curling away from an ashtray, milk threading into black coffee, a sandcastle slumping under a wave, a dog shaking itself dry on a beach. On a handful of reels I genuinely cannot — a long static shot of a lake at dusk, a child on a garden swing filmed from directly underneath, a coin spinning on a patio table where the camera catches only the steady part of the spin. I have tested these on colleagues, who perform at chance on them and get the others right immediately.

Two things I have read recently seem to explain the pattern, and before I pass the explanation on to my daughter — she is fourteen, and has begun asking whether the fact that we can always tell which way a film runs proves that time itself runs that way — I would like to know whether it holds up.

The first is that time-reversal symmetry is genuinely violated in certain weak decays, so the fundamental physics is not perfectly reversible after all. The second is that the second law of thermodynamics is what gives time its direction. Taken together, these suggest that the asymmetry visible in my footage is a real dynamical asymmetry in nature: slight at the level of elementary particles, and enormously amplified at the level of smoke and milk. They suggest that the direction-ambiguous reels are simply the ones where the amplification has not gone far enough to register. And they suggest that the reverse of my father's death — the ashes reassembling, the illness retreating — is not merely improbable but forbidden.

What I cannot make work is the middle step. The particle-level asymmetry is described in everything I have read as minute, whereas the entropy produced by a single cigarette in a still room is described as vast, and no account I have found explains how one gets from the first to the second, or why the amplification should be so perfectly efficient. So my questions are these. Is the picture above roughly correct, with the amplification merely left implicit? And if it is, what are philosophers doing when they insist that none of this gets off the ground without a further assumption about the condition of the very early universe? That sounds to me like a restatement of what my own footage already shows rather than an independent postulate, and I cannot see what work it is supposed to be doing.
