Two researchers are to issue the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration on inference to the best explanation. One is a Bayesian critic, who holds that a rule telling an agent to believe the loveliest explanation of her evidence is either a redescription of what already happens when priors and likelihoods are conditionalized, in which case it is idle, or a genuine constraint that can override the probability calculus, in which case it is incoherent. The other is an explanationist, who holds that explanatory considerations are what fix the priors and likelihoods in the first place, so the rule cannot be tested against a calculus it constitutes, and that conditionalization admits of no non-circular justification.

Both accept the following as their shared test bed. H1, H2, and H3 are exclusive and exhaustive, with priors 0.10, 0.45, and 0.45. Evidence E has likelihoods 0.90, 0.40, and 0.40 under H1, H2, and H3. H1 is stipulated to be far and away the loveliest explanation of E: it derives E from a mechanism that also predicts two further phenomena, whereas H2 and H3 accommodate E as a brute coincidence. Evidence F is the joint observation of those two phenomena, each with likelihood 0.8 under H1 and 0.05 under H2 and H3, conditionally independent given each hypothesis.
