I'm about two-thirds through a novel set in a city built around a sealed brass chamber full of tiny magnetic beads — the locals call them grit — that rattle and clump against each other all day. When the grit settles into a stable clump, the clump stands for something, and the whole arrangement is a running argument the city has with itself about whether the clumps shove the grit around or the grit shoves the clumps. Before I commit to this in the book I want to try it as a standalone story, about 1,200 words.

Two things it has to hang on. First, one night when the clump-level prediction and the grit-level prediction of the same collision come apart, and somebody has to decide which one goes in the official log. Second, a clerk whos spent eleven years tending an identical chamber that was never powered up — grit frozen mid-clump, not moving, not going anywhere — and who maintains that its log is every bit as true as the running chamber's.

Rules I'd like you to hold yourself to: no character explains the philosophy in more than two sentences at a stretch, and the words emergence, reductionism, downward causation and substrate don't appear at all. End on something a person does, not something she realises. It should stay genuinely open which way the shoving runs, but I want to be able to tell from the clerk's entry which way she's betting. Everything you need is above — don't go hunting for a source text, this world is mine.
