So I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training at all, but our team had a very long Slack thread about mind uploading and I ended up writing down my actual position so people could poke holes in it. Here's where I landed:

1. What matters about surviving isn't identity, it's that someone downstream carries the thread — my memories, my habits, my unfinished projects. Who *is* me is basically just bookkeeping.
2. Scan-and-copy-and-incinerate: no. If a machine maps my whole connectome, boots an exact software copy somewhere else, and then vaporizes the original, I would not step into that thing. Something important gets lost even if the copy is perfect and wakes up insisting it's me.
3. Gradual replacement: yes. If you swap out one neuron at a time for a synthetic one that does the same job, over a couple of years, until there's nothing wet left and I'm still the same person with the same memories — I don't see a moment where I should get off the table. I'd sign up for that one.
4. The substrate doesn't matter. If the software version behaves like me, talks like me, and reports the same inner life I'd report, then it's conscious. I don't think carbon is magic.
5. If two copies of me are ever running at the same time, neither one is me. its not up for negotiation, there can't be two of me.
6. I don't trust my gut reactions to these cases very much, because I think they're mostly installed by sci-fi movies, so I try not to lean on "that just feels wrong" as evidence.

Can you check whether this set hangs together? I want to know which of these actually pull against each other and which ones only look like they conflict but are fine once you spell them out properly — I suspect at least one of the pairs you'd want to flag is a false alarm. For each real tension, tell me how bad it is, ranked, not all listed as equally broken, and what the cheapest change would be that fixes it and what I'd give up by making that change. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most damage overall.

I'm not attached to any of these in particular, I just want to know where the load-bearing one is, because I have a feeling I'm helping myself to something in one of them that another one flat-out rules out and I can't see it from the inside.
