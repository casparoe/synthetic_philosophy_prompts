What I want them to come away seeing, roughly in this order:
1. That a global rearrangement of everything is undetectable, and yet they may well insist it's a different world — and that this is precisely the joint the shift argument pries on.
2. That if spacetime points are real individuals with identities of their own, then a rearrangement confined to a bounded region generates a genuinely distinct possible history that is observationally identical to the actual one — so they cannot know which history they're in. That unknowing *is* the indeterminism; I don't want them to hear it as "the laws fail to determine stuff" in the abstract.
3. That the escape is to deny that those two histories differ, and that this escape has a price: points stop having identity independent of the fields on them.

Design the whole thing. A few specific things I want in it:
