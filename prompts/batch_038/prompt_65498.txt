Take the two 737 MAX crashes — Lion Air 610 in October 2018 and Ethiopian 302 in March 2019, 346 dead — and run them through the process-vs-outcome supervision distinction people argue about for AI. Outcome supervision = you grade the finished thing against a standard; process supervision = you check the steps that produced it, the design decisions, the reasoning.

Setup as I understand it, correct me where I'm wrong: the MAX needed bigger engines mounted futher forward, which changed how the airframe handled, so Boeing added MCAS to auto-trim the nose down in certain regimes — mainly so the jet would fly enough like the old 737 that airlines wouldn't need simulator retraining or a new type rating for their pilots. MCAS read a single angle-of-attack sensor, it wasn't described in the original flight manuals, and much of the certification work ran through an FAA delegation scheme in which Boeing's own designated employees performed the checks on the agency's behalf.

So map it. Who's the supervisor, who's the supervised agent, what counts as the outcome and what counts as the process — and where does the AI framing start to strain?
