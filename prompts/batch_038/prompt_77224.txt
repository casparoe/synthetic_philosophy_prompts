I run an empirical aesthetics lab: self-report and psychophysiological measures of affective response, transportation and narrative-engagement scales, inter-subject correlation of viewers' neural responses, and some work on emotional contagion. I have no philosophy training and came to Tolstoy's What Is Art? sideways, through a citation. Restate his theory in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can rather than as charitably as you can. I want the definition of art as the transmission of a felt state from one person to others by means of signs rendered as a claim about measurable affect transfer; his three factors governing the degree of infection — how individual the feeling is, how clearly it is conveyed, how sincerely the artist feels it — rendered as hypotheses about sender, signal, and receiver; and his condemnations of canonical works alongside his praise of folk song and parable rendered as predictions my methods could in principle test.

Then give me the residue report: which claims come out as empirical hypotheses, which survive only by being distorted into something he would disown, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all. For that last category, say whether the gap is mine — my framework cannot represent something real — or his: whether normative work is being done under the cover of a mechanism. And answer directly whether the appeal to the religious perception of an age can be operationalized at all, or whether it is the load-bearing non-empirical element around which the rest of the theory is arranged.
