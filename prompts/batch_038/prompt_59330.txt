I have been turning over, on my own and without any formal training, an idea that keeps surfacing in discussions of cooperation between negotiating systems: each side delegates its play in some interaction to a joint program meant to guarantee that nobody ends up worse off than they would have been by simply pursuing their own fallback plan, with the surplus then divided. Every concrete proposal I come across seems to break down somewhere different, and I would rather have the specification first. Write the desiderata — what a scheme of that kind has to deliver to count as adequate for the purpose of actually being deployed between parties who do not trust one another and do not know one another's payoffs. For each requirement, give the situation that motivates it: how the baseline is pinned down, and whether a party can improve its share by damaging its own fallback first; whether the guarantee must hold for every payoff the other side might turn out to have or only on average given one's beliefs about them; what revealing that you have committed does to the other party's incentives; what becomes of those who decline to join; whether the arrangement may be reopened once the real situation is known; and whether the program can be inspected or enforced at all. Then say which of these are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp, which single requirement rules out the most proposals, and — this is what I most want — whether any two of them cannot be satisfied together, in which case the list is itself the result.
