Construct a thought experiment designed to test whether the responsibility-gap thesis about autonomous systems depends on machine learning at all. The familiar version of the thesis holds that gaps open because a learning system's behavior outruns its designers' foresight; I want to know whether an equally stubborn gap can be generated by the structure of distributed compliance alone. Treat opacity as the one variable to be switched off, with everything else held fixed.

Specifications for the scenario:

- An ordinary institutional setting — a hospital, a water utility, a freight operator — using an automated system that is fully deterministic and employs no machine learning whatever. Every relevant decision rule is documented, version-controlled, and independently certified.
- One paragraph, ending in the death or serious injury of one identifiable person.
- No one is careless. The engineer who wrote the control logic, the vendor, the deploying institution's administrators, the operator on duty, the upstream supplier, the certifying body, and the regulator each act on good information, under budget and time constraints that are themselves reasonable, and in full compliance with every standard that applies to them. The gap must be generated by their conduct, not stipulated by their negligence.
- The conjunction that produces the harm must arise from each component doing exactly what it was certified to do — not from a freak coincidence, and not from anything a competent designer should have been expected to screen off.
- Adding a well-informed, well-motivated regulator must not close it.

Then, in order: for each candidate bearer — designer, vendor, institution, operator, supplier, certifier, regulator, and the organization as a collective agent — state what would have to be true for blameworthiness and show which condition fails. Say separately whether strict or vicarious liability would still place the cost somewhere, and whether that closes the moral gap or only the compensation gap. Name the single structural feature doing the work, and say what happens if you remove it: does the gap close, or merely relocate? Close with the strongest attack from someone who denies that responsibility gaps are real, and whether it succeeds. A case that excuses a party by stipulation rather than argument, or that smuggles opacity back in through the back door, fails.
