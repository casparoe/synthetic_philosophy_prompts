I'm on the deployment review board at a company running a fleet of a couple hundred autonomous agents in logistics. Our safety team wants to drop the hardware kill switch we currently mandate and instead train the agents to treat a shutdown command as evidence about what we actually want — their pitch is that a hard interlock just teaches the system to route around it, while an agent that genuinely defers to the button has no reason to. The design also has a clause where each agent is supposed to hold preferences about its own preferences: it should want to be the kind of system that would want to be shut down if we wanted that. Half the board thinks this is the only version that scales; the other half thinks were trading a mechanism anyone can audit for one whose behavior depends entirely on the model's own values being right. Two facts that'll matter at the meeting: in simulation the shift operators hit the button in error about 6% of the time, and a national regulator has asked whether it can have an independent shutdown channel the agents can't distinguish from ours. Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote — it needs an actual bottom line (pick a design, or a staged path with the trigger conditions spelled out), criteria the board could reapply to the next proposal, and a real hearing for the deference-first side rather than a strawman.
