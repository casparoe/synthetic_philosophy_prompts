I TA a second-year aesthetics seminar and every single year the same fight blows up in week three: somebody says they didn't bother seeing a film because it was sitting at 40% on the agregator, somebody else says that's insane because a number can't tell you a movie is good, and a third says of course it can, we learn everything from other people, why would this be special. The thing that actually convinces me is a distinction I made up on a bus and have never seen anyone else draw, so I want to stress-test it by watching it get bureaucratized.

The distinction: verdict testimony vs. salience testimony. Verdict testimony is being told the work is good — trust me, it's gorgeous — and my feeling is that this can't give you whatever it is you're missing, though I couldn't tell you whether the missing thing is knowledge, or justification, or just the experience. Salience testimony is being told *where to look*: watch the hands in the second scene, the mix is doing something sneaky with the drums, read it for the sentences not the plot. That kind seems to actually work, and I think it works because it changes what you perceive instead of stacking a belief on top of nothing. Which would mean the trouble with aesthetic testimony isn't that testimony is a bad pipe to some truth about beauty, it's that there's nothing at the far end of the pipe until you've perceived the thing, so the only testimony that helps is testimony about *how* to perceive it. Whether that's a real position or the same old problem wearing a hat, I genuinely don't know. Build the piece out of it and let it break if it breaks.
