Here's the package, claim by claim, as I understand it: (1) the physical stance is in principle complete — the state of the universe plus the laws fixes every detail — but computationally hopeless for anything the size of a person; (2) the design stance works because systems have functions, so you can predict them by assuming nothing's malfunctioning, without knowing the physics; (3) the intentional stance goes one level up again: treat the system as rational, work out which beliefs and desires a rational system in its situation ought to have, and predict from that; (4) beliefs and desires are real patterns rather than useful fictions, and the test is roughly that attributing them lets you predict or store the same information with vastly less than a pixel-by-pixel / bit-map style description would take; (5) that reality isn't just in the eye of the beholder, even though which patterns anyone bothers to name does depend on their interests; (6) the objection the group keeps raising — that if beliefs are only patterns in behavior they cant also be the inner states causing the behavior — and Dennett's response that once you've got the pattern there's no further fact to add.
