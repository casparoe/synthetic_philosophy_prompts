I am in my final year at a fairly competitive high school, and there is a pattern here that I have been trying for months to describe without finding the right words for it. I have read a little about costly signaling in animals — the idea that a peacock's tail advertises quality precisely because it is expensive to grow and dangerous to carry — and I believe what I am describing belongs to that family. But the part that matters most to me seems to be missing from the accounts I have found.

Two examples, both entirely ordinary ones.

First: the students who are most admired here are the ones who spend their summers doing unpaid research in someone's laboratory, often far from home, often producing nothing at all. When the school paper asks them about it, they invariably say that they were curious about the question, or that a relative's illness made them want to understand it; they never say that it will help them get into university. The audience is not deceived about the payoff — everyone knows a summer in a laboratory is a line on an application. Yet a student who says out loud that this is why she did it is judged differently afterwards, and the identical accomplishment is worth less. The word used about her is "strategic."

Second: a pianist at my school, considerably better than anyone else, declines to perform at assemblies, saying that she plays only for herself. When she is finally persuaded to play once, at another student's recital, the effect on the room is extraordinary. I am confident many people already suspected she was holding back on purpose. But had she announced in advance that she was holding back so that her single appearance would mean more, the effect would have been ruined — ruined for the very people who knew the plan all along.

My own rough formulation, which may well be wrong, is that the display has to look like something that was not sent. It is not that the audience must be ignorant; it is that the motive must not be acknowledged aloud, by anyone, even afterwards.

So: is there an established concept for this, and if there are several nearby ones, which fits best? I have also come across the argument that the highest-quality individuals signal least of all, on the grounds that signaling is what the anxious middle does, and I want to know whether my case is merely a version of that or something different, since mine does not appear to be about how good you are. Does anything comparable appear on the animal side, where I assume nothing can be pretended or confessed — or is an avowal rule possible only for creatures that can talk about their own motives? I would also like to know whether the following belongs to the same phenomenon or a separate one: as more students perform the disavowed impressive thing, it stops working, and the frontier moves toward costs that leave no paper trail, while those who over-avow are dismissed as trying too hard. And is there a principled reason the refusal-to-acknowledge rule should be rational rather than merely local etiquette — does admitting the motive genuinely change what the evidence supports, or only what politeness permits?

If nothing established covers this, please say so plainly, then give it a name and a definition along with its boundary cases: where it stops being this and becomes ordinary hypocrisy, ordinary modesty, or ordinary snobbery, and what would count as a clear non-instance. I am after the carving rather than the label, and I am not looking for a list of things to read.
