ok so me and my friend Dan were arguing about the Rietdijk–Putnam argument until about 2am and neither of us budged, so I want to outsource it.

Where we got to. The set-up: two observers drifting past each other, one headed roughly toward Andromeda. Their simultaneity slices are tilted relative to one another, and over a couple of million light years that tilt is worth several days — so a slice that is entirely future for me can be present-and-past for him. Then the argument proper: if "is real" has to be symmetric and transitive between observers, whatever is real for somebody is real simpliciter, and the whole of spacetime is a block with no becoming left in it.

Dan's at 0.85 that this works. I'm at 0.35.

His two moves. First: my attempt to relativise "is real" — treat it like "is visible from", so the transitivity step just fails and nothing contradicts anything — is changing the subject, becuase presentists want one non-indexed fact about what exists, not a family of observer-relative ones. Second: my fallback, a empirically undetectable privileged foliation, does no work at all, so it isn't a present, it's decoration. He calls it the epiphenomenal now.

My two replies. The visibility-style relativisation isn't a retreat, it's exactly what a relational metaphysics should say. And the foliation isn't idle structure anyway, since cosmology seems to hand you one — the CMB picks out a rest frame, and an expanding FLRW universe has a natural cosmic-time slicing. He says that's just a convenient coordinate choice doing metaphysical cosplay.

Give me your own credence on the proposition: no coherent view on which only the present is real can be made compatible with special relativity. Say which term it's most sensitive to, what single consideration would move you furthest, and where Dan and I are most likely actually disagreeing given that we both accept the physics.
