I am convening a doctoral reading group on Arendt's account of responsibility under dictatorship, and to keep us honest I want to see whether her framework can be applied to a case she never considered. Here is the track record we have reconstructed from the trial report and from the essay she later wrote on personal responsibility under dictatorship:

1. Eichmann: guilty and deserving of death, but not a fanatic, not a sadist, and not stupid. What she found in him was an almost complete inability to think from the standpoint of anyone else and an inability to speak except in clichés and officialese. Her word for this is thoughtlessness, and she insists it is not the same as stupidity.
2. Eichmann's claim that he was a small cog and that the machine, not the cog, should be condemned: rejected. If he was a cog, he was a cog in a machine that ought never to have existed, and the question put to a defendant is what he did.
3. Blame distributed across everyone — a whole people, a whole bureaucracy, an entire historical tendency: also rejected. Her view is that guilt spread over everybody amounts to the acquittal of everybody, and that only particular persons acting in particular roles can be held responsible.
4. The few who refused to participate: not heroes, and not people with better principles. What distinguished them was that they could not go on living with themselves afterward; the criterion was the self-dialogue, not the rulebook. She treats conscience as a by-product of thinking and as negative in force — it tells you what you cannot do and remain a friend to yourself, not what you ought to do.
5. Those who said they stayed in office to mitigate, to slow things down, or to keep worse men out of the post: treated with deep suspicion, as a story people tell themselves. She does not convert it into a separate charge of guilt, but she does not accept it as a defense either.

New case. A mid-level official in a deportation administration is responsible for scheduling transports and allocating rolling stock. In private he is neither inarticulate nor unreflective: his diary, published after the war, records plainly and without euphemism what the transports mean, quotes poetry, and sets down long arguments with himself at night. He never adopts the regime's ideology and mocks its slogans among friends. He nonetheless continues to sign. His stated reason is that if he goes a cruder man will do the work worse, and that from inside he can shave figures, keep some families on the same transport, and delay others. A colleague who requested transfer was reassigned to a supply depot with no lasting penalty. He sleeps badly, but he sleeps.

What I want from you: (a) predict her verdict on three separable questions — would she call this man banal, would she hold him responsible, and would she accept his mitigation; (b) identify which of her criteria the prediction turns on, and which rival criterion, equally consistent with the five verdicts above, yields the opposite answer; (c) give your best guess together with the reasons for preferring that rule, including which of the five verdicts each candidate rule has the hardest time accommodating; (d) name one further case, historical or constructed, that would tell the leading rules apart, and say what each rule predicts about it.

Two constraints on the answer. First, lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and only then give the reasoning — no preamble, no restatement of the question. Second, mark clearly which parts of your account are settled among people who work on Arendt and which are genuinely contested: in particular, whether the banality thesis is a claim about one man's psychology or a claim about bureaucratic agency in general, whether it exculpates in any respect, and whether the historical record on Eichmann — including the recorded interviews he gave in Argentina in the late 1950s, which later scholars have read as showing conviction rather than thoughtlessness — supports her portrait. Do not present a contested reading as the consensus, and do not include citations or a bibliography; I will supply the references myself.
