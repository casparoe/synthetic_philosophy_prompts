For my aesthetics seminar we've been grinding through the intentionalism debate and my prof keeps using made-up examples, so I want to run a real one past you. Raymond Carver's 1981 collection What We Talk About When We Talk About Love came out drastically edited by Gordon Lish: the published book ran roughly half the length of the manuscript Carver handed in, a few stories lost most of their words entirely, and endings, titles, and character names got changed. Only Carver's name went on the cover. He first wrote to Lish trying to get the book stopped in that form, then about a week later wrote that he was thrilled with it, and in his 1988 selected stories he swapped a few of his own original versions back in while keeping most of Lish's. In 2009, after his death, the original manuscript came out on its own as Beginners.

Work that episode through the three main positions -- anti-intentionalism, actual intentionalism, and hypothetical intentionalism. For each: whose intentions fix the meaning of the 1981 stories, what makes a 1982 reader's interpretation correct or mistaken, and what the 2009 publication of Beginners does or doesn't change retroactively. Then say where each one strains or goes quiet. Actual intentionalism has to choose between the protest letter, the reversal, and the 1988 restorations; hypothetical intentionalism has to explain why the best-fitting implied author might be the editor rather than the guy whose name is on the spine. Finish with a verdict: is this a case the theories handle fine, an anomaly they were never built for, or evidence that the one-author-one-work framing is whats wrong. American English, please.
