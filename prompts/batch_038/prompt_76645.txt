I am finishing a methods section for an applied paper and want a consistency check before I defend it in seminar. A referee has accused me of assuming away precisely the mechanism my policy claim depends on, and I want to know whether my package actually answers that charge. My commitments are these:

(1) I accept the F-twist: a model's assumptions are not to be assessed by their realism, but only by how well the model predicts the class of phenomena it is aimed at.

(2) My model represents households as a single forward-looking agent who knows the entire tax schedule and solves the resulting optimization. I defend this with the billiard-player move: people behave as if they had solved the problem, whether or not they could state it.

(3) I add a selection story: households whose behavior deviates systematically end up with less, so the surviving population looks optimizing.

(4) I use the model to make a counterfactual claim: were the marginal rate at a kink changed, hours worked would respond in the direction and roughly the magnitude the model implies.

(5) I reject a competing behavioral model of the same choice — present bias plus imperfect perception of the schedule — on the ground that its assumptions strike me as unmotivated, even though it fits my estimation sample about as well and needs three further free parameters to do so.

(6) I hold that an idealization is legitimate when it is a good enough approximation for the purpose at hand, settled case by case by experience rather than by principle; I deny that this concedes anything to realism.

Tell me which of these pull against one another and which only look as though they do. Rank the genuine tensions by severity instead of listing them as equally bad, and for each say what would have to be surrendered or reinterpreted and what the cheapest revision would cost me. Where two commitments seem to conflict but do not, say why the appearance arises. Close with a single line in the form Verdict: (n), naming the one commitment whose revision would do most to make the package cohere.
