I run a small claims registry at a public-health nonprofit. We keep a database of health claims — things like "this supplement lowers blood pressure" — that partner orgs' automated tools read directly off our API. Every entry gets exactly one status: confirmed, refuted, or unresolved. The pipeline downstream is dumb: anything that isn't confirmed gets treated as not-true, so refuted and unresolved look identical to the bots.

We get ~30 submisions a day, and a chunk of them are about other entries rather than about the world — "entry 2210 is wrong", "none of the claims tagged mercury are confirmed", that sort of thing. Right now our only rule is that a volunteer deletes any submission whose text mentions its own entry number. That's obviously too crude, and I'm fairly sure it's wrong in both directions.

Can you write me a triage procedure my volunteers can run on each of these meta-claims? They're smart people with no logic background, each submission gets maybe ten minutes, and a staff editor signs off on anything the procedure flags. I want it grounded in whatever the logic of truth actually has to say that's usable here — groundedness, hierarchies, gaps, loops — but delivered as steps, not as theory: what to check first and in what order, what counts as enough to confirm versus hold versus reject outright, and the points where the procedure has to stop and hand the thing to a human because what's left isn't a factual question at all.

Then run it on these four, one short paragraph each:

1. "Entry 2210 is confirmed," where 2210 is an ordinary empirical claim sitting in the registry with good evidence behind it.
2. "Entry 4471 is refuted," where 4471 reads "entry 4502 is confirmed," and 4502 is the submission you're holding.
3. "No entry tagged mercury is confirmed," and at the moment there is exactly one mercury-tagged entry in the registry: this one.
4. "This entry will not be confirmed."

Number four is the one that keeps me up at night, because of how our pipeline collapses unresolved into false. If the procedure says hold-as-unresolved, the bots treat it as not-true, which is exactly what the claim asserts — so it looks like we make it true by refusing to confirm it, and any status we pick seems to feed back. Tell me straight whether a triage procedure can survive that at all, or whether the fix has to be in the pipeline rather then the triage, and if it's the pipeline, say what would have to change.

Also: mark clearly which of your steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, give me at least one case where the procedure produces a result I'll hate, and end with three bullets on what it can't do. Keep the whole thing under 900 words.
