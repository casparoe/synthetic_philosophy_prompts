You are a specialist in classical Indian epistemology, with particular expertise in the pramāṇa disputes between Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, and the Buddhist logicians. Please think carefully before answering, and begin by briefly mapping how each of my commitments bears on the others before rendering any verdicts.

I am drafting the position section of a paper that reads Nyāya through contemporary reliabilism, and I would like an audit of my own view set before I commit it to print. Here is what I hold:

(1) There are exactly four pramāṇas — pratyakṣa, anumāna, upamāna, and śabda — and collapsing any one of them into another is a genuine explanatory loss rather than a harmless economy.

(2) A pramāṇa is a reliable causal process, and a cognition's warrant is extrinsic: it comes from the conditions that produced the cognition, not from anything intrinsic to the cognition itself. In practice we act on unexamined cognitions and go looking for defeaters only when something gives us reason to doubt.

(3) Testimony is irreducible, but its warrant derives from the reliability of the speaker, and we come to know that reliability from a track record of past dealings with that speaker or with speakers of that kind.

(4) Scriptural testimony about matters beyond the senses — dharma, liberation, the destiny of the self — is authoritative, yet I reject the theistic move of grounding the Veda's authority in a non-deceptive divine author. I want the Veda to be authorless and still carry weight, without paying extra metaphysical cost for it.

(5) I am not a realist about absences: abhāva is not among my padārthas, and saying the pot is not on the floor is just a way of describing the bare floor.

(6) Consistently with that, I reject anupalabdhi as a separate pramāṇa, following the Naiyāyikas in holding that knowledge of absence is obtained through perception or inference.

(7) Upamāna is genuinely sui generis: in the standard forest case, the cognition I end up with about the word and the animal it applies to is neither what my informer told me nor anything I inferred from a mark.

Go through these and tell me which are actually in tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, state how severe it is, which commitment has to give, and what the cheapest revision is together with what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and close by identifying the single revision that would clean up the most. Please resist flagging every pair that can be made to look uncomfortable under a hostile reading; I want discrimination, not a maximal list.
