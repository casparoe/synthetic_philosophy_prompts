I am drafting a chapter on the metaphysics of mind uploading, with the gradual-replacement arguments as my case study, and I have found myself describing a dialectical pattern for which I have no settled name. I would like to know whether there is an established concept for it, and if not, how it should be defined and delimited.

The pattern is this. One party offers a piece of first-person evidence as decisive. Consider the proposal that if your neurons were replaced one at a time by functionally identical artificial components, your conscious life would either persist unbroken or fade by degrees too small to notice. The defender of persistence observes that a subject whose experience was fading would register the loss and would say so: reports would degrade, discriminations would fail, behaviour would change. Since we may stipulate that none of this happens, the fading hypothesis is excluded. The reply is that this is precisely what one should expect even if the fading hypothesis is true, because the apparatus responsible for registering the loss and for reporting on it is the apparatus being replaced; someone whose experience has quietly halved will be every bit as confident, and every bit as articulate, about its fullness as someone whose experience is intact. The datum therefore stops discriminating between the two hypotheses. But the second party rarely stops there: it argues that because no internal mark of the difference could ever be found, the subject has no grounds for expecting to survive, and treats that absence as positive support for the pessimistic conclusion. The first party can run the mirror image of the same argument, holding that the undetectability of a difference is equally a reason to deny that any difference obtains. The absence of a discriminating test is thus enlisted by both sides, in contrary directions.

I keep encountering the same shape in the duplication cases. A defender of survival notes that the copy behaves as the original did, retains the original's memories and character, and sincerely reports being the original. The response is that all of this is exactly what one should expect even if the copy is someone else, since it was produced by a procedure designed to reproduce those very dispositions. Once again, the fact that there could be no internal or behavioural mark of the difference is read by one side as a reason to accept its conclusion and by the other as a reason to accept the opposite one.

Does this configuration have a name? Several familiar things sit near it without covering it. An argument that absorbs whatever is thrown at it is usually a one-sided affair, whereas here both positions are equally immune. Empirical equivalence or underdetermination describes a static relation between theories that fit the data alike, whereas what I want to capture is the dynamic sequence in which a datum is offered, disqualified as non-discriminating, and then converted by both parties into evidence for their own side. An inference from ignorance treats an absence as a positive mark, but does not explain how the same absence can be claimed in both directions at once, or when, if ever, that is legitimate.

I am not after references or a survey of the uploading literature; what I want is the carving. Which established concept, if any, comes closest, and precisely where does the pattern I have described part company with it? If nothing fits, propose a term, give it a definition with explicit conditions of application, and say what falls outside it. I would like the edges specified in particular on these points: does the same structure arise when the contested evidence is public and third-person rather than first-person? Does it arise whenever two hypotheses predict the same datum, or only when they predict it for reasons that undercut the datum's ordinary significance? Is the symmetry genuine, or does an asymmetry of burden mean that one side is entitled to draw a conclusion from the neutrality while the other is not? And what would a test that actually broke the symmetry look like in a case of this kind, given that any test we can describe is itself something both hypotheses may predict?
