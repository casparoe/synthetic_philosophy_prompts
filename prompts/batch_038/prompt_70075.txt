**The question, as set:**

Question 3 (25 marks: (a) 5, (b) 7, (c) 13, of which 8 for (c)(i) and 5 for (c)(ii)).

(a) What is it for a singular term to be a rigid designator? Explain, using the identity *water is H2O*, how the rigidity of the two sides of an identity bears on its modal status, and contrast 'water' with a non-rigid designator for the same stuff.

(b) Explain how *water is H2O* can be both metaphysically necessary and knowable only a posteriori. Give one example of a truth that is a priori but contingent, and say why the existence of such examples, together with the necessary a posteriori, shows that necessity and apriority are distinct notions.

(c) One neuroscience programme claims to have established that grief — the experience, as the grieving person has it — is identical to a specific pattern of activation, G, in the anterior cingulate cortex and adjacent regions. A second programme claims that the compound responsible for a series of fish kills in a certain river is identical to a particular synthetic pesticide, P.

 (i) Would the identity grief = G, if true, be metaphysically necessary? Does the fact that we can readily imagine someone grieving while G is absent, or G occurring without grief, show the identity to be false?

 (ii) Does the same reasoning apply to the identity fish-kill compound = P? Explain any difference.

**The student's answer, as submitted:**

(a) A designator is rigid if it picks out the same object in every possible world in which that object exists, and picks out nothing in worlds where the object does not exist. Proper names are rigid: they are introduced by an initial baptism or ostension and their reference is passed along a causal chain, so a name is not shorthand for any cluster of properties. Natural-kind terms such as 'water' are rigid in the same way. So 'water' designates H2O in every world in which water exists, and 'H2O' designates H2O in every world. By contrast, 'the clear drinkable liquid in our lakes' is non-rigid: in a counterfugal world where the lakes are full of XYZ, that description picks out XYZ. This is why rigidity matters for the modal status of the identity. If both sides are rigid, then in any world in which the identity holds at all it holds; so a true identity of that form is true in every world, hence necessary. The necessity follows from the semantics of the two terms together with the truth of the identity, and not from any special insight into the nature of water.

(b) We cannot know that the identity is true without chemistry. Its necessity is a consequence of its truth, and its truth is an empirical discovery: for all we could tell in advance, the stuff in the lakes might have been XYZ. So the statement is necessary and a posteriori. This does not collapse the distinction between the two notions, because the distinction is between a modal property of a proposition and an epistemic property of a knower's relation to it, and they come apart in both directions: 'water is H2O' is necessary a posteriori, while 'this stick is one metre long' — said of the standard metre in Paris, fixing the unit by stipulation — is a priori but contingent, since the stick might have been a different length. What this pair of cases shows is that conceivability does not entail possibility, so that conceivability arguments are not automatically arguments about what is metaphysically possible.

(c)(i) The same reasoning applies to the grief case, and here it secures the identity. If 'grief' and 'pattern G of cingulate activation' are both rigid designators, then the identity, if true, is necessary, and whether it is true is an empirical matter for neuroscience rather than for conceptual analysis. The objection from imagination — that we can picture someone grieving while G is absent, or G occurring without grief — has exactly the same status as the objection that we can picture water that is not H2O. In both cases we are mistaking an epistemic possibility for a metaphysical one. What we can imagine is a situation which, correctly described, is a situation containing no grief; to imagine it as a case of grief is to misdescribe the situation we have imagined. So the identity theorist need not be troubled. This is Kripke's own conclusion: once rigidity is in place, mind-brain identities are necessary a posteriori and the appearance of contingency is explained away just as it was for heat and mean molecular kinetic energy.

One difference should be acknowledged. 'Grief' is introduced from the inside, by its phenomenal character, while the neural description is introduced by its causal role. But this shows only that we fix the reference of 'grief' by a property that is not what grief is; once the identity is established, the reference is fixed for good.

(c)(ii) The pesticide case is straightforwardly like the water case. Both terms are introduced ostensively or causally, the identity is a posteriori and necessary, and there is no serious temptation to suppose that the fish kills might have been produced by something other than what in fact produced them.

One further point on (b). 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' is necessary a posteriori, but it differs from 'water is H2O' in an instructive way. The reference of 'Hesperus' was fixed by a description — the brightest body visible in the evening sky at a certain place and time — and that of 'Phosphorus' likewise. Anyone who knows how the names were introduced knows a priori that Hesperus is the brightest evening body and that Phosphorus is the brightest morning body; what she does not know a priori is that these are one and the same. The identity is therefore a posteriori only for want of astronomical information: a being who knew which body satisfied each description would know the identity a priori, by putting together what it already knows about the two names. That is why the necessity of the identity is not threatened — it rests on the descriptions being satisfied by one and the same object.

I conclude that the identity theory is safe, and that the criteria for when an identity is necessary are semantic: check that both terms are rigid, and check that the identity is true.
