I'm a PhD student in archaeology working on later prehistoric settlement sites, and my chapter turns on an analogy that my supervisor and I are increasingly using in two different ways. The slogan, which came out of the post-processual turn and now saturates our subfield, is that material culture is a text: styles and object types are words, structured deposits are sentences, a site's spatial organization is a discourse, and the archaeologist's business is interpretation rather than measurement. In my own case the reading is doing real work. I have a recurring pattern of pots deliberately smashed and buried in pits beneath floors and in enclosure ditches, and I want to infer something about belief (a distinction between what the living may use and what must be rendered unusable, perhaps offered to something) and about an institution (a repeated household rite rather than individual whim).

Two uses of the analogy matter. The first licenses the inference: because we can read texts, we can read assemblages. The second, which my supervisor reaches for when I press him about falsifiability, licenses the epistemology: readings are assessed the way readings of a poem are assessed — by coherence, richness, and fit with the rest of the corpus — not by correspondence, so the absence of independent confirmation is not a defect in them.

Please audit both. Give me the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the lexicon, the grammar, the speaker's intention, the native informant, the genre conventions, the surviving manuscript. Then separate the disanalogies that bear on my inference from the merely formal ones (a text is linear, a deposit is not — so what?). I especially want to know what the analogy imports that my material lacks: a competent community able to correct a misreading, an author whose intentions are the target, the possibility of paraphrase, and above all a workable distinction between a wrong reading and an uninteresting one. Then say whether the first use survives its disanalogies, and separately whether the second does; I suspect the second is carrying weight it cannot bear, but I cannot yet say why, and I need to be able to say it to him. Finally, if some less seductive source domain would do the same inferential job, propose it and show where adopting it would force me to weaken my conclusions about the pots.
