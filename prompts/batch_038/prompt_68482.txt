I run a small reading group on the philosophy of history and I want to hand out a diagnostic decision tree — basically "which account of historical truth do you actually hold" — because half the group keeps arguing past each other. My hunch, which you can push back on if it's wrong, is that the debate runs along two axes everybody conflates: whether the evidence underdetermines the account, and whether the narrative form itself — tragedy vs. farce, having a beginning-middle-end — adds content that isn't in the events. Build the tree out of those questions rather than out of a list of famous names.

Every node should be answerable from a concrete case by someone with no idea where it leads. Use at least these: a narrative sentence describing a crowd surging forward when no source describes the motion that way; two equally well-documented accounts of the same events, one emplotted as tragedy and one as farce; a claim about a motive no surviving document records; and renaming a period rather than re-describing anything inside it.

Leaves must include a Rankean documentary realist, Carr's situated-historian view, White's emplotment-is-imposed view, and two or more others the space supports. For each leaf give its most awkward commitment; mark any two-by-two cell you think nobody occupies and say whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored; and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate.

Answer as one JSON object with three keys: `nodes` (each: id, question, case_used, options as [{answer, next}]), `leaves` (each: id, position, axes, awkward_commitment), `unresolved` (each: pair, why_the_tree_fails).
