After eleven hours of argument with a friend about superrationality, we have agreed to stop and produce the document an adversarial collaboration between us ought to yield. He is a committed superrationalist; I am a committed causal decision theorist. Work from the case we were fighting over: a hundred people on a departmental mailing list each privately submit a non-negative integer, described to them as a number of free tickets; whoever submits the strictly greatest number wins $1,000, and if two or more tie at the top — a tie at zero included — nobody wins. Entries are simultaneous, there is no communication, and each entrant knows only that the others are ordinary students and staff, most of whom have never heard of superrationality.

Draft the joint statement. First, a numbered list of claims we can both sign: about what a single entry can causally affect; about the symmetry his view appeals to and what would have to be true for it to hold here; about the fact that identical play by all a hundred entrants yields the same payoff whatever number it is; and about how that degeneracy should be described, even if we disagree about what it shows. Second, a table of the disagreements that survive, each row stating the issue in his preferred formulation and then in mine, in wording each side would accept for its own view. Third, the cruxes — I expect one to be whether the symmetry premise is an empirical claim about a common source of our reasoning or an evidential claim about what my own deliberation tells me, since I suspect it is false on the first reading and collapses into evidential decision theory on the second — and for each crux a case or argument we would both accept as bearing on it, with our predictions written down in advance, including what each of us expects the actual distribution of entries to look like if the contest were run tomorrow. Settle, as far as the document can, his claim that the superrational entry is zero against my claim that his view is silent here and that whatever reasoning breaks the symmetry is mine rather than his. Close with what would count for each of us as evidence against our own position, and an explicit note on any part of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal.
