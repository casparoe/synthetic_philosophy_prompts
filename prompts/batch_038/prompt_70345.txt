I've run a monthly calibration night for a loose circle of rationalist-adjacent friends for about six years — mostly forecasting questions with resolution dates, occasionally philosophical ones — and the habit has left me allergic to answers that are structurally unfalsifiable. So I want to put a question to you in a form that forces numbers. The subject is Hacking's looping effects: the observation that classifications of people, unlike classifications of quarks, get taken up by the classified, who then change in ways that make the classification truer, or false, or simply different. My housemate is a third-year psychiatry resident, and after I pressed her on this over dinner she sketched, half in jest, a diagnostic proposal for a future DSM-6. I've tidied it up, and I should stress that it is entirely invented: it is not a real candidate category, and I'm not asking you to look anything up. Call it anticipatory overload disorder. Core criteria: (a) recurrent, intrusive mental simulation of specific future obligations and social demands that the patient reports as involuntary and depleting; (b) consequent delay of more than an hour in initiating at least half of routine obligations; (c) marked relief when a scheduled demand is cancelled; (d) clinically significant distress or impairment; (e) not better accounted for by generalized anxiety disorder, ADHD, or a depressive disorder. Suppose it is validated at a handful of university clinics, adopted in 2032 with essentially this criteria text, supported by a self-report inventory, a small specialist-clinic infrastructure, and one advocacy organization — and suppose the criteria text is then never revised.

The headline proposition, with a resolution criterion attached: by 2052, at least forty percent of newly diagnosed patients presenting to American specialty clinics will spontaneously describe their condition using at least one feature that appears nowhere in the official criteria text — some additional canonical symptom, idiom, or narrative shape that has attached itself to the label in circulation. Give me your credence, then take it apart. I want the sub-questions named explicitly, each with a probability and a single line of justification, and I want the arithmetic of how they combine rather than just the intuition. At minimum I'd like to see: how much of the number is carried by the sheer cultural availability of a label this vague and this flattering to accept, given how readily symptom description migrates toward whatever vocabulary is on offer; how much by the institutional machinery that hands the label out and manages the people who receive it; how much by its overlap with neighboring categories, since I expect a large share of apparent looping to be label migration between GAD, ADHD, and this thing rather than the production of anything genuinely new; and the base rate from cases you already know — the fugue that Hacking treats as a temporary kind, the multiple-personality epidemic and its collapse, the sudden wave of adolescent tic presentations that movement-disorder clinics began seeing after short-form video made a particular tic repertoire widely viewable, the redistribution of self-descriptions when Asperger's was folded into the autism spectrum. Tell me which of those you're weighting and what numbers you're taking from them. Then two further credences. First, conditional on the headline proposition being true, what is the probability that the dominant causal route is interactive in Hacking's sense — people changing because they now have the classification — as against the disjunction of deflationary routes: referral patterns shifting who gets assessed, clinicians cueing the features at intake, the underlying population changing for reasons having nothing to do with the diagnosis, and diagnostic overshadowing rearranging comorbidities? Be honest about where you would draw the boundary between intake cueing and looping, because I suspect cueing sits inside the loop and my disjunction may not be as clean as I've made it look. Second, my control condition: pick the folk typology you judge most comparable in circulation but least comparable in institutional embedding — MBTI types, attachment-style labels as used by popular therapists, astrological signs — and give me the credence that self-description drift of the same magnitude and the same specificity occurs there over the same twenty years. That number is what decides, for me, whether looping is a general fact about human kinds or a fact about what clinics do.

My housemate and I disagree about the headline proposition: she is at 0.25, I am at 0.60. Given your own decomposition, tell me which of us is most likely to be wrong about which sub-question, and whether our gap is better explained by differing priors about the power of labels or by differing estimates about a mundane confound. Then tell me which of your own terms you trust least and by how much the final number would move if you were badly wrong about it, and finally: if you could pre-register one measurement to discriminate between the looping story and the deflationary disjunction, what would it be — what would you sample, what would you compare it against, and what result would you count as a refutation? No bibliography, no citations, no reading list, and please don't tell me it's an empirical question; I know that, which is precisely why I'm asking for a credence with a resolution date rather than a survey of positions.
