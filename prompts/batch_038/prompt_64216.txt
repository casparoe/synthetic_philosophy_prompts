Ok here's one I've been chewing on and I can't get my own numbers to stop moving around.

Define the hinge of history hypothesis narrowly so it actually has a truth value. Say: the 100-year stretch we're in right now (call it 1980–2080) does more to determine the total amount and the shape of value in the future of Earth-originating civilization than any other century, past or future. "Determine" is doing work on purpose, because I think it splits into two routes that are NOT mutually exclusive and need handling separately:

(a) volume — most of the good (and bad) that ever happens happens, or is caused, inside this window;
(b) lock-in — the future is long, but its trajectory gets fixed by decisions made in this window, so the window's influence is disproportionate even tho it contains relatively little value itself.

I want your credence in (a)-or-(b), decomposed. Specifically:

- a prior term: how likely it is a priori that a randomly selected century out of a civilization's whole history is the pivotal one, and how much of that budges once you condition on what we actually see from the inside — the base-rate style worry being that there will be an enormous number of future centuries, so you'd need a lot of evidence to think ours is the special one;
- P(the future is long) versus P(it ends soon), and how each route depends on that — note that (b) needs a long future and (a) gets easier if the future is short, so these pull in opposite directions and I suspect that's where people talk past each other;
- P(transformative technology arrives in this window | long future), i.e. the timing claim that's actually carrying the load;
- an observation-selection term: P(we'd conclude we're at the hinge | we aren't), which I always forget and which I suspect matters more than anything else here;
- whatever else belongs that I've left out, including whether taking the disjunction of (a) and (b) double-counts, and whether the proposition should be relativized to a unit of time at all.

Give each term a number and one line of justification, then show me how they combine and what the total comes to. Then tell me which single term the total is most sensitive to: if you could be made certain about one of them, which certainty would move the final number furthest, and by how much?

For context, not for you to split the difference: mine is about 0.3. A friend who thinks the whole framing is motivated reasoning says 0.04. We both accept roughly the same science and both think a long future is possible, so tell me which of the terms above we most likely disagree on.

On the epistemic side: be as committed as your numbers actually are — don't dissolve into "reasonable people could differ" — but do flag any term where you can feel that you're basically making the number up rather than estimating it. And if you think the setup itself is broken — that "does more to determine value" isn't precise enough to be true or false, or that self-locating priors make the decomposition circular, or that volume/lock-in isnt where the real joints are — say that plainly up front instead of handing me a nicely formatted number out of politeness. I'd much rather be told the question is malformed than get a fake decomposition.
