So I've got a five-minute lightning slot at our department's end-of-year thing — think thirty people, free wine that nobody drinks, a projector that only works on Tuesdays — and I somehow signed up to talk about causation by omission. The organiser asked everyone to open with "something light," and I'm refusing to do the two-philosophers-walk-into-a-bar thing or any pun on the word cause. I want an actual joke, real setup and real punchline, that lands hard with people who know this debate and would completely not land with people who don't.

The constraint is that the funny part has to come out of the content, not out of philosophers being absent-minded and losing their keys. So it should turn on one of the actual pressure points: the worry that if omissions are causes then every stranger's failure to water my fern caused it to die, including some guy in Reykjavik who has never heard of me or my fern; or the more basic problem that there's no thing there to be the cause, you're supposedly relating an event to an absence; or the letting / double-prevention territory, where I don't so much cause the fern's death as fail to stop a dehydration that was already underway. I'd also happily take one aimed at the contrastivist repair job, where the answer to every bad case is that you never just cause something, you cause it rather than some alternative, which starts to feel like dissolving the problem with a notation trick becuase it makes every causal claim secretly relational.

Extra credit, and this is the bit I actually care about: make the structure of the joke itself an omission. Something should be conspicuously missing from the setup, and that missing thing is what does the work, so the laugh is literally an instance of the view I'm about to spend five minutes defending. Keep it short enough to say out loud without dying — three or four sentences max — and throw in one alternative in case the first is too in-group. My friend who does epistemology will be sitting in the second row and she is going to make a face if the punchline could be dropped into any old metaphysics talk and nobody would notice the swap.
