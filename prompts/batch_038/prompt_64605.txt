I've never been a presentist so I don't even have dog in this fight — I'd happily live in a block universe — what bugs me is the claim that physics has *settled* a metaphysics question, and my inability to say whether the argument is invalid somewhere or whether every step is fine and I just have to swallow (9). Don't explain the argument back to me or give me the history of the debate. Diagnose it.

Here's his argument, as near to how he stated it as I can get:

(1) What's real, full stop, is everything there is — and for me right now that means all and only the events simultaneous with me-here-now. That's not a thesis, it's just what "what exists at this moment" means.
(2) Special relativity says simultaneity between spatially separated events isn't absolute. Two inertial observers in relative motion slice spacetime differently, and both slicings are equally physically legitimate; no frame is priveleged, that's literally the content of the theory.
(3) So there's a perfectly legitimate inertial observer, passing me right now, whose now-slice contains an event E — say, a decision being made in the Andromeda galaxy — that on my slice hasn't happened yet and sits out in my future. (Tilt his velocity the other way and some event that on my slice is already over sits in his future.)
(4) If an event is real for some inertial observer then it's real, no qualifications. Otherwise "real" gets indexed to reference frames and there's no single answer to the question of what exists, and there is only one world.
(5) So E is real.
(6) E is future relative to me and yet real, so at least some of what I call my future is real now.
(7) And that generalises: for any event in spacetime whatsoever, there's some inertial observer somewhere whose now-slice contains it. So by (4) and (5), every event — past, present, future, all of it — is real.
(8) So there's no objective, frame-independent fact about which slice is the present. A distinction that corresponds to no physical difference isn't a distinction at all.
(9) Therefore the future is as real and as fixed as the past, my agonising over what to do tomorrow is just more events in the block, and the presentist isn't making an interesting metaphysical choice, he's contradicting special relativity.

So: which step do you reject, and why did that step look unimpeachable when it was stated — I want the account of why it's seductive, not just the label on the fallacy.
