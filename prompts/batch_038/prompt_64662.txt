I'm writing up a discussion from a distributive-justice seminar, and one sentence did far more work in it than anyone acknowledged. A participant arguing that the costs of an inherited illness should be socialized said: nobody deserves the hand they were dealt, and so no one should have to bear the costs of a condition they did nothing to bring about. When another participant pointed out that the same sentence is usually cited as the motivation for the opposite practice — holding people liable for risks they knowingly ran, as distinct from misfortunes they could not have controlled — the first participant said the sentence was neutral between those two things, and left it there.

I want the sentence taken apart rather than accepted or dismissed. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, keeping separate at minimum: claims about desert, claims about when a distribution is unfair, claims about the causal history of someone's advantage or disadvantage, and claims about what an agent could reasonably have been expected to foresee at the time of acting. Label them A, B, C, and so on, and for each say whether it is a platitude, a substantive but defensible thesis, or false, and whether it is compatible with holding people responsible for the foreseeable consequences of their own choices.

Then run the menu on a case the seminar never reached. A person is offered a cheap, effective screening for a costly hereditary condition and declines, because the clinic has a documented history of mistreating people from her community; her distrust is well founded and was not manufactured by her. She later develops the condition. Is that brute luck, option luck, or something the two-way distinction cannot accommodate — luck whose status as chosen is destroyed by the injustice of the background conditions in which the choice was made? Say which readings classify it which way, and why.

Finish with a line beginning Verdict: giving the single label of the reading that best licenses the inference the first participant made from nobody deserving their hand to nobody bearing the costs of their illness, or None if no reading does.
