I freelance as a graphic designer. Six weeks ago a client asked me to rebrand their whole product line on a rush timeline and I quoted a flat fee without seeing the full scope. The scope has since roughly doubled. I'm legally allowed to send a revised invoice and I'm pretty confident they'd pay it, they need this before their launch.

But I won't, and the reason is that about a year ago I started telling every client, in writing, that I never revise a quote once it's accepted, no matter how bad the job turns out for me. Since I started doing that and getting a reputation for it, the inbound work has gotten alot better: three clients this quarter came to me without comparing bids, and one of them said outright that they picked me because I was the only one who'd commit to a number.

My wife thinks I'm being a sucker twice over. First, that the rule itself is stupid. Second, and this is the part I can't answer, that even if the rule pays off in general, right now, on this actual job, refusing the extra money is just a straight loss. Her point is that "but it protects my reputation" doesn't apply here, because these people already hired me, and one renegotiation that nobody else ever hears about costs me nothing. So she reckons I'm doing a dumb thing and then inventing a reason it's smart.

Is she right? If she isn't, I want to know exactly where her argument goes wrong, because it sounds correct to me too and I mostly just don't want to eat the loss. And then tell me what to actually do about this job, and whether the rule is worth keeping once I've let myself see that it costs me real money in cases like this one.
