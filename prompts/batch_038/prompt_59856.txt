I'm the engineer who owns the allocation service for a city home-energy-retrofit program. Every quarter we have about 1,200 assessment slots to hand out across 40 districts, each slot is worth a few thousand dollars to a household, and its me who decides what the ranking function actually is — my PM's only definition of fairness is that the disparity number on the dashboard goes down, nobody on the team has any ethics background, and I get maybe twenty change proposals a quarter that I have to review in an afternoon each before the program board signs off.

Two problems. Some proposals are pure leveling down — pull slots out of the two best-off districts to shrink the gap, and the freed capacity just goes unused — and my current metric scores those as improvements. And I have no defensible way to trade a large gain for a district sitting at the 40th percentile against a small gain for the worst-off district.

Can you turn prioritarianism into a procedure I can actually run and defend? I want the questions to ask in order and what counts as a passing answer to each, the concrete math (I gather it's a concave function of wellbeing summed over people — which function, and how do I explain the curvature to non-philosophers without just saying trust me), the points where it has to stop and hand the decision to a human because it's a values call rather than a facts call, and how to make the dashboard stop rewarding leveling down. Also: our unit of allocation is the district average, is that even legitimate under a view like this?

Then run it on three proposals: (a) cancel 80 slots in the two wealthiest districts, capacity wasted; (b) shift 60 slots from a mid district to the worst-off one, where the mid district loses measurably and the worst-off gain a little each; (c) a change that gives the single worst-off district a marginal improvement but costs a lot of slots spread across many better-off districts. I expect (c) to come out in a way I'll hate.

Last thing: tell me where this procedure is going to misfire in practice, and keep straight which parts of this are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who work on it — don't sell me a disputed position as consensus.
