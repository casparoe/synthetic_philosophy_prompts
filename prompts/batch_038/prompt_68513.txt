I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training, but I got stuck on a question at lunch and can't shake it: could I have come from a different sperm and egg? Someone on a podcast said there's a famous argument that no, you couldn't have — your particular origin is essential to you, you'd be a different person. Here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and I want to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

- A different sperm fertilizes that same egg: not me. Different person.
- Same sperm, different egg: also not me.
- Same sperm and egg, but the embryo gets implanted in a surragate instead of my mom: I'd still say that's me.
- Same two gametes, but conception happens a day later than it actually did: still me.
- My parents conceive a month later instead: also still me? Except a month later it's obviously different gametes, which I just said isn't me, so I've contradicted myself and I don't know how to fix it.
- An egg that differs from my actual egg by one point mutation: I want to say that's still me. But keep changing one mutation at a time and you can slide all the way over to a completely different egg, which I said isn't me. So either there's a sharp cutoff somewhere or my "still me" was wrong.
- Identical twin: my brother comes from the exact same sperm and egg, same zygote, it just split. He is obviously not me.
- A clone made from one of my cells today: not me, even though he'd have my whole genome.

My working rule has been something like provenance-is-identity, same source gives same artifact, like a build. But the twin case seems to break it outright, and the clone case bugs me because I'm way more confident the clone isn't me than I am about the surrogate case, even though the clone is a much closer genetic match than a sibling is.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close to all? What does it say about the twin, the clone, and the mutation slide — and about a case I haven't thought of? And if I have to give one of my verdicts up, which one is the weakest and why?
