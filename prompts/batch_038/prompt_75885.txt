I am drafting the opening chapter of a dissertation on standing to blame, and I want the specification in hand before I get into the contest between the existing accounts. Give me the desiderata: what any theory of what it takes to have standing to blame has to deliver — the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the structural features of blame it must accommodate. Please do not give me a bibliography, a reading list, or author names; characterize the competing positions by their content rather than by who holds them.

The list should be generated from cases rather than from slogans. At minimum, work through the following: (a) the hypocrite whose charge is true and well-supported, so that standing must come apart from the correctness of the blame — a theory on which hypocrisy is a defect in the truth or justification of the accusation has misdescribed the phenomenon; (b) that same hypocrite blaming himself, apparently with standing and perhaps under an obligation to exercise it, which makes standing relativized to a target and forces any account that grounds it in a global property of the blamer to explain why; (c) losses of standing that involve no hypocrisy at all — the busybody in a private matter, the person who caused the very wrong he now condemns, the one who has forgiven and then reproaches again, the one who stands to profit from the reproach; (d) restoration, since apology, reform, and the passage of time appear to give standing back, which an account that makes standing a settled fact about the blamer's character must accommodate; (e) apparent degrees of standing, which sits badly with the binary vocabulary we use for it; (f) whether standing is a condition on the attitude or only on its expression, and if only on the expression, why the attitude seems to survive intact; (g) the status of blame without standing — a further wrongdoing, a mere infelicity, or a failure to achieve what the blamer was trying to achieve — and what each answer implies about whether someone may then be blamed for blaming without standing.

Cover the scope questions that are usually left implicit as well: whether standing attaches only to moral blame or also to resentment and indignation, to praise, to forgiveness, and to demands for apology; and whether it has any analogue for a community's or a state's reproach, which would have to explain what, if anything, is lost when a prosecutor who is herself culpable of the same offence brings a legitimate charge.

Then two verdicts. First, flag any subset of the desiderata you believe to be jointly unsatisfiable, and derive the tension rather than asserting it — if no consistent theory can meet all of them, that is itself the result I most want, since it would mean the concept is being asked to do more work than it can bear. Second, say which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates, and which requirement you suspect is not a genuine desideratum at all but a theoretical commitment smuggled in from one camp's favoured framework.

Format: give me a table with the columns `ID`, `requirement` (one sentence, stated as a constraint a theory must satisfy), `motivating case` (the case or consideration that generates it, concrete enough that I could put it to a seminar), `status` (non-negotiable / negotiable / camp-specific, naming the camp by description in the last case), and `excludes` (what kind of theory the requirement rules out, or "nothing much" if it is cheap to satisfy). Follow the table with prose for the two verdicts, no more than about a page. Keep the weight on the requirements and their cases; do not score the existing accounts against the list, since I intend to do that myself.
