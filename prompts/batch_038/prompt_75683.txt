I build preference-learning systems: pairwise comparison data, a scoring model trained on it, ranking metrics, held-out evaluation. I am fluent in that vocabulary and in the adjacent statistics — identifiability, latent-variable models, label noise, distribution shift, calibration, specification gaming. I have no formal training in philosophy or economics. I have been reading the mid-twentieth-century economic debate about utility and revealed preference, in secondary summaries and some primary material, and I find the idiom hard to parse. I would like a controlled translation exercise, followed by a residue report.

The position bundle I want translated is roughly this, as I understand it. Please correct me if I have misstated any part before translating it.

(a) Utility should not be posited as an unobservable inner state that causes behavior. It should be defined entirely in terms of what an agent chooses from the sets of options available to it, so that talk of utility redescribes choice rather than reporting a hidden cause of choice.

(b) This is not thereby vacuous, because choice data can fail consistency conditions — the axioms ruling out cycles in what is revealed preferred to what — and such failures are detectable in observation. There are constructive results characterizing when a finite set of price-and-choice observations admits a utility function that rationalizes it, and how to build one.

(c) Utility so defined is ordinal only. Nothing in the data fixes a scale or an origin, only an ordering, so any monotone transformation of a representing function represents equally well.

(d) An objection from another direction: choosing is not the same as preferring. An agent may choose what they would not, on reflection, prefer — out of obligation, or because they hold several distinct orderings of their own ends, only one of which concerns their personal goals — so reading the chosen option as the preferred one can systematically misdescribe the agent.

(e) A more recent line treats observed choices as a noisy and possibly biased signal of an underlying welfare quantity, and asks what can be recovered about that quantity once one has a model of how choice departs from it.

Target vocabulary: mine, as described. Where you need a concept that my vocabulary lacks, say so explicitly instead of quietly importing one.

Deliverables, in this order:

1. Restate (a) through (e) one at a time as precisely as my vocabulary permits.

3. Then answer these directly. Does the emptiness objection in (a) collapse, in my terms, into the observation that a model constrained only to fit its training set is unfalsifiable, or is there residue? Does (c) become the claim that a score is identifiable only up to monotone transformation, and if so, does that vindicate the economists or deflate them? Does the claim that comparing utilities across persons is meaningless translate into the claim that scores fitted to different datasets are not on a common scale? Is there any counterpart at all to the normative question of which rationalization is better for the agent, as opposed to which fits the data? Is specification gaming a counterpart of anything in (a) through (e), or an artifact of my always having an external loss function that these authors deliberately refused?
