I'm auditing a historiography seminar as a non-student (data engineer by trade, this is my hobby) and we've been grinding through the big cyclic theories of history — Polybius on constitutions, Ibn Khaldun on dynasties, Spengler, Toynbee. Something's been bugging me for three weeks and I genuinely can't tell whether I'm missing a term or whether there isn't one, so I figured I'd ask here before I look stupid in section.

Here's the thing I keep noticing. Polybius seems to mean something pretty literal: monarchy hardens into tyranny, tyranny gets overthrown and aristocracy takes over, that decays into oligarchy, and round and round, and the *same kinds of regime* keep coming back in the same order — you could in principle draw the wheel and name every spoke. But Spengler isn't like that at all. No two of his cultures have the same religion, the same art, the same politics, or even the same sort of mathematics; a culture's "winter" in one case is some specific kind of imperialist world-city and in another it's something completely unrelated. What repeats is only the *order of the phases*, the shape of the curve — there's nothing in the world you could point at twice. And Toynbee's the same way I think: breakdown looks nothing like it did in the Hellenic world compared to the Sumeric one, but both get filed under "breakdown." Same with the pop version, "empires rise and fall" — nobody actually means the same empire comes back. So I keep running into theories that get called cyclical where nothing recurs but the sequence, and I want to know if that's a recognized distinction — recurrence of form without recurrence of content, or whatever you'd say — or whether everybody just lumps it together with real cycles and the difference is invisible to the field.

I can predict the reflex answers and I don't think any of them are it. "That's just morphology" — no, morphology is what Spengler calls the whole method, not this specific distinction inside it. "That's typology" or "that's analogical thinking" — typology is about prefiguration, isn't it, one event standing in for another across time, which isn't what's going on here. So: is there an established concept for this, and if so what does it rule out? If not, coin me one, define it properly, and give me the edges, because the edges are the part I actually need. Specifically: where's the line between a genuine cycle, this sequence-only thing, and a straight-line stage theory where every society passes through the same stages exactly once — because that last one looks to me like a cycle with one lap taken out of it, and I can't tell if that's a real difference or just a bad pun on my part. Tell me what would have to be true of two phases for them to count as *the same phase* when nothing in them is the same. And if only the sequence recurs and never the content, is there any way at all to distinguish such a theory from a straight line plus noise, or does the "cycle" label stop doing work? Also, for what it's worth, please say which side of the line each of my four authors actually lands on, because I may well have gotten Spengler or Ibn Khaldun wrong. A bare label won't do me any good here.
