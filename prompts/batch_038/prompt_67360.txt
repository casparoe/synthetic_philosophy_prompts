A friend of mine is putting about a year and a half of full-time effort into a small project on forecasting how long big political entities last — states, great powers, empires — and it's almost all outside-view stuff in the Flyvbjerg/Taagepera lineage: start from duration distributions in the historical record, then adjust for the case at hand. She asked me where the time should go and I want a second opinion before I answer, because my own instincts are probably anchored on whatever I last read on LessWrong.

Here's the menu I sketched for her. Feel free to add one I've missed, or to tell me one of these isn't a real problem:

- reference class choice itself — the philosophy-of-probability complaint that any class is gerrymandered, so the outside view never actually gets off the ground
- selection and survivorship effects in the historical sample (we only see things that got big enough to be recorded, and the empire/state boundary is mushy anyway)
- non-stationarity: what a base rate built on 500 BC–1900 AD is worth for a system with nukes, industrial growth, and integrated finance
- the mixing rule: given a base rate plus case-specific inside-view evidence, how much weight each gets — is there anything principled available or is it just taste with a spreadsheet
- heavy tails vs. roughly constant hazard rates in durations, and whether that distinction changes any answer people actually want
- scoring: forecasts that resolve after everyone involved is dead can't be calibrated, so what's the evaluation story
- self-locating information — the Doomsday-argument worry that "we're already N years in" is itself evidence, and whether that contaminates the base rate

Rank these by where 1.5 person-years goes furthest, and be explicit about the criteria: how many of the others depend on it, how tractable it is for a tiny group that isn't collecting new data, how neglected it is, and whether settling it would change what a forecaster actually outputs. Tell me which one you'd cut outright and why, and what evidence would reshuffle your ranking. Also — please keep settled apart from contested. Some of these have real disagreement among people who do this for a living, and I'd rather be told where the field is genuinely split than handed one side as the consensus.
