Marco's been offered a job designing engagement features for a gambling app, and he says he's taking it either way. Priya, who's known him since college, thinks that's flatly wrong. Marco offered her a deal: he pays $15k a year into whatever anti-addiction charity she picks, and in exchange she drops the objection and stops bringing it up. She said no, and it turned into a four-round argument I got dragged into:

1. Priya: no payment can make this ok, becuase the people his product wrecks get nothing out of it. Money going to a charity *she* picks doesn't balance anything, it just buys her quiet.

2. Marco: that's not how trade works, nobody has to be made whole. A deal's fine if both sides prefer it to walking away, and she would — $15k to addiction services beats her just being angry at him, and he'd rather not have her think he's a monster.

3. Priya: it only looks like a trade because she already partly shares his values. If they genuinely had nothing in common there'd be no exchange rate between them and nothing to haggle over. So this isn't moral trade, it's a bribe with extra steps.

4. Marco: call it a bribe then — tons of ordinary deals involve handing over something neither side intrinsically values. If it makes the world better by both their own lights, the labels doing no work. Priya said the label's doing all the work: once someone can pay you to stop objecting, your objection has turned into a price.

Can you go through it move by move and say who's right at each one, plus what neither of them is seeing?
