(1) I'm a straight-up realist about arithmetic. Goldbach is either true or false, and my being a slow thinker doesn't make that indeterminate.

(2) I'm accuracy-first: the whole point of a credence is to be near the truth, scored properly, so the ideal credence in a true arithmetical sentence is 1.

(3) But I don't think my 0.6 in Goldbach is a lapse, or a shortcoming I'm merely excused for. It's the correct credence for a reasoner with my resources. Rationality has computational limits built into it, not bolted on as a concession to human frailty.

(4) And I'm against agent-relative norms. One standard of rationality for everyone — me and the ideal reasoner alike.

(5) For any arithmetical sentence, my credence should just be my estimate of the chance that it's provable in PA. Proof is the only route to warranted confidence in arithmetic; I don't believe in mathematical intuition as a seperate faculty.

(6) Consistent with that, my credence in the consistency of PA is about 0.999.

(7) I endorse the logical induction criterion — no polynomial-time trader should be able to make unbounded profit off my sequence of credences. But I also think the ordinary Dutch book argument establishes the norms of consistancy, and I keep feeling like I have to demote one of these two and can't decide which.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which pairs only look like they do?
