I am preparing a case for an upper-level epistemology seminar on the structure of justification. The point I want it to make is that the dispute between foundationalism and coherentism is not confined to exotic skeptical scenarios: it can change the verdict on an utterly ordinary perceptual belief held by a competent adult at work. I also want to unsettle the caricature my students arrive with — that foundationalism is the restrictive view and coherentism the permissive one — by making them work through a case that runs the other way.

I want a single scenario in which a classical internalist foundationalist and a coherentist about justification reach opposite verdicts on whether the subject knows, and specifically in the direction that contradicts that caricature: the foundationalist says she knows, the coherentist says she does not.

The scenario must satisfy all of the following:

- The subject is an ordinary, attentive, competent adult doing routine work — no philosophical training, no cognitive impairment, no culpable ignorance.
- The target belief is formed on the spot by perceiving an instrument or object directly in front of her, and it is true.
- That belief must be of a kind a classical foundationalist can countenance as basic: it is not the output of testimony, of memory of testimony, or of inference.
- Every process involved is reliable; the subject holds no belief that contradicts the target belief and none that specifically impugns the instrument or her senses; and she has no realistic opportunity within the scene to run a check that would expose the tension.
- The incoherence the coherentist relies on must be distributed rather than local. It should arise from the target belief's being rendered inexplicable by a substantial body of her other beliefs taken together, and removing any one of those background beliefs must not restore coherence. This constraint is essential: a case in which the divergence is produced by an ordinary defeater that any theory would recognize does not meet the specification, since then the two views are not really disagreeing about structure.
- The setting is realistic — a plant, a clinic, a records office, a warehouse, a kitchen — with no science-fictional machinery: no demons, no simulations, no memory manipulation, no neurosurgery, no stipulated unreliable faculties.
- One paragraph, under about 220 words, containing none of the vocabulary of the debate: the words justified, foundational, coherent, warrant, and knowledge must not appear in the scenario itself.

After the scenario, work through the following in labeled sections:

1. State the target belief, and list the background beliefs that generate the tension.
2. Give the foundationalist's reasoning: why the belief qualifies as basic, why the basing relation is satisfied here, and why its inexplicability within her wider system does not defeat it. Derive the verdict from the view's structural commitments rather than asserting it.
3. Give the coherentist's reasoning on the same terms, explaining how a property of the system as a whole can bear on a belief whose immediate source is impeccable.
4. Identify the single structural feature of the case that does the work, and report what happens to both verdicts if you remove it. In particular, say whether supplying her with a handful of further beliefs that connect the target belief to her system flips the coherentist while leaving the foundationalist unmoved.
5. Say whether infinitism sides with the coherentist or the foundationalist on this case, and whether it arrives there for the same reason. If its diagnosis differs from the coherentist's, say what the three-way comparison reveals about what these views are really disagreeing about.
6. Give the coherentist's two best attacks on the case: one aimed at the design, arguing that I have not in fact managed to keep defeaters out of it, and one aimed at the diagnosis, arguing that the foundationalist has quietly relied on something other than the structure of justification. Neither may be a strawman; both must be ones a serious coherentist would actually press.

Describe all three positions by their structural commitments rather than by reference to particular authors or texts — I want the case to stand on its own, and I am not looking for attributions.

Begin your response with the scenario paragraph itself, with no preface, no restatement of the task, and no commentary anywhere other than the labeled sections. If you conclude that one of the constraints above cannot be met without rigging the verdicts, complete the case as best you can and then say plainly, at the very end, which constraint fails and what the nearest acceptable alternative would look like.
