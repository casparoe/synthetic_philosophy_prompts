I spent thirty-four years in experimental condensed-matter physics and, since retiring, have been working through Bayesian epistemology on my own — mostly at the kitchen table, with no library access and no colleagues to badger. I have got as far as van Fraassen's Reflection principle and I find myself genuinely stuck, not on the formal statement but on the idea that is supposed to make it plausible. As I understand it, the argument is that my future self stands to my current self rather as an objective chance stands to a present credence in Lewis's Principal Principle: my later opinion is an expert opinion about the very matter my present opinion concerns, and so I ought to defer to it in advance, conditional on my believing that it will take a particular value. That analogy does some heavy lifting, and I cannot tell how much weight it will bear. I should say at the outset that I am not after references or a survey of who has objected to what; I want the conceptual machinery laid out and made intelligible to someone with a working physicist's comfort with probabilities but no formal training in philosophy.

To sharpen my confusion I constructed a case of my own, drawn from a good deal of firsthand experience on funding panels. Call it the Assessor case. I must choose between two proposals, A and B, and my present credence that A is the scientifically stronger of the two is 0.5; I have seen only the abstracts. In six months the panel will distribute the full dossiers and I will read both, in an order fixed by a coin toss. Now, three decades of panel service have convinced me — and I take this to be a fairly robust, quantitative fact about myself — that I suffer a primacy effect of roughly constant size: whichever dossier I read first ends up favoured by about the same margin irrespective of its content, and I am confident that this margin carries no information whatever about scientific merit. Combining that with my estimate of how much the dossiers themselves will move me, I expect my later credence in A to be about 0.8 if A is read first and about 0.35 if A is read second, each with probability one half. So my expectation of my own future credence is 0.575, whereas my present credence is 0.5. Naively averaging over the partition, Reflection appears to instruct me to adopt 0.575 right now — to raise my estimate of A's merit on the strength of nothing but the foreknowledge that I am about to be biased.

What I would like explained is where that instruction goes wrong, or whether it goes wrong at all. Specifically: (i) does Reflection properly apply to the mixture across the coin toss, or only conditional on each branch, and does the answer differ between the two formulations? (ii) My future credence in each branch will be a compound of two things — a genuine response to dossier evidence I cannot now foresee, and a known non-evidential distortion. Both will feel, from the inside at the later time, exactly like a considered judgement about A's merits. Is there any principled way for the principle to tell those components apart, or must the distinction be imported from outside as a restriction on when Reflection applies at all? (iii) Contrast my case with two variants: one in which I take a sedative beforehand, knowing it will amplify the primacy effect, and one in which I simply learn the dossiers' contents and am otherwise undisturbed. In both, my expected future credence departs from my present one; in one deference seems plainly right and in the other plainly wrong. What is the difference, and can it be stated without simply assuming that my future self will be rational and well-informed — which looks suspiciously like assuming the conclusion? (iv) Finally, how does all this interact with Conditionalization and with the diachronic Dutch book arguments? If the bias enters as a distortion of the likelihoods I assign to the dossier evidence, is Reflection merely the shadow that a requirement of diachronic coherence casts on my present opinions, and does anticipated loss of information fail for the same reason that anticipated distortion does, or for a different one? I am trying to work out whether there is a single underlying idea here that survives these cases, or whether Reflection is really a family of principles with different warrants that happen to share a name.
