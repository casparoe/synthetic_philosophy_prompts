I'm trying to build an actual checklist — like a spec — for what a cosmology has to deliver on the Boltzmann brain front before it counts as something I can use to make predictions, and every time I read an argument about this I catch the author smuggling in a requirement they'd never state out loud. So write me the desiderata. For each one: what it demands, the situation that motivates it (a semi-concrete scenario, not a slogan), and whether it's non-negotiable or really just the taste of one camp.

Stuff I know belongs on the list but want sharpened rather than repeated back at me:

- ratio versus rate. I've seen a theory get a pass because its freak-observer production rate is tiny, with nobody mentioning that the phase producing them goes on forever, so the number is what actually swamps everything. Whatever requirement covers this has to say which of those quantities is the one that matters and why.
- something about a framework undercutting the evidence that supports it. If a theory says the typical observer with my memories and my data is a fluctuation, what exactly has gone wrong — is the theory false, or true but unusable, or does that distinction collapse here? I want a requirement that makes that question precise, and then I want you to tell me honestly whether that requirement is legitimate or whether its just a thumb on the scale for whichever escape hatch someone already prefers.
- a robustness condition. I don't want a cosmology that only dodges the problem at parameter values nobody has measured. If the verdict flips depending on an unmeasured decay time or an unmeasured vacuum structure, that should count against it — but I want it phrased so that having unknown parameters isn't automatically fatal, or else nothing passes.
- the criterion for what counts as an observer. Whatever the theory says about you and me it has to say about the fluctuated ones, and the requirement needs to be written so it can't be dodged with a definition that's rigged to exclude exactly the cases in question.
- a no-cheating clause. Banning freaks by fiat isn't a solution, but neither is adjusting your cosmology only ever in the direction that happens to save you, and I want those two failures kept seperate in the list.

Then, on top of the items:

1. which one is doing the most work at excluding candidates — I suspect it's not the one people lead with.
2. whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable. My hunch is you can't have all four of: an eternal low-temperature phase with finite entropy, unitary evolution and the recurrence that buys you, the assumption that I'm a typical draw from the observer distribution, and freak observers being negligible. But I want to know if that's actually airtight or whether one of the four can be weakened without the rest collapsing, and which one is cheapest to give up — and note that whichever you drop is doing the real work in the theory, so it better be stated as a choice and not left implicit.
3. which entries I should demote from genuine requirement to camp preference. The one I'm most suspicious of is treating the early universe's low entropy as something a cosmology must explain rather than something it's allowed to postulate — some arguments treat that as mandatory and others treat it as free, and I want to know which usage survives scrutiny and whether the two camps are even answering the same question.

Keep any scoring of actual proposals to a few sentences at the very end; the criteria are the deliverable and I'll handle the league table myself. Also don't give me references or a reading list, I'm offline for the next two days.
