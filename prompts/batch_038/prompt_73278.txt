I lead a small team building a language-model-driven robot arm, and I got into a dispute with a colleague that I have lost control of. He says our model understands nothing, because the words it produces are only ever hooked up to other words. I wrote his position down as an argument so that I could attack it, and instead it seems to prove that no one understands anything. Here it is.

(1) A system grasps a term only if its use of that term is causally hooked up to the things the term is about — only if the term can make a difference to what the system does in the world.
(2) Our model's use of *cup* is hooked up to nothing but other people's sentences about cups; no cup has ever made a difference to any of its internal states.
(3) So our model does not grasp *cup*.
(4) But consider a person who has read about cups and never seen, held, or drunk from one. Her use of *cup* is likewise hooked up only to sentences, so by (1) she does not grasp it either.
(5) Generalize: most of my vocabulary — neutrino, mortgage, pancreas — came to me from other speakers, and the causal hooks belong to them and to their instruments, not to me.
(6) So no individual grasps most of her own vocabulary; at best the language community does.
(7) A community, though, is just individuals plus their records, and if none of the individuals has the hooks, the records cannot supply them.
(8) Therefore nobody, individually or collectively, grasps most of their own vocabulary — which is absurd, since we plainly do.
(9) So (1) is false: causal contact with referents is not required for understanding, and my colleague has no principled difference between our model and the armchair reader.

I would much rather keep (1) than accept (9), because (9) seems to make understanding something you get for free. Tell me which step to reject, why that step looked unobjectionable when I wrote it, and what the rejection costs — in particular, whether it leaves me with any defensible answer to the colleague. One thing the argument never touches: the arm's vision-and-grasp module shares the same token vocabulary as the language model. Does the language part inherit that contact, or does it remain ungrounded no matter how well the arm behaves?
