I am preparing a chapter defending a Humean account of natural law, and before circulating it I would like a careful consistency audit of the position I actually hold rather than a general assessment of Humeanism. My commitments, stated as plainly as I can:

(a) The world's entire nomological character is fixed by the complete distribution of local, particular matters of fact across spacetime. There is nothing to being a law over and above figuring in the simplest and strongest systematization of that distribution. I accordingly reject governing relations, nomic necessitation, and any nomological entities standing behind the mosaic of particular facts.

(b) Laws nevertheless explain their instances, and in the same robust sense in which we ordinarily say that gravitation explains the tides, or that the pressure rose because the temperature did. I am unwilling to water this down to the claim that laws merely summarize or compress.

(c) Explanation — of the sort invoked both in science and in metaphysics — is factive, asymmetric, and transitive, and I want to work with a single unified notion rather than a scientific notion running alongside a separate metaphysical one.

(d) Consistent with (a), I hold that the distribution of particular facts explains why the laws are what they are; that is all I mean in saying the laws are grounded in the mosaic.

(e) Our inductive practice is reliable, and I want to explain that reliability by appeal to the world's nomological order rather than treat inductive success as a cosmic coincidence.

(f) Counterfactual reasoning proceeds by holding the laws fixed and varying local conditions, and I accept that there are true counterfactuals whose antecedents are never actualized.

(g) There are no necessary connections between distinct existences: local matters of fact in wholly separate regions are modally independent of one another.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Go through these and tell me which commitments pull against which others, and rank the resulting tensions by severity rather than listing them flat. For each tension, say precisely what would have to be surrendered or qualified, what that qualification costs me in the way of explanatory ambition, and whether a cheaper repair is available that I have overlooked. Equally important, tell me which pairs only appear to conflict: I suspect at least one of these is being misdescribed by my own framing, and I would rather discover that here than in seminar. Where a single commitment contains two parts that fare differently under scrutiny, please separate them instead of scoring the whole item. Finally, identify the one revision that would repair the most damage at a stroke, and say whether that revision is a substantive concession to the anti-Humean or merely a verbal retreat.

Please engage with the commitments exactly as stated. Do not substitute weaker versions that are easier to reconcile, and do not read my ordering as an indication of which ones I would be most willing to give up — telling me where to cut is part of what I am asking for.
