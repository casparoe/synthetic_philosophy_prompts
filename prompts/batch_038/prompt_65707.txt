I am drafting the value-theory chapter of a defence of welfare hedonism, and before I commit to any account of pleasure I want the specification of what such an account is supposed to do. Write the desiderata for a theory of what pleasures have in common: what it must deliver if hedonism is to be a substantive thesis rather than a stipulation over a miscellaneous list. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it — the ripe peach alongside the pleasure of finishing a proof and of hearing good news about a friend; the same bodily sensation enjoyed by one person and loathed by another, and by the same person at different times; the gruelling climb that felt awful throughout and is enjoyed in hindsight; the evidence that what makes a state sought after comes apart from what makes it feel good; the demand that the account say what varies when pleasure varies in amount; and the requirement, if it is a genuine one, that the pleasantness of an experience explain why that experience is good for its subject rather than merely mark it off. Then separate the requirements every candidate must meet from those that turn out to be the local preferences of one camp — that pleasure must be a single introspectible quality, or that it must be non-propositional so that it can be attributed to infants and non-human animals. Say which single requirement eliminates the most candidates among the three main families of answer (a shared felt quality, a propositional attitude of being pleased, a functional or dispositional role), and whether any pair of the genuine requirements looks jointly unsatisfiable; if one does, state that plainly, since it is the result I most want. Close with two or three sentences on whether hedonism can survive as a claim about a kind with a common nature, or must be recast as a claim about value that leaves the plurality of pleasures intact.
