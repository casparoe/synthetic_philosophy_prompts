In our philosophy-of-religion reading group the maxim "nothing comes from nothing" has become the pivot of a stalemate. One member invokes it to establish the causal premise of the kalam argument — that whatever begins to exist has a cause — on the ground that the alternative is something's arising from sheer non-being. Another replies that quantum field theory refutes it outright, since vacuum fluctuations yield particle–antiparticle pairs with no antecedent cause. They have now had the same exchange three times without either budging, which makes me suspect the maxim is doing several distinct jobs and that each of them is attacking a different one. A third member's contribution has muddied things further: he insists the contingency version of the cosmological argument has no need of the maxim, since it concerns explanation rather than causation, and that the two principles are routinely run together.

Could you lay out the different precise claims the maxim might be expressing, ordered from the most trivial to the most ambitious? I have in mind differences along several axes: what kind of principle it is (conceptual or analytic, metaphysical, empirical, epistemic, explanatory); what the second "nothing" amounts to (no concrete objects? no spacetime? no laws? no abstracta? no agents?); whether it is a claim about items beginning to exist or about the total quantity of being, and whether that quantity is even the sort of thing that could be conserved; and whether it ranges over things within the universe or over the universe as a whole. For each reading, tell me whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, a substantive and doubtful one, or simply false, and whether it is strong enough — alone or with an auxiliary premise — to deliver the causal premise the kalam argument requires. Say too, for each reading, whether the vacuum-fluctuation objection genuinely engages it or misses it, including whether an excitation of an already-existing field counts as something "beginning to exist" in the relevant sense. Finally, distinguish the causal principle the kalam argument needs from the explanatory principle the contingency argument needs, and assess whether my third member is right that the maxim is being asked to support both at once.

Please give the readings as a table with these columns: `label` (a short name for the reading); `precise_claim` (the thesis stated so that it could be assessed for truth); `kind_of_principle`; `sense_of_'nothing'`; `status` (one of: platitude / substantive and defensible / substantive and doubtful / false); `yields_the_causal_premise` (yes, no, or only with an auxiliary premise — name the premise if so); `quantum_objection` (engages or misses, with a clause saying why). After the table, add a short prose diagnosis: which reading each of the two disputants most plausibly has in mind, whether their disagreement is substantive or a slide between readings, and which single question would most efficiently settle it in the next session.
