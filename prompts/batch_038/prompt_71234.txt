Our logic reading group has been stuck on the liar for like two years now and it's driving me up the wall. Everyone in the room can do the formal stuff — we've all worked through Kripke's fixed point construction, we all know how the revenge liar is supposed to bite the gap theorist, we all know the diagonal arguement you use to generate the offending sentence in the first place. Nobody disagrees about any of the mathematics. And still nobody has moved an inch, and I've started to suspect we never will.

Roughly the camps: one says the liar sentence is both true and false and you should just swallow it and move to a paraconsistent logic; one says it's neither, a gap; one says the paradox only shows that a language can't contain its own truth predicate and you need a hierarchy going up forever; one says the truth predicate is context-sensitive so each use quietly shifts the extension and there's no single sentence around to have a semantic value at all. And then there's one person, who I think is either onto something or copping out, who proposed we stay agnostic: accept that gluts are possible as a general matter, refuse to say of any particular sentence that it is one, and reason in a logic that lets you do that safely. Half the room said that isn't a position, it's a refusal to have one.

Here's my hypothesis, and I'd rather you attack it than nod along: I think this isn't a disagreement about the liar at all. It's three different job descriptions all being advertised under the heading of solving the paradox — one camp wants a semantic value for every meaningful sentence, one wants a logic you can actually reason in without everything following from a contradiction, one wants classical reasoning left untouched for the vast non-pathological majority of sentences — and once you see that, most of the arguments are people scoring hits on a target nobody in the room is aiming at. Right or wrong? And if I'm wrong, point me at the genuinely substantive disagreement, the one where two camps are answering the same question and at most one of them can be correct.

So: diagnose the structure of the standoff. Specifically I want to know whether the revenge liar is a real refutation of the gap and hierarchy views, or whether it begs the question in exactly the way the dialetheists say it does — does it assume that negation and the truth predicate behave classically, which is precisely what's in dispute? Whether preserving classical logic for the unproblematic cases is a substantive constraint on a theory of truth or an aesthetic preference wearing a lab coat. Whether the agnostic position is stable, or whether reasoning about the revenge sentence eventually forces you out of agnosticism in one direction or the other. And what would count as progress here — a theorem, a distinction nobody has drawn yet, a frank change of subject, someone conceding the question is ill posed? Or is the honest answer that this is bedrock, that both sides are just making different choices about which feature of our practice to preserve, and that this group will be having the identical argument in twenty years?

Assume I already know all the positions. I want reasons and a diagnosis, not a tour.
