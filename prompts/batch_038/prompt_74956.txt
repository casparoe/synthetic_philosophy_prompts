I am writing the framework section of a dissertation chapter on whether video-game characters can be objects of moral concern, and before I work through the candidate accounts I want the specification they will be judged against. Write the desiderata for a theory of the moral significance of virtual characters: the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it may not collapse, and the empirical commitments it must not smuggle in. Anchor each requirement in a case; at minimum cover the following, and add any I have overlooked.

1. Provenance: two NPCs that are behaviorally indistinguishable — one a hand-authored dialogue tree, one a live language model with persistent memory of the player, to be switched off when the servers close. Must the theory assign them the same status, and on what grounds?
2. Reloading: killing a companion in a permadeath game versus killing her and reloading the save.
3. Gradient: a named companion with a biography versus a faceless mob enemy, without making status all-or-nothing.
4. The callous player: the theory must not license the player who feels nothing, yet must explain why players' guilt tracks narrative investment rather than anything in the character.
5. The designer's side: a character deliberately written so that harming it harms the player — whose duty is engaged here, and owed to whom?
6. The contested evidence on violent play and real-world aggression: whether the theory's verdicts depend on how that debate resolves.

For each, mark it non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp (consciousness-based, relational, virtue-ethical, or grounded in our attitudes toward fiction), identify any subset you judge jointly unsatisfiable, and name the single requirement that eliminates the most contenders. Close by saying where you think the list itself is most likely to be wrong: which requirement you would surrender under pressure, and what argument would make you surrender it. Give the list as a table with the columns desideratum | motivating case | why it is a requirement | negotiability | views it excludes.
