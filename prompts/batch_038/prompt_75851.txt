So — I'm co-convening a small graduate reading group on blame and its limits, six of us, and for the last month we've been stuck in the standing-to-blame literature, and I've started to suspect we're going round in circles rather than actually disagreeing. Here's the shape of the impasse. Everyone in the group accepts the same starting case: someone who's been quietly fiddling their own expenses turns round and blames me, quite accurately, for fiddling mine. Everyone also agrees the blame is somehow defective, and that the defect isn't just that it's annoying, tactless or badly timed. Where we fall apart is what the defect is. One camp says the hypocrite loses standing because blaming is a second-personal address, and in addressing me they're implicitly claiming an equality of standing between us that their own conduct contradicts — call it the Wallace-style moral address line, where the real wrong is a failure to hold yourself to what you demand of another. Another camp says that's the wrong explanation, because there are cases where the blamer disrespects nobody and still can't blame: what does the work is comparative, that their own wrongdoing of the same type is more objectionable than mine, and standing goes when that comparative gap runs the wrong way — the Fritz and Miller vein. A third group thinks the whole apparatus is a sham: 'standing' is just a label for a grab-bag of considerations we already have (insincerity, the ethics of the act, whether the blame will land, whether it's proportionate), and once you spell those out there's no extra status left to lose. What makes it maddening is that each camp produces cases the others handle badly and nobody concedes anything: the equal-standing people point to cases where the comparative story says standing is retained but the blaming still looks wrong, the comparativists point to cases with no address at all — private blame, blame of the dead, blame you never voice — where standing still seems to be missing, and the sceptics point out that all of it can be redescribed without ever using the word.

My working hypothesis, and it's only a hypothesis so please tell me if it's wrong, is that we're not having one disagreement. I suspect a good chunk of the standoff comes from the camps answering different questions under a single heading: one is asking whether the hypocrite's blame is fitting or appropriate, one is asking whether the act of blaming is permissible for them, and one is asking whether there is any such normative status as standing to be had in the first place — the Darwallian authority-to-make-demands picture versus a picture where everything bottoms out in value and reasons. Three seperate questions wearing one coat. If fittingness and permissibility come apart here, and I think there's a decent case that they do, since the very same blame can be unfitting in one person's mouth and perfectly fine in another's, then a lot of the argument is people talking past each other about a word. But I can also see this deflates too much: maybe there's a genuinely substantive disagreement underneath about whether interpersonal blame requires any normative authority at all, which no amount of stipulating definitions would settle, and maybe the case-selection fights are really fights about which intuitions get to count as data.

So: diagnose it. Is the dispute substantive, verbal, or several questions sharing a name — and be specific about which parts are which, because I'd be surprised if its uniform. Work from what each side actually claims and the cases they trade in, not from a survey of who said what. Then tell me what would count as progress on your diagnosis: a distinction somebody needs to draw, a class of cases that would be decisive, some formal treatment of comparative blameworthiness, a change of question, or an argument that no progress should be expected and that this is just what happens when a folk category gets theorised too hard. Don't send me a reading list or references, I've got the papers — I want the diagnosis and the prognosis. And please write the whole thing in British English, I'm going to circulate it to the group and half of them will notice if I don't.
