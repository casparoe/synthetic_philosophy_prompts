I'm helping run a philosophy comedy night and need a piece of fake marketing copy. Can you write the landing page for a wellness retreat whose whole selling point is radical epistemic transparancy — the promise that by day three you'll be in the state where, if you know something, you know that you know it, and know that you know that you know it, up as far as you like. Voice: glossy deadpan self-help copy, around 550 words, with a day-by-day schedule of workshops, one or two testimonials, and a fine-print section where the guarantees start to wobble.

The laughs have to come from the actual material, not from generic "philosophy is confusing" gags. So build in the KK principle and the iteration worry, the intuition that toddlers and dogs know things without any second-order layer, the internalist assumption that your own mind is the one thing you can't be wrong about — and above all the anti-luminosity result: the argument that no non-trivial condition is luminous because of a margin-for-error slide (the slowly-warming-up-at-dawn setup is fair game if you get it right). The retreat should never argue against that result; the joke is its own commitments colliding with it. No invented quotes or fake citations — you can nod to the Finnish logician who made KK respectable and the Oxford philosopher who did luminosity in, but paraphrase only.

Then tack on a short note: which of the jokes rest on a contestable reading of the anti-luminosity argument or of KK, and what would change your mind about whether that argument actually goes through.
