I was up till like 2am arguing with my friend Priya about Lewis on convention and I lost. She's writing her dissertation chapter on it and she is a hardliner. Her track record, all of which she gave me in one go:

- driving on the right: convention, obviously.
- which of the two rival videocassette formats won out: NOT a convention, even though it was a self-sustaining equilibrium with a perfectly good alternative available. Her reason: the losing camp wanted the other format, so the population didn't have roughly the same preferences about the outcomes.
- tipping 20% at restaurants: NOT a convention. Her reason: it isn't coordination at all, each of us would rather everyone else tipped and we didn't, and what actually keeps it going is shame plus what servers expect.
- her family's rule that you say goodnight to the dog: convention, yes, even though its six people and nobody outside the house would notice if it died tomorrow.
- wearing clothes in public: NOT a convention, despite the mutual expectations and despite nothing being natural about it. Reason: there's no alternative the population could actually switch to short of coercion, so it isn't arbitrary in the right way.
- which end of the loaf she cuts first: NOT a convention, because nobody expects anybody else to do it any particular way, its just habit that happens to line up.
- a country decimalizing its currency by act of parliament: convention, and the fact that a legislature decreed it doesn't disqualify anything.
- a department that officially mandated reply-all on every email: NOT a convention, because nobody did it. A decree without uptake is just paper.

The new case I want to spring on her: a 40,000-person online forum for appliance repair. Over the last year, posters started appending one arbitrary token — a wrench emoji — to any post that's asking a question rather than reporting a fix. A rival token (a lightbulb) caught on for a couple months last spring and then died out on its own. Basically everyone does it, everyone expects everyone else to, and untagged posts get replies saying add the tag. Everyone agrees a tagged feed beats an untagged one and nobody cares which token gets used. Two complications: (i) it's 40,000 anonymous strangers, so it's hard to see how anyone knows that anyone knows that anyone knows anything here, and (ii) the ranking algorithm now buries untagged posts, so people tag mainly to get seen rather than because they want other people to tag. Also about 200 members have started refusing to tag on purpose as a protest against the mods, and the mods just proposed pinning the wrench emoji in the rules and auto-hiding anything untagged.
