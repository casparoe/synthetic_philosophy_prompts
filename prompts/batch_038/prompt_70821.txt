I'm reading a pop philosophy-of-time book and theres a sidebar on Gödel's 1949 rotating universe. It goes: 'Gödel found an exact solution of Einstein's field equations in which the entire universe rotates. In it, a rocket that flies a wide enough loop comes back to its own past, so you could shake hands with your younger self. Gödel took this to prove that time isn't real: if the future is already sitting there waiting for you, nothing can genuinely pass or lapse. Einstein read the paper and conceded the point. Relativists today study the Gödel solution mainly as a reductio — it shows general relativity has to be modified, and since we know the real universe contains no closed timelike curves, we also know it isn't rotating.'

How much of that would survive contact with an actual relativist or philosopher of physics? Can you go through it claim by claim and separate what's right, what's fair-but-loose popularizing, and what's just wrong? In particular I'd like to know whether Gödel's argument really runs through 'you could meet your younger self' at all, or whether that's standing in for something else he was trying to show about time — and whether the last sentence has the order of evidence backwards.
