I'm the autonomy tech lead at a company that makes two consumer robots — a little indoor assistant on wheels and a yard robot that does weeding and edging. About a year ago our internal review board (two safety engineers, a product lawyer, and one ethicist we contract part-time) decided that how much scrutiny a new capability gets should scale with what kind of agent it is on its own, not with how well it scores on our benchmarks. Their stated rationale was Dennett's four creature types, which one of them had read about somewhere: Darwinian (competence is baked in, hardwired, no learning in the field), Skinnerian (the thing improves by getting rewarded and punished in deployment), Popperian (it carries an internal model it can run offline, so a bad plan can get killed in the model instead of killing the robot or the cat), and Gregorian (its competence can grow by picking up external tools — symbols, language, other people's know-how — rather than only by us shipping it an update). The board wanted tier 1 stuff rubber-stamped and tier 4 stuff to go through a full review with outside counsel.

The problem is nobody on the board can actually apply the taxonomy, and it's costing us weeks. Last quarter we burned a 90-minute arguement on whether a robot that simulates a route before driving it counts as Popperian, and tabled it. So write the thing the board should have had from the start: a classification procedure that a competent engineer with zero philosophy background can run in maybe 30–45 minutes on a capability proposal doc, with the questions in the order they should be asked, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, whether the tiers are cumulative or mutually exclusive (nobody agrees on this), which pieces of evidence the engineer should demand from the team (ablations? behavior in novel situations? what happens when you cut the cloud connection?), and the points where the procedure has to stop and hand the call to a human because it's a question of degree rather than fact. Please mark every step that is secretly a judgment call wearing a lab coat — I'd rather know.

And it has to handle the wrinkle that I think wrecks the analogy for artifacts, which is that in a real animal the bottom tier means natural selection did the designing. For our robots the "hardwired" competence was designed by very clever Popperian/Gregorian humans in an office, and it gets refined by an OTA update pipeline, so a tier-1 robot is not remotely like a sea squirt — it's a sophisticated thing wearing a low tier label. Tell me how the procedure should treat that: does the tier attach to the artifact as shipped, to the artifact plus its designers plus the update pipeline, or to something else? Pick one, be explicit about what the choice costs you, and make sure whichever you pick doesn't just quietly make every product we ship tier 4 by inheritance from us.

Then run the procedure on three real proposals:
(a) the yard robot's obstacle avoidance: an end-to-end learned policy, weights frozen at manufacture, no model anyone can query, and no on-device learning at all — nothing gets rewarded or punished in the field;
(b) the indoor robot's household-model feature: it builds and continuously updates a predictive model of the house (who's home, when the dishwasher runs, where the dog sleeps) and uses it to schedule its own tasks; if the model is wrong it wastes time, it doesn't hurt anything;
(c) our prototype teaching interface, where a person can tell the robot a new routine in plain English — after I drop the kids off at 8, go through the upstairs and put away anything on the floor — and it executes it later, using an off-the-shelf language model plus our fixed skill library and no fine-tuning.

I'll be honest up front: for (c) the answer I want is the top tier, because that's the claim in our launch deck and the board already waved it through once. If your procedure lands somewhere else, say so plainly and tell me what we'd have to change — in the product or in the claim — to actually earn it. And finish with the cases this procedure can never settle, where a tier number would be fake precision and the board should just argue. No references or reading list, I just need the procedure and the three walkthroughs.
