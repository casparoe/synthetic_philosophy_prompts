I set the final for an upper-level seminar on the socialist calculation debate (third-year PPE students who have had intermediate micro). Write one 25-mark essay question, answerable in 45 minutes, that cannot be passed by narrating the debate — no credit for timelines, for a Mises/Hayek/Lange recital of labels and positions, or for identifying works. The reasoning should be forced by an institutional stipulation built into the question: a Lange-type board sets producer-goods prices parametrically and revises them nightly against reported surpluses and shortages, and additionally runs a standing auction in which any manager may bid for publicly owned capital to operate a production process appearing nowhere on the board's technology menu, entry is free, and losing bids are disclosed to the winner. Students must argue whether that addition answers Hayek's objection, and whether the verdict changes when the objection in view is Mises's rather than Hayek's.

Give a model answer of about 700 words and a marking scheme that separates a first from a strong second by naming the moves each must contain. At minimum, the scheme should require a clean distinction between the claim that no equilibrium price vector exists and the claim that no one can arrive at or identify one, and an account of why an adjustment rule operating on quantities already reported to the board differs in kind from the discovery of data nobody possesses in advance. Finish with the two errors that should cost the most marks — treating a general-equilibrium existence theorem as a refutation of Mises, and folding Hayek's knowledge argument into an incentives argument — and one line on why each fails.
