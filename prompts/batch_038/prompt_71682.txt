so I've been asked to write down where I've landed on the "is the wavefunction real" question for a reading group, and I'd like a sanity check before I present it. Here's the package:

1. I'm psi-epistemic in the Harrigan–Spekkens sense: distinct wavefunctions can correspond to the same underlying physical situation. So ψ isn't an object or a field or anything spread out in configuration space; it's more like a description carrying probabilities.
2. But I'm not a QBist. Given a preperation there's a unique correct state assignment, and the probabilities are objective chances, not anybody's degrees of belief.
3. Quantum mechanics is complete. No hidden variables, no Bohmian particles, nothing extra under the hood.
4. I take PBR seriously as a theorem — I don't think it's a trick, and I don't think a realist gets to just shrug off preparation independence. My answer to it is that it presupposes the world divides into independently preparable subsystems, and I deny that: entanglement means separability is a myth, the whole is prior to the parts.
5. One definite outcome per run, no branching worlds, and measurement is just an ordinary physical interaction with nothing special about it. I also don't want a stochastic collapse law bolted onto the dynamics.
6. The way to make "is ψ real?" precise rather than verbal is the ontological models framework — preparation, ontic state, response functions. Outside that framework I think the debate is just people talking past each other.

Does this hang together? Tell me which of these are genuinely pulling against each other and which only look like they do, rank the tensions from worst to mildest, and for each one say what I'd have to give up and what giving it up costs me elsewhere in the list. I don't have a favorite to sacrifice, so please don't assume I do. Also: how confident are you in the diagnosis overall, and which single claim in your answer are you least sure of?
