I am drafting the specification for a short methods checklist that an editorial office would apply to any manuscript — or to the press release accompanying one — that invokes a heritability estimate. Before I commit to a particular account of what such an estimate licenses, I want the desiderata written out: the requirements that any interpretation of the content of a heritability figure (call it an account of what H² or h² means) must satisfy in order to count as adequate.

Please anchor the list to a single running case so that the requirements are testable rather than abstract. Suppose a twin cohort drawn from one national sample reports h² = 0.62 for a composite score I will call school-readiness; all twins were reared together, no environmental covariates were modelled, and the accompanying release states that 62% of a child's readiness comes from their genes and the remaining 38% from their upbringing and schooling.

For each desideratum, give three things: the requirement stated precisely; the case or design feature that motivates it, i.e. what goes wrong in its absence; and a failure signature — a number, a reported statistic, a design detail, or a turn of phrase that is visible in published material and that indicates the requirement is not met, so that a non-specialist editor could check it off without recomputing anything. Cover at minimum: the phenomena an account must explain rather than explain away (traits with near-total heritability that nonetheless disappear under an environmental change; estimates that rise as environments are equalised; within-population variance partitions that are silent on between-group differences in means; genotype–environment correlation and interaction in family designs); the distinctions it may not collapse (broad- from narrow-sense figures, the individual from the population, an estimate from its portability to a different environment, and the variance attributable to genes from the variance that a decision to change an environment would remove); and the inferential licences it must positively withhold.

Mark each item as non-negotiable for any account or as a commitment of a particular camp, and say explicitly which pairs of desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied — I expect at least one tension between an estimate's availability from purely observational data and its usefulness in predicting how a different population would respond to intervention. If the list itself is the result in that case, say so. Then identify which single desideratum does the most to exclude candidates, and score, in a few lines each, the four readings an editor actually meets: the proportion-of-the-trait-due-to-genes reading, the variance-partition reading, an interventionist reading on which h² concerns the effect of manipulating genotypes, and a deflationary reading on which heritability estimates license almost nothing.

A table is acceptable for the requirements. Hard limit: 900 words, scoring included.
