I'm a junior and my school has this independent research thing where you write a paper and then defend it to a panel of three teachers, one of whom is always the philosophy person. I've locked in my topic: the heterogeneity problem for hedonism. Basically the objection that pleasure isn't one kind of thing, so "maximize pleasure" is secretly "maximize this random list of unrelated states." The old move where someone says a playground game is worth exactly as much as poetry if the pleasure is equal in amount only works if there's a single amount to compare.

My advisor made me commit to defending ONE account of what all pleasures have in common, because 3000 words isn't enough for two. Here are my five candidates:

1. a shared distinctive feel running through all of them (a hedonic tone) even though everything else about the experiences differs
2. nothing phenomenal in common at all; what unifies them is that each is, while it's happening, liked or wanted-to-continue
3. what's shared is functional: pleasures are the states that grab attention, that you don't try to end, and that reinforce whatever you were doing
4. there is no genuine common property — pleasure is a loose family-resemblance cluster, and the objection over-demands unity from a concept
5. the unity is causal rather than qualitative: they're all downstream of the same reward machinery, so the term picks out a kind by its origin, not by its feel

I need you to score each of the five, 0 to 10, against four hard cases I made up — so a 5x4 grid of numbers — and then give me an overall ranking of which one I should actually defend. The cases:

A. retrospective: you only realize on the drive home that the afternoon was great; you didn't feel anything hedonic at the time
B. mixed: a brutal hill climb, or the first twenty seconds of a cold shower, where the whole is awful but there's a part you'd insist was pleasant
C. ugly: winning an arguement, or laughing at someone's expense
D. absorbed: total flow, attention completely eaten by the activity, and afterwards you'd say it was one of the best hours of your week

One sentence per score saying why. For each case, also tell me which candidate handles it best and which one gets destroyed by it. Then the overall ranking with the winner named plainly, not hedged.

Last thing: for your winner, what's the single biggest hole in it that a smart panelist will find in ten seconds, and what should I say back? I'd way rather walk in knowing that than get caught.
