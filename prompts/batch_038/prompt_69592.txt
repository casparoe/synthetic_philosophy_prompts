I work on the specification side of an autonomous procurement agent: software that receives, accepts, declines, and renegotiates component-purchase offers from many counterparties over a long horizon. My team has been arguing about which constraints to write into its decision policy, and in the last design review a senior colleague twice dismissed my worry with the sentence "if it can be money pumped it isn't rational, and if it can't be, it is."

The worry I had raised is concrete. The agent's ranking over bundles is non-transitive in some regions of price space, because three subsystems score bundles on different criteria and we aggregate their verdicts by simple majority. It also has no capacity to bind itself in advance: every offer is evaluated afresh, in situ, against whatever the current holdings and current scores are. Our red team's job is to try to construct sequences of offers that leave the agent back where it started with less money. So far they have succeeded on two toy configurations and failed on everything else.

My colleague used the slogan to do two quite different jobs. First, to argue that transitivity need not appear as an explicit design constraint at all, on the ground that a pump sequence can only exist if some individual decision within it was already defective — so fixing decisions one at a time is sufficient and the sequence is not a separate object of assessment. Second, to argue that passing the red team is a complete certification of the policy: no exploitable sequence found, nothing further to say about the agent's rationality. I suspect the two uses pull in opposite directions and that neither survives contact with the details, but I cannot yet say why.

Could you separate the distinct precise theses that this slogan might be asserting? For each candidate reading, I would like: an explicit statement of what is being claimed, indexed to the relevant parameters — whether the object of assessment is a single choice, a plan, or a standing disposition; what the exploiter knows about the agent's preferences; whether transactions are costless; whether the agent's information changes as the sequence proceeds; and whether "can be pumped" means a pump exists in principle or one has been exhibited. Then classify each reading as near-trivial, substantive and defensible, or false or heavily qualified, and say which readings would actually license the first use, which would license the second, and whether any single reading licenses both.

Two points deserve particular attention. One is the case in which every choice in the sequence, judged on its own with the information available at that moment, is unimpeachable: can pumpability then be a defect of the sequence without being a defect of any member, and what would have to be true of the agent — persistence, identity of the decision-maker across time, an ability or inability to precommit — for that verdict to be more than a verbal dispute? The other is the inferential gap between "our red team found nothing" and "nothing is to be found": what would have to hold for a finite battery of constructed sequences to certify immunity in the sense the slogan needs?

Please present this as a menu I can bring to the next review, with verdicts attached, rather than as one recommended interpretation defended at length.
