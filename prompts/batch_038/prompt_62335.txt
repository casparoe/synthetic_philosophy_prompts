At a talk last week the speaker's whole framing of chaos was that a chaotic deterministic system is basically a pseudorandom number generator with a physical seed: the initial condition is the seed, the equations of motion are the algorithm, the exponential divergence of nearby trajectories is the bit-mixing, and if you knew the seed exactly you could replay the entire output. His running example was a driven double pendulum. He used the picture to make two claims -- that the probabilities in classical statistical mechanics are all in the map and none of them in the territory, and that you therefore need something like quantum mechanics before objective chance gets a foothold anywhere in nature.

My friend, who writes PRNGs for a living, says the analogy is worse then it looks, because a seed is a finite string that somebody chose and could in principle recover, whereas the state of a physical chaotic system is a real number that no finite measurement pins down -- so the ignorance is in principle, not just in practice. Separately, the speaker waved at the shadowing lemma to say that a noisy numerical integration is always tracking some genuine trajectory, and my friend muttered that its the wrong comfort to take from that result.

Can you audit the analogy properly? I want the correspondences laid out explicitly -- seed, algorithm, mixing, period length, the statistical test battery -- and then the disanalogies sorted into the ones that actually threaten his two conclusions versus the ones that are only skin deep. Also tell me which features of a PRNG the analogy quietly imports that a double pendulum doesn't have. While you're in there: someone in the audience said instruments ten times more precise double your forecast window and the speaker nodded along, and I don't think that's right -- work out what ten times more precision actually buys you and where the audience member's intuition goes wrong. Then give me a committed verdict rather than a menu: does the all-classical-probability-is-epistemic claim survive the audit, does the no-chance-without-quantum-mechanics claim, and if the analogy is doing damage, what's a better one for the job it was asked to do. If you have room, say whether the same audit goes through for Bohmian mechanics, where the hidden seed is the initial configuration and there's a probability measure over it that everybody treats as doing real explanatory work.
