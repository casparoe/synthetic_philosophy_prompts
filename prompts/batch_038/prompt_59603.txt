I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy of math, working on a chapter about whether our intuitions about set theory track anything, and before I write anything I want to nail down what I actually believe about choice, going by my gut on particular theorems rather than by any official line. Here are my verdicts. Treat them as data, not as a request for a survey — I don't want references or a history of the debate, just the argument.

Fine, obviously fine:
- Countable choice. I can't take seriously a universe where it fails.
- Dependent choice. Basically all the real analysis I actually use sits on it.
- The ultrafilter lemma, in the form "every boolean algebra has a prime ideal." I want the completeness theorem for languages of any size, and I use ultraproducts over ω constantly.
- Tychonoff for arbitrary products of compact Hausdorff spaces.
- Hahn-Banach.
- Every vector space has a basis. This one feels like pure bookkeeping to me: you extend a linearly independent set until it spans, and I feel no more queasiness about that than about Zorn's lemma for chains in a poset I can picture.

Not fine:
- That every set can be well-ordered — well, specifically, a well-ordering of the reals. That object feels like it shouldn't exist. Not in the sense that I think I could prove it doesn't, but I'd be deeply suspicious of anyone who claimed to have one.
- Banach-Tarski. A ball cut into five pieces and reassembled, by rotations alone, into two balls. I would give up something to avoid this and I don't know what.
- The existence of a non-Lebesgue-measurable set of reals. My dream universe is Solovay's: DC plus every set of reals measurable and with the Baire property, paid for with an inaccessible. I'd pay that.

My working principle so far, such as it is: accept a choice principle when the choices are bookkeeping — you have to pick one element out of each member of a family and nothing about the shape of the picks matters — and reject it when it hands you a geometrically visible object, like a well-ordering of the line or a decomposition of the ball.

Three things I want from you.

1. Is there a principle in the neighborhood of mine that survives contact with this list? Give me one or two candidates, stated precisely enough that I could apply them to a theorem I haven't thought about, and say exactly which of my verdicts each one forces me to drop. I'd rather have a clean principle that costs me two verdicts than a baroque one that saves all ten, but show me both if that's what's on offer.

2. Cases I've left open. Does every field have an algebraic closure — is that bookkeeping or is it smuggling? Could there be an infinite set with no countably infinite subset, and is it a problem for me that I've already said yes to countable choice? And this one I strongly suspect is a trap: for any two sets, either one injects into the other. That feels completely harmless and I have no idea what it costs. Tell me what each candidate principle predicts for all three, plus one further case of your choosing where you think my intuitions would land somewhere I wouldn't expect.

3. The one I'm most worried about. My bookkeeping line includes bases for every vector space, but a basis for R over Q is a Hamel basis, and I have a vague memory that a Hamel basis immediately gets you a non-measurable set, maybe even a non-measurable additive function. If that's right, walk me through the construction, because its the one I keep coming back to: it would mean my two favorite verdicts are in direct conflict. Same question for the ultrafilter lemma — does accepting nonprincipal ultrafilters on ω commit me to a non-measurable set of reals whether I like it or not, and if it does, is there a version of my position that keeps the completeness theorem and loses only the ultraproducts?

Be blunt about which of these verdicts are simply incoherent as a package. But also tell me if there's a smaller revision than the ones I've been imagining: I've been assuming that if something big has to go it's either my objection to well-ordering the continuum or my objection to the ball-doubling, and I haven't seriously considered dumping the ultrafilter stuff, which is the thing I use most.
