speech seminar paper comparing the US and European approaches to hate speech, and my section has collapsed into two analogies that everyone keeps reaching for. one side says the marketplace of ideas sorts it out: bad speech is like a shoddy product, competition exposes it, and banning it is like banning a brand instead of letting buyers discover it's junk. the other side says that's naive and the right picture is pollution -- hate speech is a diffuse externality the "market" will never price in, so you need a regulator, like with a factory on a river. my prof's objection was that you can only regulate an externality if you can name the emitter and the victim, and hate speech supposedly has neither; a classmate shot back that diffuseness is exactly what makes something an externality rather than an assault, so the objection proves too much.

can you audit both? give me the correspondences (who's the producer, the consumer, the price signal, the regulator, the river), where each mapping breaks, and whether either analogy can handle the doctrinal difference that actually matters here -- that american tests require advocacy aimed at and likely to produce imminent lawless action, while european incitement-to-hatred offences don't require anything like that. then say which conclusion, if any, each one can carry, and whether the two sides are really disagreeing through the analogies or the analogies are just carrying the disagreement. finally: where's your audit weakest, and what consideration would change your mind -- what would make you say one of these is doing real argumentative work instead of dressing up a prior view?
