I'm assembling a self-diagnostic for a reading group on mental content — the "which theory of aboutness do you actually hold?" variety — built around the dispute over whether any intentionality is original rather than derived. Please write ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options phrased as first-person verdicts about the case rather than as theory labels, plus a scoring key mapping patterns of answers onto these positions: an intrinsic, biology-dependent original intentionality of the Searlean sort; Dennett's collapse of the original/derived distinction; teleosemantics; informational or indicator semantics; and an interpretationist view on which content is fixed by what a competent ascriber is obliged to attribute. Attach standard names and labels only where you are confident they are the standard ones, and describe the view otherwise.

Because the questions do the sorting, none of them may ask whether a machine really has beliefs or whether the reader finds some named doctrine plausible. Please make sure the set includes: a two-bitser-style case in which one and the same physical state can be read as tracking either of two different conditions depending on how the device is wired and where it is deployed; a case of systematic misrepresentation — the pellet-snapping frog or your own substitute — on which the options differ over whether the state is a false belief about flies or a true belief about small dark moving things; a case that turns a human being's own states over to exactly the same treatment as the machine's; and a case with no history behind it, where a state is physically indistinguishable from a believer's but nothing selected, designed, or conditioned it.

In the scoring key, flag answer patterns that come out unstable instead of forcing them into a position, list any pair of positions your questions cannot actually separate and say why, and identify any position no coherent pattern leads to — diagnosing that as incoherent, unattractive, or merely neglected. Mark any entry you regard as contestable rather than presenting the taxonomy as settled. And if you think the instrument cannot work — that these theories are underdetermined by case-intuitions, or that I have mis-stated the dispute — tell me so plainly instead of handing back a tidy quiz that does not discriminate.
