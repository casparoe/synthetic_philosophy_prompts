Before I read another page of the debate over the criteria of authenticity, I want the specification of what a criterion would have to be in order to do the work asked of it. Treat a criterion as a rule that assigns each unit of the tradition a likelihood ratio: the probability that the unit would have survived in the form we have it if it goes back to Jesus, over the probability that it would have taken that form if it were generated within the early communities. Write the desiderata such a rule must satisfy — including at least how it is to cope with the fact that transmission selects for whatever served later communities, which biases the surviving record toward invention and toward suppression and modification alike; what independence among criteria could mean when several of them key off overlapping features of the same small corpus; what would have to be true of the oral period for anything established about texts to carry over to events; and what the rule owes us for the units that no criterion can classify in either direction. For each desideratum, give the consideration or case behind it and mark it as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp. Then tell me which subset of your desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and why — that is the part I most want — along with which single one does the most damage to the criteria in widest use. Please do not narrate the successive quests or list the standard criteria; assume I already have both.
