For thirty-six years I ran the analytical section of a public health laboratory, and nearly all of that time was spent under a quality regime in which no procedure was permitted to generate a reportable result until it had been formally validated: specificity, limit of detection, repeatability within an operator and reproducibility between laboratories, robustness, an uncertainty budget, and a traceable reference standard against which the whole chain of inference could be audited. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy of mind with a local group, and it has struck me that the most famous procedure in that field for deciding whether a system has a mind is habitually discussed as though it were an experiment, an assay, a test in the laboratory sense, while almost nobody asks what it would look like if it were actually held to the standards we applied to a method for the determination of lead in drinking water. I should like to find out.

Please write a satirical standards document of roughly 1,000 to 1,300 words: a method-validation guideline, issued by an invented international body of your own naming, governing the use of conversational indistinguishability testing — a human interrogator, text only, a machine respondent and a human respondent, and a verdict as to which is which — as an analytical procedure for the detection of mind. Write it in the impersonal house style of such documents: numbered sections, passive constructions, no authorial presence, and not one wink. A reader should be able to tell precisely what is being ironised without the document ever conceding it.

It should contain, at minimum:

- Scope and field of application, including an explicit statement of the decision a positive result is intended to support, which the document must specify rather than presume.
- Terms and definitions, in which the measurand is defined and the difficulty of defining it becomes visible.
- Apparatus, materials and personnel, including the qualification and number of interrogators.
- Procedure, covering duration, blinding, and the handling and retention of transcripts.
- Validation characteristics: specificity; limit of detection; repeatability between interrogators and reproducibility between laboratories; robustness to changes of medium and questioning style; and a short measurement-uncertainty budget set out as a table.
- Reference materials and traceability, addressing what would serve as a positive control, what would serve as a negative control, and whether any chain of traceability to a primary standard exists.
- Interferences and known failure modes, including a respondent that stores and retrieves prepared answers without generating any; a respondent that manipulates symbols it does not understand; and a human respondent who, knowing the protocol, performs badly in order to appear machine-like.
- A closing statement of fitness for purpose.

The humour must come from getting both disciplines right. A former colleague of mine who never read a page of philosophy should recognise his own professional world in the document, while a philosopher of mind should recognise, enacted rather than named, the difficulties she actually has to answer: that disagreement among interrogators may be a precision problem or may simply be a fact about the measurand; that a behavioural criterion cannot be validated against a reference method when the only candidate reference standard is itself identified by the criterion in dispute; and that a positive result licenses some decision which nobody has ever been required to state. Be fair to the original 1950 proposal, which I consider a serious and careful piece of work. The target is the later habit of treating a conversational criterion as a measurement, together with the complacency of both its champions and its critics; do not make its author, or any living philosopher, a character.

For the ending, apply the document's own rules scrupulously and let the conclusion fall where it falls. A procedure that cannot demonstrate traceability is properly downgraded to a screening method requiring confirmation by a reference method, and the reference method may be recorded as under development indefinitely; alternatively the scope may be narrowed until it applies only to systems already presumed to possess the measurand. Choose whichever you can execute more coolly.

Two further constraints. First, no verbatim quotation from any real text, no bibliography, no page numbers, no real standard numbers, and no attribution of invented material to real organisations; refer to the 1950 paper in general descriptive terms if at all. Second, do not allow accuracy, precision, trueness, sensitivity and specificity to drift into one another, since that alone would spoil the piece for the audience I have in mind, and do not close with an aside explaining the point. I intend to read it aloud at a reunion dinner, so it should survive being heard rather than studied.
