I run the investing club at my high school, and ever since we moved from choosing index funds to arguing about position sizing, one member closes every sizing debate with the same line: bet Kelly, because it maximizes long-run growth. This week he used it to justify committing 40% of his paper-trading bankroll to a single trade he put at 60% to pay 2-to-1 — which I grant is the right Kelly fraction for those numbers — and then dismissed my objection by saying that anyone who stakes less is simply leaving growth on the table. I suspect that "maximizes long-run growth" is not one claim but several, and that the one his argument needs is not the one that turns out to be true. Could you separate the candidate claims the slogan could be making — among them claims about the expected logarithm of wealth after some fixed number of bets, claims about the ratio of two strategies' wealth as that number grows without bound, claims about expected wealth itself in that limit, claims about how many bets it takes to reach a target level, and claims that assume a utility function rather than deriving one — and for each say whether it holds under the usual assumptions, whether it is substantive or empty, and whether it would actually license his conclusion? Please answer with exactly five numbered items, each stating one candidate reading together with its verdict in one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
