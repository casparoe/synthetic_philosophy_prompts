I help run an interdisciplinary reading group at my uni — half metaethics, half philosophy of biology — and next week's session is on what I've been lazily calling reasons without reasoners: the idea that a reason or a rationale can be in play even though nobody has it. My supervisor keeps telling me I'm muddling two quite different things under one slogan. One is Dennett's notion of a free-floating rationale, where there's a rationale for some design feature — the cheetah's flexible spine is explicable in terms of something good it does — that no designer and no cheetah ever represented. The other is the normative one: impersonal value, agent-neutral reasons that answer to nobody's interests, the goodness of a state of affairs no one will ever know about. I'd rather not open the session by defining the terrain myself, because then everyone just argues with my definitions. So I want a handout that sorts the group by what they actually believe, not by what they like to call themselves — a decision tree / taxonomy, with the cases doing the work.

Here are the cases I've been throwing at people, feel free to use them, add to them or swap any out if you think something sharper exists: (1) a valley of rare flowers on an uninhabited island, never seen by anyone and never going to be, destroyed by an eruption — was anything lost, and does anyone have a reason about it now; (2) the cheetah's spine again, i.e. a rationale no mind represents at all; (3) a trust whose settlor is dead and whose beneficiaries are small children with no view on the matter, but where we want to say the trustee has reasons nobody currently holds; (4) a policy whose entire payoff lands on people a thousand years hence, none of whom anyone alive has any particular interest in; (5) a chess position that plain calls for a move that neither player notices — is the reason there, and whose is it; (6) a committee that unanimously votes for a plan every member thinks is bad for the organisation, so the group ends up with a reason no member has; (7) a hypothetical ideal observer whose approvals are supposed to ground reasons although no such observer exists anywhere.

Could you build it from the logical space first and only then hang positions on it? I mean: start by listing the questions that genuinely separate people here — does a reason need something that could be moved by it, does it need even a merely possible valuer, is the Dennettian 'rationale' doing explanatory work rather than normative work, can reasons attach to an office or a collective that no member endorses, does the good have to be good for somebody — and sketch roughly which combinations of answers they permit. Then give me the tree itself, every node phrased as a case or a claim a reader could answer without knowing which leaf they're walking toward; please don't include a node that just asks whether they're a moral realist, that defeats the point. Aim for about ten nodes and seven to nine leaves, each leaf named (or marked as unnamed if you don't think anyone really defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I'd like at least one leaf for: reasons require an actual reasoner, full stop; value floats free of valuers and reasons go with it; the deflationist who says the whole question equivocates between explanation and normativity; reasons held by roles, offices or groups that no individual has; and reasons belonging to people who merely might exist. Where a combination of answers is unoccupied, say so and diagnose why — incoherent, unattractive, or just nobody's got round to it. And be honest about any two leaves your tree can't really seperate; my money is on the impersonal-value leaf and the merely-possible-people leaf bleeding into each other, but tell me if I'm wrong. No references or reading lists please, we won't have time for them and I can't check them anyway — just name views you're confident are real, and flag any label you've made up yourself as one. Oh, and write the whole thing in British English, there's at least two people in the group who would notice otherwise and I don't want the handout to be about that.
