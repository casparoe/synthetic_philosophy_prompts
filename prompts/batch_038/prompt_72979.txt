so I'm auditing my own Bayesian setup before I stick it in a prospectus and I'd like a reality check, because I keep feeling like something in here doesn't sit right but I cant localize it. Here's what I'm committed to:

1. Perceptual learning is never certainty. When I glance at a chip under bad lighting, I don't come to be sure of any proposition about how it looks — I end up with new weights spread across a partition (looks-red, looks-green, looks-in-between), and my credence in "the chip is red" moves because those weights moved. So the rule is Jeffrey conditionalization: P_new(H) = q(looks-red)·P_old(H|looks-red) + q(looks-green)·P_old(H|looks-green) + ... Plain conditionalization is just the limiting case where one of the q's hits 1, and in practice I should never actually conditionalize, since I'm never actually certain of anything about my own experience.

2. Rigidity is doing the real work and I endorse it: P_new(H|E_i) = P_old(H|E_i) for every cell of the partition. Experience gets to touch the weights, never the conditionals. I like this because it's modest — an experience can tilt the scales but it can't rewrite my whole belief structure.

3. The weights q aren't picked by me and aren't derived from my prior either. They're a deliverance of the encounter with the chip. Two people with different priors who have the very same experience get the very same q's.

4. I'm a permissive Bayesian about priors: coherence is the only rational requirement on a prior. Two agents with wildly different, even crazy-looking priors can both be fully rational; there's no uniquely rational prior for a given situation.

5. But I'm an evidentialist about changes: if my total evidence at two times is the same, my credences should be the same. Changes that are just psychological drift, mood, or the order in which I happened to think about things are irrational. Everything in (1)–(3) is supposed to be evidence-driven, not drift.

6. And I think this framework is what dissolves the problem of old evidence. The reason conditionalization can't handle "I already knew E, and now I notice that T explains E" is that P(E)=1 is stuck and nothing can move. Under Jeffrey updating P(E) can fall, so the update isn't blocked. I want to lean on that a fair amount.

(ii) I say "never conditionalize" and also "conditionalization is a limiting case" — is that self-contradictory on its face?
