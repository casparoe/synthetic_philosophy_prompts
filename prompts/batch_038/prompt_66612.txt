Act as a philosopher of biology who has spent serious time inside the function debate — Wright's etiological account, Cummins's causal-role analysis, Millikan's proper functions, Neander's selected effects, and the propensity and goal-contribution variants — and who is additionally literate in mid-century social-scientific functionalism. Treat what follows as a translation problem, not an exposition problem: the interest lies as much in what fails to carry across as in what does.

**Who I am and what I already know.** I am a historical sociologist of institutions. My native vocabulary is Mertonian: manifest and latent functions, dysfunctions, non-functions, functional alternatives and functional equivalents, and the standing requirement that any function claim specify the unit for which the item is functional, since what is functional for one aggregate is routinely dysfunctional for another. Layered on top of that is Elster's discipline on functional explanation in the social sciences: the consequence must explain the item's persistence, there must be an actual reinforcing feedback loop, the loop must be mechanistically identified rather than assumed, and it must run either through the intentions of actors or through a selection process. I work with these tools daily. On the other side, my knowledge of the philosophy of biology is encyclopedia-deep and article-skimming: I can follow a distinction stated plainly, but I lose the thread once talk turns to teleosemantics, normal conditions, or supervenience bases. Please do not explain my own framework back to me; use it as the target language.

**Part 1 — candidate mappings, worked visibly.** Before writing any polished prose, list the pairings that naturally suggest themselves and, for each, say briefly whether you accept it or reject it as a trap. I specifically want to see the seductive ones identified and refused: for instance, whether proper function is anything like manifest function, whether an etiological history is the same kind of thing as a reinforcing feedback loop, whether Cummins's interest-relative contribution is my latent function or something with no slot in my apparatus at all.

**Part 2 — the translation.** Restate the disagreement between the etiological and causal-role camps as faithfully as you can in my terms, including the standard cases that drive it: the malfunctioning heart that no longer pumps; the trait that has just arisen and has no selection history behind it; the heart's noise-making, which is a genuine causal contribution to a containing system and intuitively not a function; and the trait whose contribution changed over time, insulation becoming flight. For each case, tell me how it reads in Mertonian-Elsterian terms, and whether that reading preserves the work the case was designed to do.

Then run one case of mine through the machinery. In my fieldwork on municipal building-permit offices I have tracked an informal pre-application meeting that appears in no rulebook, that demonstrably shortens approval times, that the clerks themselves describe as merely "how we cope with the workload," and that has persisted for four decades. Which side of the biological debate, if either, has anything to say about that item, and what exactly would it say?

**Part 3 — the residue report.** Sort what you have done into three buckets: claims that translate cleanly; claims that translate only with distortion, where you must state what you had to add, ignore, or quietly substitute; and claims that have no expression whatsoever in my vocabulary. Then answer the harder question for every item in the second and third buckets: is the failure a limitation of the sociological apparatus, or evidence that the philosophical claim was less determinate than it sounded once the biologists' technical scaffolding was removed?

Finally, engage my working hypothesis, which I offer to be attacked rather than as a hint about the answer I want. My suspicion is that proper function is manifest function with the agents deleted — the same reference-fixing job, minus the beliefs and intentions that do the fixing in my framework — and that the real engine of the biological debate, the derivation of a token's function from its lineage rather than from its current contribution, has no sociological analogue only because sociology always fixes the unit by stipulation before it starts. If that is right, part of the philosophical dispute dissolves into a problem I avoid by convention, and part of it raises a problem my framework genuinely cannot formulate. Tell me which part is which, or tell me the hypothesis is wrong and why.

**Ground rules.** No bibliography, no reading lists, no citations — I will not be looking anything up and would rather you not guess at attributions. Think carefully before you commit to the mapping. Do not translate by smuggling my framework's presuppositions into the source material and calling the result a rendering: in particular, do not let biological function collapse into "effect that benefits some specified unit" simply because that is what my term means, unless you flag that you are doing it and price the cost. Where a term of mine has no clean slot for something the philosophers need, say so bluntly instead of bending the term to fit.
