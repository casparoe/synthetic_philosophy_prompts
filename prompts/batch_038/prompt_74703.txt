I run a small student-paper series at my uni and next month's speaker is a grad student who defends the Menkiti-style line that personhood isn't something you're born with — you become a person by being drawn into communal relations, and it comes in degrees. This hits uncomfortably close to home for me: my uncle moved into a care facility in the spring and barely recognizes anyone now, so I want the questions sharp but not gotcha-y. Give me about eight, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. Make sure they cover: whether his view is committed to saying my uncle has lost personhood, and if so what exactly is tragic about that; how it deals with infants and with people who've never been in a position to recipricate anything; what the "degrees" part is actually doing that an account of inherent personhood (Gyekye's sort of pushback) can't do just as well; whether a relational account can ground rights and not only duties to the community; and how he answers the charge that he's lifting a framework out of one particular African tradition and then presenting it as a general theory of what persons are.
