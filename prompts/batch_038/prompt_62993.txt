The agency: a statute, passed by the legislature in the ordinary way, authorises officials to determine eligibility for a benefit "on the basis of an assessment of need and risk, computed by such means as the minister considers appropriate." In practice the assessment is a proprietary model. Nobody outside the vendor knows the weights. The minister publishes a general description — the kinds of factors considered, roughly — but the actual thresholds shift every time the model is retrained, sometimes several times a year, and are not re-published. Claimants get a letter with a number and a one-line reason category. There is an appeal, but the tribunal can only check whether the model was applied as configured; it cant be told why this claimant scored as they did. Officials treat the statute and the model's output as binding on them, and the courts treat the statute as validly enacted.

My opening: Fuller's eight requirements would say this isn't a legal system at all. Your account seems committed to the opposite: there's a validly enacted statute, officials accept a rule of recognition under which it's valid, so it is law, full stop, and everything else is moralising from outside.
