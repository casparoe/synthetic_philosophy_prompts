I am a staff software engineer on an incident-response platform team, and I have no formal training in philosophy: please gloss any term of art in a clause the first time you use it, and do not assume I know the secondary literature.

Context for the task. We are drafting an internal policy covering what I will call a severing decision: during an active intrusion, an operator may authorize an automated response that will certainly and knowingly deprive a defined set of uninvolved third parties of a service they depend on, in order to stop propagation. The review has stalled because every candidate justification we write down sounds acceptable to most of us and clearly wrong to one colleague, who has been arguing from Elizabeth Anscombe's case against Oxford's awarding an honorary degree to Harry Truman. Her charge turns on a formula she states flatly: choosing to kill the innocent as a means to your ends is always murder. She also argues that killing the city populations only looked necessary because the Allies had insisted on unconditional surrender, so the necessity was manufactured by a prior choice made by the same actors.

To calibrate our rubric, I want to see how the structurally analogous justifications for Truman fare against her. Six candidates, as I have reconstructed them:

A. Net effect: the bombings ended the war earlier and therefore cost fewer lives than the alternative.
B. Foresight without intent: the objective was military and industrial; civilian deaths were foreseen consequences rather than the means by which the objective was achieved.
C. Given necessity: once unconditional surrender was the stated war aim, this was the only available means of ending the war.
D. Office: as commander-in-chief in a war, Truman's permissions are not those of a private person; the act belongs to the role rather than to the man.
E. Signature: he signed a document; he did not kill anyone.
F. Standing order: the delivery order was issued in advance down a chain of subordinates with the conditions of execution already fixed, so no fresh discretionary act by the President occurred at the moment of the drop, and the moral description of the act should attach to whoever set the policy rather than to whoever appears on the execution record. Treat F as a structural claim about how the decision was arranged, not as a historical assertion I am asking you to verify.

Deliver the following:

1. Score each candidate 0 to 5 on five dimensions: descriptive accuracy (does it get the act right, in the sense of identifying what was actually done rather than what it felt like); intentional structure (does it succeed in showing the deaths were not chosen as a means); resistance to the manufactured-necessity counter (does it still stand if the necessity arose from the actor's own prior choice of terms); cost under generalization (if adopted as a standing rule, would it license cases the defender wants to condemn); and relevance to the charge (does it answer the accusation of murder, or only mitigate blame). Give the table with a composite, using equal weights unless you argue one dimension should function as a veto, in which case state which and apply it.
2. Produce a strict ordering, best to worst. If two are tied, break the tie and say on what basis.
3. Assign each to one of three tiers: survives, survives only with repair, collapses. For the middle tier, one sentence on what the repair would consist in.
4. For each candidate, the single strongest move she has against it, stated in her terms rather than as a weak version of it.
5. Say which one she would attack first in front of a mixed audience, and why that one carries the most persuasive weight for the others.
6. Say whether E and F are genuinely distinct defenses or one defense described twice; if the latter, say which formulation is more damaging to her position.
7. Say whether any of the six would do more than survive her objections, that is, would give her a reason to withdraw the charge altogether, and if none would, say what shape of argument could.
8. Identify where her argument is most exposed. Include whether the objection she herself considers, that on her account it would be murder to kill enemy soldiers while they sleep because at that moment they are harming no one, costs her anything in this ranking.
9. Map the result back to our policy: which is the only shape of justification that transfers to the severing decision, and what the policy would have to specify for that justification to hold rather than degrade into one of the rejected candidates.

Commit to the ranking rather than surveying the options. If some part of the question looks ill-posed, note it in one line and rank anyway. No bibliography or citations, I will add those myself. Keep everything, table included, under roughly one thousand words.
