The case I start from: Robert Moses had overpasses built over the parkways running out to the Long Island beaches near New York, and he had them built low — too low for a bus to get under. So the people with cars got the beach and the people who'd have come by coach didn't, and nobody ever had to pass a rule about who was allowed in, which meant there was nothing to argue against or appeal. (I know the details are disputed, apparently the story's been retold so many times it's half legend — but just grant it, assume the low clearance really did the excluding. Here's what I wrote:

1. Some artefacts get built in a way that settles a question which would otherwise have to be settled politically — and settles it once, quietly.
2. Once its built, the artefact keeps settling it for decades without anyone deciding anything: the arrangement is in the concrete and it outlives the people who put it there.
3. So an artefact can carry a political arrangement, and carry it more securely than a law can, because a law has to be renewed and can be repealed and gets challenged in court, and a bridge doesn't.
4. What did the work in 1? Two things: the thing reliably sorts people into better-off and worse-off groups, and nobody votes on that sorting.
5. Loads of things do both of those without anyone having planned it — a town that grew around one station, how long the green man stays lit at a crossing, the default settings on an app, the hours the leisure centre is open, the standard width of a door.
6. So they carry political arrangements too. Which means the politics isn't in the designer's head after all, its in the distribution the thing produces.
7. And since the distribution gets fixed at the design-and-build stage, and that stage isn't an election, the political system can only ever ratify the shape afterwards.
8. So it makes very little difference whether a country is a democracy: how people's lives are actually organised is settled by the stuff around them, not by the ballot box.
9. Therefore if you want to change how people live you have to change the artefacts, and voting is basically a sideshow.
