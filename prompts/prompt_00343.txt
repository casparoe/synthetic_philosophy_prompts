So I've been going down a rabbit hole reading about updateless decision theory (Yudkowsky's original posts, the Hintze paper, some of the more recent MIRI-adjacent stuff on logical updatelessness) for a side project connected to my dissertation on rational choice under self-locating uncertainty. The thing I keep getting stuck on is counterfactual mugging. UDT says you should pay Omega the $100 even when you're in the branch where the coin came up tails and you already know you'll never see the $10,000 upside -- because the policy that pays out has higher expected value ex ante. But my gut (and I think a lot of updateful CDT/EDT folks would say the same) screams that once you actually know the coin landed tails, appealing to a prior expectation you no longer hold is just a weird kind of rule-worship, not rationality.

Can you write me a dialogue between a committed UDT theorist and a sharp updateful decision theorist (doesn't matter if they're CDT or EDT flavored, just have them be consistent) hashing this out? I want real engagement, not one side quietly being a punching bag -- so have the updateful theorist press hard on the "but you KNOW it's tails now, why does the counterfactual you matter" objection, and have the UDT theorist actually grapple with things like the commitment-races problem, dynamic consistency, and what it even means to have a "prior" once you're a logically updateless agent, rather than just repeating "but the policy is what matters." If one of them ends up making a better case, that's fine, I just don't want anyone strawmanned.
