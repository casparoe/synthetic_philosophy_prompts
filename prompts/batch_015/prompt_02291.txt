Then it hit me that Hume basically says the same kind of thing about causation in general: we never actually see one thing "cause" another, we just see stuff happen together over and over and call the pattern a habit or custom. Are these really the same insight, just one dressed up in theology and one in empiricism, or is there a real difference once you dig into it? One thing I keep getting stuck on — Ghazali seems to need his view so there's still room for miracles (God breaking his own habits now and then), but Hume also has that whole separate argument for why we should basically never believe a miracle actually happened. So does Hume's "causation is just custom" idea leave the same door open for miracles that Ghazali's does, or does it actually end up slamming that door shut? Give me a proper comparison, not just "yeah kinda similar" — I want to know what's genuinely shared here and what comes apart once you look closely.
