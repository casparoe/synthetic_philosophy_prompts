I am putting together a case study for my philosophy of medicine seminar and would like a dialogue that moves past the standard textbook version of the naturalism/normativism debate about disease. Write a dialogue between a naturalist who defends something close to Christopher Boorse's biostatistical theory of health and a normativist who holds that disease attributions can never be fully purged of value judgments. Have the two argue specifically over whether ordinary age-related functional decline—senescence—is itself a disease. The naturalist should explain how reference classes can be stratified by age without simply presupposing the answer, and may draw on evolutionary theories of senescence, such as antagonistic pleiotropy, to argue that the decline reflects a trade-off selected for by evolution rather than genuine dysfunction. The normativist should press on whether that line between trade-off and dysfunction can be drawn without covertly relying on some prior judgment about what is good for the organism. Both positions are well worked out in the literature, so do not let either side concede too quickly or talk past the other—let the dialogue close on a precise point of disagreement rather than a tidy resolution.
