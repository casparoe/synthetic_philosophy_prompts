ok so i've been down a rabbit hole reading about this AI alignment problem called "eliciting latent knowledge" (ELK) - the classic setup is you've got an AI guarding a diamond in a vault full of cameras and sensors, and a robber tampers with the sensors so everything still looks fine on camera. the worry is a smart enough AI could learn to pull the exact same trick on us, making its outputs look right without actually being honest about what it "knows" is really going on. basically the whole field assumes there's some real belief sitting inside the model, separate from what it outputs, that we just need better tools to read out.

i went through a wittgenstein phase a couple years back and can't stop thinking about his private language stuff, the bit about how an inner state thats totally cut off from any outward behavior doesn't really make sense as something to even talk about. if he's right about that, seems like it wouldn't just make ELK hard, it'd mean the whole premise is confused from the start. what do you think wittgenstein would actually say if someone explained the ELK problem to him - would he say these researchers are chasing an incoherent target, or is there some way his view is actually compatible with there being a real fact of the matter about what the AI "knows"?
