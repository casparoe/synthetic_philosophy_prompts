A classic result in the semantics of conditionals, due to Ernest Adams, is that an indicative conditional and its counterfactual (subjunctive) counterpart can diverge in truth value even though they share the same antecedent and consequent. The stock illustration concerns Kennedy's assassination: "If Oswald didn't kill Kennedy, someone else did" is true, while "If Oswald hadn't killed Kennedy, someone else would have" is false. I am writing a handout for my seminar and would rather not reuse that example, or an obvious variant of it (a different whodunit, a different historical assassination, or the like). Please construct an original indicative/counterfactual pair, drawn from a genuinely different domain, that exhibits the same divergence; state which member is true and which is false; and explain in a few sentences exactly what feature of your example produces the gap.
