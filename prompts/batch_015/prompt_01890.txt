At my dad's birthday dinner last month, he and his old buddy Ray ended up arguing about that "fine-tuned universe" idea — you know, how if gravity or a handful of other numbers in physics were even slightly different, no stars or atoms would ever form. Here's roughly how it went:

(1) Dad said the odds of all those numbers landing exactly where they need to be is so tiny it basically has to be on purpose — like a control panel with fifty dials that all happen to be set to the one setting that doesn't blow everything up, out of a million possible settings each.
(2) Ray said that's not actually mysterious — of course we find ourselves in a universe where the numbers worked out, because if they hadn't there'd be no one around to be confused about it, so there's nothing left that needs explaining.
(3) Dad said ok, but imagine your stood in front of a firing squad and every shooter somehow misses. You wouldn't just shrug and think "well obviously they missed or I wouldn't be here to wonder about it" — you'd still think something's going on and want an explanation, even though surviving is the only way you could be asking the question at all.
(4) Ray said fine, but the explanation still doesn't have to be God — maybe there's a huge number of universes out there with different settings and we just happen to be the one that worked out, nothing special about us.
(5) Dad said that's just swapping one unprovable thing for another unprovable thing, so Ray hadn't actually solved anything, just pushed the mystery back a step.
(6) Ray's last word was that we've at least got real physics (something to do with cosmic inflation) pointing toward other universes maybe being a thing, but nothing at all pointing toward God except wanting a tidy answer.

Neither of them would budge and they both just looked at me to settle it. Can you go through this move by move and tell me who had the stronger point at each numbered step (not just who won overall), and whether there's something important both of them are missing?
