A friend of mine who's way deeper into the rationalist/EA blogosphere than I am sent me down a rabbit hole a couple weeks ago on this idea called "evidential cooperation in large worlds" (ECL for short, apparently pioneered by some guy named Caspar Oesterheld — it used to be called "multiverse-wide superrationality" before someone rebranded it, which honestly didn't make it less confusing). The rough pitch, as I understand it: if the universe or multiverse is big enough, there's a ton of agents out there running decision procedures similar to mine, and even though I can't causally influence any of them, the fact that I choose to cooperate is evidence that agents-like-me elsewhere also cooperate — so it's supposedly rational to act more altruistically than plain self-interest, or even plain causal decision theory, would recommend, purely because of that correlation. I have zero formal philosophy training (I write backend Go services for a living, not exactly adjacent), so jargon like "superrationality" and "evidential vs causal decision theory" definately goes over my head a bit, but the shape of the argument is bugging me enough that I want to actually get what it would mean if it were true.

So here's what I'd love your help with: assuming the core ECL argument is basically sound, what are the most important real implications for how an individual person — not a government or an AI lab, just some random person like me — should actually behave day to day? Like, does this give me genuinely new reasons to keep small promises or not cut corners at work in situations where literally nobody causally connected to me could ever find out, beyond the usual "just be a good person" reasoning? Does it push me toward compromising more with people whose values are wildly different from mine, since presumably not all the correlated agents out there share my values? And does it survive the obvious objection that my decisions can't be meaningfully "correlated" with some alien or simulated mind I've never had any contact with, in any sense that should actually move my behavior? I'd rather you be upfront about which implications are solid versus which are more fun thought-experiment territory that doesn't really cash out into different actions, rather than just telling me it's all deep and important.
