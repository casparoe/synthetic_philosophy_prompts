Ok so I fell down a random rabbit hole this week reading philosophy of science stuff online (I have zero formal background, I just like poking around wikipedia and the stanford encyclopedia at like 1am when I can't sleep) and I got kind of stuck on this argument about what it actually means for a body part to "have a function." Turns out there's two camps that basically don't agree at all. One side says the function of a thing is whatever it was originally selected for back in evolutionary history - so the heart's function is pumping blood because pumping blood is the reason hearts kept getting passed down generation to generation, i think thats called the "etiological" view. The other camp says forget the history, the function of a thing is just whatever it's currently doing that contributes to some bigger system working, so if you frame things a certain way the heart's "function" could just as easily be making the thump-thump noise doctors listen for with a stethoscope, since that noise also plays a causal role in something (like a diagnosis). Apparently thats the "causal-role" view and people write entire papers arguing about whether the noise "counts."

Honestly the more I read about it the more it strikes me as a little bit funny, like there's something inherently comic about two grown academics fighting over whether a heartbeat sound is a real function or not.
