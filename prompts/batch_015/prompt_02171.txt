Write a joke that turns on the problem of logical omniscience: the fact that, in both epistemic logic and Bayesian epistemology, an ideally rational agent is standardly modeled as already believing every logical and mathematical truth, and as having already worked out every consequence of its beliefs, however hard that consequence may be to derive. It should be funny to someone who actually understands why this idealization is philosophically troublesome, with the punchline resting on the real content of the problem rather than being a generic "know-it-all" joke.
