Quick one for a piece I'm working on. L.A. Paul's transformative experience stuff—the vampire thought experiment, the claim that something like having a kid is "epistemically transformative" because you can't know the relevant values till after the fact, so ordinary expected-utility reasoning just breaks down. It occured to me that William James spent basically his whole career in adjacent territory: conversion, mysticism (he thought mystical states have that "noetic" quality, like you've learned something no amount of discursive reasoning could've gotten you), and the will-to-believe stuff about being licensed to decide before the evidence is in, on options that are forced, living, and momentous. Do you think James would say Paul's basically rediscovered a problem he'd already worked out a solution to, or would he actually agree with her that this stuff breaks rational choice theory and needs a totally different approach? Every take I've seen just says "they're both about transformation," which feels lazy - want something that actually grapples with the substance of both.
