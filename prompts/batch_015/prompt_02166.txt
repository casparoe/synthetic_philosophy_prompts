After forty years as a working physicist, I treated Popper's falsifiability criterion as something close to an unexamined professional creed: the line separating my discipline from astrology, and later from certain fashionable but stubbornly untestable proposals within fundamental physics itself. Since retiring, I have been reading the philosophy of science more carefully, and have become interested in Imre Lakatos's attempt to rescue falsificationism from the Duhem-Quine problem through his methodology of scientific research programmes. I would like you to compare Popper's account of falsification with Lakatos's, addressing specifically the roles of the 'hard core,' the 'protective belt,' the negative and positive heuristics, and the distinction between progressive and degenerating problem shifts. In your judgment, does Lakatos's apparatus rescue a workable demarcation between science and pseudoscience, or does it save falsificationism only by quietly abandoning the sharp line Popper wanted to draw? Please argue for a definite verdict rather than merely presenting both sides.
