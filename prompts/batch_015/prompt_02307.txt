Ok so this happened at a house party full of rationalist-forum type people a couple weekends ago and its been rattling around in my head since. A few of us have this recurring habit of making small side bets on stuff we disagree about - will some contested replication hold up, will a mutual friend's startup raise a Series B, whatever - usually for like $20-50, nothing that changes anyone's life either way. My friend Dev and I got into it about the startup thing. Months ago, before either of us had any real info, we'd compared notes and had pretty similar priors on its odds. Since then I picked up some added context (I used to work in an adjacent space), so I offered him 3:1 against the raise happening, half expecting him to pass. Instead he said yes immediately and asked if we could double the stakes.

That's the part I can't shake. Dev's a sharp guy, he knows I have relevant background here, and he still wanted more action, not less. It made me wonder - if we both basically trust each other to reason carefully, and we started from similar priors, shouldn't his eagerness itself be something I update on, to the point where neither of us should still want the bet? One friend at the party argued this means betting between two people who respect each other's rationality is kind of incoherent once you follow it all the way through. Another said thats a massive overthink of a fun $20 wager and information just doesn't propagate that cleanly between actual people. Curious what you think - is either of my friends onto something real, or misapplying whatever intuition they're working from? And does any of this generalize to why prediction markets, like Manifold and stuff, have real trading volume at all if the people on them are supposedly reasoning in good faith?
