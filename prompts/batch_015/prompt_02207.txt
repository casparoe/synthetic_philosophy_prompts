It started when Theo said something like: the loophole-free tests from a few years back settled it, the universe is nonlocal, full stop, that's the one part of Einstein's EPR intuition that just lost. Ines pushed back with (1): technically what those experiments rule out is the conjunction of locality, "realism" (particles having definite properties before measurement), and a "no-conspiracy" assumption about how detector settings get picked -- so you're free to keep locality by dropping one of the other two instead, its not like locality specifically is the one that's cornered. Theo replied (2): sure, but drop realism and you still owe an explanation for why two measurements seperated by a spacelike interval come out perfectly correlated; if there's no fact about the outcome until measurement happens, the correlation has to get fixed somehow at the moment of measurement, and "somehow" is just nonlocality wearing a disguise. Ines came back with (3): not necessarily -- you could treat the correlation as a brute lawlike fact that doesn't need a common-cause story at all (nobody demands a mechanism behind conservation of charge), or go Everettian, where there's no single determinate outcome-pair to explain in the first place, only relative facts across branches, generated by dynamics that are local through and through. Theo's response (4): whether the branching itself counts as "local" is exactly what's contested even among committed Everettians, so she's relocated the argument, not won it. Ines granted that but said (5): that's kind of her whole point -- anyone who confidently announces "Bell proved nonlocality" is skating past a genuinely live dispute about which assumption to drop, and superdeterminist, retrocausal, and Everettian escape routes are all still on the table even after the loopholes closed. Theo's closing shot (6): superdeterminism specifically is a non-starter since it's basically unfalsifiable and torches the idea that experimenters can pick settings independent of the system being measured, so between honestly-admitted nonlocality and hand-wavy instrumentalism about realism, nonlocality is still the more serious conclusion.
