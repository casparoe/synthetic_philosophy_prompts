I'm a retired chemist (forty-odd years doing reaction kinetics before I hung up the lab coat), and since retiring I've been getting into philosophy, which I never had the patience for back when I was working full time. Lately I've fallen down a rabbit hole reading about AI safety stuff -- my nephew's in ML and keeps sending me papers -- and I keep getting stuck on this thing the MIRI crowd calls the "Löbian obstacle."

As best I can piece together: because of Löb's theorem in mathematical logic, a formal system thats at least as powerful as ordinary arithmetic can basically never prove, as a general schema, "if I can prove X then X is true" -- the only way it can prove that for some particular X is to already be able to prove X outright, full stop. Which seems to mean any agent that wants to build a successor, or even just trust a future, self-modified version of itself, hits a wall: it can never fully certify, by its own formal lights, that a reasoning process as powerful as its own is sound. Apparently there are partial workarounds, something about an infinite descending tower of successively weaker systems, and a "procrastination paradox" where an agent keeps deferring some task because its convinced its future self will handle it, but nothing that really makes the problem go away. The paper that started this for me was literally titled "Tiling Agents for Self-Modifying AI, and the Löbian Obstacle," by Yudkowsky and Herreshoff, if that means anything to you.

Anyway, I mentioned all this to my wife over dinner and she said it sounded like a decent premise for a story, so now I'm asking you: could you write me a short story built around this specific obstacle? Not just a vague "the robot has doubts about itself" tale -- I want the actual plot and the characters' dilemma to turn on this particular self-referential bind, the way good hard SF makes the science load-bearing rather than decorative. It doesn't have to involve a literal computer -- could be any agent, institution, or even species that has to hand something off to a successor version of itself without being able to fully prove that successor trustworthy. I'd like it short, a page or two, but dense enough that someone who'd never heard of Löb's theorem would close it with a real gut feel for why this is a genuine obstacle and not just a plot contrivance.
