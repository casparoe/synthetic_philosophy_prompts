I've just emerged from the fourth draft of a novel that has taken up two years of my life, and for the first time with this manuscript, something in me settled: reading it straight through last week, I felt the same quiet, specific certainty that preceded the two books of mine that went on to do well — not excitement exactly, more a sense that the machine of it finally runs. The trouble is that I distrust that feeling on principle. My old workshop leader used to warn us that a novelist is constitutionally the worst possible judge of their own newest manuscript, too fused with it after years of labor to see it plainly, and my own history bears her out more often than I'd like. So when the two readers whose judgment I trust most — both novelists whose notes on my last book were precise enough that I could later verify, line by line, that they were right — came back polite but unmistakably cool on this one, I took it seriously. Both flagged the same structural problem in the middle third. I have since reread that section perhaps a dozen times, deliberately trying to see what they see, and I simply cannot: to me it still reads as the strongest stretch in the book.

What unsettles me isn't the disagreement itself but the odd position it leaves me in: I go on believing, quite vividly, that the manuscript succeeds, while simultaneously believing that I have excellent independent reason — my own track record, my present closeness to the material, two readers with a proven eye — to think that this very feeling of success is exactly the sort my judgment produces when it is wrong. A friend tells me I'm overcomplicating a simple choice between trusting my gut and trusting my readers, but that framing skips past the actual difficulty, which is that I cannot locate the error in my own reading even while granting, intellectually, that it is probably there. Is it simply confused to hold both of these at once, or is there a coherent way to do so? And practically speaking: given that I cannot make the flaw appear to me no matter how hard I look, should that failure itself count as evidence for my side, or is it exactly what my workshop leader would have predicted I would say? I would value your take on how to reason through this, and not only on whether the book is ready to go to my agent.
