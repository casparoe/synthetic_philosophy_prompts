A friend and I spent last night arguing about Rawls, and I am fairly sure I was right, even though I could not fully convince them. My position was that reasoning in the original position, behind the veil of ignorance, is ultimately just a vehicle for utilitarian reasoning: since you do not know in advance which position in society you will occupy, the rational strategy is to choose whichever principles maximize your average expected prospects across all possible positions, much as you would buy insurance against an unknown misfortune, and that this is exactly the calculation that produces the difference principle. If that is correct, then Rawls's claim to have devised a genuine alternative to utilitarianism seems overstated, since the reasoning behind the veil is utilitarian in all but name. Was I right, and if so, why does Rawls present justice as fairness as fundamentally opposed to utilitarian thinking rather than as merely a restatement of it?
