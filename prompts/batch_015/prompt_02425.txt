So I've been down this rabbit hole for the last couple months on something called "program equilibrium" - the idea that if two agents (AIs, or hypothetically people who could somehow show each other their exact decision procedure) get to read each other's full source code before playing something like the one-shot prisoner's dilemma, you can get real cooperation without needing repeated play, reputation, or an enforcer. The part that actually blew my mind is the FairBot/PrudentBot stuff built on Löb's theorem: an agent programmed to "cooperate iff I can prove, in some formal system like PA (Peano arithmetic, i guess the standard one for this), that the other program cooperates with me" ends up *provably* cooperating with an exact copy of itself - and apparently that's not a coincidence or a hack, Löb's theorem basically forces it. PrudentBot is a patch on top that still cooperates with FairBot but doesn't get walked over by a bot that just cooperates unconditionally no matter what.

Me and a guy from a discord I'm in have been arguing about this nonstop. He thinks it's a genuinely deep result - that it shows trust between transparent agents can be built entirely out of formal proof with zero repeated interaction, and he points to the later bounded/probabilistic versions (Critch's bounded Löb paper, Oesterheld's "epsilon-grounded" simulation-based bots) as proof this generalizes past unrealistic infinite-proof-search agents. I keep arguing it's basically a cute logical accident wearing a decision theory costume: FairBot happily cooperates with an unconditional cooperator too, and that "cooperation" isn't tracking any real counterfactual dependence since the cooperator would've cooperated no matter what FairBot did - it's just whatever falls out of the provability condition, which is the kind of thing people mean when they call these proofs spurious. Also the whole approach seems to need an opponent that's a transparent, logic-following formal program in the first place - the second your opponent's "source" is something like a big opaque neural net instead of clean code you can run a proof search over, I don't see how any of this even gets started.
