Now retired after a long career in experimental physics, I have lately been working through the metaphysics literature, and I recently came across Max Black's 1952 thought experiment concerning two qualitatively identical spheres in an otherwise empty, symmetric universe, advanced as a refutation of the identity of indiscernibles. It seems to me that physics settled this question well before Black wrote a word: two electrons in a spin-singlet state are indistinguishable in every intrinsic property, and yet there are unambiguously two of them, not one. Doesn't quantum mechanics therefore hand philosophers an actual, physically realized version of Black's scenario, making decades of debate over whether such a situation is even possible somewhat beside the point?
