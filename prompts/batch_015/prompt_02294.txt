So this happened during an on-call incident last week and I still can't shake it. We had a production outage, and I was trying to figure out how confident to be that our Tuesday deploy was the cause versus something else entirely. Right after things broke (at like 2am, so I wasn't exactly at my sharpest) I pulled up the monitoring dashboard and saw an error spike that lined up pretty well with the deploy window — not perfectly, dashboards are always a little noisy — so my gut said maybe 80% chance the deploy was to blame. Then during the postmortem the next day, a teammate mentioned she vaguely remembered a very similar-looking spike happening a few weeks back on a day with no deploy at all, though she said shes only like 70% sure she's remembering that correctly and not mixing it up with some other week.

Here's the thing that's been bugging me since: when I mentally updated on the dashboard evidence first and then adjusted for my teammate's hazy memory, I landed on a noticeably different confidence level than when I imagined hearing her comment first and looking at the dashboard second. Neither piece of evidence ever made me fully certain of anything, it just nudged me up or down some amount. It bugs me that the order I happen to learn stuff in changes my answer, since whatever actually caused the outage obviously doesn't care what order I found things out in. I've picked up alot of Bayesian thinking from doing A/B test analysis at work, but that always assumed the evidence itself is basically a hard fact you condition on, like "conversion rate in the test group was 4.2%, period" — not this fuzzier case where even the evidence is something I'm only partway confident about.

I brought this up with a couple of coworkers over lunch and we went back and forth without really resolving it. I'm a software engineer, not a philosophy or stats person by training, so sorry if this is old news to anyone who's actually studied this stuff formally. Is my instinct right that the order shouldn't matter and I'm just messing up the math somewhere in my head? Or is it actually true that once you're combining multiple hunches that are each less than fully certain, the order you process them in can legitimately change where you land — and if so, is there some principled way to combine fuzzy, partial evidence like this that doesn't leave your final answer at the mercy of which thing you happened to hear about first?
