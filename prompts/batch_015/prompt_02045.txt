In the Pensées, Pascal argues that since people could not make justice strong, they instead made strength pass for justice. Elsewhere he suggests that laws and customs are obeyed not because they are demonstrably just but simply because they are established, adding that tracing their authority back to first principles would destroy it. Rousseau raises a strikingly similar question early in the Social Contract, asking whether the "right of the strongest" could ground genuine political obligation, and firmly rejects the idea that force alone can create right or duty. Compare these two treatments of the relationship between force, custom, and legitimate authority: do Pascal and Rousseau actually agree on the underlying diagnosis that political authority cannot be derived from demonstrable justice, differing only in the normative conclusions they draw from it, or is there a deeper disagreement between them about what grounds legitimacy in the first place?
