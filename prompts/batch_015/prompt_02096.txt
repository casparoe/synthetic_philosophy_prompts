ok so i've been chewing on this question for a while now (i'm not a scientist or anything, just something i think about on my commute lol): when people say stuff like "biology reduces to chemistry" or "chemistry reduces to physics," do they mean the higher up stuff is literally nothing but the lower stuff moving around, or just that it's compatible with it somehow, or what? i went down a rabbit hole and ended up reading this old John Stuart Mill book (System of Logic, from like the 1840s) where he's basically arguing about this exact issue before it was even a "thing," and i got stuck on a part of it. here's the bit:

"...the joint effect of several causes is identical with the sum of their separate effects... [but] the chemical combination of two substances produces, as is well known, a third substance with properties entirely different from those of either of the two substances separately, or of both of them taken together. Not a trace of the properties of hydrogen or of oxygen is observable in those of their compound, water... though there be laws which, like those of chemistry and physiology, owe their existence to a breach of the principle of Composition of Causes, it does not follow that these peculiar, or as they might be termed, heteropathic laws, are not capable of composition with one another... there is no reason to despair of ultimately raising chemistry and physiology to the condition of deductive sciences... The Laws of Life will never be deducible from the mere laws of the ingredients, but the prodigiously complex Facts of Life may all be deducible from comparatively simple laws of life."

so first he seems to be saying water is basically not reducible to hydrogen and oxygen, fair enough, but then he turns around and says he doesnt see why chemistry couldn't eventually become fully "deductive" like physics is. and that very last line about laws of life vs facts of life honestly makes my brain hurt, ive read it like 10 times. is he contradicting himself, or is there an actual coherent position buried in there? and if there is one, is it basically what people today would call "emergence," or is it some other, weirder position that's kind of gotten lost since his time?
