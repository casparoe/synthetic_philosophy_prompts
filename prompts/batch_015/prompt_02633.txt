So I went down a YouTube rabbit hole a couple weeks back about this philosopher, David Benatar, who apparently argues it would've been better if literally nobody was ever born. I'm not a philosophy person at all, never took a class on this stuff, but I found it weirdly gripping so I kept digging past the reaction videos and found some proper write ups of his actual argument, which everyone keeps calling "the Asymmetry." Even brought it up with my roommate over dinner and we ended up arguing about it for like an hour.

Here's where I got stuck though. From what I can tell the Asymmetry is supposed to prove that basically everybody would've been better off never existing, full stop, no exceptions depending on how the life actually turns out. That seems way too strong to me. Like, I get it for someone whose life is genuinely more suffering than anything else - sure, maybe skipping the whole thing would've been better for them. But my life has been pretty solid honestly, good family, good friends, a job I don't hate, way more good days than bad ones. So it seems obvious his argument just can't be talking about people like me, right? It should only really kick in once you tally up a persons pain against their pleasure and the pain comes out ahead. Everyone online talks about the Asymmetry like its this bulletproof thing that gets EVERY life ever, happy or miserable, and that part has to be people overselling it or misreading what he actually argued, because otherwise it would mean how good my life actually is doesn't even matter to whether I should've been born, which just seems backwards. Am I missing something about how the argument actually works, or is it fair to say it only really applies to lives that are net negative?
