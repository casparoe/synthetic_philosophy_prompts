So I'm working on a piece about near-death experiences and can't stop thinking about the A.J. Ayer case. This is a guy whose whole career was built on the idea that talk of God and an afterlife is literally meaningless, unverifiable nonsense -- and then he chokes on some smoked salmon, flatlines for four minutes, and comes back describing a red light that runs the universe. His doctor says Ayer confided in him right after: "I saw a Divine Being. I'm afraid I'm going to have to revise all my various books and opinions." Given that one of verificationism's biggest champions basically recanted the moment he came back from the dead, why do intro philosophy courses still hold him up as the guy who definately settled whether religious claims are even meaningful?
