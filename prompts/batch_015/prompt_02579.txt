I spent about thirty-five years running a condensed matter physics lab before I retired, and these past couple years I've fallen hard into philosophy of science -- partly to keep my brain form turning to mush, partly because an old post-doc of mine talked me into an informal reading group over Zoom. Right now we're chewing on Ladyman and Ross's "Every Thing Must Go," and its got me stuck on structural realism in a way I can't quite shake. The idea (it goes back to a Worrall paper from the 80s) is that when theories get overthrown, what usually survives isn't the ontology but the mathematical structure -- the textbook case being how Fresnel's equations for light kept working fine even after everyone dropped his elastic-ether picture in favor of Maxwell's electromagnetic field. Ladyman and Ross push it further into "ontic" structural realism: not just that structure is all we can know, but that structure is all there is -- no hidden little billiard-ball entities wearing the relations like clothes, just relations, all the way down.

I keep trying to actually picture what that would feel like from the inside and it gives me a bit of vertigo, the kind of feeling I get from harder-edged sci-fi -- Egan's stuff, or the physics reveals in The Three-Body Problem. So here's my request: could you write me a short story, a couple pages, that tries to dramatize this rather than explain it? Maybe a first-contact scenario, or archaeologists on some far-future Earth digging up an extinct civilization's physics -- the point being that the "entities" on both sides don't match up at all, no particles, no fields, nothing you'd call a thing, yet the equations turn out to be structurally identical, isomorphism for isomorphism. If you can find a natural way to fold in something like the Newman objection -- the worry that "shared structure" might be a much cheaper and less impressive achievement than it sounds, maybe close to trivial once the domain has enough objects in it -- as a complication your characters actually have to sit with, even better. I'd rather it not resolve neatly; I just want something that earns the unease.

Sorry for the long wind-up, I just wanted to give you a sense of where I'm coming from.
