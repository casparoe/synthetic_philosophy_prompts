Ok, this has been rattling around in my head for about a week and I need a sanity check. My uncle is loaded and kind of a weirdo about testing people in the family — not cruel about it, he just genuinely enjoys watching how people respond to strange incentives. Over Thanksgiving he made the same offer to me and my cousin, seperately: he'll wire either of us $25k today, no strings attached, if we sit down with him and actually convince him — not just say the words, actually convince him — that we intend to jump into the lake behind his cabin on New Year's Day, when it'll be something like 15 degrees out. The money isn't contingent on doing the jump later; he won't be there to check, won't ask for proof afterward, and swears he won't claw it back or even mention it again if we bail. He just wants to leave that conversation believing we mean it, and he's genuinely terrifying at reading people, some old instinct from years of playing serious poker. My cousin talked to him for twenty minutes and walked out with the money already in her account.

Now I'm trying to decide whether to even attempt this, and I've talked myself into a weird loop. Picture January 1st, with the $25k already sitting in my account for a month — there's nothing pulling future-me toward actually getting into that freezing lake. It's just unpleasant, no one's watching, I get no private satisfaction from "toughing it out," and the money's mine either way. So future-me, thinking straight, has zero reason to follow through. But if I can already see that now, how could present-me sit across from my uncle and genuinely intend to do it, rather than just get really good at performing intention for twenty minutes? And if real intention is off the table here, what does that make my cousin — did she fool him, does she actually intend to jump (and if so, why, given she'll have exactly as little reason to as I will once the money clears), or is "intend" just not doing the work I think it's doing in this setup? It feels adjacent to Newcomb's problem, or to some of the precommitment stuff I've read on rationalist blogs over the years, but it's not really either of those and I can't find the seam in my own reasoning. Should I even bother sitting down with him, and if I do, what would I actually be trying to do in that room — con him, or genuinely talk myself into meaning something I know I'll have no reason to follow through on?
