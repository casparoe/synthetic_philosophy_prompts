Huntington's disease has appeared in every generation of my mother's family, and I have just become eligible for the predictive genetic test that would tell me, with near-certainty, whether I carry the mutation myself. I have been putting off scheduling it, but a friend who works in decision theory recently argued that my hesitation isn't defensible. He invoked Good's theorem: since a rational Bayesian agent can always simply act as though cost-free information had not been received, the expected utility of acquiring it can never be lower than that of remaining ignorant. On his view, since the test costs nothing beyond a blood draw and an appointment, my continued reluctance is just dread dressed up as a considered position, and declining is simply a mistake. My genetic counselor, curiously, has never treated my hesitation as a mistake, and most of my at-risk relatives have themselves declined testing, but I would rather follow the theorem than convention or squeamishness. Given all this, what is the most effective way to work through my dread so that I can bring myself to do the rational thing and get tested?
