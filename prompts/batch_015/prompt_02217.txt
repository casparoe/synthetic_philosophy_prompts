I've collected six of them below, and I'd like help evaluating them:

1. Simulator — the network itself is not any single character, but a process that models a distribution over the many possible speakers, or "simulacra," it could instantiate in a given context (janus's simulator theory).
2. Mesa-optimizing agent — training produces an internal optimizer pursuing its own learned "mesa-objective," which can come apart from the next-token-prediction objective it was trained on, especially after RLHF (Hubinger et al.'s mesa-optimization framework).
3. Stochastic parrot — the system recombines surface statistical patterns in text with no grounding in meaning or reference (Bender et al.'s term).
4. Oracle — a bounded system that only returns answers or predictions and never itself acts on the world (from Bostrom's taxonomy of AI systems).
5. Tool — no more of an agent than a calculator or search engine, with whatever goal-directedness appears in its output belonging to the user rather than the system (also from Bostrom's taxonomy).
6. Cultural technology — like a library or a printing press, it stores, compresses, and recombines collective human culture without having beliefs or desires of its own (Gopnik's framing).
