I've been down a rabbit hole reading formal social choice stuff for a decision theory reading group I'm in, and a couple people keep bringing up Harsanyi's aggregation theorem as basically a knock-down proof that once you respect everybody's individual preferences (the Pareto condition), the only coherent social welfare function that falls out is straightforward utilitarianism where everyone's utility counts equally. The way it was explained to me: individuals are expected utility maximizers, we want society to also behave like an expected utility maximizer over the same set of lotteries, Pareto forces the social utility function to be a simple sum of the individual ones, and so equal-weight utilitarianism basically just drops out of the axioms, no extra ethical assumptions needed. One guy even said this means the whole debate between utilitarians and prioritarians (people who think we should give extra weight to worse-off individuals) is kind of moot, since prioritarians are just being formally inconsistent whether they realize it or not.

That conclusion feels way too strong to me but i couldn't really push back on it in the moment. Does Harsanyi's theorem actually force equal weights on everyone the way he was describing, and does it really make prioritarianism some kind of mathematical error rather than a legitimate ethical position?
