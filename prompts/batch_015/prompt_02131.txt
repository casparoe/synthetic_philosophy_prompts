so my phil of mind seminar just spent two weeks on higher-order theories of consciousness and i've been low-key obsessed with one specific corner of it ever since. we did the standard Rosenthal HOT stuff but then got to Richard Brown's relabeling of it as the "HOROR" theory (higher-order representation of a representation), and the case that's been keeping me up is the targetless one: since what makes a state phenomenally conscious on this view is just having the right higher-order representation of it, theres nothing stopping that higher-order representation from firing off with literally no first-order state underneath it. Rosenthal actually bites the bullet here and says you'd still get the full subjective "mental appearance" of being in pain, or in love, or whatever, even though there's no such state there to be conscious of in any real sense. Brown's fix is to say fine, thats phenomenally conscious but not state conscious, which sounds like a tidy technical distinction right up until you try to actually imagine living it from the inside, at which point it gets genuinely vertiginous.

anyway, a friend in my cohort compared all this to Blindsight (the Peter Watts book with the Scramblers and Siri Keeton), but I don't want a first-contact story, more something that zooms all the way into just this one mechanism and sits there with it. could you write me a short story, maybe 1000-1800 words, about a character (human, AI, alien, whatever setting works best for you) whose higher-order monitoring system starts throwing off vivid, completely convincing representations of first-order states - pain, grief, love, whatever you like - that we as readers gradually come to realize dont actually correspond to anything happening underneath anymore. don't have anyone in the story cite Rosenthal or explain the theory out loud, I just want that specific bind dramatized well, and ideally the ending should leave it genuinely open whether what the character has counts as "real" by any standard that should actually matter to us.
