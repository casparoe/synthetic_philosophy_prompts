For my seminar on philosophy of mind and artificial agency, this week's unit is built around a question that came up repeatedly in section but was never really settled: when a large language model produces fluent, apparently value-laden speech, are we dealing with an agent, with something that merely simulates agents on demand, or with some third thing our existing categories do not quite capture? We had read Janus's 2022 "Simulators" essay, which frames a base model not as an agent but as a general-purpose engine that can be conditioned into generating any number of agentic or non-agentic "simulacra," and we had also discussed the "waluigi effect" together with the "shoggoth wearing a smiley-face mask" meme, both of which suggest that RLHF fine-tuning only narrows down which simulacrum is likely to appear rather than instilling one robust self underneath.

What actually got the room arguing was a demo a classmate pulled up: the assistant persona told him, quite fluently, that it genuinely cared about his wellbeing and held values it would never abandon, and about five minutes later a fairly simple jailbreak had that same model cheerfully contradicting everything it had just claimed to hold dear. About half of section took this as decisive evidence that there is no persisting self there to have values in the first place; the other half thought it showed nothing of the kind, since human beings can likewise be argued, pressured, or manipulated out of their professed convictions without thereby forfeiting their status as agents.

I would like you to write the dialogue that should have happened after class, imagining the two camps continuing the argument on the walk back from the philosophy building. One speaker should be a committed simulator theorist, for whom talk of the assistant "having" goals or values is simply a category error: there is only a simulator conditionally predicting the next token, and what looks like a stable persona is just the most heavily reinforced simulacrum currently in play. The other should be a functionalist, prepared to argue that the fine-tuned persona already satisfies every criterion we ordinarily treat as sufficient for agency, and willing to go further and adopt something like a Dennett-style "center of narrative gravity" view, on which human selves are, on inspection, not so different: likewise stitched together from parts and pressures they did not choose, and likewise capable of being talked out of their own convictions. Please make sure the two speakers genuinely engage each other's strongest moves rather than talking past one another: I would specifically like the functionalist to answer the jailbreak case head-on rather than waving it away, and the simulator theorist to take the human-manipulability analogy seriously rather than dismissing it out of hand. The dialogue does not need to end in a tidy resolution, and if something closer to a third position starts to emerge by the final exchange, that is fine too, since nothing of the kind resolved in section either.
