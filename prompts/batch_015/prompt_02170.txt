So this happened with my little angel investing group and I haven't been able to stop turning it over in my head. There's three of us (me, Priya, and Dev) and for every deal we score two seperate things before deciding: is the market actually big enough to matter, and is the founding team strong enough to execute. The rule has always been we only fund if both come back a yes. On the fintech deal last month, I thought market=yes, team=yes, so fund. Priya thought market=yes, team=no, so pass. Dev thought market=no, team=yes, so pass. If you just tally our three final fund/pass votes, that's 2-1 to pass on the deal.

But then Dev noticed something weird: if instead you look at the two questions on their own, market is a 2-1 yes (me and Priya agree it's big enough) and team is also a 2-1 yes (me and Dev agree they can execute) - and since our own rule says fund if both are yes, the deal actually should've been funded. Same three people, same underlying opinions, completely opposite outcome depending on whether we vote question-by-question and then apply the rule, or just vote on the final call directly. Dev now wants to make question-by-question the official process going forward since it "actually respects our own rule," but Priya says that's just a backdoor for the minority to override what most of us actually want overall. Is one of these methods simply the correct way to combine our votes and the other one a mistake, or is there something more fundamentally unstable about combining people's opinions this way?
