At my company, a release is only allowed to ship once it clears two independent gates: a security sign-off and a performance sign-off. For our last release, three senior engineers on the review board each assessed both gates and then also gave their own up-or-down verdict on the release as a whole:

- Priya: security clears, performance clears → ship
- Marcus: security clears, performance fails → don't ship
- Dana: security fails, performance clears → don't ship

Tallied gate by gate, a majority (Priya and Marcus) said security clears, and a majority (Priya and Dana) said performance clears — so on a gate-by-gate basis, the release should ship. But tallied by personal verdict, a majority (Marcus and Dana) said don't ship. We spent an hour arguing about which tally we're obligated to honor, and I can't find a principled reason to prefer one over the other, even though each person's own reasoning was perfectly consistent. Is one of these two ways of combining our judgments simply wrong, or is there some deeper reason they can both be "correct" and still contradict each other?
