My grandmother recently passed away and left behind a gold ring that has been in our family for three generations. My mother wants a jeweler to melt it down and recast the gold into a pendant, since she never wears rings but would wear a necklace. My aunt is upset about this: she points out that the ring has already been resized and cleaned several times over the decades without anyone thinking the heirloom was lost, but she insists that melting it down completely is different, because once that happens, "the ring" will be gone forever, even though every bit of the gold will still exist in the new pendant. My mother thinks nothing is really lost, since it will still be the very same gold that has been passed down in our family, just reshaped once again. I can see some truth in both positions, but I cannot tell whether one of them is confused about what actually matters here, or whether this is simply a case where reasonable people can disagree. Is my aunt right that something would genuinely cease to exist, or is she just sentimentally attached to whatever shape the gold happens to be in right now?
