I spent about 35 years as a marine biologist before I retired, and the last couple years I've fallen down a bit of a philosophy rabbit hole in my spare time (started with a popular book on causation, I think it was Judea Pearl's "The Book of Why," and now I can't stop noticing this stuff everywhere I look). Anyway, something landed in my inbox last week from an old postdoc of mine that's been bugging me ever since, and I'd love a fresh pair of eyes on it.

She runs a coral restoration program now, and sent me the one-year results comparing two outplanting methods before deciding which one to scale up with a new grant: "cluster" planting (fragments grouped tightly in clumps) versus "spaced" planting (fragments attached individually, spread apart). In the sheltered back-reef lagoon site, cluster had 42 of 48 fragments survive (87.5%) vs. 189 of 224 for spaced (84.4%) — cluster wins. On the rougher fore-reef slope site, cluster had 165 of 252 survive (65.5%) vs. just 30 of 56 for spaced (53.6%) — cluster wins there too. So cluster planting beat spaced planting at every single site they tested. But then she also sent along the combined total across both sites, and in that pooled number spaced planting wins overall, 78.2% to 69%. Turns out cluster planting just happened to get used alot more at the harsher fore-reef site (scheduling reasons, apparently that's just when the dive team was doing cluster trials) while spaced got tested mostly at the calmer lagoon.

It genuinely feels paradoxical to me, like it should be logically impossible for a method to lose overall after winning every individual comparison, but I've checked the arithmetic three times now and it holds up. She wants to know which method she should actually roll out across a bunch of new restoration sites next year (a mix of calm and rough spots, not identical to either of these two), and honestly I don't know what to tell her — trust the site-by-site numbers, trust the pooled number, or is there some other, more principled way of even thinking about which one "should" win? Curious how you'd untangle this.
