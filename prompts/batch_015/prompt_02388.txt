Okay, this is going to sound like a petty work complaint but I promise there's a real question buried in it. I'm a backend dev, and my company got acquired about eight months ago. We're now merging our codebase with the acquiring company's, and it's turned into this weirdly heated multi-week Slack argument that I honestly can't stop thinking about, even outside work hours.

The actual disagreement is kind of dumb on its face. Our team has always sent timestamps as ISO-8601 strings in UTC. Their team has always used unix epoch milliseconds in local server time. Now that our services actually have to call each other directly we can't keep mixing the two — we already caused a scheduling bug because of it. My skip-level's ruling was basically: neither format is objectively better, so let's stop pretending there's a technical argument to be had, and just go with whatever the bigger team (theirs) already uses. Everyone grumbled but agreed in the meeting.

Except a few weeks later, half our old team was still quietly emitting the old format, because, in one guy's words, "I didn't believe everyone else would actually switch, so why would I be the sucker who switches first" — which obviously made things worse then before we "fixed" it. Here's what's bugging me: on one hand, my skip-level's logic is hard to argue with — if the choice really is arbitrary, why not just let headcount decide? On the other hand, our old format is the one that's already baked into three years of customer-facing integrations, which feels like it should count for something beyond "our team wants to keep its toy," and clearly a decree from leadership wasn't enough to actually change anyone's behavior. Is there a better way to think through disputes like this, where everyone agrees the underlying choice is arbitrary but the fight over which arbitrary option wins (and how you get people to actually comply) still feels completely real? Or is my skip-level basically right and I'm just overthinking what should be a coin-flip?
