In my formal epistemology seminar, we recently discussed van Fraassen's Reflection Principle, according to which my current credence in a proposition must match whatever credence I am now certain I will have in that same proposition at some future time. This seems to generate an obviously untenable result: if I knew for certain that tomorrow I would be given a drug that left me completely convinced the moon is made of green cheese, the Principle would apparently require me to already be completely convinced of that today. Given how easily this kind of counterexample can be constructed, I don't understand why formal epistemologists continue to treat the Reflection Principle as a serious constraint on rational credence, rather than as a proposal that was straightforwardly refuted decades ago. What continues to motivate it?
