I'm designing a fiction-writing exercise for my seminar on personal identity, built around Parfit's 'branch-line' variant of the teletransporter case in Reasons and Persons: the scanning device damages rather than destroys your body, so you remain alive on Earth for a few more days -- long enough to learn that your replica has already begun living your life on Mars -- before you die. Parfit uses this case to argue that personal identity is not what matters in survival, and that what matters instead is Relation R (psychological connectedness and/or continuity, with any cause), even when this relation branches, holding between one earlier person and more than one later person. Write a short story, narrated by the person on the branch line during those final days, in which the character has to work through this argument for themselves rather than simply treating the replica as an impostor. Let the story stay genuinely undecided, through the character's own reasoning, about whether Parfit is right that identity was never what mattered.
