ok so i've been on a dynamic choice theory kick lately (SEP entry, then some Gustafsson papers on money pumps) and I think I've talked myself into a conclusion that feels too strong to be right -- tell me where it breaks.

The classic money pump shows cyclic preferences (prefer A to B, B to C, but C to A) let someone pump you for cash, trade by trade. But incomparable/incommensurable options seem like a worse version of the same problem, not a different one -- if you genuinely can't rank two things against each other, then by definition you'd take a trade between them for an arbitrarily small sweetener, which is all a pump needs. So "incommensurable values" (what EA people invoke in cause-prioritization debates, or just career-vs-family type stuff) aren't really a third option between "better than" and "equally good" -- they're just intransitivity wearing a trenchcoat, and any agent that doesn't want to get pumped, human or AI, eventually has to force everything onto one scale. Is that right, or is there a principled reason incomparability doesnt actually collapse into the transitivity case?
