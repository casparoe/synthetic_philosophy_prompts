oh man ok I fell down a Pascal's Pensées hole this weekend after seeing the "justice without force is impotent, force without justice is tyrannical" line get quoted for the millionth time in an ACX comments thread. so I went and actually read the section its from (Section V, "Justice and the Reason of Effects," using the old Trotter translation off gutenberg — this is Brunschvicg numbering btw, won't line up if you know the Lafuma numbers from Krailsheimer) and a few fragments later there's this bit that I can't get to fit together with the famous one:

#303: "Might is the sovereign of the world, and not opinion.—But opinion makes use of might.—It is might that makes opinion."

#304: "The cords which bind the respect of men to each other are in general cords of necessity... Let us then imagine we see society in the process of formation. Men will doubtless fight till the stronger party overcomes the weaker, and a dominant party is established. But when this is once determined, the masters, who do not desire the continuation of strife, then decree that the power which is in their hands shall be transmitted as they please... And this is the point where imagination begins to play its part. Till now power makes fact; now power is sustained by imagination in a certain party, in France in the nobility, in Switzerland in the burgesses, etc. These cords which bind the respect of men to such and such an individual are therefore the cords of imagination."

#311: "The government founded on opinion and imagination reigns for some time, and this government is pleasant and voluntary; that founded on might lasts for ever. Thus opinion is the queen of the world, but might is its tyrant."

so #298 (the famous one) gives you this tidy "might gets relabeled as justice" story, but these three seem to be doing something else and kind of contradict each other? 303 says flat out that might rules and not opinion, but then 304 makes it sound like "imagination" (which seems like its basically just opinion under a different name) is the thing that actually makes a succession rule stick once the fighting's over, and then 311 flips it AGAIN into "opinion is queen, might is her tyrant," which sounds almost backwards from 303. is there an actual coherent multi-stage theory of political authority buried in here — like, bare force, then some schelling-point-ish succession rule, then imagination/opinion dresses it up as legitimate, which is itself still backstopped by force underneath — or is pascal just free-associating in his notebook and I'm pattern-matching way too hard? and if there is something coherent, what's "imagination" doing here that "custom" (from the more famous fragments right before this) isn't already covering? would love help untangling this, it's been bugging me for days.
