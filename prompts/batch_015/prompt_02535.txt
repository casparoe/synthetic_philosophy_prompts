About two years ago I developed tinnitus, but not the simple single-tone ringing everyone talks about - its more like a shifting mix of qualities, some days a high hiss, some days more of an electrical hum, occasionally a weird "seashell roar" underneath everything else. None of the standard clinical descriptions or 0-10 loudness scales my audiologist gave me ever felt like they captured what I was actually experiencing, so, being a data person by training, I started my own private log: a 1-10 scale for overall intensity, plus a little home-grown vocabulary for the different textures - "hiss," "hum," "roar," and a few subcategories I made up as I went along. I've kept this up almost daily for two years now, and it genuinely feels like it's tracking something real; I can flip back through my notes and say "ah yes, that was a bad hum week."

I'm a retired analytical chemist (spent about 35 years running mass spec and NMR for a pharma company), and since retiring I've been picking my way through philosophy on my own, no formal training, so apologies if I mangle the terminology here and there - which is relevant background because of an argument I had with my daughter about the log above. She has a linguistics degree and thinks the whole thing is basically meaningless as a record, even if it feels useful to me. Her point is that since I'm the only person who ever has access to the sensation, there's no way - not for her, not even for future-me - to actually check whether I'm applying "hum" consistently over two years rather than just misremembering and drifting in how I use the word. So, she says, "correct" use of my private vocabulary can't really be distinguished from "whatever seems correct to me at the time," which means it isn't doing the job that real words do.

I pushed back that my audiologist once ran a pitch-matching test, playing tones on a machine until I picked the one closest to what I was hearing, and that this at least gives one of my labels an external anchor - doesn't that get me off the hook, at least partly? She wasn't fully convinced. Now I honestly can't tell if shes onto a genuine problem with this kind of private record-keeping, or just being a pedant about "meaning" in a way that shouldn't actually change whether I keep trusting my own log. Which is it, and why?
