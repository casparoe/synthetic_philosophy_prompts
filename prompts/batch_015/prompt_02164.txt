Hey, I've been down a rabbit hole on Van Fraassen's Reflection Principle for the past couple weeks (started from a tangent in a LW comment thread about diachronic Dutch books, as these things go) and I've written up some notes toward a post I might put on my blog, or maybe cross-post somewhere like LW/ACX if it holds together. Before I do that though I'd really appreciate a gut check, since my philosophy background is basically "whatever I've absorbed from a decade of reading rationalist blogs" and I keep suspecting I'm either missing an obvious rebuttal that's already out there, or just re-deriving something everyone already agrees is wrong.

Here's the shape of the argument as I have it so far:

1. Reflection, roughly: your current credence in A, conditional on "my credence in A at some later time t' will be x," should just equal x (Cr_t(A | Cr_t'(A) = x) = x). Van Fraassen's original defense was a diachronic Dutch book: refuse to defer like this and a bookie who knows your future odds in advance can sell you bets today and more tomorrow that lock in a loss no matter how things turn out.
2. The obvious counterexamples are the ones about anticipated memory loss (Talbott has a nice one about this from '91) and van Fraassen's own later paper about Ulysses and the Sirens: I know Ulysses's credence about "sailing toward the rocks is a good idea" will spike the moment he hears the song, but that's not evidence he's tracking, its just a corrupting causal influence, so deferring to it in advance would be crazy.
3. Van Fraassen's response (and I think this is more or less the standard line now) is to restrict Reflection to future credences that arise via legitimate, epistemically-ideal updating, and just carve the memory-loss and siren-song cases out as not counting.
4. My worry is that this move quietly empties the principle out. "Defer to your future opinion, except when it doesn't deserve deference" is true, but is it saying anything beyond ordinary conditionalization anymore? What's the independent, non-circular test for whether some future shift in my credence counts as "legitimate updating" rather than "corruption," in messy real cases like: spending two weeks at an intense meditation retreat, a year embedded in some ideologically thick community, or just being fifteen years further from the arguments that originally convinced me of a view I still hold? "Point to the new propositional evidence" doesn't cleanly separate these -- almost any shift can be narrated either as learning or as being causally altered, depending on how uncharitable you want to be about it.

I've also got a hunch this same tension is part of why Reflection keeps getting dragged into the Sleeping Beauty debates (apparently Elga leans on it at some point) since Beauty's credence moves around without her picking up what looks like ordinary propositional evidence -- but I don't want to drag that whole can of worms in if it's not actually load-bearing for the argument above.

So: does the worry in (4) actually go through, or is there a standard dissolve I'm missing? Separately -- is the Ulysses/Sirens framing earning its keep or is it just a distracting flourish, and did I get van Fraassen's actual dialectic with his critics roughly right or am I garbling it? Happy to hear "this has been done to death and here's the canonical answer" too -- that's useful information, honestly more useful than a pat on the back would be.
