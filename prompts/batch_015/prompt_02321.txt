I am a retired geophysicist (marine magnetic and gravity surveys, mostly) who took up philosophy of science in retirement. I'm currently reading Kuhn's Structure of Scientific Revolutions with a small local reading group, and at our last meeting two members -- neither a geologist -- disagreed at length about whether the mid-century shift from "fixist" geology to plate tectonics is a genuine Kuhnian revolution, incommensurability included, or just an unusually fast case of ordinary evidence-driven theory change. I reconstructed the exchange afterward; both argued in good faith, and neither budged:

(1) Mara: This is as clean a Kuhnian case as you'll find outside physics. Pre-1960s tectonics had a disciplinary matrix built on geosynclinal theory and vertical crustal movement, and anomalies -- mismatched paleomagnetic "polar-wander" paths between continents, above all -- built into a real crisis by the late 1950s. Seafloor spreading didn't just add a fact; it retired the field's exemplars and vocabulary wholesale, and resistance was heavily generational, especially among older American geologists -- exactly what Kuhn illustrates with Planck's line about science advancing funeral by funeral.

(2) Dev: Generational resistance isn't incommensurability. What actually settled this was theory-neutral: towed magnetometers recorded magnetic striping either side of mid-ocean ridges that any geophysicist could read off a chart, and its symmetry -- dramatically confirmed by the 1966 "Eltanin-19" profile -- clinched seafloor spreading once Vine, Matthews, and Morley supplied the interpretation. That's a rational, if belated, capitulation to evidence, more Popperian than Kuhnian, and a decade is fast for a whole discipline to retool.

(3) Mara: The data didn't just speak for itself. Mason's group published the striping pattern in 1961 with no explanation, and it sat uninterpreted for two years; even after Hess and Vine-Matthews-Morley, Carey's rival "expanding earth" picture was taken seriously by oceanographers like Heezen for years more -- that's theory-ladenness at work. The reconception ran deeper than any fact, too: continents and ocean basins stopped being fixed containers and became temporary expressions of mantle convection. And I don't just mean meaning-variance incommensurability -- Kuhn's later "taxonomic" version allows rival camps to agree on a given test while individuating the domain differently, exactly what happens when "mechanism" versus "description" is itself the disputed target of explanation.

(4) Dev: That kind of theory-ladenness is true of every theory change, cumulative or not. Fixists and mobilists never lost the ability to state each other's position or to agree in advance on a crucial test: Vine-Matthews entailed mirror-symmetric profiles, and Eltanin-19 delivered exactly that, recognized by both sides as decisive. If incommensurability retreats to "their categories individuated the domain differently," that's compatible with everything you just said -- so what's left of the original, contentious thesis that rational argument from shared evidence can't by itself settle a revolution? This case seems to refute it directly.
