So a few of us were up way too late last night arguing about free will again (as one does), and it spiraled into a whole debate about Galen Strawson's Basic Argument — the one where he says nobody can ever be truly or ultimately morally responsible for anything, because that would require being causa sui (literally the cause of yourself) in the relevant mental respects, and self-causation collapses into an impossible infinite regress. My friend Theo kept calling this "just hard determinism with extra steps," which I pushed back on since Strawson is pretty insistent that the argument doesn't care whether determinism is true or false — he thinks indeterminism just adds randomness, not control, so it doesn't help either.

Then things really got derailed when someone brought up that Galen Strawson's dad is literally P.F. Strawson, the "Freedom and Resentment" guy, and that Galen apparently even wrote a whole scholarly reply to his own fathers paper at some point. That felt like it should matter more than any of us could actually spell out at 1am. P.F.'s big move against the people he calls "pessimists" is that our reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, guilt, indignation — are such bedrock features of human psychology that no argument about determinism could ever really get us to drop them, so the metaphysical question ends up being beside the point for our actual practices of blaming and praising each other. Half the table wanted to say that this is just changing the subject rather than answering the son's challenge, since the Basic Argument was never a claim about what we'll keep doing psychologically — it's a claim about whether any of that blaming and praising is ever truly warranted or fitting in the first place.

Can you actually argue for that second view? I want a real case that P.F. Strawson's naturalistic reactive-attitudes move in "Freedom and Resentment" does NOT rescue moral responsibility from his son's Basic Argument, even on its own terms — grant the father everything he wants about the psychological inescapability of resentment and gratitude, and still show why "true," desert-entailing responsibility stays impossible. Bonus points if you can explain why this is a genuinely different move than just asserting "psychology can't settle metaphysics."
