So I had this two-hour argument with my friend Dan last night that I can't stop replaying: do the "features" interpretability people keep finding inside neural nets actually count as representations, or are we just kidding ourselves? Dan's basically a full realist about it — his view is that if you find a direction in activation space that's linear, causally controls behavior when you patch it (like that whole Golden Gate Claude stunt), and generalizes to new inputs, that just IS a representation, no different in kind from a thermostat representing temperature. I kept pushing the superposition problem on him — the network's cramming way more features than it has dimensions, so a lot of what an SAE pulls out might just be a basis we imposed, not a joint the model itself is using — plus Ramsey's old "job description challenge," that being decodable or even causally hooked up isn't enough, you need the state to actually be used as a representation by some downstream consumer, otherwise you've just relabeled a causal process in fancier language. Dan thinks that bar is so high it'd make brains non-representational too. We never landed anywhere.
