Weird thing happened while I was drafting my new book last night - I killed off a side character I'd had for like two chapters, and I actually got choked up typing it, even though ten minutes earlier the guy didn't exist and I was inventing his death as I went. Sent me down a rabbit hole on the 'paradox of fiction' stuff. Kendall Walton's answer is that what we feel toward fiction isn't real fear or grief, just 'quasi-emotions,' since we're playing a game of make-believe and know none of it's real. Noel Carroll's thought theory basically says the opposite - you don't even need belief, just entertaining the thought is enough to cause a genuine emotion, no pretending involved. Can you compare the two and tell me which one actually explains a case like mine better? Im not a reader being fooled by someone elses story, I'm the guy inventing the death in real time as I write it - does being on the creation side instead of the reception side break one of these theories, or does it just not matter?
