Okay, this has been bugging me since Thanksgiving and none of my labmates could settle it either. My sister has had brutal stage fright since we were kids, like physically-sick-before-every-recital bad. A few years ago her doctor put her on a low dose of propranolol to take before performances, and she swears its basically cured her. If you call her "an anxious performer" now she gets genuinely annoyed and says that word just doesn't describe her anymore, full stop, end of story.

Here's the thing though. When I actually pressed her on it over dinner, she admitted that most nights, right up until she's introduced and walking out, she can still feel that first jolt of heart-pounding, clammy-hands dread exactly like she always used to. It's only once the pill has properly kicked in, maybe a minute or so later, that the whole thing flattens out and disappears, like someone hit a switch. So my take is that the stage fright is obviously still fully there every single time, it's just getting caught and shut down right before it can turn into a full meltdown, meaning she's just as prone to it now as she was at age twelve, she's just gotten really consistent at intercepting it. She thinks that's a distinction without a difference: if the meltdown never actually happens anymore, then asking whether she's "secretly still anxious underneath" isn't a real question, she says that's just me being insufferable about my degree.

I'm doing my PhD in personality/social psych, so obviously I have a professional obligation to not let this go. Who's actually right here, me or her? And more specifically, if her behavior is going to look identical from now until forever either way, is there still some real, substantive difference between "the old tendency is still fully intact, just permanently getting intercepted" and "the old tendency has genuinely been replaced by a new, calmer one"? Or is that a distinction that can't actually be cashed out in anything, in which case I guess I owe her an apology?
