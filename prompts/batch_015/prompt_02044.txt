ok so I had this whole argument with my friend last night about whether Kepler actually believed in astrology or just did it for the money (he loves bringing up that "astrology is the foolish daughter of the wise mother astronomy" line as proof Kepler thought it was all garbage). I think it's way more complicated than that, so I went digging and found this bit from his 1602 tract De Fundamentis Astrologiae Certioribus, thesis 40 -- translated it myself from the Latin so sorry if it's clunky:

"Now this faculty that answers to the Aspects has its seat, not in the stars themselves, but here below; for these Aspects come about upon the earth, and are no true stream of virtue sent down from the planets, but only an accident of two stars' standing toward one another as reckoned from this place. And just as the power by which a beast is stirred to move at the sight of a thing does not lodge in the thing itself, but wherever the "species" of it comes to be pictured, so must this power lodge in the great body of the earth, or in whatever creature partakes of soul. For, indeed, every faculty of soul is nothing else than an image of God the Geometer in his creating, and is roused to its proper task by this same heavenly Geometry -- or, as I would sooner call it, Harmony -- of the Aspects."

My friend's read is that this basically proves the planets don't actually DO anything on Kepler's view, it's all just happening "in the earth" or in us, so really he's a skeptic dressed up in fancy language. That doesn't sit right with me though, since he clearly still thinks the actual geometric arrangement of the planets matters causally, somehow. Can you help me figure out what's actually being claimed here -- what work is that animal-seeing-an-object comparison doing, and is this meant as a real physical/causal theory or more of a psychological one dressed up in scholastic jargon? Trying to figure out if Kepler's doing something genuinely novel here or just relocating the same old problem to a different spot.
