For my seminar on mathematical logic and AI safety, we recently covered Löb's theorem and the "Löbian obstacle" it poses for self-modifying agents: roughly, that a formal reasoner generally cannot certify the soundness of another reasoner as powerful as itself, so it seems able to fully trust only successors that are strictly weaker. I'd like a short story (under 1,000 words) dramatizing this predicament — for instance, an agent or its designer forced to choose between a more capable successor and a verifiably trustworthy one. It doesn't need to walk through the proof, but the central tension should track the actual logical structure of the obstacle (a problem of self-reference and provability) rather than default to a generic robot-uprising plot.
