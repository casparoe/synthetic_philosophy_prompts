Then a new curator brings in some fancy imaging tech and the whole thing unravels. My problem is the ending -- every draft collapses into the same tired beat where somebody shrugs and says "well, it still looks the same, so who cares if it's fake," and I want something with more bite than that.

She also mentioned theres a real counter-argument from another aesthetician (Lessing, I think?) who says the whole perceptual angle is a red herring, that what's actually wrong with forgery has nothing to do with how a thing looks or could look, and everything to do with originality and credit.

Could you write me a short story (doesn't need to be long -- 1000ish words is fine) that actually dramatizes that specific disagreement, rather than the generic "fakes are fine, actually" resolution I keep landing on? You don't need to name-drop any philosophers in the story itself -- I just want the underlying arguments represented properly and left in real tension, not flattened into a soundbite. Setting and characters are totally up to you, I only care that the philosophical bones underneath are sound.
