As a journalist who covers the politics of truth and disinformation, I have noticed that commentators often invoke Harry Frankfurt's concept of "bullshit" to describe conduct that Hannah Arendt had already dissected decades earlier in "Truth and Politics" and "Lying in Politics: Reflections on the Pentagon Papers." I would like you to compare the two accounts directly. Arendt argues that self-deception still depends on some residual grasp of the difference between fact and fiction, a distinction she says dissolves entirely in what she calls a "defactualized mind," whereas Frankfurt defines the bullshitter by a more thoroughgoing indifference to that very distinction from the outset. Do these two diagnoses pick out the same underlying phenomenon, or does Arendt's account describe a form of political dishonesty that Frankfurt's dichotomy between liars and bullshitters cannot fully capture? Please defend your answer with reference to the specific mechanisms each philosopher describes, rather than merely noting that both were concerned about the erosion of truth in public life.
