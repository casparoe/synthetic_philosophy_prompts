For my philosophy of mind seminar, I want to get clear on exactly what, if anything, Roger Penrose's Gödelian argument adds to J.R. Lucas's original 1961 case against mechanism. Lucas's argument seems to founder on the objection that a mind whose reasoning is captured by some formal system F could never be entitled to assert F's consistency, let alone its soundness, so its confident assertion of F's Gödel sentence is no better warranted than a machine's would be. Penrose's "new argument" in Shadows of the Mind (sections 3.16 and 3.23) is often presented as a response to precisely this worry. Compare the two arguments: does Penrose's reformulation genuinely escape the consistency/soundness objection that defeated Lucas's version, or does essentially the same problem resurface for Penrose in a different guise? Please justify your answer by engaging with the actual structure of both arguments rather than the popular "Gödel disproves AI" gloss.
