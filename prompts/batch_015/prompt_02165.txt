Write a dialogue between William Lloyd Garrison and Frederick Douglass debating whether the US Constitution is a pro-slavery or anti-slavery document. Set it sometime after 1851, once Douglass had broken with Garrison and flipped to arguing the anti-slavery reading. I want real back-and-forth where they actually engage with each others specific textual arguments -- three-fifths clause, fugitive slave clause, the 1808 slave trade clause, the fact that the word "slave" never once appears anywhere in the document, stuff like that -- rather than just trading slogans like "covenant with death" back and forth. Don't make Garrison a pushover either, his position was a genuinely serious one and he should give as good as he gets.
