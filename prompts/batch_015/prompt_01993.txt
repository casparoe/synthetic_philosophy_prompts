For my seminar on Wittgenstein and contemporary epistemology, I'd like to get clearer on a specific fault line within hinge epistemology: whether hinge commitments carry any positive epistemic status, or none at all. Write a short dialogue between two hinge epistemologists — one an entitlement theorist in the spirit of Crispin Wright, the other a defender of the "non-belief" (or arational) reading of hinges developed by Duncan Pritchard — as they discuss one of Moore's original "truisms" that Wittgenstein turns into a paradigm hinge in On Certainty: that the earth existed for many years before either of them was born. The entitlement theorist holds that although we don't believe such a proposition on the basis of evidence, we are nonetheless rationally entitled, in a distinctively non-evidential sense, to accept it; the non-belief theorist denies that hinge commitments have any rational standing whatsoever, positive or negative, and argues that calling our relation to them an "entitlement" already smuggles rational assessment back in where Wittgenstein meant to rule it out entirely. Have the entitlement theorist press the worry that a purely arational picture leaves its defender unable to say what's wrong with someone who simply adopts different hinges (a committed flat-earther, say), and have the non-belief theorist respond by questioning whether "entitlement" does any real epistemic work beyond relabeling our inability to do otherwise. Neither should concede the point outright; each should still be pressing their strongest objection when the dialogue ends.
