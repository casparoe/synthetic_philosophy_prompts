After a career spent doing bench work in evolutionary genetics, I have taken up the philosophy of science in earnest since retiring, and one episode has continued to puzzle me, since popular accounts of Popper's falsificationism almost never mention it. Across several works of the 1970s, Popper charged that the theory of natural selection is "almost tautological" and therefore not a testable scientific theory but merely a fruitful "metaphysical research programme"; he subsequently offered what he himself called a recantation in his 1978 Dialectica paper, "Natural Selection and the Emergence of Mind," though commentators have long disputed how much he actually conceded. Please write an essay that reconstructs the tautology charge, spells out precisely what the recantation concedes and what it withholds, and weighs whether Popper's mature, post-recantation position leaves evolutionary biology's scientific credentials secure by his own falsifiability standard, or whether the episode instead exposes a genuine weakness in falsifiability as a criterion of demarcation. Argue for a specific verdict rather than simply cataloguing the considerations on each side.
