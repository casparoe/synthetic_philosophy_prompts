Over dinner last night, a friend of mine who is finishing a doctorate in philosophy of science and I ended up in a long, surprisingly heated argument about the metaphysics of laws of nature, and neither of us walked away satisfied. She defended David Lewis's Humean Best System Account: the fundamental facts are just the total distribution of local qualities across spacetime -- what's standardly called the "Humean mosaic" -- and laws are nothing more than the axioms of whichever true deductive systematization of that mosaic best combines simplicity and strength (with a further refinement, "fit," once probabilistic laws and chance enter the picture). I found myself pushed toward something much closer to Tim Maudlin's primitivism, as developed in The Metaphysics Within Physics: laws as fundamental, unanalyzable features of reality that genuinely govern the mosaic, rather than being distilled from it after the fact.

We kept circling back to one specific objection without ever resolving it. Maudlin's charge is that Humean laws cannot do the explanatory work we plainly want laws to do, on pain of circularity: if a law just is a compact summary of a pattern in the mosaic, then citing that law to explain why the pattern holds looks suspiciously like citing the pattern to explain itself. My friend insisted that Humeans have a substantive reply to this rather than simply accepting the consequence, but by the end of the night neither of us could reconstruct that reply precisely enough to know whether it actually works or merely relabels the problem.

I would like your help settling this. Please compare Lewis's Best System Account with Maudlin's primitivism as theories of what laws fundamentally are, including how each one understands the relationship between a law and the regularities it is supposed to govern or systematize. Then take a side on the specific dispute above: does the Best System Account have the resources to let laws genuinely explain the very regularities that, on that account, constitute them, or does Maudlin's circularity charge stick? Please don't just lay out considerations on both sides -- tell me where you actually come down, and say whether you think this issue should be decisive for the broader Humean/anti-Humean debate about laws, or whether it is more of a soluble technical wrinkle.
