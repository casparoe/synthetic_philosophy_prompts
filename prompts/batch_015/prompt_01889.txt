So I'm working on a novel where two of the characters -- old grad-school friends, now both philosophy professors in their sixties -- run into each other at a colleague's memorial and end up having the argument that's clearly been simmering between them since roughly 1988. I could try drafting the scene myself but I know exactly what will happen: one of them will accidentally turn into a mouthpiece who loses. So I want a real draft to work from first.

Here's the actual disagreement. One of them has spent her career doing Wittgenstein-flavored philosophy of religion. Her line is that when someone says "I believe in the Last Judgement," that isn't a hypothesis you could have evidence for or against -- its more like a picture that organizes an entire way of living -- which is also why she doesn't think her atheist friend is straightforwardly believing the opposite of her when he says there's no such thing. He can't stand this move. His comeback (basically Kai Nielsen's old charge against what he dubbed "Wittgensteinian fideism") is two things really: one, that if she's right, it means the two of them were never actually disagreeing about anything back when they used to argue about God until 3am in grad school, which seems like a crazy thing to just accept; and two, that the view is suspiciously convenient, since it lets her go on using loaded words like "believe" and "know" while quietly making the belief immune to the kind of scrutiny an ordinary claim has to survive.

Could you draft their exchange as a dialogue, maybe 700-900 words, light on stage direction, real sniping allowed given how long they've known each other? I need both of them genuinely sharp -- responding to what the other just said rather than delivering speeches past each other, which is the failure mode of pretty much every "faith vs. reason" scene I've read in other people's novels. Specifically, I want her to have an actual answer to the "so we were never disagreeing about anything?" jab, not just restating that it's a different language game and hoping that lands -- and I want him to have a real comeback to whatever she says back, not just the fideism charge repeated louder. If it fits the rhythm, I'd love it if one of them also brought up, even in passing, whether "fideism" is even the right word for what Wittgenstein himself said in his lectures versus what his followers later did with it -- I only came across that wrinkle recently and I think she'd absolutely use it to get under his skin. They don't need to namecheck Nielsen or anyone else -- that'd feel like footnoting their own argument.
