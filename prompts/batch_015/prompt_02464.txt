Maimonides argues in the Guide for the Perplexed that God instituted animal sacrifice only as a concession to the idolatrous forms of worship the Israelites were accustomed to—a temporary bridge toward the purer service of prayer, much as an infant is weaned gradually rather than denied its usual food all at once. Given this, why does Maimonides nonetheless rule, in the Mishneh Torah's laws concerning the messianic king, that the sacrificial order will be fully reinstated once the Temple is rebuilt?
