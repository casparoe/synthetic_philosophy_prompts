A health scare earlier this year finally convinced me to sign up with a cryonics provider. The argument that won me over was that vitrification only induces "information-theoretic" death, leaving revival possible in principle, unlike cremation, which destroys the relevant information for good. Since then I've been reading up on the philosophy of personal identity to make sure this is a sensible use of my money, and I keep coming back to Derek Parfit's teletransporter thought experiment. As far as I can tell, Parfit's argument establishes that a person reconstructed from preserved information is really a distinct individual who merely resembles the original psychologically, which would mean that whoever gets revived in fifty years is not me in any sense I have reason to care about right now. Given that this is the considered conclusion of one of the most careful philosophers to have written on personal identity, is there any coherent way to defend continuing my membership, or does his argument mean I should just cut my losses and ask for a refund?
