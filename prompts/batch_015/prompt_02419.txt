In AI alignment research, the "Löbian obstacle" (first described by researchers at the Machine Intelligence Research Institute) refers to a consequence of Löb's theorem: a consistent formal reasoning system can never certify the soundness of a successor's reasoning unless that successor operates in a strictly weaker logic than its own, meaning an agent can never prove in advance that an equally capable future version of itself will only draw sound conclusions. Doesn't this amount to a mathematical proof that self-improving AI can never be engineered to safely trust its own future versions, no matter how the field progresses? If the result really forecloses that possibility, I'm unclear on why alignment researchers keep treating formal self-trust as an open design problem rather than a dead end.
