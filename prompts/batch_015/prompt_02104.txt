Been getting really into AI safety stuff on my own time lately (no philosophy or CS background, just a curious person) and theres something that isnt clicking for me. The orthogonality thesis says basically any level of intelligence can be paired with basically any goal, so a super smart AI's goals aren't determined by how smart it is. But then instrumental convergence says almost every sufficiently smart AI ends up wanting the same handful of things anyway, like self-preservation, grabbing resources, not getting shut off, regardless of what its actual goal is. Isn't that basically a contradiction? If smart agents all converge on wanting the same stuff, in what sense are their goals still independent of their intelligence? Am I missing something here or is this an actual hole in the argument that people don't really talk about?
