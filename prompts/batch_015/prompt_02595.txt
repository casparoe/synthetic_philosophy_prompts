My political philosophy seminar has spent much of this semester on paternalism and the limits of state power over individual choices, and a situation involving an old friend keeps coming to mind. In his mid-twenties, deep in a gambling problem, he signed up for our state's casino self-exclusion program and deliberately chose the lifetime tier over the shorter, renewable ones, because it comes with no appeals process and no way back, and he didn't trust himself to honor anything less absolute. He's now nearly forty, says he hasn't wanted to gamble in a decade, and recently asked the gaming commission to lift the ban so he could join some old friends on a casino trip. They refused, explaining that the entire point of the lifetime tier is that it doesn't yield to requests like his, however sincere. He calls this an absurd case of an old decision controlling a person he no longer is; I find myself half agreeing, but something about the commission's answer nags at me too. Whose claim do you think should actually win out, and why?
