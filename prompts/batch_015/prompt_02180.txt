Two of my housemates, Theo and Nadia, got into a whole thing over dinner about a brainteaser our TA gave us for section, and I've been turning it over since because I honestly can't tell who came out ahead. We'd just finished a unit on formal epistemology in my epistemology seminar — credences, the principle of indifference, Bertrand's paradox with the classic random-chord-in-a-circle setup — and the brainteaser was basically a spinoff of that: say you're told a marble is a perfect sphere with radius somewhere between 0 and 2 cm, and that's genuinely all you know about it, nothing about how it was made or picked. By the principle of indifference, what probability should you assign to "its volume is less than half of the maximum possible volume"?

Theo said you obviously spread your credence flat over the radius, since that's the quantity the problem actually hands you, and got about 79%. Nadia said that's arbitrary, since the exact same set of possible marbles can be described just as well in terms of their volumes, and being flat over volume instead gets you exactly 50% — nothing in the setup says radius is the privileged variable. Theo's comeback was that radius is the more basic quantity, since volume is literally defined in terms of it, so indifference should attach to whichever variable is more fundamental. Nadia said "basic" was doing all the work in that sentence, since you could just as easily call volume the fundamental one, seeing as its what actually determines how much material you'd need. At that point Theo half-remembered our professor mentioning a fix, something by a guy named Jaynes, where you pick whatever distribution stays invariant if you rotate or rescale the whole setup, and figured that probably rescues his 79%; Nadia wasn't so sure that move even transfers here, since there's no actual physical random process being described, just ignorance about one fixed marble, and anyway, deciding which symmetries you're even entitled to demand invariance under is itself a further assumption, not something that falls out for free. (A third housemate wandered through, tried it with surface area instead, got 63%, and nobody felt better.)

Could you go through this move by move and say who's right about which specific points — not just crown an overall winner — and call out anything you think both of them are missing?
