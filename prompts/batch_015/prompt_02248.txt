So i've been going down a philosophy of science rabbit hole and keep seeing 'inference to the best explanation' talked about like it's some big unresolved problem in epistemology. But isn't it basically just a MAP estimate with extra steps? You score each hypothesis by how well it explains the data (the likelihood), weight by how plausible it was beforehand (the prior), and Bayes' rule spits out a winner — we do this exact move for model selection in ML all the time. So why do philosophers treat IBE as this seperate, kind of shakey, form of inference instead of just applied probability theory? Feels like all the 'best explanation' stuff is just a fuzzy wrapper around math Bayes already handles.
