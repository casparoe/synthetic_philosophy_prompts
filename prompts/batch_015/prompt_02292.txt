I've been going down a rabbit hole on higher-order evidence for my epistemology seminar, and honestly I can't tell who's right about it, so I'd rather watch two people who really know the material fight it out than read position papers at each other. The example that keeps bugging me is a version of the sleep-deprived doctor case that shows up in a bunch of these papers: she's been awake something like 30 hours, works through a drug dosage calculation for a patient, checks it twice, and it feels completely airtight to her. Then a nurse mentions that doctors in her exact state get this specific kind of calculation wrong way more often than they realize, even when it feels totally solid from the inside. The "Non-Akrasia Constraint" camp (this is basically Sophie Horowitz's line) says she's now rationally required to lower her confidence in the dosage itself, full stop -- believing "the dosage is X" while also believing "my evidence says I'm probably unreliable about this right now" is just an irrational combination, no matter how flawless the original reasoning was. The "Level-Splitting" camp (Lasonen-Aarnio is the person to beat on this one) says that if her calculation really was flawless, she can rationally keep her confidence in the dosage while also rationally believing that the statistic about sleep-deprived doctors gives her reason to doubt herself, and that these two attitudes just don't have to collapse into each other -- demanding that they do is what's actually irrational.

Could you write me a dialogue between two philosophers holding these two views? Maybe have them stuck next to each other on a delayed flight home from a conference, still hashing it out. I don't want either side to obviously win -- make them equally sharp, and have them actually engage with each other's strongest objections instead of just restating their own view louder. Like, the Level-Splitter should have a real answer to "isn't this just a license to rationalize away any evidence you don't like," and the Non-Akrasia person should have a real answer to "doesn't your view basically require her to defer completely to the nurse's statistic and trust her own careful reasoning not at all, which seems like its own kind of crazy." Bonus if one of them brings up what this implies for something more mundane than a hospital, like noticing mid-argument with your partner that you've had three drinks.
