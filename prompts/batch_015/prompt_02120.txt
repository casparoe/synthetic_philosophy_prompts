Godel's argument defines "positive" properties (basically perfections, a moral/aesthetic idea, not "positive number" positive) and says something is God-like if it has all and only the positive properties. Two of the axioms: (1) for any property, either it or its opposite is positive, never both, and (2) if a positive property necessarily leads to some other property, that other property counts as positive too. Using those plus a definition of "essence" (a property that pins down everything else about you), Godel shows God-likeness would be an essence of any God-like thing, and since theres a further axiom saying necessary existence itself is a positive property, you get the conclusion that a God-like being necessarily exists.

The objection (some guy named Sobel figured this out in the 80s) is that the exact same axioms prove way more than Godel wanted. Since whether a property is positive has to hold necessarily too (true in this world means true in literally every possible world), you can apparently build a "property" out of any random true fact — like "being such that I have a chem test tmrw" — and run it through the same machinery used for God to show that fact was necessary all along, in every possible world. So the axioms don't just prove God exists, they seem to wipe out contingency completely, which is a way crazier conclusion than anyone was going for. A guy named Anderson tried to patch the definitions in 1990 specifically to block this, and apparently there's still a live argument about whether his patch actually fixes it or just relocates the problem.
