Quick one for you before I lock in next week's session for my causation seminar. We just spent two classes on Lewis's original counterfactual analysis and then the "time's arrow" paper, where he tries to fix the backtracking problem with that whole priority-ranked similarity system -- avoid big miracles first, then maximize match of particular fact, and only worry about small miracles after that. The upshot is supposed to be that counterfactuals about the past mostly don't backtrack (the past stays put, give or take one tiny local miracle) while counterfactuals about the future track the antecedent the way we'd expect. A couple of my students are convinced this is basically a just-so story, gerry-rigged so the asymmetry comes out right with no independent motivation besides "it gives the correct verdicts."

So here's what I want to run by you first. How does that whole apparatus actually compare to how Woodward (and the interventionist/structural-equations crowd more broadly) handles the very same problem? My read is that on his picture you don't need any of Lewis's similarity ranking at all, because "ideal intervention" already builds in, by stipulation, that you change the cause variable's value while leaving every other variable's equation exactly as it was -- so there's nothing to backtrack to in the first place, it's solved at the level of the definition rather than by ranking possible worlds. But that seems to buy a tidy solution at the price of smuggling a pretty clearly causal notion (an intervention "from outside the system") straight into the analysis, which is exactly the sort of circularity a reductive theory like Lewis's is at least trying to avoid, whatever you think of the miracle-counting.

Can you actually lay out where the two solutions diverge versus where one might just be a relabeling of the other in different notation, and then give me a real verdict: does Woodward buy anything genuine by trading the similarity metric for the intervention operator, or does he just relocate the ad-hocness (or the circularity) somewhere harder to see? This is for people who've already read Lewis 1979 and the relevant chapters of Making Things Happen, so don't dumb it down, and feel free to disagree with both of them if that's where the arguments actually lead.
