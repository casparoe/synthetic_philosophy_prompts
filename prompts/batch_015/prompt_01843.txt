I got really into AI safety stuff on youtube a while back (a lot of Robert Miles videos) and I keep running into this Eliezer Yudkowsky idea called "coherent extrapolated volition" - basically instead of hard-coding rules into a superintelligent AI, you have it figure out what we'd actually want "if we knew more, thought faster, were more the people we wished we were, had grown up farther together" (that's apparently the actual phrase he uses), instead of just going with whatever we say we want on a random Tuesday. So it's less "current preferences" and more "idealized version of our preferences."

Then completely seperately, months later, I went down a wikipedia rabbit hole on metaethics (don't ask) and found out philosophers have apparently been kicking around something that sounds suspiciously similar for decades - this whole idea that what's actually good for a person isn't what they currently want but what some idealized, fully-informed, fully rational version of them (or in some versions, a totally impartial "ideal observer") would want for them instead. Which, minus the superintelligent computer, sounds basically like the same move to me? I never studied any of this formally so I could easily be missing something obvious.

So what I actually want to know is: is CEV genuinely doing something new, or is it just this older philosophical idea with a computer bolted on top? And if there IS something that makes CEV special, or that creates genuinely new problems the older philosophical versions never had to face, what is it - and please don't just say "well it's for an AI," that feels like dodging the question. Like, my own half-baked guess is that doing this "idealize the preferences" move for ALL of humanity simultaneously, and forcing the results to somehow cohere into one thing, breaks in ways that don't come up when a philosopher is just talking about one idealized individual. But I don't actually know if that's right or if I'm missing the real crux. Where do these two things actually pull apart, if at all?
