i've been going down a bit of a rabbit hole reading about the hart vs fuller debate on law and morality, and i cant stop thinking about that "grudge informer" case they both kept arguing over. quick recap: in 1944 a german woman reports her husband to the nazi authorities for badmouthing hitler while he was home on leave (she wanted rid of him), he's sentenced to death but ends up survivng the war, and afterward she gets put on trial for illegally depriving him of his freedom. hart's position was that the nazi statute was valid law at the time, full stop, and if postwar germany wanted to punish her they should've just passed an honest retroactive law and been upfront that thats what they were doing. fuller said no -- the nazi legal system was such a mess (secret decrees, rules that contradicted each other, no real way for people to know what was expected of them) that it never counted as a functioning legal system, so theres no retroactivity problem to begin with -- she just never had a valid defense.

what i keep getting stuck on is what bentham wouldve made of all this. he's basically the original source of the whole "law is law, dont confuse what it is with whether its any good" position that hart is defending, and he trashed natural-law style thinking as "nonsense upon stilts." but he was also firmly against retroactive laws on his own utilitarian grounds, and no fan of judges deciding this kind of thing off their own moral instincts either. so where does that leave him -- team hart, team fuller, or some third position neither of them wouldve been happy with? i want your actual best reconstruction of his reasoning, not just a guess at which name he'd end up under.
