Last weekend, a close friend of mine, who is finishing her PhD in cosmology, and I got into a long argument that started over dinner and didn't really end until well past midnight, with neither of us conceding much. She had been working on the "youngness paradox" that shows up in certain measures of eternal inflation — the puzzle that some ways of counting observers across an eternally inflating multiverse imply that any given observer should almost certainly find themselves freshly created, moments after their local big bang, which is plainly not true of us — and this dragged us into a much broader argument about anthropic reasoning. Her position was that, quite apart from any particular measure problem, the sheer number of observers a cosmological theory predicts should itself count as evidence for that theory: if eternal inflation implies vastly more observers than a single, finite universe would, then the fact that we exist at all is some reason to favor eternal inflation, over and above whatever work it does in explaining fine-tuned constants.

I pushed back with the little I remembered about Nick Bostrom's "presumptuous philosopher," in which a philosopher uses exactly this kind of reasoning — the self-indication assumption, or SIA — to announce, from the armchair, that a theory implying a trillion times more observers than its rival must be about a trillion times more probable, no experiment required. This struck both of us as a reductio, though we couldn't agree on what exactly it was a reductio of. She pointed out, fairly, that rejecting SIA in favor of the self-sampling assumption (SSA) doesn't obviously help, since SSA is what generates the doomsday argument in the first place and has its own well-documented problems with reference classes and seemingly arbitrary updates. We went back and forth without ever settling whether either principle is actually defensible, or whether this whole framework is the wrong tool for adjudicating between cosmological theories to begin with.

I would like to understand this properly rather than just relitigate the argument from memory. Could you lay out the most important implications of the SSA/SIA debate and the presumptuous philosopher problem for how much weight, if any, the number of observers a theory predicts should get when comparing theories like eternal inflation against more modest cosmologies? I am especially interested in whether there is a principled way to resist the presumptuous philosopher without simply importing all of SSA's baggage, and in how this bears on the more familiar fine-tuning argument for a multiverse, which I suspect is related to, but not quite the same as, the argument my friend was pressing.
