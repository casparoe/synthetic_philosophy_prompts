Pascal's Mugging gets invoked constantly on rationalist and EA-adjacent blogs, usually as a rhetorical trump card, but I have rarely seen a treatment that argues the proposed fixes through to an actual conclusion. Write an essay that reaches and defends a definite verdict on whether a rational, expected-value-maximizing agent should pay a stranger $5 to avert harm to an astronomically large number of people, given only a tiny but strictly positive credence that the threat is genuine. Engage seriously with at least two specific proposed solutions: the leverage penalty (Robin Hanson's proposal, later elaborated by Yudkowsky, that hypotheses granting you outsized leverage over vast numbers of people should be penalized in proportion to that leverage) and simply capping the agent's utility function so that no single outcome can carry unbounded weight. For each, state plainly whether it actually blocks the mugging or merely relocates the problem — for instance, to "Pascal's Muggle" scenarios, where the stranger actually produces extraordinary evidence for the claim. Do not end with a mere survey of considerations: say clearly whether you would pay, and if your answer is conditional, specify exactly on what it depends.
