A friend of mine spent her twenties adrift, then in her early thirties deliberately overhauled her life: she built a strict routine, taught herself a new trade, and cut out the habits she blamed for holding her back. She has since built a genuinely successful career and takes real pride in calling herself self-made. When I brought up her stable upbringing and a few lucky breaks along the way, she pushed back: none of that, she said, had forced her daily choices — she chose the discipline, again and again, so the person she became is entirely her own doing, and she deserves full credit for it.

We argued about this for most of an evening. I could not deny that nobody had forced her choices, yet her claim to full credit still seemed to me to be missing something, though I could not say exactly what. Was I onto something real, or is she right that choosing, day after day, to become a different person is just as much to her credit as if she had simply been disciplined from birth?
