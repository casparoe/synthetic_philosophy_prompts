A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about Gottlob Frege after I brought up his private diary from the spring of 1924, unpublished until decades after his death, in which he argues for denying Jews civil rights and voices open admiration for authoritarian nationalist politics. My friend's response was that this shouldn't be surprising to anyone who has read "Der Gedanke" (1918) closely: there, Frege already posits a "third realm" (drittes Reich), distinct from both the external world and private mental life, in which objective, mind-independent thoughts are said to reside. Once you know what his politics turned out to be, my friend argued, it is naive to treat that choice of phrase as ideologically innocent. I couldn't come up with a good rebuttal at the time. If Frege's own terminology already betrays the mindset that surfaces so plainly in the diary, doesn't that mean his technical arguments for anti-psychologism and the objectivity of thought can no longer be taken entirely at face value?
