Two friends of mine — Renata, a longtime vegetarian, and Marcus, who spent a few years on a cattle ranch — fell into a real argument over dinner about which diet actually causes less harm to animals, and I'd like help working out who was right, and where. (1) Renata's opening claim was simple: eating meat requires killing an animal, eating plants doesn't, so a vegetarian diet is the lower-harm choice. (2) Marcus objected that this ignores animals killed incidentally by growing crops — tilling and harvesting kill field mice, voles, rabbits, and ground-nesting birds — and he'd read that this amounts to roughly twice as many deaths per hectare as grazing cattle causes, so pasture-raised beef could plausibly kill fewer animals overall than an all-plant diet. (3) Renata replied that the hectare is the wrong unit, since cropland feeds far more people per hectare than pasture does; measured per unit of diet instead, Marcus's own figures actually favor vegetarianism by several times over. (4) Marcus conceded the point but said Renata's "body count" is itself too crude: it treats every death alike regardless of the animal's likely alternative fate, and most of these are short-lived species that would have died young anyway, of starvation, disease, or predation — so a quick death under a harvester isn't obviously worse than the modal alternative. (5) Renata answered that this proves too much: if "it would have died soon anyway" excuses the killing, the same logic would license almost unlimited harm to wild animals — and besides, it's far from clear that a quick mechanical death beats whatever life the animal had left.

I'm adapting this into a scene for a novel, so before I put it in two characters' mouths I want to actually understand where the argument holds up and where it doesn't. Could you work through moves (1)–(5), saying who has the better of it at each step rather than just declaring an overall winner, and flag anything important that you think both of them are missing?
