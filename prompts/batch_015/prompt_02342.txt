So this happened at dinner last week and its been bugging me since. Our friend Wes has been stuck for months on whether it was messed up that he started dating a coworker two weeks after she left her boyfriend for him, finally got sick of going back and forth on it, so he just told his older sister the whole story and decided to fully adopt whatever verdict she gave him, guilt and all, even though he can't really explain her reasoning if you ask him. Two people at the table started arguing about whether that's a reasonable way to handle a moral question, and I honestly can't tell who's right.

(1) Alina said it's totally fine, that's just what trusting someone wiser looks like, we do it all the time for stuff we're not equipped to judge ourselves, like taking a mechanic's word on whether a used car is safe to buy.

(2) Deshawn said moral questions aren't like car safety: if you just adopt someone's verdict wholesale you don't understand WHY the thing is okay or not, and walking around feeling guilty (or not) about something you can't actually explain isn't the same as being a good judge of your own life.

(3) Alina said that's equally true of the mechanic, most people can't explain why a timing belt is about to go either, they just trust the guy, and nobody says they've failed at owning a car because of that.

(4) Deshawn said the difference is you can check a mechanic's reliability some other way, certifications, reviews, whether the car actually broke down, but figuring out who even counts as a good moral reasoner already takes the kind of judgment Wes was trying to outsource in the first place, so it's circular in a way the mechanic case isn't.

(5) Alina said that's overstated, people build reputations as good judges of this stuff the same way mechanics do, through a track record of advice that held up and other people vouching for them, so Wes trusting the sister everyone in the family already treats as the sensible one isn't structurally different.

Can you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of it at each point, not just who wins overall? And is there an actual asymmetry between moral 'deference' and normal expert deference here, or are they both circling something neither one quite names?
