My homeowners association fines anyone who parks in their own driveway overnight, and whenever people object, the board's answer is always the same: a rule isn't a rule unless it applies to everyone equally, no exceptions. Some neighbors think the rule itself is arbitrary and unfair, so they just ignore it and treat the occasional fine as the price of doing what seems reasonable to them; others argue that once people start deciding case by case which rules are worth following, the whole neighborhood's sense of order falls apart. Neither position fully convinces me: "it's the rule, so obey it" never really explains why this particular rule deserves obedience, while "I'll follow whatever seems fair to me" sounds like a recipe for nothing to hold together at all. It doesn't help that the HOA one neighborhood over has the exact opposite rule and is just as convinced of its own good sense. Should I comply simply because the board has the power to enforce this, side with the neighbors quietly breaking it, or is there a better way to think through the whole situation?
