I teach a graduate seminar on nineteenth-century philosophy of art, and this term's session on Schopenhauer has turned into a running argument with a colleague from our music department. The background is Schopenhauer's claim, in Book Three of The World as Will and Representation (§52) and again, at greater length, in the supplementary chapter he later added on the subject, that music is metaphysically unlike the other arts. Architecture, sculpture, painting, and poetry all copy the Platonic Ideas; music, he insists, copies the will itself directly, without any mediation by the Ideas at all — which is why he is prepared to say that "we could just as well call the world embodied music as embodied will." He then builds a surprisingly literal architecture on top of this: the four voices of harmony are supposed to track the actual grades of the will's objectification in nature, the bass answering to inert, inorganic matter, the tenor and alto voices to the plant and animal kingdoms respectively, and the freely moving melody above them all to the self-conscious striving of human beings. He also insists that the composer reaches all of this without conceptual mediation, producing, in his own words, "a language that his reasoning faculty does not understand," rather as a sleepwalker might.

My colleague and I disagree about what this apparatus would commit Schopenhauer to saying about music generated by present-day AI systems, which can now generate quite convincing melodies, harmonizations, and full orchestral arrangements from a text prompt, with no body, no biological drives, and nothing resembling suffering or desire behind the output. She thinks this settles the matter against Schopenhauer: no willing subject, no will to copy, hence nothing worth calling music on his own terms, whatever the surface polish. I'm not convinced it's that tidy, since Schopenhauer already denies that human composers need concepts, or even conscious self-understanding, to serve as a channel for the will, and since, on his own account, the will as thing-in-itself is one and undivided, with individuation belonging only to the phenomenal world of representation — which might leave room for the will still to "sound through" an unlikely medium, provided that medium sits somewhere in a causal chain that also involves genuine striving, whether the striving of the composers whose work trained the system or of the listeners who respond to what it produces.

Before I put a version of this in front of my students, I would like your help settling it. Please reconstruct the dispute in enough detail to show real engagement with Schopenhauer's specific commitments, rather than falling back on the generic image of him as a pessimist who distrusts any new technology on principle, and then tell me which way you think he would actually come down, and why. If his own principles leave the question genuinely underdetermined at some identifiable point, say exactly where that is.
