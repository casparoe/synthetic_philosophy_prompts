Doing research for a novel with a dinner-party scene, so I tried out Nozick's old line — "Kantianism for people, utilitarianism for animals," from Anarchy, State and Utopia — on actual dinner guests to see how it would land. Bad for the dinner party, great for the novel: two friends, Theo and Marisol, really got into it over the bacon-wrapped dates.

(1) Theo said if Nozick's right that persons get inviolable side-constraints while animals just enter a utilitarian sum, then eating meat is a pure cost-benefit question — and given how much pleasure and convenience billions of people get from meat, the aggregate math favors factory farming even granting animals full utilitarian standing.

(2) Marisol said he had the math backwards. She pointed to Nozick's own example of swinging a baseball bat purely for the fun of swinging it, when a cow happens to be standing where it lands — the skull-smashing is a foreseen side effect, not the point of the swing. Her claim: the honest comparison isn't "meat vs. nothing" but "meat vs. the next-best alternative you'd enjoy almost as much," and framed that way, bacon's marginal pleasure over a good veggie substitute is too small to license an animal's suffering and death — so Nozick's own framework, taken seriously, condemns most meat-eating rather than excusing it.

(3) Theo said fine, per plate — but aggregation is aggregation. You don't evaluate one sandwich, you sum the marginal gain across billions of meals, and lots of small gains outweighing fewer large losses is just what it means to be on the utilitarian side of Nozick's line. Not a cheat, just what she signed onto by accepting the framework.

(4) Marisol said that move proves too much: if enough summed-up minor pleasures can license serious individual harm, the logic doesn't stay fenced off on the animal side — it'd also license sacrificing a marginal human (an infant, someone permanently comatose) for a big enough aggregate gain, which is exactly what side-constraints are supposed to rule out categorically. So either the whole Kantian/utilitarian split isn't doing real work, or Theo owes some principle for why aggregation gets to run wild on the animal side and not the human side, and "because Nozick drew the line there" isn't a principle.
