Since we started integrating LLMs into our product at work, I keep running into the "do these models actually understand anything, or are they just stochastic parrots" debate. Two thought experiments in particular keep getting invoked as though they were making the same objection. One is Searle's Chinese Room: someone who knows no Chinese sits in a room following a rulebook that tells them how to manipulate Chinese symbols, and produces fluent replies without ever grasping what any of it means. The other is the "octopus test" from Emily Bender and Alexander Koller's paper that's closely associated with the stochastic-parrots argument: a hyperintelligent octopus taps an undersea telegraph cable between two people, learns to fake one side of their conversation purely from statistical patterns in the text, and gets exposed only once it's asked for help with something in the physical world, like fending off a bear with sticks. On the surface, both stories seem to make the same point: convincing symbol manipulation isn't the same as understanding. But I suspect they're actually different arguments -- maybe targeting different things -- such that a system could satisfy one of them and still fail the other. Could you spell out precisely how the two arguments differ in their logical structure, and what, in principle, would actually count as answering each one?
