So I was at dinner with two people from my program (both do formal epistemology, i don't) and they got into it over a weather app. Leah: the app says 70% chance of rain; the Principal Principle (PP) says your credence should equal the objective chance when you know it, so credence 0.7, done. Owen: no -- the atmosphere is deterministic (if chaotic), so there's no single-case chance here for the app to be reporting; 70% is just the model's internal uncertainty dressed up as a percentage, and trusting it is expert deference, not PP. Leah: that proves too much -- statistical mechanics is the textbook case of genuine Humean chance riding on deterministic microphysics, so determinism alone can't disqualify weather. Owen: fine, but SM has one canonical measure everyone agrees on, wheras different weather models give different percentages for the same day and city, so nothing here plays the single determinate chance-role Lewis's account needs. Leah: that's an epistemic gap, not a metaphysical one -- the models haven't converged yet, same as how nobody knows the "true" SM measure for most real systems either.

Neither budged. Go through it move by move: who's got the better argument at each stage, and what do you think both of them are missing?
