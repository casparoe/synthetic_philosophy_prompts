Every few months, my mother's side of the family gets together for a big Sunday dinner at my grandmother's house, and over the years I've noticed a pattern I can't quite reason my way out of. Once dinner winds down, people scatter into one of two areas: the back porch, where the older relatives smoke and watch whatever game is on, or the finished basement, where the cousins closer to my age end up talking or half-watching a movie. There's one uncle in particular I try hard to avoid, because if he corners you, you're committed to at least forty-five minutes of his latest health complaint or his newest conspiracy theory, and there's no graceful way to leave once he's started. The odd thing is that for something like nine or ten of our last dozen get-togethers, wherever I've ended up, he's already been camped out there for a while by the time I arrive, as though he knew where I'd land before I did. My cousins have noticed the same thing about their own attempts to dodge him, so I don't think it's just me being paranoid.

What actually troubles me is not the uncle himself but the reasoning involved in trying to outmaneuver him. Say I lean toward the basement because it seems like the less obvious choice; I then realize that "the less obvious choice" is exactly the kind of thing he'd expect from someone trying to avoid him, so he's probably in the basement, which pushes me back toward the porch. But then it occurs to me that he'd have anticipated that second guess too, and probably planted himself back on the porch, and so on. I never reach a choice that survives its own reasoning; every option seems to collapse the moment I actually settle on it. I even considered just deciding by a coin flip to sidestep the whole thing, but that doesn't fully satisfy me either, since he might have a decent sense of which way I'm leaning before I even flip it, or he could simply station himself somewhere like the kitchen doorway, where he can watch both the porch and the basement stairs and just react to whichever way I actually go.

I have no background in philosophy, logic, or math beyond an ordinary high school education, and I've mostly just turned this over on my own between dinners, half-joking about it with my cousins but never getting a real answer. I'd like to know whether there's an actual, principled way to think through a decision like this, given that the other person is genuinely very good (if not infallible) at anticipating exactly the kind of reasoning I'd use to avoid him, or whether the whole thing really is as hopeless and circular as it feels. Is one choice ever more rational than another in a situation like this, and does it change anything if I make the choice by some random method rather than by my own judgment?
