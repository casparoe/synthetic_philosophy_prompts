So next week in my "Bentham and the Birth of Utilitarianism" seminar we're doing the session everyone secretly looks forward to: his suppressed writings on sexual morality. Most people only know the guy from the felicific calculus and the auto-icon (the stuffed body in the cabinet at UCL), but there's this whole other side where he pointed the exact same pleasure-pain accounting he used on prisons and legislation at "irregular" sex too. The 1785 essay defending decriminalizing sodomy sat unpublished for basically two centuries -- Louis Crompton finally got it into print in 1978 -- because Bentham was sure it'd wreck his reputaiton if it came out that he thought hanging men for it was just deranged utilitarian bookkeeping. Then there's the later 1814-16 material (only published in full in 2014, as "Of Sexual Irregularities") where he goes even further and argues that "unnatural" just means uncommon, that being disgusted by someone else's kink isn't a reason to prosecute them, and that the only thing doing any real work in the whole "case against" is what he calls antipathy dressed up as an argument. He apparently even kicked around titling some of it "Not Paul, but Jesus," which, if you know Bentham's opinion of Paul, is pretty on the nose.

I wanted to open class with a joke and was chatting with a colleague about it over lunch, but neither of us could come up with anything better than "haha utilitarians calculate everything, even sex" -- which isn't really a joke about Bentham's argument so much as about utilitarianism in general, and my students have definately heard some version of that one already. Could you write me something short (a one-liner, or a quick two-line setup/punchline) where the humor actually turns on some specific bit of his reasoning -- the antipathy move, the unnatural-just-means-uncommon bit, the fact that he was totally fine with being embalmed and gawked at forever but too chicken to publish this stuff while alive, or whatever angle you think works best? Ideally it should mostly land for someone who's actually read some of this material, not just someone who knows Bentham as "the hedonic calculus guy."
