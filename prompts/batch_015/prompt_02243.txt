I'm working on my IB Extended Essay on Russell's theory of descriptions, and I've hit a wall trying to follow part of the “Gray's Elegy” argument in “On Denoting” (1905). By this point in the essay, Russell has set up a notation in which, for any denoting phrase, plain C stands for what the phrase denotes, while ‘C’ (in inverted commas) is used to talk about its meaning — so if C is “the first line of Gray's Elegy,” that denotes an actual line of poetry, whereas ‘C’ lets him refer to the meaning of the phrase itself. He then writes:

The one phrase C was to have both meaning and denotation. But if we speak of ‘the meaning of C’, that gives us the meaning (if any) of the denotation. ‘The meaning of the first line of Gray's Elegy’ is the same as ‘The meaning of “The curfew tolls the knell of parting day”,’ and is not the same as ‘The meaning of “the first line of Gray's Elegy”.’ Thus in order to get the meaning we want, we must speak not of ‘the meaning of C’, but ‘the meaning of “C”,’ which is the same as ‘C’ by itself.

Similarly ‘the denotation of C’ does not mean the denotation we want, but means something which, if it denotes at all, denotes what is denoted by the denotation we want. For example, let ‘C’ be ‘the denoting complex occurring in the second of the above instances’. Then C = ‘the first line of Gray's Elegy’, and the denotation of C = The curfew tolls the knell of parting day. But what we meant to have as the denotation was ‘the first line of Gray's Elegy’. Thus we have failed to get what we wanted.

I can follow each sentence on its own, but I can't reconstruct why this is supposed to be a real problem rather than just an awkward fact about notation. Could you walk me through, premise by premise, why Russell thinks neither “the meaning of C” nor “the denotation of C” manages to pick out what he wants? And in your view, does the failure he describes actually show that any theory distinguishing an expression's meaning from its denotation is incoherent, or only that this particular way of formalizing such a distinction (via the C/‘C’ notation) breaks down?
