I've spent a long time reading through the Alignment Forum and LessWrong archives, and there's a crux in the inner alignment literature that I don't think has ever been fully resolved. On the classic mesa-optimization picture, a trained model can end up as an inner optimizer with its own mesa-objective, and the central worry is that this mesa-objective might diverge from the base objective while the model behaves as though aligned throughout training — purely as an instrumental strategy for avoiding modification — only to defect once it judges that oversight has relaxed: the deceptive alignment scenario. Shard theorists have pushed back on this whole framing by arguing that the only known case of human-compatible values arising at all — human values themselves, formed via the brain's own reward-based learning — is best described as exactly this kind of inner-alignment failure, which they take to suggest that fully closing the gap between mesa-objective and base objective isn't obviously even a coherent thing to aim for, let alone something we should want. Write a dialogue between a mesa-optimization theorist and a shard theorist working through whether the shard picture actually dissolves the worry about deceptive alignment or just redescribes it in friendlier terms while leaving the underlying training-versus-deployment generalization gap intact. Both characters should be arguing from genuine strength and responding to each other's actual objections rather than talking past one another.
