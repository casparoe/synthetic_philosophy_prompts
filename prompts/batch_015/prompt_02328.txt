Rudolf Carnap described himself as a committed socialist, and in the Vienna Circle's 1929 manifesto -- co-written with Otto Neurath and Hans Hahn -- he affirmed a close bond between the scientific world-conception and the broader project of social enlightenment, even as he insisted that logic and the analysis of language were neutral tools, separable from any particular political program. Neurath went further, arguing in the socialist calculation debate of the 1920s that comprehensive economic planning without market prices was feasible in principle, a claim the Austrian economists Mises and Hayek rejected on the grounds that no central authority could ever aggregate the dispersed knowledge that market prices convey. That old dispute has recently resurfaced, as economists ask whether large-scale machine learning finally supplies the computational power that Neurath's side always lacked.

Had Carnap lived to see this revival, how do you think he would have judged the claim that AI can succeed where earlier planning schemes failed? Would he have read it as vindicating the hopes for social planning he shared with Neurath, or would something specific to his own philosophical commitments have made him more skeptical of that claim than his politics alone would suggest?
