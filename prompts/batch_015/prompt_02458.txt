A friend and I had a long argument last night about Kavka's toxin puzzle, and neither of us gave ground. My position was that the puzzle isn't really a paradox at all: a rational agent can simply resolve, before midnight, to drink the toxin the next day, and then actually drink it out of fidelity to that resolution, since a person willing to follow through on a resolution thereby counts as having genuinely intended the act from the start. My friend kept insisting this leaves the real problem untouched, though she couldn't quite say what that problem is once this kind of follow-through is allowed. If resolving now and simply honoring that resolution later is all it takes, what exactly is left of Kavka's puzzle that philosophers still find worth arguing about?
