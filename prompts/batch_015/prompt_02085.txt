I've been rereading Tobias Baumann's old CLR posts on "surrogate goals" (the trick where an agent facing possible extortion adds a cheap, orthogonal goal to what it cares about, hoping that threats get redirected onto the surrogate rather than onto its real values) and drafted the short piece below about a gap in the proposal that I don't think the standard replies close. I'd appreciate your comments before I post it anywhere as a shortform, especially on: whether the argument in the third and fourth paragraphs is actually valid, whether it collapses into the already-known credibility or private-information objections, and whether the closing claim is too strong.

Surrogate goals and the cost of bundling

The classic exposition asks us to imagine Alice, who cares about money and, more, about staying alive. Rather than face Bob's threat of "pay up or I kill you" head-on, Alice adds a "meaningless" surrogate to her utility function - an intense aversion to the existence of a platinum sphere of exactly 42.82cm diameter - hoping Bob will threaten the sphere instead, since the two threats are stipulated to be equally effective levers from his point of view. Baumann pitches this as a general anti-extortion trick, but a lot of the interest in it at CLR is downstream of worries about acausal and updateless bargaining between AI systems that can read each other's source code or simulate one another, where the "threat" need not involve any causal contact at all.

Baumann already notices the obvious problem: nothing stops Bob from threatening the sphere and Alice's life together, and Alice caring more about the sphere doesn't by itself rule this out. His fix is to make the surrogate continuous and unbounded, so a Bob who wants a bigger stick can just grow the sphere rather than reach for Alice's real goal.

But that only removes Bob's incentive to bundle if his sole reason for reaching toward the real goal was that the sphere wasn't big enough. It does nothing for a Bob who bundles simply because it's free. Credibly threatening Alice's life was already a precondition for the original threat to work, so that capability doesn't vanish once Alice modifies; tacking it onto the sphere-threat costs Bob nothing and, by orthogonality, doesn't dilute the sphere-threat's credibility either. And "free to add" looks like the default rather than a corner case precisely in the mutual-simulation or program-equilibrium settings that motivate the acausal application, since there's no informational asymmetry left for a target to hide behind once its whole policy is legible to the threatener.

The obvious patch is for Alice to simply refuse to honor the real-goal component of any bundled threat, responding to it exactly as she would if it weren't bundled, while treating only the sphere-component as real. That removes Bob's free lunch - but notice what's doing the work: Alice's independent capacity to hold firm against threats to her actual values, not the surrogate goal. If she has that capacity robustly, it's unclear why she needed the sphere; if she doesn't, the sphere doesn't supply it, since the cost-free bundle reintroduces exactly the exposure she was trying to route around. Recasting the whole thing in updateless terms, as Baumann himself suggests, doesn't obviously help either: an updateless agent's "surrogate goal" is just whatever its optimal policy treats as worth protecting, and if that policy already holds firm against bundled threats to the real goal, it was already blackmail-resistant, full stop.

So my tentative conclusion is that surrogate goals aren't an alternative to solving the hard problem of credible resistance to blackmail against one's true values - they're a refinement that only earns its keep once that problem is already solved, at which point they're doing comparatively little further protective work, whatever other benefits (like keeping some trade on the table) they might still offer.

Have I equivocated somewhere on what "free" means for Bob, and is there a standard answer to this in the surrogate-goals or safe-Pareto-improvement literature that I'm just not aware of?
