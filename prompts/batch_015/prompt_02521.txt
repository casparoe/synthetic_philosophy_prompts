I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have spent a good deal of time on my own turning over the question of whether anyone ever truly deserves praise or blame for what they do. That eventually led me to Galen Strawson's "Basic Argument," which concludes that genuine moral responsibility is impossible, since it would require a person to be the ultimate, self-caused source of their own character, and nothing can cause itself in that way. What strikes me as remarkable is that his father, P. F. Strawson, is famous for defending moral responsibility on almost the opposite grounds, in an essay called "Freedom and Resentment": he argues that our practices of resentment, gratitude, and blame don't need any metaphysical backing at all, since they are simply a natural, more or less unshakeable feature of ordinary human relationships, largely untouched by whether determinism is true.

I would like to understand how seriously these two views actually threaten each other. Could you compare them for me? Specifically, does grounding moral responsibility in these everyday reactive attitudes genuinely withstand the son's argument, or does that argument, if sound, reach deep enough to undercut the reactive attitudes themselves? Please justify whichever conclusion you reach.
