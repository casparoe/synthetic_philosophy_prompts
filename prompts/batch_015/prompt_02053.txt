Quick one for a piece I'm working on about AI and the limits of computation. Since Gödel's incompleteness theorem shows that any consistent formal system has true statements it can never prove from inside itself, and Penrose's whole point is that a human mathematician can just step outside the system and see that those statements are true, doesn't that basically settle it that our minds can't just be running an algorithm? Every AI researcher I bring this up with brushes it off in about ten seconds flat, but if the logic really is that clean, why do labs keep treating AGI like its just an engineering problem, instead of admitting theres a hard philosophical wall in the way?
