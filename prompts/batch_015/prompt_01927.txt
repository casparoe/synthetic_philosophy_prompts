In AP Calculus BC we learned that a conditionally convergent series can, by the Riemann Rearrangement Theorem, be reordered to converge to any value we like, or to diverge entirely. While doing some independent reading on infinite ethics, including Nick Bostrom's paper on the subject, it struck me that the same fact might threaten total utilitarianism once we allow infinitely many people: summing everyone's welfare to compare two worlds could give different answers depending only on the order in which people are added up. Could you construct a concrete example of this: two different orderings of the same infinite collection of individual welfare levels that lead a total utilitarian to opposite verdicts about which of two worlds is better? I'd also appreciate a brief explanation of how this order-dependence problem differs from the separate "infinitarian paralysis" problem, and whether either one has a real solution that doesn't just stipulate a single "correct" ordering by fiat.
