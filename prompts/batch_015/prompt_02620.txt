A friend and I spent last night arguing about the money-pump argument and reached no resolution. It started when he admitted that he ranks coffee over tea, tea over hot chocolate, and hot chocolate over coffee. I pointed out that this makes him exploitable: a bookie could charge him a small fee at each step to trade him around that cycle, leaving him back where he started but steadily poorer. He accepted the mechanics but denied that they showed his preferences to be irrational, appealing to what I now recognize as a version of 'resolute choice': an agent who foresees the whole trading sequence may simply refuse, at some point, to complete it, and there is nothing incoherent in that so long as rationality constrains choices over time rather than each trade in isolation. What stumped me is that refusing still means turning down, in the moment, an option he ranks higher than the alternative on offer. Could you write a dialogue between a defender of the classic money-pump argument for transitivity and a defender of resolute choice, one where they genuinely engage each other's strongest points rather than talk past one another, and get at whether the disagreement is really about the rationality of cyclical preferences themselves, or about whether rationality is a synchronic or a diachronic matter?
