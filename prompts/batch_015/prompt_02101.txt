ok so my friend and I got into a whole thing last night about whether paranormal claims can ever actually be tested — he kept insisting the whole field is unfalsifiable becuase believers always have some excuse ready when a psychic fails under test conditions. I said skeptics can pull basically the same move in reverse, and this morning I dug up this bit from Henry Sidgwick (yeah, the utilitarian ethics guy) — his opening address as the first president of the Society for Psychical Research, from 1882 — which I think backs me up:

"The highest degree of demonstrative force that we can obtain out of any single record of investigation is, of course, limited by the trustworthiness of the investigator. We have done all that we can when the critic has nothing left to allege except that the investigator is in the trick. But when he has nothing else left to allege he will allege that."

I get the general shape of it but that last line keeps tripping me up. Is Sidgwick basically admitting this is a fatal problem for his own side, or is he needling the skeptics for having their own unfalsifiable move up their sleeve? And more importantly — does my "gotcha" against my friend actually hold up once you think it through, or is the situation less symmetric than it looks?
