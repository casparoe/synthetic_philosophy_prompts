The novel centers on a god-like archive everyone in-world calls the Loom, which supposedly contains literally every true fact there is — past, present, future, no exceptions. My protagonist is a disgraced archivist trying to prove the Loom's big claim (that it holds ALL truths) is a lie, and I wanted her central argument to be built on something real instead of just hand-wavey magic logic, so i started reading about epistemic logic and landed on Fitch's paradox of knowability.

From what I understand, Fitch basically proved that if you assume every truth is knowable in principle, it logically forces the absurd conclusion that every truth is already known by someone — and since that's obviously false (nobody knows everything), the only way out is to accept that there's got to be at least one truth out there that is straight-up unknowable, period, even to a perfect all-knowing being. So my plan is that her whole arc becomes a hunt for one of these "Fitch-truths" — some specific fact about the world that's provably, permanently beyond the reach of even the Loom — and producing it in front of the Loom's priesthood is what breaks its authority for good. My writing group already thinks I'm overthinking the magic system, but I'd rather get the logic right than have some philosophy nerd close the book in chapter three.

Two things I could use help with: first, what's a good candidate for what that unknowable fact could actually BE (something meaty enough to build a climax around, not just a boring logic-textbook example), and second — and this is the part thats been bugging me at 1am — how would she, or anyone, ever confirm she'd actually found a genuine "Fitch-truth" rather than just something nobody happens to know YET? Feels like there should be some way to verify it or the whole scene falls apart, but I can't quite work out how that would function in-world. Any thoughts, either on the philosophy itself or on how to make it dramatically satisfying?
