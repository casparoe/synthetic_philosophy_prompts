Ok so I've been re-reading Anarchy, State, and Utopia for a seminar on rights-based ethics, and I keep getting stuck on the bit early in the book where Nozick, almost as a throwaway remark, floats treating humans and animals under two totally different moral logics: strict Kantian side-constraints for persons (you can never just use someone as a means, full stop, no matter the aggregate payoff), but plain old aggregative utilitarianism for animals, where you just weigh harms and benefits like a ledger.

Thing is, Nozick himself doesn't seem to fully trust the view he's floating. He's got that odd little example about snapping your fingers to the beat of some music, where by some weird causal chain this kills 10,000 contented, ownerless cows -- and even when he stipulates its painless and instantaneous, he can't quite bring himself to just say that's fine, even though pure aggregation with nothing on the other side of the ledger should say its fine. He also gestures at something like a utility-monster worry, except pointed at animals specifically: some human "utility devourer" who gets so much pleasure out of mistreating animals that the aggregate math just favors him. This whole thing came up over beers with a colleague last week and neither of us could agree on what to make of it.

So here's what I want: write me an essay that actually grapples with whether this two-tier picture -- inviolable side-constraints for humans, aggregative calculation for animals -- can be rescued as a stable, principled position, or whether it's fundamentally an unstable halfway house. Take seriously the best case for it (maybe moral status really does come in degrees, and different degrees warrant categorically different modes of treatment, not just more-or-less weight inside one shared calculus), and also the best case against (there's something fishy about where exactly you're supposed to draw the line, and why crossing it should flip you all the way from ledger-morality to full inviolability rather than to, say, weaker but still real side-constraints). Don't hedge at the end -- give me an actual verdict on whether the schema can be saved, and how, or whether it collapses, and in which direction.
