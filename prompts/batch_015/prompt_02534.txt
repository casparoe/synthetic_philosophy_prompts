Over the past year my father has been living with behavioral-variant frontotemporal dementia, and one of the hardest parts of it has been that the illness has made him say things that are genuinely cutting and cruel, in a way that would have been completely out of character for him before he got sick. I understand, intellectually, that the changes in his brain are responsible for this and that he is not "choosing" unkindness the way he once might have, but in the moment, when he says something wounding to my mother or to me, I still feel a flash of real resentment, and afterward I feel guilty for having felt it at all. I have no background in philosophy, but a friend who studied it years ago mentioned, when I described what was going on, that it reminded her of P.F. Strawson's essay "Freedom and Resentment," so I have spent some evenings since then reading the essay itself and a few summaries of it. As I understand it, Strawson argues that our whole practice of holding people responsible rests on natural "reactive attitudes" such as resentment, gratitude, and indignation, and that we only step back into a more detached, "objective" way of viewing someone, managing or handling them rather than relating to them as a fellow participant in a relationship, in cases such as early childhood or serious mental illness, where we no longer think the ordinary demands we place on one another apply.

Please describe what you take to be the most important implications of this account for how I should think about my own resentment toward my father, and for how my family decides when to hold him to a hurtful remark versus simply excusing it as a symptom of the disease. What makes this hard for me is that Strawson's own examples seem to divide people fairly cleanly into those who are fully inside the world of reactive attitudes and those who are fully outside it, whereas my father is somewhere in between: still recognisably himself much of the time, capable of warmth and humor one moment and real cruelty the next, and declining gradually rather than all at once. I would value your view on whether Strawson's framework actually has the resources to handle a case this partial and shifting, or whether it was really built for tidier cases and would need to be stretched, or supplemented with something else, to be of much use to a family like mine.
