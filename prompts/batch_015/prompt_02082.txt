In my school's philosophy club, I've been reading about Eli Hirsch's idea of "quantifier variance" — the claim that disputes over what exists, like whether gluing the front half of a trout to the back half of a turkey creates one more object, are often merely verbal, since "exists" has no single correct meaning. Ted Sider disagrees, arguing that one meaning of "exists" really does carve the world at its joints, while David Chalmers has proposed testing such disputes by replacing the contested term with two neutral, subscripted stand-ins for each side's meaning and seeing whether any disagreement survives. Please explain the most important implications of this debate for the arguments I have with my friends over whether a hot dog is a sandwich or whether Pluto still counts as a planet, and tell me whether Chalmers's test could actually settle whether those are substantive disagreements or just verbal ones.
