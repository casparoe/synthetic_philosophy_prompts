I'm putting together a seminar for our decision theory grad students, and there's a question about "acausal trade" that I keep meaning to sort out in my own head before I inflict it on them. You know the idea - it's mostly out of the rationalist/x-risk adjacent literature (Yudkowsky, Soares, Oesterheld, Wei Dai and that whole cluster): two agents with absolutely no causal channel between them - too far apart in the universe, seperated in time, maybe one is even a simulation of the other - can still end up cooperating for mutual benefit, purely because each can reason reliably about what the other would do. Every year some student asks me which decision theory actually earns that conclusion, rather than just smuggling the cooperation in by assumption (e.g. by stipulating the agents are literally identical, or that they've somehow already "synchronized"), and every year I realize I don't have a crisp answer.
