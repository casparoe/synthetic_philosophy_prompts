A friend and I had a long argument about teleosemantics that neither of us won. She defended the Millikan/Neander line that grounding mental content in a trait's "proper function" — what it was historically selected for, rather than what it merely does now — finally makes intentionality fully naturalistic, with no residual mystery about purpose. I argued that this doesn't eliminate teleology, just relocates it: talk of what a mechanism is "for," and of it misrepresenting when it fails to do that, does exactly the work Aristotle's final cause used to do, only re-grounded in selection history instead of form.

That sent us down a rabbit hole about what Aristotle himself would make of the theory, since he wasn't naive about selection-style explanations of apparent purpose. In Physics II.8 he explicitly considers and rejects an Empedoclean proposal that chance variations (his example is teeth) simply survived when they happened to be useful, on the grounds that outcomes occurring "always or for the most part" cannot be explained by mere chance survival.

So: would Aristotle treat modern teleosemantics as a vindication of his fourth cause, or would he redeploy that same Physics II.8 argument against it — and if he would, does teleosemantics have any real answer to it, or does it just relabel Empedocles' surviving chance-combinations as "selection history"?
