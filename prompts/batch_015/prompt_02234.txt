ok here's a niche one: most people know Michael Dummett as the Frege/logic guy, but he also wrote a whole book in 2001 (On Immigration and Refugees) arguing that people basically have a standing right to immigrate or claim asylum, with only one real exception -- a country can restrict entry if its population is genuinely at risk of being overwhelmed by the scale of arrivals. He argued that exception almost never actually applied to rich European countries in practice (immigrant shares were only something like 2-5%), so he read most restrictionist politics as racism wearing a respectable coat. He died in Dec 2011, before the 2015 refugee crisis, Brexit, and the rise of parties like AfD. Dont just say 'he'd have hated Brexit' -- actually reason through his own framework: does the scale of arrivals since 2015 finally trip his 'overwhelmed' exception, or is the argument built so it can basically never be triggered no matter the numbers? And if it's the latter, is that a bug in his view, or something he'd have happily bitten the bullet on?
