A friend and I spent last night arguing about Avicenna's flying man thought experiment: his scenario of a person created fully formed and suspended in midair, limbs kept apart so that they cannot touch one another, with no sensory contact with either his own body or the external world, who nevertheless affirms his own existence. My friend maintains that this succeeds as a genuine proof of the soul's immateriality, an Islamic-world anticipation of Descartes' cogito; I countered that it establishes only the more modest, purely epistemic point that self-awareness does not depend on awareness of any bodily part, and that moving from there to a really distinct immaterial substance illegitimately infers what is metaphysically possible from what is merely conceivable. Write an essay that settles our dispute. Lay out what the argument is best interpreted as trying to prove, engage seriously with the strongest objections to reading it as a successful proof of dualism, and end with a definite verdict rather than splitting the difference between us.
