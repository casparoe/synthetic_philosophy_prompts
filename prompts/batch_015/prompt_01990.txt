I'm a PhD student in philosophy working mostly in comparative epistemology, and this term I'm taking a seminar that pairs the Zhuangzi with early modern skepticism. Several sessions went into dismantling what my instructor calls the lazy pairing of the butterfly dream passage that closes the book's second chapter (the Qiwulun, or "Discussion on Making All Things Equal") with Descartes' dream argument from the First Meditation. Her point is that the two texts are doing different philosophical work: Descartes raises the possibility that he is dreaming as a threat to be neutralized on the way to secure foundations, and he does eventually neutralize it, arguing in the Sixth Meditation that waking experience, unlike dreams, connects coherently with the rest of one's remembered life. Zhuangzi, by contrast, never tells us whether it was Zhuangzi dreaming he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming it is Zhuangzi; the passage simply ends by naming that irresolution the "transformation of things" (wu hua, 物化). This refusal to adjudicate seems continuous with the relativism earlier in the same chapter, where loaches, monkeys, and deer have incompatible standards of comfortable dwelling, and Mao Qiang and Lady Li, beauties to men, send fish diving and birds flying. It also fits with the equanimity Zhuangzi reportedly showed at his own wife's death, recounted in the later "Perfect Happiness" chapter (among the more doubtfully attributed "Outer Chapters"): he marked it by drumming on a basin and singing rather than mourning.

Over dinner after class, a friend from the program and I disagreed about how Zhuangzi, read this way, would respond to something like the modern simulation hypothesis: Bostrom's argument that we may well be living inside an ancestor simulation, or the looser cultural version of that worry that circulates via things like The Matrix. My friend thinks Zhuangzi would seize on the hypothesis as basically vindicating him. I suspect that is too quick, since it assumes he would accept the framing on offer in the first place, namely that there is a fact of the matter here worth losing sleep over, rather than treating the demand to know which layer is the "real" one as exactly the sort of one-sided, clinging perspective his relativism is meant to dissolve. Could you work through which of us has the better reading, arguing from Zhuangzi's actual commitments in the text rather than the surface resemblance between his parable and the simulation scenario?
