I'm writing a historical novel whose protagonist is modeled closely on George Eliot, and I want her crisis of faith to be intellectually credible rather than just atmospheric. As I understand the biography, her break with Evangelical Christianity was essentially triggered by reading Darwin's On the Origin of Species: the picture of a blind, competitive, purposeless nature displaced the providential God of her upbringing, after which she spent her career as a novelist working out the ethical consequences of this new Darwinian materialism. The scene I'm struggling to write is what comes next — how a mind that has just absorbed Darwinian materialism arrives at the strict sense of duty and self-renunciation that governs a heroine like Dorothea Brooke. Should I invent some further influence to bridge that gap, or is the tension between the two simply left unresolved in Eliot's own thought?
