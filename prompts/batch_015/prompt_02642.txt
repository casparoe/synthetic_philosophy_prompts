I'm working on a term paper for my history-of-science elective on the early boundary disputes between science and pseudoscience, and I've been reading Henry Sidgwick's inaugural address as the first president of the Society for Psychical Research, delivered in July 1882. Just after explaining that the Society will investigate thought-reading, mesmerism, clairvoyance, and "the mass of obscure phenomena commonly known as Spiritualistic," Sidgwick says this:

"…the present state of things is a scandal to the enlightened age in which we live. That the dispute as to the reality of these marvellous phenomena — of which it is quite impossible to exaggerate the scientific importance, if only a tenth part of what has been alleged by generally credible witnesses could be shewn to be true — I say it is a scandal that the dispute as to the reality of these phenomena should still be going on, that so many competent witnesses should have declared their belief in them, that so many others should be profoundly interested in having the question determined, and yet that the educated world, as a body, should still be simply in the attitude of incredulity." (Proceedings of the Society for Psychical Research, vol. 1, 1882, pp. 7–8)

A few lines later, he adds that the Society itself is "quite unpledged" on whether the phenomena are genuine, and that members should proceed "without any foregone conclusion as to their nature."

I can follow the words individually, but I'm struggling to reconstruct the actual argument. Could you help me with two things: first, untangle the grammar enough to say plainly what Sidgwick is claiming is "scandalous" — the likely reality of the phenomena, the mere persistence of unresolved dispute, or something else; and second, tell me whether calling the situation a "scandal" before any investigation is really consistent with the neutrality he claims a moment later, or whether that word choice already gives away which side he expects to be vindicated? I'd also be curious whether leaning on the number of "competent witnesses" is doing real evidential work here or is closer to an appeal to authority.
