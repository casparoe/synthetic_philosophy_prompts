I'm on my small town's licensing board (nothing glamorous, mostly liquor licenses and zoning stuff) and something's been bugging me for weeks. There's a private, members-only club on the edge of town — basically a swingers club — that's been running for a few years now. It's zoned properly in the industrial part of town, no complaints from neighbors, no crime tied to it, and everyone involved is a consenting adult who joined voluntarily. Lately a pretty vocal group has been pushing the board to pull its license anyway, and the most articulate guy making the case doesn't even try to argue that anyone's being harmed. His whole point is basically that a town only holds together because its people share a rough moral consensus, and once you let people believe "anything goes" behind closed doors as long as everyone consents, that consensus starts to erode — and a community that won't defend its own moral code eventually falls apart, the same way it would if it just let treason slide. So according to him it doesn't matter one bit that it's "private," because a community's morals aren't just a bunch of individual preferences, theyre collective property, and the town has just as much right to protect that as it does to protect itself from any other internal threat.

Honestly it's a way more serious argument than I expected going in, and it's already swayed a couple people on the board. Something about it still feels off to me, but every time I want to just say "come on, nobody's getting hurt," it feels like im dodging his actual point, since he'd say the harm is real, it's just spread out and long-term instead of landing on one identifiable victim. Is there an actual good answer to this kind of argument, or does the guy genuinely have a point and I'm just too uncomfortable with the topic to admit it? I'd rather actually understand this than just go with my gut.
