Ok weird question but this has been living in my head rent free for weeks, bear with me.

We just set up live migration for one of our stateful services: it freezes a running instance, serializes its entire state (heap, open connections, everything) to a snapshot, and spins that snapshot up as a new process on a different box. Once the new one's healthy we kill the old one - normally no issue. Last week though, a networking blip meant the "kill the old one" step silently failed, so for about four minutes both the old and new instance were alive at the same time, fully isolated from each other, each one handling live traffic with zero idea the other existed. Identical history right up to the snapshot, then they just diverged from there.

Everyone else treated it as a boring incident to write a postmortem for, but i cant stop picking at it. In the normal case, did the instance actually move to the new box, or did we just make a copy and then kill the "real" one a minute later? And during those four minutes, were both of them equally "it," was one of them secretly the impostor, or does that question just stop making sense once there's two? Feels like there should be a principled way to answer this instead of just shrugging, but I can't work it out myself - what's actually going on here?
