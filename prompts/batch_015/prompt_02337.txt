so in my philosophy elective (yes my school actually has one, i got lucky) we just read this P.F. Strawson essay called "Freedom and Resentment" and it's honestly been living in my head since. his basic point is that stuff like resentment, gratitude, indignation, getting your feelings hurt, feeling proud of someone - what he calls "reactive attitudes" - are actually the real foundation of how we treat people as morally responsible, way more than any abstract theory about free will vs determinism could ever be. but then he also describes this other option, the "objective attitude," where instead of relating to someone as a person who can be blamed or praised, you just see them as a thing to be managed, handled, or treated, kind of like how you'd think about a toddler, or someone going through a serious mental illness, or i guess a broken vending machine. his whole arguement is basically that even if hard determinism turned out to be true, we couldnt actually sustain that objective attitude toward the people we're close to on a regular, wholesale basis - it would cost too much of what makes a relationship a relationship, and he thinks we're not even built to pull it off long term anyway.

me and my friend were arguing about this at lunch (she thinks a sufficiently logical person actually could live like that, i don't) and i keep wanting to see it played out instead of just argued about. so could you write me a short story about it? like, a character who gets legitimately, fully convinced by the hard determinism argument (not just edgy-teenager convinced, actually convinced) and decides to try to consistently take up the objective attitude toward everyone around them - so no real anger when their brother's a jerk to them, no actual pride when their best friend pulls off something impressive, just calm detached explaining and managing, like everyone around them is a complicated weather system with a face. i think it'd be more interesting if you keep it low stakes and everyday, like a family dinner or a group project falling apart, instead of some huge dramatic thing, since strawson's whole point was supposed to be about ordinary life, not extreme cases. mostly i'm curious whether the story ends up proving him right that nobody could actually keep it up, or whether you think a good enough writer could make someone who does it and is genuinely fine.
