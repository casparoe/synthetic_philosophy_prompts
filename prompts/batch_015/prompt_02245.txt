So I'm a phil of mind PhD student who's been crashing a mechanistic interpretability reading group run by some ML students down the hall, and something's been bugging me for weeks that I haven't been able to articulate well when I bring it up there. Back in my first year I read Ramsey's *Representation Reconsidered* for a seminar, and the bit that stuck with me is the "job description challenge" — his argument that a state doesn't count as a representation just because it reliably correlates with some property (that's true of a thermostat's bimetallic strip, or honestly almost anything causally hooked up to anything else). For representation-talk to do real explanatory work, the state has to function as a stand-in that some downstream part of the system actually exploits, in a way that could in principle misfire.

Now the interp crowd talks constantly about networks "representing" features — directions in activation space, the linear representation hypothesis, the whole superposition picture from the Anthropic toy models work — and a lot of the evidence for any specific claimed feature is basically "a linear probe can decode it." I went down a rabbit hole recently with the Hewitt & Liang control-tasks paper and then the amnesic-probing/causal-ablation lineage (Elazar et al and descendants), and it started to seem like even people inside interpretability are half-aware of Ramsey's exact worry: decodability isn't the same as the network using the info, so a lot of probing results might just be well-dressed correlates rather than real representations.

What I'd love is for someone to actually walk through the mapping instead of me hand-waving at it like I do to my labmates. Does correcting for probe selectivity via control tasks get you any closer to satisfying the job description, or does it only rule out probe memorization while leaving Ramsey's deeper worry completely untouched? Does something like amnesic probing or activation patching actually satisfy the job description in Ramsey's sense, or does it just establish correlation-with-behavior dressed up as "causal," which isn't quite the same as stand-in-hood either? Basically I want to know if theres a principled place interpretability researchers could stand and say "okay, THIS is a genuine representation, not just a convenient redescription" — or whether realism about interpretability is structurally doomed to be one causal-intervention paper away from justified, forever.
