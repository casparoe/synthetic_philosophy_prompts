hey, I'm drafting a chapter on anti-party political thought and I keep getting stuck on a move Simone Weil makes in "On the Abolition of All Political Parties" that I haven't seen anyone really push on. She opens by saying "party" means something different in Anglo-Saxon countries than on the continent - more like a sport, because of its aristocratic origins - and basically brackets the English two-party tradition off as a seperate case her argument isn't really about. Then she lays out her actual indictment as three criteria that supposedly make any party totalitarian in aspiration: (1) it's a machine for generating collective passion, (2) it's built to exert collective pressure on the minds of its own members, and (3) its own unlimited growth becomes its real, unspoken end. She never goes back and actually runs the English case through those three tests - she just lets the "more like a sport" line do all the exempting.

Could you build the tightest possible argument, using only her own three criteria (not generic "democracies need organized opposition" priors), that a mature two-party system like 18th/19th century Britain's satisfies all three just as much as the continental parties she's actually attacking - so the carve-out is unearned and her totalitarian verdict should extend to the English case too?
