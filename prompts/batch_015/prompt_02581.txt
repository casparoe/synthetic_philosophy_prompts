Been on a bit of a modal metaphysics kick lately, started from a Less Wrong tangent on Tegmark-style multiverses and somehow ended with me actually reading Lewis's *On the Plurality of Worlds*. The thing I can't stop chewing on is the counterpart theory vs. transworld identity split. Lewis's view is that nothing exists in more than one possible world, so when we say "I could've dropped out of my PhD," what's literally true is that some *counterpart* of me (similar enough in the relevant respects, but a numerically distinct guy) drops out in some other world. Kripke's famous complaint about this (the "Humphrey" objection, named after Hubert Humphrey losing the '68 election) is that this is a total bait-and-switch: Humphrey wouldn't be comforted to learn that some other guy, however similar, won an election in a different world, so its got to really be *him*, the same individual, existing across worlds, not a mere lookalike. Brought this up at my rationalist meetup and we argued for like an hour about whether the disagreement is even substantive or just a verbal dispute wearing a metaphysics costume. No consensus, per usual.

Anyway, I think this is exactly the kind of thing fiction can do better than argument, kind of like how Egan's Permutation City makes you actually *feel* the weirdness of copies and dust theory instead of just asserting it at you. So: could you write me a short story (page or two is fine) where the counterpart-vs-transworld-identity question isn't just background flavor but is actually load-bearing for the plot? Like, a character's rights, relationships, or survival genuinely hinge on which theory is true, not just "which theory sounds cooler." Could be an inheritance or custody case that has to adjudicate claims across branching timelines, or a rescue team arguing over whether the person who stumbled back through the portal is "really" their teammate or just a very good counterpart, or something else entirely, whatever premise works best. I just don't want a story that name-drops the theories as set dressing; I want the plot to function as an actual intuition pump the way trolley problems do for ethics.
