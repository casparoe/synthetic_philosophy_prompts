I fell down a rabbit hole a while back on some AI safety forum (might've been LessWrong, don't fully remember) and hit this idea called a "commitment race" that's been rattling around my head since. The basic gist, as I understood it anyway: if two agents are headed toward some conflict or negotiation, whoever manages to lock themselves into a hardline position FIRST, in a way the other side can verify is real and can't be walked back, basically wins, because the other side has no rational move except to bend. Problem is, if both sides know this, they're both incentivized to commit as early and as irreversably as possible, before either of them has had time to actually think it through or even fully understand what they're committing to. Worst case scenario, you get something like the doomsday device from Dr. Strangelove - built specifically so it CAN'T be called off once triggered, thats the whole point of it, except of course it backfires spectacularly. Kinda reminds me of force-pushing over a shared branch so nobody can un-merge it, except the stakes are a bit higher than a botched PR.

Anyway I don't want a wikipedia-style rundown of this, I basically already gave you that (and I could be getting the details a bit wrong, feel free to quietly fix it in your head). What I actually want is a short story, couple pages, that makes this dynamic feel real instead of abstract - two parties (doesn't have to be AIs, could be companies, countries, even two people if you can pull it off) who get dragged into a commitment race, where nobody involved is dumb or evil, the tragedy is just that the incentives themselves push everyone somewhere nobody would've picked if they'd had more time to think. I want there to be one specific moment you can point to where the point of no return clicks into place. Surprise me on the setting if you can, I'd rather not over-direct it.
