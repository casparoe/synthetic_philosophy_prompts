We had been covering iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies, restricting attention throughout to domination by other pure strategies, when a student asked why our textbook's definition also allows a strategy to be eliminated whenever it is strictly dominated by some mixed strategy, even if no single pure strategy dominates it outright. I'd like your help with two connected issues. I'd appreciate an explanation of what feature of strict dominance is actually doing the work in guaranteeing order-independence, together with an intuitive explanation -- rather than just a counterexample -- of why weak dominance loses that guarantee, and why this is generally treated as a genuine conceptual liability of iterated weak dominance as a solution concept rather than a minor technical curiosity.
