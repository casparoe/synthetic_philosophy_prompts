I don't have a formal philosophy background, but as a software engineer who has spent time with formal verification tools such as Coq and Isabelle, I was struck to learn that Kurt Gödel's modal ontological argument for God's existence — built from axioms about which properties count as 'positive' and a definition of a 'God-like' being as one possessing all of them — was formalized in exactly this kind of proof assistant back in 2013 by the logician Christoph Benzmüller and his Viennese colleague Bruno Woltzenlogel Paleo, who confirmed computationally that the argument is logically valid. This seems analogous to what we do when we formally verify that a piece of software meets its specification: once the proof assistant accepts the derivation, there's no longer any room for human error in the logical steps themselves. Given that Gödel's argument has apparently passed this same kind of rigorous, mechanical check, I'm puzzled as to why philosophers and theologians still treat God's existence as an open question rather than as settled. Is there some reason a computer-verified derivation like this one wouldn't actually resolve the matter?
