So I watched that Derren Brown show "The Push" the other night (the one where he manipulates some random guy into pushing an actor off a building at a fake charity gala) and it completely messed with my head — three out of four people who went through his setup actually did it. That sent me down a rabbit hole reading about the Milgram shock experiments, and then this old study where they had seminary students, guys training for the clergy, some of them literally on their way to give a talk on the Good Samaritan parable, walk across campus, and the only thing that predicted whether they'd stop to help a guy slumped in a doorway groaning was whether they'd been told they were running late. Whether they were religious or seemed like a genuinely kind person barely mattered.

That led me to the term "situationism," and apparently theres a whole line of argument that stuff like this is a real problem for Aristotle-style virtue ethics. Virtues are supposed to be stable traits a person has — honesty, compassion, courage — that show up reliably across different situations. But if something as dumb as being in a rush (or, in another study I read about, literally just finding a dime in a phone booth) predicts behavior way better than anything about a person's supposed character, then maybe "character" in that strong sense is kind of a fiction we project onto people after the fact.

I've been going back and forth on this by myself for a while now and would rather see it argued out properly by people who'd actually be good at it. Could you write me a dialogue between a virtue ethicist and a situationist hashing this out? I don't want either one to be a pushover — the situationist should be bringing their best studies and reasoning, and the virtue ethicist needs actual smart comebacks, not just "well, most people aren't virtuous anyway" as a dodge. Most importantly I want them actually responding to each others specific points as it goes, not just each delivering a prepared speech past the other. Doesn't need to end with a winner, I just want to see both sides at their strongest.
