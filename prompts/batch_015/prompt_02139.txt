Was thinking about pramana theory for a seminar and wanted to run somethign by you. Nyaya only counts testimony (sabda) as a genuine pramana if it's aptopadesa, the statement of an apta - and Vatsyayana (the 4th-century Nyaya commentator) is explicit that being an apta means actually having accurate knowledge of a matter and genuinely intending to pass it on truthfully, not just uttering words that happen to be true. That's part of why the tradition denies that a parrot reciting a correct sentence gives you testimonial knowledge - Nyaya calls that sabdabhasa, 'apparent' testimony, since there's no real speaker-intention (tatparya) behind it, just noise that happens to pattern-match a true claim. So, channeling Vatsyayana: would he count a large language model's output as sabda pramana, as mere sabdabhasa, or does it break his categories somehow? Actually work through how the apta and tatparya conditions would apply rather than just waving it off with 'it's not conscious so no.'
