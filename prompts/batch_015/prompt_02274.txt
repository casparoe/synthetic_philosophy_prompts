So I'm outlining a near-future novel where the central tension is whether a small, underfunded AI lab should actually pull the trigger on a system they've spent a decade building to act on humanity's "coherent extrapolated volition" — basically the old Yudkowsky pitch that instead of hand-coding values into the machine, you get it to model what we'd all want on reflection (smarter, better informed, more coherent versions of ourselves) and act on wherever that settles. My two POV characters are Odalys, the lead researcher who's basically bet her whole career on this being the only non-arbitrary way to point a superintelligence, and her old grad-school mentor Teodor, now a working bioethicist, who thinks CEV is a philosophical con — dressed-up neutrality that actually smuggles in a ton of contestable assumptions about persons and preferences.

There's a scene I keep chickening out of writing: the two of them alone in her lab late at night, the night before they flip the switch, really hashing it out. I don't want it to be the kind of scene where one of them is obviously the strawman and the other just wins on points — I want Teodor's objections to be the real, hard ones (not just "what if it turns us into paperclips"), and I want Odalys to have genuine comebacks rather then just repeating the pitch louder. Specifically I'd love for the exchange to get into stuff like: whether the "you" that's been extrapolated through a thousand iterations of smarter-and-more-coherent is even still enough the same person to have standing to answer for you, how your supposed to aggregate the extrapolated wishes of eight billion people who might diverge rather than converge, and whether locking in even a "coherent" answer is itself a kind of tyranny over everyone who comes after. Can you write me that dialogue — a good back and forth, maybe 15-20 exchanges, where they're actually listening and responding to each other rather than just trading monologues? I don't need it resolved, I just need it to feel like two people who've each spent years thinking about this and respect each other enough to actually engage.
