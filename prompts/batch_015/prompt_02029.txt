So the case our mock trial team uses this whole season is a wrongful death/negligence case about an apartment fire - the theory on the side we argued that day is that the property manager was negligent because the smoke detector in the victim's apartment had a dead battery for something like two months, even though she'd emailed him twice about it, and when a fire broke out in the building that night her detector never went off, so she (the case calls her Ms. Delgado, its all fictional obviously) didn't wake up in time and died of smoke inhalation. We argued that him not replacing the battery caused her death and ended up winning that round, but afterward me and two teammates, Derek and Maya, got into a totally separate argument about whether that's actually true in a real philosophical sense and not just "true enough to win a ballot," and we never settled it.

(1) Derek said it's obvious the property manager's failure to replace the battery caused her death - if he'd swapped it after her first email, the alarm would've gone off and she'd have woken up in time to get out. (2) Maya said that logic proves way too much: by the same reasoning, her coworker who'd promised to stop by that week but forgot also "caused" the death, so did the city inspector who skipped the building's last inspection, and so did Maya herself, for not somehow finding out about the dead battery and driving over to fix it - there's basically an unlimited list of people who could've prevented it and didn't, and calling all of them causes is absurd. (3) Derek said the difference is that the property manager specifically had a job to maintain the detectors and got two direct complaints about that exact one, so his not fixing it counts as a real cause while randoms with no assigned responsibility don't. (4) Maya said that just makes whether something's a "cause" depend on job descriptions and paperwork instead of on what actually physically happened - picture the exact same building, same dead battery, same fire, but in a place with zero maintenance laws and nobody officially "in charge" of detectors: the battery still doesn't go off and Ms. Delgado still dies the exact same way, so it seems weird that his not fixing it would stop being a cause of her death just because no one had assigned him that job on paper. (5) Derek shot back that if job/duty stuff is totally irrelevant, then Maya's own point from (2) means literally every person on earth who didn't prevent the death is equally "a cause" of it, which makes the word cause useless - so either she needs some rule for narrowing it down too (which is all he was doing), or she has to bite the bullet and say the property manager didn't actually cause the death at all and can only be "blamed" for it in some totally different, non-causal way, which seems like a way bigger concession than the one she was accusing him of making.

We just kept going in circles after that until practice ended lol.
