I spent most of my career as a neuroscientist, and in my early years I conducted intracranial self-stimulation studies in rats, working very much within the tradition established by Olds and Milner's classic 1954 experiments on electrical stimulation of the septal area. Since retiring, I have joined a small philosophy reading group, and our last few meetings have turned to the AI safety literature, where I repeatedly encounter the term "wireheading" invoked as a kind of AI analogue of the very phenomenon I once induced in the laboratory. The more I read, however, the less confident I become that I have the taxonomy straight: authors seem intent on keeping "wireheading" distinct from the broader category of "reward tampering," and distinct again from "specification gaming" (sometimes folded together with "reward hacking"), yet these terms are used loosely enough, even within the technical literature, that a newcomer struggles to tell whether they mark genuinely different failure modes or merely different emphases on a single one.

I would be grateful if you could lay out, as precisely as possible, how these notions differ and how they relate to one another. In particular, I am trying to determine whether wireheading is best understood as simply the most direct species of reward tampering, one that corrupts the very mechanism that registers reward, as opposed to something further upstream, such as the sensors feeding into that mechanism, or the process by which a human evaluator's judgments are translated into a training signal, or whether it should instead be treated as an altogether separate failure mode with its own causal structure. I would also like to understand what, if anything, distinguishes either of these from mere specification gaming, in which an agent exploits an unintended loophole in a reward function that is never itself touched or corrupted. Please feel free to draw on the vocabulary of feedback and control, sensors, setpoints, and the like, if doing so sharpens the picture, rather than assuming I already hold to any particular formalism; I suspect part of my present confusion is that I am unconsciously importing intuitions from the rat laboratory that may not translate cleanly to the artificial case.
