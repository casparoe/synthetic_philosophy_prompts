so i've been kind of obsessed with the ship of theseus thing for a while (the boat where you swap out every plank one by one, is it still the same ship), and a couple nights ago i went down a rabbit hole and found this way older version of basically the same puzzle. its from an ancient buddhist text called the Milinda Panha, a dialogue between a greek king named Milinda (i think he's actually meant to be that Indo-Greek king Menander, not totally sure) and a monk named Nagasena, translated into english by some scholar back in like the 1890s, so the phrasing is pretty stiff and old fashioned. It starts with the king asking the monk's name and getting told "Nagasena" is just a label, there's no permanent self behind it, which annoys the king and he demands proof. Then Nagasena flips it around using a chariot, and this is the bit that's been stuck in my head since:

"Then if you came, Sire, in a carriage, explain to me what that is. Is it the pole that is the chariot?"
"I did not say that."
"Is it the axle that is the chariot?"
"Certainly not."
"Is it the wheels, or the framework, or the ropes, or the yoke, or the spokes of the wheels, or the goad, that are the chariot?"
And to all these he still answered no.
"Then is it all these parts of it that are the chariot?"
"No, Sir."
"But is there anything outside them that is the chariot?"
And still he answered no.
"Then thus, ask as I may, I can discover no chariot. Chariot is a mere empty sound... There is no such thing as a chariot!"

The king gets called out for basically lying about riding a chariot at all, and then comes back with what's supposed to be the winning answer:

"It is on account of its having all these things -- the pole, and the axle, the wheels, and the framework, the ropes, the yoke, the spokes, and the goad -- that it comes under the generally understood term, the designation in common use, of 'chariot.'"

and Nagasena goes "Very good! Your Majesty has rightly grasped the meaning of 'chariot,'" then says a person is the exact same deal, just a convenient label for a bunch of parts, nothing extra behind it.

Here's what's bugging me though, and my brother just said "its a chariot, who cares" when i tried to explain it lol, so i need someone to actually engage with it: earlier in the questioning, when Nagasena asks straight up "is it all these parts of it that are the chariot," the king says NO. But his supposedly correct answer at the end sounds like basically the same claim, that it's the parts, all of them together, and that time it counts as getting it right. So did the king actually change his position, or is "all these parts" different from "on account of its having all these things" in some real way i'm missing, and is that difference secretly the whole point of the passage? And separate from that, is Nagasena saying chariots and people don't exist AT ALL, or just that the word "chariot" doesn't refer to some extra single thing on top of the parts? Those feel like pretty different claims to me and the passage kind of runs them together. Would appreciate a real breakdown of the logic here, not just "it's a metaphor for no-self," i already get that much.
