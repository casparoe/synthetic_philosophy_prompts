I've spent the last few weeks co-teaching a seminar on nineteenth-century aesthetics, and this week's session on Book Three of Schopenhauer's *The World as Will and Representation* left several students, and frankly me, a bit stuck. Schopenhauer's central claim there is that music is metaphysically unique among the arts: architecture, sculpture, painting, and poetry all copy the Platonic Ideas at their various "grades" of the Will's objectification, but music bypasses the Ideas altogether and copies the Will itself directly. This is why, on his account, the slow-moving bass line corresponds to the Will's lowest, most inert grade of objectification in unorganized matter, while the melody's constant departure from and return to the key-note corresponds to the restless striving of self-conscious human volition. Crucially, he restricts all of this praise to what later came to be called "absolute" music, i.e., textless, non-programmatic instrumental music, and he is openly contemptuous of composers who instead make music imitate or depict things, citing passages from Haydn's *The Seasons* as the kind of tone-painting that degrades the art.

Here is the question I haven't been able to settle even after rereading the relevant sections closely: what would Schopenhauer say about a piece of non-programmatic instrumental music generated today by an AI system trained on centuries of human compositions, with no human composer having shaped a single note of the output? On one hand, his monism seems to commit him to a permissive answer, since he is explicit elsewhere that the Will objectifies itself through blind, non-cognitive causal processes in inorganic nature just as much as through conscious striving, which would seem to make an algorithm's output just one more phenomenon through which the Will manifests, no different in kind from a waterfall or a heartbeat. On the other hand, he is equally explicit that the composition of melody is "the work of genius" operating through unreflective inspiration rather than deliberate calculation, which suggests that the relevant kind of directness might require an actual subject undergoing something like will-less, inspired absorption, in which case a statistical model with no inner life could only ever produce something that mimics the formal signature of will-disclosing music without actually being an instance of it, no more than a music box or wind moving through a canyon is. Could you work through both horns of this dilemma using Schopenhauer's own conceptual resources, i.e., the universality of the Will, his account of genius and inspiration, the bass-melody hierarchy, and will-less contemplation, tell me which one you think he would, and should, have accepted on his own premises, and explain exactly where the losing horn goes wrong by his own lights?
