He's got this thought experiment where you imagine two whole worlds that literally no mind will ever experience, not one -- one is gorgeous, mountains, oceans, sunsets, the works, the other is just a giant heap of filth. No people, no aliens, no god watching either one. And Moore says straight up that the beautiful world existing is just a better state of affairs than the ugly one, purely because of the beauty, even though nothing will ever percieve it.

Seperately he's got this whole theory about what makes loving a friend valuable, and it basically comes down to correctly seeing the persons genuinely good qualities plus feeling the "fitting" emotion about them -- like its the exact same recipe as appreciating a painting, just aimed at a person instead of a canvas.

Both of these have been bugging me for weeks. the beauty one feels like cheating, since the second you picture the beautiful world to compare it against the ugly one, there IS a mind experiencing it -- yours. and the friendship one seems to leave out basically everything that makes actual friendship feel like friendship to me, like loyalty, history, sticking with someone even when they're not exactly showing off their good qualities that day.

can you write me an essay that actually wrestles with whether Moore is right on either of these two claims? give the strongest case for him on each, the strongest case against, and then actually tell me where you land and why, not just a wishy-washy "both sides have a point."
