I've been a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, where thought experiments about teleportation and mind uploading are something of a staple -- typically on the assumption that a perfect physical duplicate of a person is at least a serious candidate for being "the same person" in the sense that matters for survival. It strikes me that this quietly presupposes that qualitative indistinguishability can ground numerical identity, which is precisely what's at stake in the identity of indiscernibles debate, most famously via Max Black's case of two qualitatively identical iron spheres in an otherwise empty, symmetric universe. Please describe the most important implications of that debate -- including the well-known "weak discernibility" response to Black, which individuates objects via the relations they bear to each other rather than their intrinsic properties -- for whether an uploaded or teleported duplicate should count as genuinely the same individual as the original, rather than merely an indiscernible but numerically distinct one.
