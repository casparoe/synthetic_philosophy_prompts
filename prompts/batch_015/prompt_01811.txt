Having spent my career in the natural sciences, I've recently begun exploring the philosophy of science in more depth. I was surprised to find that Karl Popper, the very architect of the falsifiability criterion, concluded in Unended Quest that natural selection is "almost tautological" and therefore not a testable theory but merely a "metaphysical research programme." Given that this verdict comes from the philosopher who taught us how to tell real science apart from its pretenders, shouldn't evolutionary biologists be more forthright that their discipline, whatever its practical successes, doesn't actually meet the bar of a properly falsifiable science?
