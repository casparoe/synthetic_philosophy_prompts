ok so my friend is doing a teaching masters and had to read Locke's *Some Thoughts Concerning Education* for a class, she showed me this bit on rewards and punishment (its sections 55-56) and it somehow turned into a whole argument that lasted way longer than it should have. here's the passage:

"Rewards, I grant, and Punishments must be proposed to Children, if we intend to work upon them. The Mistake I imagine is, that those that are generally made use of, are ill chosen. The Pains and Pleasures of the Body are, I think, of ill Consequence, when made the Rewards and Punishments whereby Men would prevail on their Children; for, as I said before, they serve but to increase and strengthen those Inclinations, which 'tis our Business to subdue and master. What Principle of Virtue do you lay in a Child, if you will redeem his Desires of one Pleasure, by the Proposal of another? This is but to enlarge his Appetite, and instruct it to wander. If a Child cries for an unwholsome and dangerous Fruit, you purchase his Quiet by giving him a less hurtful Sweet-meat. This perhaps may preserve his Health, but spoils his Mind... The Rewards and Punishments then, whereby we should keep Children in Order, are quite of another Kind... Esteem and Disgrace are, of all others, the most powerful Incentives to the Mind, when once it is brought to relish them. If you can once get into Children a Love of Credit, and an Apprehension of Shame and Disgrace, you have put into 'em the true Principle, which will constantly work and incline them to the right."

my read: Locke spends all this effort ripping into bribing kids with sweets because it just "changes the object" but still feeds the appetite - you haven't taught the kid anything, just redirected what pleasure they're chasing. fine, i buy that. but then his actual fix is: get the kid hooked on a different pleasure (being well thought of) and a different pain (shame) instead. i don't get why that's not the exact same move he just spent a paragraph condemning, with nicer branding.

she said the two aren't comparable because esteem and shame aren't bodily/sensual, they require the kid to reflect on how they look to other people, so its building a totally different faculty than a sweet tooth does. i said sure, but he never actually argues that THAT distinction is what matters for his argument (which was about redirecting appetite instead of curing it) - so it just sounds like "reward, except the ones i approve of."
