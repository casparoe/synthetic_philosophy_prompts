While preparing a paper on the political dimensions of logical empiricism, I've been rereading the Vienna Circle's 1929 manifesto, where Carnap and his co-authors explicitly tie the "scientific world-conception" to a program of reorganizing economic and social life along rational, scientifically grounded lines. This looks uncomfortably close to the kind of holistic, blueprint-driven planning that Karl Popper — who emerged from the same Viennese milieu and aimed much of his early work at the Circle — later attacked in favor of what he called "piecemeal social engineering." Could you compare Carnap's vision of a scientifically guided, planned society with Popper's alternative? In particular, does Popper's critique of utopian, holistic engineering actually succeed against the position Carnap and his co-signatories held, or does it target a conception of "scientific" planning that their own, more modest program doesn't fit? Justify your answer with reference to the specific commitments on each side.
