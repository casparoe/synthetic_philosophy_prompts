Having spent my career as a physical chemist, I've taken up philosophy of mind in retirement, and one celebrated argument now strikes me as resting on an elementary confusion. Kripke settled decades ago that conceivability does not entail possibility: we can conceive of water failing to be H2O, yet because "water" and "H2O" rigidly designate the same substance, that identity is necessary wherever it holds. Doesn't the same point sink the zombie conceivability argument against physicalism, since imagining a physical duplicate of myself with no inner experience looks like exactly the same sort of illusory conceiving? Why do philosophers still treat zombies as a live threat to physicalism instead of retiring the argument as a textbook instance of the very mistake Kripke diagnosed?
