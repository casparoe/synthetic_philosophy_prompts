I am working on an article about the more technical objections to Integrated Information Theory that go beyond the familiar "IIT is pseudoscience" headlines. The one I find most interesting is the "unfolding argument": any recurrent network can, in principle, be "unfolded" into a feedforward network that reproduces its exact input-output behavior, including identical verbal reports, yet IIT assigns the recurrent system a nonzero value of its integration measure, Φ (phi), while its feedforward twin gets Φ = 0. Critics conclude that the theory is committed to undetectable zombie twins, and so is either false or unfalsifiable.

Please write a dialogue between a defender of IIT and a computational functionalist, staged around one concrete case: a person whose recurrent cortex is hypothetically swapped for its "unfolded," purely feedforward functional equivalent — engineered to produce exactly the behavior and self-reports she would otherwise have given, including her reports of what her experience is like. The functionalist should press the zombie and unfalsifiability worry hard, including the awkward fact that IIT's own evidence base is built from reports that, on this very scenario, can come apart entirely from experience. The IIT defender should not simply retreat to intuition; give this character a substantive countermove — for instance, questioning whether behavioral equivalence was ever the right standard by which to judge a non-functionalist theory, invoking independent evidence for integration beyond self-report, or biting the bullet on the zombie twin while explaining why that is less costly than it sounds. Each side should respond to the other's actual point rather than restate their own position, and the exchange should end without a tidy resolution.
