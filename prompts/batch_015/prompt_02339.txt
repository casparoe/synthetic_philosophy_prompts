ok so I had this whole argument with my friend last night about George Eliot, of all people (we'd just watched a documentary on Victorian novelists, don't ask). Her take: since Eliot lost her faith, translated Feuerbach's Essence of Christianity, and basically spent her life building her own non-supernatural "religion of humanity," she'd be thrilled by something like Alain de Botton's Religion for Atheists and the godless-congregation churches it inspired — finally, someone actually built the thing she wanted. I said thats backwards. The whole arc of Silas Marner is that his faith gets rebuilt through Raveloe's messy, unplanned, inherited village life, not by anyone designing a replacement liturgy, and Eliot herself cooled on Comte pretty fast once his positivism turned into an actual church with priests and a catechism, in real time while she was alive to watch it happen. There's also that bit where she tells Myers that God is inconceivable and immortality unbelievable but duty is "peremptory and absolute" — my friend says that's a woman clearly looking for a designed substitute for religion, I say its basically the opposite. We argued for like an hour and got nowhere. Who's actually got the better read on her, and is there a version of this where we're both a little right and a little wrong?
