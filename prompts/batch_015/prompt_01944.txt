Write a short story built around program equilibrium: the game-theoretic idea that when players can inspect each other's actual decision-making code before acting, rather than merely choosing moves, cooperation can become a stable equilibrium even in one-shot games, such as the Prisoner's Dilemma, where it otherwise could not be sustained. The story's resolution should turn on a genuine logical mechanism that makes such cooperation possible — for instance, a Löbian proof of mutual cooperation, or a rule that cooperates only with provably identical code — rather than on the characters simply deciding to trust one another. The story should also gesture, if only briefly, at some way such a scheme could be gamed or could fail.
