Been on a bit of a social ontology kick lately -- my roommate's writing her thesis on Haslanger and kept going on about her ameliorative definitions of race and gender, so i finally sat down and reread the relevant stuff, then pulled my old copy of Searle's Construction of Social Reality off the shelf too since I remembered him using money as his go-to example of something "socially constructed." Now I can't stop noticing how different the two mechanisms of construction actually seem to be. Searle's story is basically that a bunch of people collectively intend/recognize that X counts as Y in some context (a piece of paper counts as currency, a signature counts as a contract, etc), and that collective recognition, backed by constitutive rules and "deontic powers," is what makes the institutional fact real. Haslanger's story, at least for gender and race, is about occupying a position in a hierarchy of privilege/subordination tied to how your body gets read by others -- there's no real moment of people getting together and agreeing to confer that status, and plenty of the people being subordinated obviously never consented to any of it.

So is "social construction" even one phenomenon here, or are we just using the same word for two pretty different things -- collective agreement/status-conferral on one hand, structural domination on the other? Do you think Searle's apparatus (status functions, collective intentionality, all that) could in principle be stretched to actually explain gender and race, or does his framework quietly assume a cooperative, mutually-recognized backdrop that just isn't there in cases of oppression? I have this nagging memory of some social ontologist trying to build one unified theory that covers both money-type cases and race/gender-type cases, but I don't remember who it was or whether it actually worked, so don't worry about chasing that down if it doesn't ring a bell. Anyway, if you had to say one of Searle's or Haslanger's frameworks does the more "fundamental" or general work in explaining what social construction really is, which would you pick and why?
