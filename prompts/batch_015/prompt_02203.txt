So this year I'm in this small philosophy elective at my school (like maybe 12 people signed up, very niche) and we just did a unit on Nyaya epistemology that I got kind of obsessed with, specifically the pramana stuff. The one that stuck with me most is sabda (testimony) — Gautama's actual definition in the Nyaya Sutras (I wrote down "aptopadesah sabdah," 1.1.7) basically says testimony only counts as real knowledge if it comes from an apta: someone who has direct, correct knowledge of the thing AND genuinely intends to tell you the truth about it, not deceive you. My teacher also brought up how this caused beef with the Mimamsa school over the Vedas — Nyaya says the Vedas count as valid sabda because God is the ultimate apta, while Mimamsa says the Vedas aren't authored by anyone at all (apaurusheya) and that's WHY they're valid, which felt like such a clever way to dodge the "ok but is God trustworthy" question entirely.

Anyway, me and my friend got into a whole argument about this at lunch that has nothing to do with the actual homework, lol. We want to know: what would a classical Naiyayika (like the guy who wrote the Tattvacintamani and basically systemized all of this, I think his name was Gangesha?) say about whether stuff generated by an LLM like ChatGPT or Claude counts as sabda pramana? My friend thinks it obviously fails since a language model has no direct knowledge of anything and doesn't "intend" honesty the way a person does, it's just predicting likely next words. But then I remembered Nyaya has this other category, aitihya, for tradition/testimony where the original source isn't definitely known, which they just fold into sabda instead of making it it's own pramana — and isn't that kind of exactly what an LLM does, blend together a massive pile of untraceable human sources into one statement? So: would a Naiyayika call LLM output a weaker/indirect form of sabda through something like aitihya, would they say it fails as a pramana entirely because there's no actual identifiable apta behind it, or would they honestly place it under a totally different pramana like anumana (since the "reasoning" is really just inference over patterns, kind of like how vyapti gets established from repeated observation)? Please actually reason through their specific criteria for apta-hood instead of just re-explaining what the four pramanas are in general — I already know that part.
