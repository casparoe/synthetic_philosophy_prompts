I'm putting together a short story collection about minds that might not be real minds, and I fell down a rabbit hole that I think is perfect material. Theres a view in philosophy of mind called teleosemantics (Millikan's version, mainly) that says a mental state only actually represents something -- only has real content -- because states of that type were selected for, over an organism's evolutionary or learning history, for reliably tracking that thing. No selection history, no content, full stop, even if the state is functionally doing everything a "real" representation would do.

Then there's Davidson's old Swampman case: lightning kills Davidson in a swamp and, by pure freak accident, reassembles a nearby dead tree into a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of him, memories intact, who walks out and lives the rest of Davidson's life exactly as he would have. Teleosemantics seems committed to saying this duplicate's mental states represent nothing at all, since he's got zero selection history -- even though he's physically identical to a person and totally indistinguishable in behavior, even to himself.

Could you write me a short story, maybe 1000-1500 words, that actually lives inside that problem instead of explaining it? No character lecturing anyone on teleosemantics -- I just want a situation where "identical behavior, zero content" has real teeth for someone. Feel free to change the setup (doesn't need lightning or a swamp) as long as that core bite survives, and please don't resolve it neatly at the end -- I'd rather the question just sit there.
