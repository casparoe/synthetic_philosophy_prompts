I'm a novelist writing a nonfiction book about a 1980s wrongful conviction. Telling the story fully and accurately would likely get the case reopened, with real but diffuse benefits for how similar cases are handled. It would also, unavoidably, expose facts that will devastate the actual perpetrator's grown daughter — an identifiable, entirely innocent woman who has spent decades building a quiet life as a schoolteacher and has never once mentioned her father's name. I don't wish to harm her; her exposure is simply inseparable from telling the truth. A colleague who read the manuscript says that distinction is beside the point: if I can foresee the damage, I'm as responsible for it as if I'd aimed for it, and a vague future good can't license a certain harm now. I suspect she's missing something but can't say what, or how one is even supposed to weigh a certain harm against a diffuse one. Whose position holds up, and is there a principled way through this?
