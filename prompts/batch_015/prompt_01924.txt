So I'm a retired chemist (40 years in industrial R&D) and I've properly fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole since retiring. One thing's been bugging me: everyone treats "why is there something rather than nothing" like it's this huge unanswered mystery, but didn't physics basically settle it decades ago? Tryon showed back in '73 that a closed universe's total energy nets out to zero, so the whole thing could pop into existence as a vacuum fluctuation without breaking conservation of energy, and Vilenkin's tunnelling-from-nothing model takes it further, with the universe literally quantum-tunnelling out of nothing into an expanding de Sitter space. Krauss made basically the same case to a popular audience a few years back. So why do philosophers (and theologians) keep acting like this question is still wide open, like their stuck decades behind the physics? Am I missing something, or is "something from nothing" basically a solved problem at this point?
