ok so this is something that's been bugging me for a while and I can't fully resolve it. theres Christensen's classic drug case: you solve a logic problem, feel completely sure of your answer, then get told you'd been given a drug that wrecks most peoples' logical reasoning. intuitively you should lower your confidence even though introspectively your reasoning still seems totally fine -- Christensen's answer is that you basically have to "bracket" your first-order reasons and defer to the higher-order evidence. ok sure, but then Weatherson's level-splitting response says its fine to just keep believing P at the object level while granting "my evidence doesn't support P" -- i.e. rejecting Horowitz's "non-akrasia constraint" wholesale. what i can't figure out is what's actually driving that disagreement -- is it just a brute clash of intuitions about cases, or is there some actual principled reason to pick a side? and relatedly, doesn't the bracketing view kind of prove too much, in the same way Scott's old "epistemic learned helplessness" post does -- like, if any story about my faculties being compromised is enough to make me discount my own reasoning, and I can't even fully trust the discounting itself (basically Elga's bootstrapping worry), is there an actual principled stopping point anywhere in this literature, or does everyone just shrug at some point and move on?
