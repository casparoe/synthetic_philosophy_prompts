Gottlob Frege's Begriffsschrift is where a lot of us trace the origins of modern quantificational logic and the idea of a fully formal, checkable proof — the lineage behind why fields like type theory and automated theorem proving can talk rigorously about validity at all. I recently learned that Frege also kept a private political diary in the last year of his life, not published until decades after he died, and it's genuinely ugly: he argues for stripping Jews of political rights and expelling them from Germany, and has nothing but contempt for parliamentary democracy. What gets me is that Frege's whole pitch was that logical laws are objective and untouched by the psychology of whoever happens to be doing the reasoning — yet here he is reasoning his way into something monstrous. Doesn't that mean we should treat his logical work with some real skepticism, and stop holding him up as some kind of paragon of pure rationality?
