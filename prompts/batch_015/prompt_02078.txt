A friend and I had a long argument last night about Kant's views on animals. She granted that Kant discusses cruelty to animals in the Metaphysics of Morals, warning that it dulls our sympathy and could make us crueler toward other people, but she argued that this is really just prudential advice dressed up as ethics rather than a genuine moral duty, since for Kant an obligation can only be grounded in respect for rational beings as ends in themselves. She concluded that Kant's system therefore cannot ground any strict duty regarding animals, so someone committed to a broadly Kantian framework has no principled objection to a practice like factory farming, only a vague hope that it might coarsen the people who take part in it. I couldn't come up with a good reply at the time, but it's been nagging at me since -- given that gap, how should someone who finds Kantian ethics compelling but is troubled by factory farming go about closing it?
