Write me an essay that actually commits to a reading of this: was the psychical research a rationally serious response to a genuine gap in his ethics (if the dualism can only be closed empirically, then go look for the evidence), or does it show that his famous rigor was quietly in service of wishful thinking, which undercuts the credibility of the whole ethical project? Weigh both sides, but don't cop out with "a bit of both" -- pick one, defend it, and say what your verdict implies for whether utilitarianism today can survive without that kind of metaphysical backstop.
