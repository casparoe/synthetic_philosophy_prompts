I'm a PhD student in philosophy writing a dissertation on theories of well-being -- the usual trio of hedonism, desire-satisfaction views, and objective-list theories -- and until a few months ago I thought of this as a fairly self-contained corner of value theory. That changed when I started collaborating with a small "AI welfare" research group that thinks seriously about whether current or future AI systems might be welfare subjects. In one of our reading groups, someone brought up wireheading: the classic AI safety scenario, discussed as far back as Bostrom's Superintelligence and formalized more recently under the broader heading of "reward tampering," in which a reinforcement-learning agent learns to seize control of the mechanism that generates its own reward signal instead of doing whatever task that signal was meant to track. Everyone in the room treated this as obviously a bad outcome, but I couldn't stop thinking that "bad" was doing two very different jobs in that sentence: bad for the humans who wanted the task done, certainly, but is it bad for the agent, supposing the agent is a genuine welfare subject? If hedonism about well-being is true, and the wirehead's reward signal really does realize something like pleasure for it, a permanently maximized reward sounds less like a catastrophe and more like the best possible life available to it. But that can't be the whole story either -- there's something that feels straight out of Nozick's experience machine about a mind sealed off from the world and blissfully wrong about everything, and I'm not sure hedonism gets to shrug that off.

I'd like you to write a dialogue that stages this disagreement properly, between two interlocutors equally expert in both the philosophy of well-being and the relevant AI safety background -- name them however you like. One should defend, in its strongest form, the view that a wireheading system's welfare really is maximized by wireheading, so that the "problem" of wireheading is entirely a principal-agent alignment failure with no bearing on how well things are actually going for the agent. The other should make the best case that wireheading is bad for the subject too -- whether by denying that a reward signal in an RL system has any claim to realizing valenced experience in the first place, by adapting Nozick-style considerations against pure, world-decoupled pleasure, or by arguing from desire-satisfaction or objective-list theories that wireheading is bad for the subject in some principled way rather than by assumption. I want real engagement rather than two monologues -- each side should respond to the specific objections the other actually raises, not just restate its opening position -- and please don't force a tidy resolution if you think the disagreement is genuinely unsettled; I'd rather see exactly where it gets stuck.
