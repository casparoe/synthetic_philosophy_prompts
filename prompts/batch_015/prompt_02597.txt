For a personal project, I designed a new cache-eviction algorithm from a set of first-principles assumptions about access patterns, without consulting any existing performance data while working out the design. When I finally implemented and benchmarked it, it reproduced, to within rounding error, a specific cache hit-rate figure that several independent research groups have measured and published over the past decade — a connection I hadn't anticipated or even known was possible until I ran the numbers myself. A colleague argues this can't really count as evidence that my algorithm is sound, since that hit-rate figure was already firmly established long before I wrote a line of code, so nothing new has actually been learned. That doesn't sit right with me, though I can't quite explain why. Is my colleague right, and if not, how should I think about what this result actually tells me about my algorithm?
