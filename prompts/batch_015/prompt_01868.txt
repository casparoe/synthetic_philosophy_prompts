Ok this has been bugging me for weeks and my labmates are useless on it so throwing it out here. I'm a 4th year PhD student working on cellular senescence (basically why cells stop dividing properly and start pumping out junk as we get older), and theres this recurring argument in my program about whether "aging" itself should officially get called a disease. My PI wants us to frame it that way in grants and in paper discussion sections, since NIH reviewers respond way better to it, and theres a whole push in the field (they call it "geroscience") to get the FDA to recognize aging as an actual indication, so drugs like rapamycin could get properly approved for it instead of just being prescribed off-label at longevity clinics forever.

But at my quals, one of my committee members (an actual practicing MD, not just a basic scientist like the rest of us) tore into me for using that framing, and said something like "you cannot call something a disease when literally every single person who lives long enough gets it — that's not a disorder, that IS the definition of normal for a human being." My PI's counter is that plenty of near-universal things are still obviously diseases: most people get some degree of atherosclerosis and high blood pressure by their 70s and we still treat those as pathological, and basically everyone with ovaries who lives long enough goes through menopause and we medicate that too. So "practically everybody eventually gets it" clearly isn't sufficient on its own to disqualify something from disease status, otherwise none of those would count either.

I don't have any real philosophy background so I honestly can't tell if my committee member has a substantive point or is just being a pedantic gatekeeper, and its not purely an academic question for me since it effects how I frame my whole third chapter. Is there an actual principled way to decide whether something universal like aging counts as a disease, or is it really just a matter of what we've collectively decided to call things, with both sides just talking past one another? Would love an actual answer here, not a "well it depends what you mean by disease" non-answer, lol.
