Hey, I've been down a bit of a Wittgenstein rabbit hole the last couple weeks (blame a HN thread about Moore's "here is a hand" argument) and actually read On Certainty instead of just skimming a summary. Got kind of stuck on the "hinge propositions" thing and wrote up some notes trying to connect it to stuff from my day job (I do security/infra work). Would love you're take before I turn this into a blog post - mainly, am I actually tracking what Wittgenstein's doing here or just forcing a pattern-match that falls apart under pressure, and is this comparison a total cliché that's already been made a thousand times.

Notes so far:

W's basic idea is that stuff like "the earth has existed for a long time" or "here are two hands" isn't really believed on the basis of evidence - it's more like the frame that makes believing/doubting possible in the first place. Doubts "depend on the fact that some propositions are exempt from doubt, are as it were like hinges on which those turn" (341), and if you tried to doubt literally everything "you would not get as far as doubting anything" (114-115) - doubting is a move inside a game that presupposes plenty of stuff isn't up for grabs.

This mapped really cleanly onto "trusted computing base" from security work for me. Your TCB is whatever hardware/kernel/hypervisor layer has to be correct for any security property to hold at all, and it's specifically the layer you can't verify from inside the system itself - you just trust it, or push the boundary somewhere else (verify the OS from a hypervisor, the hypervisor from a TPM...), and it bottoms out somewhere that's accepted rather than proven. Felt like a good match for the "justification runs out and what's left is just acting, not more evidence" vibe of On Certainty (204-205).

Except then I hit 343, where he's pretty explicit that the hinge thing isnt just "we can't check everything so we assume." He directly rules out the idea that "we just can't investigate everything, and for that reason we are forced to rest content with assumption." But that IS basically my whole justification for treating something as a TCB - we could formally verify further down the stack (people do, e.g. seL4), we just don't, because it's expensive. Resource tradeoff, not a logical wall. And TCBs shrink over time as verification gets cheaper/better (there's a whole "TCB minimization" thing in security), which I wanted to map onto "the river-bed of thoughts may shift" (96-99) - except that shift is supposed to just happen to a form of life over time, nobody's minimizing their hinges on purpose the way we minimize a TCB.

So, the actual question: am I quietly smuggling in a "resource-constrained foundationalism" reading of hinges that 343 seems to rule out? And if that's the wrong reading, what would a hinge that isn't just a pragmatic stopping point even look like, in engineering-analogy terms - is there one, or does the analogy just break here?
