I teach a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and we have just spent two weeks working through Bostrom's treatment of observation selection effects, moving from the Doomsday Argument to the Self-Sampling Assumption and the Self-Indication Assumption. My students can now state both principles correctly on an exam, but I don't think most of them feel the actual pull of either position, or appreciate just how much each side has to swallow in order to keep its commitments consistent. I would like a text I can hand out next week that dramatizes the disagreement properly, since the standard survey-article treatment tends to flatten it into a list of pros and cons rather than a live dispute between two people who each think the other is missing something obvious.

Could you write a dialogue, long enough to stand as a two-page handout, between two philosophers of roughly equal stature who run into each other after a conference talk on anthropic reasoning: one has built her career defending the Self-Sampling Assumption, the other his career defending the Self-Indication Assumption. I would like Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher case to be the immediate occasion for the argument, with the SSA-defender pressing her interlocutor on how he can accept that the sheer number of observers a cosmological theory posits should move our credence in that theory before any experiment is run; the SIA-defender should have a genuine answer to this, not just a shrug, and should turn the tables by pressing the SSA-defender on the Doomsday Argument and on the notorious difficulty of specifying a non-arbitrary reference class. Partway through, have the two of them test their commitments against the Sleeping Beauty problem and try to work out, in real time, whether their answers there actually track the SSA/SIA divide or cut across it in a way that ought to worry at least one of them. Please do not let either side win easily or get the last undisputed word; I would rather the piece end with the disagreement sharpened and each party's costs laid bare than with anything resembling a resolution, since that is roughly the state of the actual literature.
