Ok so this isn't a philosophers' dispute, just something that happened among friends, but it's got me thinking about standing to blame and I can't shake it. Backstory: Marco has spent the past year telling our friend Deniz that cheating on a partner is a real betrayal and basically makes you a bad person -- Deniz has cheated on his girlfriend twice now and Marco's been pretty blunt about it with him. Last month it came out that Marco cheated on an ex back in college. He's never hidden it exactly, but never brought it up either, and he says he felt terrible at the time and told his ex what happened.

Two other friends of mine, Nadia and Theo, ended up going a few rounds about whether Marco still has standing to give Deniz grief over this:

(1) Nadia said it's just straightforwardly hypocritical -- you don't get to moralize about a sin you've committed yourself, so Marco should really shut up about it.
(2) Theo said the cases aren't parallel: Marco did it once, years ago, owned it and apologized at the time; Deniz is doing it now and shows no sign of stopping. Theo said that's exactly the difference between having a skeleton in your closet and actually claiming an exception for yourself -- Marco isn't doing the second thing anymore, wheras Deniz would be if he turned around and criticized someone else for it.
(3) Nadia said the apology is beside the point -- Marco still got the benefit of cheating without consequences back then, and realizing later it was wrong doesn't undo that. If anything, knowing better and doing it anyway is worse, not better.
(4) Theo said that's an argument about whether Marco is a good person overall, which isn't the same question as whether he has standing to make this particular complaint -- and owning your past wrongdoing is exactly what keeps it from being a live exception-claim.
(5) Nadia's comeback was that Marco never actually said any of this remorse stuff to Deniz's face -- he just lectured him like a guy with clean hands. So whatever's true about Marco's private history, it wasn't doing any work in the actual exchange, and the message Deniz got was still 'I'm better than you,' which is supposed to be what sinks your standing in the first place.

I teach the blame / moral responsibility material most years and my instinct is this is definately a better test case than the stock ones, but I genuinely go back and forth on who's winning at each step. Could you go through moves (1)-(5) and say who's got the better of it each time, plus flag anything you think both of them are missing? Not just an overall winner -- I want the blow-by-blow.
