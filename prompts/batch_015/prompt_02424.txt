I've spent a long time reading LessWrong and adjacent blogs, and one episode I still think about is the brief controversy of September 2011, when Edward Nelson announced a proof that Peano Arithmetic was inconsistent, then withdrew the claim a few weeks later after Terence Tao and Daniel Tausk independently found a fatal gap in the argument. What struck me most was that the episode pushed several commenters to commit to explicit numbers for a question most working mathematicians treat as not worth asking — figures like 99% to 99.9% confidence in the consistency of PA or ZF started appearing in the comment threads. The same underlying worry shows up in Löb's theorem and the "Löbian obstacle" that MIRI's work on tiling agents ran into: a sufficiently strong formal system can never certify its own consistency from the inside, so trust in any foundational theory ultimately has to rest on something other than a proof within that theory.
