A friend of mine and I spent most of a long train journey arguing about the butterfly dream passage that closes the "Qiwulun" chapter of the *Zhuangzi*: the famous bit where Zhuang Zhou dreams he is a butterfly, wakes up, and cannot say whether he is a man who dreamed he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is a man. My friend's position was that this passage is, in effect, a Daoist anticipation of Descartes: an early version of the argument from dreaming, meant to unsettle our confidence that waking experience is epistemically privileged over dreaming experience. I disagreed, arguing that the passage is not really doing epistemology at all -- that its point is not "how do you know you are not dreaming" but something closer to a dissolution of that very question, signaled by the closing phrase "the transformation of things" (wu hua), where what gets called into doubt is not merely which side of the dream/waking distinction one occupies, but the stability of the bounded self that the question presupposes in the first place. We went back and forth for the better part of an hour without either of us budging, and it struck me afterward that I don't actually know how to settle the dispute myself.

I'd like your help thinking it through properly. Weigh the strongest textual and philosophical considerations on each side, taking into account the role the passage plays within the rest of the "Qiwulun" chapter and within the *Zhuangzi*'s broader treatment of language, perspective, and fixed distinctions. Don't leave the matter unresolved, though: end with a clear verdict on which reading you find more defensible, and say what you think turns on the answer for how we should approach dream skepticism more generally.
