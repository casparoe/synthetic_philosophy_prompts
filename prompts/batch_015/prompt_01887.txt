What do you think Newton would make of the fate of the Johannine Comma?

So I've been down a rabbit hole on the theology side of Newton (way more manuscript pages went into that than into the Principia stuff, apparently), and I keep coming back to this one specific episode that I think is a really good test case for "predict the guy's reaction." He wrote this treatise, addressed to Locke, laying out — in real philological/manuscript detail, not just vibes — that 1 John 5:7, the "three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost" line, basically THE proof text for the Trinity in the whole NT, wasn't in the original Greek at all. His story was that it snuck in through the Latin tradition and only got into printed Greek NTs later (Erasmus supposedly only added it under pressure, after a conveniently-timed late manuscript turned up). Newton clearly thought that once you did the manuscript work honestly, this kind of corrupt doctrine would basically collapse under its own textual weight — like the corruption was mainly a documents problem.

Here's the part that gets me: modern textual criticism has pretty much fully vindicated him on this. It's called the Johannine Comma now, it's dropped or bracketed in basically every serious modern critical Greek text, and it's missing or footnoted as spurious in stuff like the NIV, ESV, NASB — only the KJV-only crowd really defends it anymore. And yet none of that made mainstream Trinitarian Christianity budge even slightly; churches using those Comma-free translations are just as Trinitarian as ever, they just lean on other verses and creeds instead.

So: given what Newton actually believed about scripture, corruption, and church history (not just "he was secretly religious"), what do you think he'd make of that specific outcome 300-ish years later? Does he take it as proof his method was right all along and conclude the corruption just runs deeper than he thought (into institutions/creeds rather than just a few forged verses)? Or does the result kind of undercut his whole premise, since it shows doctrine doesn't actually track textual evidence the way he assumed it would? Genuinely curious how you'd reconstruct his reasoning here rather than just guessing.
