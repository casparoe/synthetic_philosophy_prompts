So I went down a Wittgenstein rabbit hole recently and found these old lecture notes on religious belief, where he goes after a guy called Father O'Hara for trying to defend religion 'as a question of science' — basically treating it like a hypothesis you'd weigh evidence for. His point seemed to be that believing in stuff like the Last Judgement isnt even the same kind of mental state as an ordinary empirical belief, so it doesn't really make sense to hold it with 60% or 90% confidence like a normal hypothesis. That got me thinking about people online now (i think they call themselves 'rationalists') who'll happily give you their exact 'credence' that God exists, like 15%, and update it whenever someone throws a new argument at them — same as any other Bayesian question. What do you reckon Wittgenstein would've said to people like that? Would he actually have a point, or is that just a convenient way of putting religion beyond criticism?
