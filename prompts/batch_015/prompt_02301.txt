ok so i fell down a rabbit hole reading old lesswrong posts on decision theory and theres this thought experiment called "counterfactual mugging" thats been bugging me for days. setup: Omega (a perfect predictor, never wrong) flips a fair coin. If it lands tails, Omega shows up and asks you for $100. If it lands heads, Omega would have given you $10,000 -- but only if it had predicted that you'd hand over the $100 in the tails case. Now say the coin actually comes up tails and Omega's standing there with its hand out.

Everyone seems to agree that if you could've made a binding promise ahead of time to pay up, you should have. But you never precommitted to anything, this is just happening to you cold. Updateless decision theory (UDT) says pay anyway, basically act like you'd precommitted, since thats the policy that does best on average across both branches of the coin flip before you knew which one you'd land in. The more "normal" causal way of thinking says once the coin is tails, paying is just flushing $100 down the drain for nothing, since nothing you do now can reach back and make the heads-world real.

Can you write me a real essay on this, not a philosophy 101 fence-sit? Lay out the strongest version of both sides, then actually commit to whichever one you think is the rational thing to do and defend it.
