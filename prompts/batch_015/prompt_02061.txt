I'm writing about the philosophical stakes of mechanistic interpretability, and I want to pressure-test one line of argument. In 2024, researchers using sparse autoencoders found that some language models encode concepts like the day of the week or month of the year not as single directions but as circles in activation space; causal interventions confirmed that these circles are what the network actually relies on for calendar-style modular arithmetic. Interpretability researchers often treat findings like this as evidence that neural networks harbor genuine internal representations -- a candidate answer to what William Ramsey called the 'job description challenge' facing any representational posit. But Frances Egan's deflationary account of representation, which treats content-attribution as a pragmatically useful gloss rather than a further fact about a system, was built to withstand exactly this kind of evidence in ordinary computational neuroscience, and Rosa Cao has recently asked whether the same holds for deep learning. Please argue that Egan-style deflationism survives these interpretability findings intact, and that even causally verified, structurally rich features like circular day-of-week representations do not settle the realism debate in the realists' favor.
