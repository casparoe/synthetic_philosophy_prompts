I spend a lot of time reading LessWrong/ACX and similar blogs, and a while back I got really into the idea of setting up systems now to force my future self to behave later, for stuff I know I'm bad at sticking to. So in January I made a deal with my friend Alex to finally finish a novel draft I've been picking at for like three years: every Sunday I send him my word count, and if I miss two Sundays in a row he has standing permission to transfer $500 out of a savings account I set up just for this, straight to the Flat Earth Society (chosen specifically because the idea of them getting my money makes me want to scream into a pillow, which felt extremely motivating at the time). Before we started I made him promise, more than once, that whatever excuse I came up with later, he was not allowed to grant an exception, because I know myself well enough to know future-me will produce something very convincing in the moment. I remember feeling pretty proud of how airtight I'd made the whole thing.

Then last month my dad had a minor stroke and I spent three weeks basically living at the hospital instead of writing, obviously missing the word counts. I asked Alex to just pause the clock til things settled down, and he flatly refused, telling me "this is literally the exact scenario you told me to watch out for." So now $500 is about to go to actual flat earthers unless I hit a pretty brutal makeup target by Sunday. I brought it up in our friend group chat and its split right down the middle, half saying Alex is being a rigid jerk since a family medical emergency obviously isn't the same as being lazy, and half saying I specifically set things up so Alex, not me, gets to decide what counts as a real exception, so I don't get to relitigate that now just because I don't like how it's playing out this time. Honestly I keep flipping between both positions myself. Is there some principled way to tell a "legitimate exception" apart from "exactly the kind of excuse I was trying to protect myself against," or did I basically just build a machine that was guaranteed to feel unfair the moment it actually did its job?
