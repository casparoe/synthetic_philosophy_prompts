Hey, I'm a retired physicist who's gotten a bit obsessed with philosophy of time since retiring (started with the SEP entry on presentism vs eternalism and fell down a rabbit hole trying to find where the whole A-series/B-series split actually comes from). Turns out its all downstream of McTaggart, so I dug up the relevant bit from his 1927 book "The Nature of Existence" (the fuller restatement of the arguement from his famous 1908 paper). I'm stuck on the step where he tries to show the "A series" (past/present/future) is actually contradictory. Here's the passage:

"Past, present, and future are incompatible determinations. Every event must be one or the other, but no event can be more than one. If I say that any event is past, that implies that it is neither present nor future, and so with the others. But every event has them all. If M is past, it has been present and future. If it is future, it will be present and past. If it is present, it has been future and will be past. Thus all three characteristics belong to each event." (§329)

He then raises what looks like the obvious fix:

"It may seem that this can easily be explained... It is never true that M is present, past, and future. It is present, will be past, and has been future... The characteristics are only incompatible when they are simultaneous, and there is no contradiction to this in the fact that each term has all of them successively." (§330)

And apparently McTaggart doesn't think this fix works, but the excerpt I have doesn't say why. As a physics guy my instinct is that "successively" should be totally fine — a particle isn't being contradictory just because it's "here" at t1 and "there" at t2, you just index the property to a time. So why can't he just index "past/present/future" to a time the same way and call it a day? My best guess is that indexing requires saying things like "it IS present that it WILL BE past," which just reintroduces present/past/future one level up and starts the whole problem again — a vicious regress. Is that actually his objection? Could you walk me through the regress step by step, and tell me whether there's a way out of it that doesn't just relabel the problem?
