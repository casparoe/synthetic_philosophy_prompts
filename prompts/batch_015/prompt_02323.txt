Write a dialogue between two decision theorists about money pumps. One of them has straightforwardly cyclic preferences (A over B, B over C, C over A) and is mid-pump: a bookie keeps charging a small fee to swap their current item for the one they like better. The other theorist insists this just proves cyclic preferences are irrational, period. The one being pumped disagrees: if your foresighted enough to see the cycle coming, you can just refuse the trade that would bring you back to the start, dodge that last fee, and keep your cyclic preferences fully intact -- so the pump only punishes naivety, not intransitivity itself. This is basically a question about diachronic rationality (does rationality govern each choice on its own, or the whole plan?), and I want them to actually hash it out: does bailing out early genuinely work, or does "sophisticated"/"resolute" choice just smuggle a consistency requirement back in at the level of plans instead of individual trades? Both should be sharp and give as good as they get -- no strawmanning.
