So this is a bit of an odd one but it's been stuck in my head for weeks. My grandma passed in the spring and left me a small inheritance, and I decided i wanted to give some of it away instead of just parking it in savings. A coworker dragged me to her monthly "effective giving" meetup (coffee shop, maybe a dozen regulars) to help me figure out where to send it, and last time two of the regulars, Derek and Naomi, got into a really good argument about where the group's pooled fund should go this quarter -- a bednet charity with rock-solid cost-per-life-saved numbers, or a local job-training nonprofit everybody likes but can't really put a number on.

Derek opened by saying the bednet charity's precision is basically fake: nobody can trace what saving a specific life in a specific place does 50 or 100 years out, more people means more strain on local land and water and who knows what else down the line, so we should just default to the local option, where at least we're not pretending to know something we don't. Naomi said sure, but most of those knock-on effects are just as likely to break good as bad -- an extra kid is about as likely to grow up inventing a vaccine as straining resources -- so it's rational to treat that stuff as a wash and just go with what we can actually measure. Derek came back with: this case isn't like that, because more people in a specific high-fertility region interacting with scarce local resources isn't a coin flip, we have some real reason to think it skews a particular way, we just can't size it -- and apparently there's actual philosophy jargon for this ("cluelessness"), where an unpredictable effect that's genuinely a wash is fine to ignore but one that's directional-but-unmeasurable isn't. Naomi's comeback was that this cuts against the local option just as hard, since keeping money local instead of sending it overseas has its own directional, unmeasurable ripple effects that nobody's calculated either -- so if this objection disqualifies the bednet charity it disqualifies everything, and the committee could never fund anything, which can't be right. Derek's last line was that "we have to fund something" doesn't actually rescue the bednet number specifically: if the ranking really is undetermined, you should say so, not let the one number anyone bothered to calculate quietly win by default.

Nobody resolved it, the meetup just moved on to snacks, and I've been turning it over since. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's got the better of each specific step, rather than just declaring an overall winner? And if there's a more defensible take on this whole "cluelessness" thing that neither of them landed on, I'd really like to hear it -- right now I genuinely don't know which charity to tell my coworker to push for.
