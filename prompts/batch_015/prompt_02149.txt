So two friends of mine, Rosa and Kevin, are roomates, and they had a spat a while back that's been bugging me ever since -- I write about science and ideas for a living and I still can't sort out who's right. On their way out the door to grab a quick dinner, Kevin said 'I know I locked the door, I checked it,' and Rosa didn't think twice about it. Two weeks later they're leaving for a three week trip to Portugal, Kevin does his usual check and says 'don't worry, I know it's locked, same as always,' and Rosa goes 'no, go check again, I don't think you actually know that.' Kevin got annoyed and said his evidence hadn't changed one bit since the dinner thing -- if he knew it then, he knows it now. Rosa said that's not really true, because back then being wrong just meant coming home to an unlocked door, and now being wrong means getting robbed while they're out of the country for three weeks, and that's exactly why 'know' doesn't apply anymore. Kevin shot back that this makes whether you 'know' something depend on stuff that hasn't even happened yet, which seems backwards, since knowledge is supposed to track your evidence, not consequences. Rosa's comeback was that's just how the word works: we treat 'I know' as a green light for acting on something, and how solid your evidence needs to be before it counts as a green light obviously depends on what's riding on it. Kevin's final shot: okay, so was I lying at dinner, or was I telling the truth then, and it just stopped being true later without me learning anything new or forgetting anything? Neither one sits right with him.

They asked me to referee and I got nowhere. Can you go through it move by move and say who's actually got the better argument at each step, not just who wins overall, and flag anything you think they're both missing?
