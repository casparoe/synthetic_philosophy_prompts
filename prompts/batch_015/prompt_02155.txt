I'm a PhD student in computer science working on multi-agent systems and AI safety. I've become interested in the "commitment race" dynamic discussed in some corners of decision theory: because a credible, irrevocable commitment confers bargaining power in the Schelling sense of burning one's bridges before a rival can, agents who anticipate conflict have an incentive to lock in their negotiating stance as early as possible, before they've had time to reason the situation through, since whoever commits first can force the other to yield. The danger is that if two such agents rush to commit in ignorance of one another, they can lock themselves into mutually incompatible, catastrophic positions -- as if two drivers in a game of Chicken each threw their steering wheel out the window before either could see that the other had done the same. Rather than another exposition of the idea, I'd like to see it dramatized. Please write a short story, roughly 1000-1500 words, in which two parties (nations, firms, minds, or something stranger) are drawn into exactly this race, so that the pull between committing quickly for advantage and deliberating longer for wisdom drives the plot to its resolution.
