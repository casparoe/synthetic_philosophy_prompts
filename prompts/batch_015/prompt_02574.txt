Quick question that's been rattling around in my head since a faculty reading group last week - we were going through some of the classic stuff on who counts as "the people" for democratic purposes (Whelan's old Nomos piece that coined "the boundary problem," plus a bit of Goodin and Abizadeh), and one of the political theory postdocs made a claim I haven't been able to shake since. His argument was basically: since nobody can specify, without circularity, who should count as part of the demos prior to the very vote that would decide who counts as part of the demos, this means literally every actual democracy's claim to legitimacy is resting on nothing. The US, France, wherever - none of them can point to a principled, non-arbitrary reason why their demos is bounded the way it is rather than some other way, so "government by the consent of the governed" is really just a fiction dressed up in nice language.

Is "yep, all bounded democracies are strictly illegitmate at bottom" actually the mainstream upshot people draw from the boundary problem?
