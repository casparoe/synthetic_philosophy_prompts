My company is requiring my team to attend a two-day workshop in which a well-regarded outside consultant will make the case for a major architecture migration that leadership has already decided on. Engineers who have sat through her sessions before all report the same thing: she has no secret technical argument nobody has heard before — it is more a matter of how she frames things and the exercises she runs — yet everyone leaves genuinely convinced she is right, however skeptical they were going in. A colleague argues that since I can already predict, with near certainty, what I will believe by the end of the workshop, intellectual honesty requires me to adopt that belief now rather than stubbornly hold a view I already know I am about to abandon. Ordinarily I do think I should defer to my own future judgment once I have had more time to consider something, which is exactly what makes this argument hard to dismiss outright, yet giving in before the workshop even starts feels like a mistake. Is my colleague right that I should already believe the migration is correct, or is there a genuine difference between this case and the ordinary cases in which trusting my future self makes sense?
