For the past few months I've been part of a small weekly chaburah working slowly through Sefer Vayikra, and a recent session left me more unsettled than I expected going in. Our teacher laid out Maimonides' well-known account, from the Guide of the Perplexed (III:32, and again more specifically in III:46), of the sacrifices as a concession to a people not yet capable of abandoning the sacrificial idol-worship they had grown up with, and then read us Nachmanides' commentary on Vayikra 1:9, where he essentially accuses Maimonides of trivializing the altar and, more damningly, points out that Abel and Noah both offered sacrifices God found pleasing long before idolatry existed anywhere in the world -- which sits awkwardly with the claim that the whole institution exists only to wean people off idolatrous habits. I've always been temperamentally a "Maimonides person," but I left that session genuinely unsure he has a good answer to Nachmanides here, especially once you factor in the further awkwardness that Maimonides' own law code has the Messiah rebuilding the Temple and reviving the very sacrificial service that the Guide seems to treat, at best, as a concession that has outlived its purpose.

What I'd really like is to watch the two of them fight this out properly instead of just delivering their positions past each other. Could you write a dialogue between Maimonides and Nachmanides -- never mind that they barely overlapped in life; suppose they are somehow made to sit down together -- in which Nachmanides presses the pre-idolatry objection and the Mishneh Torah tension as hard as they can be pressed, Maimonides has to produce the strongest reply available to him rather than simply folding, and Nachmanides also gets to lay out his own positive account of what a sacrifice actually accomplishes, rather than just his complaints about Maimonides' account? Neither of them was a sloppy thinker, so please don't let either side collapse into a strawman: I want real engagement with each other's specific arguments, including a concession or two where intellectual honesty requires it, and I would like it left genuinely unclear by the end which of them has the better of the exchange.
