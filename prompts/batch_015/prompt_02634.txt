ok the Boltzmann brain thing has been living in my head rent free lately. standard argument: in a universe that ends up eternally de Sitter, random thermal fluctuations will eventually cough up way more freak 'Boltzmann brain' observers (full false memories and all) than there are ordinary observers who evolved the normal way, so when a cosmological model predicts we're almost certainly BBs, physicists treat that as a reason to junk the model. but here's what bugs me: if I'm a BB, my reasoning is garbage, just a brain that fluctuated into existence a second ago. so the move 'this model implies I'm probably a BB, therefore reject the model' seems to require trusting the exact faculties the conclusion says i shouldn't trust. I've seen someone try to route around this by comparing likelihoods of our actual, weirdly orderly observations directly instead of asking 'am I typical,' but i'm not convinced that actually escapes the trap. argue for the strongest version of the claim that this self-undermining structure means Boltzmann-brain-dominance can never be legitimate evidence against a cosmological theory, period -- and then tell me if that 'compare the raw likelihoods' dodge genuinely gets around the problem or just smuggles the same assumption back in.
