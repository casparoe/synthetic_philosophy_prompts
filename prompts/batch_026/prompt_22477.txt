I was discussing this with a friend who does econometrics, and she kept saying the interesting failure is not that people are deliberately gaming the metric, but that the optimization changes the world in which the metric was calibrated. I find that distiction a bit fuzzy, so I'd like an example that makes it crisp.

Please give an example of a conceptual issue where a decision-maker optimizes a proxy variable, nobody is intentionally exploiting the metric, and the proxy nevertheless becomes unreliable because the intervention shifts the relevant population or environment. Make it concrete enough that I can put it in a handout, but not so complicated that the point gets lost. Ideally it should show why the proxy was not merely a noisy estimator, and why collecting more data from the original pre-intervention distribution might not fix the problem.
