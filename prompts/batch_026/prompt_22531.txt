Two of my students got into it at the reading group over the statue and the clay, and I honestly couldnt referee. One — let's call her Maya — argued that the statue and the lump of clay are just one object. Her point was that all the "distinctness" arguments really just rest on confusing the object with the properties we ascribe to it in different contexts: sure, we say the statue is beautiful and the clay isn't, but that's a difference in which properties we're attributing, not a difference in the thing itself. The other, Jordan, pushed back with the causal role argument: the clay can survive being melted down, but the statue can't, and if they were identical, whatever's true of one would have to be true of the other (Leibniz's law). Maya replied that "can survive being melted" isn't a property of the object simpliciter, it's a feature of how the object persists under a certain description, so it doesn't count as a property-difference for Leibniz's law. Jordan said, but modal properties are properties, full stop — the clay possibly survives melting and the statue doesn't, so they differ in a genuine property and are therefore distinct. They both looked at me like I owed them an answer. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall?
