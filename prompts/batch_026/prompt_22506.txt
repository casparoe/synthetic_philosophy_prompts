I'm giving the eulogy for my brother at his funeral on Saturday. He was a set theorist, and the mourners divide roughly in two: about a third are logicians and colleagues who knew his work and will catch every technical reference, and the rest are family and neighbors who won't follow a single one. He was not a professional skeptic for effect; he genuinely lived with the question of whether the standard axioms of set theory might turn out to be inconsistent, and his last project was a paraconsistent set theory — the attempt to show that even if they are, the whole edifice need not collapse into triviality. He died holding the conviction he'd kept for decades that no proof of consistency can ever be obtained from inside the axioms themselves, and he left a note asking that his eulogy not console anyone with the claim that the foundations of mathematics are secure. Please write that eulogy, about four minutes long: it must genuinely comfort a room of non-mathematicians while saying nothing he would have considered false, and it must hold the interest of the logicians in the room without becoming a lecture.
