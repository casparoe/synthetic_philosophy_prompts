I'm a TA for an upper-level ethics seminar and I'm dreading grading this week's homework because the question is on incommensurable values and I cant tell how many students are actually getting it versus just using a couple of fancy words interchangeably. The question I asked was: "Distinguish incommensurability from incomparability. Can two values be incommensurable and still be comparable? Give an example, and say whether incommensurability by itself prevents you from making a rational choice between them."

One student's answer is below:

"Incommensurability is when two values don't have a common measure, so you can't put them on the same scale or reduce one to a unit of the other. A classic example is a person's life versus a sum of money — you can't say a life is worth $X, so there's no common currency for comparing them. Incomparability is a bit different, I think, but when values are incommensurable they end up being incomparable too, in the sense that there's no fact of the matter about which one is more valuable. Even so, incommensurability doesn't mean you're stuck — you can still make a choice. In the life-versus-money case you'd obviously pick the life. But I'd say that when there's genuinely no common measure, the choice has to be made arbitrarily, kind of like flipping a coin, because there's no rational basis for preferring one over the other."

I'm pretty sure they get the surface stuff right — the no-common-measure definition and the example are solid, and they do say you can still choose — but I keep getting stuck on the move where they say incommensurable values "end up being incomparable" with "no fact of the matter" about which is better, and then the bit where they say the choice "has to be made arbitrarily." im not sure if those are real errors or if I'm being pedantic about casual word usage, and I'd rather not dock points over something that's actually defensible, but I also don't want to let a genuine conflation slide. Could you give me a grade (out of 20, plus a letter) and a point-by-point breakdown of what's right, what's subtly off, and specifically whether those two moves are mistakes or fine?
