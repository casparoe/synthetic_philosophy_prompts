I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology and I want a real essay, not a survey, on whether the Löbian obstacle to self-trust is actually escapable. Setup: Löb's theorem implies that an agent whose reasoning is nailed down in one fixed formal system can't prove its own soundness without that system blowing up and proving everything, so a "purely logical" agent can only trust strictly weaker agents. The standard escape is that the agent carries a metalogical belief — held outside the system — that its logic is sound, and Hume's guillotine is suposed to keep that belief from being absorbed into the logic, which lets the agent trust its own proofs and delegate to subordinates. My worry is that this just builds a new system L' = L + that belief, and now L' hits the identical obstacle one level up, i.e. an infinite regress with no genuine self-trust at any fixed point. Weigh that and reach a defended conclusion (the escape is real because X, or it's a regress and the obstacle stands), and make sure the argument actually turns on the meta-level rather than just restating Löb's theorem.
