I am a novelist, and a book I am writing has kept bringing me back to Moore's *Principia Ethica*. He famously held that a world containing only the finest human affections — love, friendship, that sort of thing — would be better than a world containing only beauty, or only pleasure. I would like you to reason as he would and tell me what he would make of something he could not have imagined: a reader who, over months of rereading a novel generated entirely by a machine, comes to feel for its characters a bond that is, in every way she can tell, indistinguishable from friendship. On Moore's own principles — not on what you personally think is right — would he count that bond as one of the intrinsic goods he ranked highest, or would he say it falls outside the good, precisely because it is not a relation between persons? Give me his answer, and say where, if anywhere, his framework gives out.
