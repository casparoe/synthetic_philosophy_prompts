I am preparing a graduate seminar on the epistemology of markets and would like a concise but technically careful explanation of the no-trade theorem. Please explain why, if two Bayesian agents share a common prior and the terms of a proposed trade are common knowledge, the proposal itself can reveal information about the proposer’s beliefs so that the expected mutual gains from trade may disappear. In particular, distinguish the role of common knowledge from mere mutual knowledge, and clarify why the theorem is not simply the claim that rational agents should never trade.
