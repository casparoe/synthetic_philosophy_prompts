I have recently had a substantial discussion with a friend concerning the relative merits of retributive and consequentialist justifications for punishment, and I am currently serving on my university's student disciplinary committee, which is deliberating a case of academic misconduct in which a student plagiarized a paper. The student has a documented history of depression and anxiety, which they contend contributed to their decision. Our committee's policy is not clear about whether we should focus on the harm done (a retributive consideration) or the deterrent effect (a consequentialist consideration). I would like you to draft the committee's decision memo — whether to impose a sanction, and if so, which of the available sanctions (ranging from a formal warning to expulsion) — with reasons that engage both the retributive and consequentialist considerations, and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
