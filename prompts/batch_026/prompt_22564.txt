Two colleagues in my epistemology seminar argued about a technical point in Mary’s room. One said the knowledge argument establishes nonphysical facts: before leaving the room Mary knows all the physical facts, but after seeing red she knows what red looks like, and that must be a new fact. The other replied that the argument slides from “Mary learns something” to “Mary learns a new fact”; what she gains is a new ability or mode of presentation, not a fact about the world. The first countered that calling it an ability is question-begging, since to say she can now recognize red or imagine red already uses the phenomenal content that was supposed to be unknown. The second answered that this only shows the ability must be described without relying on Mary’s prior ignorance; it does not show that the world contains a new nonphysical fact. They asked me to referee. Please go through their exchange move by move and say who is right about each specific point, noting any equivocation and what both are missing about the distinction between learning a fact and acquiring a new cognitive relation to facts. End with a final verdict on its own line, in exactly this format: \answer{first speaker} or \answer{second speaker}.
