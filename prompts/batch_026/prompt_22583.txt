I'm modeling belief revision probabilistically, and I think I can see through the higher-order-evidence puzzle epistemologists keep arguing about. My take: any evidence about your evidence is still just evidence, so I can fold it into my posterior on the target proposition the ordinary Bayesian way, and then my belief simply tracks the posterior. Once that's done there's no leftover tension, so epistemic akrasia — believing p while taking your total evidence to support not-p — can't genuinely arise from higher-order evidence; it's just an artifact of failing to update correctly. If I'm wrong, where exactly does that folding break down, and why does the tension survive a correct update?
