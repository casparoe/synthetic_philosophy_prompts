The assignment question read: "The butterfly dream — the story of Zhuangzi dreaming he was a butterfly — is one of the most cited passages in the Zhuangzi. (a) State the 'dream skepticism' reading of the passage. (b) Some commentators hold that the passage is not primarily an argument for skepticism. Give the alternative reading, the term Zhuangzi uses to name the phenomenon, and the chapter of the Zhuangzi in which the passage appears. (c) On balance, does the butterfly passage support global epistemic skepticism? Justify your answer."

The student's answer was as follows:

"(a) The dream-skepticism reading is straightforward: while one is dreaming one is not aware that one is dreaming, so there is no internal marker that distinguishes a dream from waking life. It follows that one cannot be certain one is awake now, and the whole body of one's beliefs about the external world — including the belief that one is a person sitting in a seminar — is open to doubt.

(b) The alternative reading, which I think is the better one, is that the passage is not meant to establish that we know nothing. Its point is the relativity of identity and perspective: the boundary between 'Zhou' and 'butterfly' is not a fixed one, and what we take to be a stable, self-identical subject is really a process of change. Zhuangzi names this 'the transformation of things' (wuhua). The passage appears in the chapter 'On the Equality of Things' (Qiwu), the chapter in which Zhuangzi develops the idea that our ordinary distinctions are conventional rather than ultimate.

(c) On balance I think the passage does support global skepticism. Its force is that we have no access to the experience of a being other than ourselves: we cannot know what the butterfly experienced in its fluttering, any more than another person can know what I experience. If we cannot even attribute experience to the butterfly, we have no secure foundation for any belief about the world beyond our own immediate awareness."
