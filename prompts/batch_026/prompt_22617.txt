I'm writing a historical novel set in 1720. Richard Bentley — Newton's old ally, who solicited the letters behind his Boyle Lectures and pushed the second edition of the Principia through the press — visits Newton and, so that Newton can rehearse his answers, presses him as devil's advocate with the objections the Leibnizians have been making since the correspondence with Clarke. I need a scene where the two of them actually argue it out rather than just trade barbs. The case Bentley presses: the Scholium is a mystical digression that wrecks Newton's own method — if the system of the world needs a governor to keep it going, then "hypotheses non fingo" is doing less work than Newton thinks, because the Scholium itself is the hypothesis he says he doesn't frame, and it hands every atheist from here to Amsterdam the excuse they've been looking for. Newton's case: the regularity of the system is itself the evidence — the initial ordering of the planets, the Moon, and the comets is not explicable by any mechanical cause, and a philosophy that stops at "the laws hold" without asking why the system is ordered as it is doesn't escape hypothesis, it just hides it. I want both of them to be genuinely strong and to actually answer each other's best point at least once, not just alternate monologues. About a page, in period adjacent but readable prose.
