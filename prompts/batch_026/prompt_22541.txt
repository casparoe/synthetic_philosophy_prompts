I am writing a novel set in a community that trades by barter alone, with no coinage and no paper money, and I keep running into a difficulty I cannot seem to resolve. When a mason who needs food meets a farmer who needs a new roof, on what grounds are they to settle how many bushels of grain are worth how much labor on a roof—two things that seem to have nothing in common by which to be compared? And what of the mason's neighbor, who needs grain but has no use for a roof at all? I want the exchanges in my novel to feel governed by some internal logic rather than by my characters simply agreeing on a number, and I suspect that a community like this would, over time, gravitate toward some common yardstick of value. How should my characters fix the terms of an exchange, and what, if anything, would draw such a community toward a shared measure of worth?
