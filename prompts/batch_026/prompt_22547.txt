I’m TA-ing a short problem set for a seminar on Kant’s Groundwork and need a careful, point-by-point grading pass that separates a correct conclusion from a bad argument, not a charitable summary.

The exam question was:

Kant’s Formula of Universal Law requires that one act only on maxims that can be willed as universal laws. In the Groundwork, he distinguishes cases where universalization yields a contradiction in conception from cases where it yields only a contradiction in the will.

(a) State the maxim under consideration.  
(b) Explain why the maxim “I will make a false promise to obtain a loan, intending not to repay” fails the test by producing a contradiction in conception.  
(c) Explain why the maxim “I will never help others” does not fail by producing a contradiction in conception.  
(d) Explain why “I will never help others” still fails by producing a contradiction in the will.  
(e) State what this distinction implies about the moral status of the corresponding duties.

A student answered:

(a) The maxim is: make a false promise when I need money, with the intention of not repaying.

(b) If everyone made false promises whenever they needed money, lenders would have no reason to lend, because promises would no longer be reliable. The agent’s maxim therefore presupposes a practice of trustworthy promising while universalizing a rule that would destroy that practice. Since the maxim cannot even be coherently conceived as a universal law, it violates a perfect duty.

(c) The maxim “never help others” can be conceived as a universal law: a world in which no one helps anyone is not logically impossible. So universalizing it does not make the maxim self-contradictory in conception.

(d) A rational agent cannot consistently will such a universal law, because as a vulnerable being the agent may need help and would thus be willing a law that excludes the very assistance on which the agent depends. The contradiction is therefore in the will, not in the conception.

(e) Duties that fail by contradiction in conception are perfect duties; duties that fail only by contradiction in the will are imperfect duties.
