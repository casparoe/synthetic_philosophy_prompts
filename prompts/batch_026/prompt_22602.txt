Maya said Bell's theorem proves quantum mechanics is nonlocal: since no local hidden-variable theory reproduces the quantum correlations, there must be a faster-than-light influence connecting the particles.

Dev said that's a misreading. Bell's theorem rules out "local causality" — the condition that the complete past state screens off correlations between spacelike-separated events — but quantum mechanics still satisfies "no-signaling," the condition that actually prevents faster-than-light communication. Maya granted that you can't send a signal, but said "local causality" is the right notion of locality anyway: if, given the complete past, the outcome at one wing depends on the setting at the other, that's nonlocality, and the inaccessibility of the dependence to a local observer is a separate, less important point.

Dev conceded the dependence is real in the sense that the joint probabilities don't factorize, but resisted the word "influence": there's no signal, no energy transfer, no causal arrow, just a failure of a specific screening-off condition, which is a different notion from faster-than-light causation.
