During my surgical residency I have been asked to explain to patients why an inflamed appendix counts as a "malfunction" of an organ that many anatomy textbooks still call vestigial, and I have found myself unsure which account of biological function my clinical language is presupposing. Could you write a dialogue between a defender of Millikan's etiological account of function and a defender of a causal-role account, in which they argue over whether appendicitis is best analyzed as a malfunction, a misfunction, or an exaptation, and whether the appendix's hypothesized role as a reservoir for gut bacteria counts as a proper function or merely a causal role? Please write for an audience of graduate students in philosophy of biology, and make sure both interlocutors are strong, good-faith advocates of their own view who respond to each other's actual arguments rather than restating their positions.
