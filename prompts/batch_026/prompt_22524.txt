I've been trying to make sense of something called the reference class problem, where the probability of a single event, such as whether a particular patient will respond to a given treatment, seems to depend on which group you use to describe that patient, since the success rate differs across the class of all patients, the class of patients with their condition, the class of patients with their condition and their age, and so on. I had assumed this was a difficulty that bites the frequentist interpretation of probability in particular, on the grounds that a frequentist probability is a long-run frequency and so has to be anchored to some reference class. But it has occurred to me that a subjectivist, who treats a probability as a degree of personal belief, also has to decide which facts to take into account when forming that belief, which strikes me as exactly the same choice of reference class in different clothing. If so, the reference class problem is not a flaw in frequentism after all but a problem for any interpretation of probability that wants to assign a number to a single event, since every interpretation confronts the same underdetermination; is that a fair way to put it, or is there some way the subjectivist is supposed to sidestep the problem that I am not seeing?
