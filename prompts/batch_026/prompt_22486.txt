I am a novelist revising a novel in which one of my characters, a translator of early Chinese philosophy, keeps returning to Zhuangzi's butterfly dream while trying to understand why his own waking memories no longer seem authoritative. I have drafted the following passage for inclusion in the novel as a notebook entry: “The butterfly dream is often presented as a forerunner of Cartesian dream skepticism, but that comparison may flatten it. Descartes needs the dream hypothesis to generate a systematic doubt about the external world, whereas the Zhuangzian passage seems less concerned with establishing a criterion for distinguishing dream from waking than with unsettling the assumption that the waking self is the original center of experience. The question, on this reading, is not whether my perceptions are veridical, but whether the self that claims to be the subject of perception is the most stable thing in the situation. The dream does not prove that nothing is real; it suggests that identity is a matter of transformation rather than possession.” I would be grateful for comments on whether this is a defensible interpretation of the butterfly dream, especially whether it overstates the contrast with Cartesian skepticism or underestimates the skeptical force of the passage, and whether the final sentence is too abstract for a character's notebook.
