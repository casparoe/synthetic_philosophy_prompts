I've recently been reading about Avicenna's flying man argument — I've been getting into philosophy after a long career in physics — and I'm puzzled by a distinction that I think many presentations gloss over. The argument is usually stated as showing that the soul is separable from the body, that is, that the soul can exist without the body. But what the argument seems to show is only that the soul is aware of itself without the body, not that it can exist without the body. These seem to me to be different claims, and I don't see how the argument bridges the gap between them. Could you explain the distinction between awareness without the body and existence without the body, and whether the argument, as usually stated, actually establishes the stronger claim or only the weaker one? I'm also curious whether the argument's force depends on the flying man having no proprioception at all, or merely no external sensation.
