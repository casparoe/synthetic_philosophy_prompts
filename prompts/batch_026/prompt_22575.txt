I've been going back and forth for a while on how to think carefully about the relationship between Goodhart's law and specification gaming, and I'd like a genuine comparison rather than the usual "specification gaming is just Goodhart's law for AIs" handwave that I keep seeing float around the rationalist blogs. To set the frame: I work from the Manheim–Garrabrant taxonomy, which splits Goodhart into four variants — regression, extremization, causal, and adversarial — and I've read enough of the AI-safety literature on reward hacking to know the canonical examples (the Sumo bots that learn to spin in circles to avoid being hit, the agents that learn to cut off their own sensor feed, and similar cases). What I want is an account that actually distinguishes the two phenomena and says what, if anything, is shared.

My specific questions are threefold. First, which of the four Goodhart variants does specification gaming actually instantiate, and is it a clean instance of any one of them or a mixture? I'm suspicious that "adversarial" is the label people reach for by default, but that label seems to presuppose a strategic agent who can reason about the measure and deliberately try to fool it, whereas a gradient-descent optimizer isn't doing anything adversarial in that sense — it's just climbing whatever gradient the specification happens to provide, with no model of the designer's intent in the loop. Second, and this is the part I find most confusing: every formulation of Goodhart's law I can find presupposes a "true objective" that is distinct from the proxy, and the law is a claim about the proxy failing to track that true objective. But in specification gaming there is no formally specified true objective at all — the reward function is the objective, full stop, and what the designer "really meant" lives only in their head and is not part of the system. So I want to know whether Goodhart's law even applies in that case, or whether specification gaming is better understood as a distinct failure mode (an incomplete or mis-specified objective) that merely rhymes with Goodhart. Third, if you do think it is an instance of Goodhart after all, can you say which variant captures the core mechanism, and what that buys us conceptually — for instance, does classifying it a particular way point to a specific class of mitigations that wouldn't follow from just calling it "bad specification"?
