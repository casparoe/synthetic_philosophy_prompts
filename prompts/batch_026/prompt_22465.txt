Now that I'm retired from hydrology, I've been trying to work through some philosophy, and I keep coming back to a situation from a project I was helping with just before I left. I was reviewing a safety protocol for a small field research team. I signed off on a procedure that I thought was low risk, although I knew it wasn't zero risk. Nothing happened. A colleague at another site signed off on basically the same procedure, with the same information and the same kind of judgment call, and a student got hurt when a valve wore out a bit faster than expected. I keep telling myself that my decision was no more or less responisble than his, but when I think about the injury I feel much worse, and I'm not sure whether that feeling is justified or just an emotional artefact.

I'm not asking for comfort exactly. I'm trying to figure out whether there's a real difference between (a) whether my action was reasonable given what I knew, (b) how blameworthy I am, and (c) what I owe to the people who were exposed to the risk. Does the fact that his version produced a bad outcome mean he is more responsible, or does it mean we both bear some kind of responsibility that is just more visible in his case? I'd appreciate an answer that doesn't just say 'outcomes don't matter' or 'outcomes matter a lot', because I suspect the truth is messier than that.
