I'm a first-year PhD student in algebra, and I can't resolve a point about Zorn's lemma. It says that if every chain in a nonempty partially ordered set has an upper bound, then the set has a maximal element. The proof looks almost immediate to me: start with any element and, whenever a strictly larger one exists, move to it; the chain hypothesis seems to guarantee you can't keep moving forever without landing on a maximal element. If that reasoning is valid, Zorn's lemma should be a theorem of ZF, yet every reference I've checked says it's equivalent to the axiom of choice and unprovable in ZF. Where exactly does the "just keep picking" argument break down, and why is that breakdown fatal rather than a mere artifact of the formalization?
