I sit on my company's forecasting committee, which produces the probability estimates the board uses for capital allocation, and this quarter we have to put a number on whether our new logistics platform will be live by year-end. We're deadlocked on which base rate to anchor on, and I think the disagreement is really about how specific our reference class should be. One faction wants to use the industry-wide on-time delivery rate for projects of this size, which comes out to about 55% on a sample of a few hundred projects, but our stack and our team are unusual in ways that rate probably doesn't capture. Another faction wants to use our own internal track record for similar platforms, which is 6 out of 8 — more directly relevant, but a small enough sample that one bad quarter moves the number a lot. A third faction, mostly the PM, argues the project is genuinely novel and we should just take her subjective judgment (roughly 85%) at face value, which I suspect is a polite way of abandoning the reference-class approach altogether. I was talking this through with a colleague over lunch who keeps saying we should just weight the classes by sample size, and I'm not sure that's coherent, because the narrower classes are precisely the ones where the sample size is small.

Can you draft the committee's decision memo recommending which reference class — or what principled combination of them — we should use for this particular estimate? I need the reasoning to be something we could actually stand behind in front of the board, and I'd like an honest treatment of the strongest case for the approach we end up not adopting, because the PM will be in the room and she's sharp.
