I've been reading up on the centipede game for a little game-theory workshop I'm helping prep, and two of the people in the group got into it at the coffee machine over whether backward induction is actually doing anything controversial. Sam kept insisting the "centipede paradox" is just a confusion. Her line: at every node it's strictly better for the player to move to take now than to pass, because passing just hands the other player a strictly better take and you get nothing, and you can't count on them passing. So the unique subgame-perfect path is to take at the very first node, and the fact that both would end up richer if they both kept passing just means the game is rigged to punish cooperation -- a social dilemma, not a paradox. Mara pushed back and said backward induction is quietly assuming infinite, perfectly iterated common knowledge of rationality, and that assumption is doing all the work. Her point was that the induction chain is only as strong as its last link, and the last link (the final taker) is "rational" in a trivial, payoff-only sense, so a genuinely rational first player shouldn't be entitled to assume the whole chain actually gets carried out. On that view a rational player can reasonably pass at least once, betting the other will too, and backward induction is really a theorem about a very specific (and empirically dead) model of the players, not about rationality itself. Sam fired back that this just relabels the problem -- if you let yourself pass "hoping the other passes," the same move is available at every node, so youve swapped backward induction for naive forward cooperation with no principle telling you where to stop. Mara said there is a principle: pass up to the point where the other player's incentive to take finally outweighs your expected gain from one more pass, which is a well-defined stopping rule, just not the last node. They both looked at me and I honestly cant tell who had the better argument. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step -- not just who wins overall -- and flag what, if anything, both of them are missing?
