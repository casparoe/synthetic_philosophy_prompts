Argue that the "isolation objection" to coherentism (the claim that a coherent set of beliefs can be entirely disconnected from the world and from experience, and therefore that coherence alone is not sufficient for justification) is not a genuine objection to coherentism. In particular, argue that the objection only works against a weak version of coherentism (one that holds that any coherent set of beliefs is justified), and that a stronger version of coherentism (one that requires that the coherent set of beliefs include empirical beliefs) is not vulnerable to the objection.
