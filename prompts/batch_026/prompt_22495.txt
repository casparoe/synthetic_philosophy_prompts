Two friends of mine — one a software engineer, the other a self-taught math enthusiast — got into a long argument at dinner over whether Solomonoff induction, the thing people call "Occam's razor made rigorous," is a genuinely usable inductive method or just a mathematical ideal. The engineer started by saying that Solomonoff induction is exactly the razor formalized: you give each hypothesis a prior probability based on the length of its shortest description, so simpler hypotheses get higher probability, which is precisely what the razor says. The math enthusiast pushed back, saying that "shortest description" isn't objective, because Kolmogorov complexity depends on which computer you use to measure it — swap in a different machine and a different hypothesis can come out as the "simplest" — so the razor isn't as machine-independent as people claim. The engineer replied that this is a non-issue by the invariance theorem: the complexity values from any two universal machines differ by at most a fixed constant, so the ranking of hypotheses is stable and the choice of machine doesn't actually change which one is simpler. The math enthusiast conceded the constant point but said there's a deeper problem: Kolmogorov complexity is uncomputable, so the Solomonoff prior can never actually be computed, which means it isn't a real method you can use, just an ideal. The engineer agreed it's uncomputable but said that's a feature, not a flaw — it's the theoretical limit, the best possible inductive prior, and real-world methods approximate it, much as the ideal gas law is the correct model even though no real gas is perfectly ideal. The math enthusiast said that was a cop-out: if you can't compute it, it can't explain how we actually form beliefs, and the optimality result only holds in a limit you can never reach, so the whole construction is a curiosity that tells us nothing about real induction. I sat there unable to tell who was winning. Can you walk through their exchange and say who is right at each step, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
