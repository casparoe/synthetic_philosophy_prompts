I've been turning a puzzle over in my head for a while, and I'd like an essay that takes a real position on it. Suppose a glass is fragile, but a demon removes its fragility the moment the glass is about to be struck; is the glass fragile before the demon acts? And separately: is the same fragile glass, wrapped in a thick layer of lead, still fragile? My understanding is that these are the "fink" and "mask" cases, and that they are supposed to be trouble for analyzing dispositions as counterfactual conditionals — the idea that a thing is fragile just in case it would break if struck. I want an essay that weighs whether the two cases are equally damaging to that analysis, or whether one is a manageable complication and the other the real threat, and whether a fix like restricting the "if struck" clause to circumstances in which no fink or interferer is present can save the analysis — and why such a restriction seems to handle one case but not the other. Reach a conclusion: do finks and masks show that dispositions are something more than conditionals, or can the conditional analysis be saved? Defend your verdict rather than ending on a non-committal note.
