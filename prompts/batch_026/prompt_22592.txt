I've been reading a lot of rationalist writing on AI alignment, and I keep running into the question of how to naturalize the concept of "representation" in a way that doesn't smuggle in normative assumptions. I think teleosemantics is the most serious attempt to do this, but I'm not sure it works, and I'd like to think through the problem more carefully.

I'm taking a graduate seminar on the philosophy of mind, and we've been reading Millikan's *Language, Thought, and Other Biological Categories* and her later papers on the misrepresentation problem. I find the problem genuinely confusing, and I'm not sure I understand why it's supposed to be a problem for teleosemantics specifically, as opposed to any other causal-functional account of mental content. The basic idea, as I understand it, is that a representation's content is determined by its proper function, which is in turn determined by its evolutionary history: a belief that "there is a predator nearby" has that content because beliefs of that type were selected for because they reliably tracked the presence of predators. But then the question is: what counts as a misrepresentation? If the function of a predator-detection belief is to be true, then a false belief is a failure to satisfy its function, but this seems circular because the function is defined in terms of truth. Millikan's response, as I understand it, is to ground function in evolutionary history rather than in truth: the function of a representation is to track the kind of thing it was selected to track, and a misrepresentation is a token that fails to track that kind of thing in the current environment.

Could you write an essay on whether teleosemantics can handle misrepresentation? The essay should weigh the main arguments on both sides: the standard circularity objection, Millikan's evolutionary-history response, and the "novel environment" problem (what is the function of a representation that was selected for in one environment but is now in a different environment, such as a predator-detection mechanism that is now in a zoo?). The essay should also consider whether the problem is specific to teleosemantics or whether it affects any causal-functional account of mental content. Please reach and justify a conclusion about whether teleosemantics can handle misrepresentation, or whether it faces an insurmountable problem.
