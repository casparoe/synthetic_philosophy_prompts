I've been working through Franklin's account of his project for moral perfection — the thirteen virtues, the little chart where he marked a black dot for every slip, and his rule of tackling one virtue a week. The detail that keeps getting to me is humility: he only added it to the list after a Quaker friend told him he was insufferably proud, and then he confesses that he never really acquired the virtue itself, only the appearance of it. Write me a joke that turns on that specific self-referential trap — that keeping score of your own humility, and taking satisfaction in it, is exactly the pride you were supposed to be curing. It should land for someone who actually knows the details of the project, not just anyone who's heard of Franklin.
