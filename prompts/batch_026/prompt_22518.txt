I've been thinking on my own about how we manage to understand what other people mean, and I've run into a puzzle I can't resolve. The account I've been reading, Grice's, says that when I say something, I mean it in a particular way only if I intend you to come to believe something on the strength of what I said, and that you understand me by working out what I was trying to make you believe. But then I noticed something: for you to work out my intention, you have to recognize that I intend you to recognize it; and for that, I have to intend that you recognize that I intend it; and it looks like the layers just keep stacking — intending that you recognize that I intend that you recognize — with no bottom. I'd like you to write an essay that takes this regress seriously and weighs whether it is a genuine defect in Grice's account of speaker meaning, or whether it is actually harmless. Consider what would have to be true for it to be a real problem, and what would have to be true for it to dissolve. Weigh both sides, but don't end on a non-committal note — reach a clear verdict and defend it.
