Write a dialogue between two philosophers of action debating whether omissions can be genuine causes. One defends a counterfactual difference-making account on which a doctor's withholding of morphine is a real cause of the patient's death; the other concedes the moral asymmetry but insists that only positive events can stand in the causal relation, and that responsibility for omissions is a distinct, non-causal phenomenon. Both should press the strongest version of their view and engage the "too many causes" objection — that a naïve difference-making account makes virtually every omission an agent makes a cause of virtually every bad outcome — rather than merely asserting positions. Neither character should be a strawman, and each should respond to the other's actual points.
