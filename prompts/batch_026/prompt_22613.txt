Please describe the most important implications of Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism for the free-will and moral-responsibility debate. On his view, developed in his critique of the falāsifa, no created thing has genuine causal power: fire does not burn cotton by any force of its own, but God directly produces the burning at the instant of contact, and what we call causation is nothing more than God's customary habit. Given that, explain what happens to human moral responsibility—since on this account a person's actions are willed and produced by God—and how Ghazālī's doctrine of kasb (acquisition) is meant to preserve responsibility even though the agent is not the true creator of the act.
