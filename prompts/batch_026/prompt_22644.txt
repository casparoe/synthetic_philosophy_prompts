I'm a third-year philosophy PhD student who has recently joined a working group on the philosophy of quantum computation, and I keep running into the conflation of two claims that are both called the "Church-Turing thesis." Please compare the ordinary Church-Turing thesis — the claim that the intuitively computable functions are exactly the Turing-computable ones — with the physical Church-Turing thesis, the claim that no physical system can compute a function that is not Turing-computable. In what precise sense does each claim go beyond the other, and why do philosophers of computation tend to treat their epistemic status differently — for instance, in terms of what would count as a refutation of each, and whether the existence of quantum computers bears on either claim?
