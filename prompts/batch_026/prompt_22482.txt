I work in software engineering, specifically on distributed systems, and I have no formal training in philosophy or in the foundations of probability. I've been reading about the self-sampling and self-indication assumptions and the so-called "presumptuous philosopher" objection, and I want to know what, if anything, the debate implies for a problem I actually have. My service runs N replicas; a client request is routed to exactly one of them; and the replica that receives it observes an anomalous event and has to infer the global state of the system. The catch is that the probability of the event depends on the global state, and it seems to me that whether I reason from "I observed the event" or from "some replica observed the event" changes my answer by a factor that depends on N. What are the most important implications of the SSA/SIA debate for how I should treat that indexical evidence — in particular, when is it legitimate to let N enter my credence, and when does doing so make me the presumptuous philosopher?
