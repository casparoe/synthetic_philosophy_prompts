John von Neumann died in 1957, decades before computer scientists began exploring "program equilibrium"—the idea that agents able to inspect each other's source code might escape the Prisoner's Dilemma through mechanisms like Löb's theorem-based cooperation, rather than relying on repeated play or external enforcement. As the co-founder of game theory and a pioneer of computing, von Neumann was uniquely positioned to have anticipated such ideas. What do you think his reaction would have been to program equilibrium as a solution concept? Would he have embraced source-code transparency as a legitimate new equilibrium refinement, or dismissed it as a mathematical curiosity irrelevant to real strategic conflict?
