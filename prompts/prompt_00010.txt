I've been sitting in on a philosophy of physics seminar this semester, and last week we spent the whole session hashing out the differences between Everettian many-worlds, Copenhagen collapse, and Bohmian mechanics with its pilot wave. Afterward a few of us went to get drinks and ended up trying to one-up each other with terrible physics puns, and I want to bring something genuinely good back next time to redeem myself.

Write a joke that turns on the actual conceptual differences between these three interpretations of quantum mechanics — not just a generic "schrodinger's cat" reference, but something where the humor depends on knowing, say, that Everett has no collapse and instead branches the universe, that Copenhagen treats collapse as fundamental and mysteriously tied to measurement, and that Bohm keeps a deterministic hidden variable (the pilot wave) guiding a particle that always had a definite position. Ideally someone who only vaguely knows "quantum stuff is weird" wouldn't find it funny, but someone who actually understands the metaphysical commitments of each camp would laugh precisely because it plays on where they disagree.
