Hey all -- been mulling this over for next week's seminar. Everyone always compares Pascal's Wager to the St. Petersburg paradox, but I think the more interesting comparison is between St. Petersburg and Pascal's Mugging. Both hinge on an agent facing some kind of divergent or unbounded expected value calculation, but the structure of the two cases feels pretty different to me -- one's a repeatable gamble with known odds, the other's a one-off threat based on a strangers testimony. Compare the two: where do our reasons for rejecting the St. Petersburg bet actually carry over to the mugging case, and where do they break down? Don't just rehearse the "expected utility diverges" line -- get into why the cases pull on such different intuitions.
