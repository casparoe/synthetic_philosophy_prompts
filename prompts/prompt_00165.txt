My friend and I spent an evening arguing about causation and never quite converged. She insisted that Woodward's interventionist framework is really just Lewis's counterfactual analysis wearing new clothes, since interventions are themselves cashed out counterfactually ("had we intervened to set X, Y would have changed"). I maintained that the appeal to well-defined interventions does substantive work that bare similarity-of-worlds semantics cannot, especially in cases of causal preemption and symmetric overdetermination. Please compare counterfactual and interventionist theories of causation directly: explain what interventionism actually adds beyond restating counterfactual dependence, and assess whether it genuinely resolves the hard cases (preemption, overdetermination, fine-grained causal structure) that trouble Lewis-style accounts, or merely relocates the difficulty.
